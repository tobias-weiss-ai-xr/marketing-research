# Research Proposal — G1: A Conceptual Framework for Contextual Intelligence in Marketing

**Working title:**
*Contextual Intelligence in Marketing: A Conceptual Framework for AI-Driven,
Situation-Aware Value Creation*

**Type:** Conceptual / theory development paper (Fills the `analytics/theory` and
`b2b/theory` thin cells; complements Haas's conceptual-papers agenda.)

---

## 1. Purpose & Research Question

**Problem:** The marketing field uses "contextual" operationally (contextual
advertising, contextual targeting) and industry uses "contextual intelligence"
as adtech vocabulary, but **no academic construct defines what it is, what it
comprises, or how to measure it** in the modern AI era.

**Research questions:**
- **RQ1 (constitutive):** What is *contextual intelligence in marketing (CIM)*?
  How does it differ from identity/behavioral-based personalization on one hand,
  and from the 2007-08 strategic-management "contextual intelligence" on the other?
- **RQ2 (dimensional):** What are its constituent dimensions (situational,
  spatial/temporal, channel/omnichannel, social/interpersonal, intent)?
- **RQ3 (process):** How do AI/agentic systems acquire, represent, and act on
  context across the marketing value chain?
- **RQ4 (nominological):** What are its antecedents, moderators, and performance
  outcomes? How does it relate to trust, privacy, and incrementality?

## 4. Conceptual architecture (draft)

**Definition (draft):**
> Contextual intelligence in marketing (CIM) is the dynamic capability of an
> AI-enabled marketing system to sense, represent, and act on the situation in
> which a customer decision, message, or transaction occurs — integrating
> situational, temporal-spatial, channel, social, and intent signals without
> reliance on persistent identity data.

**Dimensions (5):**
| Dimension | Definition | Corpus anchor |
|-----------|-----------|---------------|
| 1. Situational | moment-of-truth environment, mind-set, decision stage | consumer-behavior/case-study |
| 2. Temporal-spatial | when/where (time, geolocation, device) | digital-marketing/case-study |
| 3. Channel/Omnichannel | fused real-time state across touchpoints | cx-retail/framework |
| 4. Social/Interpersonal | social context, influence, norms | social-media/case-study |
| 5. Intent | expressed & latent goal signals, buying situation | privacy-data (intent graphs) / b2b |

**Distinguishing CIM from alternatives:**
- vs. identity-based personalization → context-based, privacy-preserving
- vs. 2008 strategic "contextual intelligence" → operational/AI, not just market-orientation
- vs. Kutz leadership CI → system-level (not individual-leader) capability
- vs. plain "contextual advertising" → a general capability, not just the
  programmatic-cookie-substitute playbook

**Distinguishing CIM from the Umeå thesis (the nearest competing claim):**
Häglund (2025, Umeå) occupies the *computing-science / NLP* space: opinion-unit
extraction, aspect-based sentiment analysis, media-context effects on ad perception
(spillover, brand-safety), and a contextual-vs-personalized trade-off guide. It is a
**technical, algorithmic program** — it does **not** deliver a marketing *construct*.
It has **no** definition/dimensionalization of CIM as a firm capability, **no**
marketing-theory nomological net, **no** measurement scale, and **no** agentic /
omnichannel / B2B dimension. Its flagship paper ("AI-Driven Contextual Advertising:
Toward Relevant Messaging Without Personal Data", J. of Current Issues & Research
in Advertising 2024) is **already in this corpus** (papers.yaml) as part of the
cookieless thread. **Positioning: CIM (ours) is the marketing-theory complement to
Häglund's computer-science program — different discipline, level of analysis
(system capability vs. NLP method), and research question (construct & outcomes vs.
method & mechanics).** Cite Häglund (2025) and its list of papers as the
nearest-prior-art anchor to make the construct-claim explicit and non-overlapping.

**Process model (SAO-CIM, adapted from Endsley):**
1. **Sense** — context acquisition (signals, first-party, ambient)
2. **Represent** — context state/representation (world-model / situational state)
3. **Act** — adaptive decision & message/offer/engagement selection
4. **Orient/Adapt** — feedback loop, learning, counterfactual/incrementality eval
   → ties to the measurement agenda (G2/G3).

**Propositions (sample):**
- P1: CIM moderates the relationship between message personalization and
  engagement, such that high-CIM systems outperform identity-based personalization
  in privacy-restricted settings.
- P2: CIM improves incrementality by reducing irrelevant exposures (less waste)
  rather than by more precise targeting.
- P3: CIM's effect is stronger in B2B (complex buying situations) and in
  omnichannel journeys with fragmented identity.
