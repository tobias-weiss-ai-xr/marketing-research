# Fifth-Round Adversarial Review — Reviewer 2

**Manuscript:** *CAM-Sim: A Reproducible Benchmark for Situational-Awareness Ablations in Marketing Agents* (`paper/g4_academic_paper.md`, DRAFT v1.0)
**Reviewer stance:** Four prior rounds, four REJECTs, all on the same dispositive axis: an author-designed reward surface whose dominant policy is context-free. v0.9 added the matched-family ablation and the majority floor; round 4 proved the floor was mis-specified and that a constant `EDUCATIONAL|EMAIL|bid 0.3` policy earns `+$252.41`, beating every deployable agent. v1.0 does not attempt to fix that; it *discloses* it, reframes the claim, and defers the fix. This round I read the paper in full, re-ran the committed code, re-ran my own full constant-policy grid, and re-ran the nine-environment replication. All numbers below are my own computations from the authors' unmodified `paper/cam_sim.py` and `paper/cam_sim_ablations.py`.
**Venue claim:** *NeurIPS Datasets & Benchmarks* / *KDD* / *Journal of Interactive Marketing* / *Journal of Marketing Analytics*.

---

## Summary

v1.0 is a real integrity step. Of the six round-4 attacks, four are cleanly resolved, one is partially resolved, and one is disclosed but not fixed. Specifically:

- I re-ran `paper/cam_sim_ablations.py` unmodified. It reproduces the paper's default numbers to the cent: `multisignal_vs_inferred` `+$101.87` CI `[+$98.41, +$105.49]`; `ms_learned_vs_learned` `+$50.01` CI `[+$46.62, +$53.69]`; `ncc_intent_vs_ncc_multi` `−$103.85` CI `[−$107.55, −$100.13]`. The sign convention in §8.6 (`cam_multisignal − ncc_intent_only = +$103.85`) matches the code after the direction flip. NA-4 is genuinely fixed.
- I independently re-ran the full constant-`(action, channel, bid)` grid on the EMAIL channel (6 action types × 11 bids) and confirmed that `EDUCATIONAL|EMAIL|bid 0.3 = +$252.41` is the default-economics optimum, not a hand-picked single point. The disclosed arbitrage number is correct.
- I independently re-ran the nine-environment replication. The context-blind EMAIL policy beats the best *deployable* context-aware agent in exactly **7 of 9** environments (default `+252.41` vs `+206.13`; uniform `+168.05` vs `+160.67`; decision `+151.31` vs `+65.31`; crisis `+159.11` vs `+141.93`; high_costs `+248.41` vs `−57.32`; weak_signal_bonus `+254.56` vs `+180.08`; budget_constrained `+252.41` vs `+195.40`), losing only in `retention_heavy` (`+177.31` vs `+237.26`) and `concave_returns` (`+398.09` vs `+675.46`). The paper's "7 of 9" is accurate.

So the arithmetic is honest and the disclosure is not spin. The problem is that disclosure plus reframing is a statement *about* the benchmark defect, not a repair of it — and v1.0 then leaves the defect in the artifact, keeps the practical recommendation that the defect falsifies, and adds two new factual errors (an unqualified "beats every context-aware agent" and a non-reproducible §8.7) while committing no code for the new section. Round 5 does not move the verdict, but it narrows the distance: the paper is now one config change away from a defensible benchmark, and it deliberately did not make that change.

## NA-Resolution

**NA-1 (context-blind EMAIL policy dominates; FATAL in round 4) — DISCLOSED, NOT RESOLVED.** v1.0 adds §8.7, §9.2 #25, the abstract caveat, and §10.3 #20. The disclosure is complete and accurate: `BASE_REWARDS` is indexed by `(action_type, situation)`, not channel, while `ACTION_COSTS[EMAIL] = 0.05` vs `SEARCH = 1.20`, so a constant cheap-channel policy harvests the full reward table at near-zero cost. The paper now explicitly says the multi-signal gain is "measured *within* the context-aware ladder, not against all context-free policies." I credit all of that. But the fix — making reward channel-dependent — is deferred to v2.0 (§10.3 #20), and none of the nine robustness presets varies the defect. Every headline number in §8.1–§8.6 is still measured on a reward function the paper admits is arbitrageable. Disclosure changes what the paper *claims*; it does not change what the benchmark *measures*. NA-1 is now a disclosed benchmark-construction defect rather than a hidden fatal flaw, but it is not closed.

**NA-2 (matched-family ablation not matched on fitting; text misdescribes it) — RESOLVED, with a residual wording error.** §8.6 now discloses that `cam_multisignal` uses hand-set generative centroids (the true means) while `ncc_intent_only` uses fitted centroids from seed 999999, and adds the correctly matched `cam_multisignal_learned` arm. I verified that `MULTISIGNAL_CENTROIDS` equals `SITUATION_CHANNEL_QUALITY`/`SITUATION_COMPETITIVE_DENSITY`/intent-strength means exactly, so "hand-set = true means" is accurate. Residual error: the same sentence says "the 3-signal arm is therefore a lower bound." A classifier built on the true generative means *upper-bounds* the fitted NCC estimator, not lower-bounds it; the intended meaning (NCC accuracy is a lower bound on the Bayes rate, per §7.1/§9.1) is different and contradicts the local context. The next sentence — "differs only in signal count" — is falsified by the disclosure two sentences earlier (it also differs in fitting method). Both are one-line fixes but both are factual, in the paragraph written to fix a factual error.

**NA-3 (reported gap is the maximum over three one-signal comparators) — PARTIALLY RESOLVED.** The paper now states a "fair value band: +$50 (learned-vs-learned) to +$103.85 (family-matched maximum)" and labels roles in §9.3. But the labelling is internally inconsistent, which creates a new attack (NA-5.3 below): §8.6 and §9.1 call `+$50.01` "the fair learned-vs-learned gap," while §10.3 #11 calls `+$103.10` (`cam_multisignal_learned − ncc_intent_only`, both NCC and both fitted) "the fair learned-vs-learned gap." Two different numbers carry the same "fair" label, and the one that is actually matched on both family and fitting method (`+$103.10`) is nearly double the headline `+$50`. Partial credit.

**NA-4 (sign convention on `+$103.85`) — RESOLVED.** The §8.6 table now reads `cam_multisignal − ncc_intent_only = +$103.85` with bootstrap CI `[+$100.13, +$107.55]`; §10.1/§10.2 carry `+$103.85`. This matches the code (`ncc_intent_vs_ncc_multi` returns `−$103.85` because the script computes `ncc_intent − cam_multisignal`; the paper's flip is consistent). Confirmed.

**NA-5 (`d_z = 7.89` is `t/√50`, not an empirical effect size) — RESOLVED.** §8.6, §10.2 #3, and §10.3 #11 all label it as `t/√50` from a deterministic paired comparison. Minor asymmetry: the other paired `d_z` values (`3.96`, `7.88`, `1.43`) are not given the same caveat, though they are the same quantity. Acceptable.

**NA-6 (abstract still sells the weak floor and an unqualified multi-signal claim) — PARTIALLY RESOLVED; new errors introduced.** The abstract now discloses the arbitrage. But it (a) writes "beating every context-aware agent" without the body's "deployable" qualifier, which is literally false (see NA-5.2), (b) keeps "A majority-action floor (35.1%, −$15.38) retires the random-action baseline" as if the floor question were closed, and (c) writes "The advantage holds across nine environments (per-seed ρ ≥ 0.93)," which conflates the multi-signal-profit advantage (only the hand-set arm is run per-environment; the learned arm is default-only, §10.3 #11) with the label-free ρ consistency check. The parity problem has shrunk but has not closed.

## New-Attacks

**NA-5.1 (MAJOR, reproducibility): §8.7 is not reproducible from any committed script, and the paper's own reproducibility footer says it is.** The v1.0 commit `df03efd` touched only `paper/g4_academic_paper.md`; `paper/cam_sim_ablations.py` was last changed in the v0.9 commit (`7d24bb5`). That script contains `MajorityActionAgent` (SEARCH), `NCCIntentAgent`, and the paired bootstrap — and **no EMAIL policy, no `(action, channel, bid)` search, and no per-environment context-blind run**. Its agent list is `["baseline", "majority_action", "ncc_intent_only", "cam_inferred", "cam_learned", "cam_multisignal", "cam_multisignal_learned"]`. Yet §10.1's closing footer states: *"context-blind policy search regenerates from `paper/cam_sim_ablations.py`."* It does not. The `+$252.41`, `+$236.46`, `+$214.62` table and the "7 of 9 environments" claim are unbacked by the artifact. I had to write my own script to confirm 7/9. This is a direct violation of the paper's stated reproducibility contract and of the repo rule that all numbers regenerate from committed code; for a paper whose stated contribution is *a reproducible benchmark*, it is disqualifying until fixed.

**NA-5.2 (MAJOR, factual overclaim): "beating every context-aware agent" is false as written in the abstract, §10.1, and §10.2 #3.** The body §8.7 and §9.2 #25 correctly say "beating every **deployable** context-aware agent." The abstract (line 25), the summary (§10.1, line 547), and contribution #3 (§10.2, line 552) drop the qualifier. The context-blind EMAIL policy earns `+$252.41`; `situation_only` earns `+$294.27` and `bid_calibrated` earns `+$530.45`. Both are context-aware agents in the paper's ladder. The unqualified claim is therefore false twice. The fix is trivial (restore "deployable"), but the abstract is the most-read sentence in the paper, and currently it asserts a false universality.

**NA-5.3 (MAJOR, magnitude/consistency): the "fair learned-vs-learned gap" denotes two different numbers, and the headline uses the family-confounded one.** There are four one-vs-three signal contrasts in play: `cam_multisignal − ncc_intent_only` `+$103.85` (same NCC family; hand-set vs fitted — *both* family and fitting differ), `cam_multisignal_learned − ncc_intent_only` `+$103.10` (same NCC family, same fitting, differs only in signal count — the cleanest design), `cam_multisignal − cam_inferred` `+$101.87` (hand-set vs handicapped threshold), and `cam_multisignal_learned − cam_learned` `+$50.01` (NCC vs interval — confounds family with signal count). §8.6/§9.1 call `+$50.01` "the fair learned-vs-learned gap / the conservative estimate"; §10.3 #11 calls `+$103.10` "the fair learned-vs-learned gap." Both cannot be "the fair" gap. Worse, the abstract and §10.1 headline the `+$50` figure, which is precisely the comparison that *mixes classifier families* — the confound round 3 raised and NA-2 was supposed to retire. The properly matched estimate (`+$103.10`) is roughly double the headline and has no committed CI. The paper must pick one estimator, define "fair" once, and use it consistently; if "fair" means "same family and same fitting," the headline is `+$103.10`, not `+$50`.

**NA-5.4 (MODERATE, validity): the reframe leaves the practical recommendation standing on the admitted defect.** §10.1 still calls "invest first in **multi-signal situation classification**" the paper's "central practical finding," and §9.3 still says multi-signal awareness "raises **deployable profit**." The paper's own §8.7 shows a context-blind constant is the better investment in 7 of 9 environments. A within-ladder ablation is a legitimate scientific object, but the recommendation is not within-ladder — it is advice to practitioners, and it is derived from an instrument the paper says is broken. The nine-preset robustness suite reinforces the wrong confidence: none of the nine presets makes reward channel-dependent, so "replicates across 9 environments" is orthogonal to the one defect that dominates the outcome. The one cheap fix (a channel-match bonus/penalty) was available and was not run.

**NA-5.5 (MINOR, residual NA-1 inconsistency): the floor language survives in three places.** §8.6 still calls the SEARCH-channel policy "the strongest context-blind baseline"; §10.1 still says the majority-action floor "retires the random-action baseline" without noting it is SEARCH-only; and contribution #1 (§10.2) still lists the profit span as "−$170.03 … to −$15.38 (majority-action floor) to +$294.27 … to +$530.45," omitting the true context-blind floor of `+$252.41` that sits inside that span. §8.7/§9.2 #25/§10.3 #19 correct these, but the earlier text was not updated, so the paper contradicts itself within one manuscript.

## Minimum-Viable-Experiments

Ordered by cost; #1–#3 are sufficient to reconsider the verdict.

1. **Fix the reward and re-run (hours).** Add a channel-match term (or make `BASE_REWARDS` channel-indexed) so wrong-channel assignments cost reward, then re-run the full ladder and the nine presets. This is the decisive experiment. If the learned multi-signal gain (`+$50`/`+$103`) survives the fix, the benchmark becomes defensible as an ablation instrument and the practical recommendation regains support; if it does not, the paper is a demonstration that its own reward table, not situational awareness, drove the result. Either outcome is publishable. Deferring this to v2.0 while publishing v1.0's recommendations is the wrong order.
2. **Commit the §8.7 search (hours).** Put the constant-`(action, channel, bid)` grid into `paper/cam_sim_ablations.py` (or a new `cam_sim_policy_search.py`), including the per-environment table that backs "7 of 9." Without this the reproducibility footer is false and the central new section is unverifiable.
3. **Standardize the matched contrast (hours).** Report `cam_multisignal_learned − ncc_intent_only` (`+$103.10`) with its paired CI and p-value from the committed bootstrap, and use exactly one definition of "fair learned-vs-learned" throughout. Fix the §8.6 "lower bound" and "differs only in signal count" sentences.
4. **Run `cam_multisignal_learned` across the nine presets (hours).** Then "the advantage holds across nine environments" is literally true for the learned arm; otherwise delete the clause from the abstract.
5. **Abstract/contribution parity pass (an hour).** Restore "deployable" in three places; correct the profit span in contribution #1; drop or qualify "the strongest context-blind baseline" in §8.6.
6. **Contextual-bandit / nonlinear comparator (a day).** Still absent, still deferred (§10.3 #12). Not required to change my verdict, but it is the difference between "multi-signal beats the authors' own single-signal classifier" and "multi-signal helps against a competitive learner."

## VERDICT

**REJECT at the claimed venues (NeurIPS D&B / KDD / JIM / JMA) in current form — major revision, with a bounded and now unusually concrete path.**

**Justification.** v1.0 is the most honest version of this manuscript so far, and I want to be precise about what it earns. Four of the six round-4 attacks are cleanly resolved: the hand-set-centroid disclosure (NA-2), the sign convention (NA-4), the `d_z` interpretation (NA-5), and the multi-comparator magnitude discussion (NA-3, mostly). I re-ran the ablations and reproduced every reported number to the cent, and I independently confirmed the `+$252.41` optimum and the 7/9 environment count. The authors did not fudge the disclosure; they surfaced the exact defect I found in round 4, in the body, the limitations, and the abstract. That is real scientific integrity and it materially changes the character of the paper.

But a benchmark paper is judged by what its benchmark measures, and v1.0's benchmark still measures a channel-cost arbitrage that it admits dominates its headline capability. The decisive repair — channel-dependent reward — is a small config change the authors chose to defer to v2.0, leaving every result measured on an instrument the paper itself flags as invalid for the deployable claim. On top of that, v1.0 introduces two new correctness problems: the abstract/contributions claim the context-blind policy beats "every context-aware agent" when it loses to `situation_only` and `bid_calibrated`, and §8.7 is not reproducible from any committed script despite the footer's explicit claim that it is. And the headline multi-signal magnitude is still the family-confounded `+$50` while the properly matched same-family, same-fitting estimate is `+$103.10`, labelled "fair" only in a future-work bullet. None of these is fatal on its own; together, with the still-deferred fix, they keep the paper short of a venue bar.

The path is short and I would likely flip on experiments #1–#3. To their credit, the authors have already done the hard part: they found and documented the flaw. What remains is to fix it, or to stop making the practical recommendation that depends on it.

*Skipped in this review: re-verifying the ML-methodology and domain-expert round-5 claims (other reviewers cover them), and the field-validation design (§10.3 #1), which remains future work and is not on the critical path for a benchmark venue.*
