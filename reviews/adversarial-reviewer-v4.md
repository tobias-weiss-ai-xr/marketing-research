# Fourth-Round Adversarial Review — Reviewer 2

**Manuscript:** *Context-Aware Agentic Marketing: A Situational Awareness Framework for Autonomous Marketing Systems* (`paper/g4_academic_paper.md`, DRAFT v0.9)
**Reviewer stance:** Three prior rounds, three REJECTs, all on the dispositive point (author-designed reward surface). This round I read v0.9 in full, re-ran the paper's own `paper/cam_sim_ablations.py`, re-ran the main ladder, and independently probed the two questions §8.6 claims to have closed: the classifier-family confound (New-Attack 1) and the engineered floor (New-Attack 4). I also ran my own context-free policy search and a corrected matched-family contrast. All numbers below are my own computations from the authors' unmodified code.
**Venue claim:** *NeurIPS Datasets & Benchmarks* / *KDD* / *Journal of Interactive Marketing* / *Journal of Marketing Analytics* (retargeted from JM/MS in v0.9).

---

## Summary

v0.9 does what round 3 asked, in a narrow sense: it runs the matched-family ablation, runs the majority-action floor, and adds a paired-difference bootstrap. I reproduced all three from the authors' code:

- **Majority-action floor:** `35.09%` match, `−$15.3834`. The paper reports `35.1%` and `−$15.38`. **My round-3 number is reported faithfully** — I confirm the paper did not fudge it. (The v0.9 commit message's `-5.38` is a typo in the commit text only; the paper, abstract, §8.6, §10.2 and §10.3 all carry `−$15.38`.)
- **Matched-family ablation:** `ncc_intent_only` `72.7%` / `+$102.28`; `cam_multisignal` `98.9%` / `+$206.13`; gap `+$103.85`, `p = 5.16e-46`, `d_z = 7.89`, paired-bootstrap CI `[−$107.55, −$100.13]`. Reproduces exactly.
- **Paired-difference bootstrap:** `B = 2000`, percentile, `seed = 123`. `cam_multisignal − cam_inferred` `[+$98.41, +$105.49]` vs normal `[+$98.30, +$105.44]`; learned-vs-learned `[+$46.62, +$53.69]` vs `[+$46.42, +$53.60]`. Within `$0.30`. Confirmed.

So the arithmetic in §8.6 is correct and the reproduction contract holds. The problem is that the experiment the paper runs to *retire* the engineered-strawman objection does the opposite: it certifies one point in the context-free policy space and declares the floor retired, while a one-line search over that same space produces a context-blind policy that out-earns **every deployable agent in the paper**, in **7 of 9 environments**. The matched-family ablation is likewise real, but it is not the matched comparison the text describes, and it is run in exactly one environment. Round 4 therefore does not move the verdict — it supplies a new, cheap, verified falsifier of the paper's headline deployable claim.

## Round-3-Attacks-Resolved

**Round-3 New-Attack 1 (classifier-family confound in the flagship) — PARTIALLY RESOLVED.** The confound was: `cam_learned` is a greedy **interval** rule on one feature; `cam_multisignal_learned` is **nearest-centroid** on three. §8.6 now adds `ncc_intent_only`, nearest-centroid on intent alone, at `72.7%` / `+$102.28`. I verified the code (`fit_ncc_intent_centroids`, one-dimensional Euclidean distance, same `CAMOracle._bid` as the multi-signal agent) and the number. The gap `+$103.85` is therefore genuinely present **within the NCC family and holding the bid policy fixed** — I re-derived it two ways: learned-3-signal minus learned-1-signal `= +$103.10` (`d_z = 7.71`) with oracle bidding, and `+$97.60` (`d_z = 7.82`) with both on flat bidding. The signal-count effect survives. That is the strongest thing v0.9 does. But (a) the ablation compares a **hand-set** three-signal centroid to a **fitted** one-signal centroid while the prose claims "the same calibration sample" (see New-Attack 2), and (b) it is reported only in the default environment, not the `default + 9 presets` round 3 specified. Partial credit, not resolution.

**Round-3 New-Attack 4 (engineered strawman / majority floor) — NOT RESOLVED; WORSENED.** The paper is correct that an always-EDUCATIONAL flat-bid agent beats the random `baseline` (`−$15.38` vs `−$170.03`). It is wrong to call this "the strongest context-blind baseline." It is one hand-picked cell of the context-free policy space — action `EDUCATIONAL`, channel `SEARCH` (cost `1.20`, the second-most-expensive channel), bid `1.0`. I searched the full product of action × channel × bid with the paper's exact harness and the fixed policy `EDUCATIONAL | EMAIL | bid 0.3` earns **+$252.41** (`match 35.1%`); bid `0.5` earns `+$236.46`; bid `1.0` earns `+$214.62`. All three **beat `cam_multisignal` (+$206.13), `cam_multisignal_learned` (+$205.39), `cam_learned` (+$155.38), and `cam_inferred` (+$104.26)`** — i.e., every deployable context-aware agent in the paper. An independent direct simulation (no agent-order RNG coupling) gives the same result (`+$250.65`). Across the nine presets (40-seed replication) the context-blind email policy beats the best deployable context-aware agent in **7 of 9** — default, uniform, decision-, crisis-, retention-shifted economics included — and loses only in `retention_heavy` and `concave_returns`. Under `high_costs` it wins `+$248.53` to `−$56.28`. The floor is not retired. It is the new fatal result.

**Round-2 New-Attack 15 (bootstrap underpowered / marginal not paired) — RESOLVED.** The paired-difference bootstrap is implemented correctly (`paired_bootstrap_ci`: percentile on the vector of 50 seed differences, `B = 2000`, fixed seed), and I confirm the `$0.30` agreement with the normal approximation. The caveats I would add are minor (New-Attack 4 below).

**Carried, unchanged:** circularity (#1; see next section); missing nonlinear/bandit comparators (#13); match-rate-not-profit (§9.2 #11/#22, now weaponized against the paper by my floor experiment); sensing decoy (#24, disclosed); references-as-corpus (#10, still non-citable). No field evidence — design promised, not begun.

## New-Attacks

**New-Attack 1 (FATAL): The context-blind floor dominates every deployable agent, and §8.6 certifies the wrong cell.** This is New-Attack 4 from round 3, now proven. §8.6's claim — "the floor is now a majority-action policy, not a random agent" — silently fixes channel and bid. The environment's `ACTION_COSTS` are `EMAIL 0.05`, `DISPLAY 0.60`, `SOCIAL 0.80`, `SEARCH 1.20`, `VIDEO 2.50`, while `BASE_REWARDS` are channel-independent. EMAIL is therefore a near-free channel that pays full rewards, and the framework's `SITUATION_CHANNEL` mapping routes only `RETENTION` (10% of the default distribution) to it. A constant EMAIL policy harvests the cheap channel on 100% of traffic. The paper's headline claim — "multi-signal situation classification raises deployable profit" (§10.1) — is false under the paper's own environment against the natural context-free comparator. This is not a request for another robustness preset; it is a demonstration that the benchmark's best *context-free* policy beats its best *context-aware* policy. A benchmark whose sophistication loses to a constant is not evidence for the capability it names. It is fatal to the deployable reading at *any* venue, including a datasets-and-benchmarks track, because it is a benchmark-construction defect, not an external-validity quibble.

**New-Attack 2 (MAJOR): The matched-family ablation is not matched on fitting method, and the text misdescribes it.** §8.6 states: "Because both classifiers are nearest-centroid with the same calibration sample, the gap is signal-driven." Read the code. `ncc_intent_only` uses `fit_ncc_intent_from_env(...)` — centroids estimated from the `seed=999999`, `n=2000` calibration draw. `cam_multisignal` uses `MULTISIGNAL_CENTROIDS`, which the source itself labels "hand-set centroids" and which are the generator's **true means**, not a fitted quantity and not drawn from the calibration sample. The matched comparison on fitting method is `cam_multisignal_learned` (fit on `999999`/`2000`) vs `ncc_intent_only`: `+$103.10`, not the reported `+$103.85`, and `cam_multisignal` never touched a calibration sample at all. Two consequences. First, a concrete factual error sits inside the fix for the previous factual error. Second, the ablation isolates the signal-count effect for the NCC family **in one environment only**; the per-environment version round 3 requested is absent, so the paper still does not show that the matched-family signal advantage survives distribution shift, which is precisely the regime (F5/F6) where it claims the advantage matters most.

**New-Attack 3 (MAJOR, magnitude): The reported signal-driven gap is the maximum over three one-signal comparators.** The paper now has three intent-only comparators: `cam_inferred` `75.7%` / `+$104.26`, `cam_learned` `87.5%` / `+$155.38`, and `ncc_intent_only` `72.7%` / `+$102.28`. The matched-family ablation compares against the **weakest** of the three and reports the resulting gap (`+$103.85`) as the signal-driven effect, while the fair learned-vs-learned estimate is `+$50.01`. The paper is aware of the distinction (§9.1) but promotes the larger number into the abstract and §10.2 #6 ("isolates the gain as signal-driven"), where "the gain" is ambiguous. A matched-family design answers "does signal count matter holding family fixed?" — yes, by `~$98–103`. It does **not** license replacing the `+$50.01` headline with `+$103.85`. Both should be stated, with roles labelled.

**New-Attack 4 (MINOR, bootstrap): Implementation is correct; the inference still leans on the t-test and on not-quite-paired seeds.** The percentile bootstrap on paired differences is right. But (a) the `p`-values in §8.6 are from `compute_statistics` (paired t-test), not the bootstrap, so the bootstrap confirms intervals only; (b) the `50` "paired" seeds are paired on context but **not on decision noise** — the paper's own §9.2 #10 says the seed-level correlation is `−0.23` to `+0.08` — so the paired bootstrap inherits the same weak pairing and buys no power; (c) the matched-family table row is sign-inconsistent: it is labelled `cam_multisignal − ncc_intent_only` yet shows `−$103.85` with CI `[−$107.55, −$100.13]`, while the prose and §10.2 report `+$103.85`. The code's contrast is `ncc_intent_only − cam_multisignal`, so the table label or the signs are wrong. Fix the row; keep the prose.

**New-Attack 5 (MINOR, interpretation): `d_z = 7.89` is `t/√50` from a deterministic paired comparison, not an empirical effect size.** Both matched-family classifiers are deterministic functions of the shared context sequence; the only seed-level variance is the context draw. `d_z` therefore measures simulation reproducibility, not generalization. Reporting it beside the `$103.85` gap in the abstract and §10.2 invites exactly the over-reading §9.2.1 warns against ("differences are statistically detectable at n = 50, even if economically negligible"). Report the match-rate gap (26.2 pp) and the profit gap with its CI; drop `d_z` from the abstract for this contrast.

**New-Attack 6 (MINOR, parity): Abstract still sells the weak floor and the unqualified multi-signal claim.** The abstract writes "a majority-action floor (35.1% match, −$15.38) retires the random-action baseline" — literally true, but presented as floor-closing while New-Attack 1 shows the true context-free floor is `+$250`. It also writes "matched-family ablation (same nearest-centroid family, 1 vs 3 signals) confirms the gain is signal-driven" without the "hand-set vs fitted, default-only" caveat the body now needs. The abstract/body parity problem from rounds 2–3 has shrunk; it has not closed, and this round it moved into the new ablation.

## Circularity and the Venue Reframe

The reframe from JM/MS to *NeurIPS D&B* / *KDD* / *JIM* / *JMA* **half-neutralizes** the circularity attack. It correctly concedes that no field claim is on offer, and at a benchmark venue, disclosed author-designed reward surfaces (limitation #1) plus an ablation study are publishable if the claims are diagnostics. But it does not neutralize the deeper objection, because v0.9 did **not** do the two things round 3 attached to the reframe: run the adversarial-mapping condition (§10.3 #3, still future work for the fourth round) and convert the deployable language to diagnostics. §10.1 still says multi-signal classification "raises … deployable profit" and §9.4 still cites `ρ ≥ 0.93` as evidence "Endsley's Level 1–2 model applies to marketing agents" while the body demotes `ρ` to a partly-mechanical consistency check.

More importantly, New-Attack 1 shows the circularity is no longer the *only* fatal problem. The environment is not merely self-confirming; it is **arbitrageable by a context-blind constant**, which means the benchmark cannot support the capability claim even as a diagnostic. A benchmark for context awareness whose best context-free policy wins is measuring the cost table, not context awareness. So the venue change fixes the *venue mismatch* objection (I no longer hold the paper to field evidence) but not the *measurement validity* objection, and the latter is now empirically demonstrated rather than argued.

## Minimum-Viable-Experiments

Ordered by cost. I have already run #1 and #2 and will hand over the code on request.

1. **Fix the floor, then re-issue the claim (hours).** Define the context-blind comparator as `argmax` over constant `(action, channel, bid)` policies evaluated with the paper's own harness. It is `EDUCATIONAL | EMAIL | bid ≈ 0.3`, `+$252`, beating every deployable agent. The paper must either (a) so fix `ACTION_COSTS`/`BASE_REWARDS` that no context-free policy dominates (the honest fix — the current EMAIL asymmetry is an author choice), or (b) drop the "context-aware agents outperform" deployable claim and reframe the contribution as "an environment exposing a context-blind channel/bid arbitrage." Without this, no other experiment matters: the headline is contradicted by six lines of policy search.
2. **Run the matched-family ablation properly and per-environment (a day).** Same family **and same fitting method** (`cam_multisignal_learned` vs `ncc_intent_only`), across all nine presets, with the corrected `+$103.10` default number and the per-preset table. Then the confound I raised in round 3 is actually closed, not just relocated.
3. **Macro-F1 and per-class recall (hours).** Requested in round 3, still absent. The majority-class match rate is `35.1%`, so raw accuracy is unbalanced and `ncc_intent_only`'s `72.7%` needs per-class visibility to be interpretable.
4. **Adversarial-mapping condition (≈ a week).** The single experiment that addresses the author-designed mapping; fourth round as future work. Either run it or move the framework-validity claim explicitly and completely to future work — including §9.4 and the abstract.
5. **Contextual-bandit baseline (a day).** `ε`-greedy or LinUCB on the same three signals; the deployable-policy comparator §5.4 promises.
6. **Registered field pilot (§10.3 #1).** Not needed for the benchmark venues; mandatory for JIM/JMA field claims.

## VERDICT

**REJECT at the claimed venue (NeurIPS D&B / KDD / JIM / JMA) — with a clear major-revision path.**

**Justification.** v0.9 is a genuine step: the reproduction contract holds to the cent, my round-3 majority-floor number is reported faithfully (`35.09%`, `−$15.3834`), the matched-family gap survives my corrected controls (`+$103.10` learned-vs-learned, `d_z = 7.71`), and the paired-difference bootstrap is implemented correctly and agrees with the normal approximation within `$0.30`. I credit all of that. But the two attacks v0.9 set out to kill are not dead. The classifier-family confound is statistically weakened yet textually misdescribed — the "same calibration sample" sentence is false for `cam_multisignal`, which uses hand-set generative means — and the ablation covers one environment, not the nine round 3 specified. The engineered-strawman objection is not only unresolved, it is now decisive: the paper's "majority-action floor" is a single hand-picked `(EDUCATIONAL, SEARCH, bid 1.0)` cell, and a trivial search over constant policies finds `EDUCATIONAL | EMAIL | bid 0.3` at `+$252.41`, beating **every deployable agent in the paper** and winning in **7 of 9** environments, often by large margins (`high_costs`: `+$248.53` vs `−$56.28`). Under the paper's own environment, the best context-free policy beats the best context-aware policy. That falsifies the central deployable claim, and it is a benchmark-construction defect that survives the venue reframe. The reframe does correctly retire the JM/MS field-evidence objection, and I no longer press it; the paper's remaining problems are its measurement validity and the fourth-round deferral of the adversarial-mapping remedy, and both are now empirical, not rhetorical. The three-round convergence on numerics is real; the science still has to survive a context-free constant. Reject, with experiments #1–#2 (and, for the framework claim, #4) as the minimum to reconsider.
