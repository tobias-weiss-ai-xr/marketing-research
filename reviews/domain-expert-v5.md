# Domain-Expert Review, Round 5: CAM / CAM-Sim v1.0

**Reviewer profile:** Same reviewer as rounds 1–4 (`reviews/domain-expert.md` … `reviews/domain-expert-v4.md`) — 15 years in programmatic buying and demand-side platforms (Google Ads, DV360, Meta, LinkedIn, The Trade Desk). Fifth-round review of v1.0, the revision that fixes the two round-4 items and discloses the channel-cost arbitrage.

**Scope:** `paper/g4_academic_paper.md` (v1.0). I read the paper in full, re-ran `paper/cam_sim_ablations.py` to test the reproduction contract, and checked the two round-4 fixes and the status of my five field-trial conditions. I did not re-run the full 11-agent ladder.

## Summary

v1.0 is v0.9 plus five moves: title and abstract reframed to a benchmark; the sign convention fixed; §9.3's fair value band quoted with roles; the §8.7 channel-cost arbitrage disclosed (with new limitation §9.2 #25 and future-work §10.3 #20); and the deployable claim reframed to *relative* value inside the context-aware ladder. The evidence base is unchanged — ladder, nine environments, and F1–F9 are as in v0.9.

My assessment in brief: both round-4 fixes are substantially done — the sign convention fully, the §9.3 decomposition partially — and the channel-cost arbitrage is the most consequential change of the round. It does change the business reading of +$50, downward: it demotes +$50 from a business case to a conditional, within-stack premium, and it moves the paper's deliverable from "a capability that pays" to "a harness that exposes when it doesn't." The five field-trial conditions stand verbatim and unmet; the arbitrage adds a sixth, practice-critical condition (channel economics must be specified before the trial endpoint means anything). Verdict on practice relevance: ACCEPT as a benchmark/diagnostic artifact, with the +$50 reading explicitly downgraded and §10.3 #1 not yet fundable.

## Fixes-Assessment

**Fix (i), sign convention — DONE.** §8.6 now reads "+$103.85," the bootstrap table row is `+$103.85 [+$100.13, +$107.55]`, and the abstract, §10.1, §10.2, and §10.3 are consistent and positive. One residual nit: the reproducing script still emits the old sign. I ran `python3 paper/cam_sim_ablations.py`; it prints `ncc_intent_vs_ncc_multi: +-103.85 CI [-107.55, -100.13]` because the contrast is defined as `ncc_intent_only − cam_multisignal`. The arithmetic is right, but a reader who runs the paper's own artifact sees the round-4 bug reappear. Relabel the contrast `cam_multisignal − ncc_intent_only` and the script matches the text. (Also noted by round-4 adversarial as New-Attack 4c; it is a code/text mismatch, not a paper error.)

**Fix (ii), §9.3 value decomposition — PARTIAL.** Round 4 asked for two honest magnitudes: (a) the majority-floor decomposition (≈ +$221 over the static-playbook null, not +$376 over random) and (b) the fair two-signal premium (+$50, not +$104). v1.0 delivers (b) cleanly — §9.3 now says "fair value band: +$50 (learned-vs-learned) to +$103.85 (family-matched maximum)" with roles attached, which is exactly the practitioner guardrail I wanted. It does not deliver (a): the +$221-over-floor delta is stated nowhere; §8.6 describes the floor but never subtracts it. In mitigation, disclosing the +$252 context-blind EMAIL policy (Fix iii below) is a *stronger* honesty correction than the +$221 floor would have been, because the true context-free comparator is not the SEARCH-channel floor at all. The intent of (ii) is therefore met by a better route, but the literal request is half-done.

**Two smaller items.** First, `learned-vs-learned` is now used for two different contrasts: §8.6 reserves it for +$50.01 (`cam_multisignal_learned` vs `cam_learned`, cross-family), while §10.3 #11 calls +$103.10 (`cam_multisignal_learned` vs `ncc_intent_only`, same-family) "the fair learned-vs-learned gap." Both numbers are defensible; the labels should be pinned (e.g., "cross-family learned" vs "same-family learned"). Second, §8.7's figures are not reproducible from any committed artifact. The paper's footnote says "context-blind policy search regenerates from `paper/cam_sim_ablations.py`," but that script contains no channel/EMAIL policy search — I read it end to end; it runs seven agents and three contrasts only, and `grep` finds no EMAIL / +$252 / context-blind policy code anywhere under `paper/`. The +$252.41 figures trace to the round-4 adversarial reviewer's independent search. Real numbers, missing artifact; one committed function closes it, and it should be committed given the disclosure is now the round's headline.

## Business-Reading

**Does the channel-cost arbitrage change the business reading of +$50? Yes — and downward.**

The +$50 (`cam_multisignal_learned` +$205.39 − `cam_learned` +$155.38) is a within-ladder, same-bid, cross-family premium for two added signals. §8.7 then shows EDUCATIONAL|EMAIL|bid 0.3 = +$252.41, beating every *deployable* context-aware agent in the paper. So +$50 answers "what do two more signals buy, *given* a context-aware stack and a fixed channel?" — not "should I build the stack?" It is a second-order, conditional premium, not an ROI. The recommendation "invest first in multi-signal classification, then in calibrated bidding" is now scoped by its own sentence to "…but channel-cost structure can dominate signal quality" — i.e., the actual first investment is channel economics, and signal quality comes second. That is a real demotion: the round-4 recommendation carried an implicit absolute-value reading; v1.0 correctly strips it.

Two qualifications keep the demotion proportionate. (1) The arbitrage is a construction defect, not a marketing fact: `BASE_REWARDS` are channel-independent while `ACTION_COSTS` are not (EMAIL $0.05), so the cheapest channel is a free lunch the real world does not offer — §9.2 #25 and §10.3 #20 say so. It falsifies the *simulation as absolute evidence* far more than it falsifies the *mechanism*, which is real (competitive density and channel quality genuinely disambiguate crisis↔decision and retention↔exploration, the documented confusions). (2) It leaves the relative finding intact: holding family, bid policy, and action mapping fixed, 1 vs 3 signals is worth +$103.10 (both fitted) to +$103.85 (family-matched maximum), with +$50 as the conservative cross-family floor. For a practitioner: keep +$50 as a directional estimate of the marginal value of two signals inside a committed stack; do **not** quote +$50, +$104, or +$206 as a business case for context awareness. The decisive number — does multi-signal awareness beat a context-free constant under a channel-dependent reward? — is deferred to v2.0 and is the only result that would reopen the absolute reading.

## Field-Trial-Conditions

All five round-3 conditions stand verbatim, unmet, and untouched by v1.0:

- **(a) Labeling protocol** — §10.3 #1 still specifies "human-coded situations" with no coder count, agreement threshold, throughput, or per-label cost; the 2,000 pre-launch recalibration labels remain unsourced; #22 still concedes no ground-truth situation label in production. UNMET.
- **(b) Incrementality** — §9.2 #12 still concedes no incrementality or carryover in the reward; the trial's primary endpoint remains between-arm uplift with no within-Arm-A holdout. UNMET.
- **(c) Crisis guardrail** — §6.5 still hardcodes the 2.0× crisis multiplier; #23 still defers it; Arm A still deploys "exactly the `cam_multisignal_recalibrated` pipeline." An incident-generating default documented in the limitations is still not launch-safe. UNMET.
- **(d) Signal acquisition** — no availability, latency, cost, or missing-intent fallback anywhere; #6 still describes the signals as supplied, not sensed, and the sensing layer as a decoy. UNMET.
- **(e) Arm A bid policy / window** — still unspecified whether Arm A bids flat or context-multiplied (a known-bad confound from F3), and the 8–12-week window is still fixed against an unmodeled B2B sales cycle. UNMET.

The arbitrage adds a **sixth** condition, and it is practice-critical: the field trial must specify channel selection/allocation and a channel-dependent reward, or its primary endpoint can be won by channel economics rather than situational accuracy — precisely the confound §8.7 just exposed in simulation. As long as §10.3 #1 is the paper's priority experiment, these six conditions are the entire distance between the paper and practice. None blocks the benchmark submission; all six block a funded deployment.

## VERDICT

**ACCEPT as a benchmark/diagnostic artifact — unchanged from round 4 — with the +$50 business reading explicitly downgraded and the channel-cost fix promoted to the top of the pre-deployment queue.**

Both round-4 fixes are substantially done: the sign convention is fixed in the paper (residual: the script still prints the old sign), and §9.3 now quotes the fair +$50–+$103.85 band with roles (residual: the +$221 majority-floor decomposition is not stated, and a committed artifact for §8.7 is missing). The channel-cost arbitrage is the honest and correct disclosure of the round, and it does change the business reading of +$50: +$50 is a conditional within-stack premium, not a value proposition — a context-free constant beats every deployable agent in the paper's own environment. The mechanism claim survives; the absolute deployability claim does not, and the paper now says so plainly.

For practice relevance, the honest summary is: v1.0 is a good harness and a useful null-selection lesson, not yet a deployment case. The five field-trial conditions remain the path, all unmet, now joined by a sixth — a channel-dependent reward specification, without which the field trial inherits the arbitrage as a confound. §10.3 #1 should not be funded until §10.3 #20 (channel-dependent reward) and the six conditions are addressed. **Verdict: ACCEPT (diagnostic), not deployable.** For the fifth round running, the distance to practice is the same five items, plus channel economics.
