# Desk-Review, Round 3 (Final): Journal Editor Assessment

**Paper:** *Context-Aware Agentic Marketing: A Situational Awareness Framework for Autonomous Marketing Systems* (`paper/g4_academic_paper.md`, v0.7)
**Reviewer persona:** Senior editor, *Journal of Marketing* / *Marketing Science* (same editor as rounds 1–2, `reviews/journal-editor.md`, `reviews/journal-editor-v2.md`)
**Decision window:** 10 minutes, desk review only (no external referees consulted)
**Editorial question this round:** v0.7 closes the statistical emergency flagged in round 2. Is the manuscript now submission-clean, do the structural objections still stand, and what is the final disposition of this three-round exchange?

## Summary

v0.7 is the round-2 repair executed correctly. The critical attribution error is fixed everywhere it appeared: the learned-vs-learned contrast (+$50.01) now carries its own statistics (p = 8.5e-32, d_z = 3.96) in the abstract, F9, §9.1, §9.3, §10.1, and §10.2, and the orphaned d_z = 0.56 / p = 2.5e-04 values are correctly reassigned to the contrast they actually belong to (hand-set vs. learned multi-signal, §9.2.1). The hand-set/learned conflation is gone — the abstract now pairs 87.5% learned with 98.7% learned, and the hand-set 98.9% is consistently labeled a nearest-centroid lower bound with the MAP ceiling (≈99.2%) disclosed. The stranded "privacy-safe by design / cookieless" claim is deleted and replaced with an honest statement that the intent scalar behaves like fully-observed identified-user intent. ρ(match, profit) is reframed as an environment-consistency check, the B = 200 bootstrap is demoted from confirmatory to diagnostic, the non-monotone convergence path is acknowledged, and two new limitations — #23 (the crisis 2.0× bid multiplier is likely backwards for most crises) and #24 (the implemented sensing layer emits a decoy signal uncorrelated with the real one) — complete the picture. I verified the six occurrences of the corrected statistic and the removal of the privacy claim directly; the fix is clean.

This is now a statistically clean, unusually honest, fully reproducible simulation benchmark. It is also — unchanged, and now with even more of the authors' own evidence — not a *Journal of Marketing* or *Marketing Science* article. The revision has reached the point where the honesty itself has become the diagnosis: the paper's own limitations section now impeaches the two framework layers (sensing, action-mapping) that make it a "framework paper" rather than a benchmark paper.

## Round-2-Concerns-Addressed

1. **Statistical mis-attribution in the headline contrast — RESOLVED.** p = 8.5e-32, d_z = 3.96 now appears with every statement of the +$50.01 effect, and §9.2.1 correctly carries 2.5e-04 / d_z = 0.56 for the hand-set-vs-learned contrast. One copy-edit residual: F9 reports the learned-vs-learned CI as [+$46.4, +$53.6] where the round-2 audit's recomputation gives [+$46.5, +$53.5]; immaterial rounding, but reconcile before any submission.
2. **98.9%-vs-98.7% conflation — RESOLVED.** All six affected locations now distinguish hand-set (98.9%, nearest-centroid lower bound, MAP ≈99.2%) from learned (98.7%, the empirical result). §7.1's inline note is exactly right.
3. **Stranded privacy claim — RESOLVED.** Deleted; the replacement §9.3 bullet concedes the signal regime cookieless environments remove.
4. **ρ as environment-consistency check — RESOLVED** (§9.1 H4 caveat, §10.2 note 5). Note the abstract still compresses this to "dose-response evidence" without the caveat; tolerable in an abstract, but referees at a quantitative venue will ask.
5. **Bootstrap demotion, convergence candor — RESOLVED in the body, NOT in the abstract.** §10.2 and §9.2.2 do the demotion correctly. But the abstract and §10.1 still say the advantage "is confirmed by bootstrap BCa CIs (200 resamples) and seed-count convergence" — confirmatory language the body has explicitly retracted ("demoted from confirmatory to diagnostic"; "convergence is not formally established"). This is the one place v0.7's left hand contradicts its right. Change "confirmed" to "checked" or "diagnostically supported" and the manuscript is internally consistent.
6. **Limitations #23 and #24 — added, and they are significant admissions.** #23 concedes the framework's only marketing-authored content (the situation→action matrix) contains a crisis response that standard brand-safety practice says is backwards and that an autonomous agent could amplify an incident with. #24 concedes the implemented Sensing Layer emits a `quality_score` uncorrelated with the actual signal — i.e., the layer the framework is named for is not the thing being tested. Both are accurate; both cut against the paper's title-level claims.

## Statistical-Integrity

Resolved, with the exceptions noted above. The inferential spine — paired t-tests over 50 shared-context seeds, Bonferroni-surviving p-values, normal CIs as primary with bootstrap as diagnostic, per-seed Spearman ρ with CIs replacing pseudo-inferential ladders — is now internally coherent, and the effect at issue (t ≈ 28, d_z ≈ 4) would survive any multiplicity scheme anyone could name. What remains is known, disclosed, and deferred with a numbered plan: paired-difference bootstrap at B ≥ 2,000 (#14), common random numbers (#5), class-conditional noise (#13), matched-family ablation (#11), per-environment learned-vs-learned (#11 in §10.3). None of these threatens the headline result; all of them are cheap relative to the polish they buy. For the record: three rounds ago the corpus pipeline impressed me; two rounds ago the statistics did not survive an afternoon; now they do. The authors fixed every numerical and attributional defect raised, in the direction of making their own claims smaller. That is the rarest revision behavior I see.

## Structural-Issues-Status

All three round-1/round-2 objections stand, now partially self-documented by the authors:

1. **No field or experimental evidence — UNCHANGED.** §10.3 #1 remains a pre-specified design; profit remains a scaled-reward proxy with "no industry analogue" (#12). No text revision can cure this, and none was attempted.
2. **Theory is a transplant — UNCHANGED, and slightly worsened.** The Endsley mapping still carries no marketing-specific proposition, Level 3 remains specified-but-not-benchmarked, and new limitation #23 means the one piece of domain content in the framework (the action matrix) is an admitted unvalidated author default with a flagged harmful case. A framework whose sensing layer emits a decoy (#24) and whose crisis response may be backwards (#23) is not yet a framework contribution; it is a hypothesis with a measurement apparatus.
3. **Scope fit — UNCHANGED.** Content balance is still ~70–75% simulation mechanics and results; the contributions list is still benchmark-shaped (reproducibility, BCa CIs, convergence diagnostics). The 24-item limitations section — four of them (#2, #7, #8, #9) still defensive "tested" rebuttals folded into a concessions list, a rhetorical mixture I flagged in round 2 — has grown into the paper's de facto conclusions.

On the honesty question directly: it is **exemplary, not excessive** — but it is now misaligned with the framing. Every disclosure is accurate and load-bearing; I would hold items #11–#17 and #23–#24 up as a model of what a limitations section should be. But a title that says "A Situational Awareness Framework" over a body that concedes the sensing layer is decorative, the action table is partly speculative, and the outcome is a proxy is a title the honesty has outgrown. The findings that survive the paper's own audit — multi-signal classification beats single-signal (+$50, learned-vs-learned); miscalibrated bidding is worse than flat; systematic bias under shift is worse than noise and recalibration fixes it — are real, interesting, simulation findings. They are not framework validation, and the manuscript should stop implying they are.

## Venue-Recommendation

This journal pair is the wrong instrument, and at desk level I am now confident saying so finally rather than inviting a fourth round:

1. **Submit CAM-Sim to a computational venue** — NeurIPS Datasets & Benchmarks, a KDD applied track, or on the marketing side *Journal of Interactive Marketing* / *Journal of Marketing Analytics*, where a reproducible paired-seed benchmark with this level of statistical hygiene would be competitive. First run the cheap deferred experiments that a methods-oriented referee will demand: adversarial-mapping condition (§10.3 #3), one contextual-bandit baseline (#12), matched-family ablation (#11), paired-difference bootstrap (#14), common random numbers (#5). Days of compute, not months.
2. **Execute the §10.3 #1 field validation as its own project.** With field data attached — ≥40 campaigns per arm, profit uplift as primary endpoint — the *empirical* paper that results is a *JM/MS*-shaped article, and the CAM framework can ride along as the organizing theory rather than having to carry the paper alone.
3. **Retitle and reframe whatever version ships first.** "CAM-Sim: a reproducible benchmark for situational-awareness ablations in marketing agents" claims exactly what the evidence supports; the current title claims more than limitation #24 permits.

## VERDICT

**DESK-REJECT — final at this journal, with an explicit invitation to return with field data.** Three rounds, three desk rejections, and the reasoning has been stable throughout: the defects are structural (no field-adjudicable evidence, no marketing-specific theory development, wrong scope), not rhetorical, and v0.7 has now exhausted what revision-without-new-research can do — the statistical record is clean, and the remaining problems are ones the authors have diagnosed more precisely than I could. The authors should read this verdict as what it is: the strongest possible encouragement to take an honest, reproducible, statistically sound benchmark to the audience that can adjudicate it, and to bring this journal the field experiment next. Disposition for the authors' file: (1) fix the abstract/§10.1 "confirmed by" contradiction and the $46.4/$53.6 rounding before any submission anywhere; (2) run the five cheap deferred experiments; (3) submit the benchmark to a computational venue; (4) field-validate, then return. I would be glad to see the field paper.
