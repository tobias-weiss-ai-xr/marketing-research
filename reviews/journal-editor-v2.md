# Desk-Review, Round 2: Journal Editor Assessment

**Paper:** *Context-Aware Agentic Marketing: A Situational Awareness Framework for Autonomous Marketing Systems* (`paper/g4_academic_paper.md`, v0.6)
**Reviewer persona:** Senior editor, *Journal of Marketing* / *Marketing Science* (same editor as round 1, `reviews/journal-editor.md`)
**Decision window:** 10 minutes, desk review only (no external referees consulted)
**Editorial question this round:** The authors revised in response to four first-round reviews. Were the concerns addressed, is the manuscript now adjudicable at this journal, and does it go to reviewers?

---

## Summary

v0.6 is the same study wearing better-tailored clothes. The revision is entirely textual — the authors' own commit record states "no simulation changes, all numbers unchanged" — and consists of (a) re-anchoring the headline on the fair learned-vs-learned comparison (+$50) with the handicapped +$102 demoted to secondary, (b) correcting the Bayes-rate mislabel (98.9% is now described as the nearest-centroid lower bound, with the learned 98.7% as the empirical result), (c) narrowing "first framework" to "no paper in our corpus" with an explicit keyword-search caveat and a new §5.4 positioning against contextual bandits, (d) relabeling the field design "pre-specified" rather than "pre-registered," and (e) expanding the limitations section from twelve to twenty-two items. Every one of these changes is an improvement, and several are unusually honest. None of them changes the three facts on which my round-1 desk decision rested: there is still no field or experimental evidence; the theoretical contribution is still a one-to-one transplant of Endsley's model with no marketing-specific proposition; and the manuscript is still, by content audit, a simulation benchmark (~70–75% infrastructure and results) rather than a marketing-theory or empirical paper. A text-only revision could not have cured these defects, and it did not.

I also note, from the review file trail, that the accompanying round-2 methodology audit (`reviews/ml-methodology-v2.md`) reports a reproducible reporting error in the very contrast this revision was supposed to fix: the learned-vs-learned +$50.01 effect is attributed the statistics of a different, trivial contrast (d_z = 0.56, p = 2.5e-04 appears to belong to hand-set-vs-learned; the audit computes d_z ≈ 3.96, p ≈ 8.5e-32 for the stated comparison), and the abstract's "87.5% → 98.9% (learned)" still quotes the hand-set classifier's match rate where the learned is 98.7%. I have not independently re-run the simulation, but the audit's arithmetic is plausible against the reported CIs, and the error appears in the abstract, F9, §9.1, §9.3, and §10.1. Whatever the venue, this must be resolved before resubmission.

## Round-1-Concerns-Addressed

My round-1 top-3 concerns, and their disposition:

1. **No field or experimental evidence — NOT addressed.** The §10.3 design is now honestly labeled "pre-specified" rather than "pre-registered," which is correct (there is no registry entry), but the honest label underscores that it remains a promise. §9.2 #12 still concedes the profit measure is a scaled-reward proxy with "no industry analogue," and §9.2 #6 still concedes the sensing layer is decorative. The evidence base is unchanged in kind.
2. **Theory is a transplant, not a development — NOT addressed.** No new propositions, no marketing mechanism, no falsifiable marketing hypothesis was added. The Level-3 projection layer remains specified-but-not-benchmarked (§6.6). §5.4's new contextual-bandit positioning is the closest thing to theoretical movement, and it is a *differentiation table*, not a derivation: the claimed distinctions (situation taxonomy vs. context vector; Endsley levels vs. reward-maximizing policy) are asserted, and the empirical test that would show the distinction matters (a LinUCB/ε-greedy baseline, §10.3 #12) is deferred.
3. **Corpus-bound novelty claim — ADDRESSED.** This is the genuine fix of the round. "First framework" is now "no paper in our corpus," the keyword-search basis is disclosed inline (§5.3), the adjacent literatures (contextual bandits, context-aware recommenders, Dey 2001) are cited and correctly characterized as "the closest algorithmic neighbor," and the zero-intersection finding is reframed as a terminological gap rather than conceptual novelty. Round 1 called this claim indefensible as written; it is now defensible. This is the model of how to concede a positioning error.

Secondary round-1 points: the abstract still reads as a results dump (now with two statistical corrections layered in, which does not help); the reference list still cites bulk placeholders ("All 70 agentic papers … papers.yaml"); and — a new inconsistency — §9.3 still claims "Privacy-safe by design … aligned with cookieless targeting" even after limitations #6, #20, and #22 concede that the intent signal behaves like fully-observed identified-user intent, exactly what cookieless environments remove. The revision's own candor has stranded this claim; it should be deleted.

## Remaining-Issues

- **The adversarial-mapping condition (§9.2 #1, §10.3 #3) is still deferred.** This remains the single experiment that would separate "CAM wins" from "following the authors' scoring rubric scores points." Until it runs, the central positive result is self-confirming by the paper's own admission.
- **No trained-ML or bandit comparator (§9.2 #13), classifier family = generative family (§9.2 #14).** The honest +$50 learned-vs-learned effect is measured on author-constructed, well-separated Gaussian clusters where the benchmark classifier is Bayes-optimal for the generator. As an empirical claim about "awareness," this is still a property of the construction.
- **Match rate still carries the dose-response.** Limitation #22 concedes match rate is not a practitioner KPI, yet H4/F7 still operationalize the framework's core dose-response as Spearman ρ(match rate, profit). The genuinely interesting finding — error *placement* modulates profit (F6) — remains a sub-result.
- **The reporting error above.** A desk editor cannot forward an abstract whose headline statistic is mis-attributed.
- **References remain submission-incomplete** (corpus placeholders, "REFS from corpus" in §4.3).

**On the 22-item limitations section specifically:** it is both the paper's greatest strength and its own refutation. As *rigor*, it is exemplary — items #14–#17 in particular (classifier family = generative family; B = 200 bootstrap underpowered and bootstrapping marginal rather than paired quantities; uniform rather than class-conditional label noise; vacuous N = 10 convergence) are disclosures most authors would bury. But read as a whole, the section is now effectively the paper's outstanding-TODO list: adversarial mapping, bandit baselines, matched-family ablation, common random numbers, macro-F1, paired bootstrap, perception/bid de-confounding — the experiments that would make the claims defensible are enumerated and unrun. A limitations section that documents this much unrun corrective work is telling the editor the study is not finished. Note also that four items (#2, #7, #8, #9) are labeled "tested" and read as defensive rebuttals folded into a concessions list; that rhetorical mixture is advocacy, and referees will notice.

## Scope-Fit

Unchanged from round 1, and unchanged by the revision: this is a reproducibility-disciplined benchmark paper with marketing vocabulary. The content balance (~25–30% positioning, ~70–75% simulation mechanics and results), the enumerated contributions ("reproducible paired-seed statistics," "bootstrap BCa CIs," "seed-count convergence"), and the venue-appropriate literatures it now engages (bandits, recommenders) all point to a computational-marketing, AI-systems, or marketing-analytics venue — where, for the record, this would be a competitive submission. It is not a JM/MS article in scope.

## Evidence-Adequacy

Unchanged in kind, and this remains decisive. Nine author-chosen environment presets, a curvature sweep, label-noise corruption, and budget-pacing wrappers are internal-consistency checks, not external validity — §9.2 #7 concedes this exactly. No field data, no human subjects, no archival grounding. The pre-specified field design is the right *next* paper and would be the *first* piece of evidence this journal could adjudicate. Reframing the headline (87.5% → 98.7%, +$50) makes the claim smaller and truer, which I credit, but a smaller true synthetic number is still a synthetic number.

## VERDICT

**DESK-REJECT** (second round, final at this journal). The revision addressed what a text revision can address — novelty claim, comparator fairness, statistical labeling, candor — and I want to be clear that the authors responded to criticism the way one wishes all authors did: precisely, without defensiveness, and mostly by conceding accurately. But my round-1 decision did not rest on rhetoric; it rested on the absence of field-adjudicable evidence, the absence of marketing-specific theoretical development, and scope fit. None of those was touched, because none could be touched, without new research. The newly flagged statistical mis-attribution in the headline contrast reinforces that the manuscript is not submission-ready anywhere yet. **Disposition:** decline with encouragement and a concrete path — (1) resolve the d_z/p attribution error and the 98.9%-vs-98.7% conflation; (2) run the adversarial-mapping condition and at least one contextual-bandit baseline, which are cheap relative to the credibility they buy; (3) either complete the §10.3 field validation and return to this journal as an empirical paper, or submit the benchmark to a computational-marketing/AI venue where it already meets the bar.
