A. INTRODUCTION
Employee benefits have long been recognized as a strategic element
in human resource management, functioning not only as a means of
compensating employees' investment in organizational performance but also
influencing customer experience through their presentation and
interpersonal interactions (Ari et al., 2020). Marketing context, employee
appearance, and communication skills shape customer perception and brand
loyalty (Putra, 2022). Among the wide range of benefits offered by
organizations, facilitative allowances, such as beauty, clothing, and
communication allowances, specifically support employees by enhancing
their readiness, appearance, and effectiveness in carrying out their
professional duties. These allowances go beyond basic salary structures,
addressing aspects of employee well-being and professional presentation that
are particularly important in roles requiring high visibility, direct client
interaction, and consistent brand representation (Laundon et al.,  2024;
Laundon et al., 2019).
In the marketing profession, the way employees present themselves
and communicate is closely tied to customer perceptions, trust building, and,
ultimately, sales outcomes. Theories in marketing and service management
consistently highlight that tangible cues, such as appearance, grooming, and
attire, combine with intangible factors, such as communication style, to
influence consumer decision-making and brand image (Kotler & Keller,

2022). Compensation extends beyond the base salary and includes various
allowances designed to improve morale and employee loyalty (Afandi 2018).
Well-designed employee benefits increase organizational commitment and
strengthen the psychological contract between employers and employees.
Organizational support in the form of targeted allowances for appearance
and communication can be considered a strategic marketing investment. This
enables employees to maintain a professional and appealing presence, which
in turn strengthens customer engagement and loyalty.
Modern human resource management emphasizes the strategic value
of benefits and nonfinancial incentives as tools not only for compensation
but also for enhancing employee performance and supporting organizational
goals (Bratton, 2021). Facilitative benefits, such as allowances for beauty,
clothing, and communication, are classified as performance-oriented support
systems that directly enhance professional appearance, productivity, and
communication, particularly for employees in public-facing positions
(Afandi, 2018; Bratton et al., 2021). From a human resource perspective, the
provision of facilitative allowances can influence several critical dimensions
of employee performance, including motivation, job satisfaction, and
productivity. Studies have shown that when employees perceive benefits as
relevant, adequate, and aligned with their work demands, they demonstrate
higher commitment, lower turnover intentions, and improved task
performance (Allui, 2016). Gitau et al., 2021; Gitau et al., 2024). Such benefits
also serve a symbolic role, signaling organizational appreciation and
recognition, which reinforces psychological contract fulfillment and
strengthens the employer–employee relationship. In competitive sectors,
such as retail and services, where marketing staff serve as the primary point
of contact with clients, these effects can be particularly pronounced.
Studies that examine the link between employee benefits and
marketing performance are limited. Most prior studies have examined
employee benefits in relation to internal outcomes, such as retention,
satisfaction, or organizational commitment, without extending the analysis to
external performance indicators such as customer engagement, brand image,
and sales target achievement. This represents a significant gap, especially in
sectors where employees' visual presentation and communicative abilities are
integral to their customer experiences and purchasing decisions.
Another gap concerns the integrated analysis of different types of
facilitative allowance. While isolated studies have explored the effects of
appearance-related benefits (e.g., uniform subsidies) and communication
tools (e.g., phone allowances), empirical research investigating their
combined and interactive effects on marketing performance is lacking. Such
integration is important because these allowances may not act independently;
instead, they can reinforce each other. For example, professional attire can
enhance the effectiveness of persuasive communication, whereas improved
communication tools can amplify the impact of well-presented personal
images.

Given these considerations, this study examines the simultaneous and
partial effects of beauty, clothing, and communication allowances on the
marketing performance of employees in the Indonesian service and retail
sectors. This study specifically focuses on how these allowances affect
employee motivation, productivity, customer engagement, and the
achievement of sales targets, as well as their role in shaping professional
image and client trust. By integrating insights from human resource
management theory, internal marketing theory, and service quality models,
this study provides a comprehensive perspective on how facilitative
allowances function as strategic tools that link employee well-being to market
performance.
This study addresses a gap in the literature because previous research
has rarely examined the combined influence of multiple facilitative
allowances on marketing outcomes. Most prior studies have treated
appearance-related and communication-related benefits separately, without
exploring their synergistic impact. This study enhances our understanding of
how cultural, economic, and industry-specific factors influence the value of
allowances within the framework of a developing economy. The findings are
expected to have theoretical implications by extending the scope of loyalty
benefits research and practical relevance, informing human resources and
marketing managers on structuring benefit policies to support competitive
advantage and market reach.

B. LITERATURE REVIEW
Over the past two decades, most studies have focused on general
compensation packages, health-related benefits, and monetary incentives as
determinants of employee performance (Kossivi et al., 2016; Azeem et al.,
2021). Few studies have focused on facilitative allowances specifically designed
to improve employees' appearance, presentations, and communication. These
allowances, which include beauty, clothing, and communication support, are
particularly relevant in customer-facing roles such as marketing, sales, and
brand representation. However, existing research tends to treat these
allowances as secondary or peripheral elements of total compensation rather
than as strategic tools with direct implications for market performance.
Previous empirical studies highlight that nonfinancial incentives, such
as allowances that help employees maintain a desired professional
appearance, can indirectly but significantly impact marketing performance by
shaping customer perceptions. For example, communication allowances can
facilitate continuous improvements in verbal and nonverbal interactions,
enabling marketing staff to respond effectively to customer needs, handle
objections, and close sales. Salary and allowances significantly influence
employee productivity in telecommunications services, and performance-
based allowances positively influence employee motivation in public
institutions (Lestari et al., 2023). Their findings suggest that appropriate and
fair benefits improve employee satisfaction and commitment. Clothing and

Facilitative Allowance
A beauty allowance helps employees maintain personal grooming and
aesthetic presentations, enhancing first impressions and perceived
professionalism. In marketing, how things look can greatly affect customer
choices, and these allowances can help make a brand more convincing and
trustworthy. Clothing allowances contribute to a standardized yet professional
appearance, aligning employees with the brand image and enhancing their
perceived credibility. The uniformity and quality of attire can also signal
organizational professionalism to customers, creating a favorable service
experience. Communication allowances, which cover tools such as
smartphones, Internet packages, and other communication devices, enable
employees to maintain continuous and effective contact with clients. This
allowance not only facilitates faster service response but also improves the
depth and frequency of customer interactions, directly impacting relationship
quality and sales conversion.

 Marketing Performances
This study defines marketing performance as the extent to which
marketing employees achieve targeted sales, maintain customer satisfaction,
and contribute to a brand's competitive position. Performance was measured
using self-reported metrics validated by managerial evaluation, capturing both
quantitative outcomes (e.g., sales target achievement) and qualitative indicators
(e.g., client feedback and relationship quality).

Hypothesis Development

hypotheses are proposed:

3. H3: Communication has a positive and significant effect on marketing

4. H4: Beauty, clothing, and communication allowances have positive and

C. RESEARCH METHOD
This study adopts a quantitative design to empirically examine the
impact of facilitative allowances —specifically, beauty, clothing, and
communication allowances—on employees' marketing performance. A
survey-based approach was employed to collect primary data from marketing
personnel in the service and retail sectors in Indonesia. The design allows for
the statistical measurement of both the simultaneous and partial effects of each
type of allowance on key marketing performance indicators. The population
consists of marketing employees of Indonesian companies operating in the
service and retail industries, where direct customer interaction and brand
representation are central to business performance. Purposive sampling was
applied, targeting individuals who occupied marketing-related positions (e.g.,
sales executives, account managers, and customer relationship officers). They
had to have received at least one form of facilitative allowance (beauty,
clothing, or communication) in the past 12 months.  A total of 150
questionnaires were distributed, and 117 valid responses were obtained,
resulting in an overall response rate of 78.38 %.
Data were collected using a structured questionnaire divided into four
sections: Demographic Information (gender, age, educational background,
work experience, and sector type).  Facilitative Allowances: The study
measured 12 Likert-scale items (1 = strongly disagree, 5 = strongly agree)
covering beauty, clothing, and communication allowances.  Marketing
Performance: measured using 10 Likert-scale items focusing on motivation,
productivity, customer engagement, sales target achievement, and professional
image. Open-ended questions facilitated the sharing of qualitative insights
from respondents regarding the perceived impact of the allowance.  The
questionnaire was adapted from validated instruments used in prior studies
(e.g., Rajab et al., 2024; Gitau et al., 2024), with minor modifications for
contextual relevance.
Independent Variables (IVs) Beauty Allowance (BA): Financial support
for grooming, cosmetics, or personal care relevant to professional appearance.
Clothing Allowance (CA): Financial support for work attire and accessories
aligned with the company's dress codes and brand image. Communication
Allowance (COA): Financial support for telecommunication expenses that
enable client contact and networking. Dependent Variable (DV): Marketing
Performance (MP): A composite measure of sales performance, client trust,
customer engagement, and personal productivity.

Multiple Linear Regression
Multiple linear regression was conducted to evaluate the impact of
beauty (BA), clothing (CA), and communication (COA) allowances on
marketing performance (MP). The regression model was statistically
significant (F(3, 113) = 75.12, p < 0.001), indicating that the model reliably
predicted marketing performance. The model explains 52% of the variance in
marketing performance (Adjusted R² = 0.512). Examination of individual
predictors showed the following:
1. Communication allowance  has the strongest positive effect on marketing
performance (β = 0.379, t = 5.57, p < 0.001), suggesting that support for
communication-related expenses significantly enhances marketing
outcomes.
2. Beauty allowance also positively influenced marketing performance (β =
0.314, t = 4.89, p < 0.001), indicating that funding personal grooming

E. DISCUSSION
This study reinforces the strategic role of facilitative, beauty, clothing,
and communication allowances, which are crucial components of employee
benefits that extend beyond traditional compensation. This study examines
how facilitative allowances for beauty, clothing, and communication affect
the marketing performance of employees in Indonesia's service and retail
sectors. The results indicate that all three types of allowances have a positive
and significant effect on marketing performance, with communic ation
allowances having the most significant effect. Consistent with human
resource management theory, these allowances serve not only as financial
support but also as targeted investments in employees' professional readiness,
appearance, and communication capabilities, which are essential in marketing

roles requiring high visibility and direct customer engagement (Rajab et al.,
2024; Laundon et al., 2019).
This communication allowance aligns with prior research highlighting
the importance of effective client interaction and networking in driving sales
performance and customer engagement (Gitau et al., 2024; Rajab et al., 2024).
Providing financial support for communication expenses enables employees
to maintain seamless contact with clients, thereby enhancing responsiveness
and service quality. The significant positive effects of all three types of
allowances on marketing performance align well with prior res earch
emphasizing the impact of employee presentation and communication on
customer perceptions, trust building, and, ultimately, sales outcomes (Kotler
& Keller, 2022). Communication allowance emerged as the strongest
predictor, highlighting the critical importance of enabling seamless client
interaction and networking in competitive retail and service sectors. This
supports the view that communication tools are not mere conveniences but
strategic resources that enhance customer responsiveness and relationship
quality.
Beauty and clothing allowances significantly contribute to marketing
performance, highlighting how professional grooming and attire enhance
employees' personal image and confidence, positively influencing customer
engagement and brand representation.  These findings echo internal
marketing and service quality theories that emphasize the symbolic and
practical value of employee appearance as a tangible cue that affects
consumer decision-making. From a human resource perspective, facilitative
allowances fulfill motivational and symbolic functions by signaling
organizational support and appreciation (Allui, 2016; Adnan et al., 2021).
This fosters stronger psychological contracts and commitment, reduces
turnover intention, and boosts productivity. The synergy observed among
the three allowances supports the argument that their combined effect
surpasses the isolated benefits, reflecting an integrated approach to employee
well-being and market-facing effectiveness.
This study fills gaps in the literature by investigating the joint influence
of multiple facilitative allowances on external performance metrics such as
sales target achievement and customer engagement within the context of a
developing economy. The Indonesian service and retail sectors exemplify
environments in which employee appearance and communication are critical
competitive factors. This offers invaluable perspectives on culturally and
economically specific applications of benefit strategies for employees in these
sectors. Limitations include reliance on self-reported performance measures
and purposive sampling, which may affect the generalizability of the results.
Future studies should consider using longitudinal approaches, assessings
objective performance metrics across various indices, andconfirmingm
andexpandingd these results.
