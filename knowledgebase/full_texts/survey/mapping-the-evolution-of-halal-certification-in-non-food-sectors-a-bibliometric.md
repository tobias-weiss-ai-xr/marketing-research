1. Introduction

The global economy is currently experiencing increasing uncertainty due to geopolitical dynamics (Khan et al., 2024),
climate change (Chancel et al., 2025; Timur et al., 2025 a), and the weakening of investment and trade flows (Gopinath et al.,
2025). In contrast to this global slowdown, the Muslim consumer segment is increasingly recognized as a new driver of global
economic growth. Dinar Stand ard, (2025) report that Muslim consumer spending across sectors of the Islamic economy and
halal industries (excluding Islamic finance) increased by 5.5% in 2023. This figure is projected to reach US$3.36 trillion by 2028,
representing a compound annual gr owth rate (CAGR) of 5.3% over five years. Furthermore, Halal Development Corporation
Berhad, (2020) report that the global halal market will expand to approximately US$5.0 trillion by 2030. This growth trajecto ry
is supported by the continuous expansion of  the global Muslim population (Population Reference Bureau, 2025) and evolving
consumer preferences toward products and services that offer higher quality standards, safety assurance, hygiene, and
stronger ethical and sustainability values (Ridlwan et al., 2025; Sari et al., 2025).
The halal food sector remains the largest segment of the Islamic economy, accounting for approximately US$1.43 trillion
of Muslim consumer spending in 2023, and is projected to reach US$1.94 trillion by 2028, with a CAGR of 6.2%. This expansion
is reinforced by regulatory policies enacted by various stakeholders across countries, requiring business actors to obtain ha lal
certification for food products (Herianti et al., 2025; Othman & Md Nawi, 2025). Such regulations further encourage consumers
to prioritize halal -certified food products (Timur et al., 2025 b). Halal certification functions as a primary standard for
determining whether a product complies with Islamic ethical and moral values (Aassouli & Syarif, 2025; Islam, 2022). I t also
enables consumers to verify the halal status of a product legitimized by authorized national bodies (Sayogo, 2018; Timur et al.,
2025c).
However, halal certification is not limited to food and beverage products. It increasingly extends to various no nfood

2. Materials and Methods

2.1. Research design

To address the research questions underpinning this study, a mixed -method approach was employed by proportionally
integrating qualitative and quantitative research methods. The identification of trends and the evolution of the scientific
literature were conducted using a quantitative method, specifically through bibliometric analysis. Bibliometric analysis is a
technique widely used to monitor the development of scholarly literature and facilitate the identification of emerging research
trends. It offers significant advantages in identifying key contributors who substantially advance a research domain, as well  as
in recognizing dominant thematic areas (Timur et al., 2025 e). In this study, several supporting software tools, including
Microsoft Excel, Publish or Perish (PoP), and VOS Viewer version 1.6.20, were used. In addition, a thematic review was
conducted on selec ted articles, with particular emphasis on local citation counts and the relevance of these citations to
commonly addressed research themes. This analytical approach aims to examine research developments and trends while
simultaneously providing visual representations of the data. The visualizations generated reflect the current state of knowledge
within a specific domain, thereby enabling researchers to identify potential strategic directions for future scholarly exploration
(van Eck & Waltman, 2010). A des criptive qualitative analysis was subsequently performed on the results of the bibliometric
and thematic review to generate deeper insights and new knowledge within the field.

2.2. Database selection and search string and keywords

This study focuses on p ublications indexed in the Scopus database. The selection of Scopus was based on its ability to
integrate a substantial number of peer -reviewed articles related to halal certification, offering broader and more rigorous
coverage than other databases, such as Web of Science, Google Scholar, EBSCO, and ProQuest (Akhter et al., 2023). The use of
a single database was justified by the extensive coverage of Scopus, its high -quality content ensured through a rigorous peer -
review process, and its comprehensive and flexible search capabilities (Napitupulu et al., 2024). Data collection was conducted
in February 2026 using a structured keyword strategy designed to retrieve articles related to halal certification in nonfood
industries. The search string applied was as follows: “halal certification” OR “halal label” OR “halal logo” OR “halal compliance”
OR “halal assurance” AND “tourism” OR “cosmetic” OR “skincare” OR “pharmaceutical” OR “fashion” OR “logistic” OR “media”
OR “recreation”. This search strategy was implem ented to ensure comprehensive coverage of the literature addressing halal
certification across various nonfood sectors.

2.3. Screening strategy

The initial search yielded 365 documents from the Scopus database. However, not all retrieved documents were relevant
to the objectives of this study. Therefore, a systematic screening strategy was applied following the PRISMA framework, as

3. Results

The primary objective of this study is to examine the development and volume of scholarly publications related to halal
certification in nonfood sectors. In particular, this research seeks to identify the most prolific authors and institutions
contributing to this field, as well as the leading journals that have frequently published studies on halal certification in nonfood
industries. Furthermore, this study aims to map the intellectual struct ure, thematic evolution, and dominant research streams
shaping the discourse on halal certification beyond the food sector. By employing bibliometric and thematic analyses, the
research provides a comprehensive overview of how the field has evolved over ti me, highlighting emerging trends, influential
contributors, and areas that remain underexplored. Ultimately, the findings of this study contribute to answering the proposed
research questions (RQ1–RQ4) by clarifying the trajectory of research development, identifying key actors within the scholarly
network, and outlining the conceptual directions that characterize halal certification studies in nonfood sectors.

3.1. Trend of publication over time

An examination of the annual distribution of publications and citation counts provides important insights into the
developmental trajectory and scholarly impact of research in this field. The longitudinal pattern highlights both phases of
consolidation and periods of rapid expansion, reflecting the evolving academic interest in the topic, as illustrated in Figure 2.

Figure 2 Trend publication and citation.

The annual distribution of publications shows a gradual but consistent upward trend. In the early period (2011 –2014),
the number of publications remained limited, ranging from one to three documents per year. Despite the low volume, citation
performance was relatively strong, particularly in 2012 and 2014, indicating that early studies played a foundational role in
shaping the field. N otable increases began in 2016 and 2017, with five and seven publications, respectively. Citation impact
peaked in 2021, reaching 365 citations, indicating the most influential year in terms of scholarly recognition. In 2022,
productivity significantly increased to 20 publications, accompanied by 318 citations, signaling growing academic interest and
thematic expansion. The highest number of publications occurred in 2025, with 43 documents. However, citation counts for
the most recent years (2024 –2026) appear relatively lower compared to earlier periods. This pattern should be interpreted
with caution, as these years represent ongoing or recently completed publication periods, and thus are not directly comparable
to earlier peak years. The observed pattern is likely influenced by the citation lag effect, where newly published articles require
time to accumulate citations.

4. Discussion

To address RQ1, the authors conducted a longitudinal publication pattern analysis derived from bibliometric techniques
using VOSviewer software. The findings reveal a clear intellectual evolution in halal certification research within nonfood
sectors. During the early phase between 2011 and 2014, the number of publications on this topic remained limited; however,
the citation density during this period was relatively high. This suggests that early studies played a foundational conceptual role
in shaping subsequent research trajectories and attracted increasing scholarly interest in the field. The strong citation
performance despite the low publication volume indicates that these early works functioned as seminal references, providing
theoretical legitimacy for research on halal certification in nonfood sectors.  From a theoretical perspective, this phase reflects
the dominance of institutional and compliance -oriented approaches, where halal certification was primarily framed as a
regulatory mechanism driven by formal rules and legitimacy pressures.
A shift in research trends became evident starting in 2016 and accelerated significantly after 2020. The peak in citation
growth was observed in 2021, when halal certification research expanded from a p redominantly regulatory discourse toward
practical applications in sectors such as tourism, logistics, and cosmetics. For instance, Ratnasari et al. (2021) and Handriana et
al., (2021) examined the role of halal certification as a determinant influencing c onsumer decisions to visit halal tourism
destinations and purchase halal cosmetic products. The substantial increase in publication output from 2022 onward, reaching
its peak in 2025, reflects thematic diversification and stronger interdisciplinary engagem ent. The decline in citation counts in
the most recent years is more appropriately attributed to citation lag effects rather than a decrease in scientific impact (Aksnes
et al., 2019). Importantly, this transition also reflects a broader shift in the liter ature from a compliance -oriented perspective
toward a market -driven approach, indicating that halal certification is increasingly embedded within competitive and value -
creation strategies rather than solely regulatory compliance.
To address RQ2, this study further analyzed the most highly cited manuscripts. The findings confirm that the intellectual
core of halal certification research in nonfood sectors is strongly grounded in marketing and consumer behavior paradigms.
The dominance of the Journal of Islamic Marketing as a primary publication outlet indicates that halal certification is increasingly
positioned as a market signal rather than merely a religious or regulatory mechanism. Highly cited articles such as Boğan &
Sarıışık, (2018), Zailani et al., (2 017), Ratnasari et al., (2021), and Widyanto & Sitohang, (2022) focus on halal tourism, halal
logistics performance, and halal cosmetic purchase intention, employing theoretical frameworks centered on trust, satisfaction,
emotional experience, and behavior al intention. This pattern suggests a significant shift in halal certification research toward
behavioral approaches , moving from institutional compliance orientation to consumer perception and market dynamics
analysis. However, the dominance of consumer behavior perspectives may also indicate a potential imbalance in the literature,
where organizational, institutional, and policy-level analyses remain relatively underexplored. This overemphasis on individual-
level behavior could limit a comprehensive under standing of how firms, regulators, and industry ecosystems shape the
implementation of halal certification in nonfood sectors.
At the author level, knowledge production in halal certification research within nonfood sectors demonstrates a
collaborative str ucture. Leading contributors are not only highly productive but also exhibit strong network centrality, as
reflected in their total link strength values (see Table 2). The presence of multiple institutional affiliations among promin ent
scholars indicates t hat cross -border academic mobility plays a critical role in enhancing citation performance and research
visibility. Interinstitutional collaboration likely expands data access, increases opportunities for publication in reputable
journals, and accelerates knowledge diffusion (Yao, 2021). This pattern illustrates that halal certification research is embedded
within an international academic ecosystem, where Southeast Asian scholars are interconnected with researchers from other
Asian countries as well as fro m Europe, the United States, Africa, and Australia. Such collaborative dynamics also suggest that
knowledge production in this field is not only regionally concentrated but also globally interconnected, reinforcing the role  of
international academic networks in shaping research visibility and impact.
At the institutional level, important distinctions emerge between citation impact and collaborative embeddedness.
Certain universities, such as Universitas Sarjanawiyata Tamansiswa (Indonesia) and Jamia Millia Islamia (India), demonstrate
high citation impact despite moderate publication output. This multidimensional structure indicates that academic influence is
determined not only by output volume but also by collaboration strategies and journal selecti on (Velez-Estevez et al, 2022;
Wang et al, 2024). The dominance of institutions from Indonesia and Malaysia confirms that Southeast Asia constitutes the
epistemic center of halal certification research. This regional leadership is supported by well -established regulatory
frameworks, institutionalized halal ecosystems, and sustained government policy support, as reflected in the strong
performance of both countries in the State of the Global Islamic Economy Report 2024/2025 (Dinar Standard, 2025). This finding
also highlights the importance of institutional support and policy alignment in fostering research productivity and academic
leadership in emerging fields such as halal certification.

In addressing RQ3, this study identifies a thematic structure based on keyword co -occurrence analysis, revealing three
interconnected research streams. The first stream focuses on governance, regulatory frameworks, and industry e cosystems,
emphasizing certification mechanisms and implementation challenges in the tourism and logistics sectors. The second stream
focuses on consumer behavior, particularly in the halal cosmetics sector, with variables such as trust, religiosity, knowl edge,
and brand image as the dominant themes. The third stream highlights technical and compliance dimensions, including material
verification, safety assurance, and legal standards. The coexistence of these three clusters indicates that halal certificati on
operates simultaneously as a regulatory instrument, a market signal, and a quality assurance system. Notably, the analysis also
reveals that three industries exhibit the highest research intensity within the halal ecosystem: halal cosmetics, halal touri sm,
and halal logistics. These findings suggest the presence of research gaps in other segments of the halal industry, including halal
pharmaceuticals, halal fashion, and halal media and entertainment, where the linkage with halal certification remains
underexplored. This imbalance indicates that future research should move beyond dominant sectors and explore
underrepresented industries to develop a more comprehensive understanding of halal certification across the broader halal
ecosystem.
Furthermore, the o verlay visualization illustrates a clear temporal transformation in the research focus. Early studies
predominantly emphasized technical compliance and regulatory legitimacy. As the field evolved, attention shifted toward
industry-level implementation and ecosystem discussions. More recent publications increasingly emphasize the behavioral and
psychological determinants of consumers, particularly within the cosmetics and tourism sectors. This progression indicates that
halal certification research has enter ed a stage of market integration (Timur et al., 2025 f). Certification is no longer perceived
solely as a religious compliance mechanism but is increasingly positioned as a strategic, trust -building instrument that enables
firms to achieve competitive advan tage in the marketplace. This trend also demonstrates that recent research has largely
focused on behavioral investigations, particularly consumer behavior studies, thereby providing important directions for future
research agendas. Nevertheless, future  studies are encouraged to integrate multi -level perspectives by incorporating
organizational, institutional, and policy dimensions to complement the existing dominance of consumer -oriented approaches.

4.1. Managerial implication

The findings of this study offer several managerial implications for key stakeholders involved in the halal industry. First,
for governments as regulatory authorities, the results highlight the importance of strengthening regulatory frameworks and
standardization of halal cert ification beyond the food sector. The findings indicate significant growth in halal tourism and
logistics sectors, while attention to halal pharmaceuticals remains relatively limited. Therefore, governments should priorit ize
the development of sector -specific halal certification guidelines, particularly in high -risk industries such as pharmaceuticals,
where compliance involves complex supply chains and rigorous ingredient verification. In addition, governments are
encouraged to provide incentives for firms that adopt halal certification. The digital transformation of certification processes,
along with the enhancement of cross -border recognition systems, can further support the expansion of global halal trade.
Second, for chambers of commerce and industry associations, this study underscores their strategic role in bridging knowledge
gaps between regulators and business actors. The dominance of consumer behavior research suggests that market demand for
halal-certified products and services is already well established. However, many firms, particularly SMEs, still face challenges in
understanding certification procedures and benefits. Accordingly, chambers of commerce can facilitate training programs,
awareness campaigns, and sector -specific workshops to enhanc e firms’ readiness to adopt halal certification, particularly in
tourism services (e.g., hotels and travel agencies) and pharmaceutical distribution networks. Third, for third -party logistics
providers (3PLs), the findings highlight the increasing importan ce of halal certification as a trust -building mechanism within
supply chains. As halal certification evolves into an integrated system encompassing production, storage, and distribution,
logistics providers play a critical role in ensuring end -to-end halal integrity. In the tourism sector, this includes halal -compliant
logistics for food supply and hospitality services, while in the pharmaceutical sector, it involves ensuring proper segregati on,
traceability, and contamination prevention throughout the distribution process. Therefore, 3PL firms are encouraged to invest
in halal -compliant infrastructure, adopt traceability technologies (such as blockchain and IoT), and collaborate closely with
certification bodies to enhance transparency and credibility.

The findings of this study reveal a dynamic and positively evolving trend in the literature on “halal certification in the
nonfood sector” within the Scopus database during the period 2011 –2025. The most significant contributions to t his theme
originate from authors affiliated with institutions in Indonesia and Malaysia. This dominance is also reflected at the

impact, and collaborative intensity within the research network. On the basis of the analysis of 161 articles published in Scopus-
indexed journals under the theme “halal certification in the nonfood sector,” three major clusters were identified: (1) hal al
certification governance and industry ecosystem; (2) consumer behavior and halal cosmetic market dynamics; and (3) product
safety, ingredients, and regulatory compliance. The findings further suggest several directions for future research. Studies
addressing halal certification in the nonfood sector remain relatively limited in industries such as halal pharmaceuticals, halal
fashion, and halal media and entertainment. These underexplored sectors offer significant opportunities for expanding the
scope of scholarly inquiry. Moreover, recent publications increasingly emphasize consumer behavior perspectives. Accordingly,
future research may deepen behavioral approaches by integrating more diverse theoretical frameworks, such as signaling
theory, legitimacy t heory, and the stimulus –organism–response (SOR) framework. Behavioral perspectives may also be
extended to organizational and policy levels to better understand how industry actors respond to market dynamics and
regulatory pressures.
Despite its significant contributions, this study has several limitations. The data selection process was conducted through
three search queries applied to titles, abstracts, and keywords, which may have excluded relevant articles that did not explicitly
include the specified keywords. However, the likelihood of such omissions is minimal and is not expected to substantially affect
the overall findings. In addition, the study did not conduct a detailed manual screening of all titles and abstracts to ensure that
each document focused exclusively on “halal certification in the nonfood sector,” opening the possibility that some articles may
have addressed broader topics. Nevertheless, the analysis of the most productive journals and the most influential articles
demonstrates strong thematic consistency regarding halal certification in nonfood industries. Furthermore, the dataset was
limited to publications indexed in the Scopus database and was predominantly composed of works authored by Indonesian
scholars, who have made su bstantial contributions to the global literature. To enhance coverage and comprehensiveness,
future research should incorporate additional databases such as Web of Science, Dimensions, and Google Scholar.
