Introduction
In recent years, gastronomy has received increasing attention
in the tourism literature, particularly in relation to visitor
experience, destination perceptions, and tourist behavior
(Wondirad & Verheye, 2023). Beyond its economic role within
the tourism experience, foodservice can also shape visitors’
sensory and emotional evaluations, prompting growing
scholarly interest in its associations with outcomes such as
satisfaction, loyalty, and revisit intentions. Robinson and
Clifford (2012) noted that gastronomic experiences engage
multiple senses, including sight, taste, smell, touch, and
hearing, and that perceived authenticity is closely associated
with the visitor experience.
Alongside this growing interest, authenticity has been
examined in gastronomy research across a range of contexts
and variables. In particular, understanding how tourists
evaluate the credibility and authenticity of a food product,
restaurant, or gastronomic offering has become increasingly
important for explaining consumption behavior. Kim and Song
(2020) demonstrated that the perceived credibility of
authenticity claims made by traditional restaurants can
influence purchase intentions through per ceptions of the
restaurant’s competence and authenticity. Similarly, DiPietro
and Levitt (2019) found that food and beverage attributes,
together with a restaurant’s connection to the local
community, play an important role in shaping perceptions of
authenticity, which in turn are associated with satisfaction and
revisit intentions.
Research on authenticity has not been confined to restaurant
experiences; it has also addressed tourists’ encounters with
different cultures, their exposure to unfamiliar enviro nments,
and their interactions with the places they visit. Tiberghien et
al. (2020) showed that encounters with unfamiliar and
culturally distinct environments can enhance tourists’
emotional engagement, understanding, and interaction,
thereby contributing to a stronger perception of authenticity.
At the same time, interest in local food does not necessarily
translate into actual consumption behavior, as factors such as
trust in whether a product is genuinely local, distribution,
price, and quality may infl uence tourists’ behavioral choices
(Birch & Memery, 2020). Taken together, these studies
indicate that authenticity in gastronomy tourism has been
investigated through diverse research questions and across a
variety of tourism contexts.
The development of this literature across different variables,
experiences, and research settings highlights the need for a
comprehensive assessment of the evolution and scholarly
structure of authenticity research in gastronomy tourism.
Bibliometric analysis provides a systematic and quantitative
approach to examining the development of a research field,
enabling the identification of publication trends, influential
scholarly actors, conceptual relationships, and citation
structures. In tourism and hospitality research, bibl iometric
methods have increasingly been used to evaluate the
development of research fields and to map their intellectual
and conceptual structures (Ülker et al., 2023; Karasakaloğlu &
Çelik, 2025; Bahtiyar, 2026). Bibliometric approaches have also
been ap plied specifically within gastronomy -related research
to examine the characteristics and development of gastronomy
tourism literature and related research domains (Ercan, 2020;
Oğan, 2024). However, despite this growing body of research,
studies on authenticity in gastronomy tourism remain
dispersed across different themes and research settings, and
the field’s overall developmental trajectory, conceptual
structure, and scholarly r elationships have not been
sufficiently synthesized from a bibliometric perspective. To
address this gap, this study aims to examine the academic
literature on authenticity in gastronomy tourism from a
bibliometric perspective and to identify the field’s
developmental trajectory, conceptual structure, and key
characteristics. In this respect, the study is expected to
contribute to a clearer understanding of the current structure
of the literature and to help inform future research directions.
1. Conceptual Framework
Gastronomy has become an important component of tourism
that extends beyond satisfying tourists’ basic food and
beverage needs, enabling them to engage with destination
culture and experience local ways of life. Gastronomy tourism
encompasses a w ide range of food - and beverage -related
experiences, including restaurant visits, festivals, producer and
farm visits, local markets, tasting events, cooking
demonstrations, and culinary classes (Kokkranikal & Carabelli,
2024). In this context, food is reg arded not merely as a means
of satisfying a physiological need but as an integral part of the
tourism experience. Kivela and Crotts (2005) emphasized the
critical role of gastronomy in shaping destination experiences
and noted that authentic gastronomic ex periences may
influence tourists’ intentions to revisit a destination.
The relationship between local cuisine and cultural identity
occupies an important place in the development of gastronomy
tourism. Food and beverages contribute to destination
differentiation through their connections with a destination’s
history, culture, economy, and social life. Local gastronomy is
therefore regarded as a tourism resource that reflects cultural
heritage and can provide destinations with a competitive
advantage (López -Guzmán & Sánchez -Cañizares, 2012).
Walter (2017) similarly emphasized that cuisine is an
important expression of cultural heritage and that culinary
tourism is closely associated with heritage tourism.
Tourists are increasingly becoming active participants  in
gastronomy tourism experiences. Cooking classes, in
particular, provide opportunities for tourists to become
familiar with local ingredients, learn traditional preparation
methods, and interact with local communities. Such
experiences are characterized  by dimensions of participation,
enjoyment, culture, and knowledge acquisition (Kokkranikal &
Carabelli, 2024). Walter (2017) likewise showed that tourists
can establish a sensory connection with local culture by
actively participating in food preparation and consumption
processes.
The value of gastronomy to destinations extends beyond its
economic and cultural effects. Sims (2009) noted that local food
can connect tourists with producers and places of production,
support local businesses, and contribute to  sustainable
tourism. Gastronomy, therefore, creates a strong link among
destination identity, cultural heritage, and tourist experience.
Within this relationship, tourists’ perceptions of the extent to
which food, places, and cultural elements are genuine , local,
and original in other words, their perceptions of authenticity
become particularly important.
Authenticity is a multidimensional concept that has long been
debated in the tourism literature and for which no universally
accepted definition has been  established. Özdemir and
Seyitoğlu (2017) noted that definitions of authenticity tend to
revolve around notions such as reality, history, tradition,
locality, culture, and otherness. Chen et al. (2020) similarly
associated authenticity with being genuine,  original, sincere,
and trustworthy, while emphasizing the absence of a single
generally accepted definition. Accordingly, authenticity is not
regarded solely as an immutable property of an object but also
as a subjective evaluation shaped by individuals’ expectations,
knowledge, and experiences.
Various theoretical approaches have been developed to explain
authenticity. Zhang et al. (2019) discussed these approaches
within the frameworks of objective, constructivist,
postmodern, and existential authenticity. Objective
authenticity focuses on the genuineness of the object being
experienced, whereas the constructivist perspective

emphasizes the socially constructed nature of authenticity as
shaped by individuals’ expectations, beliefs, and
interpretations. Th e postmodern perspective questions the
distinction between what is authentic and what is constructed,
while existential authenticity focuses on individuals’ subjective
feelings and sense of self during the tourism experience.
Together, these perspectives i ndicate that authenticity is
related to both the characteristics of the object and tourists’
perceptions and experiences.
These theoretical distinctions are also relevant in the context
of gastronomy tourism. Li et al. (2021) suggested that
authenticity in gastronomic experiences may emerge in both
objective and existential forms. Local dishes, ingredients,
preparation methods, recipes, and eating traditions can reflect
the local identity of a destination (Zhang et al., 2019). The local
production of food m ay contribute to objective authenticity,
whereas congruence between tourists’ expectations and the
food, atmosphere, and cultural heritage of a destination may
enhance perceived authenticity. Connections established with
producers, places of production, an d local communities may
further contribute to experiences of existential authenticity.
Gastronomic authenticity is not limited to the characteristics
of food itself. Chen et al. (2020) demonstrated that perceived
authenticity in traditional restaurants may  encompass
dimensions such as food authenticity, environmental
authenticity, historical and cultural value, brand value, and
nostalgia. Accordingly, authenticity in gastronomy tourism
can be understood as a multifaceted concept encompassing the
relationships among food, place, cultural heritage, and tourist
perceptions.
Bibliometric analysis provides a systematic approach to
examining the development and structure of a research field by
analyzing patterns in scientific publications and bibliographic
relationships. It enables researchers to assess publication
trends, prominent authors, journals, institutions, citation
relationships, and emerging research directions (Akba ş &
Kara, 2024; G ökçe & Ünlüönen, 2023). Bibliometric
approaches are also useful for processing large volumes of
scholarly data, identifying gaps in the existing literature, and
generating insights for future research (Aykan & Ülker, 2026).
Within tourism and gastronomy-related research, bibliometric
methods have been applied to examine th e characteristics and
development of food and beverage research, including
postgraduate studies in the field (Tayfun et al., 2018). They
have also been used to examine publication and citation trends,
Web of Science categories and indexes, and keyword
relationships, thereby providing a structured overview of the
development of a research domain (Gökçe et al., 2024).
Accordingly, author keyword co- occurrence and citation
analyses can be used to examine the conceptual and scholarly
structure of a research field, including the relationships among
its prominent themes, authors, institutions, journals, and
publications.
2. Method
The dataset for this study was retrieved from the Web of
Science Core Collection (WoSCC). The search was conducted
on June 1, 2026, usi ng keywords representing the concepts of
gastronomy tourism and authenticity, together with their
alternative forms commonly used in the literature. The initial
search yielded a total of 334 records. In accordance with the
scope of the study, only publicat ions classified as Articles or
Review Articles were considered for inclusion. Because the
search was conducted before the end of 2026, publications from
2026 were excluded to ensure comparability in the analysis of
annual publication trends. Book chapters and publications with
Early Access status were also excluded. Following this initial
filtering process, a dataset comprising 240 publications was
obtained.
In the second stage, the titles, abstracts, and author keywords
of the publications were examined to  determine their direct
relevance to the research topic. Seventy -nine publications in
which the concept of authenticity did not appear in the title,
abstract, or author keywords and was identified only in the
Keywords Plus field generated by Web of Science were
excluded. In addition, seven publications were removed
because, although authenticity was mentioned in their title,
abstract, or author keywords, they were not directly related to
the context of gastronomy tourism. Thus, a total of 86
publications were excluded during the relevance assessment,
resulting in a final dataset of 154 publications.
Prior to the bibliometric analyses, the dataset underwent data
cleaning and standardization. Variations in the spelling and
presentation of author names referring to the same individual
were standardized to minimize duplicate entries and potential
inconsistencies arising from differences in author- name
formats. VOSviewer version 1.6.20 was used to construct and
visualize the bibliometric networks. The analyses included the
annual distribution of publications, co -occurrence analysis of
author keywords, and citation analyses at the institution,
author, journal, and document levels. All bibliometric analyses
were conducted using the final dataset of 154 public ations
obtained after data cleaning and relevance assessment.
The study was guided by the following research questions:
1. What trend does the annual distribution of academic
publications on authenticity in gastronomy tourism
exhibit?
2. What are the main themes  and keyword clusters that
constitute the conceptual structure of the literature on
authenticity in gastronomy tourism?
3. What structure do citation networks at the institution,
author, and journal levels exhibit in research on
authenticity in gastronomy tourism?
4. Which key publications are most prominent within the
citation network of authenticity research in gastronomy
tourism?

4. Conclusions, Discussion, and Recommendations
This study examined the bibliometric characteristics of
academic publications on authenticity in gastronomy tourism
and identified the field’s developmental trajectory, conceptual
structure, institutional productivity, and citation relationships
at the author, journal, and document levels. Following the
screening and data -cleaning procedures, analyses were
conducted on a final dataset of 154 publications. The findings
indicate that the relationship between gastronomy tourism and
authenticity has attracted increasing scholarly attention,
particularly in recent years. Analysis of annual publication
output revealed a marked increase after 2019, with the highest
publication counts in 2024 and 2025, at 23 and 24, respectively.
These results suggest that authenticity remains a current and
increasingly visible research topic within the gastronomy
tourism literature.
The co -occurrence analysis of author keywords further
demonstrated that the field is not organized around a single
conceptual axis. The analysis identified 32 keywords grouped
into five clusters, with themes such as gastronomic and
cultural heritage, destination marketing, local identity,
destination branding, sustainability, rural development, local
food, perceived authenticity, and tourist satisfaction emerging
as prominent components of the field’s conceptual structure.
These findings indicate that authenticity in gastronomy
tourism is not confined to the genuineness of traditional foods,
but is also examined in relation to cultural heri tage
preservation, destination identity and branding, sustainability,
tourist experience, and satisfaction. Accordingly, authenticity
appears to constitute a multidimensional research domain that
encompasses both the cultural and gastronomic attributes of
destinations and tourists’ perceptions and experiences.
In terms of institutional productivity, the University of Girona
emerged as the leading institution with 15 publications,
followed by Edith Cowan University and the University of
Novi Sad with five pu blications each. The identification of 13
institutions meeting the minimum threshold of three
publications suggests that research output in this field is
distributed across a range of universities rather than
concentrated within a single academic center. L ikewise, the
grouping of these institutions into three clusters within the
institutional citation network indicates that citation
relationships are decentralized. This finding is important in
demonstrating that research on authenticity in gastronomy
tourism has developed across multiple academic centers.
At the author level, the citation network comprised 26
connected authors grouped into seven clusters. Sangkyun Kim
and Eerang Park were particularly prominent in terms of their
citation linkages, while Bend egul Okumus and Girish Prayag
also occupied notable positions within the network. Although
Francesc Fusté -Forné stood out in terms of publication
productivity, his citation connectivity was relatively more
limited than that of some other authors. This find ing
demonstrates that publication productivity and position within
a citation network do not necessarily develop in parallel. In
other words, a high publication output does not automatically
correspond to the strongest citation connectivity within the
field.
The journal-level analysis also supports the interdisciplinary
character of the research area. Among journals meeting the
minimum threshold of two publications, 28 journals with
citation links were grouped into seven clusters. The British
Food Journal occupied a particularly prominent position, while
Tourism Management, Tourism Management Perspectives,
International Journal of Hospitality Management, and
International Journal of Contemporary Hospitality
Management were also notable in terms of their cita tion
relationships. This structure indicates that research on
authenticity in gastronomy tourism has developed through
interactions among journals spanning tourism, hospitality, and
food and beverage studies.

particular, interdisciplinary collaboration could facilitate the
simultaneous examination of the cultural, behavioral,
economic, and sustainability dimensions of gastronomic
authenticity.
This study is subject to several limitations. T he analysis was
limited to publications indexed in the Web of Science Core
Collection that met the predefined inclusion criteria. In
addition, publications from 2026 were excluded because the
year was not yet complete at the time of data collection. Future
bibliometric studies may therefore incorporate publications
retrieved from other databases, such as Scopus, and compare
the resulting patterns. Moreover, the use of additional
bibliometric techniques, including co -authorship analysis,
bibliographic coupling, and co-citation analysis, may provide a
more detailed understanding of the intellectual and social
structure of the literature on authenticity in gastronomy
tourism.
In conclusion, authenticity in gastronomy tourism can be
regarded as a multidimensional research topic whose scholarly
visibility has increased in recent years and which is associated
with a wide range of themes, from cultural heritage and
sustainability to destination branding and tourist satisfaction.
The multi -cluster structure of the ex isting literature,
developed around different institutions, researchers, journals,
and influential studies, reflects the diversity of the field.
Further interdisciplinary and comparative research may
contribute both to the theoretical development of authen ticity
in gastronomy tourism and to the generation of more
comprehensive insights for destination management and
marketing practices.
