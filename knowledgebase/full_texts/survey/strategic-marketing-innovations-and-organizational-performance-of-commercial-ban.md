Abstract
With the new dynamic and competitive banking environment, commercial banks now have to think in term of marketing
innovations to develop its performance in order to remain profitable. This study examined the relationship between
strategic marketing innovations and commercial banks' performance in South-South, Nigeria with the moderating effect
of environment dynamism. This study was conducted using the theories of resource -based view theory, dynamic
capabilities theory and the contingency theory. The resear ch design adopted was the cross -sectional survey research
and positivist approach. The population of respondents were the employees of selected commercial banks in the
country who were sampled using Taro Yamane formula to obtain the size of 370 respondents , the sampling technique
was proportionate stratified and simple random sampling technique. The data for the primary data were taken by using
structured questionnaires. Descriptive data, Spearman Rank Correlation Coefficient and Partial Correlation Coeffic ient
were used to analyse the data at 0.05 level of significance. There was a positive and significant relationship between
operational efficiency and price innovation (r = 0.216, p< 0.01), product innovation (r = 0.484, p< 0.01), promotion
innovation (r =  0.396, p< 0.01) and distribution innovation (r = 0.451, p< 0.01). The study also found that strategic
marketing innovations were positively and significantly correlated to the organization performance and the positive
relationship between strategic market ing innovations and the organization performance was mediated by the
environmental dynamism. Results showed that the strategic marketing innovations play an important role to improve
the operation's efficiency concerning with environment. The study recomme nded that commercial banks need to
reinforce the marketing strategy based on innovation and closely watch environmental changes. The study yielded
empirical findings on the moderating effect of environmental dynamism in the strategic marketing innovation –
organizational performance relationship in the banking sector of the Nigerian economy which added to knowledge.

1. Introduction
The banking industry is faced with intense level of competition, technology development and high expectations from
customers. The level of performance of commercial banks within the industry is geared on it implementations of
strategic marketing innovation s. Hence, right from the inception, banks have been in search of innovative ideas to
efficiently deliver topnotch services to their customers, as a way of attracting and retaining them. This will aid in the
increase of their operational efficiencies. Strat egic marketing innovation is about developing a new product, price,
promotion and distribution to provide the organisations with a superior value and competitive advantage (Ogunnaike
et al., 2018; Chaffey & Ellis-Chadwick, 2019). In this era of financial institutions vying for customers in a more dynamic
landscape, strategic marketing innovation can be used as a tool to boost efficiency and organization's performance
(Teece, 2018; Ekwueme et al., 2023).

Product innovation includes new financial products and improving banking products to suit the customers' needs.
Therefore, Nigeria's commercial banks are beginning to understand that they can leverage on digital technologies to
make banking better and enhance customer satisfaction. Likewise, in the area of pricing models, pricing innovation will
enable banks to be more flexible in their pricing models and service fees which in turn will lead to increased customer
retention and profitability for banks. Promotion innovation is connected with communication (digital marketing, social
media) innovation which can improve the consumer recognition and involvement. Distribution innovation is the
evolution of the use of multiple ways of providing service including age ncy banking, ATM, and internet banking that
makes banking more accessible and convenient (Olanike & Oladipupo, 2024; Agu & Obi, 2024). When used together,
these innovations can enhance the operations of the company, making them more efficient, responsive, and cost -
effective, leading to better customer experiences (Agboola & Babarinde, 2024; Zeithaml et al., 2018).
However, strategic marketing innovativeness may be affected by environmental dynamism. Environmental dynamism
is the extent to which the environment of an organization (technology, regulations, economics, competition, etc.) is rapid
and/or uncertain. In ve ry dynamic environment, strategies need to be explored, updated with performance and
competitiveness (Seo, 2020, Li et al., 2020). However, in South -South Nigeria, commercial banks are operating in a
digital platform, consumers are evolving in their tastes  and regulation is evolving. Thus, the role of environmental
dynamism in the link between strategic marketing innovations and the firms' performance can be positive or negative
(Kumar, 2021; Wang, 2022).
Previous studies have examined the relationship between strategic marketing innovation and organizational
performance. For example, Ogunnaike et al. (2018) examined the effect of marketing innovation and organizational
competitiveness. Ekwueme et al. (2023 ) investigated the impact of on innovative marketing practices on firm
performance in Nigeria. Agu and Obi (2024) and Olanike and Oladipupo (2024) examined the extent to which
innovations influence organizational performance in the service sector. However,  most of these studies were on
manufacturing companies, small to medium size enterprises and general enterprises/service organisations; thus,
commercial banks in South -South Nigeria were not included. Moreover, profitability and market shares have been the
usual yardsticks in the assessment of the performance of organizations while operational efficiency has not been getting
much attention in empirical studies (Ekwueme et al., 2023; Agu & Obi, 2024). Furthermore, the interaction of four
dimensions of innovation—product innovation, price innovation, promotion innovation, and distribution innovation—
in the previous studies is rarely studied (Ekwueme et al., 2023; Agu & Obi, 2024). Further, environmental dynamism is
only perceived as context variable and not as  a moderator variable which can impact upon the effectiveness of
marketing innovations. According to Seo (2020) and Wang (2022), evaluating the environmental dynamism is a
significant aspect that affect the performance of firms since the activities of thes e are affected by the environmental
factors. Thus, it becomes pertinent to evaluate how changes in the environment can mediate the relationship between
strategic marketing and organizational performance in South-South, Nigeria.
2. Literature Review
2.1. Conceptual Framework
2.1.1. Strategic Marketing Innovations
Strategic marketing innovation is an idea, process, technology or practice that is new to the organization and provides
a customer benefit, a competitive advantage and can help the organization reach its sustainable marketing goals. It
involves incorporati on of new concepts in the new product design, pricing, promotion and distribution in view of
changing market situation and customer needs (Chaffey & Ellis -Chadwick, 2019; Varadarajan, 2020). Marketing is not
just the little better, it's the new product and  service, and the new experience that make an organization different than
its rivals – it is innovation. In the business arena of technological disruptions and highly competitive pressures, one of
the most important organization's strategic skills to survive and grow is strategic marketing innovation (Chaffey & Ellis-
Chadwick, 2019; Varadarajan, 2020). There are a number of aspects to the idea. Product innovation is to make new
product or change the product to meet the customers’ needs. Price innovation is a new approach of price which will
enhance the value creation and market penetration. Promotion innovation is a fresh media and digital marketing
instruments and integrated marketing communication to the customers. Distribution innovation is associated wit h
improving service delivery channels through technological means and solutions like digital platforms, online systems
and others that make the process more accessible and convenient as posited by Ogunnaike et al., (2018) and Olanike &
Oladipupo (2024). Ex amples of strategic marketing innovations in a bank include Internet banking, mobile banking
applications, customized financial products, agency banking, and digital banking customer engagement platform.

2.1.2. Organizational Performance
Organizational Performance is the organization's effectiveness and efficiency in implementing their strategy.
Organizational performance is the result of the whole work of the organisation and is generally evaluated based on a
single or multiple financial and non -financial criteria like profitability, productivity, market shares, customer
satisfaction, innovation, operational efficiency etc. (Teece, 2018; Richard et al., 2009). The literature of contemporary
performance of organizations proposes that, along with financial measures it is important to consider multidimensional
measuring techniques so as to grasp the overall effectiveness. Thus, effectiveness of the operational efficiency, service
level, customer retention, ability to innovate and adaptability in the organism are considered important indicators of
effectiveness. The use of the resources by an organization to produce its products or services at a lesser cost and at a
greater productivity is one of the most important measures of efficiency of an organization. Within the banking industry,
the efficiency is seen as the speed of the process, the provision of service, the response of customers, automation and
cost reduction (Chaffey & Ellis-Chadwick, 2019; Zeithaml et al., 2018).
2.1.3. Environmental Dynamism
Environmental dynamism is the extent of instability, uncertainty, complexity and unpredictability of the environment
of an organization. It reflects the speed and frequency of change in technological or economic, political, regulatory and
competitive factors and how this influences the way in which an organization operates and makes decisions. In a highly
dynamic environment, there are many technological advancements and shifts in customer preferences, intense
competition, and regular changes of regulations , making it challenging for organizations to anticipate what might lie
ahead (Seo, 2020; Wang, 2022). In today's context of business, the dynamic environment factor has become more
relevant especially in a globalized, digital and volatile environment. In dynamic situation, organisations are required to
constantly change their strategy, structure and operations to ensure their competitiveness. Inability to make effective
response to the environment could result in a decline in performance and competitive adv antage. So, environmental
dynamism can be thought of as one of the context variables that could impact the effectiveness of organizational
capacities and strategies (Li et al., 2020 and Kumar, 2021).

2.2. Theoretical Framework
This study adopted the theories of resource-based view, contingency theory and dynamic capabilities theory.
Barney (1991) followed the study of Wernerfelt, who studied firm resources and came up with the resource-based view
(RBV). According to RBV, firms will perform better, if the firm has valuable, rare, hard to imitate and non -substitutable
resources and capabilities. Product innovation, price innovation, promotion innovation and distribution innovation are
the strategic marketing innovations identified in this study and can be utilized by the commercial banks for enhancing
banks operational efficiency. Banks with customer-oriented products and flexible pricing and distribution platforms,
and digital promotional platforms can deliver a quicker speed of service, greater customer responsiveness and cost
savings to their competitors. RBV is suitable as it suggests  that the internal marketing competences can become a
competitive advantage and improve the performance of the organization in the short and long -term (Barney, 1991;
Teece, 2018).
The concept of dynamic capabilities has been developed by Teece, Pisano and Shuen (1997). The theory is how the firms
merge, build and reconfigure internal and external capabilities to adjust to the rapidly changing environment. The
Theory is relevant because of the various turbulent business environment of commercial banks in South -South Nigeria
that include Competition of Fintech, Digital Banking, Regulatory environment, Inflationary pressure and customer's
preference. In these situations, banks need to n ot only have marketing resources, they need to constantly update and
adjust their marketing resources. By combining product, promotion, price and distribution innovations, banks can
create new financial services, build a stronger online footprint, provide a more competitive price for their services and
make banking more convenient and accessible via mobile banking, agency banking and online banking. Therefore,
dynamic capabilities theory can be utilized to derive implications for the banks to develop strate gic marketing
innovation, ensuring their effectiveness in the dynamic environment (Teece et al., 1997; Teece, 2018).
The contingency theory shares similarities with the Lawrence and Lorsch (1967) and other theories that maintained
that there is no best way in which an organisation could be managed. Rather, it is congruence of strategy, structure and
environment that is c orrelated with effectiveness. This study's moderating effect of the environment dynamism is
directly related to the theory. It suggests that Strategic marketing innovations effects on the performance of the
organisation could vary in different business env ironment (stable or unstable). In such a dynamic marketing
environment, banks' reactions to the evolution of the technologies, competitors, regulations and to the customers'
expectations and marketing innovations will be even more significant. However, in a relatively stable environment, this
kind of innovation doesn't always bring about a performance improvement. Thus, the introduction of the dynamism of
the environment as a modifying factor between strategic marketing innovation and operational efficiency  is a part of
contingency theory (Lawrence & Lorsch, 1967; Donaldson, 2001).
2.3. Empirical Review
Ajoke and Olumuyiwa (2026) examined the impact of product marketing strategy on the performance of Deposit Money
Banks in Nigeria with special reference to First Bank Nigeria Plc. The research design was quantitative research design;
whereby structured que stionnaires were used to administer the questionnaires to the sampled bank employees.
Regression techniques were used to analyze data to determine the effect of product marketing strategy on the bank
performance indicators, including profitability, custome r satisfaction, and market share. The results indicate that
product marketing strategy has a strong impact on the performance of banks.
Mwangi and Kihiu (2025) investigated the impact of innovation practices on the organizational performance of SMEs in
Nairobi City County, Kenya. The results of the reviewed literature suggest that the positive impact of innovation
practices on organizational performance i s significant and positive. Product innovation enhances customer value and
market share, process innovation enhances operational efficiency and cost reduction, marketing innovation strengthens
customer engagement and market expansion, and organizational in novation enhances adaptability and strategic
alignment.
Adani (2025) investigated the degree to which innovation affect competitive advantage of chosen manufacturing
companies in the South -East, Nigeria. The research paper took descriptive survey research design. The population of
interest in the research was t wo thousand and two (2002) employees in the sampled manufacturing companies in
Southeast, Nigeria. Random sampling was used to obtain a sample size of 322 respondents. Structured questionnaire
was used to collect primary data.  To test the hypothesis, Pear son Product-moment Correlation was used which found
out that: innovation has a moderate statistical effect on competitive advantage in selected manufacturing firms in
Southeast, Nigeria.

3. Methodology
The study adopted a cross-sectional survey research design. The design was considered appropriate because it enabled
the collection of data from respondents at a single point in time and facilitated the examination of the relationship
between strategic marketing innovations and organizational performance, with environmental dynamism serving as a
moderating variable. Cross -sectional surveys are widely used in management studies because they permit the
investigation of relationships among variables within a d efined population (Creswell & Creswell, 2018; Saunders et al.,
2019).
The study was anchored on the positivist philosophy, which assumes that reality is objective and measurable through
empirical observation. Positivism supported the use of quantitative methods, structured questionnaires, and statistical
analyses in testing the study hypotheses (Bell et al., 2022; Creswell & Creswell, 2018).
The population comprised employees of twenty selected commercial banks operating in South -South Nigeria.
Respondents were drawn from the Operations, Marketing, Customer Service, Treasury, Internal Control, Compliance,
and Human Resources departments. The a ccessible population consisted of 4,986 employees as obtained from the
Human Resource Departments of the selected banks. The sample size was determined using the Taro Yamane (1967)
formula at a 5% level of precision, resulting in a sample size of 370 respo ndents. A proportionate stratified sampling
technique was used to distribute the sample across the selected banks and departments, while simple random sampling
was employed in selecting respondents within each stratum (Sekaran & Bougie, 2016; Saunders et a l., 2019).
The study utilized primary data obtained through a structured questionnaire. Responses were measured using a five -
point Likert scale ranging from Strongly Disagree (1) to Strongly Agree (5). Primary data were considered suitable
because they provided direc t information on the variables under investigation (Sekaran & Bougie, 2016; Bell et al.,
2022). Strategic marketing innovations were measured using product innovation, price innovation, promotion
innovation, and distribution innovation. Organizational perf ormance was measured through operational efficiency,
while environmental dynamism was assessed using indicators relating to technological change, market uncertainty,
competition, and regulatory changes (Ogunnaike et al., 2018; Chaffey & Ellis-Chadwick, 2019). Face and content validity
were established through expert review. Reliability was assessed using Cronbach's Alpha, with a minimum threshold of
0.70 accepted as evidence of internal consistency. A pilot study was conducted to improve the quality and cla rity of the
instrument (Hair et al., 2019; Pallant, 2020).
Data were analyzed using the Statistical Package for Social Sciences (SPSS). Descriptive statistics such as frequencies,
percentages, means, and standard deviations were used to summarize data. The hypotheses were tested using the
Spearman Rank Correlation  Coefficient, while the moderating effect of environmental dynamism was examined using
the Partial Correlation Coefficient. Statistical decisions were made at the 0.05 level of significance (Field, 2018; Pallant,
2020).

The partial correlation results in Table 4 provided evidence regarding the moderating influence of environmental
dynamism. The zero -order correlation between strategic marketing innovations and organizational performance was
0.506 (p < 0.001), indicating a moderate positive relationship. However, when environmental dynamism was controlled,

environmental dynamism exerted a moderating effect on the relationship between strategic marketing innovations and
organizational performance among commercial banks in South-South Nigeria.
5. Discussion of Findings
The findings from the Spearman Rank Correlation analysis revealed that strategic marketing innovation dimensions
significantly influenced organizational performance, measured by operational efficiency, among commercial banks in
South-South Nigeria. Specifi cally, price innovation exhibited a positive and significant relationship with operational
efficiency (r = 0.216, p < 0.01). This finding implies that innovative pricing strategies, including flexible service charges,
competitive interest rates, and custom ized pricing packages, enhanced the operational effectiveness of commercial
banks. The result supports the argument of Ogunnaike et al. (2018) that innovative pricing mechanisms improve
customer retention and organizational competitiveness. The finding is also consistent with the Resource -Based View
(RBV) Theory, which posits that unique organizational capabilities serve as valuable resources that contribute to
superior performance (Barney, 1991). Through innovative pricing approaches, banks were able to im prove resource
utilization and service efficiency.
The study further revealed that product innovation had a moderate positive and significant relationship with
operational efficiency (r = 0.484, p < 0.01). This result indicates that the introduction of innovative banking products,
digital financial service s, and customer -oriented solutions contributed significantly to improved operational
performance. The finding corroborates the empirical study of Olanike and Oladipupo (2024), who reported that product
innovation positively influences organizational sustainability and competitiveness. Similarly, Agu and Obi (2024) found
that organizations that consistently develop innovative products achieve higher levels of performance than those
relying on traditional offerings. The result aligns with Dynamic Capabilities  Theory, which emphasizes the ability of
organizations to renew and reconfigure resources in response to environmental changes (Teece et al., 1997).
Commercial banks that introduced innovative products demonstrated superior adaptability and operational
effectiveness.
Promotion innovation also exhibited a significant positive relationship with operational efficiency (r = 0.396, p < 0.01).
This finding suggests that innovative promotional activities such as digital advertising, social media marketing,
customer engagement campaigns, and integrated communication strategies enhanced organizational performance. The
result supports Chaffey and Ellis -Chadwick (2019), who argued that digital promotional strategies improve customer
acquisition, service utilization, and operational outcomes. Furthermore, Ekwueme et al. (2023) reported that innovative
marketing communication significantly contributes to organizational performance in service -oriented organizations.
From the perspective of RBV Theory, promotion innovation represents a n intangible organizational capability that is
difficult for competitors to imitate, thereby contributing to sustained performance advantages.

The findings also showed that distribution innovation had a positive and significant relationship with operational
efficiency (r = 0.451, p < 0.01). This indicates that innovative service delivery channels such as mobile banking, internet
banking, agency b anking, and automated banking systems improved accessibility, reduced transaction costs, and
enhanced operational efficiency. The finding agrees with Zeithaml et al. (2018), who emphasized that efficient service
delivery systems improve organizational effe ctiveness and customer satisfaction. It also supports the findings of Agu
and Obi (2024), who observed that innovative distribution systems positively influence organizational outcomes. The
result reinforces Dynamic Capabilities Theory because banks that c ontinuously adapted their distribution channels to
changing customer needs achieved superior operational performance.
The partial correlation analysis provided further insight into the moderating role of environmental dynamism. The zero-
order correlation revealed a significant positive relationship between strategic marketing innovations and
organizational performance (r = 0.506, p < 0.001). However, after controlling for environmental dynamism, the
correlation coefficient decreased to 0.403 (p < 0.001). The reduction in the coefficient indicates that environmental
dynamism influenced the strength of the relationship betwe en strategic marketing innovations and organizational
performance. This suggests that changes in technology, customer preferences, competition, and regulatory
requirements affected how effectively marketing innovations translated into improved operational efficiency. The
finding supports the study of Seo (2020), who found that environmental dynamism significantly influences the
effectiveness of organizational strategies. Similarly, Li et al. (2020) reported that environmental uncertainty affects the
relationship between organizational capabilities and performance outcomes. Wang (2022) further argued that
organizations operating in highly dynamic environments must continuously adapt their innovation strategies to sustain
performance. The present finding demon strates that while strategic marketing innovations positively influence
operational efficiency, their effectiveness is partly dependent on prevailing environmental conditions.
The moderating role of environmental dynamism is strongly explained by Contingency Theory. The theory posits that
organizational effectiveness depends on the degree of fit between organizational strategies and environmental
conditions (Lawrence & Lorsch, 1 967; Donaldson, 2001). Accordingly, strategic marketing innovations produced
stronger performance outcomes when they were aligned with environmental realities. Dynamic Capabilities Theory
also provides support by suggesting that organizations must continuo usly sense opportunities, seize market changes,
and reconfigure resources to maintain competitiveness (Teece, 2018). Therefore, the findings collectively indicate that
strategic marketing innovations significantly enhance operational efficiency, while envi ronmental dynamism shapes
the magnitude of that influence within commercial banks operating in South -South Nigeria.
6. Conclusion
The study concluded that strategic marketing innovations significantly enhanced organizational performance among
commercial banks in South-South Nigeria. Specifically, price innovation, product innovation, promotion innovation, and
distribution innovation exhibited positive and significant relationships with operational efficiency. The findings
demonstrated that banks that continuously introduced innovative products, competitive pricing mechanisms, effective
promotional strategies, and efficient distributio n channels achieved superior operational outcomes. The study further
established that environmental dynamism significantly moderated the relationship between strategic marketing
innovations and organizational performance, indicating that external environme ntal conditions influenced the
effectiveness of marketing innovation initiatives. Consequently, commercial banks seeking sustainable performance

Limitations of the Study
The study was limited to selected commercial banks operating in South -South Nigeria; therefore, the findings may not
be fully generalized to other regions or sectors. The use of a cross-sectional research design restricted the investigation
to a single period, making it difficult to capture changes in strategic marketing innovatio ns and organizational
performance over time. Additionally, the study relied on self -reported questionnaire responses, which may have been
influenced by respondent bias and subjective perceptions. The focus on operational efficiency as the sole measure of
organizational performance also limited the examination of other important performance dimensions such as
profitability and market share.
