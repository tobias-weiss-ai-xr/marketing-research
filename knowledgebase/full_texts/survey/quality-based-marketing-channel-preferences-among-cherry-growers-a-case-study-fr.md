ABSTRACT: The main objective of this study was to analyze cherry growers’ marketing channel preferences based on quality
attributes. Additionally, the study examine d marketing channels and sales prices according to the quality levels of cherry varieties. The
primary material of the research consist ed of data obtained through surveys conducted with 103 cherry growers operating in the
Sultandağı district of Afyonkarahisar. Farmers were categorized into three groups based on farm size, and the data were analyzed using
descriptive statistics. The findings indicated that the quality level of cherries had a decisive impact on both the sales price and the choice
of marketing channel. High -quality cherries were found to be sold at premium prices and primarily supplied to the market through
export firms and brokers. In contrast, medium -quality cherries were unable to enter export channels and were mostly directed toward
domestic merchants and the processing industry via brokers. Low -quality cherries were largely diverted to the processing industry, with
limited marketing options. Of the total cherry supply, 52% was directed to export firms, 20% to the domestic market, and 28% to the
industrial sector. Regarding varietal analysis, Ziraat 0900, Regina, and Royal King varieties were predominantly exported, whereas
Gılli, Karaballı, and Akcıllı varieties were mostly utilized in the industrial sector, reflec ting a distinct quality -based structural
differentiation. The results demonstrate d that a quality -driven marketing structure exists in the cherry sector, and quality level is a
primary factor determining growers' access to high -value-added markets. Furthermore, farm scale was found to influence marketing
success, with large -scale growers accessing more advantageous channels. In conclusion, developing quality -oriented production
strategies is of critical importance for increasing grower income levels and enhancing competitiveness in international markets.

Keywords: Cherry, fruit quality, marketing channels, farmers’ preferences.

INTRODUCTION
Sweet cherry ranks among high value -added fruit
species and holds a significant position in the global
horticultural sector in terms of both production
volume and its share in international trade. According
to FAO data, globa l cherry production reached
approximately 3.1 million tons in 2024, with a total
export value of 5 billion US dollars (FAO, 2025).
Türkiye ranks first in world cherry production with a
volume exceeding 700,000 tons in 2024, meeting a
substantial portion of  global supply ( TURKSTAT,
2025). Despite this dominant position in production,
Türkiye's share of global cherry export revenues
remains below its potential. The country exports only
11% of its annual cherry production, generating an
export value of 214 mil lion US dollars (FAO, 2025).
The primary reasons for this discrepancy include
deficiencies in quality st andardization, structural
problems in marketing, and a lack of compliance with
international quality criteria (Çelik and Sarıaltın,
2019; Başkaya, 2013).
In today’s market, where high production volume
does not automatically guarantee market dominance,
producing to meet the high -quality st andards
demanded by national and international markets has
become increasingly critical. According to the modern
agricultural approach, the fundamental objective in
fruit production is to utilize inputs efficiently to
achieve higher yields and superior quality per unit
area (Öztürk et al., 2013). In fruit marketing, quality
classification plays a decisive role in directin g
products toward different market segments (Henson
and Reardon, 2005). Especially for sensitive products
with high export potential like cherries, quality
emerges as one of the most defining elements of the
marketing process. Indeed, empirical studies hav e
demonstrated that high -quality cherries are generally
directed toward export markets and modern retail
chains, whereas low -quality products are utilized in
the domestic market or the processing industry
(Nalinci and Kızılaslan, 2019; Yüzbaşıoğlu and
Yıldırım, 2025).
Fruit quality classes in cherry cultivation are
determined by a combination of physical and
biochemical parameters, such as caliber (fruit width),
texture firmness, epidermal color intensity, and
soluble solids content (SSC). Products defined as
high-quality or 'Extra Class' typically possess a caliber
of 26 -28 mm or above, high mesocarp firmness, and
an SSC ratio exceeding 16-18%, and are characterized
by fresh fruit stems. Medium -quality (Class I) fruits,
with caliber values ranging between 2 2-24 mm, serve
as a source for fresh consumption in the domestic
market. In contrast, low -quality (Class II/industrial
grade) fruits  due to their small fruit size  (below 20
mm), high cracking index, or epidermal defects  are
mostly directed toward the processing industry
(Official Gazette, 2008; Official Gazette, 2023).
The impact of quality levels on the choice of
marketing channels also directly influences price
formation. Within the framework of Hedonic Price
Theory, the quality attributes of a product ar e
accepted as the determinants of its price (Rosen,
1974). Empirical studies in the agricultural economics
literature indicate d that an increase in fruit and
vegetable quality raised producer prices and facilitates
access to more profitable markets. Studies specifically
focusing on cherries similarly reveal ed that quality
attributes have significant effects on export success
and price levels (Nalinci and Kızılaslan, 2019;
Yüzbaşıoğlu and Yıldırım, 2025). When existing
literature was reviewed, it was observe d that studies
on quality in the cherry sector largely focus ed on
production techniques, export performance, or
consumer preferences (Aday, 2008; Adanacıoğlu,
2012; Özüsoy and Koyuncu, 2021; Güçlü et al., 2023;
İkinci et al., 2021). However, research exami ning the
direct impact of quality levels on marketing channel
preferences remains quite limited, representing a
significant research gap in the field. In this context,
studies analyzing quality -based marketing channel
preferences are expected to provide bo th a theoretical
contribution to the literature and practical implications
for industry stakeholders.

This study aimed to address the aforementioned gap in
the literature by analyzing cherry growers’ marketing
channel preferences based on quality attrib utes. Using
the Sultandağı district of Afyonkarahisar province as a
case study, the study examine d the marketing
channels to which growers allocate cherries of
different quality levels and analyze d the effect of
quality on these preferences. In this regard , the study
makes an original contribution to the literature by
investigating the quality –marketing channel
relationship from the perspective of grower behavior.
Furthermore, the findings are expected to contribute to
the development of quality -oriented production and
marketing strategies in the cherry sector, to guide
growers toward higher value -added markets, and to
assist policymakers in designing effective support
mechanisms.

significance for Türkiye’s cherry production and
marketing. Sult andağı alone accounts for
approximately one -fourth of Türkiye's total cherry
exports (Uzer, 2025).
Secondary data were obtained from the
Afyonkarahisar Provincial Directorate of Agriculture
and Forestry and the Sultandağı District Directorate of
Agriculture and Forestry. Furthermore, information
gathered from statistics, scientific articles accessed in
print and online media, proceedings, theses, books,
and reports was utilized.

Data Analysis
In this study, both descriptive statistics and non-
parametric statistical methods were uti lized for data
analysis. Initially, descriptive statistics such as
arithmetic mean ( x̄ ), st andard deviation (SD),
frequency (n) and percentage (%) were calculated to
characterize the demographic and operational profiles
of the cherry growers. In this conte xt, variables such
as grower age, general farming experience, cherry
growing experience, household size, agricultural labor
structure, total l andholding, and cherry production
area were evaluated.
Data regarding sales prices and marketing channels

statistics. Average sales prices for cherries in different
quality groups were calculated and distributions

grower preferences.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Demographic and Structural Characteristics of the
Interviewed Cherry Growers
The fundamental demographic and operational
characteristics of the cherry growers participating in
the survey are presented in Table 1. An examination
of the findings revealed several differences among the
grower groups from both demographic and structural
perspectives. Regarding age, the overall mean age of
the growers was 57.66 years, which is consistent with
the aging trend commonly observed in the agricultural
sector. When evaluated by groups, the second group
had the highest mean age (61 .01 years), while the
third group had the lowest (55.18 years). According to
the Kruskal -Wallis test results, a statistically
significant difference was found between the groups
in terms of age (p = 0 .049). This finding suggests that
larger-scale farms are managed by relatively younger
growers, reflecting a tendency among younger
farmers to own more l and or to exp and their
operations.
Analysis of farming experience and cherry production
experience variables indicate d that the growers
possess long -standing expertise. The overall means
were calculated as 33 .82 years and 29.74 years,
respectively. Although the second group of growers
exhibited higher averages in both types of experience,
the statistical analysis showed no significant
difference between the groups (p > 0 .05). This
situation indicated that different farm sizes maintain a
similar structure regarding professional experience.
In terms of household size, the average number of
individuals was 3 .32, with a significant difference
observed among the groups (p = 0 .003). The average
number of individuals engaged in agriculture within
the household was 2.21 and no statistically significant
difference was found between the groups for this
variable (p = 0.926). This finding revealed that cherry
farms are largely dependent on family labor and that
the labor structure is independent of group size.
When indicators regarding the l andholdings of the
farms were examined, distinct structural differences
emerged among the groups. The average total
landholding was 9 .70 decares (da) in the first group,
23.32 da in the second group, and 49.27 da in the third
group. This demonstrated that land size, which formed
the basis of the grouping, directly reflects the farm
structure. Similarly, the total number of plots
increased with farm size, reaching its highest value in
the third group with an average of 6 .70 plots. The
increase in the number of plots highlights l and
fragmentation as a significant factor in these farms.
Regarding the areas allocated to cherry production, it
was determined that the total cherr y area was 5 .44 da
in the first group, 14 .96 da in the second group, and
36.46 da in the third group. This finding show ed that
as farm size increase d, the area dedicated to cherry
production also increase d significantly. The total
number of cherry plots showed a similar increase,
reaching an average of 4 .95 in the third group. This
suggested that large -scale farms have a more
specialized structure in cherry production.
Overall, it can be concluded that while the
demographic characteristics of cherry growers show
broad similarities, distinct differences emerge in
landholding and production structure based on farm
size. In particular, the management of larger farms by
younger growers and their possession of more
extensive cherry production areas indicate d that these
farms may be in a more advantageous position
regarding production and marketing. These findings
suggested that farm scale is a critical factor
influencing not only production capacity but also
marketing behaviors and quality-oriented production
decisions.

toward more limited and lower value-added channels.
This situation underscores the imperative for
developing quality-oriented production and marketing
strategies within the cherry sector.
Marketing Channels and Sales Prices of Cherry
Varieties Based on Quality Levels
Sweet cherries are primarily consumed fresh.
However, quality variations resulting from varietal
characteristics and production conditions lead to
differences in consumption patterns. Consumers
generally make purchasing decisions based on the
external appearance of the fruit  such as size, color,
firmness, and the condition of the stem  preferring
high-quality, large, and visually appealing fruits
(Çelik and Hepaksoy, 2021).
High-quality and durable var ieties such as 0900
Ziraat, which holds a significant production and
market share in Türkiye, are primarily subjects of
international trade. In contrast, lower -quality or softer
varieties are utilized in the domestic market and for
industrial-type (processing) consumption. According
to cherry quality classes, a significant portion of the
production involving firm, large-fruited (generally 26-
28+ mm), and long-lasting varieties that fall into the
'Extra Class' such as 0900 Ziraat, Stella, Van , and
Bing is ut ilized in international markets, with a
smaller portion directed toward retail chains (Namdar
et al., 2023).

The marketing channel preferences by cherry variety

this variety is positioned in the high -quality segment
and faces strong dem and in international markets.
Conversely, the sale of approximately 30% to
domestic market traders indicated that while the entire
yield cannot be subject to export, a significant portion
was still utilized in relatively high  value-added
markets. The very limited amount directed to the
processor (factory) channel showed that this variety is
almost non-existent in the low-quality segment. In this
respect, 0900 Ziraat  holds a position as one of the
defining varieties of the sector in terms of both sales
volume and market orientation.
When Regina and Royal King varieties were evaluated
together, there was a total supply of approximately
195 tons, of which about 83% was directed to export
firms. The fact that the share allocated to the domestic
market was quite low, and the amount directed to the
industry was negligible, indicate d that these varieties
were produced largely for export and met high-quality
standards. Therefore, the marketing structure of these
two varieties exhibited a higher value-added character
than even 0900 Ziraat.
In contrast, the Gılli variety was presented to the
market by 67 growers with a total volume of 433 tons,
having an average sales level of 6,468 kg per farm.
However, the fact that the entire amount was directed
toward the factory channel demonstrate d that this
variety cannot find a place in fresh consumption or
export markets and was utilized entirely for the
processing industry. This situation reveal ed that the
Gılli variety is positioned in the lower quality segment
with limited marketing options.
Similarly, it is noteworthy that for the Karaballı and
Akcıllı varieties, the entire supply of 37 tons was
directed to the factory channel. The inability of these
varieties, which have limited production volu mes, to
compete in fresh consumption and export markets
indicated that they belong to the low -quality segment
and possess a marketing structure dependent on the
industry. The average sales volumes further support
this conclusion.
Of the total cherries supplied to the market, 52% were
directed to export firms, 20% to the domestic market,
and 28% to the industrial sector. Overall, sales
volumes and average values on a varietal basis clearly
revealed a distinct structural differentiation based on
quality in th e cherry sector. High -quality varieties
(0900 Ziraat, Regina , and Royal King ) both reach ed
higher sales volumes and concentrated in export -
oriented, high value -added markets. On the other
hand, varieties in the low -quality group ( Gılli,
Karaballı, and Akcıllı) were largely directed toward
the processing industry and utilized in the lowest
value-added markets. Within this framework, it is
concluded that production pattern and variety
selection are direct determinants of the income levels
and marketing performance of the growers.

CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS
In this study, the marketing channel preferences of
cherry growers in the Sult andağı district of
Afyonkarahisar province were analyzed based on
quality attributes. The findings reveal ed that the
relationship between quality, price, and marketing
channels in the cherry sector exhibits a highly distinct
and systematic structure.
According to the research results, it was determined
that as the quality  level of cherries increases, the
products are sold at higher prices and directed toward
more advantageous marketing channels. The
marketing of high -quality cherries largely to export
firms via commission agents indicates that this
product group is well -integrated into international
markets. In contrast, it was found that medium-quality
cherries are unable to participate in export channels
and are instead utilized by traders and the processing
industry, primarily through commission agents. Low -
quality cherr ies, however, were found to be directed
almost entirely to processing industry channels via
commission agents, with very limited marketing
options.
In the research region, while 52% of the total cherries
supplied to the market were exported, 48% were
directed to the domestic market (traders or the
processing industry). When evaluated by variety, the
findings demonstrate a clear quality -based structural
differentiation within the sector. High-quality varieties
(0900 Ziraat, Regina , and Royal King ) are market ed
in higher volumes and are concentrated in high value -
added channels such as export. Conversely, low -
quality varieties ( Gılli, Karaballı , and Akcıllı) are
predominantly utilized in the processing industry and
low value -added markets. Consequently, the
production pattern and variety selection emerge as the
primary determinants of farmer income and marketing
success.
These findings underscore the existence of a distinct
quality-based channel hierarchy within the cherry
marketing system. It was observed that  export
channels in the upper segment are open only to high -
quality products; as the quality level decreases,
products are directed toward lower value -added and
more restricted markets. This clearly demonstrates
that the quality level determines not only the price but
also the type of market accessible to the product.
Furthermore, the research identified certain
differences among grower groups. In particular, it was
determined that larger -scale farms (the third group)
realized sales at higher prices and demonstrated
greater strength in accessing more advantageous
marketing channels. This indicates that farm scale is a
significant factor in terms of marketing success and
quality-oriented production.
In conclusion, it was demonstrated that quality -
oriented production in the cherry sector is not merely
a technical production choice but also a strategic
marketing element. An increase in quality enables
growers to obtain higher income and enhances their
access to international markets. Conversely, low
quality directs growers toward more limited and lower

In light of the research findings, to increase their

From a policymaking perspective, encouraging

support policies should be developed; specifically,
premium payments per kilogram should be provided
to growers of high-quality cherries, access to modern
production technologies —particularly automated
grading devices for calib er and color—should be

Increasing transparency in the marketing chain and

low and medium-quality products and the
implementation of policies that increase value -added

While this study was limited to a specific region,

different production regions would make significant
contributions to the literature. Furthermore, holistic
