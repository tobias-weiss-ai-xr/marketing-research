Abstract
MSMEs constitute the structural backbone of global tourism economies, yet
their capacity to pursue innovation-driven sustainability pathways remains
systematically undertheorised and empirically fragmented. While individual
studies have examined specific innovation types or barriers in isolation, no
comprehensive synthesis simultaneously maps dominant innovation strategies,
interrogates prevailing methodological and theoretical orientations, and
critically identifies barriers, success factors, and future directions for tourism
MSME innovation. This study addresses that gap through a SLR of 63 peer-
reviewed articles retrieved from the Scopus database covering the period 2020–
2026. The PRISMA 2020 protocol guided the screening and selection process
across 217 initially identified records. Three research questions structured the
synthesis. The findings reveal seven dominant innovation strategy clusters:
digital innovation, green innovation, business model innovation, marketing
innovation, social innovation, ser vice innovation, and community -based
innovation. Methodologically, the corpus is dominated by quantitative survey-
based approaches underpinned by Resource -Based View and Innovation
Diffusion Theory, while longitudinal, ethnographic, and multi -stakeholder
designs remain scarce. Financial constraints, digital literacy deficits, institutional
voids, and fragmented governance structures are identified as primary barriers,
with leadership capacity, collaborative ecosystems, and sustainability orientation
serving as key enablers. The study's novelty resides in its cross-cutting synthesis
integrating strategy classification, theoretical critique, and future research
agenda, with explicit attention to developing-country and Indonesian MSME
contexts.

INTRODUCTION
Micro, small, and medium
enterprises represent not a peripheral
category of tourism business but its
structural foundation. Across both
developed and developing economies,
MSMEs account for between 80 and 95
percent of all registered tourism
businesses and generate the majority
of tourism -related employment
(OECD, 2022; WTO, 2023). In countries
such as Indonesia, where the tourism
sector comprises over 30 million micro
and small enterprises embedded
across accommodation, food services,
transport, guiding, and handicraft
production, this structural reality is
especially pronounced (BPS, 2023). Yet
despite this demographic dominance,
the MSME sector has remained
disproportionately marginalised in
high-impact scholarship on innovation
and sustainability, two of the most
consequential strategic concerns
shaping the future of global tourism.
The transition toward
sustainable tourism development, as
codified in the United Nations 2030
Agenda and its Sustainable
Development Goals (SDGs),
particularly SDGs 8, 12, and 17,
requires not merely the compliance of
large-scale operators but the active
participation and innovative capacity
of the millions of small enterprises that
constitute the lived fabric of tourism
destinations (Streimikiene et al., 2021).
Innovation in this context is not a
luxury but a competitive and
existential necessity: as consumer
expectations shift toward authentic,
low-impact, and digitally mediated
experiences, tourism MSMEs that fail
to adapt risk progressive
marginalisation in rapidly evolving
markets (Cordova et al., 2025;
Setiowati & Bandur, 2025).
The existing literature on tourism
MSME innovation, while growing in
volume, exhibits three structural
limitations. First, it is characterised by
fragmentation across innovation
types, with studies examining digital
innovation (Gutierriz et al., 2023) ,
green practices (Wei et al., 2023), or
business model transformation
(Nguyen et al., 2023)  in relative
disciplinary isolation. Second,
methodological asymmetry is evident:
the dominance of cross -sectional,
quantitative, survey-based approaches
Cordova et al.  (2025); Streimikiene et
al. (2021), limits the field's capacity to
capture dynamic processes and
relational dimensions of collaborative
innovation central to MSME
performance in destination
ecosystems. Third, the majority of
published studies originate from
European, East Asian, or North
American contexts (Sharuddin &
Roddin, 2025; Wei et al., 2023) ,
generating theoretical frameworks
whose transferability to developing-
country settings is rarely interrogated.
Innovation in tourism MSMEs
intersects with sustainability through
at least three distinct pathways. The
first is ecological: green innovation,
encompassing renewable energy
adoption, waste reduction, and
circular supply chain integration,
directly reduces the ecological
footprint of MSME operations (Wei et
al., 2023). The second is economic:
business model and digital innovation
enable MSMEs to reach new market
segments and reduce transaction costs

Three specific research gaps
motivate this review. First, no existing
synthesis comprehensively maps the
full spectrum of innovation strategies
deployed by tourism MSMEs, leaving
unclear which strategies are most
adopted, most strongly associated
with sustainability outcomes, and how
different strategies interact. Second,
the theoretical pluralism evident in the
MSME innovation research, with
Resource-Based View (Simarmata et
al., 2024), Dynamic Capability Theory
(Nguyen et al., 2023) , Innovation
Diffusion Theory (Cordova et al.,
2025), and Institutional Theory
(Bhuiyan et al., 2023) all appearing
across the corpus, has not been
critically synthesised to assess
complementarity and tensions
between these frameworks. Third,
barriers and enablers of MSME
innovation have been catalogued in
individual case studies (Cordova et al.,
2025) but not systematically assessed
across diverse geographic and
institutional contexts.
This study contributes to these
gaps by conducting a rigorous SLR of
Scopus-indexed literature on tourism
MSME innovation and sustainability
published between 2020 and 2026.
Three research questions guide the
synthesis:
RQ1: What innovation strategies are
most frequently implemented by
tourism MSMEs to support
sustainable tourism development?
RQ2: What research methods and
theoretical perspectives dominate
studies on innovation strategies in
tourism MSMEs?
RQ3: What barriers, success factors,
and future strategic directions are
identified in the implementation of
innovation strategies for sustainable
tourism development?

METHOD
This study employs the
Systematic Literature Review (SLR)
methodology, which applies a
predefined, structured protocol for
identifying, screening, evaluating, and
synthesising evidence from existing
research (Ateljevic & Page, 2021). SLR
is distinguished from narrative review
by its explicit, reproducible
procedures, which minimise selection
bias and enhance the transparency and
verifiability of conclusions (Ateljevic &
Page, 2021). The protocol followed in
this study conforms to the PRISMA
2020 framework, the internationally
recognised standard for reporting
systematic reviews in the social and
management sciences.

Database Selection and Search
Strategy
The search strategy yielded 217
records from the Scopus database
before duplicate removal and
eligibility screening. These records
formed the initial dataset for the
systematic review process. Data
collection was conducted exclusively
using the Scopus database, selected for

The publication window of 2020–
2026 was chosen deliberately to
capture the structural discontinuity
produced by the COVID-19 pandemic,
which triggered accelerated digital
adoption, heightened sustainability
demand, and intensified policy
attention to MSME resilience. The
search was limited to English -
language journal articles and reviews.
Earlier foundational studies published
before 2020 were retained exclusively
for theoretical background and were
not included in the SLR corpus.

Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria
Inclusion criteria required
studies to: (1) be published between
2020 and 2026 in peer -reviewed
Scopus-indexed journals; (2) be
written in English; (3) specifically
address MSMEs, SMEs, or small
enterprises in tourism, hospitality, or
experience economy sectors; (4)
examine at least one dimension of
innovation strategy; and (5) contribute
findings relevant to sustainable
tourism development or SDG-aligned
goals.
Exclusion criteria eliminated studies
that: (1) focused exclusively on large-
scale corporations without reference to
SME dynamics; (2) treated innovation
as peripheral rather than central; (3)
examined sustainability in purely
normative terms without empirical or
analytical content; or (4) were
conference papers, book chapters, or
grey literature not subject to peer
review.

Quality Assessment
Each document was evaluated against
four QA criteria:
QA1: Was the article published
between 2020 and 2026 in a peer -
reviewed Scopus-indexed journal?
QA2: Does the study specifically focus
on tourism MSMEs, SMEs, or small
tourism enterprises as the primary
unit of analysis?
QA3: Does the study examine
innovation strategy as a central
theoretical or empirical construct?
QA4: Does the study produce findings
relevant to sustainable tourism
development, sustainability outcomes,
or SDG-aligned goals?

followed the Preferred Reporting
Items for Systematic Reviews and
Meta-Analyses (PRISMA 2020)

Digital Innovation
Digital innovation constitutes the
most extensively studied cluster (18
studies; 28.6%), reflecting the broader
digital transformation imperative
reshaping the competitive landscape
for tourism enterprises since 2020.
Within this cluster, studies range from
accessible digital tools, including
social media management, e -
commerce integration, and mobile
booking platforms (Cordova et al.,
2025), to more sophisticated
applications such as data analytics for
visitor profiling, AI-assisted customer
engagement, and cloud -based
operational management (Gutierriz et
al., 2023). A consistent finding across
studies is that digital adoption by
tourism MSMEs is strongly bifurcated
by size and resource endowment:
micro-enterprises, which constitute
the numerical majority of the sector,
demonstrate adoption rates
significantly lower than small and
medium enterprises, primarily owing
to capital constraints and low digital
literacy (Gutierriz et al., 2023; Wei et
al., 2023).

Several studies document the
catalytic role of COVID -19 in
accelerating adoption among
previously resistant MSMEs, with
Gutierriz et al. (2023), framing the
pandemic as a 'forced experiment' in
digital pivot that produced durable
changes in business model orientation
among enterprises that survived.
Nguyen et al. (2023), further document
how crisis-induced digital adoption
created a bimodal distribution within
the MSME sector: enterprises that
achieved substantive digital
transformation during the pandemic
emerged with significantly enhanced
competitive positioning, while those
that adopted surface -level
digitalization without underlying
process reconfiguration reverted to
pre-crisis operational models. A
critical analytical distinction emerges
from the corpus between surface-level
digitalization, the adoption of a social
media presence or digital payment
without strategic reconfiguration, and
substantive digital innovation that
transforms value creation and
delivery. The former is far more
prevalent among tourism MSMEs in
developing economies (Efendi, 2025;
Rahman et al., 2024).

Green Innovation
Green innovation, examined in
15 studies (23.8%), represents the most
theoretically diverse cluster, drawing
on environmental management
theory, ecological modernisation, and
circular economy frameworks. Studies
examine practices ranging from
energy efficiency and renewable
energy adoption (Wei et al., 2023),
through sustainable sourcing and
waste minimisation, to environmental
certification systems such as Green
Key and GSTC standards (Wei et al.,
2023). A significant finding is that
green innovation in MSMEs is strongly
moderated by institutional context:
enterprises operating in destinations
with well-developed regulatory
frameworks and DMO -promoted
sustainability standards demonstrate
significantly higher adoption rates
than those in institutional voids (Wei
et al., 2023).
A persistent theoretical tension in this
cluster concerns financial viability:
while RBV frameworks position green
innovation as a sustainable
competitive advantage through cost
reduction and premium market access,
several empirical studies find that the
financial payback period for green
investment exceeds the time horizons
accessible to capital -constrained
MSMEs (Wei et al., 2023). Wei et al.
(2023), term this the 'green investment
gap', arguing it cannot be resolved
through firm-level strategy alone and
requires enabling institutional
infrastructure including green
financing schemes and collective
certification arrangements.

Business model innovation (10
studies; 15.9%) addresses the
reconfiguration of value creation,
delivery, and capture architectures at
the enterprise level. This cluster
encompasses experience -economy
reorientation, collaborative
consumption models, and soc ial
enterprise hybrids (Nguyen et al.,
2023). Multiple post-COVID studies
document how the pandemic forced

Marketing, Social, Service, and
Community-Based Innovation
The remaining four clusters
collectively reflect the multi -
dimensionality of sustainable
innovation in tourism MSMEs.
Marketing innovation studies (7
studies; 11.1%) document how MSMEs
leverage storytelling, authentic brand
narratives, and niche market targeting
to access sustainability -conscious
consumer segments without large -
operator marketing budgets (Cordova
et al., 2025; Sharuddin & Roddin,
2025). Setiowati & Bandur (2025), find
that marketing innovation oriented
around local identity and place-based
authenticity generates significantly
higher willingness-to-pay premiums
among sustainable tourism consumers
than generic green marketing. Social
innovation studies (5 studies; 7.9%),
while the most theoretically under-
developed, present compelling case
evidence for MSMEs as vehicles for
inclusive development and cultural
preservation (Sharuddin & Roddin,
2025). Service innovation studies (5
studies; 7.9%) examine guest
experience co -creation and
personalisation through service -
dominant logic frameworks (Setiowati
& Bandur, 2025). Community-based
innovation (3 studies; 4.8%), the least
researched cluster, documents how
MSMEs in indigenous and rural
destinations generate innovation
through participatory governance that
simultaneously advances commercial
viability and community sovereignty
(Cordova et al., 2025; Sharuddin &
Roddin, 2025).

quantitative approaches in MSME

underweights the processual,
relational, and institutional
dimensions of how small enterprises

captured through case study and
ethnographic designs. Mixed-method

explanatory power by integrating
quantitative breadth with qualitative
depth, and their relatively higher
prevalence compared to earlier SLRs
on related topics suggests a gradual
maturation of the field's
methodological repertoire.
Resource-Based View constitutes
the most frequently applied theoretical
framework, underpinning 22 of the 63
reviewed studies. Its dominance
reflects its intuitive alignment with the
MSME context: the focus on
heterogeneous, firm-specific resource
bundles as sources of competitive
advantage maps naturally onto the
distinctive competencies, often
embedded in individual owner -
managers, that characterise tourism
MSME performance (Cordova et al.,
2025; Nguyen et al., 2023; Simarmata et
al., 2024). However, several authors
raise substantive critiques: Barney's
original framework presupposes clear
property rights over resources and
relatively stable competitive
environments, neither condition
reliably present in the fluid, platform-
mediated tourism landscape of the
2020s. Dynamic Capability Theory
from Nguyen et al. (2023), is
increasingly applied as a complement,
precisely because it incorporates the
processes of resource sensing, seizing,
and reconfiguration that characterise
MSME responses to the volatile post-
pandemic environment (Nguyen et al.,
2023; Wei et al., 2023). Institutional
Theory from Bhuiyan et al. (2023),
provides the most analytically
powerful framework for
understanding how external
pressures, including regulatory
requirements, certification systems,
and destination management norms,
shape MSME innovation behaviour,
particularly in the green innovation
cluster where normative isomorphism
is empirically well documented (Wei
et al., 2023).

geographic and cultural contexts.

Financial constraints emerge as the
most pervasive structural barrier.
Tourism MSMEs, particularly micro-
enterprises, operate with minimal
retained earnings, limited access to
formal credit, and short planning
horizons that make innovation
investment, espe cially green
innovation with extended payback
periods, structurally difficult (Wei et
al., 2023). Studies in Southeast Asian
contexts document how the absence of
innovation-specific financing
instruments forces MSMEs to rely on
personal savings or informal credit
networks (Cordova et al., 2025). Digital
literacy deficits constitute a second
structural barrier with particular
severity in developing -country
contexts. Owner-manager digital
competency is consistently identified
as the single most important internal
predictor of digital innovation
adoption (Gutierriz et al., 2023; Wei et
al., 2023). A critical finding from the
corpus is that digital literacy is not
binary but a continuum: MSMEs
achieving minimal functional digital
literacy frequently stall without
progressing to strategically
transformative digital capabilities
(Efendi, 2025).
Human resource limitations
including high staff turnover, low
sectoral wages, and seasonal
employment, undermine the
accumulation of innovation-relevant
human capital at the organisational
level. Several studies document a
'training trap' in which MSMEs invest
in human capital only to lose trained
staff to larger competitors, producing
disincentives for continued
investment (Cordova et al., 2025).
Technology adoption barriers
encompass both supply -side
constraints, including cost,
infrastructure availability, and vendor
reliability, and demand -side
constraints including managerial risk
aversion (Gutierriz et al., 2023;
Nguyen et al., 2023). In rural and
remote tourism destinations,
infrastructure deficits, particularly
unreliable electricity and internet
connectivity, represent physical
barriers that firm-level strategy cannot
overcome without destination-level
investment. Institutional barriers arise
from regulatory frameworks
misaligned with MSME innovation
needs, certification systems imposing
disproportionate compliance costs,
and governance structures prioritising
large operator interests (Streimikiene
et al., 2021; Wei et al., 2023). Policy
barriers at destination and national
levels, including inadequate MSME-
specific tourism policy and
fragmented institutional mandates,
complete the structural barrier picture
(Sharuddin & Roddin, 2025; Wei et al.,
2023).

Sharuddin & Roddin, 2025) .

destination governance context
(Sharuddin & Roddin, 2025; Wei et al.,
2023).

Future Strategic Directions
The synthesis identifies six
strategic directions as priority areas for
both research and practice. AI and
data-driven personalisation represents
the technology frontier for tourism
MSME innovation over the 2025–2030
horizon, with emerging low-cost AI
tools beginning to reduce technology
cost barriers (Gutierriz et al., 2023).
Smart and regenerative tourism
frameworks, integrating digital and
sustainability innovation at the
destination level, create contexts
where MSME digital innovation is
amplified by shared infrastructure
(Nguyen et al., 2023) . Circular
economy integration in MSME
operations represents an emerging
practice frontier with limited but
promising evidence in food tourism
and accommodation (Setiowati &
Bandur, 2025; Wei et al., 2023) .
Collaborative innovation and platform
co-operatives are identified as
institutionally innovative responses to
collective action problems preventing
individual MSMEs from accessing
innovation resources available to
larger enterprises (Streimikiene et al.,

adaptive governance emerge from the
social innovation cluster as a strategic
direction with particular relevance in
the post-pandemic, climate-change-
affected landscape (Sharuddin &
Roddin, 2025). Green financing and
certification ecosystems represent the
most critical enabling infrastructure
for accelerating green innovation
adoption, with several studies
identifying co-funded certification and
blended green finance instruments as
high-leverage interventions (Wei et al.,
2023).

Implications for Developing
Economies and Indonesia
The geographic concentration of
the reviewed corpus in European and
East Asian contexts constitutes both a
limitation and a research opportunity.
The structural conditions shaping
MSME innovation in developing

regulatory environments, informal
economic practices, limited

capital constraints, require
theoretically adapted frameworks

presents a particularly instructive case.

Policy Implications
The synthesis generates three
priority policy implications. First,
MSME-specific innovation financing
instruments, including innovation
vouchers, green transition subsidies,
and collective certification cost -
sharing schemes, are identified as the
most critical demand-side policy
intervention (Wei et al., 2023). Second,
destination-level digital governance,
in which DMOs assume a coordinating
role in providing shared digital
infrastructure and collectively funded
training programmes, is identified as
the most cost-effective supply-side
intervention for expanding M SME
digital innovation access (Gutierriz et
al., 2023). Third, integration of MSME
innovation support into national
sustainable tourism certification
systems, through streamlined
certification pathways and staged
compliance frameworks, would align
institutional incentives with MSME
innovation trajectories mor e
effectively than current one-size-fits-
all regulatory frameworks (Wei et al.,
2023). From a regulatory standpoint,
policy frameworks must transition
from generic financial stimuli to
strategic, ecosystem -oriented
interventions. First, rather than
offering isolated micro -credits,
governments should tie fiscal
incentives (such as specialized KUR or
tourism credit schemes) to the
adoption of green and circular
practices among hospitality MSMEs.
Second, policies need to actively foster
hybrid collaboration models. This can
be achieved by institutionalizing
structured mentoring channels where
digital creative winners or established
private tech-platforms act as digital
aggregators for rural homestays and
community-based operators. Lastly, to
overcome the critical resource barriers
identified in the literature, national
tourism offices must shift from macro-
promotional budgeting toward
decentralized, competency -based
capacity building. This entails curating
specialized travel paths and narrative-
driven storytelling frameworks that
force MSMEs to systematically co -
create sustainable value, rather than
leaving innovation to be fragmented,
individual efforts.

CONCLUSION
This study provides the first
comprehensive synthesis of
innovation strategies in tourism
MSMEs within the sustainable tourism
development frame. The findings
reveal a field characterised by both
conceptual richness and structural
limitations. Seven innovation strategy
clusters were identified, with digital
and green innovation receiving the
most research attention, while
community-based and social
innovation remain substantially
underresearched despite their direct
alignment with sustainable tourism
principles. The theoretical landscape is
dominated by Resource-Based View
and Innovation Diffusion Theory,
frameworks that, while productive,
exhibit explanatory limits in the
relational, ecosystem-dependent, and
institutionally embedded contexts that
characterise MSME innovation in
tourism destinations. Quantitative,
cross-sectional designs account for
nearly half the corpus, limiting the
field's capacity to capture dynamic
and institutional dimensions of
innovation. Identified barriers include
financial constraints, digital literacy
deficits, human resource limitations,
and institutional and policy barriers,
which together constrain MSME
innovation capacity. The most
consistently documented success
factors point toward collective and
institutional, rather than purely firm-
level, solutions.
The review contributes
theoretically, through a critical
reappraisal of dominant frameworks
and a call for systems innovation
perspectives; empirically, by
documenting the full spectrum of
strategies and key knowledge gaps;
and practically, through an evidence-
based synthesis for policymakers,
destination managers, and MSME
practitioners.
