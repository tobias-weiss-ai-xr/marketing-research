ABSTRACT
Purpose – This study aims to map the scientific landscape of halal
certification research and examine its publication development, thematic
focus, and key contributors in the global halal literature.
Design – A bibliometric approach was used, drawing on Scopus-indexed
journal articles retrieved using  the keywords “halal certification” and
“halal product certification.” After screening and removing duplicates,
142 relevant articles published between 2010 and 2025 were analyzed
using the Bibliometrix R-package through the Biblioshiny interface.
Findings – The results indicate rapid growth in research in this field,
with an average annual growth rate of 19.24%, highlighting Indonesia
and Malaysia as the most dominant contributors . The Journal of Islamic
Marketing emerged as the most influential source, while author
productivity remains concentrated among a small number of scholars.
The novelty of this study lies in providing one of the earliest
comprehensive bibliometric mappings of halal certification, using Scopus
data and the Biblioshiny visualization tool.
Research Implications – Future research should integrate additional
databases and explore emerging themes , such as digital halal governance
and consumer protection, to strengthen halal certification studies.
Originality – This study presents the most extensive bibliometric review
of halal certification research in the global halal literature,  delivering the
first dedicated Scopus-based mapping of publication trends,  thematic
evolution, and leading contributors while introducing a novel Halal
Certification Research Landscape Framework.

INTRODUCTION
 Amid the rapid growth of the global halal economy, the phenomenon occurring in
Indonesia requires the Government to accommodate this trend by providing a positive response
and legal certainty through regulations on  halal products and halal labels (Anshori et al., 2025) .
Government regulations are needed by society not only to protect consumers but also to increase

verifies and validates the halal declaration.
 Although previous bibliometric studies have explored the broader halal industry and
related topics, dedicated analyses specifically focusing on halal certification remain relatively
scarce. Most existing works, such as Masood et al. (2022), examined halal certification only as part
of a larger halal ecosystem and covered data up to 2021. Other studies have addressed halal food,
halal products, or halal supply chain in general, but rarely positioned halal cer tification as the
central theme (Hastuti et al., 2024) Furthermore, r ecent developments in Indonesia particularly
the enforcement of the Halal Product Assurance Law and the se lf-declaration scheme for
MSMEs have not been adequately captured in prior bibliometric mappings.
This study fills these gaps by providing a more focused and updated bibliometric analysis
of 142 Scopus -indexed articles on “halal certification” and “halal product certification” from
2010 to 2025. Employing the Bibliometrix R -package and Biblioshiny interface, this research
offers one of the earliest comprehensive mappings that integrates publication trends, country and
institutional contributions, intellectual structure through keyword networks and thematic maps,
and the evolving research landscape with special atte ntion to the Indonesian context (Fadhlina &
Nor, 2024)  By doing so, the present study contributes a clearer scientific portrait of halal
certification research and introduces a novel Halal Certification Research Landscape Framework
to guide future studies and policy development in this rapidly growing field.
METHOD
 This study applies a bibliometric analysis approach to quantitatively measure and evaluate
scientific publications. In which this study focuses on articles indexed in Scopus. The initial stage
involved a document search in Scopus using the keywords “halal certification” and “halal product
certification”. This search produced 782 scientific documents across various publication types,
including journal articles, books, book chapters, conference proceedings, conference reviews,
review articles, editorials, errata, and notes. Furthermore, documents not classified as journal
articles were excluded, reducing the total to 448 articles.
 Because several articles may appear repeatedly, we merged the databases, resulting in  312
articles. To ensure relevance to our research focus, the next stage involved a manual examination
of the titles, abstracts, and keywords of the 312 articles with the following criteria: (1) the title,
abstract, or keywords clearly mention one of the main keywords that have been determined, and
(2) if the terms “halal” or “halal certification” are not explicitly mentioned together with the
context of the product or the certification process in the title, then the examination was
continued to the abstract and keywords. This screening process ultimately produced 142 articles
that were truly relevant and were used as the main data in the subsequent bibliometric analysis.
 The data in the final sample were then analyzed using the Bibliometrix R package to
obtain a clearer, more accurate analysis. This software is a tool developed by Aria and Cuccurullo
for quantitative research. The latest version of this software is a web-based application

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
 Research on halal certification has advanced rapidly and has remained a major concern
for academics over the past decade. The table below reveals information regarding articles related
to halal certification. The bibliometr ic analysis conducted using the Scopus database for the
2010–2025 period identified 142 scientific documents, all of which were journal articles and
proceedings, published across  83 different publication sources. During this period, publications
on this topic grew at an average annual rate of 19.24%, producing an average of 20.25 citations
per document and an average document age of 4.56 years. The total number of contributing
authors was 455, with a relatively high level of international collaboration at  30.28% and an
average of 3.47 co -authors per article. Only 13 documents (9.15%) were written independently
(single-authored), while the entire collection of articles cited 1,041 references. These findings
confirm that halal certification research has reached a stage of maturity, with productivity and
visibility continuing to increase, supported by relatively strong  cross-country collaboration.

Sources Analysis
 This section describes the journal sources that make the largest contribution to the
development of the halal certification literature. The relevance assessment is based on the
number of documents published (Number of Documents) as an indicator of a journal’s
productivity in the topic area . The mapping results show that the Journal of Islamic Marketing
overwhelmingly dominates, publishing 31 articles on halal certification, making it the leading  and
most productive journal in this field. The second position is held by the International Journal of
Supply Chain Management, with 6 articles, followed by the Global Journal Al -Thaqafah (5
articles) and Jurisdictie: Jurnal Hukum dan Syariah (4 articles). Furthermore, Asian Social Science,
Malaysian Journal of Consumer and Family Economics, and Uncertain Supply Chain
Management each contribute  3 articles. Meanwhile, Advanced Science Letters, Agbioforum, and
Al-Ihkam: Journal of  Hukum dan Pranata Sosial each contributed 2 articles. The Journal of
Islamic Marketing's significant dominance (31 articles) reinforces its position as the primary
publication hub for halal certification research. It reflects the academic community's strong focus
on marketing aspects, consumer behavior, and business strategies grounded in halal principles.

 An analysis of author productivity can also be viewed through the lens of Lotka’s Law,
which emphasizes that the distribution of scientific contributions is highly uneven: only a small
number of authors produce a large number of publications, while the majority contribute only a
few. (Sofyan et al., 2023) . By applying Lotka’s Law, researchers can identify elite groups of
authors and understand the structure of the scientific community in a particular field. The vertical
axis shows the percentage of authors, while the horizontal axis reflects the number of documents
published; the dashed line represents the ideal pattern of Lotka’s Law. The figure shows that
94.3% of authors published only 1 article, 5.2% published 2 articles, 1.2% published 3 articles,
and 0.5% published 4 articles ; thus, only 6.9% of authors published more than 1 article . This
pattern demonstrates that halal certification research closely follows Lotka’s Law , with most
significant contributions coming from a small number of highly productive authors.

 Information on authors can also be used to analyze institutional affiliations and authors’
countries of origin in publications on halal certification. Based on the data analysis obtained from
the application, Universiti Utara Malaysia is the most productive institutional affiliation, ranking
first and successfully publishing 15 articles, followed by Universiti Putra Malaysia publishing 11
articles, Universiti Malaya publishing 8 articles, as well as the International Islamic University
Malaysia, Universitas Airlangga, and Universiti Sains Islam Malaysia, each of which published 7

were Indonesia, which ranked first with 124 articles, followed by Malaysia with 103, China with

From a geographic perspective, Table 5 reveals a highly concentrated research landscape.
Indonesia leads with 124 publications, followed by Malaysia with 103 documents. Together, these
two countries account for nearly 80% of the total output, reflecting their dominant role as
research hubs in halal certification studies. This concentration is consistent with their status as
major Muslim -majority nations with well -established halal certification systems. Notably,
however, the most productive institutions are predominantly Malaysian universities, with
Universiti Utara Malaysia (15 articles), Universiti Putra Malaysia (11 articles), and Universiti
Malaya (8 articles) leading the list. Indonesian institutions, such as Universitas Airlangga and
Institut Agama Islam Negeri Ponorogo, appear lower in the ranking.
This pattern reveals an important nuance: while Indonesia excels in publication quantity,
Malaysian institutions demonstrate stronger productivity and possibly greater success in
publishing in high -impact international journals. The limited contribution from non -Muslim
countries (e.g., China, Australia, Canada) and the relatively modest rate of international
collaboration (30.28%) suggest that halal certification research remains largely regionally focused
and has not yet fully reflected the global expansion of the halal market.
Overall, the bibliometric findings portray halal certification research as a maturing field
undergoing a transition from a primarily technical -regulatory approach toward a more
multidisciplinary and strategic perspective. Although the food sector and consumer behavior
continue to dominate, substantial research gaps persist in non -food sectors (such as
pharmaceuticals, fashion, and tourism), digital halal governance, and comparative cross -country
studies. Future research should address these gaps to support the development of a more
comprehensive and globally competitive halal certification ecosystem.

 Word analysis in bibliometric studies involves applying various methods and techniques
to understand and explore words that appear in scientific literature and academic publications.
The purpose of this analysis is to identify emerging themes that are becoming the main focus of
researchers, as well as patterns and interconnections among words within the examined text
corpus. Keywords refer to the main words or phrases in an article that are used to reveal the
structure of the text or the crucial elements contained in the article. (Sofyan et al., 2023).
The word cloud generated by the Biblioshiny application shows that “halal certification” is
the dominant keyword in the research landscape, as indicated by its largest visual size . This
indicates that halal certification has become the main focus and the center of academic discourse
in global halal studies . (Masood et al., 2022) . This dominance confirms that halal issues are
understood not only as a religious concept but also as a regulatory and strategic instrument for
ensuring consumer trust and product quality.
In addition, the keywords “halal,” “certification,” “halal food,” and “halal logo” appear
prominently, indicating a strong linkage among  certification, labeling, and food products in halal
research. (Tumiwa et al., 2023). The presence of these terms reflects researchers’ strong attention
to the role of halal labels and logos as informational signals that influence consumer perceptions,
trust, and decisions. (Desmaryani et al., 2024)
 The word cloud also shows the emergence of geographic keywords such as “Indonesia”
and “Malaysia,” indicating that both countries are  dominant empirical contexts in halal research .
(Hasan & Latif, 2024) . This can be linked to the strategic position of Indonesia and Malaysia as
countries with large Muslim populations and relatively well -established, institutionalized halal
certification systems.
 On the consumer behavior side, the terms “purchase intention,” “consumer behavior,”
“halal awareness,” and “halal knowledge” appear in medium size, indicating that halal research
widely adopts behavioral perspectives, particularly in explaining how consumer knowledge,
awareness, and attitudes influence the intention to purchase halal products . (Putera & Rakhel,
2023). The presence of the “theory of planned behavior ” concept confirms that the TPB
framework is a key theoretical foundation for explaining the psychological mechanisms
underlying halal consumption decision-making. (Perdana et al., 2019).
 Meanwhile, keywords such as “halal logistics,” “halal supply chain,” “halal tourism,”
“halal cosmetics,” and “business performance” appear in relatively smaller sizes, indicating that
these themes remain specific and have not yet become a mainstream stream of research .

 The second cluster (blue) highlights the words “halal label,” “food industry,” and “halal

CONCLUSION
 This study concludes that research on halal certification has become  an increasingly
important and expanding field within the global halal industry literature. The bibliometric
mapping reveals that scholarly attention to halal certification is strongly influenced by issues of
consumer trust, regulatory governance, supply chain integrity, and business competitiveness, with
research contributions largely concentrated in key Muslim -majority countries such as Indonesia
and Malaysia. The findings also show that a limited number of journals and productive authors
dominate the publication landscape. At the same time, thematic trends continue to evolve toward
broader discussions on halal markets, consumer behavior, and institutional frameworks. Overall,
this study provides an evidence -based overview of the intellectual structure and research
dynamics of halal certification, which can serve as a foundation for future academic development.
Therefore, further studies are suggested to expand the scope by incorporating additional
databases, exploring specific industrial sectors such as pharmaceuticals and halal tourism, and
applying more diverse methodological approaches, including qualitative and interdisciplinary
perspectives, to strengthen the understanding of digital governance, consumer protection, and
innovation in halal assurance systems.
