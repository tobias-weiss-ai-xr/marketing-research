ABSTRACT

Accurate estimation of body weight is crucial for effective feeding, breeding and marketing strategies in goat production.
While direct weighing methods are costly for smallholder farmers, linear body measurements offer a reliable and low -
cost alternative.  Despite extensive research on this topic, a bibliometric analysis of global publication trends and
research networks have not been conducted. A bibliometric analysis was conducted using data retrieved from Scopus
and Web of Science databases covering the period 2002-2024. After removing duplicates, 145 documents were analysed
using the Bibliometric R package and Biblioshiny. Key indicators including publication trends, authorship patterns,
institutional contributions and country collaborations, were examin ed. The study revealed irregular growth in
publications with an average annual growth rate of 4.66% and a low international co -author rate of 13.1%, indicating
limited collaboration. Tyasi T.L was identified as the most productive author, while the Univers ity of Limpopo and
Odisha University of Agriculture and Technology were leading institutions. India, South Africa and Ethiopia emerged
as the most active countries. Tropical Animal Health and Production and Small Ruminant Research were the main
publication sources. Research on predicting goat body weight from linear body measurements remains more scattered,
with limited international collaboration and slow growth over time. Strengthening international partnerships, employing
advanced analytical approaches s uch as machine learning and enhancing participation from underrepresented regions
could advance the field and improve application in livestock production.

Keywords: Goats, Body weight, Bibliometric analysis, Linear body measurements, Prediction.

INTRODUCTION

 Goat farming contributes positively to the global food
security and meet human nutritional needs, since they
produce milk and meat (Koluman 2023). These animals can
withstand harsh environmental condition and be able to
produce more effectively than other livestock (FAO 2017).
Accurate estimation of live body weight is crucial for
effective feeding strategies, health monitoring, breedi ng
programme plan and setting selling price ( Mokoena et al.
2022). However, the direct weighing scales are more
expensive and labour -intensive to smallholder farmers
(Dakhlan et al. 2021). This has led to the development of
an indirect technique to predict  the body weight of goats
from linear body measurements, which is less costly and
easily applied ( Ouchene-Khelifi and Ouchene 2021).
Several studies have been conducted using both traditional
regression models and advanced computational methods,
indicated that body length, withers height and height girth
were most reliable predictors of body weight in Beetal
goats (Iqbal 2013), heart girth and body length in Kacang
goats (Ilham et al. 2023 ), heart girth and paunch girth in
Creole goats ( Mahieu et al. 2011),  while Mathapo and
Tyasi (2021) applied Classification and Regression Trees
in Boer goats.
Despite the existence of many studies on the topic of
interest, the research remains scattered across breeds,
countries and journals, and current reviews fail to provide a
systematic overview of its trends and networks ( Aria and
Cuccurullo 2017). Therefore, bibliometric analysis provides
a systematic and quantitative approach to address the
mentioned gap by mapping scientific production, identifying
the most contributing authors and uncovering research
hotspots  (Aria and  Cuccurullo 2017; Donthu et al. 2021).

Bibliometric methods help to discover which authors,
documents or sources have had a major i mpact on the
academic community  and collaboration patterns ( Moral-
Muñoz et al. 2020). In the condition of predicting body
weight from li near body measurements, bibliometric
analysis can provide a combined view of how the research
has evolved in the topic and which institutions lead to
scholarly contributions. Based on our knowledge, there is
no bibliometric study on predicting body weight using
linear body measurements. Therefore, the study aimed to
provide a comprehensive bibliometric analysis of research
trends on the prediction of body weight from linear body
measurements of goats.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Data collection
 This study used  Scopus and Web of Science (WoS)
databases to retrieve scientific literature for a detailed
bibliometric analysis.  The two databases were used to
extract scientific studies related to “Prediction of Goat
Body Weight from Linear Body Measurements”. The
search covered publications from 2002 to 2024.  A total
of 208 scientific documents (82 from WoS and 126 from
Scopus) were retrieved from January 2025 to December
2025. After removing 63 duplicates, 145 documents were
included for analysis. The search was guide d by criteria
and different combinations of search terms. The criteria
included publication types (research articles, article
review, book chapters, book chapter review and
conference proceeding papers), the language (English)
and publication year (2002 – 2024). Conference
proceedings and reviews were included to cover all
contributions to the title of interest. The study used
search strategy that included key terms such as (("Capra
hircus" OR goat OR caprine OR "small ruminant") AND
("body weight" OR "live  weight" OR "body mass" OR
biomass OR "live body weight") AND (predict OR
estimate OR assess OR determine OR model OR forecast
OR project) AND ("linear body measurement" OR
morphometric OR zoometric OR "body dimension" OR
"body size trait" OR "skeletal mea surement" OR
"external measurement" OR "biometric parameter" OR
"conformation trait")). Fig. 1 presents flow diagram
showing the selection process for studies related to the
topic “Prediction of body weight from linear body
measurements of goats” .

Data analysis
RStudio software with the “bibliometrix R package
software version 5.2.1” and Biblioshiny software were
used to analyse combined data from both Web of Science
(WoS) and Scopus.

RESULTS

 A total of 145 documents retrieved from Scopus and
Web of Science were analyzed to examine global research
trends on predicting goat body weight from linear body
measurements. The results are presented in terms of
publication trends, citation patterns, authorship
characteristics, institutional contributions, coun try
productivity, collaboration networks  and leading
publication sources.
 Table 1 shows the summary of the information
retrieved from both WoS and Scopus search databases. The
study shows that 145 scientific documents were retrieved
with an international co-authorship rate and single -
authored published documents with values of 13.1% and 9,
respectively. The average annual growth rate of the
published papers was 4.66%, with an average of 8.945
citations per document.

DISCUSSION

 Goats play a crucial part in agriculture by contributing
to the livelihoods in terms of meat and milk ( Khaskheli et
al. 2020; Idamokoro 2023). Bibliometric analysis is an
effective tool that uses quantitative methods to describe
research output and uncover patterns of scholarly activi ty
within a defined research field (Idamokoro and Nib 2024).
This bibliometric study analysed literature on the
prediction of body weight from linear body measurements
of goats using data from Web of Science (WoS) and Scopus
databases. From a total of 145 scientific documents, it was
revealed that international collaboration in this field of
interest is still low, with a co-authorship rate of 13.1%. This
shows lack of research network in the field of study. The
results disagree with the trends occurring in related
livestock research area, where collaboration is associated
with higher methodological innovation and citation impact
(Kirchner et al. 2017 ; Jiang et al. 2023). Studies on
genomic selection and precision livestock farming showed
a highly significant level of collaboration, higher than 44 to
60% which has supported the incorporation of advanced
statistical and machine learning techniques ( Chafai et al.
2023; Panigrahi et al. 2025). It further revealed that nine
articles were published by single author s. The
determination of single author publications strengthened
the limited collaboration in the field of study, restricting the
adoption of modern predictive approaches such as artificial
intelligence and computer vision ( Hajkowica et al. 2023;
Bitzenis et al. 2025).

 The average annual growth rate of publications was
4.66%, which revealed that the number of studies on
morphometric prediction were not growing well over time,
as broader livestock productivity and precision agriculture
research reported in recent years ( Banerjee et al. 2021).
However, the average of 8.95 citations per publication
revealed that these studies received a moderate attention
from other researchers. This remains lower than the citation
metrics reported in other comparable studies in the field of
animal science. Animal breeding and production frequently
report higher average citations due to stronger
combinations of with global research networks and
promising technologies (Celik 2024). This variation shows
that research project on prediction of body weigh t from
linear body measurements is still recognized as the niche,
even though is regarded for communal farming system.
 The findings revealed that number of publications
varied from year to year, and there was a constant

production of articles between the year 2002 and 2004. A
decrease was observed between 2004 and 2009, followed
by a gradual increase from 2010 to 2022. An increase in
publications was observed in 2024. The decline in period
concurs with increased emphasis on molecular genetics and
genomics which may have domin ated morphometric
studies (Haye et al. 2020). The gradual increase from 2010
and an increase in 2024 shows renewal of interest in low -
cost, field -applicable tools as well as the combination of
data science methods such as regression mod elling,
machine learning and digital phenotyping ( de Oliveira
Costa et al. 2020; Oyebanjo et al. 2023; Karna et al. 2024).
Similar trends have been observed in other livestock
studies (Sheep and cattle), where traditional measurements
are increasingly comb ined with predictive analytics
(Michelena et al. 2024).
 The citation pattern revealed variations in years and
further suggest that the field lacks constant high impact
papers that consistently drive research momentum. This
disagrees with more mature field s, where foundational
studies continue to attract citations over extended periods
(Idamokoro and Nib 2024). The citation peaks observed in
specific years may reflect the effect of isolated high impact
publications, rather than a reliable and developing research
agenda. The authorship and institutional patterns showed
that research output is concentrated among limited number
of scholars and institutions such as Odisha University of
Agriculture and Technology and the University of
Limpopo. This concentration may show centres of
expertise and also raises concerns about inadequate
geographic and intellectual diversity. Comparable studies
in livestock production have indicated that broader
institutional participation improves innovation and
contextual suitability. The dominance of countries such as
India, South Africa and Ethiopia aligns with socio -
economic importance of goats in these regions, but the
relatively low contribution from developed countries may
limit the incorporation of advanced technologies and
funding opportunities ( Day et al. 2025; Ngcobo et al.
2026).
 The low level of international collaboration observed
in the study disagree with the results from other
bibliometric analysis studies. Idamokoro and Nib (2024),
reported higher growth rate (8.53%) and wide global
participation in goat tick research, while Tyasi et al. (2024)
showed extensive publication output and stronger citation
networks in goat breeding. Similarly, global analyses in
dairy science and small ruminant production showed an
increase in cross country partnerships as a driver of
research quality and impact ( Bebe et al. 2021; Day et al.
2025). The comparatively weaker collaboration in
morphometric prediction studies suggest an urgent need for
more combination, cross-disciplinary research efforts.
 Journal issue patterns further strengthened the applied
nature of this research area, with most publications
showing in specialized outlets such as Tropical Animal
Health and Production and Small Ruminant Research. This
concentration may reduce visibility in broader high impact
journals that attract interdisciplinary audiences. Studies
indicated that publishing across different journal platforms
enhances spreading of knowledge and citation impact
(Majhi et al. 2023).
Conclusion
In conclusion, research on predicting goat body
weight from linear body measurements has grown slowly
and remains fragmented, with limited international
collaboration and concentration in a few countries and
institutions. While the field provides valuable low -cost
solutions for smallholder livestock systems, its
development is constrained by weak global research
integration. Strengthening international collaboration,
adopting advanced analytical approaches such as machine
learning, and increasing participation from
underrepresented regions are essential to enhance both
scientific impact and practical applicability of this
research area.
