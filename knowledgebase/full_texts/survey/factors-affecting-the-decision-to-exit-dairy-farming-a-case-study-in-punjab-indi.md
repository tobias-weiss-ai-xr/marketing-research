Abstract
Punjab is one of the leading dairy state in India providing subsistence to landless, marginal and small
farmers. Punjab is the land for 2.16 percent of the country's total bovine population, producing 14.07 MT
(6.4 percent) of milk annually (BAHS, 2022). F eed prices have increased by 14.1 percent over the last
three years, therefore, farmers' returns are not compensating as feed and fodder accounts for more than
70% of the cost at dairy farm. As a result, the dairy industry is having numerous difficulties due to farmers
quitting the business. In order to identify reasons for closure of dairy farms in Punjab, survey was
conducted at the Department of Dairy Economics and Business Management, GADVASU, Ludhiana.
Simple random sampling technique was applied, and a sample size of 33 farmers, who exited from dairy
farming in Punjab, was selected for the primary study. Chi square test, ANOVA and Factor analysis was
used to identify the main reasons which lead to the exit of farmers from dairy enterprise. The study
revealed that high concentrate feed prices and high dry fodder prices were among the main feeding related
reasons for the closure of dairy farms in the state. Other reasons include, high cost of labour
(management/production), high veterinary expenses, disea se incidence (health), less price of milk, less
sale price of animals and adulteration (marketing). Broader focus on dairy support programs would help
reduce the number of farmers exiting dairy enterprise.

Keywords: Closure of farms, dairy farming, garret’s ranking, loss incurred and milk price

Introduction
Dairying has been one of the major allied sectors of agriculture in Punjab since time
immemorial. It plays an important role in agricultural sector as its evident from contribution
to the total agricultural GDP in India and Punjab as 30.2 per cent and 38.2 per cent, respectively
(BAHS, 2023 and Statistical Abstract of Punjab, 2022). The enterprise as a source of livelihood
has been supplementing income to small, marginal and landless farmers. Punjab is the home
of 2.15% of the total bovine population of the country wherein buffaloes and cattle comprises
of 3.7% and 1.3% respectively. The emergence of dairy farming as a profitable enterprise has
been attributed to the increase in the population of high yielding exotic cattle in Punjab by
15.30% and also the d ecline in low-yielding indigenous and non -descript cattle by 46.9% as
recorded from census 1997 to 2019. At present, the total exotic and indigenous cattle
population is 2.11 million and 0.43 million respectively whereas buffalo population is 4.02
million which altogether produces 14.3 million tonnes of milk in Punjab (i.e. 6.2% of the total
milk production in the country). Buffaloes constitute the major share of total milk produced in
the state with 8.5 MT (59.4%) followed by crossbred cattle 4.06 MT (32.2 %) and goats with
only 0.09 MT (0.6%). Punjab ranks 7 th in milk production in the country after Uttar Pradesh
(36.2 MT), Rajasthan (33.3 MT), Madhya Pradesh (20.1 MT), Gujarat (17.2 MT), Andhra
Pradesh (15.4 MT) and Maharashtra (15 MT) (BAHS, 2023). Apart from high yielding genetic
selection of bovine population, the profitability of the dairy enterprise is also due to the higher
milk yield of species more than the national average. For instance, crossbred cattle milk yield
in Punjab is 12.83 kg/day/animal followed by buffalo (9.92 kg/day/animal) and indigenous
cattle (9.15 kg/day/animal) respectively whereas milk yield of crossbred cattle, buffalo and
indigenous cattle in India is 8.41, 6.76 and 4.17 kg/day/animal respectively (BAHS, 2023) as
shown in Figure 1.

animals), small & medium dairy farmers (3-15 animals), large/commercial dairy farms (15 to

Review of literature
Under certain assumptions, particularly, perfect knowledge
and the absence of exit barriers, the perfectly competitive
market model specifies the exit or shut-down decision rule as
being based upon the comparison of a product's market price
to an individual producer's short-run average variable costs.
Despite of this, dairy producers may not adhere to the strictly
defined economic shut-down rule due to the nature of capital
used, biological lags between breeding and milking, and
incomplete information, among other things, regarding future
milk prices (Bragg & Dalton,2004) [2]. Although total bovine
population in Punjab state has increased over the years, but
the year 2021-22 saw many milk producers in Punjab quitting
from the milk supply chain. The causes of these recent shifts
have not been thoroughly investigated for the Punjab region
in India. However, some review of literature has been useful
to cite major reasons for these economic and social changes.
(Boehlje, 1992) [1] Contends that the following five categories
of factors technology, human capital, finances, institutions,
and sociology have an impact on shifts in the organization of
agriculture. Using Boehlke’s life-cycle model, Gale (1994) [7]
discovered evidence that the likelihood of an exit decision
resembles the business cycle of a company i.e., exit is more
likely during the consolidation phase rather than during
startup or maturity. Goetz and Dibertin (2001)  [10] studied
reasons to cease farming by the U.S. farmers, with a particular
emphasis on understanding the roles of off-farm employment
and federal farm program payments and found that the net
number of farmers quitting between 1987 and 1997 is not
statistically affected by off-farm employment, ceteris paribus.
However, when the transaction costs connected with
transitioning to full -time off -farm employment decrease,
farmers leave the workforce at quicker rates. In addition,
farmers leave the industry more quickly in cases where their
land and structures are worth more, they irrigate less of their
property, they get larger government program payouts,
provided, they live in counties with high population densities
or close to a city. In contrast to this, if a farmer owns their
own farm, th eir rate of resignation is lower. (Gloy  et al .,
2002) [9] Examined the links between technology, finances,
human resource issues, and profitability at New York dairy
farms. The variables that determined the production factor
were the number of cows, production per cow, and milking
system; the profitability variable was determined by return on
assets and each of the production element was found to have
a substantial impact on the return on assets ratio using
regression analysis. (Fotlz, 2004) estimated the f actors
behind farm exits and variations in herd -size among dairy
farms under the New England Dairy Compact and found that
the pricing strategy of the Dairy Compact decreased farm
exits while substantially increasing the number of cows
whereas farm exits surged due to development pressures and
historically low unemployment rates. Furthermore, some of
the literature emphasizes the close relation between social
issues and their decision to exit dairy farming as cited by
(CBS, 2017; Zeugenhouderij, 2016; Silvas ti et al ., 2014;
Geerling-Eiff & Van der Meulen, 2008; Schnabel, 2001)  [8].
(CBS, 2017) [19, 20, 21] studied the realtion between succession
rate of farmers and their decision to exit dairy farming and
found that 60% of farmers aged 55 and older had a successor,
small dairy farms with less than 50 cows, had only a 28%
level of succession whereas in larger dairy farms, with 1 00
cows or more, 85% of the older farmers has a successor (CBS,
2017). (Booijink, 2018)  [18] also identified social and
economic dimensions by studying the reasons for closing
dairy farms in the Netherlands. Major reasons are the physical
issues including the influence of laws and regulations,
financial reasons, or the lack of a successor.

Collection of data
The questionnaire was designed to collect the data from
respondent sample households. The data comprised of total
capital investments, farm size, net Profit earned during
operation period of dairy farm, income generated after
closure of dairy farm, total l oss incurred by the farmer after
closure of dairy farm, price of milk, marketing channel
followed, etc. was collected from the respondent dairy
farmers by personal interview method using a specially
designed and pre -tested questionnaire. The required
information regarding various reasons which led to closure of
the farms were categorized in four domains viz., feeding,
management/production, health and marketing.
Statistical tools of analysis:
Chi square test was used to test the association between size
categories and various socio -economic and other general
profile parameters using the following formula:

χ2= ∑ [(O-E) 2/E]

Wherein, the summation is taken over all the k classes. O
represents observed data and E represents expected data
respectively.
The simultaneous influence of several factors on the farm size
categories was determined using two -way Analysis of
Variance. This implies the testing of relationships between
size-categories and their economic variables namely; average
herd size, land owne rship, initial capital investment, capital
generated on closure of dairy farms, by 2 -way ANOVA.
Further, Factor analysis (Principal Component Analysis) was
also performed using SPSS 16.0 computer standard statistical
software package to explain the pattern of correlations within
various categories of reasons (namely; feeding, production,
health and marketing) that influenced the decision to exit
dairy farming in Punjab.

rising over the time at the rate of 14% in last three years (i.e.
from Rs.2209.8/Qtl in April, 2020 to Rs.2520.4/Qtl in
February, 2023), milk procurement price paid to farmers by
cooperatives has only increased by 8.1% on average in last 5
years. Therefore, the rise in feed and fodder costs is not
justifying the milk procurement price paid to the farmers in
Punjab. In order to lower the gap  between total cost and
returns, it is important to make the dairy farming viable by
lowing the variable cost per month.

Major reasons identified for exiting dairy farming in

In decision to quit or to keep dairy farming, farmers usually
compare the utility they expect to derive from continuing to
farm with the utility derived from exiting the dairy farming
and becoming either fully employed by off -farm activities,
relocate or r etire. Consumption levels determine utility,
which is based on the revenue or returns on labor (and capital)
invested per unit of time in off -farm employment or
agriculture (Goetz & Debertin, 2001) [10]. However, there are

Conclusion
The closure of dairy farms in the Punjab state marks a
significant shift in the agricultural landscape of the region.
While this decision is influenced by various factors such as
economic viability, marketing patterns, feeding and
production related concer ns, its consequences ripple across
multiple dimensions. Firstly, the closure of dairy farms
impacted the livelihoods of countless individuals who depend
on them for employment and income generation as dairying
was found to be the primary occupation of around 9% of the
selected dairy farmers. Farmers, laborers, and associated
industries face uncertainty and potential economic hardship.
Secondly, there are potential repercussions for the dairy
industry itself, including disruptions to the local supply chain,
reduced availability of dairy products, and increased milk
adulteration, difficulty in maintaining self -sufficiency to
meet demand of milk and milk products. Moreover, it's
crucial to consider the social and cultural implications of such
closures. The clos ure of dairy farms in Punjab reflects the
complex interplay of economic, environmental, and societal
factors; it also underscores the need for comprehensive
strategies that balance economic development with
environmental sustainability and social equity. Any transition
must prioritize the well -being of affected individuals and
communities while striving for a more resilient and
sustainable agricultural sector.
