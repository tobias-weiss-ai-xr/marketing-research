Abstract
 Although increasing interest with respect to green products, little is known about how
innovation affects perceived value, satisfaction, and behavioural intention within niche green
product markets. This gap is particularly evident in the terrarium sector, where empirical evidence
remains scarce, especially in the Thai context. To address this gap, this empirical study, which
focused on the interrelation among innovation, perceived value, satisfaction, and behavioural
intention determinants was carried out in Thailand’s terrarium market.
 A quantitative approach using an online survey was conducted with 400 sample partici -
pants. The study implemented a probability sampling method applying systematic random sam-
pling to select the participants. A path analysis with latent variables employed structural equation
modelling (SEM) to analyze the data via AMOS version 24.
	 The	 findings	indicate	that	perceived	value	is	the	most	influential	factor	significantly	af-
fecting customers' behavioural intention. Among its dimensions, perceived emotional value and
perceived	 functional	 value	 emerged	 as	 the	 strongest	 indicators.	 Innovation	 was	 identified	 as
the	second	most	influential	factor,	with	marketing,	service,	and	cultural	innovation	serving	as	its
strongest	indicators.	Satisfaction	ranked	as	the	third	most	influential	factor,	with	meaningfulness
and	affordability	identified	as	its	most	important	dimensions.	All	hypothesised	relationships	were
statistically	significant	at	the	0.05	level.	The	standardized	total	effects	showed	that	perceived	value
had	the	strongest	influence	on	customers'	behavioural	intention	(β = .634), followed by innovation
(β = .513) and satisfaction (β = .195).
	 These	findings	provide	empirical	evidence	and	contextual	insights	into	Thailand's	terrarium
market. From a practical perspective, businesses should adopt an integrated strategy that com -
bines innovation, value creation, and customer satisfaction enhancement to strengthen market
demand for terrarium products.

Introduction
 In the current digital social era, tech -
nology is increasingly playing a major role in
Thai society. In particular, people who live in
cities or urban areas participate in many activi-
ties and pursue a range of interests, and there
can be a vast difference of opinion compared
to people who live in suburban areas. The
selection trend for urban people looking to
purchase accommodation shows a preference
for apartments or condominiums rather than
detached houses. Nevertheless, there is a limit
to the amount of land which can be used for
building condominium, and therefore with
space at a premium there are no garden ar -
eas for planting large trees. However, balcony
garden area could be provided to allow small
gardens in each room, as well as miniature
parks which could be available between con -
dominium buildings.
 Bangkok is the capital city of Thailand
and is the most economically prosperous city
in the country. The population density is high
and the city faces various environmental prob-
lems	such	as	heavy	traffic,	noise	pollution,	air
pollution, water pollution, toxic waste, and
e-waste problems. Thus, the government and
private sector have devoted their efforts to
promoting green space creation for urban areas
in order to mitigate the levels of toxic pollu -
tion.
 Hence, people who live in condomini-
ums are expected to show an increasing trend
towards green space creation in their accom -
modation by planting trees and gardening in
mini green spaces. Furthermore, the overall
market value of the ornamental plant market
and tree business indicates high growth. In par-
ticular, ornamental plant cultivation for export
reveals the overall market value equal to 3.5-4
billion baht per year (ARDA, 2024). Additionally,
data reports of registered residences in Bang -
kok and surrounding areas in the past 10 years
(2013-2022)	have	identified	that	the	numbers
of registered condominium residences have
greatly exceeded the numbers of housing
estates and built-up house residences (Open
Government Data, 2024). Thus, entrepreneurs
can see the opportunities to enter the market
in the terrarium industry with the target market
of people who live in condominiums.
 The terrarium market was selected
because it represents an emerging, innova -
tion-driven niche within green products, char -
acterized by strong aesthetic and emotional
value. Terrarium market yet remains underex -
plored in consumer behaviour research, espe-
cially in Thai context.
 Therefore, empirical evidence with
respect to green products, particularly in re -
search within the terrarium sector appears to
be	rare	to	find	it.	Thus,	little	is	known	and	un-
derstood insight about the role of innovation
influences	 perceived	 value,	 satisfaction,	 and
behavioural intention within the terrarium mar-
ket.	To	fill	this	gap,	researcher	investigated	this
empirical study, which concentrated on the
interrelationship among innovation, perceived
value, satisfaction, and behavioural intention
factors in Thailand’s terrarium market.
 Innovation, particular in green innova -
tion, appears increasingly to be important for
academia and has been the focus in recent
years (Chen, et al., 2021, p. 2). Past studies

Literature Review
Innovation
 Innovation refers to the development
of new ideas, knowledge, technologies, or
practices	 that	 generate	 value	 for	 firms	 and
enhance their competitiveness rather than
merely introducing something new (Varadara -
jan, 2024, pp. 704–706). In this study, innova -
tion is conceptualized as a multidimensional
construct comprising six dimensions: product
innovation, technological innovation, image
innovation, service innovation, marketing
innovation, and cultural innovation. The di -
mensions were synthesized from past studies
to represent the innovation construct in the
study (Chen, et al., 2021, pp. 7–8; Mutonyi, et
al., 2022, p. 2; Tomczak, 2022, pp. 118–122).
Perceived Value
	 Perceived 	 value	 reflects 	 customers'
assessment	 of	 the	 benefits	 obtained	 from	 a
product or service in relation to the costs or
sacrifices	 incurred	 during	 the	 consumption
process	 (Nafisyah	 and	 Marsasi,	 2023,	 p.	 764).

Rather than representing a single dimension,
perceived value encompasses multiple as -
pects of evaluations. The study conceptualiz -
es perceived value through four dimensions:
perceived functional value, perceived emo -
tional value, perceived monetary value, and
perceived social value. These dimensions were
synthesized from past studies for perceived
value criteria (Chen and Chen, 2010, p. 30;
Huiming and Yuning, 2020, p. 2).
Customer Satisfaction
	 Customer	 satisfaction	 reflects	 cus-
tomers' evaluation of their consumption
experience (Yesitadewi and Widodo, 2024, p.
419). This evaluation represents customers'
post-consumption judgment regarding wheth -
er	the	product	has	fulfilled	or	exceeded	their
expectations. Satisfaction is conceptualized as
a multidimensional construct through four di -
mensions: novelty, meaningfulness, complex -
ity, and affordability. These dimensions were
developed from Truong, et al. (2020, p. 9).
Customers’ Behavioural Intention
 Customers' behavioural intention
reflects	 their	 willingness	 to	 engage	 in	 future
purchasing behaviours and is widely regarded
as a reliable predictor of actual purchasing
behaviour.	Actual	purchase	is	difficult	to	ob-
serve directly, behavioural intention has been
extensively adopted as an alternative measure
in consumer behaviour research (Bosnjak,
Ajzen and Schmidt, 2020, p. 353). Intention
is conceptualized through three criteria: trial,
repurchase, and word-of-mouth from previous
studies (Chen and Chen, 2010, p. 31; Huiming
and Yuning, 2020, p. 2; Jaleel, et al., 2021, p.
1847).
Related Past Studies
Influence of the Innovation Determinant
 Past studies have recognized innova -
tion as a key driver of customer satisfaction,
perceived value, and behavioural intention. In-
novation	enables	firms	to	introduce	new	prod-
ucts, services, technologies, and marketing
practices that enhance customers' experiences
and strengthen their competitive advantage.
Thus,	firms	with	stronger	innovation	capabili-
ties are more likely to create superior customer
value and foster favourable behavioural re -
sponses.
 Empirical evidence supports the posi -
tive impact of innovation on customer satisfac-
tion. Nwachukwu and Vu (2022, pp. 7–10) and
Naveed, Akhtar and Cheema (2012, pp. 1–10)
reported	that	innovation	significantly	enhances
satisfaction by improving customers' overall
service and consumption experiences. How -
ever, more recent evidence indicates that this
relationship may not always be direct. Yeboah
and Amponsah (2025, p. 8) found that service
innovation	did	not	significantly	influence	cus-
tomer satisfaction, suggesting that innovation
alone	 may	 be	 insufficient	 to	 improve	 satis-
faction unless it is supported by high-quality
service delivery.
 Similarly, previous research has demon-
strated that innovation positively contributes
to customers' perceived value and behavioural
intention. Chen, et al. (2021, pp. 1–17) assert -
ed	 that	 innovation	 had	 significant	 positive
direct effects on both perceived value and
behavioural intention. Nevertheless, Hwang,
Lee and Cho (2025, pp. 31–46) argued that the
influence	of	innovation	on	behavioural	inten-

Influence of Perceived Value
 Perceived value has been widely
recognized as a basic factor of customer satis-
faction and behavioural intention in consumer
behaviour research. Customers who perceive
greater value from a product or service are
more likely to develop favourable evaluations,
resulting in higher satisfaction and stronger
intentions to engage in future purchasing be -
haviours. Thus, perceived value has frequently
been incorporated into relationship models
as a key antecedent of customer responses
(Jaleel, et al., 2021, p. 1846; Chen and Chen,
2010, p. 30).
 Previous empirical studies consistently
reported	that	perceived	value	exerts	a	signifi-
cant positive direct effect on both customer
satisfaction and behavioural intention. Jaleel,
et al. (2021, pp. 1845–1852) demonstrated that
perceived	value	positively	influenced	custom-
er satisfaction and behavioural intention; how-
ever, they found that customer satisfaction
did	not	significantly	mediate	the	relationship
between perceived value and behavioural
intention. In contrast, Chen and Chen (2010,
pp. 32–33) asserted that perceived value not
only had direct positive effects on customer
satisfaction and behavioural intention but also
indirectly	 influenced	 behavioural	 intention
through customer satisfaction.
 Although prior studies consistently
support the direct effects of perceived value
on customer outcomes, the mediating role of
satisfaction remains inconclusive. These incon-
sistent	findings	suggest	that	further	research	is
required to clarify how perceived value con -
tributes to customers' behavioural intention
through satisfaction across different research
contexts.
Influence of Customer Satisfaction
 Customer satisfaction has long been
recognized	 as	 one	 of	 the	 most	 influential
factors of customers' behavioural intention.
Satisfied	customers	are	more	likely	to	maintain
long-term	relationships	with	firms,	repurchase
products or services, and engage in favourable
word-of-mouth communication. Hence, sat -
isfaction has been widely regarded as a key
mechanism linking customers' consumption
experiences with their future behavioural re -
sponses (Chen and Chen, 2010, p. 32; Huiming
and Yuning, 2020, p. 5; Jaleel, et al., 2021, p.
1852).
 Empirical evidence asserts the pos -
itive	 influence	 of	 satisfaction	 on	 intention.
Singh and Söderlund (2020, p. 12) indicated
that	 higher	 levels	 of	 satisfaction	 significantly
increased repurchase intention and willingness
to recommend products to others. Similarly,
Chen and Chen (2010, p. 32), Huiming and Yun-
ing (2020, p. 5), and Jaleel, et al. (2021, p. 1852)
found that satisfaction positively and directly
influenced	 intention	 across	 different	 service
contexts.

Methods
Population and Sample
 Thai nationals and people who are not
of Thai nationality who live in the Bangkok area
were population for the research. The samples
were Thai nationals and people who are not
of Thai nationality, both male and female,
aged from 18 to 60 living in condominiums
that were located in Bangkok. Bangkok was
chosen due to its prosperity and abundance of
condominiums. Schumacker and Lomax (2016,
p. 39) advised that the sample size of 400 par-
ticipants	was	deemed	to	be	sufficient	for	data
analysis employing SEM. Taking on board this
consideration, the size of the sample equal
to 400 participants for this study was deemed
sufficient.
Data Collection
 A probability sampling method based
on systematic random sampling was conduct-
ed to recruit the sample respondents. Starting
from considering the various channels of on -
line platforms (i.e., email, Facebook, LINE), a
random method was then used to select the
first	 participant	 for	 each	 platform.	 The	 next
participant would be randomly selected in the
subsequent every k unit (assigned to k=3) and
the link or QR code of the questionnaire was
then sent to the participants via those online
platforms. Researcher decided to choose the
probability sampling method because it would
help to reduce selection bias, increase external
validity, support statistical inference, and make
the SEM results more reliable.
 The data collection period was held
during July – October, 2024. The questionnaire
provided a tick box for participants to give their
consent before participating in the survey. All
data gathered were anonymous and treated in
the	 strictest	 confidence.	 Data	 collection	 was
discontinued after receiving 400 completed
questionnaires which met the target sample
size (from 500 distributed questionnaires). The
response rate was therefore equal to 80%.
Validity and Reliability Measurement
 The online questionnaire included
closed-ended questions for evaluating in -
novation, perceived value, satisfaction, and
behavioural intention by using a Likert scale
from 1 to 5 for measurement. Content validity
analysis using the Item-Objective Congruence
Index (IOC) was performed to increase internal
validity. A pre-test was then carried out with
30	 consumers	 to	 confirm	 the	 quality	 of	 the
research tool. The study found Cronbach’s
alpha values indicate to be greater than 0.70
[0.950 for overall innovation, 0.929 for overall
perceived value, 0.914 for overall satisfaction,
and 0.882 for overall behavioural intention
(p < 0.05)]. These four latent variables were
deemed reliable and suitable to collect data
(Bryman and Bell, 2015, pp. 1-778).
Common Method Bias Diagnostic
 The assessment of common method
bias was conducted by using the Harman’s
single-factor test. The results revealed that the
first	unrotated	factor	accounted	for	39.394%	of
the total variance, which is below the thresh -
old of 50%. The results suggest that common
method bias is unlikely to be a serious concern
(Baumgartner and Weijters, 2021, pp. 7-22).

Hypothesis Testing
 To test the study’s hypotheses, the
researcher conducted SEM. The target sample
was 400 participants, which was considered
sufficient	as	this	is	more	than	the	200	respon-
dents considered essential by Hair, et al. (2019,
pp. 1-813). Hence, the data distribution can
be assumed to be normal. Based on these as-
sumptions, this study can employ SEM for data
analysis.
 Nevertheless, this study would not
employ SEM based on the assumption of
normal theory, which assessment used the
significance	test	(p-value)	provided	by	AMOS.
The study would instead conduct the boot -
strap	confidence	intervals	method	to	analyze
the data. The bootstrap method would help
to improve the reliability and accuracy of the
findings.
	 The	 Pearson’s	 correlation	 coefficient
analysis of seventeen observed variables found
that	the	correlation	coefficient	values	ranged
from 0.124-0.799, with all outcomes less than
0.8. Every pair of variables was statistically sig-
nificant	(p	<	0.05).	These	outcomes	were	also
evaluated to determine the presence or oth -
erwise of multicollinearity (Shrestha, 2020, pp.
39-42). Thus, the 17 observed determinants
were considered to be appropriate to incorpo-
rate in SEM.

Theoretical Implications (New Knowledge)
 This study contributes to the academ-
ic literature in three important ways. First, it
extends the innovation literature by concep -
tualizing innovation as a multidimensional
construct comprising product, technological,
image, service, marketing, and cultural inno -
vation. Unlike previous studies that have pri -
marily focused on service and technological
innovation, this study incorporates cultural
innovation as a key dimension of innovation.
Given that cultural innovation has received
relatively limited attention in previous re -
search,	the	findings	extend	existing	knowledge
by demonstrating its relevance in explaining
customers’ value perceptions and behavioural
responses in the terrarium market. Further -
more,	the	findings	highlight	the	potential	role
of cultural innovation in creating social value
and	 strengthening	 firms’	 competitive	 advan-
tage through culturally meaningful and cus -
tomer-oriented innovation.
 Second, this study advances the un -
derstanding of the mechanism through which
innovation	influences	customers’	behavioural
intention.	The	findings	indicate	that	innovation
indirectly	 influences	 behavioural	 intention
through perceived value, while the serial me -
diation through perceived value and customer
satisfaction provides a more comprehensive
explanation of how innovation is translated
into favourable behavioural responses. These
findings	 suggest	 that	 perceived	 value	 serves
as the primary mechanism linking innovation
with behavioural intention, whereas customer
satisfaction further reinforces this relationship.
	 Third, 	 the	 findings	 provide	 additional
theoretical support for both Cultural Identity
Theory and Value Congruence Theory. The
importance of cultural innovation suggests
that innovation aligned with customers’ cul -
tural values and identities can strengthen
customers’ value perceptions. Moreover, the
significant	 mediating	 role	 of	 perceived	 value
supports the proposition of Value Congruence
Theory that value is created when organisa -
tional initiatives are consistent with customers’
expectations and shared values. Consequent -
ly, this study enriches the application of these
theories within the emerging context of the ter-
rarium market and provides empirical evidence
for future research on innovation, perceived
value, customer satisfaction, and behavioural
intention.

 Latent variable innovation. Re-
garding	the	first	research	objective,	this	study
examined	 whether	 innovation	 influences
customers' behavioural intention to purchase
terrarium	products.	The	findings	indicate	that
innovation	 does	 not	 have	 a	 significant	 direct
effect on behavioural intention, although it
exerts	an	indirect	influence	through	other	vari-
ables.	This	finding	is	inconsistent	with	Chen,	et
al. (2021, pp. 1–17), who reported a positive
direct relationship between green innovation
and behavioural intention. However, it is con -
sistent with Hwang, Lee and Cho (2025, pp.
31–46), who suggested that customers devel -
op purchase intention only after perceiving the

	 The	 findings	 indicate	 that	 innovation
has a positive direct effect on perceived value,
which	is	consistent	with	the	findings	reported
by Chen, et al. (2021, p. 5). This suggests that
innovation enhances customers' evaluations
of the value offered by terrarium products.
For example, Innovations in product design,
technology, and sustainability may strengthen
customers' value perceptions. Nevertheless,
innovation alone was not found to directly
enhance customer satisfaction.
	 The	 findings	 indicate	 that	 innovation
indirectly	 influences	 behavioural	 intention
through perceived value as well as through the
sequential mediation of perceived value and
customer satisfaction, suggesting that innova -
tion strengthens purchase intention by creating
greater customer value.
 The measurement model further re -
vealed that service, marketing, and cultural
innovation were the three strongest indicators
of the innovation construct, with factor load -
ings of .75, .75, and .74, respectively. Therefore,
businesses should prioritise these innovation
dimensions to strengthen customers' per -
ceived value.
 From a managerial perspective, busi -
nesses should integrate cultural, marketing,
and service innovation to strengthen custom -
ers' perceived value. This may be achieved
through cultural storytelling, stakeholder
co-creation, environmentally responsible
practices, and green service initiatives, thereby
enhancing both customer value and broader
social	benefits.
 Latent variable perceived value.
Regarding the second research objective,
perceived value emerged as the strongest de-

to	 purchase	 terrarium	 products.	 The	 findings

32). In addition, perceived value was found to
positively	influence	customer	satisfaction,	sup-
porting previous evidence reported by Chen

pp. 1851–1852).
	 The	 findings	further	indicate	that	per-

intention through customer satisfaction. This
result supports the mediation effect reported
by Chen and Chen (2010, p. 32), although it
differs	from	the	findings	of	Jaleel,	et	al.	(2021,
pp.	1851–1852),	who	did	not	observe	a	signifi-
cant indirect effect of perceived value through
satisfaction.
	 The	 significant	 indirect	 effect	 of	 per-
ceived	value	suggests	that	firms	can	strengthen
customers' purchase intention by enhancing
customer satisfaction. Accordingly, business -
es should focus on delivering greater value
through product quality, product design, and
customer experience to reinforce both satis -
faction and purchase intention.
	 The	 findings	 further	 reveal	 that	 per-
ceived emotional value is the strongest indi -
cator of the perceived value construct (factor
loading = .77), followed closely by perceived
functional value (factor loading = .76). Accord-
ingly, businesses should focus on enhancing
both the emotional and functional value of

 Latent variable satisfaction.  Re -

purchase	 terrarium	 products.	 This	 finding	 is

leads to higher customer satisfaction. There -

enhance perceived value in order to strength-
en both customer satisfaction and purchase
intention.
 Meaningfulness and affordability were

terrarium products while communicating their
affordability and long-term value. Such efforts

and, consequently, purchase intention.
 Latent variable behavioural inten -

evaluating environmentally friendly products.
Therefore, marketers should strengthen both
WOM and e-WOM strategies to encourage re -
peat purchases of terrarium products.

Limitations and Future Research Sugges-
tions
 (1) Limitations for the research appear
to	be	financial	and	time	constraints.	Therefore,
Bangkok was the only selected location. Con -
ducting in a single city may limit the general -
izability of the outcomes to other geographic
areas. Expanding the outcomes to other geo -
graphical locations should be concerned with
caution.
 (2) The data were collected through
self-reported questionnaires that may emerge
response bias, social desirability bias, and ulti-
mately	affecting	the	accuracy	of	the	findings.
 (3) The enlargement of sample size
and more diverse sample are recommended
in order to gain further insight for the future
research.
 (4) Future research is suggested to be
conducted in a different product category or
industry that is associated with green products.
 (5) Future research may incorporate
omitted variables such as trust, brand image,
loyalty, and customer experience to provide
a more comprehensive understanding of the
factors affecting intention. These omitted
variables may act as mediating or moderating
mechanisms.
