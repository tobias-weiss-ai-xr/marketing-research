Abstract

This

study

investigated

the

relationship

between

relationship

marketing

dimensions

and

Customer

Lifetime

Value

(CLV)

of

commercial

banks

in

Cross

River

State,

Nigeria.

The

research

was

prompted

by

the

growing

"relational

void"

in

the

Nigerian

banking

sector,

where

the

transition

to

digital

banking

has

prioritized

technical

efficiency

over

the

"high-touch"

relationship

management

essential

for

long-term

loyalty.

The

research

adopted

a

survey

research

design.

Primary

data

were

collected

from

a

sample

of

202

respondents

using

a

structured

questionnaire.

To

ensure

data

reliability,

preliminary

tests

including

normality

and

homoscedasticity

were

conducted.

The

hypotheses

were

tested

using

Pearson

Product

Moment

Correlation

and

the

Student’s

t-test

at

a

0.05

level

of

significance.

The

study

identified

that

the

erosion

of

fundamental

pillars

like

trust

and

commitment

often

compromised

by

system

instabilities

and

impersonal

communication

directly

contribute

to

rising

customer

churn.

Empirical

results

indicated

a

significant

positive

correlation

between

relationship

marketing

dimensions

and

CLV.

Specifically,

the

calculated

t-value

of

2.729

exceeded

the

critical

value,

confirming

that

dimensions

such

as

trust,

conflict

handling,

and

shared

values

are

vital

mediators

that

convert

transactional

users

into

long-term

assets.

It

is

then

recommended

that

commercial

banks

should

restructure

their

digital

strategies

to

include

robust

conflict-resolution

mechanisms

and

personalized

communication.

Management

should

move

beyond

automated

alerts

to

foster

meaningful

dialogue

and

shared

values,

thereby

rebuilding

the

trust

and

commitment

necessary

to

enhance

customer

lifetime.
