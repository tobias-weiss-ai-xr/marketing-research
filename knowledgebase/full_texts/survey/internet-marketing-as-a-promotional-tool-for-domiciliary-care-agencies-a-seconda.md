Abstract: The increasing use of digital technologies has transformed marketing practices across service
sectors, creating new opportunities for organisations to communicate with stakeholders, enhance visibil-
ity, and support service accessibility. In the domiciliary care sector, however, adopting internet marketing
presents distinctive challenges because of regulatory requirements, safeguarding responsibilities, confi-
dentiality obligations, and resource constraints. Despite growing interest in digital marketing among small
and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), limited attention has focused on its application within regulated
care services. Beyond practical recommendations, the study offers a conceptual integration that explains
why digital marketing adoption in domiciliary care differs from adoption patterns reported in the broader
SME literature. Specifically, the review identifies regulatory compliance, safeguarding responsibilities,
trust formation, and organisational capacity as interacting conditions that mediate the relationship between
digital marketing adoption and perceived organisational value. This extends existing digital marketing
research by positioning domiciliary care as a distinct, high-trust, highly regulated service context. The
study's principal contribution is a sector-specific synthesis of internet marketing evidence for domiciliary
care providers, integrating marketing considerations with regulatory and ethical requirements often over-
looked in the broader digital marketing literature. The findings suggest that internet marketing may sup-
port organisational visibility, stakeholder engagement, and reputation management. However, the availa-
ble evidence does not establish direct causal relationships with client acquisition or organisational growth.
The study concludes with evidence-informed recommendations and identifies priorities for future research
on digital marketing effectiveness in regulated care environments.

is widely recognised as a cost -effective way to enhance visibility and customer acquisition, little evidence exists on the specific
opportunities, constraints, and outcomes of its use among small domiciliary care agencies. Furthermore, the interaction betw een

existing literature across digital marketing, SME management, healthcare management, and social care studies, this research s eeks

2021; Chaffey & Ellis-Chadwick, 2022).
2. Literature Review
The increasing digitalisation of markets has transformed how organisations communicate with customers, deliver value, and
achieve competitive advantage. Digital marketing has evolved into a strategic capability that enables businesses to enhance visibility,
engage target audiences, and strengthen customer relationships through websites, social media, search engines, and mobile technol-
ogies (Kaihlanen et al., 2023; Chaffey & Ellis -Chadwick, 2022; Dwivedi et al., 2021). As consumers increasingly rely on digital
platforms to search for information, compare alternatives, and evaluate service providers, organisations must adapt their marketing
strategies to meet changing expectations and behaviours. This shift is particularly significant in-service industries, where perceived
trust, reputation, and service quality often shape purchasing decisions (Kotler et al., 2021; Tuten & Solomon, 2023).
For small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), digital marketing provides cost-effective opportunities to compete with larger
organisations despite limited resources. However, adoption remains uneven, with SMEs facing barriers such as financial constraints,
insufficient digital expertise, limited technological capability, and uncertainty regarding return on investment (Taiminen & Kar-

The domiciliary care sector presents a particularly relevant context for examining digital marketing adoption. Prospective

as primary marketing channels (Cheung & Thadani, 2012; Johnson et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2023). While these traditional methods
remain valuable, an overreliance on offline approaches may reduce visibility and competitiveness in a market where digital engage-
ment increasingly influences customer decision-making.
2.1. Diffusion of Innovation
Rogers’ (2003) Diffusion of Innovation (DOI) theory provides an important framework for understanding how organisations
adopt digital marketing technologies. The theory argues that adoption depends on perceptions of relative advantage, compatibi lity,
complexity, trialability, and observability. Innovations that demonstrate clear benefits and align with existing organisational pr ac-
tices are more likely to gain acceptance. In contrast, technologies perceived as complex or incompatible with operational needs are
often adopted more slowly. DOI therefore offers valuable insights into the factors influencing digital marketing adoption wit hin
SMEs and regulated service organisations (Rogers, 2003; Oliveira & Martins, 2011).
Research on SME digitalisation highlights the importance of organisational resources, managerial capability, technological
competence, and environmental pressures in shaping innovation adoption decisions. Firms in competitive markets are often ince n-
tivised to adopt digital technologies to improve efficiency, customer engagement, and market reach, while organisations with limited
resources may be more reluctant to pursue technological change (Tornatzky & Fleischer, 1990; Awa et al., 2017; Kaihlanen et a l.,
2023). Adoption depends not only on the innovation's characteristics but also on organisational readiness and contextual factors that
affect implementation capability.
Within the domiciliary care sector, DOI is particularly useful for explaining variations in internet marketing adoption. Larg er
care organisations often act as early adopters because they have greater access to financial resources, specialised expertise , and
technological infrastructure, whereas smaller providers often adopt more slowly because of resource limitations, regulatory concerns,
and perceived risks (Liu et al., 2023; Hesgrove et al., 2024). Combined with the Technology Acceptance Model, DOI provides a
comprehensive theoretical lens for understanding how perceptions of usefulness, organisational capability, and sector-specific con-
ditions influence digital marketing implementation within highly regulated care environments (Venkatesh & Davis, 2000).
2.2. Digital Marketing in the Domiciliary Care Sector
Digital marketing encompasses a wide range of online activities including search engine optimisation (SEO), social media
marketing, content marketing, email marketing, online advertising, and reputation management. These tools enable organisations to
reach specific audiences, measure performance in real time, and adapt marketing strategies based on customer behaviour (Ryan,
2016; Chaffey & Ellis -Chadwick, 2022). For SMEs, digital marketing offers a cost -effective way to increase brand visibility and
market reach. Studies consistently report positive associations between digital marketing adoption and organisational outcomes such
as customer engagement, brand awareness, and business growth (Taiminen & Karjaluoto, 2015; Dwivedi et al., 2021).
Within domiciliary care services, digital marketing has become increasingly important as service users and their families rel y
on online channels throughout the decision -making process. Search engine visibility plays a vital role because consumers often
search for care providers in specific geographical areas, making local SEO a valuable strategy for generating enquiries and increas-
ing organisational visibility (Ryan, 2016; Chaffey & Smith, 2022). Similarly, social media platforms give care organisations oppor-
tunities to highlight organisational values, communicate expertise, share service information, and engage with local communit ies.
These interactions can increase awareness and build trust among prospective clients and stakeholders (Kotler et al., 2021; Tuten &
Solomon, 2023).
The influence of digital marketing extends beyond promotion to include trust building and relationship development. Contem-
porary consumers frequently evaluate online reviews, testimonials, ratings, and recommendations before selecting service providers,
particularly in high -involvement sectors such as healthcare and social care (Kotler et al., 2021). Research on electronic word -of-
mouth indicates that online reviews significantly affect consumer perceptions, purchasing intentions, and provider selection deci-
sions (Filieri, 2015; Rosario et al., 2016). However, applying digital marketing in care settings must balance promotional objectives
with ethical, safeguarding, and confidentiality requirements, highlighting the need for carefully managed, compliant digi tal com-
munication strategies (Care Quality Commission, 2025).

2.3. Customer Relationship Management (CRM) and Digital Engagement
Customer Relationship Management (CRM) provides a valuable framework for understanding how digital marketing supports
long-term customer engagement and organisational performance (Ainin et al., 2015). CRM is a strategic approach that integrates
people, processes, and technology to create value by effectively managing customer relationships (Payne & Frow, 201 3). Rather
than focusing solely on customer acquisition, CRM emphasises customer retention, satisfaction, and loyalty, making it highly rele-
vant to service industries were relationship quality influences organisational reputation and sustainability. In domiciliary care, build-
ing trust-based relationships is central to service effectiveness and customer confidence.
Integrating CRM with digital marketing technologies has improved organisations’ ability to communicate with customers more
efficiently and personally. Digital tools such as websites, email marketing systems, social media platforms, and customer databases
provide mechanisms for maintaining regular interaction and better understanding customer needs and preferences (Buttle & Maklan,
2019; Kumar et al., 2019; Mendes et al., 2024). Research indicates that organisations adopting CRM-supported engagement strate-
gies often experience improvements in customer satisfaction, service quality, and retention rates. These benefits are particu larly
significant for SMEs seeking to maximise marketing effectiveness in resource-constrained environments.
In the domiciliary care sector, CRM supports communication with service users, families, referral partners, and wider com-
munity stakeholders. Effective relationship management enhances organisational credibility, promotes continuity of care, and
strengthens long-term engagement with clients and their support networks (Buttle, 2009; Payne & Frow, 2013;). Furthermore, CRM
provides a practical mechanism for aligning digital marketing activities with broader organisational objectives. By linking customer
engagement, reputation management, and service quality improvement, CRM strengthens digital marketing's strategic contribution
within regulated care settings.
2.4. Critical Evaluation and Literature Gap
The extant literature supports the proposition that digital marketing contributes positively to organisational visibility, customer
engagement, brand awareness, and stakeholder communication (Chaffey & Ellis -Chadwick, 2022; Dwivedi et al., 2021; Tuten &
Solomon, 2023). However, evidence comes from retail, hospitality, e-commerce, and general SME contexts, where customer deci-
sion-making processes differ from those observed in highly regulated, trust -dependent service environments. Consequently, as-
sumptions about the effectiveness of digital marketing cannot be uncritically transferred to domiciliary care settings, where service
selection is characterised by elevated perceived risk, emotional involvement, and information asymmetry.
A second limitation within the literature concerns its treatment of technology adoption. Existing studies frequently identify
barriers such as resource scarcity, limited digital capabilities, technological complexity, and managerial uncertainty (Taimi nen &
Karjaluoto, 2015; Nuseir et al., 2021). However, these factors are typically examined independently of regulatory obligations, safe-
guarding requirements, and professional accountability mechanisms. As a result, current theory offers only a partial explana tion of
how care organisations adopt digital marketing, where compliance considerations may be as important as technological and eco-
nomic factors.
Furthermore, digital marketing, technology adoption, and customer relationship management have evolved as separate streams
of scholarship. While TAM explains technology acceptance (Davis, 1989), DOI explains innovation adoption (Rogers, 2003), and
CRM explains relationship-building processes (Payne & Frow, 2005), these perspectives have received limited attention in how
they interact within regulated care environments. This omission is theoretically significant because digital marketing effect iveness
within domiciliary care appears contingent not only on visibility and technological capability but also on trust formation, organisa-
tional readiness, resource availability, and governance requirements.
Accordingly, this review identifies a substantive knowledge gap . It proposes  that digital marketing adoption in domiciliary
care should be conceptualised as a multidimensional process shaped by interactions among technological, organisational, relational,
and regulatory factors (Anastasiou & Smith, 2023). By integrating insights f rom digital marketing, technology adoption, CRM,
healthcare management, and social care literature, the study contributes to a sector -specific framework that explains both the op-
portunities and constraints of digital marketing adoption in domiciliary care organisations.
3. Methodology
This study employed a qualitative secondary research design to examine the applicability and strategic value of internet mar-
keting within the UK domiciliary care sector (Johnston, 2017). Given the growing body of literature on digital marketing, technology
adoption, customer relationship management (CRM), and healthcare service provision, a secondary evidence synthesis was the most
appropriate approach to address the research objectives. The methodology was designed to ensure transparency, rigour, and rep li-
cability through the systematic identification, selection, appraisal, and analysis of relevant evidence.
The research is informed by an interpretivist philosophical stance, which acknowledges that contextual factors and stakeholder
experiences shape organisational behaviour, marketing practices, and technology adoption decisions  (Saunders et al., 2019;

ment of domiciliary care providers.
This study adopted a scoping review methodology and reported it in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Sys-
tematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses Extension for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA-ScR) framework (Smith et al., 2024; Tricco et al.,

existing evidence while identifying key themes and knowledge gaps (Munn et al., 2018). This approach is parti cularly suitable
because research on internet marketing in domiciliary care remains fragmented across multiple disciplines, including healthca re
management, digital marketing, small business management, and social care policy (Allan, 2021).
3.1. Data Sources and Search Strategy
Data were drawn from three principal categories of evidence: peer -reviewed academic literature, government and regulatory
publications, and industry reports. We retrieved academic sources from Google Scholar, ScienceDirect, JSTOR, Emerald Insight,
and Taylor & Franc is Online. We obtained government and regulatory evidence from organisations including the Care Quality
Commission (CQC) (2025), NHS Digital, the Department of Health and Social Care, the Office for National Statistics (ONS), and
Skills for Care. We also reviewed industry reports and practitioner guidance from organisations such as, Laing Buisson (2022), the
Local Government Association, and Digital Social Care to capture contemporary developments and sector-specific insights.
We conducted a structured search strategy between May 2025 and December 2025 using predefined keywords and Boolean
search operators. Search terms included combinations of “digital marketing,” “internet marketing,” “domiciliary care,” “home care,”
“social care,” “technology adoption,” “SMEs,” “customer relationship management,” and “healthcare marketing.” The search pro-
cess followed the study objectives and was designed to maximise sensitivity and relevance.
Sources were included if they were published between 2018 and 2026, originated from credible academic, governmental, or
professional sources, and demonstrated direct relevance to digital marketing, technology adoption, CRM, healthcare marketing,  or
social care service delivery. The review retained seminal theoretical contributions, including Davis (1989), Rogers (2003), and
Payne and Frow (2005), regardless of publication date, because of their enduring theoretical significance. We excluded source s if
the full text was unavailable, methodological transparency was insufficient, the content was primarily promotional, or relevance to
the review objectives was limited.

The review's trustworthiness was strengthened through several measures designed to enhance credibility, dependability, and

4. Results and Interpretation
4.1. Thematic Analysis
The analysis identified four interrelated themes concerning the adoption and effectiveness of internet marketing within the
domiciliary care sector: digital visibility and client acquisition, online reviews and consumer trust, digital marketing adop tion and
perceived value, and barriers to digital marketing adoption. These themes emerged consistently across academic studies, indus try
reports, and sector publications, indicating that digital marketing is increasingly viewed as a strategic capability rather t han simply
a promotional function. The findings suggest that a combination of technological, organisational, and behavioural factors influences
digital marketing effectiveness within domiciliary care. While digital platforms offer opportunities to increase visibility, strengthen
stakeholder engagement, and improve organisational communication, the benefits depend heavily on organisational resources, dig-
ital competencies, and the ability to build trust  among service users and their families. Consequently, in ternet marketing is under-
stood as part of a broader organisational strategy rather than an isolated marketing activity (Heponiemi et al., 2022).
4.1.1. Digital Visibility and Client Acquisition
A dominant finding across the literature is the growing importance of digital visibility in supporting client acquisition. As
service users and family members increasingly rely on online information when evaluating providers, organisational websites, local
search results, online directories, and search -engine optimisation become critical mechanisms for care organisations to establish
market presence and accessibility. The literature consistently links stronger digital visibility to more enquiries and greater awareness
among prospective service users.
However, the evidence reveals an important distinction between visibility and conversion. While organisations with stronger
online presence frequently report higher engagement levels, few studies provide robust evidence demonstrating direct effects on
client acquisition, occupancy rates, or organisational growth. This suggests that visibility functions as a necessary but insuffi cient
condition for business development (Kingsnorth, 2022). Digital presence increases the probability of service discovery, but success-
ful conversion appears dependent upon additional factors, particularly trust, reputation, and service quality.
4.1.2. Online Reviews and Consumer Trust
Trust emerged as a central mechanism linking digital marketing activity to consumer decision -making. Unlike commercial
sectors, domiciliary care involves prominent levels of perceived risk, emotional involvement, and information asymmetry. Cons e-
quently, pr ospective clients often rely on online reviews, testimonials, and ratings to assess provider credibility before making
contact (Tebra, 2025). The findings indicate that online reputation management has evolved into a critical component of digit al
marketing strategy. Positive reviews enhance perceptions of competence, reliability, and service quality, thereby reducing uncer-
tainty for prospective service users. Furthermore, organisations with a higher volume of verified reviews often achieve greater online
engagement and visibility, suggesting a mutually reinforcing relationship between digital reputation and discoverability.

Nevertheless, the literature remains underdeveloped regarding the specific role of online reviews in domiciliary care setting s.
Much of the available evidence comes from broader healthcare and consumer service contexts, limiting the ability to draw definitive

tation is strongly supported conceptually, empirical evidence remains comparatively limited.
4.1.3. Digital Marketing Adoption and Perceived Value
The analysis demonstrates increasing adoption of digital marketing practices throughout healthcare and social care sectors.
Websites, social media platforms, online directories, content marketing, email marketing, and pay -per-click advertising have be-
come common components of organisational communication and promotional strategies . Across the reviewed literature, digital
marketing is widely seen as offering advantages over traditional approaches because of its targeting precision, scalability, perfor-
mance monitoring capabilities, and potential cost efficiency. These characteristics  are particularly relevant for small and medium-
sized care organisations, where resources are constrained, and marketing investments must deliver demonstrable value. However ,
the evidence supporting effectiveness remains mixed. Although many sources report  improvements in visibility, engagement, and
communication, few studies provide longitudinal or causal evidence linking digital marketing investment to measurable organis a-
tional outcomes. Consequently, the literature supports a strong perception of value b ut provides less certainty regarding financial
returns, client acquisition, or long -term competitive advantage. This finding highlights an important gap between practitioner ex-
pectations and empirical validation.
4.1.4. Barriers to Digital Marketing Adoption
Despite its perceived benefits, organisational barriers continue to constrain digital marketing adoption. The most consistently
reported challenges include limited digital literacy, insufficient specialist expertise, financial constraints, and uncertain ty about
implementation and measurement  (Kelley et al., 2020 . These barriers are particularly pronounced among smaller care providers,
which often lack marketing personnel and technological resources. Regulatory considerations represent an additional obstacle. Com-
pliance with GDPR, confidentiality requirements, saf eguarding obligations, and professional standards creates a complex opera-
tional environment in which providers must carefully manage marketing activities (Kell ey et al., 2020). As a result, providers
demonstrate a cautious approach towards digital engagement, particularly where organisational capabilities are limited. Importantly,
the findings suggest that barriers are not solely technologica l or financial. Instead, they reflect broader issues of organisational
readiness, leadership commitment, and institutional capability. This indicates that effective digital transformation within domiciliary
care requires cultural and strategic change in addition to technological investment.
4.2. Cross-Theme Synthesis
The thematic findings suggest that digital marketing adoption within domiciliary care is best understood as an interconnected
process rather than a collection of independent activities. While digital visibility emerged as an essential prerequisite for  market
presence, the evidence indicates that visibility alone is insufficient to generate meaningful organisational outcomes. Instea d, the
benefits of visibility appear to depend upon the extent to which organisations can establish trust, demonstrate credibility, and main-
tain positive stakeholder relationships through digital channels. A key contribution of the analysis is distinguishing betwee n mar-

bility, accessibility, awareness, engagement, and reputation enhancement. However, evidence is much weaker on translating the se
outputs into measurable outcomes such as client acquisition, organisational growth, improved service quality, o r financial perfor-
mance. This distinction challenges assumptions often embedded in practitioner discourse, where increases in digital activity are
assumed to lead directly to business success.
The findings also indicate that organisational readiness is a critical mediating factor between adoption and effectiveness. O r-
ganisations possessing stronger digital capabilities, leadership commitment, and resource availability are more likely to imp lement
and benefit from digital marketing initiatives. Conversely, organisations characterised by resource constraints, skills shortages, and
implementation uncertainty often face adoption difficulties, regardless of perceived advantages. These findings are con sistent with
broader technology-adoption literature and highlight the importance of strategic and organisational conditions in shaping outcomes.
Finally, the evidence suggests that regulatory compliance functions as an important contextual moderator. Unlike commercial
sectors, domiciliary care organisations operate within environments characterised by safeguarding obligations, confidentialit y re-
quirements, professional standards, and data protection responsibilities. Consequently, the implementation and effectiveness of dig-
ital marketing cannot be explained solely through economic or technological considerations. Rather, adoption decisions reflec t a
balance between marketing objectives, organisational capabilities, and regulatory responsibilities. Based on the synthesis of  evi-
dence, four propositions are advanced:

4.3. Theoretical Interpretation
This study's principal contribution is integrating digital marketing, technology adoption, and relationship -management per-
spectives in the context of domiciliary care. Existing research has examined these domains separately, resulting in fragmente d
explanations of why organisations adopt dig ital marketing successfully while others experience limited adoption or constrained
outcomes. By synthesising evidence across these fields, the study provides a more comprehensive understanding of digital marketing
adoption within a highly regulated service sector.
The findings extend the Technology Acceptance Model by demonstrating that adoption decisions are influenced not only by
perceptions of usefulness and ease of use but also by compliance obligations, safeguarding considerations, and trust-related concerns.
Similarly, the study extends Diffusion of Innovation theory by highlighting organisational readiness and regulatory complexity  as
factors influencing innovation adoption. In parallel, the study advances CRM theory by identifying digital trust -building mecha-
nisms, particularly online reviews and reputation management, as important precursors to relationship development within care
services.
Building on these insights, the study proposes an integrated conceptual framework in which five interacting dimensions shape
digital marketing adoption: perceived usefulness, organisational readiness, digital trust formation, regulatory governance, a nd re-
source availability. This framework offers a more context-sensitive explanation of technology adoption than those commonly found
in general SME literature and contributes to emerging scholarship on digital transformation within healthcare and social care organ-
isations.
4.4. Achievement of Research Objectives
The review addressed all research objectives, although evidence quality and strength varied across areas. Compelling evidence
identified the sector's principal digital marketing strategies, including SEO, social media marketing, content marketing, onl ine re-
view management, Google Business Profile optimisation, and pay -per-click advertising. Similarly, the literature consistently iden-
tified digital capability gaps, financial constraints, and regulatory concerns as the principal barriers to adoption.

5. Discussion, Research Limitations, Future Recommendations and Conclusion
5.1. Discussion
From a practical perspective, the findings suggest that domiciliary care organisations should approach digital marketing as a
strategic organisational capability embedded within wider business, governance, and service -quality objectives. Successful imple-
mentation requires more than investment in digital technologies; it also depends upon leadership commitment, digital capability
development, performance measurement, ethical oversight, and regulatory compliance. Organisations seeking to strengthen their
competitive position should prioritise activities that enhance digital visibility and online reputation while simultaneously maintain-
ing the trust, transparency, and accountability expected within care provision. These findings are particularly relevant for smaller
providers facing increasing competitive pressures and changing consumer expectations within an increasingly digital marketplace.
A central contribution of the study is identifying an important distinction between marketing outputs and organisational out-
comes. The evidence reviewed consistently links digital marketing activities to greater visibility, stakeholder engagement, a ccessi-
bility, and reputation development. Nevertheless, evidence is weaker on how far these outputs translate into measurable improv e-
ments in client acquisition, organisational growth, financial performance, or service quality. These findings challenge assum ptions
frequently embedded within practitioner discourse and suggest that digital marketing should be conceptualised as an enabling ca-
pability rather than a direct determinant of organisational success. The review therefore contributes to ongoing debates rega rding
the effectiveness of digital marketing by highlighting the need to examine the mechanisms through which digital engagement creates
value in service organisations.
The study further contributes to theory by integrating Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), Diffusion of Innovation (DOI),
and Customer Relationship Management (CRM) perspectives within a single explanatory framework. Existing research has exam-
ined digital marketing adoption, technology acceptance, and relationship management as separate domains. By synthesising these
perspectives, the review proposes that digital marketing adoption within domiciliary care is shaped by five interdependent di men-
sions: perceived usefulness, organisational readiness, digital trust formation, regulatory governance, and resource availability. The
resulting framework extends existing technology-adoption literature by demonstrating that regulatory obligations and trust-building
requirements are not merely contextual influences but central determinants of adoption behaviour and perceived value within highly
regulated service environments.
Overall, this study argues that domiciliary care represents a distinctive context that challenges assumptions derived from th e
broader digital marketing and SME literature. The findings show that the effectiveness of internet marketing depends not only  on
technological adoption but also on an organisation's capacity to turn digital visibility into trusted relationships while operating within
complex regulatory and ethical constraints. By developing a context-specific conceptual framework and identifying key mechanisms
underpinning adoption and effectiveness, the study provides a foundation for future empirical research . It contributes to emerging
scholarship at the intersection of digital marketing, healthcare management, and organisational change. Future studies should  em-
pirically test the proposed framework and examine whether digital visibility, trust formation, and organisational readiness translate
into sustained organisational and service-user outcomes within domiciliary care settings.
5.2. Research Limitations
This study provides a comprehensive synthesis of existing knowledge on internet marketing within the domiciliary care sector;
however, it acknowledges limitations. First, the research relied exclusively on secondary data drawn from academic literature, policy
documents, industry reports, and professional guidance. While this enabled the integration of diverse perspectives, it did no t allow
direct examination of the experiences and perceptions of domiciliary care managers, employees, service users, or family  members.
Furthermore, the evidence base included studies employing different methodological approaches and varying levels of rigour, r e-
sulting in inconsistencies in the quality and strength of available evidence.
A further limitation is the transferability of the findings. The existing evidence originates from broader healthcare, servic e-
sector, and SME contexts rather than domiciliary care specifically. Consequently, although important insights are inferred, t he
unique characteristics of domiciliary care, including safeguarding responsibilities, regulatory requirements, and relationship -based

Finally, the rapidly evolving nature of digital technologies presents an inherent limitation. Changes in social media platforms,
search-engine algorithms, artificial intelligence applications, consumer behaviour, and data protection requirements may affect  the

5.3. Practical and Theoretical Implications
The findings suggest that domiciliary care providers should adopt a phased, evidence-based approach to implementing digital
marketing. Priority should be given to strengthening foundational digital assets, including website quality, local search visibility,
Google Business Profile optimisation, and online review -management processes. Organisations should also establish clear perfor-
mance indicators, such as website traffic, qualified enquiries, conversion rates, client retention, review volume, and marketing  ex-
penditure, to evaluate whether digital marketing activities generate meaningful organisational value. Given the capability challenges
identified in the literature, any investment in technology should be matched with investment in staff development, digital skills, and
performance-monitoring systems.
From a governance perspective, digital marketing activities should operate within a robust compliance framework that incor-
porates UK GDPR requirements, data protection principles, safeguarding responsibilities, confidentiality obligations, and advertis-
ing standards. Attention should be paid to the ethical use of testimonials, reviews, photographs, and client-related content, ensuring
that informed consent and privacy protections are consistently maintained. These measures are essential to preserving trust w hile
reducing operational and regulatory risks.
Future research should move beyond descriptive assessments of digital marketing activity and focus on understanding the
mechanisms through which digital engagement influences organisational outcomes. Primary studies involving managers, employees,
service users, and family carers would provide valuable context-specific insights. In contrast, comparative case studies and longitu-
dinal investigations could evaluate the long-term effects of digital marketing on client acquisition, organisational performance, and
service quality. Further empirical testing of the integrated framework proposed in this study would also strengthen theoretic al un-
derstanding of digital marketing adoption within regulated care environments.
5.4. Future Research Recommendations
The findings of this review highlight opportunities for future research that could strengthen understanding of digital marketing
adoption and effectiveness within domiciliary care settings. First, future studies should incorporate primary data collection  from
multiple stakeholder groups, including care managers, employees, service users, and family members. Such research would provide
richer insights into organisational readiness, stakeholder expectations, digital engagement behaviours, and the factors infl uencing
technology adoption decisions. By moving beyond secondary evidence, researchers are better positioned to understand how digital
marketing practices are experienced and interpreted within real-world care environments.
Second, comparative and case-study research would provide valuable context-specific evidence regarding the implementation
and impact of digital marketing strategies. Studies comparing organisations with distinct levels of digital maturity, resource availa-
bility, or market positioning could identify the organisational conditions under which digital marketing creates value. Before -and-
after evaluations of digital marketing interventions would provide stronger evidence of effectiveness and offer practical gui dance
for managers seeking to optimise digital investments within resource-constrained care organisations.
Third, future research should examine the perspectives of prospective and existing service users regarding digital marketing
communications. Given the critical role of trust, reputation, and perceived service quality in care -related decision-making, greater
attention is required to understand how individuals evaluate websites, online reviews, social media content, and other digital touch-
points. Such research would contribute to the development of more ethical, transparent, and stakeholder-centred marketing practices
that remain aligned with the values and responsibilities of health and social care provision.
Finally, longitudinal and theory-driven research is needed to examine the relationship between digital marketing activities and
organisational outcomes over time. Existing evidence primarily demonstrates associations between digital marketing and marketing-
related outputs such as visibility, engagement, and reputation. Future studies should investigate whether these outputs translate into
measurable improvements in client acquisition, organisational growth, service quality, customer retention, and competiti ve perfor-
mance. Empirical testing of the integrated framework proposed in this study, incorporating Technology Acceptance Model (TAM),
Diffusion of Innovation (DOI), and Customer Relationship Management (CRM) perspectives, would make a valuable contribution
to both digital marketing and healthcare management literature.

5.5. Conclusions
This study examined the strategic role of internet marketing within the domiciliary care sector through a scoping review and
thematic synthesis of evidence drawn from digital marketing, healthcare management, SME, and social care literature. The findings
indicate that internet marketing is increasingly becoming a critical organisational capability within care services, reflecting broader
patterns of digital transformation across service industries. However, rather than supporting a simple relationship betwe en digital
marketing adoption and organisational success, the review shows that the value derived from digital marketing depends on a co m-
plex interaction of technological, organisational, relational, and regulatory factors. In this regard, the study advances understanding
of digital marketing within a sector characterised by prominent levels of trust dependency, regulatory oversight, and service  com-
plexity.
