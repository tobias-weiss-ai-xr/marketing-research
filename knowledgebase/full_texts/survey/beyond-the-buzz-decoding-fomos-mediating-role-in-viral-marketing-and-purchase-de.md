Abstract
Jumbo is an original Indonesian animated film that once held the record as the nation’s highest -
grossing film of all time. Its remarkable commercial success was driven by an aggressive viral marketing
strategy that triggered extensive public discussion. Th is phenomenon provides a compelling case to
explore how psychological triggers like FoMO influence consumer purchase decisions. This research
examines and explains the influence of viral marketing on purchase decision, with FoMO  as a
mediating variable among audiences of Jumbo in Denpasar City. The population consists of audiences
of Jumbo in Denpasar City who have watched the film in cinemas, with a total sample of 120
respondents. The sampling method employed was purposive samp ling, with data collected through a
survey using questionnaires. This research utilizes descriptive and inferential statistical analysis
techniques, specifically Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) with Partial Least Squares (PLS). Results
indicate that viral marketing does not have a significant direct effect on purchase decision. However,
viral marketing has a significant positive effect on FoMO, and FoMO has a significant positive effect on
purchase decision. Furthermore, findings show that FoMO fully mediates the influence of viral
marketing on purchase decision. The theoretical implication of this study is the strengthening of the
S-O-R theory in explaining purchase decision behavior. Practically, results emphasize the importance
for marketers in the entertainment industry, particularly for local films, to optimize viral marketing
strategies to trigger FoMO, which is ultimately expected to enhance consumer purchase decision .

Keywords: viral marketing, FoMO, purchase decision, S-O-R model, Movie Jumbo

INTRODUCTION
The creative industry in Indonesia has witnessed a significant surge in the quality and
popularity of local productions, particularly within the animation sector. A monumental
achievement in this industry is the release of the animated film Jumbo, which has recorded over
10.2 million viewers, positioning it as one of the highest -grossing films in Indonesian cinematic
history (Javier, 2025). This commercial success is largely attributed to the strategic implementation
of viral marketing on social media, which effectively fostered massive public engagement (Thahirah
et al., 2024). However, despite the high reach of viral campaigns, academic literature reveals an
inconsistency regarding its direct impact on actual purchase decisions, where high digital
engagement does not always translate into ticket sales (Riani & Samatan,  2020; Thahirah et al.,
2024).
This research addresses this gap by employing the Stimulus -Organism-Response (S -O-R)
theory to explore the internal psychological mechanisms of consumers. According to this
framework, external stimuli (viral marketing) do not lead directly to a response (purchase decision)
but are first processed through the consumer's internal state or "organism" (Mehrabian & Russell,
1974). In the digital era, Fear of Missing Out (FoMO )—the pervasive anxiety that others might be
having rewarding experiences from which one is absent—has emerged as a powerful psychological
mediator (Przybylski et al., 2013). Consumers experiencing FoMO often feel compelled to maintain

LITERATURE REVIEW AND HYPOTHESIS FORMULATION
Stimulus-Organism-Response (S-O-R) Theory
The Stimulus-Organism-Response (S-O-R) model, introduced by Mehrabian & Russell (1974),
serves as a primary theoretical framework in environmental psychology and consumer behavior
research. This theory explains that individuals do not react directly to env ironmental stimuli;
instead, their reactions are mediated by internal psychological states. The framework consists of
three integrated components: Stimulus (S), representing the external environmental factors;
Organism (O), representing the internal emotio nal and cognitive processes; and Response (R),
which is the final behavioral outcome.
In the context of digital marketing, the S-O-R model provides a robust structure for analyzing
how online marketing cues influence consumer intentions (Sohaib et al., 2022). Recent literature
suggests that high -intensity digital stimuli, such as viral cont ent, function as powerful
environmental cues (S) that activate specific psychological organisms (O), such as anxiety or social
pressure, which ultimately dictate the consumer’s final response (R) in the form of a purchase
decision (Sohaib et al., 2022). This study utilizes the S-O-R framework to justify the mediating role
of psychological variables in the relationship between digital marketing efforts and purchasing
behavior in the cinematic industry (Ciersya et al., 2023; Tefertiller et al., 2020).
Viral Marketing
Viral marketing is defined as a digital marketing strategy that leverages word -of-mouth
(WOM) mechanisms by encouraging consumers to disseminate company or product information
through online platforms (Kotler & Keller, 2020). This approach utilizes electro nic network effects
to facilitate the rapid and exponential spread of marketing messages to a vast audience (Chaffey &
Ellis-Chadwick, 2016). Unlike traditional marketing, this phenomenon is driven by User -Generated
Content (UGC), where the success of a campaign, such as the promotion of the film Jumbo, depends
on the content's ability to trigger massive social sharing and peer -to-peer distribution (Sari &
Darma, 2024; Tefertiller et al., 2020).
To measure the effectiveness of viral marketing as a stimulus, this study adapts indicators
from recent empirical research. Kembau & Christanti (2024) identify key triggers such as the
messengers, the message, and the environment as essential components of  a viral epidemic.

Fear of Missing Out (FoMO)
Fear of Missing Out (FoMO ) is characterized by a pervasive anxiety that others are
experiencing rewarding events while one is absent, driving a compulsive need for constant social
connectivity (McGinnis, 2020; Sari & Darma, 2024). In a marketing context, this psychological state
is utilized to create a sense of urgency through product scarcity or limited -time offers, effectively
acting as the internal "organism" (O) within the S -O-R framework that mediates consumer
responses (Hodkinson, 2019; Sari & Darma, 2024). This urgency is pa rticularly potent in digital
environments where the perceived rarity of an experience compels immediate engagement.
The intensity of FoMO is predominantly fueled by social comparison on digital platforms,
where frequent exposure to curated peer experiences leads to heightened social anxiety and
doubts regarding one's own consumption choices (Alt & Boniel -Nissim, 2018; M ilyavskaya et al.,
2018). This study adopts the indicators from Soewarno  et al. (2024) to empirically measure this
phenomenon, focusing on dependency on social media, social comparisons, impulsive tendencies,
and specific psychological anxieties. These metrics are highly relevant for analyzing how viral
cinematic trends, such as the movie Jumbo, trigger the emotional urgency necessary to drive ticket
purchases among audiences who fear being socially isolated or left out of trending cultural
conversations.
Purchase Decision
Purchase decision is the pivotal stage of the consumer journey where individuals evaluate
multiple factors to select the most optimal alternative (Kotler & Keller, 2020; Mustafa & Setiawan,
2022). In the cinematic sector, this specifically involves the acq uisition of film screening services
through a process that includes need recognition, information search, and post -purchase
evaluation (Diawati et al., 2021; Rajabiyanor et al., 2022). Within the Stimulus-Organism-Response
(S-O-R) framework, this decision acts as the behavioral Response (R), representing the final
outcome of the consumer’s internal psychological processing influenced by external marketing
stimuli.
While conventional models often emphasize rational evaluation, modern purchasing
behavior for trending entertainment is frequently modified by situational emotional influences and
social urgency (Milyavskaya et al., 2018; Sari & Darma, 2024). To capture both the evaluative and
behavioral aspects of this decision, this research adapts indicators from Mardiana & Sijabat (2022)
and Diawati  et al. (2021), focusing on purchase conviction, habitual buying patterns, referral
tendencies, and post-purchase assessment. These metrics provide a comprehensive framework for
analyzing audience responses to "once -in-a-moment" cinematic events, where the  decision to
purchase is driven by the desire to maintain social relevance in the digital era.
Viral Marketing and Purchase Decision
 Viral marketing has been extensively studied for its positive and significant influence on
purchase decisions across various industries by enhancing electronic word-of-mouth (E-WOM) and
consumer product familiarity (Praditya & Purwanto, 2024; Thahirah et al., 2024). Recent empirical
evidence indicates that high -engagement social media platforms, such as TikTok and Instagram,
significantly facilitate this relationship by allowing viral content to serve as a credible social trigger
(Haryanto et al., 2021; Razali et al., 2023). In these digital ecosystems, consumers often transition
from passive viewers to active brand advocates, effectively accelerating the collective decision to
acquire products or services (Artanti et al., 2019; Hisbullah et al., 2023).
However, inconsistencies in recent literature suggest a research gap, as some studies find
that viral campaigns do not always exert a direct impact on the final purchase decision (Riani &
Samatan, 2020; Sari & Darma, 2024). These findings indicate that in certain contexts, viral marketing

Viral Marketing and Fear of Missing Out (FoMO)
 Viral marketing serves as a primary driver of the Fear of Missing Out (FoMO) phenomenon
in the digital era by creating a pervasive perception that a specific product or experience is an
unmissable trend (Abdillah et al., 2025; Ciersya  et al., 2023). Within the Stimulus -Organism-
Response (S-O-R) framework, the massive and rapid dissemination of marketing messages acts as
an external stimulus (S) that activates the consumer's internal emotional state (O) (Sari & Darma,
2024). Consistent exposure to viral content reinforces the emotional drive to remain "up-to-date,"
thereby intensifying the audience's susceptibility to FoMO as they fear being excluded from
rewarding collective experiences (Abdillah et al., 2025; Ciersya et al., 2023).
Recent empirical evidence indicates that the intensity of communication on platforms such as
TikTok significantly correlates with increased levels of social anxiety regarding missing trends,
particularly among younger demographics (Khasanah & Maftukhah, 2025; Tondang & Dwita, 2025).
As brands implement more aggressive viral marketing strategies, the psychological response of
FoMO among consumers tends to escalate (Dewi & Martini, 2025). Collective findings from Sari &
Darma (2024) and Soewarno et al. (2024) reaffirm that widespread trend-driven content serves as
a potent stimulant that compels individuals to maintain their social relevance through engagement
with viral phenomena.
H2: Viral marketing has a positive and significant influence on Fear of Missing Out (FoMO).
Fear of Missing Out (FoMO) and Purchase Decision
 Fear of Missing Out (FoMO ) is established as a significant psychological determinant
influencing purchase decisions across various digital platforms and product categories (Sari &
Darma, 2024; Septiana et al., 2024). Social pressure to remain synchronized with environmental
trends drives consumers to adopt specific purchasing behaviors to ensure continued integration
within their social groups (Ditha et al., 2025; Solaiman & Pangaribuan , 2024). This phenomenon is
particularly prominent among younger demographics, such as Generation Z, where the anxiety of
being left behind serves as a primary motivator for consumption and acquisition (Borshalina et al.,
2021; Rahmatullah & Hermeindito, 2025).
 Psychologically, FoMO operates by creating a sense of apprehension regarding the loss of
valuable experiences or interactions shared by others (McGinnis, 2020). Frequent exposure to peer
experiences on social media platforms triggers an emotional urgency that prompts individua ls to
make immediate purchases to maintain their social relevance (Chandra, 2025; Soewarno et al.,
2024). While minor variations in the efficacy of FoMO  may exist across specific fashion niches
(Mahmud et al., 2023), the prevailing consensus highlights this variable as a substantial driver of
consumer decision-making in response to massive social trends (Laili et al., 2025).

The Mediating Role of Fear of Missing Out (FoMO)
 Fear of Missing Out (FoMO) is identified as a significant mediating variable that explains the
internal mechanism through which viral marketing influences purchase decisions. This
psychological state acts as a conduit, connecting external marketing exposure to the final consumer
behavior through the creation of intense emotional pressure (Sari & Darma, 2024; Septiana et al.,
2024). In the digital era, FoMO  functions as a psychological bridge that strengthens brand
perception and accelerates the consumer’s decision -making process by emphasizing the social
necessity of the experience (Laili et al., 2025).
 Recent empirical evidence confirms that viral marketing exerts a positive and significant

RESEARCH METHODS
This study utilizes a quantitative approach with a causal -associative research design to
investigate the relationships between viral marketing, FoMO, and purchase decisions. The target
population consists of cinema-goers in Denpasar City who have watched t he movie Jumbo. Given
the unidentified total number of viewers, the study is categorized under an infinite population. A
sample size of 120 respondents was determined based on a ratio of ten times the number of
research indicators. The sampling was conducted using a non -probability purposive sampling
technique. Specific criteria for respondents include being at least 17 years old, residing in Denpasar,
having watched Jumbo at least once, and being an active user of social media platforms such as
Instagram or TikTok. Data collection was performed through an online survey via Google Forms,
divided into two phases: a pilot test conducted from March 20 to March 22, 2026, and primary data
collection from March 28 to April 16, 2026.
The material specification for this research is a structured questionnaire developed using a
five-point Likert scale, ranging from "Strongly Disagree" (1) to "Strongly Agree" (5). The instrument
consists of 12 indicators: four for Viral Marketing, four for  FoMO, and four for Purchase Decision.
The tool specification for data processing is SmartPLS  4. This software was selected for its
sophistication in performing variance -based Structural Equation Modeling (SEM), which is highly
effective for predictive modeling and does not require a normal distribution of data.
The validity check for the research results was conducted through two stages of evaluation
using SmartPLS 4. The measurement model (outer model) assessment confirmed that all indicators
reached the required thresholds, with outer loadings ≥ 0.7 and Average Variance Extracted (AVE)
≥ 0.5. Discriminant validity was also verified using the Heterotrait -Monotrait Ratio (HTMT) with
values below 0.90. Following the validity and reliability confirmation, the data were analyzed using
inferential statistics to evaluate the structural model (inner model) and test the research
hypotheses.

Discussion
The empirical results of this study provide a comprehensive understanding of the consumer
decision-making process within the digital cinema landscape in Denpasar. The most pivotal
discovery is the confirmation of full mediation provided by Fear of Missing Out (FoMO ), which
serves as the indispensable psychological bridge between digital marketing stimuli and actual
purchase behavior. The finding for H1 shows that Viral Marketing has a positive but non-significant
direct effect on Purchase Decision (𝑝 = 0.165 > 0.05; 𝑓2 = 0.017). This result is aligned with the
research conducted by Sari & Darma (2024), which suggests that in the dynamic entertainment
industry, mere exposure to viral content is insufficient to trigger immediate ticket sales without an
internal psychological catalyst. This indicates that while the promotional campaign for the movie
Jumbo successfully generated awareness, it did not possess enough direct "push" to bypass the
psychological state of the audience.
Within the Stimulus -Organism-Response (S -O-R) framework, Viral Marketing acts as the
external Stimulus that initiates the process. The analysis of indicators reveals that "the messengers"
which the individuals or users spreading the content, obtained the h ighest average score. This
suggests that the audience in Denpasar perceives information shared by peers and social media
influencers as more credible and engaging than official advertisements. When these messengers
mass-distribute content, they create a wi despread curiosity that transforms the stimulus into an
Organism state, specifically FoMO. The significant positive influence of Viral Marketing on FoMO
(H2) found in this study is consistent with the findings of Ciersya et al. (2023), Abdillah et al. (2025),
and Tondang & Dwita (2025), which emphasize that rapid and massive content dissem ination
strengthens the perception of a prevailing trend, thereby fostering a sense of social urgency.
Furthermore, the relationship between FoMO and Purchase Decision (H3) is deeply rooted
in the audience's dependency on social media, which was the most prominent indicator for the
FoMO construct. In the social context of Denpasar, high social media reliance facilitates a
continuous feedback loop o f social comparison and anxiety. Respondents feel a compulsion to
follow cultural trends, such as watching the movie Jumbo, to maintain their social relevance and
avoid the discomfort of being "out of the loop." This significant positive relationship is aligned with
the research of Septiana et al. (2024), Rahmatullah & Hermeindito (2025), and Ditha et al. (2025),
confirming that social pressure and the need to remain socially connected are dominant drivers in
modern cinema-going behavior.
The confirmation of full mediation (H4) signifies that Viral Marketing’s influence on
Purchase Decision is entirely dependent on its ability to trigger FoMO. This aligns with the findings
of Khasanah & Maftukhah (2025), Dewi & Martini (2025), and Setiawan et al. (2025), highlighting
that FoMO is the key psychological mechanism in the digital era. Practically, this implies that
filmmakers and marketers must pivot from "broadcasting" to "strategic psychological triggering."
Based on the empirical evidence from the Jumbo case study, promotional strategies for
future local film releases should prioritize cultivating organic "messengers" and incentivizing user-
generated content, as these are perceived as more authentic signals of popularity. Furthermore,
marketing narratives should intentionally emphasize the "social currency" and "exclusivity" of the

CONCLUSION
This study concludes that viral marketing alone is insufficient to drive ticket purchase
decisions for local animated films without a psychological trigger. The results demonstrate a full
mediation mechanism where viral marketing acts as an external stimul us that significantly
intensifies the Fear of Missing Out (FoMO) among audiences. It is this psychological state of FoMO
that subsequently serves as the primary driver for purchase decisions toward the movie Jumbo.
Without the mediation of FoMO, viral marketing does not exert a significant direct influence on the
final decision to buy a ticket.
The practical implications of this research suggest that film marketers should shift their focus
from mere digital reach to the creation of "moment -driven" content. To effectively drive ticket
sales, marketing campaigns must strategically craft narratives that evoke a sense of social urgency.
By positioning the film as a cultural event that consumers cannot afford to miss, marketers can
leverage the consumer’s psychological need to remain socially relevant within their circles.
However, this research has several limitations. First, the sample is limited to 120
respondents, which may not fully represent the broader population of Indonesian cinema -goers.
Second, the geographic scope is restricted to Denpasar City, potentially limiting the generalizability
of the findings to other regions with different consumer behaviors. Lastly, the study focuses
exclusively on the animated film genre, which may have different engagement dynamics compared
to other film genres.
Based on these limitations, future research is suggested to expand the sample size and
include respondents from diverse geographic locations to enhance the representativeness of the
data. Furthermore, subsequent studies could incorporate additional variabl es, such as Electronic
Word of Mouth (E-WOM) or brand image, to provide a more comprehensive understanding of the
factors influencing purchase decisions in the creative industry.
