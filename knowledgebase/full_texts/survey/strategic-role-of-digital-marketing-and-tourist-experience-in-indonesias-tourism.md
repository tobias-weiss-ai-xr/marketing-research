ABSTRACT
Indonesia’s tourism sector is undergoing significant transformation driven by digital disruption and
the growing shift toward experience-oriented travel. This study aims to examine the strategic role of
digital marketing and tourist experience in enhancing tourism competitiveness in Indonesia. An
integrative literature review approach is employed to synthesize findings from relevant peer-reviewed
studies published between 2020 and 2024. The analysis is conducted through thematic synthesis to
identify key patterns and relationships among digital marketing strategies, experiential value, and
tourism performance. The findings indicate that digital marketing, particularly through social media
marketing, electronic word-of-mouth (e-WOM), and user-generated content (UGC), plays a significant
role in shaping destination image, enhancing tourist engagement, and influencing travel decision-
making. In parallel, tourist experience—especially memorable and immersive experiences—emerges
as a critical determinant of satisfaction, loyalty, and revisit intention. The integration of advanced
technologies and human-centric approaches under the Marketing 5.0 paradigm further strengthens
value creation by enabling personalized and data-driven tourism marketing strategies. In addition,
integrated marketing communication (IMC) and event-based tourism are identified as strategic
mechanisms that ensure message consistency and reinforce destination branding. Based on the
synthesis, this study proposes an integrative conceptual framework that connects technological (digital
marketing and Marketing 5.0), experiential (memorable tourism experience), and strategic
communication(IMC)dimensionsinexplainingtourismcompetitiveness.Thefindingscontributetothe
advancement of tourism marketing literature and provide practical implications for policymakers,
destination managers, and industry practitioners in developing more competitive and sustainable
tourism strategies in Indonesia.
Keywords: digital marketing; tourist experience; tourism competitiveness; IMC; Marketing 5.0

1. INTRODUCTION
Indonesia’s tourism sector plays a
crucial role in driving national economic
growth, contributing significantly to gross
domestic product (GDP), employment, and
regional development. According to data from
Badan Pusat Statistik, the tourism sector has
shown a steady recovery in the post-pandemic
period, with international tourist arrivals
reaching over 11 million visits in 2023 and
continuing to grow in 2024. Furthermore,
reports from Kementerian Pariwisata dan
Ekonomi Kreatif highlight that tourism
contributes substantially to foreign exchange
earnings and supports millions of jobs across
hospitality, transportation, and creative
industries. Beyond its economic significance,
tourismalsofunctionsasastrategicinstrument
forculturaldiplomacyanddestinationbranding
intheglobalarena.
In recent years, the tourism paradigm
has shifted from mass tourism toward quality
tourism,emphasizingsustainability,experience,
andvaluecreationfortourists(Fauzi&Sumirat,
2023). This transformation requires
stakeholders to redesign marketing strategies
that are not only promotional but also
experiential, personalized, and customer-
oriented. The growing competition among
global destinations further intensifies the need
for countries like Indonesia to strengthen their
tourismcompetitivenessthroughinnovativeand
integratedapproaches.
The rapid advancement of digital
technology has fundamentally transformed
tourist behavior. Modern travelers are
increasingly dependent on digital platforms to
search for information, compare destinations,
readreviews,andsharetheirtravelexperiences.
Social media platforms such as Instagram,
Threads,TikTok,X,andYouTubehavebecome
dominantchannelsinfluencingtraveldecisions.
Previous studies indicate that social media
marketing activities significantly affect tourist
satisfaction, destination image, and revisit
intention(Wilopo&Nuralam,2024).Moreover,
electronic word-of-mouth (e-WOM) and user-
generated content (UGC) enhance trust and
credibility, which are essential in shaping
tourists’ perceptions and decision-making
processes(Putrietal.,2024).
From a theoretical perspective, this
transformation aligns with the concept of
Marketing 5.0, which integrates advanced
technologies such as artificial intelligence, big
data, and predictive analytics with a human-
centricapproach(Kotleretal.,2021).Marketing
5.0enablesorganizationstodeliverpersonalized
experiences, optimize customer engagement,
andcreatevalue-driveninteractions.Withinthe
tourism industry, this approach allows
destinationmanagerstobetterunderstandtourist
preferences, anticipate demand patterns, and
design more targeted and effective marketing
campaigns.
Furthermore,theconceptofexperiential
marketing has become increasingly relevant in
tourism studies. Tourists today are not merely
seeking destinations but meaningful and
memorable experiences. The concept of
memorable tourism experience (MTE)
emphasizes emotional engagement, cultural
authenticity, and social interaction as key
drivers of satisfaction and loyalty (Karo et al.,
2024). Destinations that successfully create
unique and immersive experiences are more
likely to generate positive word-of-mouth and
repeat visits, ultimately strengthening their
competitivepositioning.
In addition, Integrated Marketing
Communication (IMC) plays a vital role in
ensuring consistent and effective messaging
across multiple channels. The integration of
digital campaigns, events, and destination
branding initiatives has proven to enhance
awareness and strengthen positioning in
competitive tourism markets (JER, 2024).
Event-basedtourism,inparticular,hasemerged
as a strategic tool to attract visitors while

Despite the growing body of literature

Moreover,limitedliteratureexplicitlyconnects

This study addresses this gap by

2. THEORETICAL FOUNDATIONS AND
LITERATURE SYNTHESIS
The rapid advancement of digital
technology has fundamentally transformed the
landscape of the tourism industry, reshaping
how destinations create, communicate, and
deliver value to tourists. Digital marketing has
evolved from a supporting tool into a strategic
enabler that facilitates real-time interaction,
global reach, and data-driven decision-making
(Dwivedi et al., 2021; Buhalis et al., 2023). In
the tourism context, where information search
andevaluationarecriticalstagesofthecustomer
journey, digital platforms such as search
engines, social media, travel blogs, and online
travelagenciesplayacentralroleininfluencing
tourists’ perceptions and behavioral intentions
(Leungetal.,2022).Theincreasingrelianceon
these platforms highlights the importance of
digital presence as a key determinant of
destinationcompetitiveness.
Social media, in particular, has emerged as a
dominant force in tourism marketing,
functioning not only as an information source
butalsoasapersuasivecommunicationchannel.
Throughvisualstorytelling,influencercontent,
and peer interaction, social media platforms
shape tourists’ expectations and decision-
making processes (Tajvidi & Karami, 2021;
Zeng & Gerritsen, 2022). Electronic word-of-
mouth (e-WOM) and user-generated content
(UGC) have become critical components of
digital marketing, as they enhance credibility
andtrustinhigh-involvementdecisionssuchas
travel(Cheungetal.,2020).Comparedtofirm-
generated content, UGC is often perceived as
more authentic and reliable, thereby exerting a
stronger influence on destination image,
perceived value, and behavioral intention
(Filieri et al., 2021). Moreover, digital
marketing enables a shift from mass
communication toward personalized
engagementthroughbigdataanalyticsandAI-
driven segmentation, allowing tourism
stakeholders to better understand tourist
preferences and deliver tailored experiences
(Gretzeletal.,2020).
From a theoretical perspective, this
transformation aligns with the concept of
Marketing 5.0, which emphasizes the
integration of advanced technologies with a
human-centric approach (Kotler, Kartajaya, &
Setiawan, 2021). Marketing 5.0 incorporates
artificial intelligence, machine learning, and
predictive analytics to enhance customer
experience while maintaining emotional and
social relevance. In the tourism industry, this
perspective is particularly important because
travelexperiencesareinherentlysubjectiveand
emotionally driven (Rather, 2021). While
technology enhances efficiency and
personalization,human-centeredvaluessuchas
authenticity, empathy, and cultural connection
remain essential in creating meaningful
experiences.Therefore,Marketing5.0provides

aconceptualbridgethatconnectstechnological
capabilities with experiential value creation in
tourism.
In parallel, experiential marketing has gained
increasing attention due to the intangible and
experience-based nature of tourism products.
Tourists are no longer merely seeking
destinations, but meaningful and memorable
experiences that provide emotional, cultural,
and social value (Pine & Gilmore, 2019; Kim,
Ritchie, & McCormick, 2019). The concept of
Memorable Tourism Experience (MTE)
emphasizes that dimensions such as novelty,
hedonism, involvement, local culture, and
refreshment contribute to the formation of
lastingmemories,whichsubsequentlyinfluence
satisfaction,revisitintention,andpositiveword-
of-mouth(Kimetal.,2019;Zatorietal.,2021).
Destinations that are able to create immersive
and authentic experiences are more likely to
differentiatethemselvesinahighlycompetitive
market and build long-term relationships with
tourists.
Importantly, digital marketing and tourist
experience are not independent constructs but
are dynamically interconnected across the
tourist journey. Digital marketing shapes
tourists’ expectations in the pre-visit stage,
whileactualtouristexperiencesinfluencepost-
visit evaluations and content-sharing behavior
(Hudson&Thal,2020).Thisinteractioncreates
a continuous feedback loop in which
experience-driven content, particularly in the
form of UGC and e-WOM, reinforces digital
marketing effectiveness. As a result, the
integration of digital engagement and
experiential value becomes a critical factor in
strengtheningtourismcompetitiveness.
Furthermore, Integrated Marketing
Communication (IMC) provides a strategic
mechanismtoensureconsistencyandcoherence
across multiple communication channels. IMC
emphasizesthealignmentofdigitalcampaigns,
socialmediaengagement,publicrelations,and
event-basedactivitiestodeliveraunifiedbrand
message(Keller,2020).Inthetourismcontext,
wheremultiplestakeholderssuchasgovernment
agencies,localcommunities,andprivatesector
actorsareinvolved,IMCplaysacrucialrolein
coordinating communication strategies and
strengthening destination branding (Gretzel et
al.,2020).Event-basedtourism,aspartofIMC,
has also emerged as an effective tool for
attracting visitors while simultaneously
enhancing destination image and cultural
positioning.
Despitethegrowingbodyofliteratureondigital

theroleofIMCinconnectingdigitalstrategies

tourismdestinationssuchasIndonesia.

3. RESEARCH METHOD
This study employs an integrative literature
review approach to synthesize theoretical
perspectives and empirical findings related to
digital marketing, tourist experience, and
tourism competitiveness. Unlike systematic
literaturereviewsthatstrictlyfollowstructured
protocolssuchasthePRISMAframework,this
approach allows for a more flexible and
comprehensive integration of multidisciplinary
insights.Therefore,itisparticularlysuitablefor
theorydevelopmentandconceptualframework
construction.
The data were collected from reputable
academicdatabases,includingGoogleScholar,
Scopus-indexed journals, and other peer-
reviewed sources. The literature selection
focused on publications from 2020 to 2024 to
ensure the relevance and timeliness of the
findings, particularly in capturing post-
pandemic tourism dynamics and rapid digital
transformation.
Theselectionprocesswasconductedthrough
severalstages.First,relevantkeywordssuchas
“digital marketing in tourism,” “tourist
experience,” “memorable tourism experience,”
“destination competitiveness,” and “integrated
marketing communication” were used to
identifypotentialarticles.Second,articleswere
screened based on relevance, focusing on
studies that explicitly examine relationships
between marketing strategies and tourist
behavior or destination performance. Third,
inclusion criteria were applied, including peer-
reviewed journal articles, English-language
publications, and studies directly related to the
tourism context. Articles that were not
accessible in full text, not peer-reviewed, or
outsidethedefinedscopewereexcluded.
Atotalof55articleswereselectedforfurther
analysis. The analysis was conducted using a
thematicanalysisapproach,inwhicheachstudy
was systematically coded and categorized into
keythemes:
(1) digital marketing in tourism,
(2) tourist experience and memorable tourism
experience,
(3) destination image and behavioral intention,
and
(4) integrated marketing communication and
brandingstrategies.
This study adopts a qualitative synthesis
method to identify patterns, relationships, and
research gaps across the selected studies. The
synthesis process involves comparing findings
acrossstudies,identifyingrecurringconstructs,
and evaluating inconsistencies in previous
research. While the theoretical foundation
incorporates both classical and contemporary
literature, the analysis emphasizes recent

empiricalstudiestoensurecontextualrelevance.
Thefindingsfromthisprocessarethenusedto
develop an integrated conceptual framework
that combines technological, experiential, and

4. RESULT
This study synthesizes findings from 55
peer-reviewedarticlespublishedbetween2020
and2024toidentifyhowdigitalmarketingand
tourist experience collectively shape tourism
competitiveness. The thematic analysis reveals
four interrelated dimensions: (1) digital
marketing and tourist behavior, (2) tourist
experience and memorable tourism experience
(MTE), (3) destination image and behavioral
intention, and (4) integrated marketing
communication (IMC) and branding strategy.
Rather than operating as isolated constructs,
these themes form an interconnected system
that reflects the evolving dynamics of tourism
inthedigitalera.
The first theme demonstrates that digital
marketingfunctionsasanantecedentinshaping
tourist cognitive and behavioral responses.
Social media marketing, electronic word-of-
mouth (e-WOM), and user-generated content
(UGC) consistently emerge as dominant
mechanismsinfluencingdestinationawareness,
perceived credibility, and travel intention.
Across the literature, digital platforms are no
longer conceptualized merely as promotional
tools, but as interactive ecosystems where
touristsactivelyco-createmeaningandevaluate
destinations. This indicates that digital
marketing primarily operates at the pre-visit
stage by constructing initial expectations and
reducing perceived risk in travel decision-
making.
The second theme highlights tourist
experience, particularly Memorable Tourism
Experience (MTE), as a central determinant of
post-visit behavioral outcomes. Studies
consistently show that emotional engagement,
novelty,culturalimmersion,andhedonicvalue
contribute to the formation of memorable
experiences that extend beyond functional
service evaluation. Importantly, MTE does not
operate independently but is shaped by prior
expectations formed through digital marketing
exposure. This indicates a sequential
relationship in which digital stimuli influence
experiential perception, which subsequently
determines satisfaction, loyalty, and word-of-
mouthintention.
The third theme positions destination
image as a critical mediating construct linking
digital marketing and tourist experience to
behavioralintention.Astrongdestinationimage
is constructed through both firm-generated
content and experiential narratives shared by
tourists. The findings suggest that destination
image is dynamically formed through
continuousinteractionbetweenpre-visitdigital
exposureandpost-visitexperientialsharing.In
thissense,user-generatedcontentandinfluencer
narratives reinforce and reshape destination
image in a recursive feedback loop,
strengtheningitsroleasbothanoutcomeandan
antecedentintourismbehavior.
The fourth theme emphasizes integrated
marketingcommunication(IMC)asastrategic
coordination mechanism that ensures
consistency across multiple touchpoints. IMC
integrates digital campaigns, social media
engagement, public relations, and event-based
tourismintoaunifiedcommunicationstrategy.
TheliteratureindicatesthatIMCplaysacrucial
role in aligning stakeholder messages in
complex tourism ecosystems involving
government, private sector, and local
communities. Rather than functioning as an
isolated strategy, IMC operates as an enabler
that strengthens the coherence between digital
marketing efforts and experiential tourism
delivery.
Overall, the synthesis reveals that digital
marketing and tourist experience are not
independent variables but function within a
cyclical and reinforcing system. Digital
marketing shapes pre-visit expectations,
destination image mediates perception
formation, and tourist experience determines
post-visit behavioral outcomes. These
outcomes, in turn, generate user-generated
content that feeds back into the digital
ecosystem, reinforcing future marketing
effectiveness.Thiscyclicalinteractionindicates
that tourism competitiveness is constructed
throughanintegrateddynamicsysteminwhich
technological engagement, experiential value,
andstrategiccommunicationmutuallyreinforce
oneanother.

Thefindingsofthisstudydemonstratethat
digitalmarketinghasundergoneafundamental
transformation from a promotional instrument
into a strategic driver of tourism decision-
making and experience formation. Rather than
functioning as a one-way communication
channel,digitalmarketingnowoperateswithin
aninteractiveecosystemwheretouristsactively
participate in content creation, evaluation, and
dissemination. This shift reflects the evolution
of tourism communication into a data-driven
and experience-oriented system, as highlighted
in the digital ecosystem perspective proposed
by Dwivedi et al. (2021) and Buhalis et al.
(2023). However, this study extends their
argumentsbypositioningdigitalmarketingnot
onlyasanenablerofinteractionbutalsoasan
antecedentthatstructurallyshapesexpectations
priortothetourismexperience.
Thedominanceofsocialmediamarketing,
electronic word-of-mouth (e-WOM), and user-
generatedcontent(UGC)furtherreinforcesthe
transition toward participatory communication
in tourism. Unlike traditional marketing
communication, which is firm-controlled,
contemporarytourismmarketingisincreasingly
co-created by tourists themselves. This finding
is consistent with Cheung et al. (2020) and
Filierietal.(2021),whoemphasizetheroleof
peer-generated content in shaping trust and
behavioral intention. However, the results of
this study suggest a deeper implication: UGC
does not merely influence perception but
actively reconstructs destination meaning
throughcontinuoussocialvalidationprocesses.
This indicates that credibility in tourism is no
longer institutionally driven but socially
constructed.
Touristexperience,particularlyintheform
of Memorable Tourism Experience (MTE),
emergesasacentralmechanismthattranslates
expectationsintobehavioralloyalty.Consistent
withKimetal.(2019)andZatorietal.(2021),
experiential and emotional dimensions are
found to outweigh functional attributes in
determining satisfaction and revisit intention.
However,thisstudyadvancesthediscussionby
highlightingthesequentialdependencybetween
digital exposure and experiential formation. In
other words, MTE is not solely created at the
destination but is partially pre-conditioned by
digital interactions prior to travel. This finding
positionstouristexperienceasahybridconstruct
shaped by both physical and digital
environments.
TheintegrationofMarketing5.0provides
a conceptual bridge between technological
advancement and human-centric tourism
marketing. Kotler et al. (2021) argue that
artificial intelligence and predictive analytics
enable hyper-personalization in marketing
processes. In the tourism context, this study
suggeststhatsuchtechnologicalcapabilitiesare
meaningful only when they are embedded
within emotionally resonant experiences.
Therefore,thevalueofMarketing5.0intourism
lies not in technological sophistication per se,
but in its ability to balance algorithmic
efficiency with emotional authenticity, as also
impliedbyRather(2021).
Furthermore, Integrated Marketing
Communication(IMC)functionsasastructural
enabler that ensures coherence across
fragmented tourism communication channels.
In line with Keller (2020), IMC strengthens
brand consistency by aligning multiple
stakeholder communications. However, this
studyextendsIMC’srolebeyondcoordination,
positioning it as a mechanism that integrates
pre-visit digital narratives, on-site experiential
delivery, and post-visit content sharing. This
expanded view is particularly relevant in the
Indonesian tourism context, where destination
brandingisshapedbymulti-actorcollaboration
involving government institutions, private
sectoractors,andlocalcommunities.
Overall, this study contributes to the
literature by proposing that tourism
competitiveness is not the outcome of isolated
factors,butrathertheresultofaninterconnected
system involving digital marketing, tourist
experience, Marketing 5.0, and IMC. These
elementsoperateinacontinuousfeedbackloop
in which digital engagement shapes
expectations, experiences generate meaning,
and shared content reinforces future marketing
effectiveness. This systemic perspective offers
a more holistic understanding of how
competitivenessisconstructedincontemporary
tourismecosystems.
6. CONCLUSION AND
RECOMMENDATION
Conclusion
This study concludes that tourism
competitiveness in Indonesia is shaped by an
integrated system of digital marketing and
tourist experience. Digital marketing plays a

pivotal role in influencing tourist decision-
making by enhancing destination visibility,
credibility, and engagement through social
media platforms, electronic word-of-mouth (e-
WOM), and user-generated content (UGC).
These digital interactions primarily shape pre-
visitperceptionsandbehavioralintentions.
In parallel, tourist experience—
particularlyintheformofMemorableTourism
Experience (MTE)—emerges as a key
determinant of post-visit outcomes, including
satisfaction,emotionalattachment,andloyalty.
The findings confirm that experiential value
playsamoredominantroleinsustaininglong-
term tourist relationships compared to

Furthermore,Marketing5.0provides a
strategic framework that integrates
technological advancement with human-
centered value creation, while Integrated
Marketing Communication (IMC) ensures
coherence and consistency across multiple
communicationchannelsandstakeholders.The
interaction of these elements forms a dynamic
and interconnected system through which
tourism competitiveness is constructed in the
digitalera.
Recommendation
For theoretical development, future
research is encouraged to empirically validate
the proposed conceptual framework using
quantitative approaches such as Structural
EquationModeling(SEM-PLS).Furtherstudies
should also examine potential moderating
variables, including destination typology,
cultural context, and tourist segmentation, to
enrichmodelgeneralizability.
From a practical perspective,
destination managers and policymakers should
prioritize the development of integrated digital
marketing strategies that are aligned with
experiential tourism design. Strengthening
stakeholdercollaborationbetweengovernment,
private sector, and local communities is
essential to ensure consistent destination
branding. In addition, investment in digital
infrastructure, data analytics capabilities, and
smart tourism systems is critical to support
personalized, data-driven, and sustainable
tourismdevelopmentinIndonesia.
