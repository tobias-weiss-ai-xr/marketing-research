# Navigating the landscape of cause-related marketing: a bibliometric review of progress and prospects

Kanishk Koushik1*, Shashi Yadav1 and Madhulika P .Sarkar1
Abstract
Cause-related marketing (CRM) has become a prominent tactic companies use to match their commercial goals
with social causes. This paper conducts a bibliometric analysis of CRM research, utilising a dataset comprising 425
documents sourced from the Scopus database, encompassing publications categorised under A*, A, and B journal
tiers over the past 3 decades. The analysis incorporates trend analysis, citation analysis, co-citation analysis, and bib-
liographic coupling facilitated by VOSviewer software for a comprehensive examination. The key findings reveal
2020 as the most fruitful year within the dataset. The most cited author is Strahilevitz M.; the institution is the Uni-
versity of California, Berkeley, USA; and the nation is the USA. The most pivotal journal is the Journal of Business Ethics,
and the most influential article is “Donations to Charity as Purchase Incentives: How Well They Work May Depend
on What You Are Trying to Sell” . Five thematic clusters were identified: CRM and consumers, consumers skepti-
cism towards CRM, CSR and CRM, donation and CRM, and cause–brand fit and CRM. Theoretically, this paper maps
the foundational themes and developmental trajectory of CRM research. Practically, it highlights key publication pat-
terns, influential research agendas, and emerging domains, such as AI, social media, and prosocial behaviour, that can
inform future marketing campaigns. The study contributes to both academic and practitioner communities by offer-
ing an evidence-based foundation for navigating and advancing CRM research.
Keywords Cause-related marketing, Bibliometric analysis, Literature review, Corporate social responsibility
Introduction
Since customers have become more socially conscious
during the past 10 years, business organisations’ funding
on social issues has grown significantly [90]. The buyers
believe the organisation should practice socially respon -
sible behaviour [54, 71]. Cause-related marketing (CRM)
has become a popular strategic tool used by businesses
to enhance their brands and highlight their social respon-
sibilities [11, 68, 76, 92, 93]. The term CRM  is defined as
“the development and execution of marketing initiatives
involving a firm presenting an offer to donate a specified
sum to a chosen cause when consumers participate in
transactions that generate revenue, thereby fulfilling both
organizational and individual goals” [85], p. 60). Furman
and Maison [29] defined CRM  as “a tool that links the
selling of a product with supporting a cause” (p. 2980).
The development of CRM can be linked to corporate
social responsibility (CSR) objectives and marketing
goals [65], the primary difference is that CRM integrates
CSR with the sale of products and services [1]. American
Express, a well-known American financial services cor -
poration, launched a CRM campaign in 1983 with the
dual goals of encouraging credit card usage and gather -
ing money to rebuild the Statue of Liberty. Ultimately, the
campaign garnered $1.7 million in donations to help the
cause. Additionally, there was a 28% rise in card usage
and a 45% increase in new card applications [43], p. 952).

CRM benefits the business, the customers, and the soci -
ety simultaneously [18, 62, 94].
Past research revealed that CRM influences consumers’
purchase intent favourably [27, 68, 69, 79, 92, 93]. The
2014 Nielsen survey revealed that 55% of global respond -
ents would pay more for products from companies that
contribute positively to society [20]. A 2019 Independent
Evaluation Group (IEG) report found that US companies
donated USD 2.23 billion to charitable causes, represent -
ing a 4.6% increase over 2018 [24]. A similar study in
2020 by IBM revealed that 40% of consumers prioritise
companies that share their values, like sustainability [31].
Additionally, the growing customer demand for CSR has
been spurred by the incorporation of CRM as a critical
instrument in marketing communication [3]. Despite
its significant theoretical and practical implications for
enhancing persuasion, marketing communications, and
CSR research, this emerging area has received relatively
little scholarly attention [8]. Consequently, there is a defi-
ciency in the thorough understanding of the progression
of CRM literature over time.
The following are some ways in which the study signifi -
cantly expands upon the existing body of CRM literature.
First, there is a dearth of thorough review publications
providing new perspectives on the CRM field [43, 80],
with existing reviews primarily focusing on summarising
study findings. The timing of this review study is ideal,
given the dynamic nature of CRM and the expanding
body of research on CRM. To ensure its relevance and
openness compared to previous evaluations [95], this
study thoroughly reviewed bibliometric data on CRM
from 1988 to 2024. Second, the application of bibliomet -
ric analysis is relatively new in business research [10, 21,
37, 92]. Third, Previous bibliometric studies on CRM
have several limitations that the present study aims to
address. For instance, the study by Singh and Pathak [71]
relied solely on the Web of Science (WOS) database. In
contrast, this study utilised the Scopus database to pro -
vide a broader and more diverse dataset [73]. Another
study by Singh and Dhir [70] also limited their bib -
liometric analysis to 61 articles by using only three key -
words: “cause marketing” , “cause-related marketing” , and
“social marketing” . On the other hand, the present study
employs six keywords identified in past studies by Singh
and Pathak [71] and Zhang et  al. [94] to ensure a more
comprehensive analysis of the CRM literature. Research
by Bhatti et  al. [11] also utilised only two keywords:
“cause marketing” , and “cause-related marketing” , and this
resulted in a limited scope.
Furthermore, the study by Patil and Rahman [58]
neglected citation analysis, a critical component of bib -
liometric research, and concentrated only on co-citation
and bibliographic coupling. This study combines citation
analysis with various other bibliometric methodolo -
gies to provide more methodological depth. Addition -
ally although CRM was first introduced in 1988, Pandey
and Bajpai [55] only examined articles from 2000 to 2020,
thereby leaving some crucial studies from before and
after that period unexamined. The present research pro -
vides a more thorough temporal analysis by including
papers from 1988 to 2024, thereby filling this gap.
This study addresses the above gaps by exclusively
leveraging the Scopus database for an exhaustive bib -
liometric examination of CRM research. Moreover, only
papers classified under the highest categories—namely,
A*, A, and B—of the Australian Business Deans Council
(ABDC) in the Scopus classification system have been
included to ensure a rigorous selection process. Journals
under these categories are typically considered prestig -
ious and reputable within the academic community. They
have a larger readership and higher impact factors than
lower-tier journals because they usually have stringent
peer-review processes, ensuring that only high-quality
research is published. Additionally, several reasons led to
the selection of Scopus over other well-known databases,
such as the Web of Science (WOS). First, compared to
WOS, Scopus has a bigger database and indexes around
20% more articles [23, 73]. Second, according to Singh
et  al. [72], Scopus provides more comprehensive meta -
data, especially concerning author information, which
is essential for an in-depth bibliometric study. Third, 1.7
billion citations are accessible via Scopus, which margin -
ally outnumbers the 1.6 billion citations on WOS [23,
88]. Additionally, combining both Scopus and WOS was
not pursued due to the potential for data duplication,
inconsistent metadata formats, and notable differences in
journal indexing practices. These elements may make it
more challenging to ensure data dependability, correct -
ness, and consistency throughout the analysis.
Furthermore, Scopus offers enough depth and breadth
for an extensive bibliometric analysis due to its richer
data fields and more exhaustive coverage [55]. Thus,
Scopus was considered the most suitable and effective
option for this study. This strategic approach offers a
nuanced exploration of CRM research. It contributes to
the broader scholarly discourse by expanding the scope
of databases for bibliometric analyses in this domain.
The study utilises VOSviewer, developed by Van Eck and
Waltman [83], to analyse the available literature.
The current study responds to the following research
questions:
RQ1. What is the CRM publishing trend?

made the most impactful contributions to the CRM
literature?

Background of CRM
The evolving dynamics of global markets, prompted by
increasing globalisation and shifting marketing para -
digms, compel businesses to embrace strategies that con -
fer them a competitive edge [87]. According to Woodroof
et al. [90], a significant driving force behind this revolu -
tionary environment is the rise of socially conscious con -
sumers, who expect businesses to assume greater social
responsibilities. This pattern demonstrates the expansion
of CSR programmes into international markets (Singh
et al., 2022). CRM is one modern channel through which
businesses promote their CSR initiatives [12].
Over the past 2 decades, the idea of CRM has emerged
as a marketing strategy adopted by numerous Indian
companies [81]. CRM began when American Express
launched its first CRM campaign in 1983 to rebuild
the Statue of Liberty [71]. Varadarajan and Menon [85]
defined CRM as “marketing activity that is character -
ised by an offer from the firm to contribute a specified
amount towards a designated cause when consumers get
involved in revenue-providing exchanges” (p. 60). This
manifests that CRM is transactional. Later on, CRM is
also defined by highlighting the importance of nonprofit
organisations (NPO) in the definition, “CRM is the crea -
tion and execution of marketing strategies that involve
making a predetermined contribution to a designated
nonprofit initiative, thereby encouraging consumers to
participate in revenue-generating transactions” [52], p.
42). Furthermore, “CRM is a tripartite relationship aimed
at achieving a mutually beneficial outcome for the com -
pany, nonprofit organisation (NPO), and the customer”
[1], p. 9).
According to Lafferty et al. [43], CRM has been rede -
fined as “a marketing endeavour aimed at raising aware -
ness of societal issues through cause promotions and
related advertising initiatives” (p. 952). This has led to
increased adoption of CRM campaigns in recent years.
These days, campaigns extend beyond simply encourag -
ing people to purchase products, they also engage them
in activities that enable them to contribute directly to the
related cause [ 14, 43]. For example, Myntra, an Indian
online fashion store, launched a week-long “Fashion
Upgrade” sale, encouraging participants to give their used
clothing to the nonprofit organisation Goonj in exchange
for reward points for future purchases [22]. Similarly,
KFC India launched the “Hope” campaign, which lets
users design their own “Plate of Hope” online. For every
plate created, food is donated to needy children [30].
Furthermore, many CRM campaigns employ social
media to encourage user participation. This is typically
done by asking users to “like” or “share” CRM adverts,
which increases the campaign’s visibility and impact [43,
71]. These contemporary approaches to CRM highlight
the need for further exploration of framing variables by
researchers in this field.
Previous studies have mentioned various antecedents
and consequences of CRM [24, 34]. While numerous fac-
tors impacting the success of CRM have been scrutinised
[27], the predominant focus in the literature revolves
around the examination of the attitude of customers [35,
66, 79] and their purchase intentions [11, 39, 68, 75].
Mobarak et al. [50] and Hajikarimi and Omeyr [38] found
a positive correlation between CRM and brand image.
Researchers have also found the role of cause–brand fit
[40, 49], product type [5], cause involvement [4, 57], and
cause type [41] on CRM effectiveness. Despite increased
academic focus, few studies have been done to identify
the factors influencing CRM effectiveness [27].
The current evaluation aims to make CRM informa -
tion accessible to a broader research audience by synthe -
sising 30  years of CRM literature into a comprehensive
assessment. Through a bibliometric analysis of the CRM
literature from 1988 to 2024, as identified in the Scopus
database, this study aims to address existing research
gaps by illuminating the recent and expanding corpus of
the literature in this rapidly growing field.
Bibliometric research method
Determine the relevant search word
Following the recent reviews by Singh and Pathak [71]
and Zhang et  al. [94], a comprehensive range of search
terms was developed to extract relevant CRM literature.
The final Scopus search string used was in:
Title-abstract-keywords “Cause related marketing”
OR “Cause-related marketing” OR “Cause marketing”
OR “Cause-brand alliance” OR “Charity-linked brand”
OR”Product charity bundle” .
This string was applied using Scopus’s search inter -
face, restricted to articles and reviews published in Eng -
lish between 1988 and 2024 across relevant subject areas
(refer Fig. 1).
Acquiring data
Data have been extracted from the Scopus database,
which is known for its extensive collection of peer-
reviewed articles published in high-impact factor jour -
nals that employ a double-blind review method [33].
A systematic approach is used to reach the final stage,
involving 425 articles. The keywords “Cause related

The data were cleaned using a multi-step refinement
approach to ensure accuracy and reliability. First, the
extracted dataset was manually reviewed to verify the
accuracy of journal names, author details, and affilia -
tions. Next, duplicate articles were removed using their
unique DOI, ensuring each publication was represented
only once. This method maintained data integrity and
prevented biases in the analysis. Furthermore, articles
were filtered post-cleaning to retain only those that met
the A*, A, and B category journal ranking criterion from
the 2022 ABDC Journal Quality List. However, upon fil -
tering the results, we found that no articles were pub -
lished in A*, A, and B category journals between 1988
and 1991, and the earliest relevant publication appeared
in 1992. Therefore, the final dataset includes articles pub-
lished from 1992 onward.
Selecting the analysis method
This research used VOSviewer, a software developed by
Van Eck and Waltman [83] at Leiden University, for bib -
liometric analysis and knowledge mapping. VOSviewer is
renowned for its ability to visualise and analyse various
bibliometric networks, including publications, authors,
journals, institutions, and countries [83]. The software is
particularly advantageous for theme mapping and clus -
ter analysis, enabling researchers to identify clusters of
similar items within the dataset, which aligns well with
the study’s aim of elucidating the conceptual framework
of CRM research.
A primary advantage of VOSviewer is its ability to
group similar items based on co-occurrence or co-
citation patterns [17]. The clusters are displayed using
colour-coding to distinguish themes, with node size
indicating prominence in the network,larger nodes rep -
resent more significant and often cited works [82]. This
capability enables researchers to identify core themes
and pivotal studies, making VOSviewer a crucial tool for
mapping the evolution of research fields over time [36,
45]. VOSviewer is preferred over other bibliometric tools
due to its intuitive interface and powerful visualisation
capabilities, which enable the generation of clear, inter -
pretable maps that illuminate emerging trends and the
development of specific research domains [48].
Several bibliometric analytic methods have been
applied to understand the notion of CRM research. Apart
from its capacity to manage extensive datasets [21], it
identifies publication patterns, recognises emerging top -
ics, and creates visual representations of thematic evolu -
tion [91]. This enables the examination of past research
and the prediction of future research areas [15, 19, 59].
Several bibliometric analytic techniques, including cita -
tion analysis, bibliographic coupling, co-citation, and co-
occurrence analysis, were employed in the study.
Findings
Evaluation of performance
The most productive year was 2020, with 56 Publica -
tions (see Fig.  2). The very first paper on CRM was writ -
ten by Vardharajan and Menon in 1988. However, the
journey starts with Barnes and Fitzgibbons [6 ] and Ross
et al. [66]. In 2013, the publication in CRM reached 20
articles for the first time (see Fig.  2). This is due to the
growing global emphasis on CSR and sustainability,
which is likely to drive the rise in CRM publications.
The approaching 2015 deadline for the UN’s Millen -
nium Development Goals (MDGs) amplified global

Additionally, rising consumer demand for socially

Numerous pivotal research studies were published in
2020 that significantly influenced the discourse and gar -

[27] performed a cross-country investigation linking
CRM to customer purchase intention, which has since
been cited over 80 times. Likewise, Christofi et al. [14]
investigated customer empowerment and the efficacy of
choice in CRM efforts. Tanford et al. [77] analysed the
impact of social media framing and message congruity
on perception, while Coleman et  al. [16] explored the
emotional responses of pride and guilt in CSR. Chang
and Chu [13] advanced the donation framing research
by examining the efficacy of incentive-based CRM.
The diversity and volume of these studies indicate that
2020 was not only quantitatively rich but also themati -
cally expansive, touching on psychological, behavioural,
cultural, and technological dimensions. Therefore, the
year’s productivity is both contextually and academically
justified, marking a pivotal moment in CRM research
development.

Leading journals in the CRM field
The Journal of Business Ethics, with 1884 citations and 18
articles, stands out as the prominent journal in the realm
of CRM research (refer Table 2). Recognised for its focus
on ethical business practices, stakeholder involvement,
and corporate responsibility, it has consistently pub -
lished significant CRM research that examines the con -
gruence between company principles and societal issues.
Its extensive conceptual range and multidisciplinary
attraction establish it as a seminal journal for research -
ers engaged at the convergence of marketing, ethics, and
strategic management.
The Journal of Advertising has 1,496 citations, derived
from eight publications, and ranks second (refer Table 2).
The journal is esteemed for disseminating experimental
and psychological research that examines the framing of
CRM messages, customer emotional responses, and the
impact of advertising congruity on perceived sincerity. Its
contributions have facilitated the establishment of criti -
cal insights into message design, emotional persuasion,
and customer perceptions of authenticity in CRM.
Similarly, the Journal of Business Research ranks third
with 1,322 citations (refer Table 2). It is recognised for its
empirical emphasis and methodological variety, includ -
ing survey-based investigations, experimental research,
and model-driven analysis. It has included CRM studies
related to donation framing, consumer skepticism, brand
loyalty, and cultural adaptation, making it a key contribu-
tor to the empirical development of the field.

Knowledge foundation of CRM research
In co-citation analysis, VOSviewer identifies pairs of doc-
uments that are frequently cited together by other docu -
ments. The frequency with which two documents are
co-cited indicates their relatedness. By analysing co-cita -
tion patterns  (refer Figure  3), VOSviewer helps uncover
the intellectual structure of a research field and identify
seminal works [51, 83].
According to Donthu et  al. [21] and Mukherjee
et  al. [51], bibliometric analysis often applies citation
thresholds to identify influential works that have signifi -
cantly shaped a field’s intellectual development. In this
study, a threshold of 10 local citations was used to con -
struct the co-citation map. This setting ensured the inclu-
sion of both seminal contributions and relatively recent
yet impactful studies, allowing for a comprehensive rep -
resentation of core research areas in the CRM discourse.
The analysis reveals three major clusters in red, blue, and
green colours (refer Figure 3).
Cluster 1, in red, represents a dense central network
of CRM literature rooted in consumer response, skepti -
cism, and campaign design. Prominent scholars include
Barone, M.J., Miyazaki, A.D., Pracejus, J.W., Lafferty b.a,
Guerreiro j., and Olsen, G.D., whose work has focused on
the experimental evaluation of CRM campaigns, message
effectiveness, attribution theory, and persuasion mecha -
nisms. This cluster encompasses the evaluative and psy -
chological foundations of CRM research.
Cluster 2, in green, highlights strategic and conceptual
foundations. Prominent authors in this context are Vara -
darajan P .R., Menon A., Bhattacharya C.B., Sen S., Brønn
P .S., Drumwright M.E., and Broderick A. These studies
highlight the importance of strategic positioning, stake -
holder interactions, and integrating CSR and marketing
philosophy into CRM frameworks. This cluster repre -
sents a macro-theoretical perspective, emphasising busi -
ness models and the consequences of cause alignment at
the company level.
Cluster 3, in blue, includes researchers such as Dacin
P .A., Brown T.J., Ellen P .S., Yoon Y., Vlachos P .A., and
Gurhan canli Z., who concentrate on consumer-brand
relationships, attitude development, and the impact of
CRM on brand equity. These studies often investigate
brand trust, value congruence, and identity-based assess -
ments, therefore enhancing a relational marketing per -
spective in CRM research.
Importantly, authors such as Barone M.J., Varadarajan
P .R., and Drumwright M.E. appear as central nodes, act -
ing as intellectual bridges between clusters. Their works
are co-cited across both theoretical and applied litera -
ture, highlighting their cross-cutting influence in unify -
ing CRM’s behavioural and strategic domains.

Research implications
Theoretical implications
This research enhances the theoretical understanding of
CRM by providing an extensive bibliometric analysis of
the area spanning over three decades. The study reveals
the intellectual structure and theme progression of CRM
research through advanced techniques, including co-
citation analysis, bibliographic coupling, and keyword
co-occurrence analysis. The delineation of five funda -
mental theme clusters CRM and consumers, consumer
skepticism and CRM, CSR and CRM, donation and
CRM, and cause–brand fit and CRM—offers a systematic
framework for future academics to explore and expand
upon the current body of knowledge. The study also
highlights the interconnectedness of theoretical founda -
tions with applied contributions, primarily through the
bridging roles of key authors such as Vardharajan and
Menon, Strahilevitz M., and Barone M.J. Moreover, the
overlay visualisation provides significant insights into the
temporal evolution of the discipline, demonstrating how
prior conceptual frameworks such as CSR have progres -
sively led to the emergence of contemporary, multidisci -
plinary concerns like social media impact, religion, and
skepticism. This layered temporal and thematic analysis
aids in theory formation by identifying gaps and emerg -
ing notions that need further empirical exploration.
The study provides a conceptual foundation for future
CRM research by clarifying the evolution of academic
discourse, its current status, and promising avenues for
scholarly investigation.
Practical implications
This research provides essential information for market -
ers, business strategists, charitable organisations, and
public policy advocates involved in cause-related mar -
keting efforts. The analysis highlights the evolution of
CRM from a peripheral branding tool to a fundamental
element of value-driven, consumer-focused marketing
strategies. Terms like “social media” , “advertising” , “ AI” ,
and “donation magnitude” indicate that digital tools
and data analytics are progressively influencing modern
CRM campaigns, necessitating marketers to use technol -
ogy-driven customisation in their outreach efforts. The
emergence of themes such as “skepticism” , “religiosity” ,
and “prosocial behaviour” indicates increased consumer
vigilance about authenticity and ethical congruence. This
shows that for CRM efforts to be successful, firms must
guarantee openness in cause selection, consistency in
message, and alignment with the fundamental values of
their target audience. The study’s results underscore the
significance of strategic cause–brand alignment and the
capacity of CSR to improve brand image, loyalty, and
consumer engagement when executed authentically. For
practitioners, bibliometric trends offer a reflection of
industry direction and provide evidence-based guidance
for aligning marketing tactics with evolving consumer
expectations and social norms. Consequently, the study
serves as a decision-support tool for formulating CRM
initiatives that appeal to contemporary consumers while
generating societal benefits and a competitive advantage.
Limitations
The following are the main consequences of doing this
bibliometric review. First, by employing a network analy -
sis approach, researchers can identify key research works
and top authors, as well as the primary areas of focus
within the CRM study. Second, the review offers insights
into the field’s evolution and identifies emerging areas
of research, guiding future research endeavours. Third,
to the best of our knowledge, no bibliometric study has
been conducted on CRM in A*, A, and B category jour -
nals of the Scopus database. The current research has
notable implications for a range of stakeholders, includ -
ing academicians and practitioners, among others. From
a theoretical standpoint, the research furnishes research -
ers with insights into the current landscape of CRM
scholarship and potential directions for future research.
Every research has some sort of limitations that open
the scope for future research. Only the Scopus database
has been used in the present study for bibliometric anal -
ysis. Future studies may utilise other databases, such as
Web of Science (WoS), or combine different databases.
The present study utilises peer-reviewed articles and
review papers as its primary document types. Future
