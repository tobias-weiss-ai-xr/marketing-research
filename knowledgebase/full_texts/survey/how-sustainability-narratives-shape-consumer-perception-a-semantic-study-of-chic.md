Purpose. This study examines how semantic cues in food marketing, particularly
sustainability-oriented narratives, influence consumer perceptions of chicken meat.
Methodology. The study was based on a survey of 128 consumers conducted in the retail
chains Silpo, ATB, and MIDA in Mykolaiv, Ukraine. Respondents assessed various semantic
configurations of chicken meat labelling, which differed by script (Latin versus Cyrillic), language
(Ukrainian versus English), and the inclusion or omission of the “ECO” prefix. Evaluations were
provided using a five -point scale. The analysis examined the extent to which these semantic
variations shape consumer perceptions acr oss six key dimensions: perceived product quality, taste
expectations, purchase appeal, social prestige, environmental friendliness, and perceived support
for local communities.
Results. The findings indicate that consumer perceptions of chicken meat in Uk raine are
strongly influenced by visual and semantic elements embedded in product naming, particularly
language choice, script type, and the inclusion of sustainability -related markers. The study
highlights the significant role of sustainable development n arratives in shaping product image.
Empirical evidence confirms that the “ECO” prefix serves as a powerful signal, reinforcing
associations with environmental responsibility and social value, while at the same time potentially
prompting more critical consu mer judgment. Furthermore, product names that reflect national
linguistic identity generate greater trust and a stronger sense of authenticity compared to foreign -
language or transliterated alternatives. In general, the findings suggest that the incorporat ion of
sustainability-oriented semantic cues into food marketing strategies can strengthen consumer trust
and contribute to building a positive brand image within the Ukrainian chicken meat market.
Originality. This study provides empirical evidence of how  semantic patterns, specifically
language choice, script, and the “ECO” prefix, shape consumer perceptions of chicken meat in
Ukraine. The study identifies the role of eco -labelling in influencing expectations of quality, taste,
and social responsibility, highlighting both positive and critical consumer responses. It also
demonstrates that semantic cues not only affect purchasing behaviour, but also contribute to the
formation of sustainability-related narratives and consumer trust in local food production.

1. INTRODUCTION
In recent decades, food marketing has undergone profound transformations,
reflecting broader societal shifts toward sustainability, ethical consumption, and
responsible production. Narratives that emphasise environmental sustainability, social
responsibility, and long -term well -being are increasingly replacing traditional
strategies, which once prioriti sed maximi sing consumption through persuasive
imagery and price competition . This transition is closely connected with the global
movement toward a green eco nomy, as well as with the adoption of sustainable
development goals that encourage businesses to integrate ecological and social
considerations into their operations and communication strategies.
Within this context, the semantic dimension of marketing ( how meanings,
symbols, and narratives a re constructed and communicated) has become a critical
factor in shaping consumer perceptions and behaviours. Semantic approaches do not
simply trans mit information about a product,  they frame its identity by evoking
associations with quality, authenticity, cultural values, or ecological responsibility.
For food products in particular, where purchasing decisions are strongly influenced
by trust, sensory expectations, and ethical considerations, semantic cues such as
language, labels, and imagery can substantially impact consumer choices.
The Ukrainian market provides a distinctive setting for exploring these dynamics.
On the one hand, it is marked by a strong cultural attachment to local food traditions
and cautious attitudes toward foreign branding, shaped in part by historical experiences
such as the so -called “Bush legs” phenomenon of the early 1990s. On the other hand,
Ukrainian consumers are increasingly exposed to global sustainability discourses and
European integration processes, which promote environmentally friendly production
and socially responsible consumption. This tension between local identity and global
narratives makes Ukraine an important case study for examining how semantic
approaches influence consumer responses to sustainability-oriented marketing.
Particularly relevant in this context is the marketing of chicken meat, one of the
most widely consumed protein sources in Ukraine. As producers seek to strengthen
competitiveness and align with sustainability goals, the choice of language, script,
and labels ( such as the widely used prefix “ECO”) becomes a decisive factor in
shaping product image and purchase intent. However, despite growing academic
attention to semantic strategies in global food marketing, research focusing on their
role in the Ukrainian agricultural sector remains limited.
Addressing this gap, the present study evaluates how sustainable development

2. LITERATURE REVIEW
2.1. Semantic cues and consumer perception in food marketing
Recent research in food marketing increasingly emphasi ses the role of semantic
cues in shaping consumer perception and behaviour. Semantic elements such as
wording, labelling, visual presentation, and narrative framing influence how consumers
interpret product attributes, including quality, taste, and a uthenticity. Several studies
[1–4] demonstrate that these cues act as signals that help consumers evaluate products
under conditions of limited information. For instance, research [1] highlights the
importance of message consistency and shows that handwritten fonts and first -person
narratives e nhance perceived authenticity and trust. Similarly, [2] finds that the
integration of cultural identity into packaging design significantly strengthens
consumer appeal and supports the positioning of traditional food products.
In addition to visual and lin guistic elements, studies on food labelling and
descriptive wording indicate that semantic framing directly affects consumer
expectations prior to consumption. Research [3] demonstrates that evocative and
sensory-rich descriptions can influence both percei ved taste and actual purchasing
decisions. This is supported by findings in [4], which suggest that the strategic use of
semantic cues enhances brand differentiation and facilitates consumer decision -
making in competitive markets. Moreover, concept mapping  approaches [5] reveal
that consumers form complex associative networks around food products, linking
them to ideas of naturalness, quality, and origin.
At the same time, emerging research highlights the growing importance of non -
verbal and paralinguistic elements in shaping consumer perception. For example, [6]
introduces the concept of brand paralinguistic, demonstrating how elements such as
emoji contribute to communicating brand personality and emotional tone in digital
environments. Similarly, studi es on multisensory marketing [7 ] show that auditory
signals can shape expectations and influence product evaluation, further expanding
the scope of semantic influence beyond traditional textual cues.
Despite these contributions, much of the existing literature focuses on general
food categories, organic products, or plant-based alternatives [8 –10]. While these
studies provide valuable insights into consumer p erception, they often overlook
conventional food products, particularly meat, where semantic framing may operate
differently due to cultural, ethical, and sensory factors. This indicates a need for more

2.2. Sustainability narratives and trust in food markets
The growing importance of sustainability in food systems has led to increased
scholarly attention to sustainability -oriented narratives in marketing com munication.
These narratives emphasi se environmental responsibility, ethical production, and
social impact, shaping consumer attitudes and influencing purchasing behaviour.
Studies [11–18] demonstrate that sustainability-related messaging enhances perceived
product value, strengthens brand trust, and aligns consumption with consumers’
ethical and environmental concerns. In particular, the use of eco -labels and references
to natural origin has been shown to positively affect purchase intentions, espe cially
among environmentally conscious consumers.
At the same time, research suggests that consumer responses to sustainability
narratives are complex and someti mes ambivalent. For example, [19 –22] highlight
that consumer attitudes are shaped by multiple, and occasionally conflicting, factors,
including taste preferences, health considerations, and ethical values. Similarly, [ 23]
shows that individuals with strong sustainability beliefs may reduce their meat
consumption altogether, indicating that sustainab ility messaging in the meat sector
may produce both positive and critical reac tions. Furthermore, studies [ 24–27] reveal
that marketing strategies in the meat industry often rely on framing techniques that
normalise consumption while downplaying environmen tal and ethical concerns,
illustrating the strategic role of communication in shaping consumer perception.
Another important dimension concerns the role of local identity and cultural
context in building trust. Research [ 28] indicates that consumers tend to associate
local products with authenticity, quality, and social responsibility, while foreign or
standardised branding may evoke lower levels of trust. This is particularly relevant in
emerging markets, where historical experiences and cu ltural memory can influence
perceptions of food products. In this context, semantic cues such as language choice
and script may serve as markers of origin, reinforcing or weakening consumer trust.
Despite the growing body of literature on sustainability an d food marketing,
limited attention has been paid to the interaction between sustainability narratives and
semantic framing, particularly in the context of specific product categories and
regional markets. In the Ukrainian context, where consumer perceptio n is shaped by
both local traditions and exposure to global sustainability discourses, this gap
becomes especially significant. Existing studies [ 11; 29] highlight the importance of
semantic cues in shaping perceptions of environmental friendliness and soc ial
responsibility; however, empirical research focusing on how these mechanisms
operate in the chicken meat market remains scarce.
2.3. Consumer decision-making and sustainability communication
Contemporary research on consumer behaviour in the meat market reveals a
complex interaction between traditional dietary preferences and emerging
sustainability-oriented values. In particular, studies conducted across different
countries indicate a persist ent attachment to meat as a core component of diets,

shows that consumers simultaneously express openness to plant -based alternatives
while maintaining strong emotional and cultural ties to meat consumption. Similar
conclusions are drawn by the researchers  [31], whose segmentation of game meat
consumers identifies diverse motivational profiles ranging from tradition -oriented to
novelty-seeking behaviour. These findings suggest that consumer behaviour in the

At the same time, economic and behavioural approaches emphasi se the

research [33] show s that purchase intentions for meat -based products are strongly

together, these findings indicate that alt hough sustainability considerations are
becoming increasingly relevant, traditional determinants of demand remain central,
albeit increasingly complemented by symbolic and communicative dimensions.
A distinct line of research focuses on the role of labelli ng, informational cues,
and sensory perception in shaping product evaluation. The authors [34] demonstrate
that the concept of “clean label”  significantly influences both sensory perception and
consumer understanding of the product, while the authors  [35], using eye -tracking
analysis, show that labelling elements determine the focus of consumer attention
during product selection. Furthermore, the research  [36] confirm s that local origin
and organic attributes increase consumers’ willingness to pay, highligh ting the
importance of combining informational and value -based signals. In this context,
semantic elements such as product naming, language, and labelling can be considered
key carriers of meaning that shape perceptions of quality and safety.
A growing body of literature also explores the impact of sustainability narratives
and communication strategies on consumer decision -making. The authors  [37] find
that consumers increasingly consider sustainability aspects when choosing meat
products, although these factors are not always decisive. In turn, the researchers [38]
demonstrate that storytelling about sustainable production significantly enhances
product acceptance, preference, and satisfaction. Similarly, the study [39] highlights
the role of message framing in shaping attitudes toward alternative food products.
These findings suggest that communication strategies based on narratives not only
inform consumers but also actively shape their perceptions and preferences.
In parallel, research on emerging food technologies highlights shifting consumer
attitudes in the context of sustainability transitions. Studies by the scientists [40; 41]
reveal ambiv alent consum er perceptions of cultured meat . The authors  [42] further
emphasise that social norms and dietary identity play a crucial role in determining
consumers’ willingness to adopt such innovations. This suggests that even
technologically advanced foo d solutions require effective semantic and
communicative framing to gain broader acceptance.

2.5. Research hypotheses on semantic framing and sustainability narratives
in Ukrainian chicken meat marketing
Based on the analysis of prior studies [1 –5; 8; 16 –20], several research
hypotheses were developed regarding the specific characteristics of the semantic
approach in food marketing, with a focus on how sustainable development narratives
influence the promotion of agricultural products in the Ukrainian market :
H1: The widespread integration of environmental narratives  into public
discourse, driven by the European Union’s green policy agenda and adopted by
associated member states, including Ukraine, significantly shapes consumer
behaviour. Within this context, the use of the prefix “ECO” strengthens the semantic
link b etween sustainability -oriented meanings and the perceived image of chicken
meat, making it more appealing and trustworthy compared to cases where such a
prefix is not employed.
H2: The original English name of chicken meat, or its transliteration into
Cyrillic, may evoke more negative expectations among consumers regarding quality,
taste, sustainability, or its impact on local community development.
H3: Consumers may assign lower or  more critical evaluations to environmental
and social dimensions (ES, CD) for products labelled with the “ECO” prefix
compared to non-ECO alternatives.
The research was additionally designed to address the following questions:
RQ1: Which elements of the chicken meat image show the strongest alignment
with each of the visual-semantic patterns under analysis?
RQ2: Does the presence of the prefix “ECO” change the priority of the
association of the image of the food product with the visual -semantic patterns
included in the analysis?

semantic images. The study examined these perceptions through the lens of
expectations related to product quality (using chicken meat as a case), taste, purchase
appeal, social status, as well as environmental and communit y impact.

5. DISCUSSION
The results of the consumer survey enabled the verification of the proposed
hypotheses and provided clear answers to the research questions . The results of this
study provide empirical confirmation that semantic elements in food marketing
significantly influence consumer perception, particularly when these elements are
aligned with sustainability narratives. The findings demonstrate that the use of the
“ECO” prefix, together with linguistic and script choices, plays a decisiv e role in
shaping associations related to product quality, environmental safety, and social
responsibility. At the same time, the results reveal a more nuanced and sometimes
contradictory nature of consumer responses, which requires deeper interpretation.
First, the strong positive effect of sustainability -oriented semantic cues
identified in this study is consistent with prior research emphasi sing the importance
of ethical and ecological messaging in food marketing [4; 16–20; 31–33]. Similar to
earlier fin dings, the integration of sustainability narratives enhances consumer trust
and purchase intention. In particular, the present study confirms that eco -labelling
strengthens associations with environmental responsibility and product quality, which
aligns wi th studies demonstrating that sustainability claims positively affect
consumer evaluations and willingness to buy.
However, this study extends existing knowledge by revealing that the “ECO”
prefix does not universally improve all perception dimensions. Whi le it increases
trust, attractiveness, and perceived environmental safety, it may also lead to more
critical consumer evaluations, especially regarding the product’s broader social and
environmental impact. This finding partially supports the conclusions o f studies [11;

Agricultural and Resource Economics: International Scientific E-Journal
https://are-journal.com
Vol.  12, No.  2, 20 26 74 ISSN 2414-584X
12], which highlight the complexity and potential ambivalence of consumer attitudes
shaped by multiple, sometimes conflicting factors such as health, ethics, and risk
perception. Thus, the observed paradox , where eco -labelled products are
simultaneously trusted more but also evaluated more critically , adds an important
contribution to the existing literature.
Second, the results demonstrate a clear preference for semantic patterns that
reflect national linguistic identity, particularly those using Ukrainian-language names
in Cyrillic script. These findings are in line with studies [2; 5], which emphasi se the
importance of cultural authenticity and local identity in strengthening consumer trust
and product appeal. At the same time, the negative  or weaker perception of English -
language or transliterated names supports the hypothesis related to the lasting
influence of historical and cultural memory.
Third, the study confirms that semantic framing operates as a multidimensional
mechanism influencing not only individual product attributes (such as taste or
quality) but also broader social constructs, including perceived contribution to local
communities. This supports the conclusions of studies [9; 41 –42], which argue that
effective food marketing increasingly relies on integrating economic, social, and
environmental narratives. The relatively strong association between perceived
product quality and cont ribution to local development identified in this research
highlights the growing importance of socially responsible branding in shaping
consumer behaviour.
At the same time, some findings diverge from existing research. For example,
while previous studies [3; 13 –15] emphasise the strong role of sensory and
descriptive cues in shaping taste expectations, this study shows that taste perception
is less influenced by the “ECO” prefix and more strongly associated with familiar
linguistic forms. This suggests tha t, in the Ukrainian context, cultural and linguistic
familiarity may outweigh abstract sustainability cues when it comes to sensory
expectations. Such divergence underscores the importance of considering regional
and cultural specificity in the application of semantic marketing strategies.
The key advantage of this research lies in its empirical approach to analysing
semantic patterns in food marketing. Unlike many previous studies that focus
primarily on general sustainability communication or isolated lab elling effects, this
study simultaneously examines multiple semantic dimensions , such as language,
script, and eco -labelling, and their combined influence on consumer perception.
Furthermore, it introduces the perspective of cultural memory as a determinan t of
semantic interpretation, thereby enriching the theoretical understanding of how
meanings are constructed in transitional markets such as Ukraine.
In terms of contribution to the literature, this study fills a gap regarding the
limited number of empiri cal studies on the application of semantic approaches in
food marketing within the Ukrainian agricultural sector. By providing quantitative
evidence on how semantic cues influence consumer perception, the research
strengthens the methodological and empirical foundation of this emerging field and
contributes to its further development. From a practical perspective, the findings offer

several important implications. Food producers and marketers should carefully
consider the interacti on between sustainability narratives and cultural -linguistic
elements when designing product names and labels. The use of the “ECO” prefix can
be an effective tool for enhancing trust and perceived environmental value, but it
should be supported by credibl e information and consistent branding to avoid
consumer scepticism. Moreover, emphasi sing local identity through language and
culturally familiar naming can significantly strengthen consumer trust and product
attractiveness.
The present findings are broad ly consistent with previous international research
demonstrating the significant role of semantic cues in shaping food -related
perceptions and consumer decision -making. Studies conducted across European
markets have shown that linguistic elements, labels, and symbolic representations
influence consumers’ evaluations of food products beyond their objective
characteristics. For example, research among Italian wine consumers by G.  C. Piscè
et al. [4] demonstrated that semantic components of wine labels contrib ute to
perceived product value and purchasing intentions, while C.  C. Seitz and
J. Roosen [5] found that regional and cultural associations strongly influence
perceptions of Bavarian food products in Germany.
Despite its contributions, the study has severa l limitations. The sample size is
relatively small and geographically limited to the city of Mykolaiv, which may
restrict the generali sability of the results. In addition, the study focuses on a single
product category (chicken meat), which may not fully c apture the diversity of
consumer responses across different food segments. Future research should expand
the geographical scope, include a larger and more diverse sample, and explore
additional product categories, including plant -based alternatives. Furthe rmore,
qualitative methods could be employed to gain deeper insights into the cognitive
mechanisms underlying semantic perception.
An important contextual factor that should be considered when interpreting these
findings is the ongoing full-scale war in Ukraine. The present study was not designed
to directly assess the relative importance of sustainability -oriented narratives
compared to economic factors such as price, affordability, or household financial
constraints. Nevertheless, the wartime environment may influence consumer priorities
and decision -making processes. While the results indicate that sustainability -related
semantic cues, particularly the “ECO” prefix, continue to positively shape consumer
perceptions, it remains unclear whether sustainability considerations have become less
influential than economic concerns under conditions of prolonged uncertainty and
economic pressure. Future research should therefore expand the survey instrument to
include variables related to price sensitivity, purcha sing power, and wartime
behavioural adaptation in order to better understand how sustainability narratives
interact with economic and situational determinants of food choice.

6. CONCLUSIONS
The study confirms the relevance of the semantic approach in food marketing

and its significant influence on consumer perception. The first hypothesis was fully
supported, demonstrating that the integration of environmental narratives, shaped by
the Europe an Union’s sustainability agenda, has a measurable impact on consumer
behaviour. The use of the “ECO” prefix strengthens positive associations with
environmental safety and enhances product trustworthiness.
The second hypothesis received partial support. P roduct names using the
English term “CHICKEN” or its transliteration tend to evoke more negative
consumer perceptions compared to the local equivalent “KURKA,” particularly in
relation to environmental safety and the product’s perceived contribution to loc al
community development. At the same time, differences in perceived quality and taste
remain relatively minor, indicating that semantic effects are more pronounced in
sustainability-related dimensions than in sensory expectations.
The third hypothesis was  confirmed, showing that products labelled with the
“ECO” prefix are evaluated more critically in terms of the relationship between
environmental safety and community impact. This suggests a higher level of
consumer awareness and more complex evaluative cr iteria applied to environmentally
positioned products.
The correlation analysis revealed that the strength and structure of relationships
between key elements of product image, such as quality trust, taste expectations,
attractiveness, status, environmenta l safety, and community impact, vary depending
on the visual -semantic pattern. In particular, the “ECO” prefix alters the
configuration of these relationships, often strengthening links between environmental
and quality -related attributes while simultaneou sly reducing the coherence between
sustainability and social impact perceptions.
Overall, the findings indicate that semantic cues in product naming play a
crucial role in shaping consumer attitudes, especially in the context of sustainability.
The results  highlight the importance of culturally embedded language choices and
demonstrate that localised naming strategies may be more effective in fostering
positive perceptions than foreign or transliterated alternatives. These insights provide
a basis for furth er research into semantic marketing strategies in the agri -food sector,
particularly within the framework of sustainable development.

7. LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE RESEARCH
Although the study offers meaningful contributions, it is important to recogni se
several constraints that frame its outcomes. From a methodological perspective, the
analysis was grounded in a survey of 128 respondents, which, while adequate for
detecting general patterns, does not capture the full complexity of consumer
behaviour. The reliance on self -reported evaluations introduces the risk of subjective
bias, and the absence of experimental or longitudinal techniques limits the depth of
interpretation. Future investigations should therefore integrate a wider set of
methodological to ols to enhance both the reliability and explanatory power of the
results.
Data availability also posed certain restrictions, as the research relied

Agricultural and Resource Economics: International Scientific E-Journal
https://are-journal.com
Vol.  12, No.  2, 20 26 77 ISSN 2414-584X
exclusively on primary information gathered in selected retail chains within a single
region. Broader datase ts, including national -level consumer statistics, market
performance indicators, and comparative international evidence, would allow for
more comprehensive and generalisable insights.
The geographical coverage of the study represents another limitation. Focusing
only on the Mykolaiv region restricts the extent to which regional differences in
consumer culture and economic conditions are reflected in the findings. Extending
research to other parts of Ukraine and incorporating cross -country anal yses would
provide a richer understanding of how sustainable development narratives are
perceived across diverse contexts.
The temporal dimension of the research should also be considered. Conducted
within a short period , the study offers only a momentary view of consumer attitudes.
Perceptions of sustainability and branding are dynamic and may shift in response to
changing economic realities, global trends, and policy developments. Long -term
studies repeated over several years would provide a more accurate  picture of how
semantic approaches influence consumer choices over time.
In addition, the price dimension was not considered within the scope of this
study. While this allowed for a focused analysis of visual and semantic perceptions of
product attributes such as quality, safety, and attractiveness, it also limits the ability
to assess how economic factors interact with sustainability -oriented narratives in
shaping consumer preferences. Future research should incorporate price sensitivity
and affordability  indicators to provide a more holistic understanding of consumer
decision-making.
Despite these limitations, the value of the results remains significant. The
findings not only contribute to academic discourse but also have clear practical
relevance, offer ing a solid platform for further research  and serving as a useful
reference point for businesses and policymakers aiming to strengthen marketing
strategies through the integration of sustainability-oriented narratives.
