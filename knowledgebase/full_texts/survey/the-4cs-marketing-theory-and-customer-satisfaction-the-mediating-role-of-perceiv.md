As the digital economy expands, customer orientation has become increasingly critical to
enhancing satisfaction and maintaining competitiveness. However, limited research has
examined the application of the 4Cs Marketing Theory among China’s Specialized and New
small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs). This study examines the relationships among
consumption factors, perceived value, and customer satisfaction, with particular attention to
the potential mediating role of perceived value. Data were collected from 384 enterprise
customers of 38 Specialized and New SMEs in the information technology sector in
Shandong Province, China. Confirmatory factor analysis and structural equation modelling
were conducted using AMOS. The findings revealed significant posit ive associations
between consumption factors and customer satisfaction (β = 0.292), consumption factors
and perceived value (β = 0.478), and perceived value and customer satisfaction (β = 0.389).
The estimated indirect effect through perceived value (β = 0 .186) accounted for
approximately 38.91% of the total standardized effect, suggesting a potential partial
mediation mechanism that requires further statistical confirmation through bootstrap
confidence intervals. These findings contribute to the 4Cs Marketing Theory by highlighting
the importance of perceived value in customer satisfaction. The study recommends that
SMEs prioritize customer needs, cost efficiency, convenience, and effective communication
to strengthen perceived value and customer satisfaction.

1.0 INTRODUCTION
The rapid expansion of the digital economy has transformed business operations, customer expectations,
and how companies develop and maintain customer relationships (Li et al., 2023). The proliferation of
digital platforms, emerging technologies, and inter active communication channels has given customers
easier access to information about products and services. Consequently, customer expectations regarding
quality, convenience, responsiveness, and value have increased (Jo & Park, 2023). In response, businesses
are adopting customer -centric marketing strategies to adapt to evolving market conditions and maintain
competitiveness (Lubis, 2023; Alnoor et al., 2024).

In today's markets, understanding customer needs, managing the costs of purchasing and using products or
services, improving convenience, and maintaining clear communication are essential (Tamara et al., 2023).
Customer satisfaction remains central in marketing research because it reflects individuals’ evaluations of
their experiences with products, services, and organizations. These evaluations are influenced not only by
product or service attributes, but also by how customers assess the trade-off between benefits and sacrifices.
Perceived value provides a framework for understanding how customers evaluate commercial offerings and
determine their satisfaction (Purnomo, 2023; Alnoor et al., 2024). Integrating perc eived value with
customer-oriented marketing variables offers a more comprehensive explanation of customer satisfaction
than examining these factors in isolation.
The 4Cs Marketing Theory, introduced by Lauterborn (1990), emphasizes four key areas: customer needs,
cost, convenience, and communication. Unlike the traditional 4Ps framework, which centers on product,
price, place, and promotion, the 4Cs approach views marketing from the customer's perspective (Hu, 2023;
Ali & Ilyas, 20 16; Qian et al., 2023). These four dimensions help analyze how consumption factors
influence perceived value and customer satisfaction. These relationships are also significant for China's
"Specialized and New" small and medium -sized enterprises (SMEs), which emphasize specialization,
refinement, distinctiveness, and innovation.
In technology -driven markets, understanding how customers evaluate products, services, and business
interactions is essential for developing effective customer -focused strategies. However, the connections
among consumption factors, perceived value, and cus tomer satisfaction require further investigation,
particularly among enterprise customers of Specialized and New IT -sector SMEs in Shandong Province,
China. To address this gap, this study examines the interconnections among these variables and assesses
whether perceived value mediates the relationship between consumption factors and customer satisfaction
within the 4Cs Marketing Theory framework. Ultimately, the findings provide context -specific evidence
and practical recommendations to enhance customer -oriented marketing strategies and improve customer
satisfaction in specialized industries.
2.0 LITERATURE REVIEW
2.1 Theoretical Foundation
Multiple theoretical perspectives contribute to the understanding of customer satisfaction. Expectancy -
Disconfirmation Theory conceptualizes satisfaction as the outcome of comparing customer expectations
with actual experiences (Pizam & Milman, 1993; Elkhani & Bakri, 2012; Chatterjee & Suy, 2019; Chen et
al., 2022). Market -Oriented Theory emphasizes customer orientation, competitor analysis, and cross -
functional coordination (Alhakimi & Mahmoud, 2020). The 4Ps Marketing Theory addresses product,
price, place, and promotion as key marketing elements  (Xu et al.,2026). Collectively, these theories offer
complementary perspectives on how customers evaluate products and how marketing strategies function.
Lauterborn's (1990) 4Cs Marketing Theory serves as the study's primary framework, emphasizing customer
needs, cost, convenience, and communication (Hu, 2023; Qian et al., 2023).  The 4Cs framework
underscores the need to understand customer requirements, account for both financial and non -financial
costs, facilitate convenient transactions, and maintain clear communication (Wang, 2020; Xu, 2023; Durak
et al., 2023).

This customer-centric approach is especially relevant in today's digital business environment (Jo & Park,
2023; Alnoor et al., 2024). In this context, consumption factors are defined according to the four dimensions
of the 4Cs framework. This study examine s the relationships among these factors, perceived value, and
customer satisfaction, as well as perceived value's mediating role, among enterprise customers of
Specialized and New IT-sector small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) in Shandong Province, China.
2.2 Specialized and New SMEs in China
China’s “Specialized and New” small and medium -sized enterprises (SMEs) emphasize specialization,
refinement, distinctiveness, and innovation. These enterprises typically focus on niche markets,
technological advancement, and industrial competitiveness (Yidana et al., 2023). Within the digital business
environment, understanding customer needs, managing costs, enhancing convenience, and maintaining
effective communication are essential for strengthening customer relationships (Lubis, 2023; Li et al., 2023;
Jo & Park, 2023). These characteristics highlight the relevance of customer -oriented marketing strategies
in specialized industries. Accordingly, this study examines enterprise customers of Specialized and New
SMEs in the information technology sector in S handong Province, China, focusing on the relationships
among consumption factors, perceived value, and customer satisfaction.
2.3 Customer Satisfaction
Customer satisfaction refers to customers' evaluation of products and services based on their expectations
and actual experiences (Iqbal et al., 2023). Expectancy -Disconfirmation Theory posits that satisfaction
occurs when outcomes meet or exceed expectati ons, whereas dissatisfaction arises when experiences fall
short (Pizam & Milman, 1993; Elkhani & Bakri, 2012; Chatterjee & Suy, 2019). This study assesses
customer satisfaction using five dimensions: perceived performance, disconfirmation, willingness to
recommend, willingness to repurchase, and complaint handling. Established models such as SCSB, ACSI,
ECSI, and CCSI further indicate that perceived value significantly determines satisfaction. The study uses
customer satisfaction as a dependent variable and  examines its direct relationship with consumption in
enterprise-to-enterprise transactions among specialized and New IT-sector SMEs.
2.4 Consumption Factors
Consumption factors are customer -focused aspects that influence how people buy and use products or
services. Following Lauterborn's (1990) 4Cs Marketing Theory, this study conceptualizes consumption
factors in four dimensions: customer needs, cost, convenience, and communication (Hu, 2023; Qian et al.,
2023). Customer needs encompass brand image, service quality, and product quality (Wang, 2020; Wu &
Li, 2018). Cost includes monetary, time, physical, and psychological components (Nofrizal et al., 2023).
Convenience pertains to the ease of accessing, purchasing, or utilizing products and services (García -
Fernández et al., 2018; Josimovski et al., 2023; Al -Adwan & Yaseen, 2023). Communication involves
information clarity, relevance, emotional experience, and o rganizational responsiveness to customers
(Durak et al., 2023; Tamara et al., 2023). Collectively, these dimensions constitute the consumption-factors
framework examined in relation to perceived value and customer satisfaction.

2.5 Perceived Value
Perceived value refers to customers' evaluation of the benefits they receive relative to the sacrifices they
make when purchasing goods or services (Chen et al., 2017; Kim & Cho, 2016). This concept encompasses
not only price and quality, but also function al, social, and emotional dimensions of the consumption
experience (Pham et al., 2018; Blut et al., 2024). In the present study, perceived value is conceptualized as
comprising three components: functional value, which concerns the utility and effectiveness of the product
or service; social value, which pertains to social recognition or acceptance; and emotional value, which
relates to the positive feelings and enjoyment derived from use (Sweeney & Soutar, 2001; Kadhim et al.,
2021). Previous research has d emonstrated a positive association between perceived value and customer
satisfaction (Chen et al., 2017; Gumussoy & Koseoglu, 2016; Kim & Cho, 2016). Accordingly, this study
examines perceived value as a mediator between consumption -related variables and customer satisfaction
among enterprise customers of Specialized and New IT-sector small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs).
2.6 The Relationship Between Consumption Factors and Customer Satisfaction
The 4Cs Marketing Theory identifies customer needs, cost, convenience, and communication as key
components of customer -focused marketing (Lauterborn, 1990; Hu, 2023; Qian et al., 2023). Empirical
studies indicate that product and service quality, customer costs, purchasing convenience, and effective
communication are significantly associated with customer satisfaction (Wang, 2020; Rahman et al., 2023;
Ayinaddis et al., 2023; Chong et al., 2023; Negassa & Japee, 2023). These findings justify examining the
relationship between the four dimensions of the 4Cs framework and customer satisfaction.
H1: Consumption factors are positively and significantly associated with customer satisfaction.
2.7 The Relationship Between Consumption Factors and Perceived Value
Perceived value refers to customers' evaluation of the benefits they receive relative to the costs and
sacrifices they incur. Within the 4Cs framework, addressing customer needs, managing costs, enhancing
convenience, and ensuring effective communication i ncrease perceived value (Wang, 2020; Xu, 2023).
Additional research has linked customer -focused marketing strategies to perceived value (Juliana et al.,
2022; Ghorbani et al., 2023). Consequently, consumption factors are expected to be positively associate d
with perceived value.
H2: Consumption factors are positively associated with perceived value.
2.8 The Relationship Between Perceived Value and Customer Satisfaction
Perceived value critically determines customer satisfaction, as individuals evaluate products and services
based on perceived benefits and associated costs (Chen et al., 2017; Kim & Cho, 2016). Empirical studies
have demonstrated a positive relationship between perceived value and customer satisfaction across various
service and consumption contexts (Gumussoy & Koseoglu, 2016; Chen et al., 2017).  Functional, social,
and emotional values offer distinct perspectives on these evaluations (Sweeney & Soutar, 2001; Kadhim et
al., 2021). Consequently, higher perceived value is associated with increased customer satisfaction.
H3: Perceived value is positively and significantly associated with customer satisfaction.

2.9 The Mediating Role of Perceived Value
Perceived value goes beyond a direct relationship with customer satisfaction; it also clarifies how different
consumption factors link to satisfaction outcomes. Within the 4Cs framework, customer needs, cost,
convenience, and communication collectively shape the consumption experience and influence customers'
assessments of functional, social, and emotional value. These assessments, in turn, may impact customer
satisfaction. Previous research has identified perceived value as a mediating link between marketing factors
and customer outcomes (Alkufahy et al., 2023). Building on this foundation, the present study investigates
whether perceived value mediates the relationship between overall consumption factors and customer
satisfaction.
H4: Perceived value mediates the relationship between consumption factors and customer satisfaction.
2.10 Conceptual Framework
Based on the 4Cs Marketing Theory and previous research, the conceptual framework comprises three
primary elements: consumption factors as the independent variable, perceived value as the mediating
variable, and customer satisfaction as the dependent varia ble. Consumption factors encompass customer
needs, cost, convenience, and communication, while perceived value includes functional, social, and
emotional dimensions. Figure 1  illustrates three direct relationships: between consumption factors and
customer satisfaction (H1), consumption factors and perceived value (H2), and perceived value and
customer satisfaction (H3). Additionally, perceived value mediates one indirect relationship (H4).

3.0 METHODOLOGY
3.1 Research Design
A quantitative, cross -sectional design grounded in a positivist approach was employed to examine the
relationships among consumption factors, perceived value, and customer satisfaction. Consistent with the
4Cs Marketing Theory, the study treated consumption factors as the independent variable, perceived value
as the mediator, and customer satisfaction as the dependent variable. The study collected primary data
through a structured questionnaire administered to enterprise customers of Specialized and New IT-sector
SMEs in Shandong Province, China.
3.2 Population and Sampling
The target population comprised 3,251 enterprise customers associated with 38 Specialized and New small
and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) in the information technology sector in Shandong Province, China.
Using a 95% confidence level, a 5% margin of error, a 50% assumed population proportion, and the finite-
population correction, the minimum required sample size was approximately 344 respondents. A total of
384 valid questionnaires were obtained, exceeding the minimum sample requirement and representing
11.81% of the accessible population. The sample was proportionally stratified across the 38 enterprises
according to their customer populations. All 384 valid responses were included in the statistical analysis.
3.3 Data Collection Procedure
The study collected primary data through a structured questionnaire administered to enterprise customers
of Specialized and New IT -sector small and medium -sized enterprises (SMEs) in Shandong Province,
China. The survey included customers from 38 participa ting enterprises, selected through proportional
sampling. The questionnaire collected demographic information and asked about consumption factors,
perceived value, and customer satisfaction. The final dataset comprised 384 completed questionnaires.
3.4 Measurement of Variables
The study collected data using a structured bilingual (English and Chinese) questionnaire with two sections.
The first section gathered information on respondents' demographics and background, including the
enterprise from which they purchased, gender, job  position, duration of business collaboration, age,
education level, and annual income. The second section addressed three domains: consumption factors,
perceived value, and customer satisfaction.  Consumption factors (CF), the independent variable, were
measured using 17 items derived from the 4Cs Marketing Theory: customer needs (3 items), cost (4 items),
convenience (6 items), and communication (4 items). Perceived value (PV), the mediating variable, was
assessed with three items reflecting functional, so cial, and emotional value. Customer satisfaction (CSF),
the dependent variable, was evaluated using five items related to perceived performance, expectation
disconfirmation, willingness to recommend, repurchase intention, and complaint handling. All 25 ite ms
utilized a five-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (Strongly Disagree) to 5 (Strongly Agree).

3.5 Data Analysis Techniques
The study analyzed the data using SPSS and AMOS, summarized respondents' demographic information
with descriptive statistics, and conducted confirmatory factor analysis to assess the measurement model.
Further, evaluated internal consistency using Cronbach 's alpha and composite reliability (CR), examined
convergent validity using standardized factor loadings and average variance extracted (AVE), and assessed
discriminant validity by comparing the square roots of AVE with inter -construct correlations. It als o
evaluated model fit using χ²/df, GFI, AGFI, CFI, TLI, NFI, IFI, and RMSEA. It used structural equation
modeling (SEM) to test the three direct relationships and the mediating effect of perceived value. Moreover,
the study tested the indirect effect using bootstrapping with 5,000 resamples and a 95% confidence interval.

4.5 Discussion
This study investigated the relationships among consumption factors, perceived value, and customer
satisfaction in enterprise customers of Specialized and New SMEs in Shandong Province, China. The
findings address the four research objectives and provide empirical support for applying the 4Cs Marketing
Theory in business-to-business customer relationships. For the first objective, consumption factors showed
a significant positive association with customer satisfaction (β = 0.292), supporting H1. This result  aligns
with Rahman et al. (2023), Ayinaddis et al. (2023), and Chong et al. (2023), who emphasized the importance
of customer -focused practices and convenience in enhancing satisfaction. For the second objective,
consumption factors exhibited an even stro nger positive relationship with perceived value (β = 0.478),
supporting H2. This finding aligns with Wang (2020) and Ghorbani et al. (2023), who observed that
customer needs, cost, convenience, and communication influence perceived value. For the third objective,
perceived value was positively associated with customer satisfaction (β = 0.389), supporting H3. This result
is consistent with Chen et al. (2017) and García-Fernández et al. (2018), who identified perceived value as
a key determinant of customer satisfaction. Regarding the fourth objective, the estimated indirect effect (β
≈ 0.186) suggests partial mediation, with perceived value accounting for approximately 38.9% of the total
standardized effect. This finding aligns with the mediation mechanisms described by Alkufahy et al. (2023),
although statistical confirmation requires examining the bootstrap confidence interval. Collectively, these
results support applying the 4Cs framework to Specialized and New SMEs, indicating that customer -
centered marketing is associated with satisfaction both directly and through customers' perceptions of value.
5.0 CONCLUSION
This study examined the relationships among consumption factors, perceived value, and customer
satisfaction among 384 enterprise customers of Specialized and New small and medium -sized enterprises
(SMEs) in Shandong Province, China. The findings revealed s ignificant positive associations between
consumption factors and customer satisfaction (β = 0.292), consumption factors and perceived value (β =
0.478), and perceived value and customer satisfaction (β = 0.389). Furthermore, the estimated indirect effect
through perceived value (β = 0.186) accounted for approximately 38.91% of the total standardized effect,
suggesting a potential partial mediation mechanism, although statistical confirmation requires verification
of the bootstrap confidence interval. These findings contribute to the 4Cs Marketing Theory by highlighting
the importance of perceived value in explaining customer satisfaction within specialized SMEs.
Accordingly, SMEs should prioritize customer needs, product and service quality, cost efficiency,
purchasing convenience, and effective communication to enhance perceived value and customer
satisfaction.
6.0 LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE RESEARCH
This study has several limitations. The cross -sectional design limits causal inference. The focus on
enterprise customers of Specialized and New SMEs in Shandong Province, China, limits the
generalizability of the findings to other industries and regions. Reliance on self -reported questionnaires
may introduce common -method bias. Additionally, the proposed framework does not account for other
potentially relevant factors, such as customer trust, engagement, experience, brand loyalty, and relationship
quality. Future research should test the model in diverse industries, regions, and cultural contexts,
employing longitudinal or mixed-methods approaches.

Further studies should examine additional mediating and moderating mechanisms to understand customer
satisfaction better and broaden the applicability of the 4Cs Marketing Theory.
