Abstract
The growing complexity of socio-economic and environmental challenges requires new approaches
to public administration that integrate sustainable development and social responsibility into
governance systems. In this context, marketing is increasingly reco gnized as a strategic managerial
tool capable of enhancing citizen engagement, improving policy effectiveness, and supporting
behavioral change. This study aims to conceptualize the integration of marketing into public
administration and to identify its ro le within the framework of sustainable and responsible
governance. The research adopts a qualitative design based on an integrative literature review,
grounded theory methodology, and case study analysis. Scholarly sources published between 2017
and 2026 w ere systematically analyzed using a multi -stage coding process, including open, axial,
selective, and theoretical coding. This approach enabled the identification of key categories and
relationships that explain how marketing functions within public govern ance systems. The findings
indicate that marketing operates as a multi-dimensional managerial mechanism that connects citizen-

1. Introduction
The transformation of public administration in the 21st century is increasingly shaped by the
need to respond to complex societal challenges, including climate change, social inequality, and the
growing demand for transparency and accountability. Traditional bureaucratic models, characterized
by hierarchical decision-making and limited citizen interaction, are gradually being replaced by more
adaptive, participatory, and stakeholder -oriented approaches. In this context, the concept of
sustainable development has become a central paradigm guiding public governance, requiring the
integration of economic efficie ncy, social equity, and environmental responsibility into policy -
making processes. Simultaneously, social responsibility has extended beyond the corporate sector
and is now recognized as a fundamental principle of public sector management, emphasizing ethical
behavior, inclusiveness, and long-term societal value creation.
Against this background, marketing - traditionally associated with the private sector - has
emerged as a promising managerial tool in public administration (Kravchenko et al. , 2024). Its
evolution into social and public marketing reflects a shift from profit -oriented activities toward the
creation and delivery of public value. By incorporating marketing principles such as stakeholder
analysis, segmentation, strategic communication, and behav ioral change mechanisms, public
institutions can better align their policies with the needs and expectations of citizens while promoting
sustainable and socially responsible outcomes (Mozafar et al., 2023).

The successful integration of marketing in public administration requires a holistic approach
that connects ethical digital governance with modern reporting and analytics to achieve measurable
results in public sector mod ernization. Meanwhile, despite the growing recognition of public
marketing as a governance instrument, there remains a lack of comprehensive frameworks that
systematically link marketing tools with sustainable development goals and social responsibility
principles within the managerial context of public administration. Existing studies often focus either
on communication strategies or on isolated policy outcomes, without sufficiently addressing the
institutional, cultural, and strategic dimensions of this integration. This gap limits the ability of public
authorities to fully leverage marketing as a transformative mechanism for governance innovation.
Therefore, the relevance of this study lies in the need to conceptualize and systematize the
role of marketin g within public administration as an integrated managerial approach that supports
sustainable development and enhances social responsibility. The study aims to develop a
comprehensive understanding of how marketing instruments can be embedded into public
governance systems to improve decision -making, strengthen citizen engagement, and ensure long -
term societal impact. Specifically, the research seeks to identify key managerial components,
mechanisms, and conditions necessary for the effective implementation  of public marketing in the
context of sustainability-oriented governance.

2. Literature Review
2.1 Transformation of Public Administration in the Context of Sustainable Development
Recent scholarly discourse emphasizes that contemporary public administration is undergoing
a profound transformation driven by the imperatives of sustainable development and social
responsibility. Traditional bureaucratic governance models are increasingly replaced by
collaborative, network-based, and participatory a pproaches that integrate multiple stakeholders into
decision-making processes. This shift reflects the growing recognition that complex socio-economic
and environmental challenges cannot be addressed through hierarchical governance structures alone.
Studies demonstrate that sustainable public management requires not only institutional
reforms but also the adoption of integrative governance mechanisms that combine policy
coordination, citizen participation, and long -term strategic orientation (Nesterak, 2024 ). In this
context, the concept of public value has gained prominence, emphasizing the role of public
institutions in creating socially desirable outcomes through co -production and multi -actor
collaboration (Magliacani, 2023). Furthermore, sustainability-oriented governance increasingly relies

on co-creation processes, where citizens, businesses, and civil society jointly contribute to policy
development and implementation, enhancing both effectiveness and legitimacy (Torfing et al., 2024).
Toncheva-Zlatkova (2025) examines social responsibility practices, particularly volunteer
initiatives, implemented in selected Bulgarian public administrations. The study focuses on the
organization’s adoption and implementation of public social responsibility (PSR) concepts. PSR can

At the same time, the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) significantly influenced public
management paradigms by introducing measurable and inter connected targets that require cross -
sectoral coordination and innovative governance approaches. Research highlights that embedding
sustainability into public administration practices necessitates new managerial tools capable of
aligning strategic planning  with stakeholder engagement and behavioral change mechanisms
(Bisogno et al., 2025).

2.2 The Evolution of Public and Social Marketing in Governance
The integration of marketing into public administration has evolved from a narrow focus on
communication and information dissemination toward a broader strategic and managerial function.
Public marketing today encompasses a wide range of activities, including stakeholder analysis,
behavioral interventions, policy communication, and the co-creation of public value.
According to Zhengyang’s (2024) research, marketing in the field of public administration is
first and foremost a method in public administration that prioritizes the pleasure of customers and
producers of public services, which serves as a special ty pe of marketing implementation. More
specifically, the author defines this as the marketing activity of management bodies and/or their
representatives who serve as producers, consumers, or middlemen (suppliers) in the processes of
exchanging commodities, services, ideas, and other values.
Social marketing, in particular, has emerged as a critical approach for addressing societal
challenges by influencing behaviors that benefit individuals and communities. Aya Pastrana and
Obregón (2023) claim that it operat es across multiple levels - individual, community, and policy -
thereby enabling systemic change rather than isolated interventions. Recent research demonstrates
that social marketing is increasingly applied in areas such as public health, environmental
sustainability, and social inclusion, contributing to the achievement of sustainable development
objectives (Delvaux & Van den Broeck, 2023).
Bueno and Jara (2024) rightly emphasize that marketing in the public sector plays an
important role in building trus t, facilitating efficient communication between the public and the

governing body, and enhancing socioeconomic progress, and that investigating the pragmatic
implications of marketing in the sector is critical due to its ability to revolutionize the delive ry of

often still perceived through a traditional lens, focusing primarily on communication campaigns
rather than as a comprehensive managerial framework capable of shaping institutional behavior and
governance processes.

2.3 Marketing as a Tool for Citizen Engagement and Public Value Creation
One of the central themes in contemporary literature is the role of marketing in enhancing
citizen engagement and fostering participatory governance. It is broadly claimed that marketing tools
enable public authorities to better understand the needs, preferences, and behavioral patterns of
citizens, thereby improving the design and delivery of public services (Tsai, 2026; de Siqueira et al.,
2022). This shift aligns with the broader transition from “government” to “governance,” where public
institutions act as facilitators rather than sole decision-makers.
Empirical studies highlight th at participatory mechanisms such as co -creation, participatory
budgeting, and collaborative platforms contribute significantly to sustainable governance by
increasing transparency, accountability, and trust (Sinervo et al., 2024; Vedeld & Hofstad, 2024). In
this context, marketing plays a key role in facilitating dialogue, building relationships, and promoting
active civic participation (Ansell & Torfing, 2021).
Additionally, the integration of marketing principles into public administration supports the
development of relational governance models, where trust and legitimacy become central
performance indicators. The ability of public institutions to communicate effectively, engage
stakeholders, and demonstrate responsiveness is increasingly recognized as a determinant of
successful policy implementation and long-term sustainability outcomes.

2.4 Research Gaps and Theoretical Limitations
Despite the growing body of literature, several critical gaps remain. First, there is a lack of
integrative frameworks that systematically connect marketing tools with sustainable development and
social responsibility within public administration systems. Most studies examine these elements in
isolation, without addressing their interdependencies and cumulative effects.
Second, methodological limitations persist, as the field is dominated by qualitative case
studies and cross-sectional analyses, with limited use of theory-building approaches such as grounded
theory or mixed-method designs. This restricts the development of robust theoretical models capable
of explaining how marketing can be institutionalized within public governance structures.
Third, existing research often underestimates the importance of organizational culture, digital
transformation, and ethical governance as mediating factors in the integration of marketing into public
administration. The role of digital platforms, data analytics, and transparency mechanisms in shaping
marketing-driven governance remains insufficiently explored.

2.5 Positioning of the Present Study
To address these gaps, this study adopts an integrative review approach combined with
grounded theory and case study analysis to develop a comprehensive conceptual framework for the
integration of marketing into public administration. By synthesizing diverse theoretical perspectives
and empirical findings, the research seeks to identify key managerial components, mechanisms, and
relationships that underpin marketing -driven sustainable governance. The use of grounded theory
allows for the systematic development of categories and relationships through open, axial, selective,
and theoretical coding, thereby contributing to theory building in a field characterized by
fragmentation. At the same time, the incorporation of case studies provides empirical grounding and
enhances the practical relevance of the proposed framework.

3. Materials and Methods
3.1 Research Design
This study adopts a qualitative research design based on an integrative literature review
combined with grounded theory methodology and multiple case study analysis. Such a design allows
for both the synthesis of existing knowledge and the development of a  novel conceptual framework
explaining the integration of marketing into public administration within the context of sustainable
development and social responsibility. The integrative review approach was selected due to its ability
to incorporate diverse t ypes of evidence, including theoretical contributions, empirical studies, and

policy-oriented research. Unlike systematic reviews, which focus on narrowly defined questions and
standardized inclusion criteria, integrative reviews enable a broader conceptua l analysis and are
particularly suitable for emerging and interdisciplinary research fields. Grounded theory was
employed as the primary analytical strategy to generate theoretical insights from the reviewed
literature and case materials. This approach facilitates the identification of patterns, relationships, and
categories that explain how marketing functions as a managerial tool in public governance systems.
Additionally, case study analysis was used to contextualize theoretical findings and illustrate practical
applications across different governance environments.

3.2 Data Collection and Selection Criteria
The empirical basis of the study consists of scholarly publications and analytical reports
published between 2017 and 2026. The selection of sources  was guided by their relevance to three
core domains: (1) public administration and governance, (2) marketing and social marketing, and (3)
sustainable development and social responsibility.
The primary databases used for source identification included Sco pus, Web of Science, and
Google Scholar. Preference was given to peer-reviewed journal articles, academic monographs, and
high-impact conference proceedings. Additional sources included reports from international
organizations addressing sustainable governance and public sector innovation. The inclusion criteria
were as follows: relevance to the integration of marketing into public sector governance; focus on
sustainability, social responsibility, or public value; publication in peer -reviewed outlets or
recognized academic publishers; availability of conceptual, empirical, or methodological
contributions. Sources that focused exclusively on commercial marketing without relevance to public
administration, or those lacking academic rigor, were excluded from the analysis.

3.3 Analytical Framework: Grounded Theory Approach
The analytical process was conducted using a grounded theory approach, which involves
iterative coding and constant comparison of data. The analysis proceeded through four stages:
1. Open coding, where key concepts and recurring themes related to public marketing,
governance, and sustainability were identified and labeled;
2. Axial coding, which involved grouping these concepts into higher -level categories and
identifying relationships between them;
3. Selective coding, where core categories were integrated to form a coherent explanatory
structure;

4. Theoretical coding, aimed at developing an overarching conceptual model that explains the
role of marketing in public administration systems.
This multi -stage coding process enabled the transition from descriptive insights to theory
development, ensuring both analytical rigor and conceptual depth.

3.4 Case Study Selection and Analysis
To complement the literature -based analysis, a multiple case  study approach was applied.
Cases were selected based on their relevance to the application of marketing principles in public
administration, particularly in areas such as digital governance, citizen engagement, environmental
policy, and social inclusion.  The selection criteria for case studies included: demonstrated use of
marketing or communication strategies in public governance; alignment with sustainable
development or social responsibility objectives; availability of reliable and sufficiently detailed data.
The case studies were analyzed using the same coding framework as the literature sources,
allowing for the validation and refinement of emerging theoretical categories. This triangulation
enhances the robustness of the findings and ensures that the  proposed framework reflects both
theoretical and practical dimensions.

3.5 Research Limitations
Despite its comprehensive design, the study has certain limitations. The integrative nature of
the review may introduce a degree of subjectivity in source selection and interpretation. Additionally,
while grounded theory provides strong tools for theory development, it does not aim at statistical
generalization. The case study approach, although valuable for contextual insights, may also limit the
generalizability of findings across all public administration systems. Nevertheless, the combination
of integrative review, grounded theory, and case study analysis provides a robust methodological
foundation for exploring a complex and interdisciplinary research problem.

orientation, and institutional change were identified. This process allowed for the consolida tion of

4.1 Conceptual Framework of Marketing-Driven Public Governance
Building upon the results of the grounded theory analy sis, this study proposes a conceptual
framework that explains the integration of marketing into public administration as a multi -level and
systemic process. The framework conceptualizes public marketing not as an isolated function but as
a core managerial logic that connects strategic governance objectives with societal needs and
behavioral dynamics. At the core of the framework lies citizen -centric governance, which serves as
the primary organizing principle. Around this core, four interrelated dimensions are structured:
- Engagement Dimension: includes co -creation, stakeholder participation, and communication
mechanisms that enable continuous interaction between public authorities and citizens.
- Digital Dimension: encompasses e -governance systems, data an alytics, and digital
communication platforms that facilitate real -time feedback and evidence -based decision -
making.
- Sustainability Dimension: integrates environmental, social, and economic considerations,
supported by social marketing tools and behaviora l interventions aimed at achieving long -
term development goals.
- Institutional Dimension: covers organizational culture, ethical standards, transparency, and
accountability mechanisms that ensure legitimacy and trust.

4.2 Case Study Analysis: Empirical Triangulation
To validate and enrich the theoretical findings, several illustrative case studies were analyzed,
focusing on the application of marketing principles in public administration across different
governance contexts.
The first case relates to digital governance initiatives within the European Union, where digital
platforms and open data systems have been widely implemented to enhance citizen engagement and
transparency. Programs such as e-participation platforms and digital public services demonstrate how
marketing tools - particularly segmentation, targeted communication, and user-centered design - can
improve accessibility and responsiveness of public administration. These initiatives confirm the
importance of the digital dimension identified in the conceptual framework.
Grigoliene et al.  (2025) e xamine the characteristics of existing institutional governance

Klaipeda city uses a variety of modern communication mediums. The one-stop-shop idea is in place,
with users able to submit requests through the municipality’s website. Both the municipality and the
mayor have their own Facebook pages; nevertheless, there is no direct communication service, and
no public consultations occurred. Klaipeda district’s website contains information, including links to
the municipal’s Facebook profile and YouTube channel. Residents have the option to evaluate the
quality of services, and a list of public consultations was released. The Klaipeda city website stated
that all coastal municipalities are members of the Regional Development  Council. This council is
made up of the mayors of the region’s municipalities, designated members of municipal councils, and

4.3 Practical Implications and Policy Recommendations
The findings of this study provide several important implications for policymakers and public
managers seeking to modernize governance systems in line with sustainable development and social
responsibility principles.
First, public institutions should adopt a strategic approach t o marketing, moving beyond
communication campaigns toward the integration of marketing principles into policy design and
implementation. This includes the use of stakeholder analysis, behavioral insights, and data -driven
decision-making. Second, the develo pment of digital governance infrastructure is essential for
enabling effective public marketing. Investments in digital platforms, open data systems, and
analytics capabilities can significantly enhance citizen engagement, transparency, and service
delivery. Third, policymakers should prioritize citizen engagement and co -creation mechanisms as
central components of governance. Marketing tools can facilitate these processes by supporting
dialogue, trust-building, and the identification of societal needs. Fourth, the integration of marketing
into public administration requires strengthening institutional capacity, including organizational
culture, ethical standards, and performance management systems. Without internal transformation,
marketing initiatives are unlikely to produce sustainable results. Finally, public marketing should be
explicitly aligned with sustainable development objectives, ensuring that communication strategies
and behavioral interventions contribute to environmental protection, social incl usion, and long-term
economic resilience.

5. Conclusion
The conducted study advances the understanding of marketing as an integral component of
contemporary public administration, rather than a supplementary communication function. By
combining an integrative literature review with grounded theory and case study analysis, the research
develops a comprehensive conceptualization of marketing -driven governance within the context of
sustainable development and social responsibility.
The findings demonstrate th at the integration of marketing into public administration is a
multi-dimensional and systemic process that connects citizen engagement, digital transformation,
sustainability objectives, and institutional capacity. The grounded theory analysis reveals tha t
marketing operates as a coordinating mechanism that aligns public policies with societal needs,
facilitates behavioral change, and strengthens trust and legitimacy. In this regard, the proposed
conceptual framework contributes to the theoretical developm ent of public governance by
introducing the notion of a marketing-driven governance ecosystem. From a theoretical perspective,

into a coherent model that explains how marketing tools can be institutionalized within governance

and organizational transformation.
Meanwhile, the study is not without limitations. The qualitative nature of the research and the

the proposed framework in different institutional contexts.
Overall, the study emphasizes that the effective integration of marketing into public
administration is essential for achieving sustainable, inclusive, and adaptive governance in an
increasingly complex and dynamic environment.
