ABSTRAK

Penelitian ini bertujuan menganalisis pengaruh daya tarik destinasi, kualitas layanan pariwisata, strategi
pemasaran, dan bauran komunikasi terhadap pariwisata berkelanjutan di Sulawesi Selatan. Pendekatan penelitian
menggunakan metode kuantitatif dengan pengumpulan data melalui survei langsung dan daring terhadap 350
wisatawan domestik yang berkunjung ke destinasi utama, yaitu Makassar, Parepare, Tana Toraja, dan Toraja
Utara. Teknik pengambilan sampel dilakukan secara purposif dengan kriteria wisatawan yang memiliki
pengalaman perjalanan di lokasi penelitian. Data dianalisis menggunakan Structural Equation Modeling–Partial
Least Squares (SEM-PLS 3.0) untuk menguji model pengukuran dan struktural. Hasil penelitian menunjukkan
bahwa daya tarik destinasi, kualitas layanan, dan strategi pemasaran berpengaruh positif dan signifikan terhadap
pariwisata berkelanjutan, baik sec ara langsung maupun melalui bauran komunikasi. Selain itu, bauran
komunikasi terbukti memiliki pengaruh signifikan dan berperan sebagai mediator penuh dalam hubungan antara
variabel independen dan pariwisata berkelanjutan. Temuan ini menegask an pentingnya komunikasi terpadu—
meliputi periklanan, hubungan masyarakat, media sosial, dan pemasaran langsung—dalam membentuk persepsi
wisatawan serta mendorong praktik pariwisata berkelanjutan. Secara praktis, penelitian ini memberikan implikasi
bagi pemangku kepentingan dan pembuat kebijakan dalam merancang strategi pengembangan pariwisata yang
selaras dengan pelestarian lingkungan, budaya, dan kesejahteraan masyarakat lokal di Sulawesi Selatan.

Kata kunci: destinasi, pariwisata, keberlanjutan, strategi pemasaran, bauran komunikasi

ABSTRACT

This study aims to analyse the influence of destination attractiveness, tourism service quality, marketing
strategy, and communication mix on sustainable tourism in South Sulawesi. The research used
quantitative methods and collected data through direct and online surveys of 350 domestic tourists
visiting key destinations, namely Makassar, Parepa re, Tana Toraja, and Nort h Toraja. The sampling
technique was purposive with the criteria of tourists who have travel experience at the study locations.
Data were analysed using Structural Equation Modelling–Partial Least Squares (SEM-PLS 3.0) to test
the measurement and structural models. The results showed that destination attractiveness, service
quality, and marketing strategy had a positive and significant influence on sustainable tourism, both
directly and through the communication mix. Furthermore, the communication mix was shown to exert
a significant influence and to act as a full mediator in the relationship between the independent variables
and sustainable tourism. These findings emphasis e the importance of integrated communication—

INTRODUCTION
Tourism is a vital sector that contributes
significantly to economic development, social
well-being, and cultural exchange worldwide.
However, unregulated tourism growth can
lead to environmental degradation, cultural
loss, and social conflicts. Therefore,
sustainable tourism, which balances economic
benefits with environmental conservation and
social equity, has become a global priority
(UNWTO, 2018). South Sulawesi, Indonesia, is
known for its diverse tourism destinations—
ranging from natural landscapes, such as
beaches and mountains, to rich cultural
heritage sites, such as Tana Toraja. To promote
sustainable tourism in this region, it is
essential to understand how tourism
destinations, tourism services, and marketing
strategies interact and influence sustainable
outcomes (Obeng-Manu, 2022).
Well-managed, culturally preserved
tourism destinations attract more responsible
tourists and support sustainability goals.
Meanwhile, tourism services that emphasise
quality, environmental friendliness, and
cultural sensitivity enhance visitor satisfaction
and encourage repeat visits (Akroush et al.,
2016). Marketing strategies, particularly those
using an integrated communication mix
(advertising, public relations, social media,
direct marketing), are critical in shaping
tourist perceptions and behaviours towards
sustainable practices (Bramwell & Lane, 2011).
By exploring the interplay of these factors
through the lens of the communication mix,
this study aims to provide insights for
practitioners and policymakers to foster
sustainable tourism development in South
Sulawesi (Islam, 2022).
This study aims to examine the influence
of destinations, services, and marketing
strategies through communication mix on
sustainable tourism in South Sulawesi. This
study provides insight into the importance of
the influence of tourist destinations, services,
and marketing strategies through the
communication mix in supporting sustainable
tourism development in South Sulawesi, as
well as the need to consider economic, social,
cultural, and environmental impacts in
tourism management so that it can develop
sustainably (Aliman et al., 2016).
The research results are expected to
provide theoretical, practical and policy
benefits for the development of sustainable
tourism in South Sulawesi. Tourism destina-
tions are central to the success of sustainable
tourism. A well-managed destination that
prioritises environmental protection, cultural
preservation, and community engagement
contributes directly to the long-term viability
of the tourism sector (Bramwell & Lane, 2011).
Sustainable tourism destinations not only
provide memorable experiences for visitors
but also ensure that tourism development does
not degrade the ecological or social fabric of the
host region.
In South Sulawesi, destinations such as
Tana Toraja, with its unique cultural traditions
and traditional architecture, and Bira Beach,
known for its natural beauty, are examples of
areas with high tourism potential. However,
without proper planning and sustainable
practices, these destinations risk overexploita-
tion, environmental damage, and cultural
commodification. (Ashley & Roe, 2002).
Sustainable destination management
involves integrating zoning policies,
carrying capacity assessments, eco-tourism
infrastructure, and community participation
in planning and benefit-sharing (UNWTO,
2018). Responsible development ensures that

more conscientious travelers and build a
more resilient tourism economy (Arfan,

relations, sales promotion, personal selling,

services, and marketing strategies) and the
goal of sustainable tourism (Islam, 2022).
In recent studies, the communication

mediator that enhances the relationship

promotion) and sustainable outcomes
(Buhalis & Foerste, 2015). By consistently
delivering messages that emphasise
sustainability—such as eco-certification,
local culture, and community-based
initiatives—the communication mix helps
shape tourist perceptions and behaviour in a
responsible direction (Font & McCabe, 2017).
Furthermore, integrated communication
ensures that tourism branding aligns with
sustainability values, which builds trust and
long-term relationships with eco-conscious
travellers. In regions like South Sulawesi,
where local identity and ecological balance
are crucial, the communication mix plays a
vital role in connecting local initiatives to
broader markets, ensuring that tourism
development remains culturally respectful
and environmentally sound.
The significance of tourism destinations,
services, and marketing strategies in
promoting sustainability is increasingly
emphasised in recent tourism studies. For
instance, well-developed, culturally
preserved destinations tend to attract more
responsible tourists. In addition, high-
quality, environmentally friendly tourism
services contribute to positive visitor

relationships between destination attractive-

communication mix in the context of
developing destinations. Rather than claiming
entirely new theoretical novelties, this research
is positioned as testing and strengthening the
conceptual framework that has developed in
the sustainable tourism literature by
positioning marketing and communication as
mechanisms that convey sustainability values

Several contemporary studies
demonstrate the importance of integrating
these variables. Recent research confirms
that marketing communication strategies—
such as digital marketing, social media, and
destination branding—are crucial for
increasing the visibility and attractiveness of
tourist destinations in the digital age
(Hardiyanto et al., 2025). Additionally,
studies reveal that sustainability-based

competitiveness over the long term (Prastyo

indicates that sustainability practices,
supported by digital technology and
stakeholder engagement, help create
memorable tourist experiences and foster
positive perceptions of  destinations (Jokom
et al., 2025).
Thus, with an empirical focus on South
Sulawesi as a region with high cultural and
ecological richness, this study makes a
contextual contribution by providing new
empirical evidence on how the integration of
destination attractiveness, service quality,
marketing, and communication can support
sustainable tourism development in
developing regions, while expanding the
application of existing models in different
geographical and social contexts.

elements in sustainable tourism development
because they serve as the primary interface
between tourists, the environment, and local
communities. The literature confirms that
well-managed destinations—through environ-
mental protection, cultural preservation, and
community engagement—can directly influ-
ence how tourists perceive and act toward
sustainability, thereby contributing to the long-
term viability of the tourism sector (Bramwell
& Lane, 2011). However, most early studies
still viewed destination sustainability as an
outcome of internal managerial practices
alone, without clarifying how communicating
these sustainability values influences tourists'

In eveloping regions such as South

Tana Toraja and Bira Beach—face a
sustainability paradox. On the one hand,
natural and cultural riches are key attractions,
but on the other hand, without effective
management and communication strategies,
destinations are vulnerable to overexploita-

sustainable tourism but also identifies the
limitations of previous literature in explaining

research model that positions the

Tourism Services and Sustainable Tourism
Tourism services, such as accommoda-
tion, transportation, tour guides, and
hospitality, substantially shape both the
tourist experience and destination sustaina-
bility. While quality services that include
environmental responsibility, cultural sensi-
tivity, and community engagement are
known to enhance tourist satisfaction and
sustainability (Chen & Chen, 2010). Most
research treats tourism services as a direct
driver of sustainability. There is less focus on
the mechanisms by which tourists actually
perceive and internalise the sustainability
values these services offer, which is crucial in
understanding real impact.
Conceptually, sustainable tourism
services are measured not only by comfort and
operational efficiency, but also by efforts to
minimise environmental impacts—such as
energy efficiency, waste management, and the
use of local products—as well as respect for
local culture and traditions (Dodds & Graci,
2012). The literature also indicates that such
services tend to increase revisit intentions,
positive word of mouth, and local community
support. However, existing empirical findings
still show mixed results, indicating a boundary
condition: the quality of sustainable services
does not necessarily lead to sustainable tourist
behaviour when sustainability messages are
not communicated clearly and consistently.
In developing regions like South
Sulawesi, tourism service providers often
face structural constraints, including limited
financial resources, low sustainability
literacy, and inadequate training for industry
players. However, there is growing
awareness of the importance of
environmentally friendly, culturally based
services, particularly in ecologically and
culturally sensitive destinations such as Tana
Toraja and Bira Beach (Salam, 2024). Some
practices, such as eco-certification, the use of
local labour, and the provision of services
based on local wisdom, have been adopted,
but their effectiveness in promoting
sustainable tourism depends largely on how
these practices are communicated to tourists.
Critically, the literature shows that
sustainability in service delivery does not
automatically lead to responsible tourist
perceptions and behaviours, indicating that
other mechanisms influence this relationship.
Font & McCabe (2017) assert that sustainability
values must be strategically communicated to
alter tourist attitudes and choices. However,
most research still treats communication as a
complementary or contextual variable and not
as a mediating mechanism that explains how
service quality causally leads to sustainable
tourism outcomes. This gap highlights the
need for an integrative approach that positions
the communication mix as the crucial link
through which sustainable service delivery
affects tourist behaviour.
From this perspective, good service
quality will enhance the tourist experience
and strengthen support for sustainable
tourism practices.
H3: Tourism service quality has a positive
and significant influence on sustainable
tourism.

The literature review demonstrates that
the quality of sustainable tourism services is
a crucial prerequisite for destination
sustainability. However, the effectiveness of
these services is significantly influenced by
how sustainability values are communicated
(Sparks & Browning, 2011). The absence of
quantitative research explicitly examining
the communication mix as a mediating factor
represents a significant theoretical gap and
provides a key justification for the research
model proposed in this study.
Therefore, good service not only
enhances the tourist experience but also
strengthens the effectiveness of destination
communication.
H4: Tourism service quality has a positive
and significant influence on the
communication mix.

Communication Mix as a Mediating Factor
The communication mix, which encom-
passes advertising, public relations, sales
promotion, personal selling, and digital
marketing, such as social media and
influencer marketing, is a central element in
contemporary tourism management (Xiang
& Gretzel, 2010). Tourism marketing litera-
ture emphasises that the communication mix
serves not only as a promotional tool but also
as a strategic mechanism that links tourism
offerings—such as destination attractive-
ness, service quality, and marketing
strategies—with sustainable tourism goals
(Islam, 2022). However, much of the early
research still positions communication as a
supporting activity, rather than as an
explanatory variable mediating the
relationship between tourism inputs and
poverty outcomes.
Thus, the communication mix plays a
crucial role in promoting sustainable tourism
practices.
H7: The communication mix has a positive
and significant influence on sustainable
tourism.

Several studies have shown that the
communication mix can strengthen the
relationships among destination quality,
service performance, promotional strategies
and poverty outcomes (Buhalis & Foerste,
2015). Through consistent messaging
regarding eco-certification, local cultural
preservation, and community-based tourism
initiatives, integrated communication can
shape more responsible tourist perceptions
and behaviours (Font & McCabe, 2017).
However, empirical evidence supporting the
role of the communication mix as a causal
mediator remains limited, as most studies
are descriptive or position communication as
an independent variable without explicitly
examining mediating mechanisms.
Thus, the communicat ion mix can be a
strategic link that bridges the relationship
between destination attributes and tourist
behavior that supports sustainability.
H8: The communication mix mediates the
effect of destination attractiveness on
sustainable tourism.

Critically, the effectiveness of a
communications mix in promoting sustainable
tourism is heavily influenced by several
boundary conditions, such as tourists' digital
literacy levels, message credibility, consistency
between communication claims and actual
practices at the destination, and the local social
and cultural context (Pike & Page, 2014). A
mismatch between the message and reality on
the ground risks fueling tourist scepticism and
accusations of greenwashing, undermining
trust in the destination. These issues have
received little attention in quantitative studies,
particularly in the context of regional
destinations in developing countries.
Furthermore, positive service experiences
need to be communicated consistently and
widely to influence other tourists' behaviour.
Without effective communication, high service
quality does not necessarily lead to changes in
tourists' behaviour toward more sustainable
practices.
H9:  The communication mix mediates the
effect of tourism service quality on
sustainable tourism.

In South Sulawesi, the communication
mix plays a strategic role in connecting local
initiatives—such as the preservation of Tana
Toraja culture and the coastal environmental
conservation of Bira Beach—with the broader
tourism market. However, the existing
literature has not adequately explained how,
and to what extent, integrated communication
can transform destination quality, tourism
services, and marketing strategies into tourist
perceptions and behaviours that support
tourism desires (Tulung et al., 2024). This gap
highlights the need for a conceptual model that
examines the communication mix as a key
mediator in this relationship.
Thus, the novelty of this research lies in
the empirical examination of the communi-
cation mix as a mediating mechanism that
bridges destination attractiveness, service
quality, and marketing strategy to support

RESEARCH METHOD
Research Design
This study uses a quantitative, survey-
based approach to examine the relationships
among destination attractiveness, tourism
service quality, marketing strategy, communi-
cation mix, and sustainable tourism in South
Sulawesi. The research instrument is a struc-
tured questionnaire developed based on the
variables Tourism Destination (X 1), Tourism
Service (X2), Marketing Strategy (X3), Commu-
nication Mix (Y 1), and Sustainable Tourism
(Y2), with responses measured on a Likert
scale. The research respondents were domestic
tourists visiting the main destinations, namely
Makassar, Parepare, Tana Toraja, and North
Toraja. The research model is designed to
analyse the direct and indirect effects of
independent variables on sustainable tourism,
with the communication mix mediating these
effects. Data analysis was carried out using
Structural Equation Modelling based on
Partial Least Squares (SEM-PLS) to test the
hypothesis and evaluate the overall research
model.

Data Analysis
This tudy uses Structural Equation
Modelling (SEM) with a Partial Least Squares
(PLS) approach to analyse complex structural
relationships between latent variables. SEM-
PLS was chosen because it is able to simultane-
ously test the direct and indirect relationships
between exogenous variables, namely destina-
tion attractiveness (X1), tourism service quality
(X2), and marketing strategy (X 3), on the
endogenous variable of sustainable tourism
(Y2) with communication mix (Y1) as a mediat-
ing variable. This approach allows modelling
latent constructs through measurable indica-
tors and evaluating instrument reliability and
validity through testing the measurement
model, while also testing the strength and
significance of causal relationships in the
structural model. Data analysis was conducted
using SmartPLS software version 3.0, which is
suitable for exploratory research models, small
to medium sample sizes, and data conditions
that are not fully normally distributed.

Population and Sample
This study focuses on the tourism sector in
South Sulawesi, with the research population
consisting of domestic tourists who have
visited or are currently visiting major
destinations, namely Makassar City, Parepare
City, Tana Toraja Regency, and North Toraja
Regency. The sampling technique used was
purposive sampling, with the criteria for
respondents being at least 17 years old, being a
domestic tourist who is not a resident of the
destination area, and travelling for recreational
purposes. This approach ensures that
respondents have relevant travel experience to
assess tourism services, communication
strategies, and destination sustainability
practices. Determination of sample size in
quantitative research is guided by Structural
Equation Modelling (SEM) and takes into
account the number of indicators in the model.
Based on the Slovin formula and Sekaran's
recommendations (2013), a target sample of
±350 respondents was set to ensure data
adequacy and analytical strength.

ANALYSIS AND DISCUSSIONS
This study adheres to Hair et al.'s
methodological guidelines by evaluating two
core components of the research framework:
the structured modelling methodology—
including measurement model validity,
reliability testing, and hypothesis evaluation—
and the empirical application of SmartPLS
version 3 software for confirmatory structural
equation modelling (SEM). Indicators were
used to operationalise variables and ensure
comprehensive and valid measurement of
data (Tse & Tung, 2022).
The indicators for Tourist Destination
included amenities, uniqueness, infrastruc-
ture, activities, accessibility, safety, pricing,
and local friendliness. Tourism Services were
assessed based on tangible evidence,
reliability, responsiveness, assurance,
empathy, and expectations. Marketing Strategy
involved indicators of product value, pricing
fairness, distribution accessibility, promotional
effectiveness, service quality, and supporting
facilities. Communication Mix was measured by
the effectiveness of brochures, personal
interaction, public relations, promotional
offers, direct marketing, and social media.
Lastly, Sustainable Tourism was assessed using
six indicators: environment, economic
contribution, cultural preservation, resource
management, stakeholder engagement, and
political stability.
To assess Convergent validity, Average
Variance Extracted (AVE), Composite
Reliability (CR), and factor loadings were
examined. The results demonstrated that
most factor loadings exceeded the 0.70
threshold (Sarstedt et al., 2021) and that all
AVE values exceeded 0.5. Specifically, the
AVE values ranged from 0.582 to 0.651,
confirming the constructs' convergent
validity. The CR values ranged from 0.893 to
0.903, indicating strong internal consistency
reliability. These results, summarised in
Tables 3 to 5, validate the adequacy of the
reflective measurement model.

Tourist Destination Communication
Mix indicates that a tourism destination's
attractiveness, uniqueness, accessibility, and
infrastructure significantly enhance commu-
nication efforts. Well-developed destinations
that meet tourist expectations facilitate the
dissemination of effective promotional
content.
Tourism Services Communication Mix,
Quality services—measured by responsive-
ness, empathy, reliability, and service
tangibility—influence the tourism services
communication mix by building trust and
legitimacy. Specifically, well-delivered
services foster positive word of mouth,
stimulate social media sharing, and
strengthen confidence in formal promotional
materials. This demonstrates the close
relationship between service quality and
effective communication outcomes.
Marketing Strategy Communication
Mix and Sustainable Tourism clarifies that
pricing, product innovation, distribution,
and promotion shape the communication
message and, in turn, support sustainability.
When green marketing and destination
branding are integrated into strategy, the
communication mix becomes a tool for long-
term tourism development.
Communication Mix Sustainable Tourism
confirms that an effective communication mix
fosters tourist awareness of local cultures,
environmental conservation, and community-
based tourism. When tourists receive clear,
engaging, and value-aligned communication,
their behaviours become more responsible and
sustainability-oriented.
Mediating Role of Communication Mix
analysis demonstrates that the communication
mix amplifies the effects of other variables on
sustainable tourism. By shaping tourist
perception and behaviour, communication
channels serve as critical tools for translating
destination and service quality into sustainable
actions.
These findings demonstrate that integrat-
ing marketing, destination development, and
high-quality services—when mediated by
strategic communication—has a profound
impact on sustainable tourism. The empirical
evidence supports the study’s theoretical
framework and provides actionable insights
for tourism stakeholders, particularly in
regions such as South Sulawesi. Stakeholders
are encouraged to invest in destination
branding, improve service quality, and
leverage digital platforms to communicate
sustainable values effectively.
The findings also reinforce the theoretical
view that sustainable tourism depends not
only on destination or service quality but also

community involvement—into messages

CONCLUSION AND SUGGESTION
This study concludes that tourism
sustainability is not solely determined by a
destination's intrinsic strengths, the quality of
its services, or the intensity of its marketing
strategy, but is highly dependent on the
mechanisms by which these elements are
translated and channelled through the
communication mix. Unlike most previous
studies, which position communication as a
supporting variable or promotional
instrument, this study's findings demonstrate
that communication serves as a structural
mechanism that transforms tourism inputs
into sustainability outcomes. Thus, this study
closes the theoretical gap regarding how and
through what pathways destination attributes
and services contribute to sustainable tourism.
The main theoretical contribution of this
research lies in refining the role of the
communication mix as a dominant mediator,
rather than merely a partial mediator. The
resulting empirical model shows that the
direct influence of destinations and services
on sustainable tourism is relatively weaker
than the indirect influence through
communication. This finding revises the
assumption in the sustainable tourism
literature that tends to view sustainability as
a direct consequence of the quality of
resources and services, emphasizing that
sustainability values are communicative and
constructive, formed through tourists'
perceptions, meanings, and engagement.
Furthermore, this study advances the
theoretical framework of sustainable tourism
by integrating communication management
and marketing perspectives into a
destination sustainability model. By
positioning the communication mix as the
pivot connecting the destination, service, and
