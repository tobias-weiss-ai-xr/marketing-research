multidimensional conceptual framework rooted
in the Social Exchange Theory. VBM is
conceived as a higher -order measure with four
dimensions namely: value identification , value
quantification, value -based pricing, and value
communication. Customer advocacy is

successfully establish and share value with
customers, they tend to be repaid by customer
supportive and advocacy behavior. The  paper is

advocacy into one multidimensional framework.

1 INTRODUCTION

The modern business world is highly competitive and dynamic and Organizations
are increasingly shifting from transactional marketing toward value-oriented approaches.
of marketing to a higher plane where they focus on creating long -term value. Creating
superior value to customers has emerged a decisive factor in the success of organizations
because it is a key driver of customer satisfaction, loyalty, and long -term growth
(Chaudhary et al., 2026). As a result, the value -based marketing has become a strateg ic
paradigm as it aims to interpret, develop and provide value, in a manner that resonates
with the customer needs and organization goals.
In contrast to the short -term interactions of traditional marketing, value -based
marketing is based on a maximizing perceived customer value  which is achieved by a
system of integrated practices such as value identification, value quantification, value -
based pricing, and value communication (Wengler et al., 2026). The practices allow the
organizations to build and maintain important customer relationships through building
trust, satisfaction, and long-term engagement. Theoretically, the given approach is firmly
rooted in the Social Exchange Theory, according to which customers engage in
relationships based on a rational evaluation of benefits and costs, and the mutual manner
of actions like loyalty and advocacy.
Simultaneously, the concept of customer advocacy has been getting more and
more recognition as a crucial deliverable of a successful marketing plan as a step further
in terms of customer experience. It is an active type of customer behavior, where people
actively contribute to and market the organization as a result of the positive word -of-

Although the literature on value -based marketing and customer advocacy is

information sharing, and support of marketing research).

2 LITERATURE REVIEW

2.1 Value-Based Marketing (VBM)

In modern business conditions of accelerated technological change and
heightened competition, organizations are shifting from a transactional approach to value-
based marketing to value -based marketing (VBM) where the provision of superior
customer value is the primary focus of the marketing strategy (Verma & Bhattacharyya,
2017; Yaghtin & Gummerus, 2026). VBM takes a relational approach compared to the
conventional methods, which focus on short -term interactions and builds on the long -
term customer interaction, trust, and loyalty.
In principle, VBM is a strategic procedure whereby organizations design,
transmit, and convey value in line with customer anticipations as well as corporate goals.
It focuses on enhancing value when perceived benefits exceed costs, it affects how the
customers make their decisions and it encourages long term behavioral consequences
(Lopez, 2020). Notably, customer value does not remain fixed, but constantly changes
depending on customer experiences, preferences, and contextual forces and requires
adaptive and data-driven marketing approaches (Kojongian et al., 2026).
Theoretically, VBM is firmly rooted within the Social Exchange Theory (SET)
according to which people enter into relationships on the basis of rational assessments of
advantages and expenses. In this context, the ongoing provision of high -quality value
prompts the customers to pay back by providing good relational responses like trust,
loyalty, and advocacy (Liu et al., 2026). In so doing, value creation is not just a transaction
process but a major force behind long-term interaction of relations.
Moreover, the assumption of VBM is the integrated approach to customer
experience that does not simply focus on the attributes of the product, but also on the
quality of the service, brand reputation and relations. It is a unifying approach, where
organizations are required to be tenacious in their innovations and the discovery of new
sources of values to sustain the competitive edge and enhance the association with
customers (Supriadi et al., 2026; Winarko et al., 2022).

2.2 Dimensions of VBM

The implementation of VBM is operationalized through four interrelated
dimensions:
1. Value Identification:  Understanding customer needs, preferences, and
expectations to determine what constitutes value from the customer’s perspective
(Fakfare et al., 2025).
2. Value Quantification: Assessing and communicating the economic, functional,
and experiential benefits of offerings relative to costs (Lopez, 2020).
3. Value-Based Pricing:  Aligning pricing strategies with perceived value rather
than production costs, thereby enhancing fairness and transparency (Yaghtin &
Gummerus, 2026).
4. Value Communication:  Effectively conveying value propositions through
strategic communication to ensure customer awareness and appreciation (Fakfare
et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2026).
All these dimensions together constitute a well-developed system that shapes the
perception of customers and determines their behavioral reactions (Kulkarni, 2023).
Nevertheless, although previous studies have found connections between VBM and such
outcomes as satisfaction and loyalty, they have not paid significant attention to its
multidimensional influence on customer advocacy, especially in the context of the
emerging markets (Gezahegn et al., 2025). The presence of this gap indicates that an
improved approach in terms of analytical tools is required to explore the different roles
of individual VBM dimensions in advocacy behaviors.

2.3 Customer advocacy

Customer advocacy is a higher level of customer engagement than passive
satisfaction to active and voluntary support of the organization. It is a type of a relational
exchange where the customers are not just loyal but also refer others to the products and
services of the organization (Sweeney et al., 2020).
Strategically, customer advocacy is an intangible asset which can be described as
a valuable asset to the organization as it improves the performance of an organization by

prescribing the brand, experience sharing, and endorsing organizational activities, thus

In theory, SET has a strong explanation of customer advocacy behaviors. The
more customers feel that the organizations always offer better value, the higher the
chances of them developing trust and commitment which subsequently encourages the
occurrence of such behaviors as positive word -of-mouth and repurchase intention (Hsu

advocacy event.
Furthermore, customer advocacy is closely connected with the principles of
relationship marketing that imply long -term contact, creation of mutual value, and
emotional involvement. Ongoing service to customer need and development of relational
relationship encourages voluntary practices to be adopted by customers to foster
information sharing and involvement in processes within the organizations (Lucas, 2026;
Aluri et al., 2026). Such actions contribute to not only the strengthening of customer
loyalty but also competitive positioning by attracting new customers with the help of
credible and trusted communication channels (Bilgihan and Ricci, 2024).

2.4 Operational dimensions of customer advocacy

Customer advocacy can be operationalized through several key behavioral
dimensions:
1. Word-of-Mouth Recommendation: Customers who are ready to recommend the
organization to other people.
2. Repurchase Intention: The chance of the repeat buying behavior and the long -
term loyalty.
3. Information Sharing: Personal involvement in the sharing of organizational
knowledge and experiences.

3 RESEARCH GAP AND HYPOTHESES DEVELOPMENT

3.1 Research gap

Although the literature in the area of (VBM) and customer advocacy continues to
expand, there are still several areas of critical gaps that have not been fully addressed.
First, past research has focused more on the value creation when it comes to customer
satisfaction and loyalty, with little emphasis on customer advocacy as a more advanced
behavioral outcome (Gezahegn et al., 2025). Though advocacy is a more sophisticated
and powerful type of customer interaction, its connection to the value-based marketing is
unexplored.
Second, current studies have predominantly addressed value -based marketing as
a one-dimensional concept and have not considered the concept as multidimensional. In
particular, there is a lack of empirical data on the extent to which the individual
dimensions of VBM that include value identification, value quantification, value -based
pricing, and value communication vary in terms of their effects on the diverse forms of
customer advocacy behavior. Such granularity prevents the comprehension of the details
of how value is converted into customer-based outcomes.
Third, the literature lacks contextual gaps since most empirical researches have
been carried out in developed economies. As a result, few such studies are based on
emerging market situations, where the market forces, consumer expectations, and
relationship norms might be very different (Töytäri &Rajala, 2015). This gap needs to be
filled to ensure the generalizability and external validity of the value -based marketing
theories.

3.2 Hypotheses development

The customer behavior is based on the Social Exchange Theory which explains
the behavior as a result of the perceived value exchanges whereby people will reciprocate
positive actions of the organization with good behavioral response. As organizations are
effective in identifying, quantifying, pricing, and communicating value, customers will
be better position to make a positive judgment about the balance between benefits and
costs and achieve better relational performance, including advocacy (Liu et al., 2026).
Such an integrated strategy as value -based marketing should thus assume a
leading role in achieving customer advocacy. Through consistently providing high quality
values, organizations are able to build on trust, satisfaction and commitment which will
in t urn trigger voluntary behavior like recommending the organization, rebuying its
products, sharing information and supporting its initiative.
Accordingly, the following hypothesis is proposed:
H1: Value-based marketing has a significant positive effect on customer
advocacy.
Value-based marketing is a complex construct in itself; thus, the analysis of the
effects of the dimensions separately is necessary to gain a more intricate insight into how
it impacts customer advocacy. Identification of value helps organizations to match  its
offerings to customer needs and expectations, and consequently to achieve increased
perceived relevance and satisfaction. Quantification of the values helps to understand the
advantages compared to the expenses and support the ideas of equity and sens ible
decision-making. Value -based pricing also reinforces the value of the perception by
having clear and fair pricing policies whereas value communication makes the customers
fully perceive and comprehend the value provided.

4 DATA AND METHODOLOGY

4.1 Research design

The research design proposed in the current study is quantitative design using
descriptive-analytical research design in order to test the relationships among value-based
marketing and customer advocacy (Bryman and Bell, 2011). The quantitative approach
is especially appropriate when it is necessary to test theoretically based hypotheses and
examine the relationship between latent constructs with the help of sophisticated
statistical methods.
In order to meet the research objectives, cross -sectional survey design was used
to gather customer perceptions on the research variables (Dillman et al., 2014; Saunders
et al., 2011).

4.2 Population and sample

The population of interest included the customers of the Nour Al -Kafeel
Institution of animal and food products in Karbala, Iraq and was estimated to be about
500 people. The sampling strategy was a census-based one, in which 450 questionnaires
were sent, and 430 valid responses were received, leading to a high response rate of 95.6%
(Dillman et al., 2014; Creswell and Creswell, 2017).
This good response rate increases the reliability and representativeness of the
findings in context of the organisation. Table 2 shows demographics of the respondents
to study the possible differences among customer groups (Bryman and Bell, 2011).

4.3 Measurement instrument

A structured questionnaire based on scales that have been validated in the
literature before was used to gather data (DeVellis and Thorpe, 2021; Bryman and Bell,
2011). The instrument was in two substantial parts:
1. Demographic information (e.g., age, gender, education level, and experience).
2. Measurement items that reflect value -based marketing and customer advocacy,
and the dimension of both.
The items were all measured on a five -point Likert scale (1 strongly disagree, 5
strongly agree) (DeVellis and Thorpe, 2021). A panel of academic experts in marketing
and management was involved in the review of the questionnaire to achieve content
validity, clarity, and contextual relevance (Hair et al., 2024).

4.4 Data collection procedure

The questionnaire was conducted face -to-face during two weeks, which helped
clarify the points of ambiguity and had a positive impact on the high response rate
(Dillman et al., 2014). The participants were advised on the objective of the research and
assured that their answers will be anonymous and confidential and will not be utilized in
any other way (Bryman and Bell, 2011).

4.5 Reliability and validity

Multiple reliability and validity criteria were used to evaluate the
measurement model:
Internal consistency reliability was measured by Cronbach alpha and Composite
Reliability (CR), and was found to be above the recommended value of 0.70 (Hair et al.,
2024).
Convergent validity  was measured by Average Variance Extracted (AVE),
where a value exceeding 0.50 is a good convergence.
The FornellLarcker criterion  was used to determine discriminant validity
making sure that the square root of AVE of each construct was greater than its correlations
with the rest of the constructs.
The findings indicate that the scales employed in measurement are valid and
reliable.

4.6 Data analysis technique

Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) was used to test the hypotheses using
AMOS 26. The reason SEM was used is that it is able to quantify both the measurement
and structural models to provide a more detailed analysis compared to the traditional
regression techniques (Hair et al., 2022; Byrne, 2016).
The analysis has been done in two steps:
1. Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA): to test the measurements model and make
sure that the observed variables are a good representation of latent constructs.
2. Structural Model Analysis: to test the relationship between value-based marketing
and customer advocacy dimensions, which was hypothesized to be.
The following indices were used in assessing model fit:
Chi-square/df (< 3), Comparative Fit Index (CFI > 0.90), Tucker -Lewis Index
(TLI > 0.90) , Root mean Square Error of Approximation (RMSEA) below 0.08, This
strategy makes the empirical findings sound and valid.
