1 INTRODUCTION

Human economic activity is deemed to be one of the main causes of waste
problems and environmental degradation because it requires input in the form of
enormous materials and energy, as well as producing considerable amounts of waste
(Volery & Thierry, 2002). The United Nations Environment Program - in its report also
states that economic development is one of the main causes of environmental degradation
(Asnake, n.d.) . The G20 Forum also highlighted facts related to environmental
degradation and the importance of taking it seriously. It is said that although the G2 0
countries control around 80% of the world economy, they also produce around 80% of
global greenhouse gas emissions. The Environment Deputies Meeting and Climate
Sustainability Working Group (EDM-CSWG), hihlighted the priority issue of increasing
action a nd increasing resource mobilization to support environmental protection and
climate change control objectives (“Kementerian Lingkungan Hidup dan Kehutanan
Republik Indonesia,” 2022).
In Indonesia, data from the National Waste Management Information System
under Ministry of Environment and Forestry, notes that the total waste generation from
243 regencies/cities throughout Indonesia reached 30.8 million tons, and only 65.14% has
been managed, while the remaining 34.86% has not been managed (Kementerian
Perindustrian Republik Indonesia, 2022). The Ministry of Environment stated that there
are 3 (three) largest waste producing industries that are encouraged to manage waste

2.1 Social marketing

marketing, where marketing is no longer interpreted as limited to two -way transactions
for the exchange of economic resources between parties. In his argument, Kotler
emphasized that marketing needs to be viewed from a broader perspective. Generic
marketing takes a functional rather than a structural view of marketing, with four axioms
namely:

This explanation also emphasized that transactions, as a core marketing concept,

created, stimulated, facilitated, and rewarded. This concept forms the basic foundation
for the development of social marketing.
Andreasen (1994)  defines social marketing as an adaptation of commercial
marketing technology to programs designed to influence the voluntary behavior of target
audiences, with the aim of improving their personal well-being and that of the society of
which they are a part. This definition is also in line with the opinion of Peattie & Peattie
(2009)  which states that social marketing is a form of marketing that seeks to utilize
tools, techniques and concepts originating from commercial marketing in pursuing social
goals.
 The fundamental difference between social marketing and traditional marketing
lies in its objectives. Traditional marketing is more oriented towards achieving profit,
while the goals of social marketing are more directed at efforts to influence people's
behavior by understanding why and how behavior needs to be changed (Phillip Kotler &
Keller, 2016). When it comes to changing behavior, these disciplines do not stand alone.
Other fields of knowledge such as psychology, sociology, anthropology and
communications also contribute to the understanding of behavior change (Gordon, 2013).
Social marketing is based on the assumption that people will adopt new behavior if they
perceive the new behavior to be beneficial and achievable for themselves and others
(Futterman et al., 2001). Under the right conditions, this social persuasion could influence
the belief that oneself has the ability to organize and mobilize the resources necessary to
manage future situations (Liguori, Bendickson, & McDowell, 2018). This argument leads

2.2 Message framing

One technique widely used by social marketers to shape messages is manipulative
framing (Rothman & Salovey, 1997) . Framing effects are obser ved when consumers
faced with several options change their choices in response to modifications in the
reference frames of these options (Berger & Nasr, 1998). Maddux & Stanley (1986) argue
that a message can be presented in terms of potential benefits (i.e., advantages) or in terms
of losses (i.e., risks associated with not performin g the behavior). Benefit -focused
messages emphasize the benefits of adopting green behaviors, while loss -focused
messages focus on the potential downsides or the costs of not taking certain actions
(Leshner, Cheng, & Leshner, 2009).
Prospect Theory proposed by Kahneman & Tversky (1979 ) is one of the
underpinning theory for understanding "message framing".  It should be noted that
prospect theory suggests that individuals are risk -averse when faced with a choice that
involves a loss, even when the winning and losing options have the same probability (Kim
& Kim, 2014) . Some researchers employed gain - and loss-framed communications to
promote recycling and discovered that participants exposed to loss -framed messages
processed messages more carefully (i.e., had more cognitive thoughts) than those exposed
to gain -framed ones (Corner et al., 2015; Grazzini, Rodrigo, A iello, & Viglia, 2018;
Huang, Ma, & Wang, 2021).

2.3 Theory of planned behavior

The Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) is a development of the Theory of
Reasoned Action (TRA). This theory also states that a person's behavior is driven by the
person's intention to behave, but in this theory, behavioral intention is a function of the
attitude towards a behavior (attitude), subjective norms, and the person's perception of
the ease of realizing that behavior (behavioral control).

Attitude towards behavior re fers to an individual's positive or negative feelings
about carrying out a behavior. This attitude arises from the individual's evaluation of the
possible consequences of a behavior. Subjective norms are defined as an individual's
perception of how people (who are considered important to the individual) consider the
need for this behavior to be executed. Behavioral control is defined as an individual's
perception of how difficult a behavior is to carry out – is it easy to do or will it require a
lot of effort, resources, time, and many more. Ajzen, Madden, & Ellen (1992) proves that
precision in predicting the influence of behavioral intentions on behavior could be done
by assessing an individual's perception of behavioral control. This shows that by changing
an individual's perception of their behavioral control, the greater the o pportunity to
increase behavioral intentions that lead to the realization of that behavior. This provides
a conceptual basis that efforts to communicate messages on pro -environmental
sustainability that can increase intentions to act will tend to encourage behavioral change.
