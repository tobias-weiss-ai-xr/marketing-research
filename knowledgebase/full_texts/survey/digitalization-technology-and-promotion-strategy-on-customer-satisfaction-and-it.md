Abstract: The Indonesian ship agency business is at the heart of the country’s archipelagic
trade connectivity, yet their ability to translate the service digitalization and promotional
communication into long -lasting customer relationship is empirically under -explored,
especially through the lens of Customer Lifetime Value (CLV). This research aims to analyze
the effect of Digitalization Technology and Promotion Strategy on Customer Satisfaction and
its implications for CLV, with Customer Satisfaction as a mediating variable, in the setting of
a foreign ship agency service provider, PT Pelayaran Karana Line. A cross-sectional approach
was used in a quantitative explanatory research design. The primary data was collected through
an online structured questionnaire to 1 50 respondents which were determined using Slovin’s
formula from 240 shipping principals’ principal representatives from 24 shipping principals
with a purposive sampling technique. Data were analyzed using Partial Least Square Structural
Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) with SmartPLS 4.0. The measurement model showed
acceptable convergent validity (AVE 0.637 -0.693), discriminant validity and reliability
(composite reliability 0.953 -0.970) for all constructs. The structural model showed good
explanatory power (R² of 0.727 for Customer Satisfaction and 0.909 for CLV; Q² = 0.975). All
seven hypothesized relationships were statistically supported (p < 0.05). Digitalization
Technology and Promotion Strategy have a significant and positive effect on Customer
Satisfaction and CLV, both directly and indirectly through Customer Satisfaction, which is the
strongest direct predictor of CLV (β = 0.654). These findings extend the nomological network
between digitalization and marketing communication to customer value in a busi ness-to-
business maritime service context and offer practical guidance to ship agencies for reinforcing
automation, promotional value communication and relationship-continuity management.

Keywords: Digitalization Technology, Promotion Strategy, Customer Satisfaction, Customer
Lifetime Value, Ship Agency, PLS-SEM.

INTRODUCTION
Indonesia is an archipelagic country with more than 17,000 islands, relying heavily on
maritime transportation to support trade and logistical connectivity. Ship agency services have
a strategic role in this system in handling operational, documentary, and administrative
requirements of foreign shipping principals calling at Indonesian ports  (Lasse, 2018; Santoso
& Wibowo, 2022). With the growing competition among agency service providers nationally
and internationally, firms are increasingly expected to build long-term, economically valuable
relationships with their principals over transacti onal gains. Customer lifetime value (CLV) is
an important strategic lens through which agency performance can be assessed (Kumar, 2020).
This challenge is empirically described by PT Pelayaran Karana Line, a foreign ship
agency service provider operating f rom Jakarta. A preliminary survey of 30 users of the
company’s agency services showed that the dominant pattern of response across the
dimensions of repurchase intention, usage intensity and relationship continuity was to disagree,
which meant that CLV had  not yet reached an optimal level. This trend was also seen in the
company’s financial performance, with revenue growing from IDR 20.47 billion in 2021 to
IDR 121.67 billion in 2024, but saw a significant drop in 2023. Net profit after tax grew from
IDR 5.22 billion in 2021 to IDR 13.62 billion in 2022 and then decreased for two consecutive
years to IDR 7.52 billion in 2024. Because CLV is not only about the size of transactions but
also about the frequency, depth of engagement, and duration of the relationship, this volatility
suggests an incomplete alignment between the company's digital service capabilities, its
promotional communication, and the value it ultimately retains from its customer base.

Two common management levers for building stronger custome r relationships in
services are digitalization technology and promotion strategy. Digitalization technology,
defined as the incorporation of digital infrastructure, automated workflows and secure
information management into service delivery, has been shown to affect customer perceptions
and satisfaction in container shipping and international shipping platforms (Balci, 2021; Loh
et al., 2024). Promotion strategy, including the clarity, consistency and perceived value of
marketing communication, has long bee n recognized as a driver of customer perception and
relationship continuity in service marketing at the same time (Kotler & Keller, 2021; Chaffey
& Ellis -Chadwick, 2022). However, the pathway through which these two levers together
translate into long-term customer value, and the mediating role that customer satisfaction plays
in that pathway, have rarely been empirically tested within a single structural model for
maritime agency services.
A review of recent literature bolsters this gap. Research on CLV ha s explored its
association with sustainability and search -engine metrics (Segarra -Moliner, 2025), strategic
marketing practice and financial performance (Ali & Shabn, 2024), and customer relationship
management (Brando et al., 2025; Herlinton et al., 2025;  Mahendra & Indriani, 2025), while
research on digitalization in shipping has focused on customer satisfaction and trust but not
extended the analysis to CLV (Balci, 2021; Wu et al., 2023; Loh et al., 2024; Zeng et al., 2025).
Meanwhile, studies on Indones ian ship agency services have more or less discussed service
quality and operational barriers but not the combined effect of digitalization and promotion on
customer value (Haryanta et al., 2024; Sianipar & Ginting, 2024; Kusuma & Prasetya, 2022;
Imaduddiin et al., 2025). To the best of the authors' knowledge, no prior study has investigated
Digitalization Technology and Promotion Strategy simultaneously as antecedents of Customer
Satisfaction and CLV by using a full mediation model in the setting of a busi ness-to-business
foreign ship agency in Indonesia. This study fills that gap and, in doing so, provides both a
theoretical extension of the CLV nomological network to B2B maritime services, and practical
guidance for shipping agencies looking to turn digital and promotional investment into lasting
customer relationships.
Based on this background, the research examines seven questions regarding the direct
effect of Digitalization Technology and Promotion Strategy on Customer Satisfaction and
CLV, the direct effect of Customer Satisfaction on CLV, and the indirect effect of
Digitalization Technology and Promotion Strategy on CLV through Customer Satisfaction.
Correspondingly, this study aims to empirically analyze each of these seven relationships by
using data collected from the principal representatives of a foreign ship agency service provider
in Jakarta.
Digital infrastructure, automated processes and information security reduce friction and
increase trust in the delivery of service, which prior research ha s linked to higher customer
satisfaction in shipping related services (Balci, 2021; Loh et al., 2024). The regular
digitalization of services further enhances customers’ willingness to sustain and deepen the
relationship over time (Zeng et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2023). This indicates:

H1: Digitalization Technology has an effect positively and significantly to Customer
Satisfaction.
H3: Digitalization Technology positively and significantly affects Customer Lifetime Value.

Clear and consistent promotional communication helps form customer expectations and
when service delivery meets these expectations, it increases customer satisfaction and leads to
long-term commitment (Kotler & Armstrong, 2021; Farris et al., 2021; Justavi no-Castillo et
al., 2023). This line of thought leads to:

H2: Promotion Strategy has positive and significant impact on Customer Satisfaction.

H4: Promotion Strategy has a positive and significant impact on Customer Lifetime Value.

Customer satisfaction is a close driver of long -term customer value, as satisfied
customers are more likely to repurchase, deepen usage, and sustain the relationship (Zeithaml
et al., 2021; Herlinton et al., 2025; Mahendra & Indriani, 2025). Thus:

H5: Customer Satisfaction has a positive and significant impact on Customer Lifetime Value.

As satisfaction is, in theory, a mediator of the effect of service and marketing inputs on
relational outcomes (Lovelock & Wirtz, 2021), Digitalization Technology and Promotion
Strategy should also have an indirect effect on CLV via Customer Satisfaction:

H6: Digitalization Technology has a positive and significant influence on Customer Lifetime
Value through Customer Satisfaction.
H7: The mediating variable of the effect of Promotion Strategy on Customer Lifetime Value is
Customer Satisfaction.

METHODS
Research Design
The research design used in this study is a quantitative explanatory research design with
a cross-sectional approach, where the independent variables and the dependent variables are
measured simultaneously. The variables studied were the effect of Digitalization Technology
(X1) and Promotion Strategy (X2) on Customer Lifetime Value (Y) through Customer
Satisfaction (Z) as an intervening variable.

Population and Sample
The population was all of the principals/customers of foreign ship agency services at PT
Pelayaran Karana Line in 2025. The population consisted of 24 shipping principal with an

average of 10 relevant staff per princi pal (Director/General Manager, Operations/Fleet
Manager, Chartering/Commercial Manager, Shipping Manager, Port Captain/Marine
Superintendent, Finance Manager, Accounting Staff, Documentation/Shipping Administration
Staff, Logistics/Cargo Officer, and Opera tional Staff/Liaison Officer) with a total population
of 240. The sample size was determined by using the Slovin’s formula with 5% margin of error
which resulted to a minimum sample of 150 respondents. The respondents were purposely
sampled from the 24 principals in proportion to their level of direct involvement in the use and
decision making of the company’s foreign ship agency services.

Data Collection
Main data were collected through a structured online questionnaire using a four -point
Likert scale with the endpoints of 1 (strongly disagree) and 4 (strongly agree) with no option
for a neutral midpoint to prompt a definitive response bias. The instrument was pre tested on
50 respondents for validity and reliability before full scale distribution.

Data Analysis Technique
Data were analyzed using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -
SEM) using SmartPLS 4.0 with a two-stage evaluation procedure (Hair et al, 2021; Ghozali &
Latan, 2021). The measurement (outer) model was assessed for convergent validity (ou ter
loading > 0.6, AVE > 0.5), discriminant validity (Fornell-Larcker criterion and cross-loadings)
and reliability (Cronbach's alpha and composite reliability > 0.7). The structural (inner) model
was assessed by the coefficient of determination (R²), the predictive relevance (Q²), and
Goodness-of-Fit indices (SRMR, d_ULS), and the hypothesis testing was performed using a
bootstrapping procedure (5,000 resamples, two-tailed test, α = 0.05, t-table = 1.96).

Of the 1 50 valid respondents, 88.7% (n = 133) were male and 11.3% (n = 17) were
female. The majority were aged 21 -30 years (78.0%), followed by 31 -40 years (17.3%).

remainder had longer -standing relationships with the company, providing a respondent base
directly engaged in the operational use of the company's agency services.

All seven hypotheses were statistically significant at p < 0.05. Both direct and indirect
effects of Digitalization Technology and Promotion Strategy on CLV are positiv e and
significant, and Customer Satisfaction functions as a significant partial mediator in both
pathways, as the direct effects of X1 and X2 on Y (H3, H4) remain significant as well as their
indirect effects through Z (H6, H7).

Discussion
Digitalization Technology and Customer Satisfaction (H1, H3, H6). Out of the two
exogenous variables, Digitalization Technology has the most direct impact on Customer
Satisfaction (β = 0.577). This is consistent with the results that customer perception in shipping
services is influenced by digital platforms and trust-enabling systems (Loh et al., 2024; Balci,
2021; Wu et al., 2023). Among the dimensions of the construct, the dimension with the highest
loading was the Digital Information Security dimension, which means that data protection and
system security are the most salient aspect of digitalization from the customer’s perspective.
The other dimension, process automation, was comparatively the weakest dimension, although
it was highly loaded. This means that while PT Pelayaran Karana Line’s customers already
view the company’s systems as secure and reliable, there is an immediate opportunity to
improve the perception of digitalization by further automating documentation and service
workflows, in line with the recommendations for maritime logistics providers to close existing
automation gaps so that they can fully benefit from digitalization (Zeng et al., 2025).
Promotion Strategy and Customer Satisfaction (H2, H4, H7). Promotion Strategy has a
significant effect on Cust omer Satisfaction (β = 0.374) and CLV both directly and indirectly
via satisfaction. This study supports the view that consistent and credible marketing
communication shapes customer expectations and thus satisfaction and relational commitment
(Kotler & Ar mstrong, 2021; Farris et al., 2021). The most dominant dimension was the
Marketing Perspective dimension, which captures clarity and consistency of promotional
messaging, while Customer Value communication was comparatively weaker, but still strongly
loaded, suggesting the need to more explicitly frame promotional messaging around the value-
for-cost proposition of the company’s services.
Customer Satisfaction and CLV (H5). The strongest predictor of CLV in the model is
Customer Satisfaction (β = 0.654, t = 9.809) which confirms the central mediating role of
satisfaction between service/marketing inputs and long -term customer value and is consistent
with service-marketing theory (Lovelock & Wirtz, 2021) and with prior CLV studies in digital
and financial serv ices contexts (Herlinton et al., 2025; Mahendra & Indriani, 2025). Within
Customer Satisfaction, Expectation Congruence dimension – specifically the alignment
between promised service and delivered service – had the highest loading while Willingness to
Recommend was relatively the lowest. This means that while customers are satisfied with the
service delivered, the translation of that satisfaction into active promotion and referrals is still
a work in progress.
Customer Satisfaction as Mediator (H6, H7) The results show a partial mediation, since
the direct effects of Digitalization Technology and Promotion Strategy on CLV are still
significant along with their significant indirect effects through Customer Satisfaction:
Customer Satisfaction significantly im proves the impact of digitalization and promotion on

CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS
Conclusion
This study demonstrates that Digitalization Technology and Promotion Strategy each
significantly and positively influence Customer Satisfaction and Customer Lifetime Value,
both directly and indirectly through Customer Satisfaction, among customers of a foreign ship
agency service provider in Indonesia. Customer Satisfaction was confirmed as a significant
partial mediator, with its own direct effect o n CLV being the strongest relationship in the
model. All seven hypotheses were supported, with the structural model explaining 90.9% of
the variance in CLV - a level of explanatory power that underscores the robustness of the
proposed integrated framework for this service context.

Limitations
This study is limited by its cross -sectional design that enables us to only observe the
relationships at a single point in time. Furthermore, this study is limited to a single company
which may limit the generalizabi lity to other ship agency providers or maritime service
segments. Future studies are encouraged to test the model in longitudinal settings, in multiple
agency companies or other maritime B2B service categories and to investigate further
mediating or modera ting variables such as digital trust, service quality or customer
commitment to further enrich understanding of how digitalization and promotion translate to
long-term customer value in the maritime sector.
