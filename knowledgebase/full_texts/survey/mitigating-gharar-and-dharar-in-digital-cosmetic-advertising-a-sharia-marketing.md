Abstract
This study investigates the aggressive digital marketing strategies utilized by
contemporary cosmetic brands to capture evolving consumer demographics.
Specifically, the research aims to examine the mechanisms of influencer branding
deployed by Glad2Glow o n digital platforms and analyze these promotional
methods through the conceptual lens of Sharia Marketing to establish ethical -legal
boundaries for modern digital commerce. Methodologically, this study adopts a
normative juridical research design integrate d with conceptual and
phenomenological approaches. Data collection was systematically conducted
through an extensive literature review, analysis of formal Islamic jurisprudence
materials, and structural social media monitoring on TikTok, focusing on both
primary and secondary qualitative legal -ethical sources. The empirical findings
reveal that Glad2Glow’s partnership with prominent beauty influencers successfully
cultivates deep consumer trust and strong emotional attachments through a distinct
combination of radical honesty and multi -day user -progress storytelling. From a
Sharia Marketing viewpoint, this branding framework satisfies the core pillars of
Akhlaqiyyah (ethics) and Al -Waqi’iyyah (realism) by effectively mitigating
informational asymmetry ( tadlis) and commercial overclaiming ( gharar). However,
critical vulnerabilities emerge regarding the Al-Insaniyyah (humanism) and
Rabbaniyyah (theism) dimensions, where risks of physical skin irritation (dharar) and

THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK
Sharia Marketing Framework
Sharia marketing represents  an ideological shift from
conventional transaction models, defining a business paradigm where
all operational phases, value creation, and communication
frameworks are explicitly aligned with the ethical boundaries of
Islamic jurisprudence (Sharia). Unlike  conventional marketing
platforms that prioritize profit maximization, customer acquisition
velocity, and material commercial gains, Sharia marketing merges
commercial ambitions with spiritual accountability (Falah). According
to Kartajaya and Sula, this operational framework is structurally
anchored by four core philosophical pillars:7
1. Rabbaniyyah (Theistic/Divine)
This pillar dictates that every market actor must operate
under the conscious awareness that all economic instruments,
resources, and transactional spaces belong ultimately to Allah
SWT. Consequently, commercial strategies are not value-free but
are fundamentally constrained by divine laws, transparency, and
spiritual governance.
2. Akhlaqiyyah (Ethical)
This dimension governs the behavior, structural
presentation, and moral conduct of the enterprise. It demands
that promotion, pricing, and distribution channels must be
completely clean of deceptive maneuvers, vanity, and moral

and accumulated social capital of a social media public figure.
Consumers in the digital age exhibit a profound skepticism toward
traditional, corporate -centric advertisements; instead, they seek
authentic, personalized validation from peer -like figures within their

The Intersections of Gharar, Tadlis, and Dharar in Social Commerce
The massive commercial reliance on influencer branding
introduces structural loopholes that risk violating Islamic consumer
protection standards, specifically through the introduction of gharar,
tadlis, and dharar. In modern marketing, gharar manifests as acute
information asymmetry, a condition wherein the promotional
campaign actively hides critical product risks or utilizes exaggerated,
unscientific claims to manipulate consumer expectations. When an
influencer broadcasts bombastic results without sound empirical
backing, it leaves the consumer in a state of speculative uncertainty
regarding the actual efficacy and biological outcome of the
transaction.
