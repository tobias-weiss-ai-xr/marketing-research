# Analysis of Factors Influencing Youth Engagement In Agribusiness For Sustainable Food Security In Kenya: A Case Study Of Narumoru, Kieni Sub County

Abstract:
Youth engagement in agribusiness is critical for achieving sustainable food security in Kenya, particularly
in rural areas where agriculture forms the backbone of livelihoods. This study investigates the factors
influencing youth participation in agribusiness within Narumoru, Kieni Sub -County, with the aim of
identifying barriers and opportunities to enhance their contribution to food security. The research objectives
were to assess demographic characteristics, access to capital, technological a doption, the role of training,
and awareness of government policies affecting youth agribusiness engagement. A descriptive cross -
sectional survey was conducted involving 216 youth respondents aged 18 –35, selected through stratified
random sampling. Data we re collected using structured questionnaires supplemented by focus group
discussions. Quantitative data were analyzed using descriptive statistics presented in tables and figures.
Results revealed that the majority of youth participants were aged 25 –30 yea rs, with males slightly
outnumbering females. About 70% showed awareness of agribusiness opportunities, and a similar
proportion recognized agricultural technologies, with mobile applications being the most widely used
technology. However, financial constraints, notably insufficient collateral and high interest rates, emerged
as the major barriers to accessing capital. Training participation stood at 65%, positively impacting skills
in pest control, marketing, and financial management. Despite the existence  of government agribusiness
policies, only 15% of respondents were aware of them, highlighting poor dissemination. The study also
identified challenges such as high technology costs, limited knowledge, and inadequate infrastructure as
impediments to full youth participation. In conclusion, while youth in Narumoru exhibit strong interest and
potential in agribusiness, their engagement is limited by financial, technological, and informational barriers.
Addressing these through targeted financial support, capa city building, and improved policy outreach is
essential to harness youth potential and promote sustainable food security in Kenya.
Keywords: agribusiness, youth, sustainable food security
1. Introduction
Agriculture remains a cornerstone of Kenya's economy, contributing significantly to employment, income
generation, and food security (FAO, 2017). Despite its potential, youth engagement in agribusiness has
been suboptimal, raising concerns about the sector's sustainability and the nation's food security. Notably,
only about 10% of Kenyan youth are directly participating in the agricultural sector, even though
agriculture employs approximately 60% of the total labor force (FAO, 2017 Farm Africa, 2021). This low
engagement is alarming, considering that the average age of a Kenyan farmer is 60, indicating a potential
future labor gap in agriculture (Farm Africa, 2021). The study focused on Narumoru in Kieni Sub-County,
examining factors influencing youth participation in agribusiness, including access to capital,
technological innovations, agricultural training and education, and government policies.
Globally, agriculture is undergoing a transformation driven by technological advancements, climate
change, and shifting demographics. The integration of technology in to farming practices has attracted
youth in various regions. For instance, in Eastern Kenya's semi -arid counties, initiatives led by
organizations like Tegemeo have engaged youth in providing extension services and distributing bio -
fortified seeds, enhancing their participation in agribusiness (2Scale, 2023). However, existing literature
has not sufficiently analyzed the contextual effectiveness of such initiatives in remote or under-resourced
areas like Narumoru. Access to capital is a universal challenge for young agripreneurs. Limited financial
support often hinders their ability to invest in modern farming techniques and expand their ventures. A
recent study highlighted that significant determinants of youth participation in agriculture include age,
education, farming skills, social group membership, and household revenue, while factors like household
size, gender, and limited access to credit negatively impact willingness to participate (Mmbando &
Linderhof, 2022). However, these studies often focus on broader national or regional contexts and do not
offer granular, location-specific insights necessary for targeted interventions.

In Africa, the agricultural sector holds immense potential for youth employment and economic growth.
However, challenges such as limited access to land, inadequate financing, and insufficient training impede
youth participation. A systematic review identified factors such as access to finance, education, skills
training, perceived social support, and prior experience in farming as  critical to youth engagement in
agribusiness (Tufa et al., 2023). Yet, such reviews offer generalized recommendations, overlooking unique
socio-economic and ecological dynamics of semi-arid regions like Kieni.
Technological innovations have emerged as cat alysts for youth involvement in agriculture, with specific
technologies playing pivotal roles in attracting young people to the sector. One such technology is
Information and Communication Technologies (ICTs), which have been instrumental in changing the
perception of farming among the youth. A study focusing on Kenya demonstrated that the use of ICTs in
agriculture has made farming more appealing and profitable to young people, thereby increasing their
participation in the sector (Munyua, 2015). Nevertheless, the adoption and impact of ICTs specifically in
semi-arid areas such as Narumoru remains under -explored. Digital agricultural technologies, including
drones, climate applications, and the Internet of Things (IoT), have also contributed to making farmin g
more attractive to youth. These technologies present farming as a modern and lucrative venture. For
example, Kenya's leading telecom operator, Safaricom, developed the DigiFarm app to provide
smallholder farmers with access to quality farm inputs at discounted prices, input loans, and farming and
market information services, targeting tech -savvy youth (African Agricultural Transformation, 2023).
Despite their potential, barriers such as digital illiteracy and affordability continue to limit their
accessibility in marginalized regions.
Furthermore, the integration of artificial intelligence (AI) in agriculture offers promising avenues for youth
engagement. By utilizing AI technologies such as machine learning, remote sensing, and data analytics,
young farmers can access valuable insights, optimize resource utilization, and mitigate risks associated
with agricultural production. This technological advancement not only enhances productivity but also
aligns with the technological inclinations of the younger gen eration (Olabisi, 2024). However, current
literature does not adequately assess how AI can be effectively localized to cater to youth farmers in
specific areas like Narumoru.
In Kenya, youth unemployment remains a pressing issue, with the agricultural sect or offering viable
solutions for job creation. Despite this potential, youth participation in agriculture has been declining. A
study assessing the challenges hindering Kenyan youth from participating in the agricultural sector found
that factors such as access to land, agricultural finance, and insurance significantly affect their engagement
(FAO, 2017).  Access to capital remains a significant barrier for Kenyan youth aspiring to enter the
agribusiness sector. Young entrepreneurs often face challenges such  as lack of collateral, limited credit
history, and insufficient financial literacy, which hinder their ability to secure funding from traditional
financial institutions (Muthoni, 2015). Government initiatives like the Youth Enterprise Development
Fund (YEDF) and the Kenya Youth Agribusiness Strategy aim to address these issues; however, their
effectiveness is often compromised by limited outreach, bureaucratic delays, and inadequate awareness
among potential beneficiaries (Youth Enterprise Development Fund , 2019; Ministry of Agriculture,
Livestock, Fisheries and Irrigation, 2018).
The Kenyan government recognized these challenges and developed strategies to promote youth
engagement in agribusiness. The Kenya Youth Agribusiness Strategy 2017-2021 outlined mechanisms to
increase youth involvement in agriculture, addressing issues like access to land, financial services, and
markets (FAO, 2017). However, these strategies have not yielded meaningful results due to persistent
systemic barriers such as inadequate funding, limited implementation of policies, and bureaucratic
inefficiencies (Njenga & Muthomi, 2022). Moreover, youth-targeted agribusiness programs often fail due
to poor coordination between government agencies, lack of awareness among young people, and
difficulties in accessing credit facilities (Kariuki & Mugo, 2023). These shortcomings highlight the need

3
for more structured and effective interventions to ensure youth fully benefit from agribusiness initiatives,
especially in overlooked regions like Kieni Sub-County.
Agricultural training and education are pivotal in enhancing youth participation in agribusiness. Equipping
young people with relevant skills and knowledge increases their confidence and competence in managing
agricultural ventures. The Engaging Kenyan Youth in Agriculture and Nutrition (EKYAN) program aims
to provide young people, especially women, with market -driven skills and on -the-job learning for
employment and entrepreneurship opportunities in agribusiness (Generation Unlimited, 2023) . The
national and county governments of Kenya have also implemented several programs to build youth
capacity in agribusiness. The Ajira Digital Program, in collaboration with the Ministry of Agriculture,
integrates digital skills training to help youth leverage technology in agribusiness (Ministry of ICT, 2023).
Additionally, the Kenya Climate Smart Agriculture Project (KCSAP) has provided training to young
farmers on climate -resilient farming techniques, enhancing their productivity and sustainability (Wo rld
Bank, 2022).  County governments have also initiated localized agribusiness training programs. For
instance, Nyeri County has established agricultural extension services and youth -focused incubator
programs that provide mentorship and hands -on training in modern farming techniques (Nyeri County
Government, 2023). Similarly, Nakuru County launched the Agribusiness Youth Empowerment Program
(AYEP), equipping young farmers with skills in greenhouse farming, agritech adoption, and value addition
(Nakuru County Government, 2023).
Despite these efforts, challenges persist, including limited outreach, inadequate funding, and gaps in
program execution. Many youths still lack awareness of existing opportunities, and some programs fail to
address the financial and logistical constraints young farmers face in accessing training centers (Kariuki
& Mugo, 2023). Most notably, existing literature lacks comprehensive evaluations of these programs'
effectiveness in specific regions, particularly semi -arid and economically disadvantaged areas like
Narumoru. The study sought to bridge those gaps by offering a localized analysis of youth engagement in
agribusiness in Narumoru, Kieni Sub- County. The study provides insights on how targeted interventions
involving capital access, technology adoption, training programs, and policy frameworks can attract more
youth to agriculture and enhance food security in Kenya.
Statement of the problem
Despite the agricultural sector being a major contributor to Kenya’s GDP and employing over 60% of the
labor force, youth participation remains disproportionately low, with only about 10% of Kenyan youth
actively involved in agriculture (FAO, 2017; Farm Africa, 2021). This trend raises urgent concerns about
the sustainability of the sector and th e future of national food security, especially as the average age of
Kenyan farmers nears 60 years. Numerous initiatives and strategies have been developed by national and
county governments—including the Kenya Youth Agribusiness Strategy (FAO, 2017), the Ajira Digital
Program (Ministry of ICT, 2023), and county-level incubator programs—yet their impact remains minimal
due to poor coordination, inadequate funding, and limited youth awareness (Njenga & Muthomi, 2022;
Kariuki & Mugo, 2023).
Existing literature has emphasized barriers such as access to land and capital (Mmbando & Linderhof,
2022), limited exposure to agritech innovations (Olabisi, 2024), and insufficient targeted training programs
(Tufa et al., 2023). However, there is a lack of localized, empirical research that holistically analyzes how
these factors intersect—particularly in semi-arid, high-potential areas like Narumoru in Kieni Sub-County.
Moreover, few studies have deeply explored how tailored interventions involving technology, financial
inclusion, and youth-centered policy reforms could sustainably enhance youth agribusiness engagement.
The study, therefore, sought to close those knowledge and policy gaps by evaluating how access to capital,
adoption of agricultural technologies, training and education programs, and policy frameworks and
implementation influence youth engagement in agribusiness in Narumoru, Kieni Sub -County. The goal

4
was to generate data-driven recommendations that would inform more effective and inclusive agribusiness
models for youth, ultimately contributing to food security and rural development in Kenya.
2. Objectives
i. To assess the impact of access to financial capital on youth engagement in agricultural enterprise in
Narumoru, Kieni Sub-County.
ii. To examine the influence of agricultural technological innovations on youth participation in farming
and agribusiness-related activities.
iii. To evaluate the role of agricultural training and education programs in enhancing youth competencies
for productive engagement in the agricultural sector.
iv. To analyze the effectiveness of government policies and institutional frameworks in promoting youth-
driven agribusiness initiatives.
3.  Materials and Methods
Research Design
This study utilized a mixed -methods descriptive survey design, which integrates both quantitative and
approaches. The quantitative component allowed the researcher to gather structured data from a large
sample of youth engaged in agribusiness, while the qu alitative element provided deeper insights from
agricultural extension officers. This approach is suitable for examining the multifaceted factors influencing
youth engagement in agribusiness in Narumoru, Kieni Sub -county, such as access to capital, technol ogy,
education/training, and policy, as outlined in the study objectives.

Target Population and Sampling
The study targeted a population of 470 respondents, composed of youth aged between 18–35 years residing
in Narumoru, Kieni Sub -County, involved or int erested in agribusiness who are either ordinary youth
farmers or agricultural extension officers, with a sampling ratio of 70:30 respectively. This distribution
ensured that both primary stakeholders and key informants were well represented.

Stratified random sampling technique will be applied to ensure proportional representation of both youth

bias in the selection process.

5
Pretesting
A pilot study involving 10% of the  sample, 22 respondents, was conducted in Laikipi a East sub-county
which has similar characteristics to Narumoru. The pretesting resulted into clarifying questionnaire items,
ensured questions were organized in a logical sequence and allowed estimation of time for data collection.
Reliability
Reliability of the instrument was tested using Cronbach’s Alpha. A reliability coefficient of 0.72 was
obtained indicating internal consistency of the questionnaire.
  Data Collection Tools and Methods
A structured q uestionnaire was used for youth respondents. It contained both closed -ended and Likert -
scale questions aligned with the four research objectives. A semi-structured interview guide was used for
agricultural extension officers to gather expert insights on yo uth engagement in agribusiness. The
structured questionnaire was administered in person. Face -to-face interviews were conducted with
extension officers.
 Data Analysis
Quantitative data from the questionnaires was analyzed using SPSS Version 25. Descriptiv e statistics
techniques (frequencies, percentages, and means) to summarize the data, while inferential statistics (chi -
square tests and multiple regression analysis) were used to determine relationships among variables such
as access to credit, technologic al adoption, tra ining, and policy effectiveness. Qualitative data from
interviews was analyzed using thematic content analysis. Transcribed responses were coded and organized
into emerging themes based on the study objectives. This methodological approach ensured
comprehensive data collection and analysis, providing evidence -based recommendations for enhancing
youth participation in agribusiness and supporting national food security goals.

5. Discussion of Findings
Demographic Characteristics and Youth Participation
The dominance of youth aged 25 –30 reflects the active working -age group in rural Kenya. Similar to the
findings of Afande et al. (2015), who observed that youth between 25–35 are more likely to participate in
agribusiness, this age bracket represents an ideal demographic for agricultural productivity due to physical
ability and readiness to innovate.

11
Gender distribution showed a slightly higher male participation,  which resonates with Mibey and
Kaswamila (2018), who noted persistent gender disparities in agricultural participation due to cultural roles,
access to land, and control of resources.
Tertiary and vocational education among respondents signals a literate youth population ready to adopt
modern agricultural methods, aligning with Njenga et al. (2012), who emphasized the link between
education and agribusiness innovation. However, despite this potential, their marital status could influence
time and resource allocation to farming ventures.
Access to Capital
Access to capital remains a key barrier. While 70% of the respondents were aware of agribusiness
opportunities and showed high interest, personal savings were the predominant source of capital. This
reflects findings by World Bank (2014), which highlighted the limited access to formal credit among rural
youth.
Barriers such as insufficient collateral, high interest rates, and low awareness mirror the challenges
documented by FAO (2017), where financial illiteracy and rigid lending conditions excluded young farmers
from growth opportunities. The low financial inclusion significantly hampers efforts to scale youth -led
agribusinesses, thus threatening sustainable food systems.
Technological Innovation and Utilization
A high awareness (70%) of agricultural technologies was recorded, indicating positive strides in outreach.
This supports studies like Kiptot and Franzel (2012), which argue that awareness is the first step to
adoption. However, actual usage reveals a preference for accessible tools like mobile applications (50%),
with limited use of complex or expensive technologies such as greenhouses (15%) and drones (10%).
Challenges such as high costs, poor infrastructure, and limited technical knowledge hinder th e full
integration of technology. This supports Kilelu et al. (2014), who emphasized that the potential of digital
and mechanized agriculture cannot be realized without lowering adoption barriers.
Agricultural Training and Education
The findings showed that 65% of the youth had attended formal or informal training in agribusiness, which
significantly influenced their engagement, especially in pest control, marketing, and financial management.
These results align with AGRA (2015), which underscores the impac t of capacity -building on youth
empowerment in agriculture.
However, the 35% who lacked training represent a missed opportunity in improving productivity and
innovation in the sector. Expanding training could bridge the knowledge gap, enhance entrepreneuri al
skills, and increase agribusiness viability among the youth.
Role of Government Policies
Awareness of government policies promoting agribusiness was low, with 85% of respondents indicating
unawareness. Although BETA was relatively well known (75%), awareness of other key programs like the
Kenya Youth Agribusiness Strategy (5%) and KYEOP (0%) was extremely poor. This supports Wambua
et al. (2020), who found that policy impact on youth participation is often undermined by poor
dissemination, bureaucracy, and limited engagement of youth in policy design.
Respondents' call for reduced bureaucracy and better funding access reflects a broader sentiment that
current policy implementation fails to connect with youth realities on the ground.

6. Conclusion
This research focused on exploring factors influencing youth engagement in agribusiness for sustainable
food security in Kenya, using Narumoru, Kieni Sub -County. The findings revealed a number of  key
insights. In terms of demographics, youth aged 25 –30 years emerged as the leading participants in
agribusiness, highlighting their potential as a vibrant and easily trainable segment of the population. While
gender disparity was slight, it nonetheless  pointed to broader socio-cultural factors that influence
participation. Access to capital emerged as a significant barrier despite the high level of interest in
agribusiness. Most youth relied on personal savings due to challenges such as lack of collater al and high
interest rates, highlighting the urgent need for inclusive financial policies and youth -centered credit
schemes. The adoption of technology exhibited promise, with encouraging levels of awareness. However,
actual uptake was largely limited to low-cost, accessible tools such as mobile applications. High costs and
inadequate training restricted the use of advanced technologies like greenhouses and drones. The study
found that training and education in agriculture were positively correlated with yo uth engagement in
agribusiness. Participants who had received training applied their skills effectively in areas such as pest
control, marketing, and financial management. Nonetheless, a significant number of youth still lacked
access to such training, und erlining the need for more outreach and integration of agribusiness into
educational curricula. Government policies intended to support agribusiness had limited impact due to low
levels of among the youth. However, the popularity of the BETA program indicated a strong potential for
policy-driven engagement if outreach and communication were improved. Reducing bureaucratic barriers
and enhancing policy visibility could play a critical role in boosting youth participation in agribusiness for
sustainable food security.
