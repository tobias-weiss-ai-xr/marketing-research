Digital transformation in healthcare has
accelerated the use of algorithmic marketing to
strengthen patient communication, yet the
intensive use of patient data also raises concern
over data security that may affect trust recovery.
This study examines the effect of Algorithmic
Marketing on Trust Recovery and on Perceived
Data Security, the effect of Perceived Data
Security on Trust Recovery, and its moderating
role between Algorithmic Marketing and Trust
Recovery. Using a quantitative survey of 300
patients of Klinik Utama Tekad Medical Center
selected through purposive sampling, data were
analyzed with SEM -PLS (SmartPLS 4). Results
show Algorithmic Marketing positively affects
Trust Recovery (β = 0.661) and Perceived Data
Security (β = 0.876), and Perceived Data Security
positively affects Trust Recovery (β = 0.243), all
significant.

INTRODUCTION
Digital transformation has changed the way healthcare facilities build
relationships with patients. The use of artificial intelligence, electronic medical
records, customer relationship management (CRM), and various digital
communication channels has shift ed the orientation of services from reactive to
more proactive and personal. This development is in line with the strengthening
of algorithmic marketing practices, which are marketing approaches that utilize
algorithms and data analytics to produce more ta rgeted and automated
communication based on patient characteristics (Davenport et al., 2020; Dilaver
& Dilaver, 2024). In the health sector, this approach is realized through reminders
of examination schedules, personalized health education, and communicat ion
based on patient visit history.
Behind these benefits, the healthcare sector is one of the sectors most
vulnerable to data leak incidents, with recovery costs consistently above the
average of all industry sectors (IBM, 2023). In Indonesia, the strengthening of
Law Number 27 of 2022 conc erning Personal Data Protection emphasizes that
data security is no longer just a technical aspect, but part of organizational
governance and the legal responsibility of health service providers. From the
perspective of consumer behavior, perceived data se curity is one of the
determining factors for organizational success in building and restoring user
trust (Mashatan et al., 2022; Zhang, 2024).
This phenomenon is reflected in the Tekad Medical Center's Main Clinic, a
primary health care facility that has implemented EMR, CRM, and WhatsApp
Business API -based communication intensively. Based on the clinic's
management report, the number of patient complaints increased by 27.2% from
312 complaints in 2023 to 397 complaints in 2024, with most complaints related
to the intensity and personal nature of digital communication messages, no
longer the aspect of medical services. In the same period, the leve l of patients'
perception of trust in digital communication decreased from 82% to 70%. This
condition shows that the problems faced by clinics have developed from simply
building trust to trust recovery issues, namely the process of restoring patient
confidence after a decline in trust due to increased risk perception of the use of
personal data (Raza et al., 2023).
Previous studies on algorithmic marketing have generally focused on
marketing effectiveness and customer loyalty in the retail and financial services
sectors (Zhao & Tsubaki, 2025), while perceived data security has been
positioned more as a direct determi nant of trust, rather than as a moderation
variable that explains when algorithmic marketing is effective in restoring trust
(Mashatan et al., 2022; Salah & Ayyash, 2025). Research that explicitly integrates
algorithmic marketing, perceived data security, and trust recovery in one
empirical model in the context of healthcare is still very limited. This theoretical,
conceptual, contextual, and methodological gap underlies this study, which aims
to analyze the influence of algorithmic marketing on trust recovery, the influence
of algorithmic marketing on perceived data security, the effect of perceived data
security on trust recovery, and examine the role of perceived data security as a

LITERATURE REVIEW
Relationship Marketing Theory
Relationship Marketing Theory explains that the success of long -term
relationships between organizations and customers is not solely determined by
the quality of products or services, but by the organization's ability to build trust,
commitment, and contin uous communication (Morgan & Hunt, 1994). In
healthcare, algorithmic marketing is a communication instrument that has the
potential to strengthen these relationships through personalization and faster
and more consistent responses.

Privacy Calculus Theory
Privacy Calculus Theory states that individuals evaluate the benefits and
risks before accepting the use of their personal data by an organization (Culnan
& Armstrong, 1999). Patients will be more receptive to algorithm -based
communication if the perceived  benefits, such as ease of access to services, are
perceived to outweigh the risk of misuse of personal data.

Trust Recovery Theory dan Contingency Theory
Trust recovery is an organizational process of restoring customer trust after
a decline due to service failures or increased risk perception (Raza et al., 2023;
Kahr et al., 2024). The success of the process is greatly influenced by the speed of
response and consistency of recovery communication (Lu et al., 2020; Božič et al.,
2020). Meanwhile, Contingency Theory emphasizes that the effectiveness of an
organizational strategy is not universal, but is influenced by certain contextual
factors (Donaldson, 2001), so not all contextual factors can automatically function
as moderation variables.

H1: Algorithmic Marketing has a significant effect on Trust Recovery
Algorithmic marketing allows clinics to respond to patient needs quickly,
consistently, and data-driven, a characteristic that is relevant to the trust recovery
process. Raza et al. (2023) and Kahr et al. (2024) show that the speed and
transparency of post -service failure responses increases users' willingness to
trust the system again. Based on this description, this study proposes the
hypothesis:
H1: Algorithmic Marketing has a significant effect on the Trust Recovery of patients at
the Main Clinic of Tekad Medical Center.

H2: Algorithmic Marketing has a significant effect on Perceived Data Security
Algorithmic marketing involves the intensive collection and utilization of
patient data so that it helps shape the perception of data security. Balapour et al.
(2020) found that perceptions of privacy and security are shaped by the way apps
manage and comm unicate data usage to users. Therefore, a hypothesis is
proposed:
H2: Algorithmic Marketing has a significant effect on the Perceived Data Security of
patients at the Main Clinic of Tekad Medical Center.

H3: Perceived Data Security has a Significant Effect on Trust Recovery
Perceived data security is an important determinant of the formation and
restoration of user trust in digital systems, especially in services involving
sensitive medical data (Mashatan et al., 2022; Zhang, 2024; Alipour et al., 2023).
Based on these arguments, a hypothesis is proposed:
H3: Perceived Data Security has a significant effect on patient Trust Recovery at the
Main Clinic of Tekad Medical Center.

H4: Perceived Data Security Moderates the Influence of Algorithmic Marketing
on Trust Recovery
Patient responses to algorithm -based systems are thought to be strongly
influenced by risk perceptions and data security (Puntoni et al., 2021). When
perceived data security is high, the personalization produced by algorithmic
marketing is easier to interp ret as a form of attention and professionalism so as
to accelerate trust recovery; Conversely, when low, algorithmic marketing efforts
have the potential to generate resistance. Therefore, a hypothesis is proposed:
H4: Perceived Data Security moderates the influence of Algorithmic Marketing on
patient Trust Recovery at the Main Clinic of Tekad Medical Center.

METHODOLOGY
This study uses a quantitative approach with explanatory research and
cross-sectional data collection. The study population was patients of the Tekad
Medical Center Main Clinic with an annual number of visits of 19,870 patients.
The sample was determined u sing the rule of thumb approach for SEM -PLS,
which is a minimum of 10 times the number of research indicators (30 indicators),
so that a minimum of 300 respondents were selected through purposive
sampling techniques based on the criteria of patients who ha ve received digital
communication from the clinic.
Data was collected through a structured questionnaire with a five -point
Likert scale (1 = strongly disagree to 5 = strongly agree) which was compiled
based on three indicators of variables: Algorithmic Marketing (independent
variable), Perceived Data Secur ity (moderator variable), and Trust Recovery
(dependent variable). Data analysis was carried out using Structural Equation
Modeling–Partial Least Squares (SEM -PLS) with the help of SmartPLS 4,
including evaluation of measurement models (convergent validity  through
Average Variance Extracted, reliability through Composite Reliability and
Cronbach's Alpha, and discriminant validity through Fornell -Larcker Criterion,
Cross Loadings, and Heterotrait -Monotrait Ratio), evaluation of structural
models (R -Square de termination coefficient), and hypothesis testing through
procedures bootstrapping. According to Hair et al. (2022), a hypothesis is
declared acceptable if the t -statistical value is > 1.96 and the p -value < 0.05 at a
significance level of 5%.

RESEARCH RESULT
Respondent Characteristics
Of the 300 respondents, the majority were female (209 or 69.7%) and ≤24
years old (112 people or 37.3%), followed by the age groups of 25 –35 years
(25.0%), 36–45 years (23.0%), and >45 years (11.7%). The predominance of female
and young patients indicates a respondent population that is relatively familiar
with digital technology but also has a high awareness of privacy (Zhang, 2024),
making it relevant to the research's focus on the perception of data security.

Evaluation of Measurement Models
The results of the outer model evaluation showed that the entire construct
had an Average Variance Extracted (AVE) value above 0.50, Cronbach's Alpha
value in the range of 0.918–0.942, and Composite Reliability in the range of 0.931–
0.950, so that all indi cators were declared to be convergently and reliably valid
(Hair et al., 2022). Evaluation of discriminant validity through the Fornell -
Larcker and Cross Loadings criteria showed that the square root of AVE of each
construct was greater than its correlatio n with other constructs, although the
results of the Heterotrait-Monotrait Ratio (HTMT) indicated a strong conceptual
relationship between Algorithmic Marketing and Perceived Data Security, the
two were still empirically distinguishable.

DISCUSSION
The effect of Algorithmic Marketing on Trust Recovery showed a positive
and significant path coefficient (β = 0.661; t = 9.093), which means that the better
the implementation of algorithm -based communication through CRM and
WhatsApp Business API, the higher the recovery of patient trust. These findings
are consistent with Relationship Marketing Theory (Morgan & Hunt, 1994) which
emphasizes the role of quality communication in building long -term
relationships, and supports the research of Davenport et al. (2020) and Zhao and
Tsubaki (2025) which shows that algorithm-based marketing is able to strengthen
customer experience and loyalty.
The influence of Algorithmic Marketing on Perceived Data Security was
also positive and very strong (β = 0.876; t = 56.388), indicating that the quality of
digital communication, regularity of information delivery, and professionalism
of digital services w ere perceived by patients as indicators of the clinic's ability
to manage data securely. These findings support the Privacy Calculus Theory
(Culnan & Armstrong, 1999) and are in line with the research of Mashatan et al.
(2022) and Balapour et al. (2020) wh ich asserts that the design and transparency
of digital systems shape the perception of user security.
The effect of Perceived Data Security on Trust Recovery was also positive
and significant (β = 0.243; t = 3.433). These findings are relevant considering that
some respondents have felt doubts about the clinic's digital communication, so
belief in data sec urity is one of the important factors in rebuilding trust,
consistent with Mayer et al. (1995), Raza et al. (2023), and Zhang (2024).
In contrast, the role of Perceived Data Security as a moderation variable was
not statistically proven (β = -0.006; p = 0.808). This condition can be explained
through Contingency Theory (Donaldson, 2001) which states that not all
contextual factors automa tically function as moderation variables; Its
effectiveness depends on the characteristics of the research object. The high
descriptive value of Perceived Data Security in the majority of respondents
indicates that patients of Klinik Utama Tekad Medical Ce nter in general have
viewed data security as a service standard that must be met, so that the variation
in perception is not large enough to strengthen or weaken the relationship
between Algorithmic Marketing and Trust Recovery. In other words, Perceived
Data Security is more appropriate to play a role as a direct determinant rather
than as a contingency mechanism in the context of this study.

CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS
This study concludes that Algorithmic Marketing has a positive and
significant effect on Trust Recovery and Perceived Data Security, and Perceived
Data Security has a positive and significant effect on Trust Recovery, but it has
not been proven to moderate  the relationship between Algorithmic Marketing
and Trust Recovery. Overall, Algorithmic Marketing is the most dominant factor
in explaining patients' Trust Recovery, while Perceived Data Security plays a role
more as an independent variable that directly affects trust than as a moderation
variable.

Practically, the management of Tekad Medical Center's Main Clinic is
advised to: (1) continue to develop personalized, relevant, and not excessive
digital communication through CRM and WhatsApp Business API optimization;
(2) strengthen transparency in the use of patient data through clear privacy
policies and ongoing education; (3) improving information security governance,
including periodic data security audits and compliance with Law Number 27 of
2022; and (4) develop a sustainable trust recovery strateg y through fast and
responsive complaint handling. Theoretically, these findings extend Relationship
Marketing Theory and Privacy Calculus Theory in the context of digital
healthcare, and provide new empirical evidence for Contingency Theory that
perceived data security does not always function as a moderation variable..

ADVANCED RESEARCH
This study has limitations in the scope of objects (only one clinic), the
number of variables (three main variables), the cross -sectional design, and the
moderation variables that are not proven to be significant. The next research is
suggested to expand the research object to hospitals or other health facilities, add
other variables such as perceived privacy risk, digital trust, or patient
engagement, use alternative moderation variables such as digital literacy or
institutional trust, and consider longitu dinal or mixed methods approaches to
gain a deeper understanding of the dynamics of patient trust in digital health
services.
