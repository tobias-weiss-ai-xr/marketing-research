Abstract
This study aims to examine the use of labor  according to gender in greenhouse pepper
production and the role of female in farm decisions. The main material of the research
consists of primary data obtained through a survey of 64 farms engaged in greenhouse
pepper cultivation in the Aksu district of  Antalya province. The surveys were conducted
through face-to-face interviews with female producers. According to the research findings,
the average age of female engaged in pepper cultivation in the examined farms was 47
years, and their average experience in greenhouse pepper cultivation was determined to be
7.05 years. The total labor force used in pepper cultivation was calculated as 139.34
hours/year per decare; 63.70% of this was male labor and 36.30% was female labor. In
terms of labor force utilization within the family, it was determined that men were involved
in plowing, irrigation, fertilization, shadow powder application, loading, and transportation,
while female worked alongside men in solarization, planting seedling, hoeing, stringing,
pruning, and harvesting. Furthermore, the fact that 56.06% of the foreign labor force used
in farms consists of female shows that female’s labor is not limited to family farms but
occupies an important place in the agricultural labor force. However, despite female’s high
labor contribution, it was determined that their effectiveness in farm management and
decision-making processes remains limited. In this context, it is important to develop
policies aimed at increasing the participation of female producers in agricultural education,
extension, cooperatives, and marketing activities.

Introduction
The size of the population, as well as the extent to which the labor  force is utilized effectively, is
considered an important factor in the social, political, and economic structures of countries. Natural
resources available in nature are not usable on their own; transforming these resources into economic
value requires labor, which is one of the fundamental factors of production. Therefore, labor is regarded as
one of the essential and active elements of the production process (Şahin and Miran, 2008). The strong
dependence of agricultural production on climatic conditions, the structural characteristics of agricultural
enterprises, the often -patriarchal nature of employer –employee relations, and the fact that production
activities are shaped by traditional customs and practices clearly differentiate the employment structure in
the agricultural sector from that of the industrial sector (Tanrıvermiş, 2005).
In the agricultural sector, work and family life are generally considered an integrated whole. In rural
areas, men and female work together in agricultural production activities. In addition to participating in
agricultural production, females are responsible for cooking, cleaning, and caring for children. Female who
also engage in off-farm agricultural or non-agricultural income-generating activities contribute significantly
to the household economy through their intensive labor and play an important role in improving household
welfare (Alkan and Toksoy, 2009; Demir et al., 2016).
The literature includes studies conducted in Türkiye examining gender -based labor use in greenhouse
production and producers’ participation in decisions related to the production process. For instance, Özkan
(2000) found that female constitute an important  labor force in greenhouse vegetable production and
marketing in Antalya Province, particularly contributing intensive labor to activities such as sowing, hoeing,
and harvesting. Despite this intensive labor contribution, the study found that female’s part icipation in
decision-making processes related to vegetable production and marketing activities is very low. In a study
by Brumfield and Özkan (2016), the average daily working time of females engaged in greenhouse vegetable
production in Antalya Province was 8.8 hours, whereas it was 3.2 hours for men. It was stated that the
difference in workload between female and male farmers largely stems from the household responsibilities
undertaken by female. While females spend an average of 361.1 minutes per day o n household activities,
men allocate only 30.8 minutes to the same activities. When household activities are excluded, it is
observed that the time allocated by women and men to agricultural activities is relatively similar. The study
also revealed that females are mainly involved in vegetable production, cattle and poultry farming, and food
processing activities, whereas men play a more active role in the marketing of products and in the sales
processes of farm -produced goods. Furthermore, Demircan et al. (2019), in a study conducted in the
Kumluca district of Antalya Province, Türkiye, examined gender -based labor use in greenhouse tomato
production and found that family labor use in tomato production was 242.63 hours per decare (da) per year.
The authors reported that 49.87% of the family labor consisted of men, while 50.13% consisted of women.
Similarly, Kılınç and Yılmaz (2023) reported that the average daily labor use in the cultivation of aromatic
herbaceous plants in greenhouses in Antalya Province, Tü rkiye, was approximately 9 hours, of which
62.69% was provided by female workers and 37.31% by male workers.
According to studies conducted in different regions of Türkiye, although females in rural areas play a
significant role in terms of labor contribution, they remain behind men in decision -making processes
related to farm management (Özkan, 2000; Bayoğlu, 2010; Kaya and Atsan, 2012; Kutlar et al., 2013; Alkan,
2015; Yılmaz et al., 2019; Kılıç et al., 2020). For instance, Turguter and Ağıllı (2025) conducted interviews
with 25 female producers engaged in greenhouse vegetable cultivation in Muğla Province, Türk iye. The
findings revealed that a significant proportion of these females worked without social security, for low
wages, and for long hours, indicating that their labor was exploited. The study also highlighted that females,
who are responsible for unpaid domestic and care work, expend intensive labor by simultaneously
participating in greenhouse vegetable production activities. Overall, these studies indicate that females’
labor in greenhouse agricultural activities is greater than that of men. However, de spite females’ more
active role not only in agricultural production but also in household tasks, they remain less involved in

In this context, the province of Antalya, where the present study was conducted, has significant

thousand tons, accounting for 21.94% of the total pepper production (TSI, 2026).
This study aims to reveal gender -based labor use in farms producing peppers in the Aksu district of
Antalya Province and to analyze the roles of female producers both in decisions related to the production
process and in intra-household decision-making. While most of the studies in the literature focus primarily
on labor use, this study is expected to make an original contribution to the field by addressing females’ roles
in both household and farm -level decision -making processes together. The findings obta ined are also
expected to contribute to a better understanding of the gender -based labor structure in the agricultural
sector and to the development of rural development and sustainable agricultural policies.
Material and Method
The primary data of the study consisted of face-to-face survey data collected from 64 female producers
engaged in greenhouse pepper cultivation in the Aksu district of Antalya Province. Records from the Antalya
Provincial Directorate of Agriculture and Forestry were used to determine the number of producers included
in the survey.
The number of farms to be surveyed was calculated using the proportional sampling method. The
formula given in Equation 1 was used to determine the sample size (Newbold, 1995).

survey method were first tabulated using Microsoft Excel, after which the tables were analyzed and
interpreted.

labor, while 36.30% consisted of female labor. Nevertheless, it can be stated that females play a significant
role, particularly in labor-intensive stages of the production process, such as harvesting, pruning, tying to
strings and twining. Similarly, in their study on female market vendors in Tokat Province, Uçar et al. (2023)

activities and their domestic responsibilities. The researchers emphasize that females’ labor is not limited
to income -generating activities but has a multifaceted structure that also encompasses caregiving and
household chores within th e family. This situation demonstrates that, in agricultural production, females
shoulder not only visible production activities but also the burden of invisible labor. Therefore, when
evaluating female labor, it is necessary to consider not only the amount of labor allocated to production but
also the other responsibilities that females carry out simultaneously.

When the long-term decisions of the farms were examined, it was determined that in decisions regarding
the purchase of agricultural machinery and equipment, 81.25% were made jointly by females and their
spouses, 15.62% by the spouse alone, and 1.56% by the  female alone. In decisions related to land
acquisition or sale, it was notable that 100% of the decisions were made by female farmers.
Regarding the use of farm income, 95.31% of the decisions were made jointly by females and their
spouses, while 4.68% were made by the spouse alone. Similarly, 96.87% of decisions on credit use were
taken jointly by females and their spouses, with 3.12% taken by the spouse alone. In investment decisions,
96.87% of the farms reported joint decision-making by females and their spouses, while 1.56% of decisions
were made by the spouse alone and 1.56% by the female alone.
Overall, it can be observed that in the farms, both technical decisions related to the production process
and financial and strategic decisions of the farm are largely made jointly by female farmers and their
spouses. This indicates that females play an active role not only as labor in production activities but also in
farm management and decision -making processes. The particularly high proportion of joint decision -
making in areas affecting the future of the farm, such as income allocation, credit use, and investment,
demonstrates that decisions on family farms are taken collectively. However, in some technical matters,
such as the timing of fertilizer and pesticide application, the influence of men in the decision -making
process appears to be relatively higher.
Consistent with these results, Özkan (2000) reported that while females in greenhouse vegetable
production participated less in decisions on loading, transportation, unloading, and sales, they were mainly
involved in classification and packaging in the veg etable marketing process, with only 12% participating in
decisions regarding the type of vegetables cultivated.
These results are important as they demonstrate the high visibility of females in production and
management processes in rural family farms and highlight their significant role in agricultural decision -
making mechanisms. It has been determined that females  make significant contributions to production
activities and participate in many technical and economic decisions jointly with their spouses. However,
the limited number of situations in which females are sole decision-makers indicates that decision-making
processes still largely follow a joint or male -dominated structure (Table 6). Kaşıkırık (2022) notes that,
although females constitute the majority of the agricultural workforce, they are not adequately represented
in the institutional structures where ag ricultural policies are determined, and the problem of
underrepresentation in decision -making mechanisms persists. When the collaborative decision -making
structure observed at the farm level is considered alongside the low representation of females at the
institutional level, it can be said that there remains a significant disparity between the roles females play in
production and their level of representation in management processes.

Conclusion
This study aimed to examine the participation of females in labor and their influence on farm decision -
making processes in greenhouse pepper production in the Aksu district of Antalya, Türkiye. The findings
indicate that female producers are disadvantaged in terms of educational attainment and access to social
security. Total labor utilization in the farms was 267.57 hours/da, of which 52.08% comprised family labor.
Within family labor, 63.70% was contributed by males and 36.30% by females, whereas among hired labor,
43.94% was male and 56.06% female.
Females played a particularly prominent role in labor -intensive activities such as harvesting, hoeing,
tying, and pruning, a pattern reflected in both family and hired labor contributions. However, the level of
females’ labor participation in the productio n process did not fully correspond to their representation in
decision-making mechanisms. Agricultural production and investment decisions were mostly made jointly
by spouses, indicating that females’ independent decision -making authority remains limited. Conversely,
females exerted greater influence over household consumption and expenditure-related decisions.
This study contributes to the existing literature by providing empirical evidence on gender-differentiated
labor use and decision -making dynamics in greenhouse pepper production in Antalya, one of the most
important greenhouse production regions in Türkiye.
Enhancing institutional and organizational structures that support female participation in decision -
making is crucial. In particular, policies aimed at improving female access to agricultural training, extension
services, producer organizations, and social  security systems may strengthen their role in farm
management and decision-making processes.
 In addition, increasing field studies that examine both the quantitative and qualitative dimensions of
female labor in rural areas, while accounting for regional variations, is considered essential for a more
comprehensive understanding of gender dynamics in agricultural production. However, the findings of this
study should be interpreted with caution, as they are based on data collected from a specific district and
may not fully represent other greenhouse production regions in Türkiye. Future research co uld expand the
geographical scope and conduct comparative analyses across different regions to better capture variations
in gender roles, labor participation, and decision-making patterns in agricultural production.
