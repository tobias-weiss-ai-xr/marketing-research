 As gamification becomes an increasingly essential strategy to
enhance customer engagement and brand loyalty, there remains
a need for comprehensive reviews of its academic landscape.
This study aims to synthesize and analyze the trajectory of
gamification research in marketing using a bibliometric
analysis. Data was collected from the Scopus database,
comprising peer-reviewed articles published between 2010 and
2022, and analyzed using the bibliometrix R package. The
analysis reveals a steady growth in publications, highlighting
influential authors and foundational articles. Furthermore, the
findings identify five major thematic clusters: marketing and
consumer engagement, psychological foundations, education,
digital integration, and sustainability. The study concludes that
gamification is a versatile tool extending beyond commercial
marketing into broader social impacts. Future research should
focus on cross-disciplinary collaborations and leveraging
emerging technologies to address societal outcomes.

INTRODUCTION
Gamification is defined as the application of game design elements in non-game contexts
to enhance user engagement and motivation (Robson et al., 2015). This concept emerged
from the digital media industry, with its first documented use in 2008, gaining
widespread attention by 2010 (Deterding et al., 2011).
Furthermore, gamification also has emerged as a significant trend in marketing,
capturing the attention of researchers and practitioners alike (Kasurinen & Knutas,
2018). As businesses strive to engage consumers in increasingly competitive markets,
gamification offers innovative strategies to enhance customer interaction, loyalty, and
overall brand experience (Wrona, 2012).
Gamification has seen a significant rise in adoption across various industries. By 2015, it
was estimated that 50% of large companies would use gamification techniques for at

LITERATURE REVIEW
Gamification
Gamification refers to the application of game design elements in non -game contexts to
enhance user engagement and motivation (Hyzy, 2023). It is distinct from related
concepts such as serious games, which are complete games designed for non -
entertainment purposes, and playful design, which focuses on creating playful
interactions without necessarily incorporating game mechanics (Nicholson, 2015).
Emerging from the digital media industry, gamification was first documented in 2008
and gained significant trac tion by 2010 (Deterding et al., 2011). It involves integrating
game-like elements such as points, badges, and leaderboards into various domains like
business, education, and healthcare, aiming to drive participation and influence
behavior (Robson et al., 2015; Kasurinen & Knutas, 2018). At its core, gamification seeks
to utilize the motivational drivers inherent in games, such as positive reinforcement and
emotional engagement, to create meaningful experiences that encourage specific
behaviors. Moreover, th e MDE framework —mechanics, dynamics, and emotions —
offers a structured guide to gamification design, emphasizing the importance of defining
goals, rules, and rewards (mechanics), understanding user interactions (dynamics), and
evoking emotional responses (emotions) (Faust, 2021; Robson et al., 2015).
The MDE framework highlights how gamification can transform traditional processes
into engaging experiences by leveraging both intrinsic and extrinsic motivators. For
example, mechanics provide the structural foundation, dynamics reflect how users
interact within the system, and emotions evoke the sense of satisfaction and achievement
that drives engagement (Robson et al., 2015). However, the effectiveness of gamification
depends heavily on its thoughtful integration into the core service rather than being
treated as a superficial layer. Poorly implemented gamification strategies can fail to
engage users and even create negative perceptions (Richter et al., 2015). When properly
designed, gamification can offer significant advantages across various fields,
particularly in business, where it can contribute to sustained growth and competitive
advantage (Dymek & Zackariasson, 2016). One key area of potential application is

Bibliometrics Analysis
Bibliometric analysis is a quantitative research method used to study the evolution and
patterns of scientific knowledge through the analysis of related publications (Toaza &
Esztergár-Kiss, 2024)  (Marvi & Foroudi, 2023)  (Donthu et al., 2021) . By applying
statistical techniques to a body of literature, it uncovers historical developments,
authorship patterns, publication trends, and the use of scientific information
(Aleixandre-Benavent et al., 2017)  (Martínez-Toro et al., n.d.) . This method focuses on
the quantitative aspects of research output, offering valuable insights into the structure
and dynamics of scientific knowledge. In addition, Bibliometric analysis serves multiple
purposes, including facilitating comprehensive lite rature reviews by identifying
influential articles, authors, and journals in specific fields (Marvi & Foroudi, 2023)
(Ghorbani, 2024). It also uncovers emerging research trends and tracks the evolution of
scientific fields over time, broadening the understanding of research dynamics (Donthu
et al., 2021)  (Maulani & Widuri, 2024)  (Sofik et al., 2021) . Additionally, it identifies
collaboration networks among researchers, institutions, and countries, highlighting the
interconnected and global nature of scientific research (Li et al., 2024) (Rodríguez-Soler
et al., 2020) (Hasan et al., 2024).
The process of bibliometric analysis begins with data collection from scientific databases
such as Scopus, Web of Science, and Google Scholar, which provide the foundational
data required for analysis (Wulansari et al., 2020) (Riaman et al., 2022) (Lazar & Chithra,
2021). Quantitative measures, including the number of publications, citations, h -index,
g-index, and other bibliometric indicators, are employed to assess research impact and
trends (Riaman et al., 2022)  (Li et al., 2024)  (Toaza & Esztergár -Kiss, 2024). Moreover,
visualization tools like VOSviewer, Bibliometrix, and Biblioshiny are frequently utilized
to create visual representations, such as maps of scientific networks, co -authorship, co-
citation, and keyword co -occurrence, enhancing data interpretatio n (Ghorbani, 2024)
(Wulansari et al., 2020) (Lazar & Chithra, 2021) (Li et al., 2024).
Two primary techniques central to bibliometric analysis are performance analysis and
science mapping (Li et al., 2024) (Marvi & Foroudi, 2023). Performance analysis evaluates
the scientific output and impact of researchers, institutions, and countries by calculating
indicators such as the number of publications and citations. Science mapping, on the
other hand, involves creating visual representations of relationships within the scientific
literature, including co -authorship networks and keyword co -occurrence networks,
providing a clear depiction of the interconnections within specific research domains
(Lazar & Chithra, 2021) (Aleixandre-Benavent et al., 2017) (Donthu et al., 2021).

RESEARCH METHOD
This study employed a bibliometric analysis approach to examine the existing research
on the implementation of gamification in marketing. The Scopus database was selected
as the source for the literature, as it is a comprehensive and widely recognised database
for academic publications (Aria & Cuccurullo, 2017).
The search query used to identify relevant literature was "gamification" AND
"marketing." To capture the most recent developments in the field, the search was
limited to peer-reviewed journal articles and conference papers published between 2010
and 2022.

The bibliometric analysis was conducted using the bibliometrix R package, which

1. Descriptive analysis: Examination of the overall publication trends, including the

2. Keyword co -occurrence analysis: Identification of the most frequently occurring

3. Bibliographic coupling analysis: Identification of the most influential articles and the

4. Citation network analysis: Examining the published articles' citation patterns,
highlighting the connections and knowledge flows within the research field.
5. Thematic analysis: Categorization of the articles into broad thematic areas based on
the keyword co-occurrence and bibliographic coupling results.

Discussion
Based on the bibliometric analysis conducted in this study, several key insights have
emerged regarding the landscape of research on gamification and marketing . This
discussion synthesizes findings across multiple analytical dimensions, including
publication trends, keyword co -occurrence, citation network analysis, and thematic
clustering.
The first area of analysis was the publication trend . Research in gamification and
marketing has shown a clear upward trajectory over the past decade. While the dataset
does not provide a year -by-year breakdown, it is evident that scholarly interest began
intensifying around 2012, with significant growth in  the years 2015 to 2017, and
sustained momentum through to 2022. This reflects the expanding relevance of
gamification strategies in the marketing domain, as well as their increasing application
across industries. Notably, several influential authors have emerged as central figures in
this field. Juho Hamari, for example, has contributed to multiple high -impact studies,

psychological and behavioral underpinnings, educational applications, digital
marketing integration, and broader societal initiatives. Key authors such as Juho Hamari
and Kai Huotari have shaped the foundational understanding of how game elements—
points, badges, le aderboards, and reward systems —translate into strategic marketing
