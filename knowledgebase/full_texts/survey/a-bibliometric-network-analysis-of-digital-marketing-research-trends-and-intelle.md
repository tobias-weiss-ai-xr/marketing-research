Abstract - The field of digital marketing has become a
fast-developing area, although the current body of knowledge is
still disjointed between platforms, methods, and consumer
segments, with little integration in terms of thematic
development and the intellectual structure of the field. The
objective of this paper is to provide an evidence-based review of
the state -of-the-art of digital marketing by revealing main
trends, collaborative networks, and knowledge gaps. The
bibliometric network analysis was performed on the Scopus
database and included studies publ ished from 2003 to 2025. In
total, 277 papers were retrieved and filtered according to
PRISMA criteria by employing such keywords as digital
marketing, social media marketing, consumer perception,
digital transformation, and digital adaptation. Network
visualization and cluster analysis of authors, keywords, and
thematic clusters were done using the VOSviewer (Version
1.6.19). There were 250 authors, and out of those, 15 authors
fulfilled the co -authors criterion, while there were 950
keywords, and only 17 keywords met the minimum criterion of
occurrence of 7. The major research clusters that came out of
the analysis were digital marketing, social media marketing,
mobile application, and search engine optimization. The
publication trend had been low until 20 15 and thereafter
increased remarkably after 2019. It showed a peak publication
trend in the period of 2020 -2021 due to the pandemic situation
caused by COVID-19. Out of the authors, P.K. Kannan was the
one with the highest number of citations, which was 5 53. The
International Journal of Research in Marketing is the publisher
with the maximum number of citations, i.e., 655. The keyword
digital marketing is used most often, which occurs 90 times. The
link between consumer perception and social media was
recognized as one of the gaps in this research. The research
findings provide guidance for researchers, practitioners, and
policymakers who wish to enhance the field of digital marketing
research in accordance with SDG 8, SDG 9, and SDG 12.
Keywords: Digital Marketing, Social Media Marketing, Decent

I. INTRODUCTION
Small business operations have undergone substantial
changes through digital marketing, whereby these business
entities can stretch their boundaries beyond geographical
limits. Digital marketing strategy has enabled these
companies to connect with various people regardless of

III. THEORETICAL BACKGROUND
TAM and UTAUT form the theory through which the
bibliometric analysis results are interpreted. Perceived
usefulness and perceived ease of use in the TAM framework
correspond to the dominant themes that emerge from the
co-occurrence network in terms of the keywords "consumer
perception," "effectiveness," and "digital marketing
strategy," whereas the UTAUT themes of performance
expectancy, effort expectancy, and social influence
correspond to the cluster analysis themes o f social media
marketing and mobile applications. The theoretical
perspective allows for the contextual interpretation of the
bibliometric results found in the paper.
3.1. Technology Acceptance Model (TAM)
The Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) is used in the
context of understanding the usage of various digital
marketing tools like patient portals, social media campaigns,
hospital websites, and health apps. TAM argues that there are
two major beliefs that im pact the attitude toward particular
technology: usefulness and ease of use. These attitudes
determine the propensity of the user to use a technology and
how often the user uses the technology. Perceived use case is
how much patients think that technologies  like appointment
applications, WhatsApp messaging, or health campaigns
make their lives easier by providing quick access to
information or communication with their doctors in
healthcare digital marketing. Perceived ease of use relates to
the ease with whi ch one navigates hospital websites,
advertising reminders, and patient education materials (Tetik
et al., 2024).
Recent studies applying TAM to digital health indicate that
online platform adoption is stronger when visible benefits
such as ease of booking, better access to test outcomes, and
personalised notifications are clearly communicated through
marketing. On th e other hand, the perception suffers and
digital marketing campaigns fail to succeed when the
interface is filled with twists, the information is not properly
organised, and the privacy policy is not clear. A systematic
review of health information systems  with the use of TAM
reveals that recognized usefulness, trust and ease of use will
always have a role to play in either the use or non -use of
various e -health solutions by people. This highlights the
value of user -centred design and open communication in
online marketing campaigns. TAM provides that managers
need to concentrate on the ability to make healthcare digital
marketing effective by ensuring that the products are useful,
easy to use and reliable to patients instead of simply coining
new ideas (Kim et al., 2025).
3.2. Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology
(UTAUT)
The UTAUT explain why people accept or reject new
technology. It combined ideas from earlier models like the
Theory of Planned Behavior & Technology Acceptance
Model (Venkatesh et al., 2003). In recent years, UTAUT has
been widely used in digital health, e commerce, mobile apps,
and artificial intelligence systems. For example, the study
reviewed technology adoption studies and confirmed that
UTAUT remains one of the most powerful models to predict
user intentions. In healthcare, studies show that effort
expectancy & performance expectancy are strong predictors
of digital health adoption (Tamilmani et al., 2021).
UTAUT says that four main factors shape a person’s
intention to use technology. These are facilitating conditions,
social influence, effort expectancy, and performance
expectancy. Effort expectancy denotes to how easy the
system is to utilized. Social influence refers to the significant
people think the user should utilize the system. Enabling
conditions associated to the available resources and support.
The method also recommend that experience, gender, and age
can change how these aspects impact the behavior
(Venkatesh et al., 2003) . Afterwards, researchers enlarged
the model to UTAUT2 to explain use of consumer
technology. UTAUT2 included habit, price value, and
hedonic motivation as the new (Venkatesh et al., 2012).
Researchers valued UTAUT due to its clear structure. It helps
test how beliefs outline actual use and intention. It also works
well in different cultures and sectors. For digital systems such
as online healthcare services or AI based platforms, UTAUT
provides a strong base to study user acceptance.

4.1 Search Strategy
Keyword search in the Scopus database was performed in
January 2025, where studies were searched between 2003 and
2025, using the search string below:
TITLE-ABS-KEY ("digital marketing" OR "social media
marketing" OR "search engine optimization" OR "mobile
marketing" OR "digital advertising" OR "consumer
perception" OR "digital transformation") AND PUBYEAR >
2002 AND PUBYEAR < 2026 AND DOCTYPE (ar OR re)
AND LANGUAGE ( English). The search was restricted to
peer-reviewed articles and review papers published in
English. Conference papers, book chapters, editorials, and
non-English publications were excluded.
4.2 Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria
Inclusion criteria comprised peer -reviewed journal articles
and reviews in English related to digital marketing themes
published during the period 2003 -2025, which had complete
bibliometric data available. Studies excluded comprised
conference papers, book  chapters, non-English publications,
documents with incomplete metadata, and duplicates.
4.3 Research Questions
The following research questions guided the bibliometric
analysis:
1. RQ1: Which authors stand out in the realm of digital
marketing, and what important contributions have been
provided to the existing literature?
2. RQ2: In what ways has the publication landscape
transformed over the years within the field of digital
marketing, and what significant trends can be identified
in the annual publication data?
3. RQ3: What are the key terms that significantly impact
publications in the realm of digital marketing, and in
what ways do these terms shape the conversation within
the literature?
4. RQ4: What specific gaps in the current body of
knowledge in digital marketing can be identified, and in
what ways can addressing these gaps improve
understanding and insights in this important field?
4.4 Alignment with Sustainable Development Goals
The findings from the bibliometric study indicate a
significant level of congruence with three UN Sustainable
Development Goals, based on findings from the study.
SDG 8 - Decent Work and Economic Growth
An increase in the number of articles on digital marketing
since 2019 could be attributed to the increasing awareness
about digital marketing's role as an economic resilience
enabler. Digital marketing research studies indicate that
digital marketing is us ed by companies to access larger
markets and sustain their economic activities  (Lamberton &
Stephen, 2016 ; Nam & Kannan, 2020) . The keywords
"digital marketing strategy" and "business" occur 13 times
each, indicating the focus on economic development aspects
in this area.
SDG 9 - Industry, Innovation, and Infrastructure
The cluster related to SEO marketing and generative AI
research represents how innovations can transform digital
marketing infrastructure through technological progress
(Vajrobol et al., 2024). Another cluster, Mobile Applications,
illustrates how innovations in mobile technology lead  to
broader access to digital marketing platforms among
consumers, thus reflecting SDG 9.
SDG 12 - Responsible Consumption and Production
In particular, the studies in the social media marketing
cluster, focusing on firm -created content and content
marketing, stress the importance of using digital channels to
foster responsible consumption through responsible
marketing messages  (Holliman & Rowley, 2014 ; Kumar et
al., 2016) . Additionally, the keyword "digital
transformation," occurring 8 times in the corpus, also
emphasizes the evolution of research toward more
sustainable digital business practices.

5.2 Research Gap
Some specific knowledge gaps arise from the current
bibliometric analysis. Even though consumer perception is
the third most common keyword with 51 mentions, there is
still no substantial connection between the network of this
term and social media marketi ng which indicates that the
relationship was not explored before. Such advanced topics
as personalization with the help of artificial intelligence,
ethical digital marketing, and sustainable digital branding are
underrepresented in the network of keyword c o-occurrence
although their practical importance continues growing. There
is also no analysis of cross-regional collaboration patterns of
digital marketing research conducted in previous bibliometric
analyses. It is proven by the researches that thematic
convergence be tween consumer behavior, platform -specific
strategies, and sustainability-related marketing is inadequate
(Kim et al., 2021 ; Saura et al., 2021) . Thus, the current
research is justified in its attempt to map the intellectual
structure, thematic clusters, and collaborative networks of
digital marketing research through a systematic bibliometric
analysis.

6.1 Theoretical Interpretation Through TAM and UTAUT

usefulness at the organisational level, where businesses
evaluate digital tools based on measurable performance
outcomes. These alignments suggest that future research
would benefit from explicitly embedding TAM a nd UTAUT
constructs into empirical investigations of digital marketing
behaviour.

VII. CONCLUSION
This study conducted an elaborate bibliometric network
analysis of digital marketing studies from the year 2003 to
2025 using 277 peer -reviewed articles sourced from the
Scopus database. This systematic mapping explored
intellectual structure, clusters, co llaboration patterns, and
trends in publications in digital marketing literature focusing
on answering four main research questions related to
influential scholars, publication trends, keyword analysis,
and research gaps. Key statistical results were ident ified
through the bibliometric analysis conducted. The trend of
publication was low up to the year 2015 followed by a
massive increase after 2019, which hit a peak between the
years 2020 -2021 amidst the COVID -19 pandemic. Digital
marketing was revealed to be the highest recurring keyword
with 90 mentions, followed by marketing with 78 mentions,
and consumer perception with 51 mentions. Thematic
clusters included digital marketing, social media marketing,
mobile application, and search engine optimization. P .K.
Kannan was the most impactful scholar with 553 citations,
while International Journal of Research in Marketing was the
most impactful publisher with 655 citations. One of the main
gaps in research was weak network connection between
consumer perception  and social media marketing, which
offers much potential for future empirical research. The
results fit within SDG 8, SDG 9, and SDG 12. Future research
should focus on regional bibliometrics, AI integration in
digital marketing, and using more advanced bi bliometric
tools, such as co-citation and bibliographic coupling analysis.
