Introduction: Digital tourism platforms have become increasingly influential in
shaping tourists’ experiences and behavioral intentions, serving as important mar-
keting and communication channels. However, existing research has paid limited
attention to how specific platform attributes are associated with tourists’ revisit
intentions through experiential processes.
Methods: Drawing on the Stimulus-Organism-Response (SOR) framework, this
study examines how four key attributes of digital tourism platforms—platform
accessibility, platform usability, personalized recommendations, and online
review quality—are associated with revisit intention, with the association medi -
ated by the quality of tourist experience. Data were collected from 581 individuals
who had visited Beijing, and structural equation modeling (SEM) was employed
to test the proposed hypotheses.
Results: All four platform attributes are associated with the quality of the tourist
experience, which in turn significantly predicts revisit intention. Furthermore, the
quality of the tourist experience partially mediates the relationship between plat-
form attributes and revisit intention, suggesting that tourists’ experiential evalu -
ations play a central role in this relationship.
Discussion: These findings highlight the critical role of the quality of the tourist
experience in are associated with revisit intention through within a communica-
tion-based SOR framework. The study contributes to understanding how digital
tourism platforms serve as marketing stimuli that shape tourist behavior. In practi-
cal terms, the findings have implications for platform operators and destination
marketers seeking to enhance digital communication strategies and promote
sustainable revisit behavior.

1 Introduction
Due to the exponential growth in the use of digital technology, the tourism sector is under-
going a rapid digital transformation (Qin F . et al., 2025). Digitalization of tourism enhances
the long-term competitiveness of travel destinations while improving service quality and the
efficiency of tourism resource allocation (Ikhtiyorovna and Salimovich, 2026; Zhang et al.,
2025). One of the main factors associated with the success of digital transformation in tourism
and sustainable development is the caliber of visitor experiences during travel-related activities
(Walewangko et al., 2026). Therefore, the quality of the tourist experience should not be the

main priority. Digital tourism platforms are the most common exam-
ple of how digital technologies are being integrated into the tourism
sector (Tedjakusuma and Kulachai, 2026). They are crucial in connect-
ing travelers with locations. Digital tourism platforms offer services
including online booking, itinerary planning, tailored recommenda-
tions, and information search that are integrated into the entire trip
experience (Bormane and Blaus, 2024).
Prior research has examined several factors, including plat -
form information quality, technological features, and interactive
mechanisms, to examine how digital tourism platforms are associ -
ated with visitor experience, satisfaction, and behavioral inten -
tions ( David-Negre et al., 2018 ; Zainal-Abidin et al., 2023 ).
However, most prior research has focused on specific platform
features or specialized technological functions, highlighting the
association of particular elements—such as information quality or
platform interaction—on tourists’ attitudes or actions. Although
this viewpoint emphasizes the significance of specific platform
features, it struggles to fully explain how broader features of digital
tourism platforms relate to visitor experiences. Travelers using
digital tourism platforms are often associated with multiple fac -
tors, especially in real-world situations. These aspects include plat -
form availability, simplicity of use, tailored suggestions, and online
reviews.
Therefore, existing research still exhibits significant shortcomings
in two areas. First, there is a lack of systematic analysis of how tourism
digital platforms are associated with visitor experiences, considering
their multidimensional characteristics. Second, the pathways by which
platform characteristics are further associated with visitor behavioral
intentions—particularly revisit intentions—through visitor experi-
ences remain insufficiently explained. Meanwhile, visitor experience
quality is regarded as an important mediating element in determining
tourists’ behavioral intentions (Cheng et al., 2021 ). A high-quality
tourist experience can boost tourists’ overall opinion of the place and
is associated with stronger intentions to revisit (Cheng et al., 2021).
Compared with one-time spending, revisit intention better reflects
tourists’ long-term identification with a destination, carrying substan-
tial significance for building a city’s tourism image and ensuring
steady tourism market development. While existing studies have sepa-
rately examined relationships among digital platform characteristics,
visitor experience, and behavioral intentions (Qu et al., 2025 ), few
have systematically analyzed how digital tourism platform character-
istics are associated with revisit intention through the quality of visitor
experience within a unified theoretical framework. Although the asso-
ciation of digital platforms on tourist behavior has been studied in the
literature, the full theoretical linkage platform features, visitor experi-
ence, and behavioral intent has not been systematically integrated. As
a result, a comprehensive investigation of the relationships among the
features of tourism digital platforms, visitor experience quality, and
the desire to return must be conducted within a single theoretical
framework. This project aims to answer the following research issues
in light of these gaps in knowledge:
RQ1: How are the accessibility, platform usability, personalized
recommendations, and online review quality of tourism digital
platforms associated with the quality of the tourist experience?
RQ2: Does the quality of the tourist experience operate as a
mediator in the interaction between the features of tourism digi-
tal platforms and the intention of visitors to return?
The Stimulus-Organism-Response (SOR) model serves as the
theoretical foundation for this investigation. SOR theory posits that
external environmental cues are linked to an individual’s internal psy-
chological state, which, in turn, is associated with behavioral responses
(Tang et al., 2019). This point of view considers the accessibility of
digital travel platforms, their usability, tailored suggestions, and the
quality of online reviews to be significant drivers. By are associated
with passengers’ emotional and cognitive experiences, these factors
may ultimately be associated with their inclination to return. This
research focuses on tourists who have visited Beijing. In addition to
being a popular tourist destination with a variety of historical and
cultural attractions, Beijing is a market leader in digital transforma-
tion (Zhang et al., 2019). Tourists largely rely on digital tourism plat-
forms to support their trip decision-making and overall tourist
experience (Tang et al., 2023). This setting not only improves observa-
tion of how platform characteristics are associated with visitor experi-
ences but also enhances the practical interpretability of the study’s
findings.
In conclusion, this study develops a model in which visitor experi-
ence quality is the mediating variable, revisit intention is the depen-
dent variable, and the availability, usability, customized suggestions,
and quality of online reviews on digital tourism platforms are the
independent variables. We contribute to the current literature in the
following ways: First, this technique overcomes the limitations of pre-
vious research, which mostly focuses on single-platform aspects, by
systematically combining the essential functional features of tourist
digital platforms from a multidimensional perspective on platform
characteristics. As a result, it offers a more thorough understanding of
how digital platforms are associated with the travel experience.
Second, we methodically investigate how visitor experience quality is
associated with multidimensional features of digital tourism plat -
forms, demonstrating the mediating role of experience between plat-
form traits and behavioral intent, and offering a more comprehensive
explanatory framework for understanding how tourists’ behavioral
intentions are formed. Third, this study not only applies the SOR
theory within the tourism context but also extends its theoretical
applicability to research on digital platforms. Unlike previous studies
that primarily focus on environmental stimuli or service experiences,
this study incorporates multidimensional digital platform character-
istics into the “stimulus” level. It explicitly differentiates similar con-
structs such as availability and ease of use. This approach further
elucidates how digital platforms are associated with behavioral inten-
tions through the quality of the tourist experience, thereby providing
a novel theoretical explanatory pathway for applying SOR theory in
digitalized contexts. Finally, by concentrating on tourists to Beijing,
the study presents empirically grounded data with considerable real-
world application.

2.2 Stimulus-organism-response (SOR)
framework
Originally developed in environmental psychology (Mehrabian
and Russell, 1974), the SOR model has since been modified and vali-
dated across a range of fields, including information systems, retail,
and organizational behavior (Tang et al., 2019). The paradigm’s core
tenet is that environmental cues trigger people’s emotional and cogni-
tive experiences, which in turn are associated with behavioral
responses and outcomes (Fakfare et al., 2024). Three factors make up
the SOR framework: stimulus, organism, and reaction. The term
“stimulus” is used to describe aspects of the environment that people
perceive (both objective and subjective aspects). Organism usually
refers to an individual’s psychological state when confronted with an
external stimulus, encompassing cognitive processes, emotional reac-
tions, and overall judgment. Response shows the behavioral tenden-
cies or effects a person demonstrates under a specific psychological
condition. This theoretical logic aligns with research on media effects
within the field of digital communication. Relevant studies suggest
that the media environment and the way information is presented are
associated with individuals’ cognitive judgments and emotional expe-
riences; this “environmental stimulus–psychological response–behav-
ioral outcome” logic is highly consistent with the SOR mode
(Taddicken and Krämer, 2021; Widaningsih and Putranto, 2025 ).
Therefore, in the context of digital platforms, interface design, infor-
mation quality, interaction methods, and personalized recommenda-
tion mechanisms can all be regarded as forms of digital communication
stimuli; by are associated with visitors’ cognitive evaluations and emo-
tional experiences, they subsequently shape their behavioral decisions
(Armutcu et al., 2026; Jiao et al., 2022).
As the theoretical basis for our work, the SOR model demon -
strates remarkable flexibility and explanatory power. First, this model
has been widely employed in studies of travel behavior and digital
environments (Bai and Lai, 2025; Wu and Lai, 2022). For example,
research using the SOR model confirmed that the vividness of the
scenic spots offered by smart platforms, serving as stimuli, had
resulted in psychological responses, such as the sense of presence of
users, emotional engagement, flow state, and pleasure among them,
resulting in the enhanced willingness of users to participate in moun-
tain tourism (Wu and Lai, 2022). Additionally, media coverage and
organizational norms associated with travelers’ ethical actions toward
animals by reinforcing their personal norms ( Fakfare et al., 2024).
Second, digital tourism platforms have become key places for tourists
to acquire information and customize their trip experiences (Qu et al.,
2025; Sharafutdinov et al., 2020; Wang, 2026). However, no study has
systematically examined how their key qualities are associated with
tourists’ cognitive and emotional reactions, thereby altering their
intentions to return. The SOR model provides a concise, unambiguous
theoretical framework for clarifying how digital platform features are
associated with return intentions through the quality of the visitor
experience.
2.3 Availability of digital tourism platforms
and visitor experience quality
Platform availability often refers to the extent to which tourists
may seamlessly access the platform and its information, features, and
services when needed ( Almakayeel, 2023). Features of availability
include information availability, functional availability, and technical
accessibility (Y ang et al., 2024). The platform’s information availability
demonstrates its capacity to provide comprehensive and user-friendly
information, including ticket options, operating hours, and transit
statistics ( Cheung et al., 2022 ). Functional availability indicates
whether the platform functions effectively and includes all necessary
functionality, such as search, review, and sharing ( Bekele and Raj,
2025; Cheung et al., 2022). Cross-device/regional usability, as well as
the dependability of network connections, are often linked to techni-
cal accessibility (Wang, 2026).
According to this study, which integrates the SOR model, the
availability of digital tourism platforms may serve as a technological
environmental stimulus, are associated with travelers’ psychological
and cognitive states and thereby improving the quality of their travel
experiences (Tang et al., 2019). Research indicates that travel happi-
ness and decision-making confidence are enhanced by systems that
provide comprehensive, readily available information ( Stors and
Baltes, 2018). This suggests that visitors’ sense of control and security
during the trip process may increase when they have easy access to
relevant information and services, and is associated with higher expe-
rience quality of their experience. Moreover, availability also is associ-
ated with lower perceived burden and more positive experiences, easy
interpersonal experiences. Furthermore, visitors’ perception of secu-
rity and confidence may be enhanced by the reliability of technological

2.4 Ease of use of digital tourism platforms
and visitor experience quality
The degree to which a system reduces users’ operational or mental
strain is known as perceived ease of use. Perceived ease of use quanti-
fies the cognitive effort required to interact with the platform in
instances when tourists make decisions using digital platforms
(Ghorbanzadeh et al., 2025). Ease of use emphasizes users’ ability to
quickly and intuitively complete transaction procedures, functional
tasks, and information retrieval without requiring much technical
knowledge or mental effort. Perceived usefulness is therefore a key
factor in reducing the cognitive load associated with challenging tests.
It is worth noting that perceived ease of use and platform availability
represent two distinct conceptual dimensions. Availability primarily
emphasizes whether tourists can smoothly access and obtain platform
information across different devices, network conditions, and usage
scenarios (Almakayeel, 2023), with the core focus on “whether the
platform can be accessed successfully. ” In contrast, ease of use con-
cerns whether the interaction process with the platform is simple,
intuitive, and efficient once tourists have successfully accessed it (Jo,
2023), with the core focus on “how easy it is to use. ” Therefore, avail-
ability primarily reflects the platform’s access conditions and informa-
tion accessibility, whereas ease of use reflects the operational
convenience and cognitive effort required during actual use. Clearly
distinguishing these two constructs helps provide a more accurate
understanding of how different platform features are associated with
tourists’ experiences and behavioral intentions.
Travelers must consider time and money simultaneously, as well
as logistics and personal preferences, when planning vacations (Chen
et al., 2025). The benefit of digital tourism platforms in this regard is
their easy, seamless interface design, which is the principal factor driv-
ing their value. A simple, flowing platform enables passengers to focus
on summarizing the review itself rather than on technical processes
(Jo, 2023). Visitors are therefore more likely to feel competent, trusted,
and at ease when using a highly user-friendly platform. These pleasant
feelings might further improve the overall quality of the encounter
(Shamim et al., 2024). According to another study, simplicity of use is
a substantial means of minimizing operational errors and maximizing
the efficiency of work completion, thereby increasing tourists’ evalu-
ations of overall quality (Juschten and Preyer, 2023). Moreover, even
in highly engaging, time-sensitive activities such as vacation planning,
usability plays a huge role in the quality of the visitor’s experience.
Therefore, this study hypothesizes:
H2. Platform ease of use positively correlates with visitor experi-
ence quality.
2.5 Personalized recommendations on
digital tourism platforms and visitor
experience quality
Personalized recommendations involve capturing users’ behav-
ioral awareness and orientation from interaction data, predicting user
preferences, enhancing the user experience in personalized product
recommendations, and thereby are associated with user intentions and
behavioral decisions (Shao et al., 2019). Within digital tourism plat-
forms, personalized recommendations often employ algorithms to
provide passengers with individualized suggestions for holiday desti-
nations, products, or services (Generosi et al., 2025). Compared with
uniform information presentation, tailored suggestions more effec-
tively match tourists’ requests and serve as a key tool for existing digi-
tal tourism platforms to enhance the user experience ( Cheung et
al., 2022).
We also contend that personal recommendations on digital travel
platforms serve as environmental stimuli that are associated with the
quality of the tourist experience, in accordance with the SOR model.
First, personalized recommendations make travel simpler and more
effective, reducing the burden of sorting through large amounts of
travel-related information (Sam Li, 2025). Personalised recommenda-
tion systems, which sharply reduce browsing time and cognitive load,
enable travellers to receive travel information that more closely
matches their needs and interests (Generosi et al., 2025). At the same
time, personalized suggestions on platforms enhance visitor satisfac-
tion by conveying an emotionally humanized service and tailored
recommendations to distinct needs, thereby boosting visitor loyalty
and the quality of the experience (Y ang et al., 2024). Lastly, increasing
coherence and immersion throughout the visitor’s platform experi-
ence results from increasingly tailored recommendation algorithms.
In digital tourism contexts, personalized suggestions also permeate
knowledge acquisition during travelers’ journeys and post-trip phases,
dynamically enriching the holiday experience ( Wang et al., 2023).
Research has shown that high-quality recommendation systems are
more likely to induce users into a “flow state” and foster more positive
overall evaluations of the experience. Therefore, we propose:
H3. Personalized recommendations from digital tourism plat-
forms are positively correlated with the quality of the visitor
experience.
2.6 Online review quality on digital tourism
platforms and visitor experience quality on
digital tourism platforms
Online reviews are highly valued by consumers seeking travel-
related information on platforms, shaping their experiences and deci-
sion-making (Haj Khalifa et al., 2024). The quality of internet review
content may provide the most realistic depiction of tourist sites and
their service experiences (Qin Y . et al., 2025). Tourists provide feed-
back and share information about their travel experiences through
written evaluations, image descriptions, and follow-up comments
(Belén Rodríguez-Hidalgo et al., 2025). For other travelers to assess
the caliber of the visiting experience, these reviews are crucial tools
(Fakfare et al., 2024).
The tourism industry has become increasingly virtualized due to
the rapid development of mobile technology, which has heightened
passengers’ perceptions of risk ( Bekele and Raj, 2025 ; Chen and
Huang, 2025). Excellent online reviews provide tourists with thorough
information, increasing the precision of their evaluations and thus are
associated with the quality of the tourist experience (Tao et al., 2025).
According to research, tourists’ perceptions of the platform’s informa-
tion quality are positively correlated with the informative diagnostic
value and usefulness of reviews (Widaningsih and Putranto, 2025),

2.7 Mediating role of visitor experience
quality in the relationship between digital
platform characteristics and revisit intention
Revisit intention refers to the subjective desire generated by tour-
ists after finishing a travel activity to revisit the same location in the
future (Lloret-Climent et al., 2019). This intention is developed from
tourists’ overall opinions and assessments of their previous visit and
serves as a significant indicator for determining a destination’s long-
term attractiveness and sustainable growth ( Zeng and Li, 2021 ).
Revisit intention is associated with numerous factors, including the
perceived destination image and the service experience (Quintal and
Polczynski, 2010). In turn, among these, the quality of the tourist
experience serves as a crucial psychological link between the delivery
of tourism services and visitors’ intentions and behavior to return. The
characteristics of digital tourism platforms have become a major
factor in the quality of the visitor experience and behavioral inten-
tions, serving as a means for tourists to access travel-related informa-
tion and services (Qu et al., 2025). Existing research shows that digital
tourism platforms are indirectly associated with cognitive perception,
emotional responses, and interactive experiences, which in turn are
associated with related attitudes and intentions, such as behavioral
intention and destination decision-making ( Sharafutdinov et
al., 2020).
Integrating the SOR model, this study suggests that the character-
istics of digital platforms provide important environmental stimuli
that are associated with the quality of the customer experience and
psychological states. At the same time, revisit intention serves as a
response to these environmental stimuli ( Zeqiri et al., 2025 ). The
availability, usability, tailored suggestions, and quality of online
reviews on digital tourism platforms may shape the overall quality of
travelers’ experiences. These factors may be associated with their emo-
tional satisfaction, sense of trust, ease of use, and effectiveness in
obtaining information. This is eventually associated with their propen-
sity to return (Abubakar et al., 2017).
Specifically, highly accessible digital tourism platforms provide
passengers with more timely and secure travel information, thereby
reducing their information search costs when making travel decisions
(Almakayeel, 2023). Easy access to information helps tourists make
more informed choices and enhances their overall experience, and is
associated with a higher likelihood of revisit intention. Second, plat-
form ease of use makes a substantial contribution to the development
of the visitor experience; operationally simple platforms can reduce
operational burdens during use (Ghorbanzadeh et al., 2025). Using
less is easier, lessens aggravation for tourists, and maximizes happiness
and satisfaction (Chen et al., 2025; Jo, 2023), and would reinforce posi-
tive ratings of the area and intentions to return. Third, by meeting
certain visitor preferences, customized recommendations significantly
impact the quality of the tourist experience (Generosi et al., 2025).
Travelers are more likely to feel appreciated and understood when
recommendations closely match their tastes (Montero et al., 2025).
This favorable psychological experience enhances the quality of the
emotional experience, leading to persistent intent to visit. Finally, the
quality of online reviews is associated with tourists’ processes of infor-
mation evaluation and expectation formation. High-quality evalua-
tions often have greater trustworthiness and informational value (Qin
Y . et al., 2025). Reasonable expectations, based on reputable reviews,
improve assessments of actual travel experiences (Haj Khalifa et al.,
2024), thereby indirectly increasing return intentions. Therefore, this
study hypothesizes:
H5. Visitor experience quality mediates the relationship between
the availability of digital platforms and revisit intention.
H6. Visitor experience quality mediates the relationship between
digital platform ease of use and revisit intention.
H7. Visitor experience quality mediates the relationship between
personalized recommendations on digital platforms and revisit
intention.
H8. Visitor experience quality mediates the relationship between
online review quality on digital tourism platforms and revisit
intention.
Based on the above discussion, this study developed a model with
digital platform availability, ease of use, personalized recommenda-
tions, and online review quality as independent variables; visitor expe-
rience quality as the mediating variable; and visitor revisit intention
as the dependent variable see Figure 1.
3 Method
3.1 Sample and data collection
A questionnaire survey was used to gather data for this investiga-
tion. Travelers who had visited Beijing and utilized digital travel ser-
vices were the target audience for the questionnaire, which was sent
via the Credamo online data-gathering platform. Online convenience
sampling was used to recruit participants via the Credamo platform,
which draws them from its registered panel. To guarantee that the
sample fulfilled the study needs, certain inclusion criteria were devel-
oped: (1) participants had visited Beijing for tourism-related reasons;
and (2) participants had utilized Beijing-related online travel plat -
forms (such as booking or informative platforms) during their trip.
The method automatically eliminated respondents who answered
“No” to the screening questions at the start of the questionnaire.
Participants were informed of the research goal and told that partici-
pation was completely optional before completing the questionnaire.
To prevent duplication, individuals who reported having already com-
pleted the survey were excluded. Participants received guarantees that
the research would be used only for academic purposes, that all
responses would be kept anonymous, and that absolute confidentiality
would be maintained. Additionally, they were told they could leave the

survey at any time. Several data-cleaning techniques were used to
ensure data quality. Responses that were finished too quickly (speed-
ers) were eliminated (n = 18). Furthermore, surveys with obvious pat-
terns of inconsistent or inattentive responses—such as straight-lining
or failing attention tests—were eliminated ( n = 21). Additionally,
questionnaires that were either partially or completely incomplete
were excluded (n = 13). 581 questionnaires were first gathered. A final
sample of 529 valid replies was kept for study after the aforementioned
exclusion criteria were applied. Based on eligible and completed
replies, this translates to an effective response rate of 91.05%. The
sample screening flowchart is shown in Figure 2. The use of Credamo’s
panel-based recruitment system, which focuses on active, pre-regis-
tered participants, uses screening questions to ensure respondent rel-
evance, and offers a small financial incentive to encourage completion,
is responsible for the comparatively high response rate.
Among the 529 samples, males accounted for 44.6% and females
for 55.4%. The age breakdown was as follows: 19.1% aged 25–34,
38.0% aged 35–44, 34.4% aged 46–54, and 8.5% aged 55 and above.
25.0% of participants held a bachelor’s degree, 45.0% held a master’s
degree, and 30.1% held a doctorate. Descriptive statistics for demo-

3.2 Measures
This study uses existing scales to measure significant research
variables. The measurement methods used in this investigation were
selected based on the following criteria: First, they are published in
globally respected, well-known journals. Second, they have been
tested in intercultural contexts, such as in China, and thus show good
reliability and validity. In addition, the original scale language was
modified and refined through a series of iterations using the universal
“back-translation method” to enhance the accuracy and flexibility of
the measurement items. A 5-point Likert-scale questionnaire of some
key variables was the outcome of this method. Scores are from 1 to 5,
representing low to high levels of agreement. (1 - “Completely dis-
agree, ” 5 = “Completely agree”). Specific variables and associated items
are presented in Appendix A. As shown in Appendix B, all variables
have Cronbach’s α > 0.7, AVE > 0.5, CR > 0.7, and factor loadings>
0.6, indicating good reliability and validity.
3.3 Data analysis
This study uses SPSS 27.0 and Amos 24.0 for data analysis. SPSS
27.0 was used for reliability testing, descriptive statistics, and correla-
tion analysis. Confirmatory factor analysis and hypothesis testing were
conducted using Amos 24.0. Bootstrapping with 2000 resamples and

4.1 Common method variance test
Most data collected through questionnaires originates from a
single source, frequently leading to common method variance (CMV)
(Podsakoff et al., 2003). Although measures were taken during the
survey to minimize CMV , all data in this study were self-reported by
college students, making it difficult to fully ensure data consistency.
First, this study used Harman’s single-factor test, which showed that
factors with eigenvalues larger than 1 explained 74.727% of the vari-
ance. The empirical criterion of 50% was greatly exceeded by the first
main component, which accounted for 37.074%. Additionally, CMV
was further investigated by adjusting for unmeasured latent charac-
teristics. The results showed that the addition of the latent factor tech-
nique did not substantially improve model fit indices (ΔCFI = 0.002,
ΔTLI = 0.004, ΔRMSEA = 0.005, ΔSRMR = 0.001). This also implies
that the CMV in this trial was not severe. Additionally, this suggests
that the study’s common method bias is negligible, which qualifies the
data for further structural equation modeling analysis.
4.2 Confirmatory factor analysis (CFA)
To demonstrate that the variables involved in this study represent
distinct constructs, we conducted CFA. The findings show that the
six-factor model ( χ2/df = 1.368, CFI = 0.989, TLI = 0.986,
RMSEA = 0.026, SRMR = 0.029) performed much better than other
models and provided the best fit (see Table 2). The multi-factor model
showed better fit indices than models that combined several variables.
This suggests that the six factors examined in this study had strong
discriminant validity and could be easily distinguished.
4.3 Correlation analysis
As shown in Table 3, the square roots of the AVEs are all greater
than the correlation coefficients of the respective main variables, indi-
cating that the variables in this study possess good discriminant valid-
ity. The means, standard deviations, and Pearson correlation
coefficients for the study’s main variables are shown in Table 3. The
findings show that visitor experience quality is significantly positively
correlated with digital platform availability, simplicity of use, tailored
suggestions, and online review quality. Concurrently, there is a strong
positive association between the quality of the tourist experience and
the propensity to return. These results set the groundwork for further
structural equation modeling research and offer initial support for the
theoretical concepts put forward in this study. Additionally, the cor-
relation coefficients among all variables are less than 0.70, suggesting
that the research variables do not exhibit serious multicollinearity.

4.5 Robustness testing
An additional analysis was conducted by excluding all control
variables (gender, age, and education) from the structural equation
model to assess the robustness of the findings. The data demonstrate
that the hypothesized effects and correlations among the major vari-
ables remain significant and consistent with the key conclusions. In
particular, the mediated effects on return intention, and the path coef-
ficients and significance levels of the impacts of digital platform avail-
ability, usability, customized suggestions, and online review quality on
visitor experience quality, are essentially unaltered. This indicates that
the hypothesized relationships are robust and not driven by the inclu-
sion of control variables.

5.1 Theoretical implications
First, about the context of the tourist digital revolution, this
study advances research on the relationship between digital tech -
nology and tourists’ cognitive behavior. Existing Studies Focused
mainly on the macro level of digital technology on tourism effi -
ciency, business models, and destination development ( Bekele and
Raj, 2025 ; Chen and Huang, 2025 ; Sanju et al., 2025 ) have insuf -
ficiently addressed the role of digital technologies on tourists’
psychological experiences and behavioral response at the micro
level. This study uses digital tourism platforms as its entry point,
focusing on the relationship between platform characteristics and
tourists’ perceptions and behavioral responses. It reveals, at the
micro level, how digital technologies shape tourists’ experiences
and behavioral intentions. This finding contributes to expanding
digital tourism research from a macro-level industry perspective
to theoretical extensions at the micro-level of behavioral mecha -
nisms ( Armutcu et al., 2023 ).
Second, this study systematically examines the multidimen -
sional characteristics of digital platforms from an integrated per -
spective, constructing a more comprehensive framework for the
digital platform environment. It addresses research on how digital
platforms are associated with visitor psychology and behavior,
thereby compensating for existing studies’ tendency to explore
platform impacts primarily through a single technological dimen -
sion. Previous research has mainly addressed platform impacts
through individual aspects such as technological accessibility
(Zhang and Abdullah, 2025 ), information quality, or personalized
services, and has lacked a comprehensive framework of character -
istics ( Generosi et al., 2025 ; Zeqiri et al., 2025 ). This study pro -
vides a more systematic explanatory framework for examining the
combined effects of platform characteristics by integrating four
factors to holistically define the digital platform environment:
availability, ease of use, customized suggestions, and online review
quality.
Third, this study reveals the mechanism by which digital plat -
form characteristics associated with revisit intention influence
experience quality, thereby providing a more systematic theoreti -
cal explanation of the formation process of visitor behavior in
digital platform environments. It blends platform features and
revisit intention into a single analytical framework, with visitor
experience quality serving as the partial mediator. This illustrates
how the digital platform environment is associated with tourists’
behavioral intentions via the experiencing process along a thor -
ough pathway. By providing more thorough mechanistic evidence
than earlier research, these findings improve our understanding
of visitor behavior formation in digital platform contexts.
Lastly, this work expands the application of SOR theory by
examining it in a specific urban tourism context and applying it
to the digital tourist platform environment ( Wu and Lai, 2022 ).
The use of SOR theory has mainly focused on e-commerce and
general online service environments, with its systematic employ -
ment in tourism digitalization contexts being rather constrained
(Tedjakusuma and Kulachai, 2026 ; Wang, 2026). This study pro -
vides new contextual evidence for applying SOR theory to digital
tourism by conceptualizing digital platform elements as stimuli,
experience quality as organismic responses, and revisit intention
as behavioral consequences.
5.2 Practical implications
This study provides several important insights for tourist des -
tination management organizations and tourism platform opera -
tors. The results show that four digital platform elements are
important for optimizing the quality of the visitor experience and
increasing return intentions. The same argument holds for loca -
tions that promote digital tourism platforms, even though the
sample consists of tourists from Beijing. In general, digital tour -
ism platforms should be positioned by attraction management
businesses and platform operators as significant touch points for
shaping visitor experiences, with an emphasis on optimizing plat -
form features ( Wang, 2026; Zhang et al., 2025 ).
First, work should focus on increasing platform availability.
Platform availability is directly associated with visitors’ ability to
acquire information and the ease of use. In practice, strategies can
be implemented to upgrade mobile interfaces, public Wi-Fi set -
tings, and international network connectivity, enabling stable plat -
form access across varied devices and use cases ( Myrovali et al.,
2025). Integrating important information resources, such as
hotels, food, transportation, attractions, and cultural events, may
reduce switching costs for travelers across platforms ( Cheng et al.,

2021; Zhang et al., 2025 ). Increasing speed and convenience in
accessing information can effectively reduce decision-making and
cognitive costs, which in turn can improve the quality of the visi -
tor experience ( Wang, 2026; Wu and Lai, 2022 ).
Second, continually increase the platform’s ease of use. During
platform design and maintenance, maintain clear structures, pro -
vide intuitive operations, and eliminate difficult repetitive proce -
dures ( Qu et al., 2025 ). Reducing operational complexity and
usage costs enhances visitor comfort and happiness, providing
more favorable user experiences ( Cheung et al., 2022 ), consistent
with communication studies that link platform usability to posi -

Third, provide more tailored recommendations to accommo -
date different visitors’ interests. Personalized recommendations
can precisely match visitors’ interests and preferences, enhancing
the relevance and usability of travel information. Depending on
tourists’ interests, means of transportation, and budgets, algo -
rithms can provide customized itinerary suggestions and service
packages ( Generosi et al., 2025 ). Notably, recommendations
should be relevant and understandable without being overly cus -
tomized to prevent algorithm fatigue or information overload
(Shao et al., 2019 ). The quality of the visitor experience can be
improved by providing visitors with precise, comfortable, person -
alized offers that significantly increase their understanding and
engagement ( Sam Li, 2025 ).
Fourth, place a great value on the platform’s reputation and
the caliber of its online reviews. The quality of online reviews
directly impacts travelers’ trust in the platform and destination,
as well as their expectations management. This aligns with mar -
keting communication research on user-generated content shap -
ing consumer trust and intentions ( Armutcu et al., 2023 ). To
screen legitimate reviews and reduce interference from false or
harmful comments, platforms should integrate features such as
review moderation and user verification ( Cheung et al., 2022 ).
High-quality reviews might be promoted simultaneously.
Destination managers should focus on targeted responses and ser -
vice modifications to address genuine negative feedback
(Sharafutdinov et al., 2020 ). Good reviews help travelers set rea -
sonable expectations, foster trust, and ultimately improve the
overall quality of their trip.
Lastly, destination administrators can improve services to
compensate for platform shortcomings, thereby enhancing the
quality of the tourist experience and encouraging return visits
(Khuc et al., 2026 ). Delivering clear advice and real-time modifi -
cations at the destination, for example, ensures consistency
between platform information and actual offers by establishing
seamless communication between online and offline services
(Myrovali et al., 2025 ; Zainal-Abidin et al., 2023 ). These tactics
help travelers feel less uneasy and apprehensive throughout their
travels.
5.3 Limitations and future research
The primary signs of this study’s flaws are as follows. First,
Beijing visitors’ self-reported information served as the basis for
all study data. Although statistical analysis indicated that the
common technique bias in this research is not significant, it is
unavoidable. Future surveys may include a peer-evaluation
approach to boost confidence. Additionally, other procedural con -
trols like item-order randomization, different scale formats, and
psychological or temporal separation were not used in the ques -
tionnaire design, even though several procedural remedies were
used, including informing participants of the study’s purpose,
ensuring voluntary participation, guaranteeing anonymity and
confidentiality, using screening questions to ensure respondent
relevance, and deleting inattentive or incomplete responses. These
extra steps might be used in future studies to improve internal
validity and further minimize possible biases. Second, as the
study’s data are cross-sectional, care must be taken when drawing
causal inferences. Additionally, cross-sectional data are ill-suited
to capturing dynamic changes in variables, particularly revisit
intentions. Longitudinal study methods may be useful for future
research. Additionally, visitors to Beijing were the main focus of
this investigation. The representativeness and generalizability of
our results may be limited since Beijing, a popular tourist destina -
tion and a model for tourism digitalization, likely has sophisti -
cated digital tourism platforms and services. Finally, while
research indicates that factors such as tourists’ economic status
and personal digital literacy ( Qu et al., 2025 ), as well as their per -
ception of the destination’s importance, popularity, and appeal,
may act as boundaries for visitor experience quality and intention
to return ( Mardika et al., 2026 ), these aspects are not examined in
this study. These elements may be included for further investiga -
tion in future research.
