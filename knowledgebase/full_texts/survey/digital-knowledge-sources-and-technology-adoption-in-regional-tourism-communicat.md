# Digital Knowledge Sources and Technology Adoption in Regional Tourism Communication Across Generations

Digital Knowledge Sources and Technology Adoption in Regional
Tourism Communication Across Generations
Alexandra Ďurovová and Denisa Jánošová
University of Ss. Cyril and Methodius in Trnava, Slovakia
alex.durovova@gmail.com
denisa.janosova@ucm.sk
Abstract: Digitalisation is significantly transforming the way users acquire, evaluate, and utilise information about tourist
destinations. The marketing communication of regions is increasingly based on digital communication channels, which
function as sources of knowledge and play an important role in shaping the decision- making processes of potential visitors.
The aim of this paper is to examine the use of digital information sources related to Slovak regions, the perceived quality o f
digital communication, and the impact of digital technologies on the motivation to visit. At the same time, the study focuses
on identifying generational differences in user behaviour, while also considering  environmental orientation as a
complementary factor influencing trust and decision-making. The empirical part of the paper is based on a quantitative study
conducted through a questionnaire survey on a sample of 590 respondents. The questionnaire focused on three main areas:
sources of information about regions, evaluation of digital communication quality (including trustworthiness, timeliness,
clarity, visual attractiveness, and motivational effect), and attitudes towards innovative digital technologies such as
interactive maps, virtual tours, chatbots, and augmented reality tools. The data were analysed using descriptive statistics
and comparative analysis, with particular attention given to generational differences. The findings indicate that social media
represent the dominant source of information, particularly among younger users, while older respondents rely more on
search engines and personal recommendations. Despite relatively high evaluations of digital communication quality, its
ability to motivate actual visitation remains limited. A similar pattern is observed in the case of digital technologies, where
tools offering practical value in the planning process show a stronger impact than visually appealing but less applicable
solutions. Environmental information appears to strengthen trust in a region but does not function as an independent driver
of decision-making. From a knowledge management perspective, the paper interprets regional digital communication as a
system of knowledge transfer, whose effectiveness depends on the extent to which the information provided can be applied
within users’ decision -making processes. The study thus contributes to a better understanding of the role of digital
knowledge sources and technologies in regional tourism co mmunication and offers implications for improving the
effectiveness of digital communication strategies.
Keywords: Digital knowledge sources, Generational differences, Online information quality, Technology adoption, Tourism
communication
1. Introduction
Digitalisation is fundamentally transforming the way users acquire, process, and utilise information in the
decision-making process related to visiting destinations. In an environment characterised by information
abundance, the key challenge is no longer a ccess to information, but rather its selection, interpretation, and
ability to be transformed into actionable knowledge. Regions and destination management organisations
therefore face the challenge of designing digital communication in a way that goes beyond providing information
and actively supports the decision- making processes of potential visitors. Although existing research highlights
the growing importance of digital channels, social media, and technological tools in tourism, less attention has
been paid to how these elements contribute to the creation of usable knowledge and what mechanisms underlie
their translation into actual decision -making. A research gap thus remains in understanding the relationship
between the availability of digital information and its practical applicability in users’ decision-making processes.
This paper seeks to explain how digital information sources, the quality of digital communication, and digital
technologies shape users’ decisions to visit regions, considering  generational differences in behaviour. It also
considers the role of environmentally oriented content as a factor influencing trust and the evaluation of regions.
Based on a quantitative study conducted on a sample of 590 respondents, the findings suggest  that decision-
making is not determined by the mere presence of digital channels or technological solutions, but by the extent
to which the information provided is perceived as practically useful. From a knowledge management
perspective, the paper contributes by interpreting regional digital communication as a system of knowledge
transfer in which effectiveness is defined not by the form of communication, but by its ability to support
understanding and the application of information in users’ decision- making processes.
2. Theoretical Background
Digitalisation is fundamentally transforming the architecture of information acquisition and processing,
reshaping the way users approach travel planning (Gvaramadze, 2022). Within the digital environment, a

complex ecosystem of knowledge sources is emerging, in which search engines, social media, and user -
generated content (UGC) play a dominant role. UGC, in particular, is  often perceived as a more authentic and
trustworthy source of information compared to official marketing communication, thereby significantly
influencing the perception of destinations (Al Fajar et al, 2025). From the perspective of user behaviour, a key
distinction can be observed between active and passive information acquisition (Cai, 2019). Social media
primarily serve a discovery function, enabling users to passively consume content and form initial impressions
of destinations through visual and narrative stimuli (Al Fajar et al, 2025). In contrast, search engines represent a
tool for goal-oriented information seeking, through which users systematically gather specific and factual data
necessary for planning (Cai, 2019; Al Fajar et al, 2025). This duality between discovery and targeted search also
reflects broader differences in digital behaviour, which are strongly shaped by generational characteristics
(Ivancsóné Horváth et al, 2025).
The quality of digital communication represents another key determinant of how information is interpreted and
used. In the literature, perceived online information quality (Online Information Quality – OIQ) is typically
conceptualised through attributes such as trustworthiness, timeliness, clarity, and the overall attractiveness of
content (Sophiandanie and Untoro, 2025; Indriyani et al, 2024). To better understand its impact, it is essential
to distinguish between the cognitive and affective dimensions of communication. The cognitive dimension,
reflected in the informational value of content, contributes to reducing uncertainty and supports rational
information processing (Sutrisno et al, 2024; Sophiandanie and Untoro, 2025). In contrast, the affective
dimension, primarily linked to visual appeal and aesthetic design, influences users’ emotional responses and acts
as a trigger for interest and subsequent behavioural engagement (Sutrisno et al, 2024; Indriyani et al, 2024).
From this perspective, information quality alone is not sufficient to drive decision -making behaviour unless it is
complemented by elements that stimulate emotional engagement.
In this context, digital technologies play an increasingly important role as tools that facilitate knowledge transfer
and support decision-making processes (Alford and Jones, 2020; Quiroz-Fabra et al, 2022). Their significance lies
not in technological novelty itself, but in their ability to translate information into a form that is meaningful and
usable for the user. From a functional perspective, these technologies can be broadly distinguished into
utilitarian tools, which are designed to help users accomplish specific tasks (such as maps, search engines, or
booking systems), and hedonic tools, which aim to create engaging experiences and foster user involvement
(such as AR/VR or gamified applications) (Quiroz-Fabra et al, 2022; Zhang, 2025). The effectiveness of these tools
depends largely on how useful and easy to use they are perceived to be, with technology adoption closely tied
to their ability to address users’ actual needs within the decision- making process (Zhang, 2025).
The way digital sources and technologies are used also varies across generations. Generation Z tends to favour
visual and easily accessible forms of content, relies more on passive consumption through social media, and
places a relatively high level of trust in influencers (Damanik et al, 2022; Orabi, 2022). In contrast, Generation Y
adopts a more hybrid approach, combining active information search with the use of social media and online
reviews, while also taking on an active role in content creation (Dabija et al, 2018; Bravo, 2020). Generation X
approaches information more cautiously, placing greater emphasis on trustworthiness and structured
evaluation, and tends to rely more on traditional sources and personal recommendations (Dabija et al, 2018;
Ivancsóné Horváth et al, 2025). These generational differences therefore reflect not only variation in preferred
channels, but more importantly differences in expectations, cognitive approaches, and the ways in which
information credibility is assessed.
Within digital communication, environmentally oriented content is gaining particular importance as it serves as
a signal of responsibility and trustworthiness and contributes to a more positive perception of a destination
(Martinez-Navalon et al, 2020). Su ch content can strengthen users’ trust and foster emotional connections;
however, its influence on actual decision -making remains limited. Environmental factors can therefore be
understood primarily as a supportive element shaping perception and credibilit y, rather than as a primary
determinant of decision-making behaviour (Martinez-Navalon et al, 2020).
3. Methodology
The empirical part of the paper is based on a quantitative study conducted using a questionnaire survey on a
sample of 590 respondents. Data collection took place between mid -January and mid-March 2026. The sample
included respondents from different genera tional groups, with Generation Z being strongly represented (n =
455), followed by Generation Y (n = 58) and Generation X (n = 69). A small number of respondents also belonged
to Generation Alpha (n = 4) and the Silent Generation (n = 4); however, due to their limited representation, these
groups were not included in the comparative analysis. Given the uneven distribution across generational groups,

the data were analysed using percentage shares within each group, with the focus placed on relative differences
rather than absolute frequencies. The questionnaire covered three main areas: (1) sources of information about
regions, (2) evaluation of digital communication quality, including trustworthiness, timeliness, clarity, visual
attractiveness, and motivational effect, and (3) assessment of innovative digital technologies such as virtual
tours, augmented reality, chatbots, influencer marketing, and int eractive maps. An additional item measuring
respondents’ environmental orientation was included after the main data collection and is interpreted as a
supplementary variable. Data were collected through an online questionnaire created in Google Forms and
distributed via several digital communication channels, particularly social media, electronic communication, and
the ShareQuest platform, which operates on a reciprocal respondent exchange basis. While respondents from
Generations Y and X were reached primarily through online distribution channels, a substantial proportion of
Generation Z respondents completed the questionnaire within an academic environment, particularly among
university students with an interest in regional development and related topics.  The data were processed using
descriptive statistics, comparative analysis, and a chi -square test of independence to examine the relationship
between generational affiliation and the use of social media as a source of regional information.  As the study is
based on a non -probability sample with an uneven generational structure, the findings should be interpreted
with caution, particularly in the context of intergenerational comparisons. However, the analysis focuses
primarily on relative percentages and average evaluations within individual generational groups rather than
absolute frequencies, which helps reduce the effect of unequal group sizes on the interpretation of the results.
Nevertheless, future research should employ more balanced sampling strategies to strengthen the comparability
and generalisability of the findings.
4. Results
4.1 Communication Channels Used When Obtaining Information About Regions
The findings indicate a clear dominance of digital platforms, with social media emerging as the most frequently
used source of information about regions (68.1%). As illustrated in Figure 1, a closer examination of generational
differences reveals a distinct pattern in information behaviour. While Generation Z relies heavily on social media
(75.8%), its importance declines considerably among Generation X (23.2%). In contrast, search engines show
relatively stable usage across generations (Gen Y: 55%, Gen X: 52%, Gen Z: 44%), suggesting differences not only
in preferred communication channels but also in the way information is approached and processed. Older
respondents appear to engage more actively in targeted information search, whereas younger users tend to rely
more on passive exposure to content distributed through digital platforms and social media environments.

acquaintances, which are relied upon by as many as 73.9% of respondents from Generation X, compared to
46.6% in Generation Y and 36.7% in Generation Z. These findings point to a gradual shift from interpersonal and
experience-based information sources towards digitally mediated forms of content consumption, particularly
among younger generations. At the same time, the relatively stable position of search engines across all groups

4.2 Evaluation of the Quality of Regional Digital Communication
Respondents assessed the quality of digital communication across several attributes using a Likert scale ranging
from 1 to 5, where 1 indicated a very low level and 5 a very high level. As shown in Figure 2, the highest rating
was assigned to the trustwort hiness of content (3.49), followed by the timeliness of information (3.35), clarity
(3.29), and visual attractiveness (3.29). The lowest score was given to the ability of digital communication to
motivate a visit (3.12). These findings indicate a noticeable discrepancy between the informational and
persuasive functions of regional digital communication. While respondents generally perceive regional
communication as relatively credible and informative, its ability to stimulate behavioural intention and translate
information into actual travel motivation appears to be more limited. The results further suggest that the
perceived quality of information alone may not be sufficient to influence destination- related decision-making.
Although respondents evaluate inf ormational attributes positively, these evaluations do not automatically
translate into stronger motivational effects. This may indicate that users distinguish between the informational
usefulness of digital communication and its capacity to create emotional engagement or stimulate action. In this
context, digital communication appears to function more effectively as a source of orientation and information
verification than as a direct persuasive mechanism influencing travel behaviour.

Figure 2: Evaluation of Digital Communication Quality Attributes
Generational differences also point to distinct patterns in communication preferences and information
evaluation. Generation Z places greater emphasis on clarity and trustworthiness, suggesting a preference for
accessible and easily interpretable content within fast -paced digital environments. Generation Y attributes
greater importance to visual attractiveness, which may reflect stronger sensitivity to aesthetic and experiential
dimensions of digital communication. In contrast, Generation X tends to evaluate several communication
attributes more critically, potentially reflecting a more cautious and verification -oriented approach to online
information.
4.3 Impact of Digital Innovations on Motivation to Visit Regions
The findings suggest that the motivational potential of digital tools is not determined primarily by technological
novelty itself, but rather by their perceived practical usefulness within the decision- making process. As
illustrated in Figure 3, experiential events (3.97) and interactive maps with personalised recommendations (3.78)
received the highest evaluations, as they combine informational value with direct practical applicability. These
tools appear to support users not only in discovering destinatio ns, but also in translating information into

From an analytical perspective, the results make it possible to distinguish between utilitarian technologies,
which primarily facilitate orientation, planning, and decision -making, and hedonic technologies, which are
oriented more towards engagement, entertainment, and short-term attention generation. While hedonic tools
may contribute to visibility and initial interest, their influence on actual behavioural intention appears to be
more limited when not accompanied by practical informational value. Generational differences further indicate
distinct patterns of technology acceptance and digital behaviour. Generation Y appears to favour practical digital
tools that support planning efficiency and information accessibility, while Generation X demonstrates a generally
lower level of technology adoption and a more cautious approach to digitally mediated interaction. In contrast,
Generation Z seems to respond more positively to intuitive and immediately accessible digital experiences rather
than to technological sophistication itself, suggesting that simplicity and perceived immediacy may be more
important than innovation complexity in motivating younger users.
4.4 Environmental Orientation of Respondents and its Influence on Decision-making
To extend the interpretative framework of the study, an additional item focusing on respondents’ environmental
orientation was incorporated into the questionnaire during the later stage of data collection. Respondents were
asked to indicate their level of agreement with three statements using a 5 -point Likert scale (1 – strongly
disagree, 5 – strongly agree). As illustrated in Figure 4, respondents evaluated the following statements:
1. Information about environmentally friendly options in a region increases my trust in that region.
2. Digital content presenting environmental aspects of a region increases my motivation to visit .
3. I consider the environmental sustainability of a region’s offer and activities when choosing a
destination.
The findings suggest that environmental aspects play a differentiated and rather indirect role in the destination
decision-making process.  The highest level of agreement was associated with the statement that
environmentally oriented information increases trust in a region, while its influence on visit motivation and the
destination choice appears to be comparatively weaker. This pattern may indicate that environmental
communication functions primarily as a credibility -enhancing mechanism rather than as a direct behavioural
driver. In this sense, sustainability -related content seems t o strengthen the perceived responsibility and
authenticity of a destination, while its ability to independently stimulate travel intention remains more limited.

The identified sequence of influence  - trust → motivation → decision  - further suggests that environmental

In contrast, Generation Z appears to express a more declarative form of environmental sensitivity, which does
not translate as consistently into actual behavioural intention or destination choice. Generation X assigns the
lowest importance to environmental  aspects overall, indicating a more limited role of sustainability

communication can strengthen trust and positively influence destination perception; however, environmental
information alone does not appear to function as a sufficient standalone determinant of travel-related decision-
making.
5. Discussion
The findings suggest that although clear generational differences exist in preferred communication channels and
digital behaviour, the source of information itself does not appear to represent the decisive factor influencing
the motivation to visit a region. Rather than functioning merely as promotional channels, digital platforms
operate as mechanisms for knowledge distribution and interpretation, with their effectiveness depending
largely on the practical usefulness and applicability of the information th ey provide. This is reflected in the
discrepancy between the relatively high evaluations of trustworthiness and timeliness of information and the
comparatively weaker ability of digital communication to stimulate actual visitation intention.
The results further indicate that informational value alone is insufficient to influence behavioural intention
unless it is translated into a form that users perceive as relevant, actionable, and supportive of decision-making.
In this context, digital communication appears to function more effectively as a tool for orientation, information
verification, and uncertainty reduction than as a direct persuasive mechanism. These findings therefore
challenge the assumption that increasing the volume of digital communication or technological sophistication
alone necessarily leads to stronger destination attractiveness or higher motivational impact.
A similar pattern can be observed in the case of digital technologies. Their effectiveness is not determined
primarily by technological novelty or visual attractiveness, but rather by their ability to support planning, simplify
orientation, and facilitate the practical application of information. Technologies with utilitarian value, such as
interactive maps and personalised recommendations, demonstrate a stronger motivational effect than tools
focused primarily on entertainment and visual engagement. This s uggests that the adoption and effectiveness
of digital innovations in regional tourism communication are closely linked to their perceived usefulness within
the decision-making process.

Additional findings related to environmental orientation further support this interpretation. Environmentally
oriented communication appears to contribute primarily to trust formation and the strengthening of destination
credibility rather than functioning  as an independent driver of travel -related decisions. Sustainability -related
content therefore seems to influence destination perception indirectly through symbolic and reputational
dimensions rather than through immediate behavioural activation.
From a knowledge management perspective, the findings indicate that the effectiveness of regional digital
communication should not be evaluated solely according to the choice of communication channels,
technological sophistication, or communication intensity. More important is the extent to which communication
facilitates users’ understanding, orientation, and practical decision -making. Although generational differences
reveal distinct preferences in the ways users interact with information and technologies , a common pattern
remains visible across all groups: the mere availability of digital information does not automatically increase
motivation unless it can be transformed into usable knowledge that supports real decision- making situations.
6. Conclusion
The study examined the role of digital information sources, digital communication quality, and digital
technologies in shaping users’ decisions related to visiting regions, with particular attention given to
generational differences and the complementary role of environmentally oriented content. The findings suggest
that the effectiveness of regional digital communication is influenced less by the communication channel or
technological novelty itself and more by the practical usefulness and applicability of  the information provided
to users during the decision-making process.
From a theoretical perspective, the paper contributes to existing research by interpreting regional digital
communication not primarily as a promotional activity, but as a process of knowledge transfer whose
effectiveness depends on the extent to which information can be transformed into usable knowledge supporting
orientation, evaluation, and decision -making. The findings also indicate that environmentally oriented
communication contributes more strongly to trust formation and destination credibility than to direct
behavioural activation. From a practical perspective, the results suggest that regions should focus less on
communication intensity or technological sophistication alone and more on creating digital content and tools
that support users in understanding, navigating, and applying information in real decision-making situations.
The study is limited by the uneven generational structure of the sample and the use of a non- probability
sampling approach. Future research should therefore verify the identified relationships using more balanced
and representative samples while further ex ploring the mechanisms through which digital information
influences behavioural intention and destination-related decision-making.
