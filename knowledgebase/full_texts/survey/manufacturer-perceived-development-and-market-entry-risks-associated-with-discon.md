Abstract
Background Generic medicines are essential for pharmaceutical affordability and access, yet some products are never
launched or their development is discontinued even after patent expiry. Limited empirical evidence is available on how
manufacturers perceive the development and market-entry risks associated with these outcomes.
Objective The aim of this study was to identify and structure manufacturer-perceived development and market-entry risk
factors associated with generic medicine development discontinuation and non-launch in Japan.
Methods We conducted a cross-sectional survey of companies involved in generic medicine development and commer -
cialization in Japan. The primary survey outcome was company-reported experience of development discontinuation or
post-approval non-launch during fiscal years 2020–2024. Respondent characteristics were compared between companies
with and without such experience. Among companies with such experience, development and market-entry risk factors were
assessed in terms of both experience and perceived importance. All responding companies were asked to rate policy and
regulatory options on five-point importance scales.
Results Among 122 companies with valid outcome data, 52 (42.6%) reported experience of development discontinuation or
non-launch. Such experience was more frequently reported by companies with larger generic product portfolios. The mar -
ket size and profitability domain was rated highest among the risk domains (top-2 box importance, 94.2%), but regulatory
and clinical-study requirements (75.0%), formulation technology and manufacturing complexity (72.5%), and competition
and institutional uncertainty (48.0%) were also rated as important. Among individual risk factors, active pharmaceutical
ingredient (API) supply, patient-based bioequivalence or clinical-study requirements, dedicated equipment investment, and
special formulation requirements were, in an exploratory descriptive map, among the factors with relatively high reported
experience and perceived importance.
Conclusions Generic medicine development discontinuation and non-launch in Japan were perceived by manufacturers as
involving multiple development and market-entry risks extending beyond pricing to include API supply, manufacturing
complexity, regulatory uncertainty, and competitive dynamics. Respondents prioritized reimbursement, regulatory, and
manufacturing-related options for further consideration; the study did not evaluate the effectiveness, budget impact, or net
social benefit of these options.

policy discussions on generics have traditionally focused
on substitution rates, price competition, and expenditure
containment [1–3]. The use, pricing, and institutional envi-
ronment of generic medicines, including pharmaceutical

regulatory frameworks, health insurance systems, and pric-
ing and reimbursement policies, vary across countries [3 ,
4]. These institutional differences shape both the incentives
for generic entry and the types of development barriers that
manufacturers face. However, generic competition does not
arise automatically after loss of exclusivity. Entry and con-
tinued supply depend on manufacturers’ ability to absorb
development costs, navigate regulatory requirements, secure
active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs), establish reliable
manufacturing capacity, and maintain economically viable
markets [5–7].
Recent evidence suggests that generic markets can be
fragile, particularly for products with small markets, tech-
nically complex formulations, or requirements for special-
ized manufacturing or testing [5, 8]. Empirical studies have
demonstrated that manufacturing complexity and the regula-
tory environment affect generic entry decisions and market
concentration [6]. The prevalence of drug shortages has been
attributed to a multitude of factors, including manufacturing
issues, raw material constraints, business decisions, and reg-
ulatory factors [9–11]. These observations indicate that non-
launch and discontinuation of generic medicines reflect com-
mercial decisions made under multiple interacting sources
of business risk, including manufacturing, supply-chain, and
regulatory factors, rather than reimbursement prices alone.
Regulatory agencies have increasingly recognized the
need to reduce scientific and regulatory uncertainty in
generic development. The U.S. Food and Drug Administra-
tion’s Drug Competition Action Plan aims to remove barri-
ers to generic development and market entry, and its initia-
tives for complex generic products emphasize streamlined,
predictable, and science-based regulatory requirements to
reduce the time, uncertainty, and cost of development [12 ,
13]. These international developments highlight the impor -
tance of identifying which risk factors are most relevant to
manufacturers’ development decisions in specific health-
system contexts.
Japan provides a particularly informative case for examin-
ing generic development and market-entry risks. In this arti-
cle, we use the term ‘development and market-entry risks’ to
refer to the range of risks that manufacturers face in bringing
a generic product to market and maintaining its commer -
cial availability, encompassing formulation and manufac-
turing development, regulatory requirements, supply-chain
arrangements, and the commercial risks of market entry;
in contrast to novel medicines, generic medicines generally
rely on well-established active substances with substantially
lower research and clinical development costs, so the princi-
pal uncertainties concern bringing the product successfully
and sustainably to market rather than scientific development
itself. Government policies have substantially promoted
generic substitution, increasing the volume share of generic
pharmaceuticals from approximately 35% in 2007 to nearly
80% by 2021 [14, 15]. However, this rapid expansion of
generic production has coincided with quality, manufactur-
ing, and supply problems, including Good Manufacturing
Practice (GMP) noncompliance, insufficient development
and manufacturing controls, and large-scale supply disrup-
tions that have resulted in prolonged drug shortages affecting
a wide variety of products [14, 16]. While existing studies
have examined generic use, pricing, competition, and drug
shortages in Japan [ 2, 15], limited empirical evidence is
available on how manufacturers perceive the development
and market-entry risks associated with generic development
discontinuation or non-launch after approval. These issues
may be especially salient for generic products that require
additional regulatory evidence beyond standard bioequiva-
lence studies (often termed ‘complex generics’), including
modified-release formulations, drug-device combinations,
locally acting products, and complex active substances,
which pose additional technical and regulatory challenges
compared with conventional small-molecule generics. The
development and supply of such products are important for
patient access and healthcare cost containment, yet the fac-
tors underlying their underdevelopment have not been sys-
tematically characterized in the Japanese context [15, 17].
This study therefore aimed to identify and structure man-
ufacturer-perceived development and market-entry risk fac-
tors associated with generic medicine development discon-
tinuation and non-launch in Japan. Using a cross-sectional
survey of marketing authorization holders, we examined the
distribution of discontinuation/non-launch experience by
company characteristics, mapped individual risk factors in
an exploratory descriptive analysis based on both experience

and perceived importance, and assessed manufacturers’
responses to potential regulatory and policy interventions.
2  Methods
2.1  Study Design and Participants
We conducted a cross-sectional survey of companies
involved in the development, marketing authorization,
and commercialization of generic medicines in Japan. The
survey was designed to examine manufacturer-perceived
development and market-entry risks that may contribute to
development discontinuation and post-approval non-launch
of generic medicines, with a focus on market viability,
manufacturing complexity, regulatory and clinical-study
requirements, competition, and supply-related obligations.
Eligible respondents were companies that held marketing
authorization for at least one generic medicine listed in the
National Health Insurance drug price standard in Japan. The
questionnaire was distributed to 184 such companies. The
survey was conducted as part of a Health Labour Sciences
Research project funded by the Ministry of Health, Labour
and Welfare (MHLW) (Grant Number 25IA2010). The invi-
tation letter identified the survey as part of this MHLW-
funded research project, described its purpose, and stated
that responses would be statistically processed and that indi-
vidual companies would not be identified in any published
results. The complete questionnaire and the invitation let-
ter are provided in the electronic supplementary material
(ESM), Online Resource 2. The unit of analysis was the
responding company. The questionnaire was distributed by
email between December 2025 and February 2026. Each
company was asked to provide one response on behalf of
the organization. The questionnaire was sent to the contact
address available to the research team, which in most cases
was the contact point of the department responsible for regu-
latory affairs. The department and position of the person
who actually completed the response, and the internal pro-
cess by which the response was prepared, were not collected.
The questionnaire was developed by the study team based
on prior literature, Japanese regulatory and reimbursement
practice, and expert input from pharmaceutical policy and
regulatory science specialists. Before distribution, the draft
questionnaire was reviewed for face validity, clarity, and
practical relevance through preliminary interviews with six
companies primarily engaged in generic medicine business
in Japan, and minor wording refinements were made based
on their feedback. All six of these companies were eligible
for and included in the final survey and subsequently submit-
ted responses; information obtained during the preliminary
review was used only to refine the questionnaire and was not
included in the analytical dataset.
2.2  Survey Instrument
The questionnaire included structured items on company
characteristics, experience of development discontinua-
tion or non-launch, development and market-entry risk
factors, and policy or regulatory options. Company char -
acteristics included business model, annual pharmaceu-
tical sales in Japan, and the number of approved generic
products. The primary survey outcome was whether the
company had experienced development discontinuation or
post-approval non-launch of a generic medicine during fiscal
years 2020–2024. Companies that reported such experience
were asked to evaluate broad domains and individual fac-
tors contributing to discontinuation or non-launch. At the
domain level, respondents rated how strongly each domain
generally influenced the company’s decisions to discontinue
development or forgo launch, rather than its influence on a
specified product.
Broad development and market-entry risk-factor domains
included market size and profitability, formulation technol-
ogy and manufacturing complexity, regulatory and clinical-
study requirements, distribution and management costs, and
competition and institutional uncertainty. Individual devel-
opment and market-entry risk factors were assessed in terms
of both experience and perceived importance. For each fac-
tor, respondents reported whether they had experienced it
as a reason for discontinuation or non-launch and rated its
general importance as a barrier to generic medicine devel-
opment on a five-point scale (1 = not important, 5 = very
important); the importance rating was not explicitly linked
to a single product or to a specific past decision. Responses
coded as unknown were excluded from valid denominators
for importance ratings.
Policy and regulatory options were assessed using a
five-point importance scale. These options covered pric-
ing reform, international harmonization and regulatory
streamlining, support for specific products and technologies,
improved regulatory operation and transparency, and meas-
ures to support a sustainable industry structure. All respond-
ing companies were asked to rate the policy and regulatory
options. These options were assessed as respondents’ gen-
eral, forward-looking evaluations of measures for generic
medicine development, rather than as accounts of specific
past decisions.
2.3  Outcomes and Variables
The primary survey outcome was company-reported experi-
ence of generic medicine development discontinuation or
post-approval non-launch during fiscal years 2020–2024,
coded as a binary variable. Company-level descriptive vari-
ables included annual pharmaceutical sales in Japan (cat-
egorized as < 10 billion JPY, 10 to < 50 billion JPY, and ≥

50 billion JPY), the number of approved generic products
(categorized as 0–10, 11–50, and ≥ 51), and business model
(categorized as generic-focused, originator or long-listed
plus generic, and other).
For individual development and market-entry risk fac -
tors, the experience rate was defined as the proportion of
respondents with discontinuation or non-launch experience
who reported having experienced the factor. Top-2 box
importance was defined as the proportion of respondents
rating the factor as 4 or 5 on the five-point scale. For policy
and regulatory options, top-2 box importance was defined
similarly.
2.4  Statistical Analysis
Descriptive statistics were used to summarize respondent
characteristics and survey responses. Categorical variables
were reported as counts and percentages. Ordinal importance
ratings were summarized using medians and interquartile
ranges (IQR), as well as top-2 box percentages. The preva-
lence of development discontinuation or non-launch was
calculated among respondents with valid responses to the
primary survey outcome. For each item, percentages were
calculated using available cases; respondents with missing
responses, and responses coded as unknown for importance
ratings, were excluded from the denominator for that item.
Valid denominators therefore varied by item and are reported
for each item in ESM Online Resource 1 (Supplementary
Tables S1 and S2).
Respondent characteristics were compared between com-
panies with and without discontinuation or non-launch expe-
rience using descriptive statistics. No inferential statistical
testing was performed; all comparisons were descriptive.
Among companies with discontinuation or non-
launch experience, development and market-entry risk-
factor domains were summarized using medians, IQRs,
and top-2 box importance. Individual development and
market-entry risk factors were visualized using a priority
map, with the x -axis showing the proportion of respond-
ents who reported having experienced each factor and the
y-axis showing top-2 box importance. The priority map
was intended as an exploratory descriptive tool to jointly
display how frequently each factor was experienced and
how strongly it was rated, rather than to define statistically
validated priority categories. Policy and regulatory options
were summarized using top-2 box importance, and the ten
highest-rated options were displayed using a Cleveland dot
plot [18]. All analyses used available cases for each item.
Statistical analyses were conducted using R version 4.5.2.
This study was based on a survey of companies regard-
ing their business decisions and did not involve individual
patient data, personal health information, or individual-
level personal information. Institutional review board
approval was therefore not sought under applicable Japa-
nese ethical guidelines for medical and biological research
involving human subjects.
3  Results
3.1  Respondent Characteristics and Prevalence
of Development Discontinuation/Non‑launch
A total of 124 companies responded to the survey. The
response rate was 67.4% (124 of 184 companies surveyed).
Of these, 122 provided valid responses regarding experi-
ence of generic medicine development discontinuation or
post-approval non-launch during fiscal years 2020–2024.
Overall, 52 companies reported such experience, corre-
sponding to 42.6% of respondents with valid outcome data.
Among the 122 respondents with valid outcome data,
51 companies (41.8%) were generic-focused, 44 (36.1%)
had both originator or long-listed products and generics,
and 27 (22.1%) were classified as other business mod-
els (Table  1). In terms of annual pharmaceutical sales,
64 companies (52.5%) had sales of < 10 billion JPY,
32 (26.2%) had sales of 10 to < 50 billion JPY, and 26
(21.3%) had sales of 50 billion JPY or more. A total of 73
companies (59.8%) held 0–10 approved generic products,
30 (24.6%) held 11–50 products, and 19 (15.6%) held 51
or more products.
The most notable difference between companies with
and without discontinuation or non-launch experience
was in the number of approved generic products. Among
companies with such experience, 34.6% held 51 or more
approved products, compared with only 1.4% of compa-
nies without such experience. Companies with discontinu-
ation or non-launch experience also more frequently had
a generic-focused business model (53.8% vs 32.9%) and
annual sales of 10 to <50 billion JPY (38.5% vs 17.1%).
3.2  Development and Market‑Entry Risk‑Factor
Domains
Among companies with experience of discontinuation or
non-launch, the highest-rated risk domain was market size
and profitability (median 5 [IQR 5–5]; top-2 box, 94.2%)
(Table 2). Regulatory and clinical-study requirements were
rated second (median 4 [IQR 4–5]; top-2 box, 75.0%), fol-
lowed by formulation technology and manufacturing com-
plexity (median 4 [IQR 3–5]; top-2 box, 72.5%). Competi-
tion and institutional uncertainty had a top-2 box importance
of 48.0%, and distribution and management costs were rated
lowest among the five domains (top-2 box, 30.6%).

4  Discussion
4.1  Principal Findings
In this cross-sectional survey of companies involved in
generic medicine development and commercialization in
Japan, more than 40% of respondents with valid outcome
data reported experience of development discontinuation or
post-approval non-launch during fiscal years 2020–2024.
Such experience was more frequently reported by companies
with larger generic product portfolios. Among companies
with discontinuation or non-launch experience, the mar -
ket size and profitability domain was rated highest among
the risk domains. However, regulatory and clinical-study
requirements, formulation technology and manufacturing
complexity, API procurement difficulties, dedicated equip-
ment investment, and competition-related uncertainty were
also prominent. Policy and regulatory options rated highly
by respondents included not only pricing reforms but also
relaxation of all-strength requirements, support for equip-
ment renewal, manufacturing and quality-control workforce
development, international harmonization, expansion of

4.2  Interpretation as Development
and Market‑Entry Risk Factors
These findings suggest that respondents perceived generic
medicine discontinuation and non-launch as being associ-
ated with multiple interacting development and market-
entry risks rather than with reimbursement prices alone.
The difference in portfolio size between companies with
and without discontinuation or non-launch experience likely
reflects the fact that companies with larger product portfo-
lios have greater exposure to development opportunities and
launch decisions, and therefore more occasions on which
development may be discontinued or products may not be
launched. These factors should be interpreted as manufac -
turer-perceived development and market-entry risks rather
than product-level causal determinants of discontinuation
or non-launch.
The exploratory priority map further illustrates this pat-
tern. Factors with relatively high reported experience and
perceived importance were not limited to low price or small
market size. API supply difficulties, patient-based bioequiva-
lence or clinical-study requirements, dedicated equipment
investment, special formulation technology, AG-related
profitability concerns, and patent-related issues were all
positioned as frequently experienced and highly impor -
tant. These findings indicate that manufacturers perceived
the combined burden of economic feasibility, chemistry,
manufacturing, and controls (CMC) capability, clinical and
regulatory uncertainty, supply-chain constraints, intellectual-
property risk, and competitive dynamics as relevant to their
decisions to proceed with or abandon generic development;
the study did not establish how these factors shaped indi-
vidual decisions.
4.3  Relation to Previous Literature
The present findings are consistent with prior literature
showing that generic medicine markets can be fragile despite
their role in improving affordability and supporting cost con-
tainment. Frank and colleagues documented how generic
drug markets are affected by price competition, supply dis-
ruptions, purchasing structure, and supply-chain changes,
particularly in smaller markets and for injectable products
where fewer entrants and higher exit rates are observed
[5]. Wang and colleagues developed a structural model of
generic entry that explicitly incorporated manufacturing
complexity and regulatory barriers, demonstrating that these
factors significantly reduce the likelihood of generic entry
and contribute to market concentration [6]. Shukar and col-
leagues reviewed the multifactorial causes of drug shortages
globally, identifying manufacturing problems, raw material
constraints, and regulatory factors as major determinants
[9]. In the Japanese context, Izutsu and colleagues docu-
mented how GMP noncompliance and quality management
problems led to prolonged supply disruptions, highlighting
inadequate development processes and the neglect of qual-
ity-by-design approaches as root causes of manufacturing
issues [14].

The present study adds to this literature by providing
company-level evidence from Japan on actual discontinu-
ation or non-launch experience and by jointly assessing
whether each risk factor was experienced and how important
it was perceived to be. Although some factors identified in
this study reflect Japan-specific institutional arrangements,
such as reimbursement rules and product-lineup expecta -
tions, many of the observed barriers—including small
markets, manufacturing complexity, API procurement dif-
ficulties, and regulatory uncertainty—are relevant to the
sustainability of off-patent medicine development interna-
tionally [5–9, 12, 13].
4.4  Implications for Regulatory Science
The results have several implications for regulatory science
and generic medicine development. First, the high impor -
tance assigned to regulatory and clinical-study requirements
indicates that respondents regarded scientific and regula-
tory predictability as an important consideration for generic
development feasibility. For products requiring additional
evidence beyond standard bioequivalence studies, including
specialized dosage forms, drug–device combinations, and
products requiring patient-based bioequivalence or clinical
studies, respondents perceived the predictability of study
requirements, equivalence criteria, and data acceptability
as an important consideration for development feasibility,
particularly where product-specific guidance is limited [12,
13]. This reflects manufacturers’ perceptions rather than an
assessment of the consistency of Japanese regulatory deci-
sion making; the high rating assigned to expansion of PMDA
consultation capacity indicates that respondents prioritized
earlier and broader scientific dialogue as a potentially help-
ful option for addressing product-specific questions. Sec-
ond, the prominence of API supply difficulties and dedicated
equipment investment suggests that development feasibil-
ity depends not only on the approval pathway but also on
upstream supply-chain resilience, manufacturing infrastruc-
ture, and company-level shortage management systems.
This finding resonates with observations that inadequate
formulation and process development, rather than manufac-
turing noncompliance alone, can be a root cause of quality
problems and supply disruptions [14, 16]. Third, regulatory
rationalization should not be interpreted as lowering quality
standards. Rather, the findings support further consideration
of scientifically justified, predictable, and internationally
aligned regulatory approaches that reduce avoidable uncer -
tainty while maintaining product quality, efficacy, and safety.
These regulatory considerations should also be placed in
an international context. Additional evidence requirements
for modified-release products and other products that can-
not be adequately assessed through standard bioequivalence
studies are not unique to Japan. Both Japan and the European
Union require additional pharmacokinetic and dissolution
data for such products [19–21], and in the European Union,
products that are not eligible for a standard generic appli-
cation under Article 10(1) of Directive 2001/83/EC may
require a hybrid application under Article 10(3), with the
additional evidence determined by the characteristics of the
product [19]. The regulatory challenges reported by respond-
ents may therefore partly reflect the inherent complexity of
these product categories rather than Japan-specific regula -
tion. Nevertheless, respondents assigned high importance
to the review of Japan-specific requirements and to mutual
recognition of pharmacopoeial standards, indicating that fur-
ther international alignment of the remaining Japan-specific
elements is an option that respondents considered potentially
relevant to reducing perceived development burden.
4.5  Policy Implications
From the manufacturer perspective, reimbursement and
profitability were among the most highly prioritized issues;
however, the survey did not evaluate whether reimburse-
ment reform would reduce discontinuation or non-launch.
Under Japan’s National Health Insurance (NHI) system,
official reimbursement prices are set for all listed prescrip-
tion medicines, including generics. When the first generic
versions are listed, their initial reimbursement prices are
generally set at a fixed proportion of the originator price;
for oral medicines, the proportion is lower when many
generic products are listed simultaneously. After launch,
listed prices are periodically revised based on observed
market transaction prices. This pricing structure means
that manufacturers have limited control over reimburse-
ment levels, and prices may decline progressively after
launch. The highest-rated policy options were related to
reflecting rising costs, improving unprofitable-product
repricing, and reviewing off-year price revisions. These
findings indicate that manufacturers perceive reimburse-
ment and profitability as central considerations for devel-
opment sustainability. However, respondents also placed
substantial importance on non-price measures. Relaxing
all-strength requirements may reduce the burden of launch-
ing and maintaining very low-volume strengths. However,
relaxation of this requirement could also have unintended
consequences. If manufacturers were permitted to market
only the most profitable strengths selectively, formulations
that are commercially unattractive but clinically impor -
tant, such as lower-dose strengths for pediatric use or dose
titration and less frequently prescribed strengths serving
small patient populations, could become under-provided.
This illustrates the broader tension between commercial
incentives and public health needs. Any relaxation would
therefore need to be accompanied by safeguards, such as
an assessment of the clinical necessity of each strength or

arrangements to ensure that medically necessary strengths
remain available. Respondents also prioritized options
related to equipment renewal, workforce development,
international alignment, regulatory consultation, and
review of Japan-specific requirements. These ratings iden-
tify options for further evaluation but do not establish their
effectiveness, fiscal impact, or net social benefit. Because
these policy options were rated from the manufacturer per -
spective, they should be interpreted as perceived measures
to reduce development and market-entry risk, not as direct
evidence of their net social benefit or budget impact.
At the same time, these manufacturer-prioritized options
should not be interpreted as policy recommendations
endorsed by this study. Several highly rated options, such as
reflecting cost increases in reimbursement prices and provid-
ing support for equipment renewal or workforce develop-
ment, would increase manufacturer remuneration or public
expenditure. Other highly rated options, including greater
regulatory flexibility and international alignment, would
instead primarily seek to reduce administrative or evidence-
generation burdens. Whether the options that increase remu-
neration or expenditure represent an efficient use of public
resources depends on competing health-system objectives,
including affordability for patients and payers, efficient allo-
cation of limited public budgets, and the long-term sustain-
ability of the NHI system; higher generic prices would also
partly offset the savings that constitute the principal social
rationale for generic substitution. In particular, combining
public support for manufacturers’ capital investment and
workforce development with higher reimbursement prices
would place a dual burden on public budgets, and this study
provides no evidence on whether such a combination would
represent an efficient use of public resources. Any reform
package would therefore need to weigh any hypothesized
effects on product availability or continuity of supply against
fiscal costs, target support toward products with high clinical
need, limited alternative supply, and demonstrably limited
commercial viability, and incorporate mechanisms for evalu-
ating effectiveness, rather than providing across-the-board
support.
4.6  Limitations
This study has several limitations. First, the cross-sec-
tional design precludes causal inference; the observed dif-
ferences in company characteristics between those with
and without discontinuation or non-launch experience
should be interpreted descriptively. Development discon-
tinuation and post-approval non-launch were combined
because both represent manufacturer decisions not to bring
a generic product to market. However, these outcomes
may occur at different stages of development and may
be driven by partly distinct factors. Future product-level
studies should distinguish between pre-approval discon-
tinuation and post-approval non-launch. Second, the sur -
vey relied on company-reported information and may be
affected by recall bias, subjective interpretation of items,
and social desirability bias. Because respondent depart-
ments, positions, and internal decision processes were
not systematically collected, it is unclear whether the
responses represent the perceptions of the decision mak -
ers who made portfolio-level decisions, the views of a par -
ticular operational department, or the company’s broader
policy preferences, and the results should be interpreted as
company-submitted responses rather than as verified com-
pany-wide positions. In addition, the importance ratings
reflected respondents’ general assessment of each factor as
a barrier to generic medicine development rather than its
contribution to a specific past decision, and respondents
were not instructed to refer to a single product. Further -
more, responses coded as unknown were excluded from
the item denominators, and valid denominators fell to as
low as 37 for some importance ratings; if companies with
less direct experience of a given factor were more likely
to respond unknown, the reported top-2 box percentages
may be biased upward. Third, respondents were limited
to companies that participated in the survey, and non-
response bias cannot be excluded. Because comparable
information on non-responding companies was unavail -
able, the direction and magnitude of any such bias could
not be assessed. Fourth, the analysis was conducted at
the company level rather than the product level. Char -
acteristics of the affected medicines, namely therapeutic
class, dosage form, technical complexity such as modified
release, market size, reimbursement price, and the number
of existing generic competitors, were not collected. The
number of existing competitors is particularly relevant:
if discontinued developments mainly concerned products
already supplied by numerous manufacturers, discontinu-
ation could represent rational commercial behavior in
increasingly competitive markets, with different policy
implications from discontinuation or non-launch in mar -
kets with few existing suppliers. Company characteristics
such as domestic versus international operations and ver -
tical integration with API manufacturing were also not
collected. Product-level studies linking discontinuation
and non-launch events to medicine and market charac-
teristics are an important direction for future research.
Fifth, the survey was explicitly presented to participating
companies as part of an MHLW-funded research project
whose stated aims included supporting generic medicine
development and contributing to a stable supply system.
Although this MHLW-funded policy-research context
may have contributed to the relatively high response rate
(67.4%), it may also have introduced a policy-response
bias: respondents may have rated preferred policy and

regulatory options more highly than they would have in
a survey conducted outside a government-funded policy-
research context, and the same context may also have
influenced how respondents attributed previous discon-
tinuation or non-launch experience to external policy or
regulatory factors. These ratings should therefore be inter -
preted as stakeholder priorities rather than as evidence of
policy effectiveness. Sixth, the study period (fiscal years
2020–2024) overlapped substantially with the COVID-19
pandemic and its aftermath, which were characterized by
unprecedented disruption of international supply chains,
shortages of pharmaceutical raw materials, restructuring
of global manufacturing, rising transportation and energy
costs, and substantial inflation from 2022 onward, in addi-
tion to the series of GMP-related supply disruptions in
the Japanese generic medicine market. These exceptional
circumstances may have influenced discontinuation and
launch decisions independently of the structural factors
examined in this study, and the findings may not be fully
generalizable to more stable periods.
5  Conclusions
This exploratory survey described manufacturer-perceived
factors associated with generic medicine development
discontinuation and non-launch in Japan. The market size
and profitability domain was the most highly rated, while
respondents also reported factors related to API supply,
CMC and manufacturing requirements, patient-based bio-
equivalence or clinical-study requirements, competition,
and intellectual property. The findings provide a manufac-
turer perspective and identify policy and regulatory options
prioritized by respondents. Whether these options improve
market entry, product availability, affordability, or continuity
of supply requires separate evaluation.
