Abstract
The fashion clothing market has witnessed increased youth participation, largely driven by
psychological and socio -cultural factors. Among these factors, personal values play a crucial
role in shaping consumer behavior, particularly among youths. This study examined the
influence of personal values on youths’ purchase of fashion clothing in Anambra State, Nigeria.
The study adopted a survey research design, with data collected from youths across selected
urban and semi -urban areas of the state. Structured questionnaires were used as the primary
instrument for data collection, while data were analyzed using descriptive and inferential
statistical techniques. The findings of the study revealed that personal values have a positive and
statistically significant influence on youths’ purchase of fashion clothing in Anambra State. This
indicates that values such as self -expression, social acceptance, status consciousness, cultural
orientation, and personal identity significantly motivate youths’ fashion clothing purchasing
decisions. The results suggest that youths’ fashion consumption extends beyond functional needs
and is strongly driven by value -based and symbolic considerations. The study concludes that
personal values are key determinants of youths’ fashion clothing purchase behavior in Anambra
State. It therefore recommends that fashion marketers, designers, and policymakers adopt value -
based approaches in product design, marketing communication, and youth consumer education.
The study contributes to existing literature on consumer behavior by providing empirical
evidence on the role of personal values in fashion consumption among youths in a developing
economy context.

Key Words: Personal Values, Consumer Purchase, Youths, Fashion Clothing

INTRODUCTION
Personal values play a significant role in shaping individual behavior and decision -making
processes. These values, which are learned and internalized through socialization with family,
peers, culture, religion, and media, influence how people perceive themselves and their
environment. Among youths, personal values often inform lifestyle choices, aspirations, and
consumption patterns. In the context of fashion clothing, youths’ values may determine not only
what they buy but also why they buy it whether for self -expression, status, conformity, comfort,
or identity affirmation. Youths in both urban and semi -urban areas are increasingly exposed to
local and global fashion trends through social media, celebrity influence, and peer groups. This
exposure intersects with their personal values such as modernity, cultural pride, social
recognition, and economic considerations to shape decisions about fashion clothing purchases
(Canan & Sebnem, 2018).

that explicitly examines how personal values shape youths’ fashion consumption in Anambra

into consumer behavior that can guide fashion marketers, designers, and retailers in creating

can serve as a lens to interpret trends in youth culture, lifestyle choices, and social identity
formation. This study therefore seeks to investigate the influence of personal values on the
purchase of fashion clothing among youths in Anambra State.

Theoretical Framework
This study is anchored on theory of reasoned action. The Theory of Reasoned Action
propounded by Martin Fishbein and Icek Ajzen in 1975 was adopted for this study to investigate
how a consumer's psychographic characteristics social class, personal interest, personality,
personal values, attitudes, lifestyle etc. are associated with behavioural intentions toward fashion
clothing. This theory posits that beliefs (cognitions) influence the formation of attitudes, which
consequently directs behavioural intentions, nothing that individual psychographic
characteristics have an impact on beliefs and attitudes.
In relation to this study, the theory centres its analysis on the importance of pre -existing attitudes
in the decision-making process. The core of the theory is that consumers act on behaviour based
on their intention to create or receive a particular outcome. In this analysis, Fishbein noted that
consumers are rational actors who choose to act in their best interests. According to the theory,
behaviour can largely be predicted by the individual's attitudes towards performing the behaviour
in question, through the intervening effect of behavioural intention. The important attitudes in
this process are those that are specific to the specific behaviour being studied, it is not sufficient
to consider the individual's attitudes more generally (Fishbein & Ajzen 1975). The theory also
postulates that a person's intentions about performing a behaviour (which ultimately determine
whether they will do so) are influenced by social pressures or "subjective norms", which arise
from their perceptions of what others will think about them performing the behaviour in
question.
In this theoretical model, both personal attitudes and social or "normative" factors exert a direct
influence on behavioural intentions, which are the strongest predictor of actual behaviour. All
other factors in the external environment influence behaviour only indirectly, through their
influence on attitudes and subjective norms. A notable change in TRA is that attitude toward the
behaviour (the act of buying) is measured rather than simply the attitude toward the object. This

Empirical Studies
Adeola et al. (2021) conducted a study on consumer values, online purchase behaviour and the
fashion industry: An emerging market context. This study examines consumer online purchase
behaviour in the Nigerian fashion industry. Across -sectional study was conducted with a total
useable sample size of241 respondents contacted through on -site visitation. Descriptive and
inferential statistics were used to test the influence of customer value on online purchase
behaviour in the fashion industry. Consumer values are categorised into terminal (happiness,
love and satisfaction) and instrumental  (time-saving, price-saving discount, service convenience
and merchandise assortment) values. The findings show that both values have significant
influence on online consumer purchase behaviour, while fashion consciousness moderates the
relationship between consumer values and online purchase behaviour. Online fashion retailers
should focus on increasing the terminal and instrumental values of their products and making
available goods that meet the needs of different generational cohorts in society. Studies have
examined various factors, for example, consumer values that are determinants of consumer
online purchase in the fashion industry; however, there has been limited focus on the nature of
fashion and online purchasing in emerging markets, particularly those in Sub-Saharan Africa.
Canan and Sebnem (2018) investigated on perceived shopping values of young consumers in fast
fashion retailing in Istanbul. This study is about fast fashion consumption, focusing on what kind
of shopping values consumers perceive from the fast fashion retailers they visit. In order to fulfil
this major aim, the research objectives are to deﬁne fast fashion retailing, to analyse the
dimensions of shopping values and their impact on the purchase of fast fashion apparel. A series
of semi-structured focus group interviews were conducted to obtain consumers’ perceptions on
values emerging from shopping at the fast fashion retailers. Each focus group was consisted of
five young women, including questions developed after analysing the accessible literature. A
speciﬁc age group of 20 and 32-years-old was chosen to be interviewed. Regression analysis was
adopted in this study. In conclusion, the researcher noted that the finding can help fashion
retailers in understanding what values consumers consider important when visiting and shopping
as well as key dimensions describing fast fashion consumers’ motives that will serve them to
plan marketing communication activities targeted at young and fast fashion oriented women. The
study recommended that further research is required to examine within and across additional
sectors.

METHODOLOGY
The descriptive research design using survey method was adopted for this study. The study was
carried out in Anambra State. Anambra is a state in Nigeria. It is located in the South -eastern
region of the country. The study was restricted to three major urban cities in the three Senatorial
Zones in Anambra State namely; Awka (the State Capital from Anambra Central), Onitsha
(commercial hub of the state from Anambra North) and Nnewi (industrial hub of the State from
Anambra South).  The choice of the three major cities was based on its representation of the
various Anambra state economic and social strata and rapid development.
The target population of the study consists of youth consumers of fashion clothing in the three
(3) selected cities of Anambra state ( Awka, Onitsha and Nnewi ). Youths/adults within the ages
of 18-45 were considered for the study. Topman’s formula was employed to derive a sample size
of 379. The respondents for this study were accessed through a judgemental sampling method.
The study sample was selected based on availability of youth consumers of fashion clothing in
the three (3) selected cities of Anambra state (Awka, Onitsha and Nnewi). The data for this study
was obtained through structured questionnaire. Descriptive statistics and simple regression
analysis was employed in analyzing the data.

DISCUSSION OF FINDINGS
The study sought to examine the extent of influence of personal values on youth purchase of
fashion clothing in Anambra State. The results reveal that personal values positively and
significantly influence youth purchase of fashion clothing in Anambra State .  Values, as
internalized beliefs about what is important in life, guide consumer behavior and decision -
making. Youths in Anambra State who value uniqueness, self -expression, or sustainability are
likely to select clothing that reflects these values. This suggests that fashion brands emphasizing
value-based messaging may enhance engagement with the youth market.
This finding is in line with Grönroos (2007) assertion. He noted that personal value is considered
as the main object of exchange. The value is the results of evaluation by the consumer with the
seller. In support of the above, Kautish and Sharma (2018) examined consumer values, fashion
consciousness and behavioural intentions in the online fashion retail sector and found a
significant relationship between consumer values, fashion consciousness and behavioural
intention of consumers in India. Azzahra and Agung (2016) analyze consumer brand loyalty of

CONCLUSION
This study investigated the relationship between personal values and youths’ purchase of fashion
clothing in Anambra State. The findings revealed that personal values exert a positive and
statistically significant influence on youths’ fashion clothing purchase behavior. This result
underscores the fact that youth consumption of fashion clothing in Anambra State is not driven
solely by functional needs such as comfort or durability, but is largely shaped by internalized
value systems that guide attitudes, preferences, and lifestyle choices. The significant influence of
personal values suggests that fashion clothing serves as a symbolic medium through which
youths express their identity, social status, cultural affiliation, and sense of belonging. Values
such as self -esteem enhancement, peer acceptance, modernity, and individuality strongly
motivate youths to select clothing that aligns with their self-concept and desired social image. As
these values intensify, youths become more fashion -conscious and more inclined to purchase
clothing that reflects current trends and societal expectations.
Furthermore, the finding indicates that the socio -cultural environment of Anambra State
characterized by strong social interaction, exposure to global fashion trends, and increasing
digital media influence reinforces the role of personal values in shaping fashion consumption
patterns among youths. The positive relationship observed implies that changes in youths’ value
orientation are likely to translate into corresponding changes in their purchasing behavior. This
confirms the relevance of value -based theories of consumer behavior, which posit that personal
values are fundamental drivers of consumption decisions.
In conclusion, personal values constitute a critical determinant of youths’ purchase of fashion
clothing in Anambra State. Understanding these values provides valuable insight into youth
consumer behavior and offers practical implications for marketers, fashion entrepreneurs,
educators, and policymakers. By recognizing the central role of personal values, stakeholders can
better predict purchasing patterns, design appropriate fashion products, and promote responsible
consumption among youths. The study therefore contributes meaningfully to existing literature
by empirically establishing the significance of personal values in youth fashion consumption
