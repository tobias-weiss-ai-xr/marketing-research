Abstract
   Purpose – This study examines determinants of fashion product loyalty among Thai gay
consumers within the pink economy, focusing on the relationships among lifestyle, buying behav-
iors, marketing mix evaluations, and loyalty.
   Design/methodology/approach – Survey data from 229 Thai gay consumers were analyzed
using structural equation modeling (SEM). The measurement model was assessed via confirmatory
factor analysis (CFA), and structural paths were estimated using maximum likelihood.
   Findings – The results support a full mediation mechanism, lifestyle and buying behaviors
do not directly influence loyalty. Lifestyle positively influences buying behaviors ( β = .806, p <
.001), which in turn predicts marketing mix evaluations ( β = .859, p < .001), leading to loyalty ( β
= .680, p < .001). The direct effects of lifestyle and buying behaviors on loyalty are not signifi -
cant.
   Practical implications – Symbolic “rainbow-washing” is insufficient; brands should
strengthen tangible marketing mix execution (product, price, place, and promotion) in ways that
align with the segment’s behavioral patterns.
   Social implications – The study highlights the limitations of performative LGBTQ+ market-
ing and underscores the importance of substantive inclusion.
  Originality/value – The study provides empirical evidence from an emerging Asian market
and suggests that marketing mix evaluations fully mediate the relationship between lifestyle and
loyalty in this context.
Keywords: 1) Thai gay consumers 2) Fashion product loyalty 3) Marketing mix 4) Mediation analysis
5) Pink economy

Introduction
  Prior research has established several
foundational relationships relevant to this
study, yet critical gaps remain. First, while
Self-Congruity Theory predicts that lifestyle
congruence drives brand loyalty (Sirgy, 1982,
pp. 288-292; Kressmann, et al., 2006, pp. 956-
960), empirical evidence concerning these
relationships among gay consumers remains
limited, leaving it unclear whether the same di-
rect pathway operates within identity-sensitive
minority segments, for whom brand meaning
carries distinct social and psychological weight
(Oakenfull, 2013, pp. 83-86; Kates, 2002, pp.
384-386). Second, the marketing mix has been
widely studied as an antecedent of loyalty in
fashion and retail contexts (Zeithaml, Berry
and Parasuraman, 1996, pp. 31-34; Yoo, Donthu
and Lee, 2000, p. 197); however, its role as a
sequential mediator transmitting the influence
of psychographic variables through behavioral
processes before reaching loyalty has received
little empirical attention. Most prior studies
treat the marketing mix as either a direct driver
or a moderator, not as an intermediate mech-
anism within a causal chain. Third, research on
gay consumer behavior has grown substan -
tially in Western markets (Gudelunas, 2011,
pp. 53-57; Montecchi, et al., 2024, pp. 23-27),
yet empirical studies grounded in Southeast
Asian cultural contexts, where LGBTQ+ identity
expression is shaped by distinct social norms
and consumption environments, remain scarce
(Badgett, et al., 2019, pp. 2-4). Thailand pres -
ents a particularly underexplored case: despite
being widely regarded as relatively LGBTQ+
inclusive by regional standards, systematic
empirical evidence on the determinants of
fashion loyalty among Thai gay consumers is
virtually absent from the academic literature.
Fourth, existing studies on gay consumer be -
havior tend to focus on brand attitudes and
general purchase intentions (Gudelunas, 2011,
pp. 53-57; Oakenfull, 2013, pp. 83-86) rather
than on the process by which psychographic
profiles translate into sustained product loy -
alty through sequential behavioral and evalu -
ative mechanisms. No published study has yet
examined how lifestyle profiles among Thai
gay consumers are transmitted through buying
behaviors and marketing mix evaluations to
produce fashion product loyalty within a single
integrated structural model.
   Taken together, these gaps reveal
a substantive blind spot in the literature:
whether and how lifestyle drives loyalty in gay
consumer markets depends on intermediate
behavioral and evaluative processes that have
not yet been empirically mapped, particularly
in non-Western contexts. The present study
directly addresses this gap by testing a sequen-
tial mediation model grounded in the Stim -
ulus–Organism–Response (S–O–R) paradigm
(Mehrabian and Russell, 1974, pp. 283-285),
in which lifestyle (Stimulus) activates buying
behaviors and marketing mix evaluations
(Organism) before producing fashion product
loyalty (Response) among Thai gay consumers.
In doing so, it contributes both a theoretical
extension by qualifying the conditions under
which Self-Congruity Theory applies in minori-
ty consumer markets and empirical evidence
from an emerging Asian market that remains
underrepresented in the global literature on

LGBTQ+ consumption. Accordingly, this study
addresses three research questions: (1) Do
lifestyle and buying behaviors exert direct
effects on fashion product loyalty among
Thai gay consumers? (2) Does marketing mix
evaluation serve as a sequential mediator in
the relationship between lifestyle and loyalty?
(3) Does the full sequential mediation model
(Lifestyle → Buying Behaviors → Marketing Mix
→ Loyalty) fit the empirical data and provide
formal statistical support for a full mediation
interpretation?
Literature Review and Hypotheses
Loyalty in fashion consumption
 Loyalty is classically defined as a deep-
ly held commitment to rebuy or repatronize
a preferred product/service consistently in
the future, despite situational influences and
marketing efforts having the potential to
cause switching behavior (Oliver, 1999, p. 34).
In the fast-paced fashion industry, achieving
such loyalty is notoriously difficult due to the
"variety-seeking" nature of consumers and the
ephemeral nature of trends. However, for gay
consumers, loyalty extends beyond mere hab-
it; it encompasses an emotional attachment
to brands that reflect their self-identity. This is
particularly salient among LGBTQ+ consumers.
Research suggests that gay consumers often
exhibit higher levels of brand loyalty com -
pared to heterosexual counterparts, provided
that the brand authentically aligns with their
values and supports their community (Oak -
enfull, 2013, pp. 83-86; Gudelunas, 2011, pp.
61-64). Thus, in this context, loyalty is not just
a repurchase behavior but a form of identity
validation.
Lifestyle and buying behaviors
 Lifestyle, commonly operationalized
through Activities, Interests, and Opinions (AIO),
reflects consumers’ patterns of living and can
shape how they search for information, com -
pare alternatives, and assign symbolic meaning
to products. In the fashion context, Abd Jabar
and Mohd Noor (2024, pp. 923-924) found
that self-narrative and symbolic meaning were
important drivers among specialty-fashion
brand lovers, beyond purely functional con -
siderations such as price. Moreover, self-image
congruence can influence consumer responses
and brand loyalty through functional con -
gruence and related evaluative mechanisms
(Kressmann, et al., 2006, pp. 956-960). Building
on this reasoning, the present study examines
whether lifestyle influences buying behaviors
among Thai gay consumers. Consequently, it is
posited that:
 H1: Lifestyle has a significant positive
influence on buying behaviors.
Buying behaviors and marketing mix eval-
uations
  Consumer buying behavior in the
fashion context extends beyond the mere
act of purchase; it encompasses the complex
information processing and decision-making
patterns that precede acquisition. Contempo -
rary market analysis indicates a dichotomy in
purchasing approaches: approximately 48% of
consumers engage in deliberate, research-driv-
en decision-making, while 41% exhibit im -
pulse-driven tendencies (Business of Fashion
and McKinsey and Company, 2024, pp. 54-56).
Furthermore, the consumer journey has be -
come increasingly "phygital," with 63% utilizing

mobile platforms for discovery, yet 43% finaliz-
ing purchases in physical channels to scrutinize
product quality.
 Theoretically, these buying behaviors
function as cognitive antecedents to market -
ing mix evaluations. Consumers who exhibit
high-involvement buying behaviors (e.g., exten-
sive information search and comparison) tend
to evaluate the marketing mix—product qual-
ity, pricing fairness, channel convenience, and
promotional relevance more critically. This
is particularly pertinent to the gay consumer
segment, often characterized as "discerning
shoppers" or "early adopters" in the fashion
domain. Their buying behavior involves ac -
tive engagement with brand narratives; thus,
they do not merely passively receive market -
ing stimuli but actively filter them to assess
whether a brand’s product fits their aesthetic
standards and whether its promotion genuine-
ly reflects their identity or merely exploits it.
Consequently, active buying behaviors lead to
a more rigorous and clearly defined evaluation
of the marketing mix elements.
   H2: Buying behaviors positively influ -
ence marketing mix evaluations.
Marketing mix and loyalty
 The marketing mix (4Ps) product, price,
place, and promotion remains the fundamen -
tal framework influencing consumer response.
While traditional theories posit these elements
as functional tools, in the fashion industry, they
function as critical antecedents of customer
loyalty by fostering emotional connections
and brand resonance (Zeithaml, Berry and
Parasuraman, 1996, pp. 31-34). For niche de -
mographics like the LGBTQ+ community, the
marketing mix transcends functional utility; it
acts as a symbolic signal of the brand’s values
and inclusivity.
 Scholars suggest that for gay consum -
ers, Promotion is not merely about awareness
but about visibility and representation; adver -
tisements that authentically depict diverse
lifestyles signal a "gay-friendly" environment,
which significantly enhances brand attitudes
(Oakenfull, 2013, pp. 83-86). Similarly, Product
attributes (e.g., style, gender-neutral options)
and Place (e.g., welcoming retail atmospheres)
contribute to a sense of belonging. When
consumers perceive the marketing mix as fa -
vorable and congruent with their self-identity,
it reduces the perceived risk of discrimination
and fosters trust. Consequently, positive eval -
uations of these marketing tactics directly
translate into psychological commitment and
behavioral loyalty. Based on this rationale, it is
hypothesized that
   H3: Marketing mix evaluations positive-
ly influence loyalty.
Direct effects of lifestyle and buying
behaviors on loyalty
 Relationship between Lifestyle and
Loyalty Theoretically, lifestyle is posited to
have a direct influence on brand loyalty
through the lens of Self-Congruity Theory. This
theory suggests that consumers prefer brands
that reflect their actual or ideal self-concept.
When  a consumer perceives a match be -
tween their lifestyle (AIO: Activities, Interests,
Opinions) and a brand’s image, it engenders
an emotional attachment that fosters loyalty
without necessarily requiring rational evalu -
ation of the marketing mix (Sirgy, 1982, pp.

288-292; Kressmann, et al., 2006, pp. 956-960).
Previous studies in fashion marketing have con-
firmed that lifestyle congruity directly reduces
brand switching intentions.
  Relationship between Buying Behav -
iors and Loyalty Similarly, buying behaviors
specifically habitual purchasing patterns are of-
ten linked directly to loyalty. According to the
theory of habit formation, repeated behaviors
(such as consistently shopping at specific chan-
nels or following specific decision routines) cre-
ate psychological inertia. This inertia reduces
the cognitive effort required for decision-mak-
ing, leading consumers to stick with familiar
brands to minimize search costs and perceived
risks (Seetharaman, 2004, pp. 564-566). Thus, in
general contexts, established buying behaviors
are expected to directly reinforce loyalty.
 Contextual Nuance (The Gap) How -
ever, while these direct relationships are
well-documented in general consumer re -
search, they may operate differently for Thai
gay consumers. Given the complexity of their
social identity and the need for validation,
mere lifestyle alignment or habit may not be
sufficient to sustain loyalty if the brand does
not provide tangible evidence of inclusivity (via
the Marketing Mix). Nevertheless, following tra-
ditional theoretical frameworks, the baseline
hypotheses are proposed as follows:
   H4: Lifestyle positively influences loy -
alty.
 H5: Buying behaviors positively influ -
ence loyalty.
Conceptual model
  The conceptual model is grounded
in the Stimulus-Organism-Response (S-O-R)
paradigm (Mehrabian and Russell, 1974, pp.
283-285). Lifestyle is positioned as the Stimulus
because it represents the psychographic pro -
file that initiates fashion-related consumption
motives. Buying behaviors and marketing mix
evaluations are positioned sequentially within
the Organism stage because they capture inter-
nal decision-making and evaluative processes
before the loyalty response occurs. Fashion
product loyalty is specified as the Response
because it reflects the attitudinal and behav -
ioral outcome of these processes.
  The model also retains direct paths
from Lifestyle and Buying Behaviors to Loyalty
to test whether self-congruity and habitual
decision orientations exert effects beyond the
sequential mediation mechanism. All hypothe-
sized paths are shown in Figure 1; different line
styles are used only to distinguish sequential
mediation paths from direct-effect tests.

Methods
Study design and procedure
 This study employed a quantitative,
cross-sectional survey design. Data were col -
lected through an online questionnaire ad -
ministered via Google Forms and distributed
through relevant online networks and peer
referrals during February-May 2025. Before
accessing the questionnaire, respondents re -
ceived information about the study objectives,
voluntary participation, anonymity, and their
right to withdraw. Screening questions were
used to identify respondents who self-identi -
fied as gay men and had purchased fashion
products within the preceding six months.
Initially, 454 responses were obtained; after
screening, 229 valid responses from gay con -
sumers were retained for SEM analysis. The
final structural model comprised 11 observed
variables: three indicators for Lifestyle, three
for Buying Behaviors, four composite dimen -
sion scores for Marketing Mix, and one parceled
composite for Loyalty. Following the guideline
that SEM requires 10 to 20 observations per
observed variable (Hair, et al., 2019, p. 633), the
minimum required sample size was 110 (10 ×
11) and the recommended optimal threshold
was 220 (20 × 11). The obtained sample of n =
229 exceeds the optimal threshold, confirming
adequate statistical power for the SEM analy -
sis.
Participants and sampling
 Given the specific characteristics of the
target population, a non-probability sampling
strategy combining purposive and snowball
techniques was adopted. This dual approach is
widely recognized as appropriate and effective
for accessing populations that are difficult to
reach through conventional probability meth -
ods, including sexual minority subcultures
(Browne, 2005, pp. 47-50). Snowball sampling
is particularly suited to gay consumer research,
as community trust and peer referrals facilitate
access to respondents who may be reluctant

to self-identify in broader survey contexts.
 Eligibility was defined by two criteria:
(1) self-identification as a gay man, and (2) ac -
tive engagement in fashion product consump-
tion, defined as having purchased at least one
fashion item within the preceding six months.
No generational age restriction was imposed,
as the study's primary aim is to examine the
structural relationships among lifestyle, buy -
ing behaviors, marketing mix evaluations, and
loyalty among Thai gay consumers broadly,
rather than to characterize a specific birth co -
hort. The resulting sample spans respondents
aged 20-35 years, reflecting the economically
active population segment most engaged in
fashion consumption in the Thai urban market.
Respondents identifying as bisexual or other
sexual orientations were excluded to maintain
construct homogeneity for the SEM analysis,
yielding a final valid sample of 229 participants.

Measures
   All constructs were measured using a
5-point Likert scale (1 = strongly disagree to
5 = strongly agree). Measurement items were
adapted from established scales and refined
for the Thai fashion consumption context. Full
Thai item wording, English translations, and
scale sources are provided in Supplementary
File 1.
   Lifestyle was operationalized as a
multidimensional lifestyle profile based on
the AIO framework. After EFA purification, three
indicators were retained: opinion leadership
(Life_Style1), sports activity (Life_Style2), and
travel interest (Life_Style5). Because these in -
dicators reflect different AIO facets rather than
a homogeneous reflective scale, the construct
is interpreted cautiously as a lifestyle profile
index.
 Buying Behaviors were measured using
three indicators capturing rational/procedural
buying (BB_1), comparison-oriented buying
(BB_4), and deliberate decision-making (BB_6).
Marketing Mix Evaluations were measured
using product (MKM1), price (MKM2), place/
distribution (MKM3), and sales promotion
(MKM5). The advertising dimension (MKM4)
was excluded from the final model due to low
factor loading in the gay consumer subsample.
 Fashion Product Loyalty was originally
assessed using eight items (Loy1-Loy8) cov -
ering repurchase intention, recommendation,
brand commitment, identity enhancement,
satisfaction, and quality confidence. Because
these items demonstrated high internal con -
sistency, they were averaged into a composite
indicator for the SEM path diagram using an
item-parceling approach to maintain parsimo -
ny and a stable parameter-to-sample-size ratio
(Little, et al., 2002, pp. 79-82; Bandalos, 2002,
pp. 152-156).
 Reliability and validity were assessed
using Cronbach’s alpha, standardized factor
loadings, Composite Reliability (CR), Average
Variance Extracted (AVE), and HTMT ratios.
MarketingMix and Loyalty met recommended
thresholds. BuyingBehavior showed accept -
able CR with marginal AVE. The Lifestyle profile
showed below-threshold alpha, CR, and AVE,
and is therefore treated as a measurement
limitation rather than a fully homogeneous
reflective construct.
 In the final measurement model,
several cross-construct error covariances
were specified based on modification indices
exceeding 10.0 and substantive theoretical
rationale, following the guidelines of Anderson
and Gerbing (1988, pp. 411-419). These specifi-
cations were made parsimoniously to address
residual covariance attributable to common
method variance in a single-source self-report
design (Podsakoff, et al., 2003, pp. 887-891).
Data analysis
  Data analysis was conducted using
IBM SPSS Statistics for descriptive analysis and
AMOS for Structural Equation Modeling (SEM).
The analysis followed the two-step approach
recommended by Anderson and Gerbing
(1988, p. 411).
   1. Descriptive Statistics and Reliability:
First, frequency and percentage distributions
were calculated to summarize the demograph-
ic profile of the respondents. Cronbach’s alpha
coefficient was utilized to assess the internal

 2. Structural Equation Modeling (SEM):
The structural model was tested using the

 Measurement Model Assessment:
Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA) was first per-

of the latent constructs. Measurement quality
was assessed based on standardized factor

   Structural Model Assessment: The
hypothesized relationships were tested by

Discussion
 This study examined the determinants
of fashion product loyalty among Thai gay con-
sumers through a sequential mediation model
grounded in the S-O-R paradigm. The SEM
results yielded three principal findings that are
discussed below in terms of what was found,
how the mediation mechanism operates, and
the theoretical role of buying behaviors in the
causal chain.
 5.1 The Direct Role of Marketing
Mix in Driving Loyalty
  The structural results indicate that
Marketing Mix Evaluations were the only con -
struct exerting a statistically significant direct
effect on fashion product loyalty ( β = .680, p
< .001), while the direct paths from Lifestyle
(H4) and Buying Behaviors (H5) to Loyalty were
non-significant. This finding suggests that for
Thai gay consumers, psychographic alignment

with a brand however strong does not inde -
pendently sustain loyalty unless it is accompa-
nied by a favorably evaluated marketing mix.
   This result is consistent with Service
Quality Theory (Zeithaml, Berry and Para -
suraman, 1996, pp. 31-34), which posits that
perceived value derived from tangible product
and service attributes is a primary driver of
behavioral commitment. In the context of the
Pink Economy, the four retained Marketing Mix
dimensions Product, Price, Place, and Sales
Promotion appear to function not merely as
transactional mechanisms but as operational
signals through which brands communicate
genuine inclusivity. A brand's product quality,
pricing fairness, channel accessibility, and pro-
motional authenticity collectively constitute
the evaluative standard against which Thai gay
consumers determine whether sustained loy -
alty is warranted.
  A theoretically noteworthy measure -
ment finding is that the Advertising dimension
(MKM4) was excluded from the final Marketing
Mix construct due to low factor loading. Rather
than treating this only as a technical result,
the finding suggests that Thai gay consumers
may evaluate fashion brands more through
concrete product value, pricing fairness,
channel accessibility, and service experience
than through advertising claims. This pattern
is consistent with research suggesting that
minority consumers may approach LGBTQ+
brand communications with skepticism when
representation appears performative rather
than substantively inclusive (Oakenfull, 2013,
pp. 83-86; Montecchi, et al., 2024, pp. 23-27).
 5.2 The Full Mediation Effect of
Marketing Mix
 The most substantive theoretical con-
tribution of this study lies in the confirmation
of a full sequential mediation effect (Bootstrap
β = 0.074, 95% CI [0.031, 0.131]). The influence
of Lifestyle on Loyalty is entirely indirect, trans-
mitted sequentially through Buying Behaviors
and then through Marketing Mix Evaluations.
Neither Lifestyle nor Buying Behaviors exerts
a significant direct effect on Loyalty when
Marketing Mix Evaluations are present in the
model.
 This finding offers a meaningful qualifi-
cation to Self-Congruity Theory (Sirgy, 1982, pp.
288-292), which traditionally predicts that con-
gruence between a consumer's lifestyle and a
brand's image should directly generate loyalty.
The present results suggest that among Thai
gay consumers, this process is more hierarchi -
cal than the theory's direct-effect formulation
implies. Lifestyle operates as an initiating stim-
ulus that activates specific buying orientations;
however, whether those orientations ultimate-
ly translate into loyalty depends entirely on
how favorably the consumer evaluates the
brand's concrete marketing mix offerings. The
mediation chain thus reveals that identity-lev-
el resonance is a necessary but insufficient
condition for loyalty, it sets the psychological
direction of the consumer; the destination is
determined by the marketing mix evaluation.
 This pattern diverges from findings in
mainstream fashion consumer research, where
direct lifestyle-to-loyalty effects have been
more consistently reported (Kressmann, et al.,
2006, pp. 959-962). The divergence may reflect
the heightened evaluative standards character-

istic of gay consumers, who have been docu -
mented as particularly attentive to whether a
brand's operational behavior genuinely reflects
its stated values (Gudelunas, 2011, pp. 61-64;
Montecchi, et al., 2024, pp. 23-27). Brands that
project lifestyle alignment without substantive
follow-through in their marketing mix may
therefore find that initial consumer engage -

this segment a dynamic that prior qualitative

(Wait, 2025, p. 1).
 5.3 Buying Behaviors as a Cognitive
Filter
   The strong sequential path from Life -
style β Buying Behaviors (β = .806) → Marketing
Mix Evaluations (β = .859) highlights the critical
mediating role of decision-making orientations
within the causal chain. Buying Behaviors in this
model represent not overt purchase acts but
internal cognitive orientations — specifically,
the degree to which a consumer approaches
fashion acquisition through rational delibera -
tion, style-conscious evaluation, or self-expres-
sive criteria.
  The finding that buying behaviors fully
mediate the relationship between lifestyle
and marketing mix evaluations is consistent
with S-O-R theory's conceptualization of the
Organism stage as an active processing layer
rather than a passive conduit (Mehrabian and
Russell, 1974, pp. 283-285; Jacoby, 2002, pp.
51-53). Thai gay consumers in this sample ap -
pear to engage in proactive and investigative
purchasing processes, actively filtering brand
signals through internalized evaluative crite -
ria before forming marketing mix judgments.
This behavioral profile aligns with prior char -
acterizations of gay consumers as discerning,
information-seeking shoppers who apply iden-
tity-congruent standards to brand evaluation
(Oakenfull, 2013, pp. 83-86).
   These findings suggest that lifestyle
and buying behaviors primarily operate through
indirect pathways in this consumer segment.
Marketing strategies that seek to build loyalty
among Thai gay consumers may therefore
achieve greater effectiveness by focusing on
substantive improvements to product value,
pricing transparency, channel inclusivity, and
promotional authenticity rather than relying
on lifestyle congruence alone as a retention
mechanism. Future research might examine
whether these mediation patterns hold across
other LGBTQ+ subgroups or in fashion markets
with different levels of social acceptance of
sexual minority identity.
Managerial implications
  The findings suggest that fashion re -
tailers targeting Thai gay consumers should
move beyond symbolic visibility toward tangi-
ble marketing mix execution. LGBTQ+-friendly
imagery or Pride-related communication may
create awareness, but loyalty is more likely to
emerge when product quality, pricing fairness,
channel accessibility, staff behavior, and sales
promotion are experienced as credible and
inclusive.
  Managers should therefore design an
integrated physical-digital journey that pro -
vides detailed product information online,
inclusive service offline, and promotions that
validate rather than stereotype gay consumers.

Limitations and future research
  The study's cross-sectional design lim-
its causal inference, and the use of snowball
sampling may overrepresent socially visible
and digitally active members of the gay com -
munity. A further limitation concerns the mul-
tidimensional operationalization of Lifestyle
as an AIO-based profile index spanning three
distinct psychographic facets. Although this
approach is theoretically appropriate for cap -
turing heterogeneous lifestyle characteristics,
it resulted in below-threshold reliability and
convergent validity statistics, particularly Cron-
bach’s alpha and AVE. Therefore, the Lifestyle
construct should be interpreted cautiously as
a psychographic profile index rather than as
a fully homogeneous reflective scale. Future
studies should further refine and validate this
construct using larger samples, more homoge-
neous item specifications, higher-order factor
modeling, or alternative composite/formative
measurement approaches. Future research
could also extend the present model by ex -
amining whether the mediation pattern holds
across other LGBTQ+ subgroups, comparing
findings across markets with varying levels of
LGBTQ+ social acceptance, or incorporating
longitudinal designs to track how marketing
mix evaluations shape loyalty trajectories over
time.
Conclusion
 This study set out to examine the
structural determinants of fashion product
loyalty among Thai gay consumers, with par -
ticular focus on the sequential mediating roles
of buying behaviors and marketing mix evalua-
tions in the relationship between lifestyle and
loyalty. Using SEM with bootstrap mediation
analysis, the findings confirm a full sequential
mediation model in which lifestyle exerts its
influence on loyalty entirely through these two
intermediate mechanisms, with no significant
direct effects remaining once the mediators are
included in the model.
Theoretical Contributions
 This study makes three contributions.
First, it qualifies Self-Congruity Theory by show-
ing that lifestyle alignment does not directly
generate loyalty among Thai gay consumers;
instead, its influence is transmitted through
buying behaviors and marketing mix evalua -
tions. Second, it extends S-O-R applications
by positioning buying behaviors and marketing
mix evaluations as sequential Organism-stage
mechanisms. Third, it adds empirical evidence
to Pink Economy research in an underexam -
ined Southeast Asian context and highlights
the limited salience of advertising relative to
product, price, place, and sales promotion in
this sample.
