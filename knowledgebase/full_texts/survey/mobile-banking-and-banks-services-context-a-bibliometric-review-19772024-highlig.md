1. Introduction

Mobile banking has revolutionized the financial sector by providing convenience, efficiency, and an improved customer
experience. It allows users to conduct transactions, manage accounts, and access financial services remotely, reducing the need
for traditional banking methods ( Alsmadi et al., 2022). Mobile banking offers financial institutions new opportunities to reach
underserved populations, particularly in developing regions. In addition, mobile banking services offer enhanced security
measures such as biometric authentication and increase customer trust (Gupta & Pathak, 2022; Hafiz Jali et al., 2014). The ri se
of digital wallets and mobile payments has transformed business models and accel erated financial inclusion, contributing to
economic growth and promoting a cashless society (Kewal & Saxena, 2022; Sharma & Sharma, 2023).
A bibliometric analysis of mobile banking and banking services is important because it allows researchers to map the
development of research in this area systematically. It helps identify the most productive and impactful regions, institution s,
and scholars and provides a basis for future academic collaborations (Hafiz Jali et al., 2014; Kewal & Saxena, 2022; Sharma &
Sharma, 2023). By analyzing the annual growth of publications and key terms, bibliometric reviews reveal research trends and
emerging topics, enabling a deeper understanding of the development of the field. Such an alysis also highlights gaps in the
literature and provides guidance for future research to address unresolved issues and improve the effectiveness of mobile
banking technologies and services (Alsmadi et al., 2022).
The existing literature on mobile banking and banking services points to several re search gaps consistent with the
identified research questions and objectives. While mobile banking adoption and consumer behavior have been well studied,
gaps remain in understanding underbanked populations' security challenges and specific needs (Abdullah & Naved Khan, 2021;
Alsmadi et al., 2022; Patel & Siddiqui, 2023).

3. Annual Growth Trend

The data set on publication trends of mobile bankin g and banking services documents in Scopus from 1977 to 2024
shows an exponential growth pattern, especially after 2007 in Figure  2. In the early years (1977 –2006), the number of
publications remained relatively low and stable, with between 1 and 7 documents per year. For example, in 1977, there were
two documents, and in 2000, there were still only four publications per year. This period represents the early stage of mobil e
banking and its applications, where research was limited, and the technology  was still in its infancy.
However, after 2007, a significant increase in publications can be observed. In 2007, 12 documents were published, and
this number steadily increased, reaching 26 documents by 2010. This growth coincides with the global increase in mobile
banking adoption and technological advances in mobile devices, Internet penetration, and the increasing digitalization of
financial services ( Gupta & Pathak, 2022). Between 2010 and 2020, the number of publications skyrocketed, with an
exponential upward trend characteristic of mobile banking technologies' increasing importance and sophistication. By 2020,
the number of documents reached 82, demonstrating the growing research interest and crucial role of mobile banking in
modern financial systems (Alsmadi et al., 2022). The increase in publications coincides with the massive growth in mobile device
usage and the development of sophisticated banking apps and services.

4. Findings of the Review

The literature on mobile banking and financial services from 1977 to 2024 in Scopus shows a significant increase in
research, especially after  2007, reflecting the global spread of mobile banking (Tounekti et al., 2022). From 1977 to 2006,
publication numbers were consistently low and consistent, averaging less than 10 publications per year. By 2023, the number
had increased to 97, reflecting an  increased academic focus on mobile banking technologies, digital security, and financial
inclusion. The slight decline in 2024 suggests a possible plateau. Nevertheless, the overarching trend highlights the increas ing
complexity and importance of the sector (Wijaya, 2022).
Geographical research shows that India surpasses the United States in publishing output. Emerging countries such as
Malaysia and Indonesia are making a significant contribution, demonstrating their rapid adoption of mobile banking. Countries
such as China, Iran, and many African countries increasingly engage in mobile finance research, highlighting the global

5.2. Recommended theory for investigations

Based on your analysis of author keyword clustering from the VOSviewer study on mobile banking, several theoretical
studies and frameworks could be relevant for further research in each identified cluster. These recommendations follow,

• The crisis  communication theory framework can be used to analyze how banks communicated technological
innovations during the pandemic and how this impacted customer adoption and trust.
• Customer Experience Management (CEM) can provide insights to improve customer loy alty and retention in digital
banking services.
• Deterrence theory can be studied to understand customers' perceptions of security in digital banking and to analyze
how perceived threats and security solutions affect consumers' trust and usage intentions.
• The Diffusion of Innovations Theory provides insights into the diffusion of mobile banking technologies in the financial
services industry and their impact on customer behavior.
• Extended Technology Acceptance Model (TAM2) integrating social impact and cognitive instrumental processes could
improve understanding of user perceptions and adoption behavior in mobile banking environments ( Lee, 2009).
• The Financial Inclusion Framework can guide research on the impact of mobile banking on promoting fin ancial
inclusion, particularly in developing countries, highlighting barriers and perspectives for marginalized people.
• The Service Quality Model (SERVQUAL) can assess customer satisfaction and loyalty in mobile banking, emphasizing
tangible assets, reliability, responsiveness, security, and empathy (Sharma et al., 2024).
• Social capital theory can be used to analyze how mobile payments promote social and economic empowerment in
communities and to examine the connection between financial inclusion and social  networks.
• Sociotechnical systems theory can examine the interplay between technology and the social dimensions of banking,
focusing on the impact of technological improvements on economic environments and customer interactions.
• The Technology Acceptabil ity Model (TAM) facilitates the study of user acceptance of digital financial services and
emphasizes perceived ease of use and usefulness concerning security aspects ( Lee, 2009).
• The theory of planned behavior can be used to examine customers' behavioral  intentions for mobile banking,
particularly concerning perceived risk, subjective norms, and attitudes ( Robledo et al., 2015).

The Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT)  is instrumental in elucidating the factors influencing
mobile banking adoption during and after COVID -19, with particular emphasis on performance expectancy, effort expectancy,
social influence, and facilitating conditions (Martinez & McAndrews, 2023; Santosa et al., 2021).
