This study maps the development of academic literature on digital political
marketing and voter participation in the context of the 2024 Indonesian
Presidential Election using a bibliometric approach. Scopus -indexed
publications were analyzed with VOSViewer through keyword co-occurrence
analysis to identify conceptual relationships, dominant themes, and research
clusters. The findings show that “digital political marketing” appears as a
central concept connected to communication strategies, target audiences ,
voter participation, election information, personal branding, and digital
media. In addition, “social media” functions as a key node linked to platforms
such as Facebook, Instagram, and Twitter/X, as well as to social media
campaigns, political communica tion, and election political participation.
These results indicate that the literature mainly discusses digital political
marketing through the themes of campaign communication, audience
segmentation, candidate image-building, election information dissemination,
and voter engagement. This study is limited to Scopus -indexed publications
and does not use primary voter data; therefore, the findings represent
conceptual patterns in academic literature rather than causal effects of digital
marketing on voter behavior. By providing a structured bibliometric mapping
of key themes, clusters, and underexplored topics, this research contributes to
a clearer understanding of digital political marketing studies and offers
directions for future research on digital campai gn strategies and voter
participation in Indonesia.

Keywords: Digital Literacy, Digital Political Marketing, Presidential
Election, Social Media, Voter Participation.

1. Introduction
Literature on digital political marketing indicates that technological transformation has significantly reshaped
political communication practices in contemporary electoral studies. Previous academic works consistently
highlight the increasing use of social media platforms such as Instagram, Twitter/X, Facebook, and TikTok as key
instruments in political communication research, particularly in analyzing campaign strategies, information
dissemination, and political branding (Goodstein, 2023; Sanofi et al., 2025). Within this body of literature, social
media is widely discussed as a strategic medium that enables interactive communication between political actors
and audiences, thereby shifting traditional top -down political communication into more network -based and
participatory forms (Saud & Margono, 2021) . Accordingly, digital political marketing is conceptualized in the
literature not only as a campaign tool, but also as a broader communication phenomenon embedded within digital
media ecosystems.
Accordingly, digital political marketing is conceptualized in the literature not only as a campaign tool, but also as
a broader communication phenomenon embedded within digital media ecosystems.(Brad & Deac, 2025; Salsabilla
et al., 2025) . In addition, existing studies highlight that although digital media expands the reach of political
campaigns, its effectiveness in shaping political engagement remains a debated and growing topic in academic
discourse. Therefore, the literature places digi tal political marketing as a multidimensional research field that
involves communication, behavior, technology, and audience interpretation (Agus et al., 2025).

Although research on digital political marketing and political participation is increasing, there are still limitations
of studies that systematically map the intellectual structure of this field using a bibliometric approach. Most
existing studies tend to  focus on conceptual discussions or empirical case -based analysis without providing an
integrated visualization of how key themes such as digital political marketing, social media campaigns, digital
literacy, and voter participation are intertwined in the academic literature. As a result, there is a clear research gap
in the absence of comprehensive bibliometric mapping that captures thematic structures, conceptual groups, and
patterns of knowledge development in this field, especially in the context of ele ction studies in Indonesia.
This study contributes to addressing this gap by providing a mapping of the bibliometric literature on digital
political marketing and voter participation research. Using Scopus-indexed publications and VOSviewer analysis,
the study identified dominant the mes, conceptual relationships, and research clusters in the academic literature.
The contribution of this study lies in its ability to systematically visualize the intellectual structure of the field,
rather than to evaluate empirical political outcomes. A s such, the study provides a structured overview of how
digital political marketing is constructed and discussed academically across different streams of research.
The purpose of this study is to map the development of the literature on digital political marketing and voter
participation in the context of the 2024 Indonesian Presidential Election. Specifically, this study aims to identify
the main thematic structures , conceptual relationships, and dominant research directions that emerge in Scopus -
indexed publications through bibliometric analysis. This research focuses on mapping the literature and does not
directly examine electoral behavior or voter responses, but rather analyzes how these topics are represented in
academic discourse.
Based on this background, this study addresses two main research questions. First, what are the dominant thematic
structures, keyword co-occurrence patterns, and conceptual clusters in the literature on digital political marketing
and voter participation? Second, how does the intellectual structure of research in this field evolve based on
bibliometric mapping of Scopus-indexed publications, particularly in terms of emerging themes, research clusters,
and knowledge development in electoral studies?
2. Methodology
This study uses a bibliometric research design to systematically analyze the structure and development of the
scientific literature on digital political marketing and voter participation. Bibliometric methods are widely used to
map research trends, identify intellectual structures, and visualize the relationships between key concepts within
a particular academic field. In this study, bibliometric techniques were applied to examine the patterns of
knowledge production and theme development based on indexed s cientific publications. The analysis was
performed using Scopus as the main database, combined with VOSviewer for network visualization and
Biblioshiny for descriptive bibliometric analysis. This approach allows for a comprehensive understanding of how
research in this area has evolved over time.
2.1 Data Source
The data used in this study were obtained from the Scopus database, which is widely recognized as one of
the most comprehensive sources of peer -reviewed scientific literature. The literature search was conducted
using a structured Boolean keyword strategy:  “digital political marketing” OR “political marketing” AND
“social media campaign” AND “voter participation” AND “political communication” AND “digital literacy”
AND “election”. The data retrieval was performed on 15 June 2026 , covering publications from 2021 to
2025. The initial search yielded approximately 949 documents, which were exported and used as the dataset
for bibliometric analysis. The dataset was considered sufficient to represent global research trends in digital
political marketing and voter participation.

2.2 Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria
To ensure the relevance and quality of the dataset, a structured selection process was applied. The inclusion
criteria consisted of Scopus -indexed journal articles and conference papers that addressed digital political
marketing, political communication, s ocial media campaigns, voter participation, digital literacy, and
electoral studies. Publications were included if these concepts appeared in the title, abstract, or author
keywords. The exclusion criteria consisted of duplicate records, non -academic publications such as
editorials, notes, and book reviews, and studies that were not directly related to political communication or
electoral processes. After applying these criteria, irrel evant and duplicate records were removed, resulting
in a final dataset used for bibliometric analysis.
2.3 Bibliometric Procedure
The bibliometric analysis was conducted through a systematic multi -stage process. First, relevant
publications were retrieved from the Scopus database using the predefined keyword strategy. Second, the
dataset was screened based on the inclusion and exclus ion criteria. Third, duplicate and irrelevant records
were removed to ensure data quality. Fourth, the final dataset was exported into a format compatible with
VOSviewer software for further analysis. Bibliometric analysis was performed using VOSviewer software,
focusing on keyword co-occurrence analysis to identify conceptual relationships and thematic clusters within
the literature. The analysis applied a full counting method, with a minimum keyword occurrence threshold
of five (5), and used the default clustering resolution of 1.0. Network visualization techniques were employed
to map relationships among keywords and identify dominant research themes.  The final dataset consists of
approximately 949 Scopus -indexed publications , ensuring sufficient coverage for bibliometric mapping.
This methodological framework ensures transparency, reproducibility, and replicability, as all data sources,
search strategies, and analytical parameters are explicitly defined and can be replicated under identical
conditions.

3. Result and Discussion
This section presents the results of a bibliometric analysis using VOSViewer and discusses its relevance to
digital political marketing and voter participation in the context of the 2024 Indonesian Presidential Election.
The study's findings do not directly measure voter behavior, but map dominant themes, keyword relationships,
and conceptual groups that appear in the academic literature. Therefore, this section distinguishes between the
bibliometric findings resulting from the analysis of the co -occurrence of keywords and the literature -based
discussions used to interpret the findings.

3.3 Literature Discussion: The Effectiveness of Digital Political Marketing Strategies
The linkage between digital political marketing and voter participation discussed earlier shows that the
effectiveness of digital campaigns is highly dependent on the strategies used by political actors. In the
VOSViewer visualization, keywords such as personal branding, target audience, social campaign media, and
election information show that the literature discusses digital strategies as a means to strengthen candidate
imagery, tailor political messages to specific groups of voters, and expand the reach of campaign information.
Therefore, the effectiveness of digital political marketing strategies in this study is understood as a conceptual
theme in the literature, not as a result of direct measurement of voter behavior. In the context of the 2024
Presidential Election, some of the digital strategies that are widely discussed in the literature include micro -
targeting, influencer campaigns, algorithmic amplification, and artificial intelligence -based content. Micro -
targeting allows political messages to be delivered to specific audience segments according to their
characteristics and preferences, while influencer campaigns help candidates reach young voters through
digital figures that are close to the daily lives of social media users (Sernani et al., 2025; Tanusondjaja et al.,
2023).
This strategy shows that digital campaigns rely not only on the widespread dissemination of the message, but
also on the ability to tailor the message to the needs and characteristics of the audience. In addition, the
literature also highlights the use of algorithmic amplification and AI -based content in digital political
campaigns. Algorithmic amplification can increase the visibility of campaign content on social media, while
AI-based content can help with audience analysis, voter profiling, and more pers onalized messaging (Jha &
Singh, 2025 ). However, these strategies also pose risks, such as algorithmic bias, manipulation of public
opinion, the spread of disinformation, and data privacy violations. Therefore, the effectiveness of digital
political marketing strategies is not only seen from its  ability to attract voters' attention, but also from the
aspects of transparency, ethics, and accountability of campaigns  (Jost, 2023).  Keywords such as personal
branding, target audience, social media campaigns, and election information show that the literature discusses
digital strategies as a means of strengthening the candidate's image and targeting certain segments of voters.
Strategies such as micro -targeting, influencer campaigns, algorithmic amplification, and AI -based content
are relevant in the literature, but their effectiveness is discussed conceptually, not empirically measured. The
relationship of these strategies can be attr ibuted to the clusters that emerged in VOSViewer, suggesting that
the strategy is becoming a major theme in the digital political marketing literature.

3.4 Factors Influencing the Succes od Digital Political Marketing
There are several factors that affect voter acceptance and engagement in digital campaigns, including digital
literacy, trust in online political content, and the influence of social media. Digital literacy is very important
because it facilitates voters to critical ly evaluate political information and avoid disinformation, especially
for novice voters who often rely on cognitive heuristics and emotional attachments rather than rational
analysis (Khunsiri et al., 2025). Trust in online political content is also an important factor, voters' trust in the
internet and digital platforms can influence their willingness to participate in online voting systems and digital
campaigns (Powell et al., 2012)  (Romanov et al., 2025). Social media platforms such as Facebook, TikTok,
and YouTube have a dominant role in disseminating political information and shaping voter perception,
especially among the younger generation (Eskiadi & Panagiotou, 2025 ). However, the spread of
disinformation and problems related to digital campaigns can exacerbate voter skepticism and reduce trust in
digital political marketing (Rosário et al., 2025).
The personal branding of a presidential candidate can significantly influence voters' perception and decision-
making. A presidential candidate's brand image can increase voter loyalty and intention to vote, as it is often
in line with the voter's self -image and socio -economic c onditions (Van Steenburg & Guzmán, 2019 ).
Effective personal branding includes creating an authentic, credible, and community -relevant image, which
can mediate the relationship between branding and voter loyalty (Van Steenburg & Guzmán, 2019 ), but
political marketing faces several challenges, including public skepticism of digital politics and ethical
implications regarding data privacy and transparency (Dommett & Zhu, 2022). The use of digital advertising
and social media in political campaigns can cause problems such as disinformation, voter manipulation, and
degradation of democratic values. To address these challenges, campaign managers need to focus on building
trust through transparent and credible digital communication strategies (Dommett & Zhu, 2022).

3.6 Romania Case Comparison
The comparison of the 2024 Romanian Presidential Election is used in the literature as a contextual case to
illustrate the evolving dynamics of digital political communication within contemporary electoral
environments. The reviewed studies suggest that so cial media platforms, particularly TikTok, played a
significant role in enhancing candidate visibility and accelerating the diffusion of political messages through
algorithmic amplification mechanisms (Brad & Deac, 2025 ). In this context, algorithmic systems are
discussed in the literature as key structural factors that determine the visibility and circulation of political
content, thereby reshaping traditional patterns of campaign communication and information disseminat ion.
However, the literature also highlights that algorithm -driven visibility is closely associated with increasing
risks of misinformation, disinformation, and information disorder during electoral periods (Szabo & Bader,
2026). These risks are frequently discussed in relation to platform accountability challenges, particularly the
limited capacity of digital platforms to regulate political content at scale and in real time. Supporting studies
in digital democracy further emphas ize that algorithmic systems may unintentionally amplify biased or
misleading content, thereby influencing public perception in politically sensitive environments (Udo-Udo
Jacob & Vajjhala, 2025).
In relation to the bibliometric findings of this study, these themes correspond to dominant clusters identified
in the keyword co-occurrence network, particularly social media campaigns, political communication, digital
literacy, and voter participation. T he literature suggests that digital literacy is an important mediating factor
in how individuals interpret and evaluate political information in algorithmically driven environments.
Studies on internet trust and digital political systems further indicate t hat variations in digital literacy and
institutional trust can significantly shape engagement with online political content (Romanov et al., 2025 ).
Nevertheless, the reviewed literature emphasizes that the Romanian case cannot be directly generalized to
other electoral contexts, including Indonesia, due to differences in voter population size, geographical
complexity, socio -political diversity, and di sparities in digital infrastructure and literacy levels. These
contextual limitations are consistently highlighted in studies of digital democracy and electoral
communication, where platform effects are understood as context-sensitive rather than universally applicable.
Therefore, the Romanian case is interpreted in this study as a comparative reference to highlight broader
issues in digital electoral ecosystems, particularly related to algorithmic amplification, disinformation
dynamics, platform accountabi lity, and digital literacy gaps. These issues align closely with the thematic
structure identified in the bibliometric mapping, reinforcing the argument that digital political marketing
research is increasingly concerned not only with campaign effectivenes s, but also with governance,
transparency, and informational integrity in digital political communication systems.
4. Conclusion and Suggestion
The bibliometric analysis suggests that digital political marketing and voter participation research in the context
of the 2024 Indonesian Presidential Election is primarily structured around several dominant thematic clusters.
The VOSviewer results indica te that the most central keywords include “digital political marketing,” “social
media campaigns,” “voter participation,” “political communication,” “personal branding,” “digital literacy,”
“election information,” and “target audiences.” These keywords for m the main conceptual network of the
literature, with “social media” appearing as a central node connected to platforms such as Instagram, Facebook,
X/Twitter, and TikTok. The literature also highlights strong thematic connections between communication

strategies, audience segmentation, and voter engagement, indicating that digital political marketing is discussed
as a multidimensional research field within electoral studies.  In addition, the bibliometric clusters suggest that

The study is limited to Scopus -indexed publications and therefore may not fully represent studies published in

causal inference regarding the impact of digital political mar keting on voter participation.  Future research is
suggested to expand data sources beyond Scopus to include national and regional academic databases in order to
provide a more comprehensive mapping of the field. In addition, future studies may combine bibliometric
approaches with primar y data collection methods such as surveys, interviews, and focus group discussions to
better understand how digital political marketing strategies are perceived by voters. Longitudinal and experimental
studies are also suggested in the literature to examin e how digital campaign strategies such as micro -targeting,
influencer marketing, and algorithmic content distribution are associated with changes in voter perception and
engagement over time.
