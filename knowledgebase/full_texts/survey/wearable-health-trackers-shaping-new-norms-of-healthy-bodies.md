Abstract
Wearable health trackers are redefining health as a calculable, platform -mediated construct.
Health tracking companies have become epistemic  infrastructures in health communication.
Originally limited to tracking steps and sleep duration, tracking devices now analyse dozens of
physiological and behavioral metrics, transforming intimate, embodied experiences of health
and wellbeing into quantif iable and continuously monitored data streams.
Existing research focuses on behavioral outcomes, missing how platforms produce normative
models of the body. Using the Oura ring as an example, a critical analysis of the health
mediatization will be conducted. First, Multimodal Discourse Analysis (MDA) is used to analyse
the company’s marketing materials (platform blog, community stories, case studies). This
analysis examines how the platform mobilizes datafication (through scores, trends, and
visualizations) to construct a normative, algorithmically defined model of the “healthy” body.
Secondly, frame analysis is employed to examine how the epistemic logic of the wearable
medium reconfigures authority, displacing both medical expertise and embodied intuition .
Finally, the consequence of this shift to governmentality is addressed. Using online survey we
analyse how the new mediatized health norms are internalized by users as a form of self -
