ABSTRACT
In the contemporary digital economy, content creation by social media influencers (SMIs) has emerged as a dominant marketing
mechanism, fundamentally altering how promotional messages persuade consumer purchase intentions. Rather than relying on
traditional advertising, brands increasingly collaborate with influencers for their creative and engaging content to deliver genuine
and relatable narratives that drive consumers choices. The study empirically investigates the impact of influencer’s content attributes
comprising Educational Content, Engaging Content, Content Format, Visual Presentation, and Contextual Cues & Captions on the
Purchase Intention of Millennials and Generation Z (Gen Z).  The pr imary data collected through a cross -sectional survey via
structured questionnaire scored on a standardized five-point Likert scale. Data was collected from a purposive sample of 150 social
media users , residing in Chandigarh, India, divided symmetrically into two cohorts, Millennials (75) and Generation Z (75 ).
Cronbach Alpha confirmed the internal consistency across all the constructs. The empirical findings from multiple linear regression
reveal that educational and engaging content have significant impact on purchase intention. Conversely, the content format, v isual
presentation, and contextual cues & captions have insignificant impact on purchase intention. Furthermore, independent sample t-
test demonstrate a comparative study of Millennials and Generation Z across all the dimensions showed significant results for
content format and engaging content. The omnibus ANOVA verified that independent variables collectively exert a statistic ally
significant impact on Purchase Intention.

Keywords: Influencer Marketing, Influencers, Content Attributes, Purchase Intention, Millennials, Generation Z

1. INTRODUCTION
 The global proliferation  of new -age marketing communication channels has completely shifted the inclination of consumers,
brands, advertisers and viewers from traditional advertising to social media influencers (SMIs) marketing, who function and operate
as online opinion leaders and possess power to persuade consumer decision making (Abidin, 2015; Ki et al., 2020). At a glance, the
traditional advertisements are now perceived to be more corporate, scripted, and unauthentic. This has enhanced inclination of the
audience to shift to SMIs for their choices, opinions, recommendation catering to different niche domains such as travel, foo d,
sustainability, finance, education, beauty, gaming, technology and many others. The SMIs have an art to present their content  in
most educative, engaging, creative, aesthetically pleasing, and in clear manner. The content created by the influencer holds an
integral part in the entire influencer marketing campaign. The consumers response to influencer’s endorsements , does not solely
depends on source credibility: trustworthiness, expertise, attractiveness and similarity, (Hovland & Weiss, 1951) , or engagement
metrics or social proof or perceived fame, but it is also majorly driven by the specific content attributes delivered by the SMI
(Campbell & Farrell, 2020; Lou & Kim, 2019) . The content attributes are an amalgamation of different aspects such as visual
presentation, informative value, entertainment value, structural formats, and contextual sponsorship cues (Hogsnes et al., 2024;
Munaro et al., 2021). Content Attributes act as independent communicative stimulus that help SMI’s to shape consumers behaviour
(Munaro et al., 2024; Xiao et al., 2018) . Consequently, isolating these attributes to understand how they impact the consumer
purchase intentions still remain an area of profound academic and practical interest (Ki & Kim, 2019; Shah et al., 2023).

The present study aims to understand the impact of SMI’s content attributes on consumer purchase intention across two generational
cohorts: Millennials & Generation Z (Gen Z) residing in Chandigarh, India. The studies show that the digital consumption habi ts
are fundamentally distinct across these two generations (Chan, 2022; Venciute et al., 2023). The Millennials have transitioned into
digital age in their adulthood therefore they consider social media platforms for socialization, cross verifying information, selective
and rational brand evaluation (Lou & Yuan, 2019). And on other hand, Gen Z have grown in digital age, they have not known world
without internet, mobile phones, or social media platforms. Gen Z look for more authentic, visually dynamic, well-communicated,
and possess a deep desire to mimic the SMIs to meet their psychological needs (Ki & Kim, 2019; Munaro et al., 2021) . The study
uses a structured quantitative analysis to understand the different SMI’s content attributes and their impac t on purchase intent of
consumers across Millennials and Generation Z in Chandigarh. Many studies have been done related to influencer marketing
catering to different influencer related factors, strategies, product-based, or niche based studied in metropolitan cities or at national
level but few studies have focused on Tier2/3 urban, semi-urban cities such as Chandigarh. The city seems as a prominent economic,
administrative, and educational nucleus in Northern India, Chandigarh boasts its high literacy rate, high internet users &
connectivity, great blend of young & old population, increasing per capita income, high aspirational buying be haviour and highest
in online shopping (Arya Avni, 2025; PF Desk, 2025; Rana, 2024) . This makes Chandigarh a good sampling frame to understand
how consumers in Tier2 city react to social media influencer marketing and how they perceive the SMI’s content attributes that
eventually boost in persuading consumer purchase intention.

2. NEED OF THE STUDY
The modern corporate marketing budgets are shifting heavily towards influencer marketing campaigns. But it is important for the
influencers, brands, or advertisers to understand how to present and prepare the content that align with the follower’s interest. The
present study explains that influencer’s content attributes involv ing educational content that helps to seek information, clarity and
knowledge about a topic or a product/service , engaging content  boosts in mood upliftment, relaxation, or simply is stress buster ,
content format includes stories,  reels, long videos or carousels  that SMIs use to present their content on different social media
platforms, visual presentation meets the aesthetic appeal, quality and design of the content that attract viewers, and contextual cues
& captions mentioned to explain the message of a particular post. The strategic dilemma faced by influencers and brands is whether
they should invest in premium visual presentation and aesthetic designs or prioritize informational depth and educational quality of
content. This creates a practical need to establish empirical benchmarks that dissect these content attributes individually a nd
understand individually their impact on purchase intention. By measuring these attributes and subjecting them to clear generational
comparison (Millennials and Gen Z), this study provides useful insights to influencers, brands, advertisers, and digital ag encies
aiming to maximize conversion metrics based on the influencers content.

3. OBJECTIVES OF THE STUDY
The following are the research objectives of the study:
1. To identify the impact of specific influencer’s content attributes such as educational content, engaging content,
content format, visual presentation, and contextual cues & captions on consumer purchase intention.
2. To evaluate the overall impact of influencer’s content attributes on consumer purchase intention.
3. To examine variations in influencer’s content attributes (educational content, engaging content, content format,
visual presentation, and contextual cues & captions) and purchase intention across Millennials and Generation Z social
media users in Chandigarh.

4. LITERATURE REVIEW
The intersection of influencer marketing and consumer decision making can be comprehensively understood by integrating two
theories: Uses & Gratification (U&G) Theory and the Elaboration Likelihood Model (ELM). According to (Katz et al., 1973), Uses
and Gratifications Theory argues that consumers do not passively consume social media influencers’ content, instead they acti vely
share their opinions through likes, shares and comments and actively seek for the content that align with their cognitive and affective
needs. In the realm of influencer marketing, the consumers regularly seek out for certain influencers to seek distinct practi cal and
functional utilities (Ki et al., 2020). The ELM studies explains about the central & peripheral cues that consumers perceive through
different attributes while consuming SMIs content, thus impacting their consumer buying behaviour (Petty et al., 1983). The studies
related to content attributes remain the prime focus of this review of existing literature.

4.1 Educational Content and Engaging Content
The educational content directly meets the cognitive and analytical aspect of receiving high -quality information, objective product
reviews, and functional usage instructions (Shah et al., 2023) . When an SMI del ivers strong information quality, this satisfies the
need of followers for more knowledge, message credibility, and clarity, this further prompts the consumer action positively (Shah
et al., 2023; Xiao et al., 2018) . Similarly, the engaging content helps  to meet affective needs of the consumers  such as feeling
relaxed, a source of stress buster, entertainment, emotional enjoyment, and aesthetic pleasure (Katz et al., 1973) . This emotional
connection between consumer and influencer is strengthened , that further motivates the consumers to make their buying decisions
without any hesitation (Ki et al., 2020; Shah et al., 2023) . From ELM perspective, the educational information triggers the central
route of persuasion and engaging content drives the positive affective state that supports the message that is being communic ated
by SMI (Petty et al., 1983).

4.2 Content Format, Visual Presentation, and Contextual Captions & Cues
Along with the core message it is really important to understand the content format, visual presentation style, and contextual captions
& cues function primarily as peripheral persuasive stimuli within an ELM framework (Cacioppo et al., 1986; Petty et al., 1983) .
The visual presentation is necessary to enhance the visual appearance, aesthetics, and presentation of content through editin g,
lighting, and polishing to enhance the quality of the SMI’s content  that captures consumers interest and attention (Abidin, 2016;
Munaro et al., 2021). The content format includes the presentation of content in different styles depending on the type of content to
be delivered, sometimes SMIs opt for stories that last fo r 24 hours, reels for showing small videos, carousels for showcasing
combination of images and videos, static post for posting images, live streams & long videos for vlogging, selling products, product
reviews, or product tutorials. This shows that every consumer is different with different choices , this dictates how easily a user can
consume data on social media platforms and thus impacting engagement metrics and conversion rates (Munaro et al., 2021, 2024).
The contextual cues & captions include promotional codes, paid partnership labels, captions including text, hashtags & emojis that
explain the purpose of the content & brand collaborations that streamline the path to purchase (Hogsnes et al., 2024). The literature
gap reveals lack of studies that d epict whether these attributes can significantly impact consumer purchase intention when
controlling for core content quality presented by different influencers such as nano, micro, macro, or mega (Gross & Von
Wangenheim, 2018).

4.3 Generational Dynamics (Millennials and Generation Z)
The way consumers process these central and peripheral cues is heavily shaped by generational socialization and cognitive abilities
(Cacioppo et al., 1986; Chan, 2022) . The studies show that Millennials have established p roper consumer habits in a set pattern,
they value pragmatic utility of social media platforms, and are keen to educational content to verify the information from the online
figures, the social media influencers (Lou & Kim, 2019). In contrast, Generation Z are tech-savvy who are grown in digital era thus
they are more keen to visual aesthetics, entertaining content and have comparatively shorter attention, highly responsive to rapid

4.4 Research Gap
The review of current influencer marketing literature reveals that extensive resea rch has explored the impact of influencer
credibility, followers count, and brand-influencer congruence (De Veirman et al., 2017; Venciute et al., 2023; Xiao et al., 2018) but
there is lack of empirical work incorporating specific influencers’ content at tributes. Most of the existing studies treat influencers
posts as a uniform stimulus rather than breaking it down into distinct attributes such as educational, engaging, content format, visual
presentation, and contextual cues & captions. Furthermore, some  studies addressed generational variations at broad national level
or in metropolitan cities but there is dearth of studies on Tier 2 urban marketing within developing economies like India, specifically
Chandigarh. The city shows great potential as data revealed that Chandigarh has surpassed all metropolitan cities in India and stands
at first position in online buying (Arya Avni, 2025), this acts as evidence that the population is aware of digital marketing, inclined
to online shopping, have many interne t users. Finally, prior literature examined content attributes using bivariate correlations
(Munaro et al., 2021). This ignored the existence of mathematical connection between multiple variables that occur within a standard
consumer environment. This st udy fills these gaps by utilizing a multiple linear regression model alongside a localized
generationally bifurcated independent t-test.

4.5 Research Hypothesis
Based on the established literature and objectives of the study, these specific hypotheses are  formulated to guide the statistical
testing:

H1: SMI’s Educational Content (EDU) has a statistically significant positive effect on consumer purchase intention.

H2: SMI’s Engaging Content (ENG) has a statistically significant positive effect on consumer purchase intention.

H3: SMI’s Content Format (FORM) has a statistically significant positive effect on consumer purchase intention.

H4: SMI’s Visual Presentation (VIS) has a statistically significant positive effect on consumer purchase intention.

H5: SMI’s Contextual Cues & Captions (CUE) has a statistically significant positive effect on consumer purchase intention.

H6: The SMI’s content attributes have an overall statistically significant positive effect on consumer purchase intention (CPI).

H7: A statistically significant difference exists in perceived SMI’s educational content between Millennials and Generation Z.

H8: A statistically significant difference exists in perceived SMI’s engaging content between Millennials and Generation Z.

H9: A statistically significant difference exists in perceived SMI’s content format between Millennials and Generation Z.

H10: A statistically significant difference exists in perceived SMI’s visual presentation of content between Millennials and
Generation Z.

H11: A statistically significant difference exists in perceived SMI’s contextual cues & captions between Millennials and Generation
Z.

H12: A statistically significant difference exists in perceived consumer purchase intention between Millennials and G eneration Z.

5. RESEARCH METHODOLOGY
5.1 Research Design
The study adopts empirical and quantitative research approach. The primary data collection through structured questionnaire h elps
to analyze the impact of SMI’s content attributes on consumer purchase decisions (Ki & Kim, 2019) . A casual and comparative
research framework is systematically applied to meet the objectives of the study. The casual dimension evaluates how the cont ent
created and presented by the social media influencers has potential to persuade consumer perceptions and prompt them to take
actions in accordance with the influencers’ recommendations. While the comparative dimension highlights the variances between
two major digital generational cohorts: Millennials and Gen Z (Chan, 2022; Venciute et al., 2023) . To capture the respondents’
overviews efficiently, a cross -sectional survey design was deployed (Shah et al., 2023) . This approach captures consumer
perceptions, behavioural tendencies, and psychometric orientations at single point in time. Data was simultaneously collected from
both the generations for comparative and overall study of impact of SMI’s content attributes on consumer purchase intention.

5.2 Population and Sample
The target population for the study comprises active social media consumers who regularly track, engage, and follow social me dia
influencers (SMIs). Geographically, the data is collected from the respondents residing in Chandigarh, India. A region that i s
characterized by advanced digital connectivity, high literacy rate, increasing disposable income and maximum digital shoppers
(Arya Avni, 2025; PF Desk, 2025; Rana, 2024).

5.3 Statistical Tests
This section explains the statistical tests which are being used to analyze the Likert scale coded sheet. The detail is given as follows:
5.3.1 Cronbach’s Alpha
Prior to inferential testing, Cronbach’s alpha ( α) coefficients are run acros s individual items of each construct scale to confirm
adequate internal consistency and reliability of test items. The internal consistency helps to understand that multiple items of a same
construct help to understand the same thing. A 0.7 or higher alpha  value is accepted as a proof for good reliability of data. But
according to (Hair et al., 2010)  the threshold 0.7 or high er alpha value is excellent but 0.6 to 0.7 is also acceptable but below 0.6
alpha value is considered poor.

5.3.2 Descriptive Statistics
The measure of central tendency and dispersion include Mean and Standard Deviation that are computed to summarize the overall
sample overviews and evaluate the baseline item behaviour.

5.3.3 Inferential Statistics
A Pearson bivariate correlation matrix is computed to identify the basic linear paths and rule out multicollinearity concerns  (r >
0.7). Following this Multiple Regression Model paired with omnibus ANOVA f-test are calculated to test the causal structure. This
helps to determine the SMI’s content attributes collectively and individually predict consumer purchase intention when evalua ted
simultaneously within a competitive mathematical framework. Finally, an independent sample two -tailed t -test is calculated  to
measure whether the mean baseline evaluations across all the six constructs differ significantly between the two groups: Millennial
and Generation Z.

7. LIMITATIONS OF THE STUDY
A notable limitation of this study that the sample size (N=150) is relatively small and drawn exclusively from Chandigarh, India,
which limits the immediate generalizability of these findings to broader national or other cities demographics in India. The study
also relied on purposive sampling technique for collecting the sample that can introduce subjective bias. The cross-sectional design
provides only a snapshot time and doesn’t accommodate the variation in the circumstances, events or other external factors ov er
time. Another notable limitation of this research is that coefficient of determinati on (𝑅2 = 0.119), that explains that the included
predictors account for only 11.9% of the total variance in consumer purchase intention. This leaves approximately 88.1% of variance
unexplained suggesting the external, unmeasured variables heavily influence consumer choic es. Future research should introduce
additional socio- economic or psychological predictors such as influencer related other attributes, brand awareness, brand loyalty,
and engagement metrics to build a more comprehensive predictive model.

8. CONCLUSION
The study investigates the impact of social media influencers (SMIs) attributes on consumer purchase intention across Millenn ials
and Generation Z in Chandigarh. The study helps to understand different attributes related to content presented by social medi a
influencers on social media platforms. The influencers create content for the audience according to their expertise, knowledg e and
creativity. The audience slowly and steadily consume their content and turn into ardent followers as they tend to admire the content
of social media influencers for their expertise, entertaining & creative content. The presentation style, the captions, emojis, hashtags,
paid partnership labels related to the brand collaborations help in giving a clear picture to the followers regarding the content and
intent of the social media influencers. The followers tend to create para social (one -sided) relationship with the SMIs they follow
and tend to perceive them as role models that persuade them to follow the recommendations & choice s of the social media
influencers thus affecting their consumer purchase intention.

The empirical results show that SMI’s educational content and engaging content serve as primary drivers for persuading consum er
purchase intention. Conversely SMI’s Conten t Format, SMI’s Visual Presentation and SMI’s Contextual Captions & Cues lacked
individual predictive power within the regression model. Additionally the study revealed the cross -generational convergence,
showing that Millennials and Generation Z share uni form baseline behaviours across nearly all measured parameters, except for
some variation in presentation of Engaging Content and Content Format style. The study provides clear strategic direction to social
media influencers, brands, advertising agencies a nd other stakeholders . Rather than  over-allocating capital value to visual
production, aesthetic values or distinct demographic segmentation in influencer marketing campaign . A unified, value -driven
content strategy will effectively influence both generational groups simultaneously. The influencers must focus on their creativity,
expertise and communication style to drive higher conversion rates.
