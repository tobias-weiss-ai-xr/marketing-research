Abstract: This study is conducted to identify and synthesize the key success factors for
implementing a Ubiquitous Marketing (UM) strategy in an increasingly dynamic,
technology-driven, and digitalized competitive environment.  The study applies a
Systematic Bibliometric Literature Review (SBLR) using the SCOPUS database, focusing
on publications in Business, Management, and Accounting from 2020 to 2025. A
stringent screening process employing the keywords “Context” and “Personal ized” as
the core elements of  UM yields a final set of 30 publications for analysis.  The
bibliometric results indicate that, although annual publication volumes fluctuate, the
overall scholarly quality in this area is strong, with 68% of the selected works published
in Q1 journals. The screened literature also demonstrates substantial acad emic
influence, reflected in 394 total citations and an h-index of 13. The thematic synthesis
highlights personalization as the central pillar of UM, increasingly enabled by the
integration of artific ial intelligence, big data, and machine learning to support ultra -
personalization. At the same time, the literature emphasizes ethical and implementation
challenges, particularly those related to data privacy, transparency, and security, which
are essentia l to sustaining consumer trust . This study contributes a structured
theoretical basis for understanding and operationalizing Ubiquitous Marketing (UM),
clarifying the role of context and personalization, and outlining the key opportunities
and constraints that shape successful implementation.

INTRODUCTION
Due to advances in technology and digitalization, competition in the business world is
increasingly dynamic, requiring companies to make rapid and targeted developments to deliver a
superior customer experience. This advancement is shifting the marketing p aradigm moving into an
era where digital channels dominate customer interactions and engagement (Kobets et al., 2024). The
use of technology and digitalization plays a primary role in protecting a brand's competitiveness (Cioppi
et al., 2023).
Given the rapid increase in the use of technology to share information, companies are struggling
to identify the most effective marketing strategies that align with customer behavior (Soni et al., 2020).
The level of competition in an industry significantly influences marketing strategies. Companies need
to differentiate themselves from competitors to secure a favorable position in the market (Duralia,
2022). The integration of various channels allows consumers to interact with the brand seamlessly,
which is essential for optimizing customer experience and improving overall business performance
(Belvedere & Tunisini, 2020). Therefore, to make it easier for customers to interact with brands, the
aspect of omnipresence or ubiquitous is needed in marketing. The concept of ubiquitous marketing is
closely tied to the idea of creating a "ubiquitous network society," where technol ogy seamlessly
integrates into daily life, providing continuous connectivity and interaction. Ubiquitous marketing is

facilitated by the widespread use of mobile devices and wireless networks, which allow for real-time,
location-based, and personalized marketing efforts (Margulis et al., 2020).
Emerging from the convergence of e -commerce, mobile, and context -aware technologies,
ubiquitous marketing is characterized by real -time data integration, AI -driven personalization, and
seamless consumer engagement across multiple platforms and environments. Its growing relevance is
underscored by the digital transformation of industries and the growing demand for value -driven,
customer-centric marketing strategies  (Vazirani & Jaiwant, 2025) . This framework that prioritizes
dynamic, value-oriented, and customer-centric processes is a reflection of the influence of mobile, IoT,
and real-time data on the definition of marketing globally  (Mafuratidze et al., 2025) . In such cases,
Ubiquitous Marketing plays a role in integrating context (time, place, activity), timeline, and source
dimensions that extend beyond spatial targeting to continuous and personalized engagement
(Loussaief et al., 2023).
Ubiquitous marketing is evolving toward a dynamic, context -sensitive, and customer -centric
model, but still lacks a universally accepted definition and an integrative framework. The field is
fragmented, with an urgent need for a unified theoretical model, empirical studies of consumer
acceptance, and an ethical framework for emerging technologies  (Vazirani & Jaiwant, 2025) . This
requires providing a comprehensive and structured overview of this research area by analyzing
patterns, trends, and relationships across a large body of academic literature  (Hallinger & Kovačević,
2023). Therefore, this paper aims to analyze what components are the key to success in implementing
a ubiquitous marketing strategy.
To achieve this goal, this study employs a Systematic Bibliometric Literature Review (SBLR).
SBLR provides a macro -level understanding of a research domain, uncovering trends, gaps, and
influential contributions. By leveraging bibliometric tools, SBLR can rapidly process large datasets,
making it more efficient than traditional  Systematic Literature Review  (SLR) for analyzing broad
research landscapes (Kumar, 2025). SBLR helps researchers, policymakers, and institutions identify key
areas for future research and collaboration opportunities (Khaw et al., 2024).
Based on this background, the main research question of this study is: What are the key
components that contribute to the successful implementation of a ubiquitous marketing strategy?
Research on ubiquitous marketing is necessary because it can eliminate t raditional barriers such as
geographical and cultural differences, creating a seamless and omnipresent market. This expands
opportunities for global reach and market efficiency (McGuigan & Manzerolle, 2015). The findings are
synthesized based on sub -themes and their respective contributions to the development of a
ubiquitous marketing strategy.
RESEARCH METHOD
In analyzing the components that are key to success in implementing a ubiquitous marketing
strategy, a Systematic Bibliometric Literature Review (SBLR) is used. A systematic bibliometric
literature review (SBLR) is a method that combines systematic review techniques with bibliometric
analysis to provide a comprehensive overview of research patterns, trends, and gaps in a specific field
(Hallinger & Kovačević, 2023) . This method provides a broad view of the research landscape,
identifying key areas of focus and emerging trends (Passas, 2024).

An integrated literature review is carried out to synthesize existing literature in order to produce
new knowledge and insights about a particular topic. The process involves meticulous searching,
identifying, and selecting relevant studies from various da tabases and sources (Rao & Moon, 2021) .
Establishing clear criteria for including and excluding studies is crucial to maintain the integrity of the
review (Lubbe et al., 2020). This study used the PRISMA framework, which provides a clear reporting
framework for how literature studies are conducted, from the initial search and selection process to
the final results of the included studies  (Fitri et al., 2025) . By using PRISMA, researchers can ensure
that the article selection process is conducted objectively and can be traced by readers. There are four
main stages in this framework: Identification, which describes the total number of articles found from
various databases; Screening, which describes articles that have gone through the process of removing
duplication and filtering; Eligibility, which describes articles that have been read in full and assessed
for their suitability to the research criteria; and Inclusion, which describes the final articles used in the
analysis (Xie et al., 2025). The following is the PRISMA flow diagram used in this study and is shown
in Figure 1.

The primary data source for this review is the Scopus database, selected for its comprehensive
coverage of high-quality, peer-reviewed scholarly literature in the fields of business and management
as seen on Figure 1. The identification phase began with a broad search using the keyword "Ubiquitous
Marketing", which initially yielded 1,131 records. No duplicate records were identified as the search
was restricted to a single primary database. The screening phase involved a two-step inclusion process
to ensure the relevance and currency of the literature. At the eligibility stage, articles that pass the
screening are further examined based on their content's suitability to the research objectives. This
stage is necessary to ensure that each selected article contributes to the discussion of ubiquitous
marketing. The search results were narrowed to the subject area of "Business, Management, and
Accounting", resulting in 316 publications. To focus on the most recent technological advancements
and digital trends, a  temporal filter was applied to include only works published between 2020 and
2025. This stage narrowed the selection to 116 documents. For the eligibility phase, all 116 remaining
reports were sought for retrieval and full -text assessment. A more stringen t manual screening was
conducted by analyzing titles and abstracts using the specific keywords "Context" and "Personalized",
which represent the core pillars of Ubiquitous Marketing (UM). During this process, 86 reports were
excluded. The primary reason fo r exclusion was that the studies did not explicitly address the
integration of contextual data or real-time personalization as strategic success factors, focusing instead
on purely technical or unrelated computational aspects of ubiquitous systems.
In the Bibliometric Systematic Literature Review at Table 2, the process began with a broad
meta-search using the basic keyword 'Marketing', resulting in a total of 398,192 publications. The first
inclusion criterion significantly reduced the number of pub lications to 1,131. The second inclusion
criterion was filtered by limiting the subject to 'Business' to 316 publications and then filtered again
by limiting 'in the period 2020 -2025' to 116 publications. Finally, a final screening stage or inclusion
stage was conducted to identify the most specific articles. By maintaining the subject and period
restrictions, and adding the exact keywords 'Context' and 'Personalized' which are the core of
Ubiquitous Marketing, the final number of sources ready for analysis was 30 publications. The
publication consists of 26 articles, 3 book chapters, and 1 review.

DISCUSSION
Introduction to Ubiquitous Marketing
Ubiquitous marketing can be a relevant strategic approach that is adaptive, personalized, and
contextual. This strategy can include integrating customer data from various touchpoints to deliver
personalized experiences based on location and time (Kannan & Li, 2017). With this data integration,
companies are able to deeply understand customer needs and translate them into relevant
developments, optimizing the purchasing process, and tailoring brand communications (Lemon &
Verhoef, 2016).

Ubiquitous marketing is an innovation that is revolutionizing the way companies connect and
interact with consumers by leveraging customer purchase and preference data (Margulis et al., 2020).
Ubiquitous marketing leverages real-time, place-based data to create a seamless and efficient system
(Li et al., 2023). This approach leverages companies' competencies in delivering messages that focus
on understanding their customers and developing strategies that can improve customer effectiveness
(Huang & Liu, 2020).
Ubiquitous technology enables customers to connect with companies anywhere and anytime,
further facilitating the integration of customer experiences across all channels, both online and in -
store (Margulis et al., 2020). Companies need to develop shopping environments across channels and
touchpoints to manage a seamless customer shopping experience and influence consumer behavioral
responses. Ubiquitous marketing is a marketing activity that allows consumers to consistently connect
with companies from their personal devices (Hawary & Obiadat, 2021). With this approach, ubiquitous
technology can automate data processing, allowing for controlled consumer decision -making
(Darmody & Zwick, 2020). Therefore, this ubiquitous approach requires decisions regarding potential
consumer perspectives and market research (Oe & Nguyễn, 2021).
The Role of Personalization in Ubiquitous Marketing
Personalization in marketing involves tailoring messages, products, or services to individual
customer preferences and behaviors, leveraging data such as past behaviors, demographic
characteristics, and online activities (Kushnarevych & Kollárová, 2023) . Ubiquity contains messages
that focus on competence and have strategies to increase persuasion (Huang & Liu, 2020) . This
approach aims to enhance brand loyalty, consumer satisfaction, and profitability by providing more
engaging and relevant experiences (Bozkurt et al., 2024).
Personalization leads to more meaningful interactions with customers, increasing engagement
and satisfaction then it can significantly boost conversion rates and overall marketing performance
(Bozkurt et al., 2024 ; Al-Ahmed et al., 2025 ). By reducing cognitive load and decision fatigue,
personalized content helps customers make choices more easily (Chandra et al., 2022) . Personalized
marketing strategies have been shown to improve short -term goals like email open rates and
conversion rates, as well as long-term goals such as customer loyalty and lifetime value.
In its implementation, several things are considered regarding the functional and effective
capabilities of the system, the possibility of interactivity to receive user input, and personalized
communication to be tailored to user preferences (Sun, 2020) . Companies' initiatives to use this
ubiquitous approach are undertaken to make them more agile and responsive to the ever -changing
nature and behavior of potential consumers (Chatterjee et al., 2020). Based on research conducted by
Anwar et al. (2021), the concept of ubiquity is the foundation in shaping customer perceptions and can
influence customer decisions to use the services provided.
Personalization is a cornerstone of holistic marketing because it enhances the customer
experience, drives engagement, and improves business outcomes by tailoring interactions to individual
preferences and context. Personalization reduces cognitive load by  presenting customers with
relevant options, saving time and effort in decision -making (Chandra et al., 2022) . Personalization
improves short-term metrics like email open rates and conversion rates, while also driving long -term
goals like customer loyalty and customer lifetime value (Tran et al., 2024) . Personalization enables

Trends and Innovations
In today's digital context, consumers demand that brands are constantly changing, where brands
must be able to adapt to every moment in their lives (Pérez del Castillo et al., 2020) . Ubiquitous
computing embeds computational intelligence into everyday environments, facilitating seamless,
context-sensitive interactions. Technologies such as location and activity sensing, wearable
technology, GPS, accelerometers, and RFID enable real -time, personalized services based on user
context (Safa et al., 2025 ; Alexiou & Kameas, 2025 ). These systems support seamless information
sharing and collaboration between devices, enhancing user experience (Muhlbauer et al., 2016).
Digital personal assistants have become more advanced, providing proactive and effective
support for users' activities in an unobtrusive manner. These assistants leverage AI to offer high levels
of personalization (Guinea et al., 2019).  The convergence of AI and big data technologies has enabled
next-generation marketing strategies that deliver highly personalized content experiences. This
integration allows for the analysis of vast amounts of data to tailor marketing efforts to indivi dual
preferences and behaviors (Setor, 2021) . AI -driven predictive analytics forecast trends and guide
strategic decisions, allowing for more precise targeting and ultra-personalization of marketing efforts
(Batta et al., 2025). The integration of artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning has revolutionized
personalization, enabling more precise and effective marketing techniques (Kushnarevych & Kollárová,
2023; Garg et al., 2024).
Advances in information and communication technologies have led to the emergence of
ubiquitous marketing, where shopping environments are increasingly seamlessly integrated across
multiple marketing channels to deliver a cohesive customer experience (Tarabasz, 2025). The evolution
of wireless communication technologies, including 5G and the future 6G, is expected to enhance
marketing capabilities through ubiquitous connectivity and integration of AI with communication
functionalities (Singh et al., 2025).
Technologies such as artificial intelligence (AI), big data, and mobile connectivity are
fundamentally changing the way companies interact with customers by enabling personalization,
predictive insights, and increased engagement. AI -based tools like machin e learning and natural
language processing enable businesses to deliver highly tailored experiences, increasing customer
satisfaction and conversion rates (Wijethilak et al., 2024) . Companies can analyze large volumes of
data to understand customer preferences, consumption habits, and behavioral patterns, enabling more
effective marketing strategies (Singh et al., 2025) . Mobile apps and e -commerce platforms provide
customers with 24/7 access to services, fostering ongoing engagement. Features like personalization,
active control, and responsiveness in mobile apps significantly increase customer engagement and
loyalty (Alalwan et al., 2020).

The pervasive nature of ubiquitous marketing raises significant privacy concerns. The continuous

Consumers often feel anxious about the lack of transparency regarding how their personal

reluctance to engage with certain marketing practices (Beke et al., 2018) . Firms can adopt proactive
privacy practices to address consumer concerns. Privacy concerns can also drive innovation, leading
to the development of new privacy -preserving technologies and practices that can provide a
competitive advantage (Bleier et al., 2020).
Strategic Implications for Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs)
Ubiquitous marketing can significantly enhance the competitive advantage and agility of small
and medium enterprises (SMEs). SMEs leveraging digital marketing tools can achieve a competitive
edge by focusing on customer preferences and delivering personalized experiences. Digital marketing
reduces costs, increases dynamic capabilities, and allows SMEs to adapt to changing market demands
effectively (Muis et al., 2024). SMEs' flexibility and proximity to customers enable them to implement

This differentiation strategy helps SMEs remain competitive in markets with high var iability and
demand for unique products . SMEs that adopt digital tools and context -aware personalization can
quickly adapt their resources and processes to meet evolving customer demands. This agility is
essential for maintaining competitiveness in dynamic markets (Fjellström et al., 2020). The integration
of artificial intelligence (AI) with strategic agility enhances SMEs' ability to identify market trends,
forecast customer needs, and redeploy resources effectively. This capability supports product
innovation and brand upgrading, further strengthening their market position (Guo et al., 2025).
Small and medium -sized enterprises (SMEs) can leverage affordable AI and machine learning
(ML) strategies to implement real-time personalized marketing despite resource constraints. SMEs can
adopt lightweight machine learning models, such as K-Means clustering and decision trees, combined
with open -source platforms and cloud -based infrastructures. These tools enable precise customer
segmentation and personalized marketing without requiring extensive computational resources or

marketing workflows to analyze customer preferences and predict behavior. These tools are cost -

Data transparency and proactive privacy practices influence consumer trust in ubiquitous
marketing environment. Transparent data practices significantly enhance consumer trust. Perceived
transparency strengthens this relationship, as consumers value clear communication about how their
data is collected, stored, and used. Transparency fosters moral accountability, which positively impacts
trust, satisfaction, and engagement in online environments (Hassan El-Annan & Hassoun, 2024; Wang
et al., 2025). Transparency complements personalization efforts, with both acting as pillars for building
trust. Consumers are more likely to trust businesses that openly disclose their data practices while
offering personalized experiences (Wang et al., 2025) . Proactive privacy measures, such as data
protection and ethical data management, are critical for building trust. These practices mitigate privacy
concerns and enhance consumer confidence in sharing personal data. Implementing stratified privacy
strategies tailored to different consumer needs can further optimize trust and purchase intentions
(Hassan El-Annan & Hassoun, 2024 ; (Algasawneh et al., 2025) ). Adhering to privacy regulations like
GDPR and CCPA, while costly for small businesses, is a highly effective way to build long -term
consumer trust and loyalty. Small businesses face significant financial challenges in implementing
robust privacy practices and complying with regulations, but these efforts are essential for maintaining
trust (Mollay et al., 2026) . Overly specific personalization can lead to discomfort and reduced trust,
highlighting the need for a balanced approach that respects consumer privacy (Rahayu et al., 2025).
In terms of personalization on ubiquitous marketing, SMEs can deliver highly customized
content, recommendations, and offers that resonate deeply with individual customers. This approach
significantly boosts engagement and conversion rates compared to generic mass marketing strategies
(Chakranarayan, 2025). By leveraging real -time data, SMEs can dynamically adjust their marketing
efforts to meet customer needs at the moment, further enhancing the likelihood of conversion (Valdez
Mendia & Flores -Cuautle, 2022). Localization strategies, such as multilingual websites and region -
specific campaigns, have been shown to increase conversion rates for SMEs by making their offerings
more accessible and relevant to local audiences (Chhibber & Chadha, 2020) . Personalization fosters
loyalty by creating meaningful, individualized interactions. Satisfied customers are more likely to
return, increasing their lifetime value. AI -powered predictive models help SMEs anticipate customer
needs, enabling proactive engagement and personalized offers that drive repeat purchases and long -
term relationships (Al-Ahmed et al., 2025). By analyzing customer behavior and preferences, SMEs can
refine their strategies to maximize both immediate and long-term revenue opportunities.

This study aims to analyze the key components of success in implementing a Ubiquitous
Marketing strategy through a Systematic Bibliometric Literature Review (SBLR) approach to 30
publications screened from 2020 to 2025. A key component of Ubiquitous Market ing success is the
ability to perform deep personalization. Successful Ubiquitous Marketing is able to tailor messages,
products, and services to individual consumer preferences. This personality is proven to increase
engagement, customer satisfaction, con version rates, and long -term loyalty. Ubiquitous Marketing
achievements are driven by technological innovation. The integration of artificial intelligence (AI),
