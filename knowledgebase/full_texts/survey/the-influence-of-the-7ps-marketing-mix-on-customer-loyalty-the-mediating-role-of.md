ABSTRACT: The restaurant industry has become increasingly competitive, making it
essential for businesses to understand the marketing factors that influence customers’ perceived
value and loyalty. This study examined the influence of the proposed 7Ps marketing mix o n
perceived value and customer loyalty in the Maldivian restaurant industry, with perceived value
serving as a mediating variable. A quantitative research design was employed, and data were
collected from 404 customers in the Maldives using a str uctured questionnaire. The data were
analysed using descriptive statistics, confirmatory factor analysis (CFA), and structural
equation modelling (SEM) with AMOS. The findings revealed that physical evidence and
people had significant positive effects on p erceived value, whereas product, price, and place
did not significantly influence perceived value. Perceived value also had a significant positive
effect on customer loyalty. Furthermore, perceived value significantly mediated the
relationships between phy sical evidence and customer loyalty and between people and
customer loyalty, whereas no significant mediating effects were found for product, price, or
place. During the measurement model assessment, the promotion and process constructs were
excluded becau se they did not meet the required psychometric criteria; therefore, the final
model comprised the retained marketing mix dimensions. This study contributes to the service
marketing literature by providing empirical evidence from the Maldivian restaurant in dustry
on the relationships among the retained marketing mix dimensions, perceived value, and
customer loyalty. The findings also offer practical implications for restaurant managers by
highlighting the importance of the physical service environment and employees’ role in service
delivery in enhancing customers’ perceived value and strengthening customer loyalty.

RESEARCH BACKGROUND
The 7Ps marketing mix, comprising product, price, place, promotion, people, process, and physical evidence, is one of the wid ely
adopted frameworks in service marketing. Originally developed from McCarthy's (1960) traditional marketing mix and later
extended by Booms and Bitner (1981), the framework has been widely applied to understand customer behaviour, create customer
value, and enhance organisational performance (Kotler & Armstrong, 2021). Previous studies have shown that the marketing mix
dimensions can influence customer outcomes, including perceived value, satisfaction, behavioural intentions, and customer loyalty
across various service industries (Alafeef, 2020; Elgarhy & Mohamed, 2023; Lee, 2023; Mohamed, 2024).
In the restaurant industry, the 7Ps marketing mix framework has increasingly been applied to understand and improve customer
experiences and strengthen customer loyalty. Previous studies have primarily examined the direct relationships between market ing
mix dimensions and customer satisfaction or loyalty, while more recent research has also explored the role of perceived value i n
shaping customer behavioural outcomes (Az -zahra et al., 2024; Fajrin & Aprianingsih, 2024; Lee, 2023). However, empirical
evidence examining perceived value as a mediator between the marketing mix dimensions and customer loyalty remains limited,
particularly within the restaurant sector. This indicates a need for further empirical investigation into how individual marketing mix
dimensions contribute to customer loyalty through customers' perceptions of value.

Problem Statement
Although numerous studies have examined the influence of the 7Ps marketing mix on customer outcomes across various service
industries (Alafeef, 2020; Elgarhy & Mohamed, 2023; Alwinie et al., 2024), most have focused on sectors such as banking, tourism,
hospitality, healthcare, and beauty services. Moreover, previous studies have primarily investigated the direct relationships between
the 7Ps marketing mix and customer outcomes, including customer satisfaction, behavioural intentions, and customer loyalty, w ith
limited attention to the mechanisms through which these relationships occur.
Recent research has increasingly examined mediating variables, particularly customer satisfaction, in explaining the relation ship
between marketing activities and customer loyalty (Elgarhy & Mohamed, 2023; Mohamed, 2024; Fajrin & Aprianingsih, 2024).
However, the mediating role of perceived value remains underexplored, despite evidence suggesting that it is a key determinant of
customer loyalty (Zeithaml et al., 2020; Az-zahra et al., 2024). Furthermore, many studies have treated the 7Ps marketing mix as a
single construct rather than examining the individual effects of its seven dimensions on perceived value and customer loyalty  (Lee,
2023).
The Maldivian restaurant industry has experienced substantial growth due to urbanisation, tourism development, changing consumer
lifestyles, and increasing demand for convenient dining options (Razzan et al., 2024). Despite this growth, empirical researc h
examining the relationships among the proposed 7Ps marketing mix dimensions, perceived value, and customer loyalty in this
context remains limited. In particular, limited evidence is available on how individual marketing mix dimensions may influenc e
customer loyalty through perceived value among restaurant customers in the Maldives.
Therefore, this study examines the proposed 7Ps marketing mix framework in relation to perceived value and customer loyalty, with
perceived value serving as a mediating variable in the Maldivian restaurant industry. By addressing these gaps, the study contributes
to the service marketing literature by providing empirical evidence from an underexplored context and advancing understanding  of
how different marketing mix dimensions influence customer loyalty through perceived value. The study initially considers all seven
marketing mix dimensions; however, the final empirical model includes only those dimensions that satisfy the required psychometric
criteria.
1.4 Research Objectives
To address the identified research gaps, the following research objectives are formulated:
1. To examine the effect of the seven dimensions of the proposed 7Ps marketing mix framework, namely product, price, place,
promotion, people, process, and physical evidence, on perceived value in the Maldivian restaurant industry.
2. To examine the effect of perceived value on customer loyalty in the Maldivian restaurant industry.
3. To examine the mediating effect of perceived value on the relationship between the seven dimensions of the proposed 7Ps
marketing mix framework and customer loyalty in the Maldivian restaurant industry.
Review of Key Concepts
The 7Ps marketing mix is one of the most widely adopted frameworks for service marketing. Originally developed from McCarthy's
(1960) traditional marketing mix of product, price, place, and promotion, Booms and Bitner (1981) extended the framework by
adding people, process, and physical evidence to better reflect the characteristics of service industries. Kotler and Armstrong (2021)
define the marketing mix as a set of controllable marketing tools used by organisations to create customer value and achieve
organisational objectives. In this study, the 7Ps marketing mix refers to the seven service marketing dimensions that influenc e
customers' perceptions and behaviours in the Maldivian restaurant industry.
Product refers to the quality, variety, and freshness of food and beverages offered by restaurants. Price refers to customers '
perceptions of the fairness and value of menu prices. Place relates to the accessibility, convenience, and location of the re staurant.
Promotion comprises communication activities, including advertising, sales promotions, digital marketing, and social media. People
refers to employees whose professionalism, responsiveness, and interpersonal skills influence customers' service experi ences.
Process relates to the efficiency and consistency of service delivery, while physical evidence refers to the tangible aspects  of the
restaurant environment, including cleanliness, ambience, interior design, and equipment.
Perceived value is defined as customers' overall evaluation of the benefits received relative to the monetary and non-monetary costs
incurred when purchasing a product or service (Zeithaml, 1988). Woodruff (1997) emphasised customers' evaluations of produc t
attributes and consumption experiences, while Sweeney and Soutar (2001) conceptualised perceived value as a multidimensional
construct. More recent studies recognise perceived value as an important determinant of customer behavioural intentions and loyalty

Customer loyalty refers to customers' commitment to continue purchasing from and recommending a preferred organisation. Dick

Review of Related Theories
This study is underpinned by three theoretical perspectives: the 7Ps Marketing Mix Framework, the concept of perceived value, and
the attitudinal and behavioural perspectives of customer loyalty.
The 7Ps Marketing Mix Framework originated from McCarthy's (1960) traditional marketing mix, which consisted of four elements:
product, price, place, and promotion. Booms and Bitner (1981) extended the framework by adding people, process, and physical
evidence to better reflect the characteristics of service industries. The 7Ps marketing mix has since become one of the most wide ly
adopted frameworks for explaining how service organisations develop marketing strategies to influence customer perceptions,
perceived value, and loyalty (Kotler & Armstrong, 2021). Given that restaurants operate within a service environment, the
framework provides a suitable foundation for examining how different marketing mix dimensions influence customer loyalty.
The second theoretical foundation is the conceptualisation  of perceived value, proposed by Zeithaml (1988), which suggests that
customers evaluate products and services by comparing the benefits they receive with the monetary and non -monetary sacrifices
they incur. Woodruff (1997) further emphasised customers' ev aluations of product attributes and consumption experiences, while
Sweeney and Soutar (2001) conceptualised perceived value as a multidimensional construct. Perceived value has subsequently been
recognised as an important determinant of customer behavioura l intentions and customer loyalty (Zeithaml et al., 2020). In this
study, perceived value is expected to mediate the relationship between the proposed 7Ps marketing mix dimensions and customer
loyalty.
The study is also guided by the attitudinal and behavioural perspectives of customer loyalty. Dick and Basu (1994) proposed t hat
customer loyalty results from the interaction between customers' relative attitudes and repeat patronage behaviour. Oliver (1 999)
later defined customer loyalty as a strong commitment to repurchase or continue patronising a preferred product or service de spite
competing alternatives. In the restaurant context, customer loyalty is reflected through repeat patronage, positive word -of-mouth,
and continued preference for a restaurant.
Collectively, these theoretical and conceptual perspectives provide the theoretical foundation for this study. The 7Ps Marketing Mix
Framework explains how marketing strategies influence customer evaluations, the concept of perceived value explains how
customers assess service value, and the attitudinal and behavioural perspectives of customer loyalty explain how favourable
evaluations are associated with long -term loyalty. Together, these perspectives support the proposed relationships among the 7Ps
marketing mix, perceived value, and customer loyalty in the Maldivian restaurant industry.

Several studies have also examined the relationship between the 7Ps marketing mix and perceived value. For example, Lee (2023 )

et al. (2020) and Az-zahra et al. (2024) highlighted the importance of customer value in enhancing customer loyalty.

as a mediating variable (Elgarhy & Mohamed, 2023; Mohamed, 2024). Limited attention has been given to the mediating role of
perceived value, particularly in the restaurant industry. Furthermore, existing studies have largely been conducted in banking,
tourism, healthcare, beauty services, and retail settings, with little empirical evidence from the Maldivian context.
Therefore, this study seeks to address these gaps by examining the influence of the seven dimensions of the proposed 7Ps marketing
mix framework on customer loyalty through the mediating role of perceived value in the Maldivian restaurant industry.

The results show that product has a positive but non-significant effect on perceived value, indicating that food quality, menu variety,
and product attributes do not significantly enhance customers' value perceptions in the Maldivian restaurant industry. T his finding
is inconsistent with previous studies that reported significant positive relationships between product attributes and perceiv ed value
(Baig et al., 2020; Lee, 2023; Mohamed, 2024). Similarly, price demonstrates a positive but non -significant effect on perceived
value, contradicting earlier studies that identified price as a significant determinant of perceived value (Alafeef, 2020; Ba ig et al.,
2020). Place also exhibits a negative but non-significant effect, suggesting that restaurant accessibility and location convenience do
not substantially influence customers' perceptions of value. This finding differs from previous research reporting significant positive
relationships between accessibility and perceived value (Fajrin & Aprianingsih, 2024; Mohamed, 2024). Consequently, H1, H2, and
H3 were not supported.
In contrast, physical evidence has a positive and significant effect on perceived value, indicating that restaurant cleanline ss,
ambience, décor, and overall appearance play an important role in customers' value evaluations. Likewise, people significantl y
influence perceived value, highlighting the importance of employee professionalism, responsiveness, and service quality in shaping
customer perceptions. These findings are consistent with previous studies emphasising the importance of the service environme nt
and employee interactions in enhancing perceived value (Lee, 2023; Elgarhy & Mohamed, 2023; Alwinie et al., 2024). Therefore,
H5 and H7 were supported. H4 and H6 were not tested in the final structural model because the promotion and process construct s
were removed during the measurement model assessment.
The results further reveal that perceived value has a significant positive effect on customer loyalty, indicating that custom ers who
perceive greater value are more likely to revisit restaurants, continue purchasing their products and services, and recomme nd them
to others. This finding supports previous research identifying perceived value as an important determinant of customer loyalty (Baig
et al., 2020; Az-zahra et al., 2024). Accordingly, H8 was supported.
With respect to the mediating effects, perceived value significantly mediates the relationships between physical evidence and
customer loyalty and between people and customer loyalty, supporting H13 and H14. However, perceived value does not
significantly mediate the relationships between product, price, and place and customer loyalty. Consequently, H9, H10, and H11
were not supported. H12 and H15 were not tested because promotion and process, respectively, were removed during the
measurement model assessment.
Overall, the findings demonstrate that physical evidence ( β = 0.505) and people ( β = 0.468) had the largest significant effects on
perceived value among the retained marketing mix dimensions. These results suggest that the physical restaurant environment a nd
employee-related aspects of service delivery may be particularly relevant to customers' perceived value and, through perceived
value, their loyalty in the Maldivian restaurant industry.

6. CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS
6.1 Conclusion
This study examined the influence of the retained dimensions of the 7Ps marketing mix on customer loyalty, with perceived val ue
serving as a mediating variable in the Maldivian restaurant industry. Following the confirmatory factor analysis, the promotion and
process constructs were excluded from the structural model because they did not satisfy the required psychometric criteria.

Consequently, the study focused on the effects of product, price, place, physical evidence, and people on perceived value and
customer loyalty. The findings revealed that physical evidence and people had significant positive effects on perceived value ,
highlighting the importance of the restaurant environment, cleanliness, ambience, and employee interactions in enhancing
customers' value perceptions. In contrast, product, price, and place did not significantly influence perceived value. Further more,
perceived value had a significant positive effect on customer loyalty, indicating that customers who perceive greater value are more
likely to revisit the restaurant, continue purchasing its products and services, and recommend it to others. The mediation an alysis
further demonstrated that perceived value significantly mediated the relationships between physical evidence and customer loy alty
and between people and customer loyalty, but not the relationships involving product, price, and place.
6.2 Recommendations
Based on the findings, restaurant managers should prioritise investments in physical evidence and employee -related factors to
enhance customers' perceived value and strengthen customer loyalty. Managers should ensure that restaurants maintain high
standards of cleanliness, attractive décor, comfortable dining areas, and a pleasant ambience. Creating a positive physical
environment can significantly improve customers' perceptions of value. In addition, restaurants should focus on recruiting, training,
and re taining employees who demonstrate professionalism, responsiveness, and customer -oriented behaviour. Continuous staff
training programmes should be implemented to enhance service quality and customer interactions. Although product, price, and
place did not significantly influence perceived value in this study, restaurant managers should continue to maintain appropriate
standards in these areas to meet customer expectations and remain competitive.
6.3 Theoretical and Managerial Implications
This study extends the application of the 7Ps marketing mix framework within the Maldivian restaurant industry by demonstrati ng
that only physical evidence and people significantly influence perceived value. The findings indicate that not all marketing mix
dimensions contribute equally to customers' value perceptions. Furthermore, the study confirms the significant positive effec t of
perceived value on customer loyalty and its mediating role in the relationships between physical evidence and customer loyalty and
between people and customer loyalty. These findings provide empirical support for the role of perceived value in the proposed
marketing framework and enhance understanding of how service marketing dimensions influence customer loyalty in the restaurant
industry.
From a managerial perspective, restaurant managers should prioritise investments in physical evidence by maintaining clean,
attractive, and comfortable dining environments, while also focusing on employee recruitment, training, and service quality.
Enhancing these aspects is likely to improve customers' perceived value and strengthen long -term customer loyalty. Although
product, price, and place were not significant predictors of perceived value, managers should continue to maintain appropriat e
standards in these areas to meet customer expectations and remain competitive.
6.4 Limitations and Future Research Directions
This study has several limitations. First, its focus on the Maldivian restaurant industry may limit generalisability to other  contexts.
Second, the cross-sectional design limits causal inferences and assessment of changes over time. Third, self -reported data may be
subject to common method and social desirability bias. Finally, promotion and process were excluded from the final structural model
due to psychometric criteria.
Future research should examine the model across different industries and contexts, use longitudinal designs, and further validate the
promotion and process measures. Future studies could also examine additional variables such as customer satisfaction, serv ice
quality, trust, and brand image to provide a broader understanding of customer loyalty.
