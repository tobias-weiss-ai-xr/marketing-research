Abstract: The gap between the rapid growth of Islamic banking and the public's
decision to save is still relatively low, even though the majority of the population
is Muslim. This study aims to analyse the influence of knowledge, income,
religiosity, service qualit y, and trust on customers' decisions to save at Bank
Syariah Indonesia Merangin Branch. Using a quantitative approach with a survey
method involving 175 respondents and PLS -SEM, the results show that
religiosity has a positive and significant effect on sav ing decisions, while
knowledge, income, service quality, and trust do not have a signifi cant impact.
This research contributes to the development of literature on the behaviour of
Islamic banking customers by providing empirical evidence that religiosity plays
a more decisive role in saving decisions that have certain social and religious
characteristics. Practically, it's important to strengthen marketing and education
strategies that integrate Sharia and religious values, as well as community
engagement programmes to encourage people to save in Islamic banks.
Keywords: Religiosity; Savings Decisions; Service Quality; Trust

Existing literature on the factors influencing customer decisions to use and save with
Sharia banks presents inconsistent findings. First, knowledge is a cognitive factor that can
theoretically drive the decision to save; understanding Sharia banking principles, products,
mechanisms, and benefits can reduce customer uncertainty when making choices. However,
empirical evidence regarding the impact of knowledge remains mixed.
Research indicates that knowledge has a positive and significant influence on the
decision to save with Islamic ban ks; this demonstrates that the better the public's
understanding of Islamic banking, the greater their inclination to save.3 These findings align
with research highlighting the importance of understanding Islamic banking when deciding
to become a customer . Conversely, other studies indicate that knowledge or literacy does
not always directly translate into a decision to use Sharia banking services. 4 This
discrepancy indicates that possessing knowledge alone is not necessarily sufficient to drive
actual deci sions, as customers may simultaneously weigh economic, religious, se rvice-
related, and trust -based factors. Consequently, these inconsistent findings highlight the
need to re -examine the role of knowledge in explaining saving decisions within different
contexts.
Secondly, income relates to an individual's economic ability to set aside a portion of
their earnings as savings. Logically, the higher a person's income, the greater their capacity
to save. A number of studies support this argument. A study involvi ng the community of
Bungo Regency, Jambi, specifically examined the influence of income, religiosity, and
financial literacy on the decision to save with Sharia banks . Research finding that income
level has a positive and significant influence on customers ' decisions to choose savings
products at BSI in the Pandaan District area. Consistent results also indicate that income
level is a factor significantly influencing customers' decisions to save at BSI KCP Pandaan .5
However, the relationship between income and the decision to save is not always consistent.
Some studi es indicate that the decision to use Sharia banking services can arise across
demographic groups with varying economic capacities, as the decision is also influenced by
non-economic factors such as religiosity, perceptions of product benefits, trust, and service
quality. Therefore, the role of income warrants re -examination to determine whether
economic capacity is truly a determinant of the saving decision among customers of the BSI
Merangin Branch.

Thus, theoretically, i ncome plays two potential roles in the decision to save. First,
income enhances an individual's objective capacity to set aside funds. Second, the decision
to allocate that surplus to an Islamic bank still depends on how the individual evaluates the
available options. In other words, rising income creates the capacity to save but does not
automatically determine where and how those funds will be deposited.
Knowledge and income are essentially distinct yet interconnected aspects in
explaining saving decisions. Knowledge provides the cognitive foundation for individuals to
determine how financial sur pluses are allocated, whereas income provides the economic
capacity to carry out such allocation. A customer with a certain level of income will not
necessarily choose an Islamic savings product if they do not understand the characteristics
of the contract akad, benefits, mechanisms, and suitability to their needs. Conversely, sound
knowledge of Islamic banking products and contracts does not necessarily lead to a s aving
decision if the individual lacks an adequate financial surplus.
This relationship can be explained by integrating Consumer Decision-Making Process
Theory with the Keynesian Consumption Function. In the consumer decision -making
process, knowledge serv es as a source of information that helps individuals evaluate
alternatives and make choices. Meanwhile, income determines the economic scope
available after consumption needs have been met. Consequently, when an individual
possesses a good understanding of Islamic bank products and contracts alongside a surplus
income, that information can be used  to evaluate and allocate a portion of the financial
surplus to a savings product deemed suitable. Conversely, if knowledge regarding Islamic
products is limited, the income surplus may not necessarily be directed toward an Islamic
bank, as the individual might not grasp the benefits or characteristics of the available
products.
Thus, the decision to save with a sharia bank can be understood as the outcome of an
interaction between the cognitive ability to comprehend and evaluate financial options and
the e conomic capacity to provide funds for savings. This perspective also explains the
varying findings of previous studies regarding knowledge and income. High levels of

Methods
This study employs a quantitative approach with a survey design to empirically
examine the influence of k nowledge, income, religiosity, service quality, and trust on the
decision to save with a Sharia bank. The research was conducted at the Merangin Branch of
BSI in Merangin Regency, Jambi. The location was selected purposively because the BSI
Merangin Branch is a Sharia banking unit serving the community in Merangin Regency and
possesses a customer profile relevant to the study's objectives. Furthermore, the location
was chosen because the growth trend of the BSI Merangin Branch's customer base showed
signs o f slowing down during the study period, making it a  relevant setting to examine
factors that theoretically influence public decisions regarding the use of and saving with
Sharia banks. Thus, the BSI Merangin Branch is considered contextually representative  for
testing the research model, as it allows for t he examination of relationships among
individual factors, religiosity, service quality, and trust within the context of regional Sharia
banking savings decisions.
The study population consists of individuals who are customers of the BSI Merangin
Branch and meet the research criteria. Sampling was conducted using purposive sampling,
a method involving the selection of respondents based on specific characteristics aligned
with the research objectives. The respondent criteria include: first, residing in Merang in
Regency; second, being at least 17 years old or having an income; and third, being a BSI
customer with experience using Sharia banking products or services . These criteria were
established to ensure res pondents possessed sufficient experience and knowle dge to
evaluate the variables under study. A total of 175 respondents participated in the study.
This sample size is considered adequate for PLS -SEM analysis, as it meets the minimum
sample requirements based on the number of predictor constructs in the st ructural model
and provides a sufficient number of observations for model estimation and the testing of
relationships between variables. Respondent characteristics , including gender, age,
education, occupation, and income level, are presented descriptively in the results section,

The Influence Of Income On Saving Decisions
Income does not have a significant effect on customers' decisions to save (β = 0.111;
p = 0.188). This finding indicates that income level is not the primary factor  determining a
customer's choice to save at the BSI Merangin Branch. While consumer behavior theory
acknowledges that income determines a consumer's economic capacity, consumption
decisions and product choices are not based solely on financial means; prefe rences, needs,
experiences, values, and psychological considerations also shape consumer decisions.
In the  context of this study, income likely plays a greater role in determining the
capacity to save rather than the choice of a Sharia bank as the savings institution. Customers
with varying income levels may reach the same decision when other factors —such as
adherence to Sharia principles, transaction needs, financial goals, and personal
preferences—dominate the decision -making process. Thus, the lack of st atistical
significance regarding income does not imply that economic conditions are unimportant;
rather, i t indicates that the magnitude of income is not the primary differentiator in the
selection of a Sharia bank.
These findings align with those of Ningsih and Hidayat, who found that income does
not significantly influence the decision to save with Sharia ba nks. From a practical
standpoint, fund -mobilization strategies should not rely solely on income -level
segmentation but should also take into acco unt customers' needs, financial goals, and
behavioral characteristics.

stronger an individual's religious orientation, the greater their tendency to choose banking
services perceived to align with Sharia principles.
These findings can be explained through the theory of Glock and Stark, which views
religiosity across s everal dimensions: belief, religious practice, religious experience,
religious knowledge, and the consequences of religiosity in daily l ife. In the context of the

The Influence Of Service Quality On The Decision To Save
Service quality does not have a significant effect on customers' decisions to save (β =
0.155; p = 0.159). These findings indicate that, althoug h service quality exerts a positive
influence, it has not yet become a differentiating factor in the saving decisions of customers
at the BSI Merangin Branch. This result is noteworthy as it differs from some previous
studies finding that service quality positively influences customer decisions.
One possible explanation is that service quality has become a fundamental attribute
expected by customers from banking institutions. When service levels across banks are
perceived as relatively similar, service quality ceases to be the primary factor distinguishing
customer choices. Furthermore, the advancement of banking digitalization has the potential
to alter the role of face -to-face service. The use of mobile banking enables customers to
conduct transactions ind ependently, thereby reducing the frequency of interactions with
bank staff. Consequently, the service quality provided at branch offi ces may have a more
limited impact on saving decisions.
However, this explanation should be viewed as a contextual interpretation, given that
this study did not specifically examine the intensity of mobile banking usage. Future
research could investigate whether digitalization or the quality of digital services acts as a
moderating variable in the relationship between service quality and customer decisions.
These findings align with research results indicating that service quality does not
significantly influence the decision to save with an Islamic bank . Nevertheless, statistical
insignificance does not imply that service qual ity is unimportant. For bank management,
maintaining service quality remains essential as it shapes customer satisfaction, experience,
and loyalty, even though it did not emerge as a direct determinant of saving decisions in this
specific study.

Conclusion
This study demonstrates that religiosity is the primary factor shaping customer
decisions to save at the Merangin branch of BSI, whereas knowledge, income, service
quality, and trust did not show a significant influence. These findings contribut e
theoretically to the study of customer behavior in Islamic banking by showing that financial
decisions are not shaped solely by economic considerations and service attributes, but also
by the values and beliefs inherent in the individual. In the contex t of Islamic banking,
religiosity can serve as an internal factor that reinforces the alignment between religious
beliefs and economic be havioral choices.  Conceptually, this study expands the
understanding of customer behavior by demonstrating that the rel ationships between
knowledge, income, service quality, and trust and the decision to save are not always direct.
The lack of significance  of these variables suggests that their influence might operate
through other mechanisms, such as perceived benefits, s atisfaction, perceived risk, or
Islamic financial literacy. Thus, this study provides a foundation for developing customer
behavior models that look beyond direct influences to consider more complex relationships
between constructs.
The practical implicati on of this study is that Islamic banks need to strengthen
strategies based on Sharia values through communication that emphasizes adher ence to
Sharia principles, contract transparency, product benefits, and customer well -being. Such
strategies must be supported by practical Islamic financial education, improvements in both
face-to-face and digital service quality, and enhanced security and transparency to maintain
customer trust. Consequently, emphasizing religiosity should not be viewed merely as a
promotional approach but should be integrated with product and service quality so that
Sharia values are genuinely reflected in the customer experience. The study's limitations lie
in its scope covering only a single bank branch and its use of a cross -sectional design;
