This study investigates the fatigue effect of regional cultural symbols on con -
sumer purchase intention in agricultural product live streaming. Grounded in the
Stimulus-Organism-Response (SOR) model, a theoretical framework is devel -
oped that conceptualizes regional cultural symbol exposure as the environmental
stimulus, perceived authenticity, cultural identity, and psychological saturation
as organismic responses, and purchase intention as the behavioral response.
Data were collected from 387 Chinese consumers who had watched agricultural
product live streaming within the past 3 months through an online survey. Partial
least squares structural equation modeling (PLS-SEM) was employed to test the
proposed hypotheses, and the Gaussian copula approach was used to assess
potential endogeneity. The results reveal that regional cultural symbol exposure
positively influences perceived authenticity and cultural identity, which in turn
enhance purchase intention. Regional cultural symbol exposure also positively
influences psychological saturation, which negatively affects purchase intention.
The inverted U-shaped relationship between regional cultural symbol exposure
and purchase intention is confirmed, indicating that purchase intention increases
at low to moderate exposure levels but declines at high exposure levels. A multi-
group analysis further reveals that the wear-out pathway intensifies among high-
frequency viewers while the affordance pathways remain stable across viewing
frequencies. By integrating an affordance pathway with a wear-out pathway, the
study extends fatigue effect theory from traditional advertising to cultural symbol
marketing and refines the SOR model by accommodating coexisting positive and
inhibitory organismic responses to a single stimulus. The study provides empiri-
cal evidence on the boundary conditions of cultural symbol effectiveness in live
streaming contexts and offers practical implications for agricultural product mer-
chants seeking to optimize their cultural marketing strategies without triggering
consumer fatigue.

1 Introduction
Live e-commerce has proved to be a revolutionary trend in the
online retail industry. In China, this trend has been very widespread,
with over 526 million users of e-commerce live streaming in mid-
2023, which includes almost half of all internet users in this country
(Han et al., 2024). This novel form of marketing has also been adapted
in the agriculture industry, as live-stream e-commerce transaction
volumes in China in 2023 reached 4.9 trillion CNY , registering more
than 35% growth every year (Kong et al., 2024). Farm products, also
known as “trust products” since the buyer can neither see nor touch
the goods before buying them, benefit much from the visual presenta-
tion afforded by live streaming services (Li Q. et al., 2023). The inte-
gration of live streaming technology has proven particularly effective
in alleviating information asymmetry and enhancing consumer con-
fidence in agricultural product transactions (Zheng et al., 2023).
Cultural regional symbols have become an increasingly common
marketing approach in the live-streaming of farm goods. Many live
streams are conducted in rural settings or at facilities that show actual
cultural regional practices to enhance the emotional connection with
viewers (Cheng et al., 2025). These signs, which include local dialects,
traditional dresses, geographical features, cultural habits, and local
methods of production, serve to express authenticity and the geo -
graphical origin of agricultural products (He and Wang, 2017). These
cultural signs can raise the cultural identity of consumers and spark a
positive reaction emotionally to influence purchasing decisions (Liu
and Zhao, 2024). The use of cultural elements helps to create a unique
identity of agricultural products within the market through consum-
ers’ attraction to uniqueness and cultural appreciation ( Wang et
al., 2025).
The rising trend of using local cultural symbols in the live stream-
ing of farm produce has important implications for the sustainability
of its effectiveness. The wear out effect argues that repeated exposure
to a stimuli set may result in favorable responses becoming less favor-
able (Kronrod and Huber, 2019). Traditionally, empirical studies of the
phenomenon of advert repetition have been focused on commercial
advertisements, analyzing the degree of consumers’ boredom or
annoyance with identical repeated messages (Alwreikat and Rjoub,
2020). Y et the issue of cultural symbol repetition leading to consumer
fatigue has remained relatively unresearched. Branded content irrel-
evance or overload has been determined to be a major factor in the
emergence of social media fatigue ( Fernandes and Oliveira, 2024).
Repetitive and intrusive content can be perceived as irritating, poten-
tially undermining consumer engagement and purchase intentions
(Guo and Jiang, 2024).
There is a noticeable research gap regarding the fatigue effect of
regional cultural symbols in the agricultural product live streaming
context. The current literature has largely dealt with the positive effect
that cultural symbols have on consumer behavior and has overlooked
the diminishing returns that may result from overusing them. The
current research aims to fill that gap. Grounded in the Stimulus-
Organism-Response (SOR) model, this research constructs a theoreti-
cal framework that conceptualizes regional cultural symbol exposure
as environmental stimuli, consumer psychological states including
perceived authenticity, cultural identity, and psychological saturation
as organismic responses, and purchase intention as behavioral
outcomes.
There are three main contributions from this research. On a theo-
retical basis, this study broadens the application of the fatigue effect
theory from commercial advertisement to cultural symbolism market-
ing and improves the SOR model by adding the presence of co-exist-
ing positive (perceived authenticity, cultural identity) and negative
(psychological saturation) organismic responses toward the same
stimulus. In terms of methodology, it measures the stimulus–response
relationship of the curvilinear nature under the live-streaming setting
where the exposure level is highly variable across the audience mem-
bers. Practically, it establishes an inflection point that can guide agri-
cultural products merchants to calibrate cultural symbol density to
avoid eroding viewer engagement.
2 Literature review and hypothesis
development
2.1 Agricultural product live streaming
E-commerce
Agricultural products live streaming e-commerce is an innovative
integration of digital technologies with traditional agricultural mar-
keting, representing a substantial change in the distribution channels
for agricultural products (Dong et al., 2022). Such a marketing model
relies on digital video streaming in displaying agricultural products,
where consumers are given the chance to view the production pro-
cesses, interact with the streamers, and purchase products in real-time
(Huo et al., 2023). This capability for visualization inherent in live
streaming helps overcome the issue of information asymmetry that is
common within the trades of agricultural product purchases by allow-
ing consumers to assess the product through visual presentations (Li
Q. et al., 2023).
The growth of agricultural e-commerce live streaming in China
has been made possible by technological advancement in mobile
Internet infrastructure and the increasing popularity of smartphone
use in rural areas (Liu and Zheng, 2023). The live streaming platforms
act as a vital link between farmers and agricultural enterprises and city
consumers, eliminating the need for middlemen (Guo et al., 2022).
The interactive element available through live streaming makes it pos-
sible for the streamer to respond to clients’ questions directly, explain
the functions of a product, and win their trust through effective com-
munication (Li et al., 2024). The unique characteristics exhibited by
agricultural products, such as their perishability, seasonal nature, and
quality, make live streaming highly effective for communicating prod-
uct freshness to potential clients (Xu et al., 2020).
The impact of streamer characteristics on the psychological reac-
tion and purchase behavior of consumers in agricultural live stream-
ing has been empirically examined by Cheng et al. (2025) , where
streamer professionalism, interactivity, and trustworthiness are found
to have an impact. Moreover, Han et al. (2024) identify that affective
variables such as trust and resonance mediate the relationship between
live streaming features and consumer purchase intention.
2.2 Regional cultural symbols in marketing
Regional cultural symbols include a variety of elements that rep-
resent the unique attributes of specific geographic locations, such as
regional dialects, costumes, customs, geographic features, and native
methods of production ( He and Wang, 2017). These symbols can

serve as strong marketing tools in demonstrating authenticity, creating
emotional connections with customers, as well as differentiating
brands in competitive markets (Rodrigues et al., 2024). Use of symbols
in marketing communications can also invoke the cultural identity of
customers in a way that generates positive emotional responses,
thereby enhancing the image of the brand among the customers (Liu
and Zhao, 2024).
The use of cultural symbols in marketing is effective because cul-
tural symbols can help to establish meaningful ties between products
and cultural beliefs to which the consumer subscribes in terms of their
self-identity (Li X. et al., 2023). Traditional cultural symbols possess
aesthetic and cultural value that can be leveraged in product design
and marketing communications to enhance perceived authenticity
(Tian et al., 2020 ). Zong et al. (2023)  found that cultural identity
served as a mediating variable between consumers’ recognition of cul-
tural symbols and their behaviors, such that those who identified more
with certain elements of their culture felt more emotionally attached
to products that contained these symbols.
In the field of agricultural product live streaming, cultural sym-
bols of the region are expressed through the use of local dialects by the
live streamers, local farming practices, and the setting of local scenery
as the background of the live stream ( Cheng et al., 2025 ). These
aspects add to consumer belief about the authenticity of the product,
which is of utmost significance to agricultural items wherein origins
tend to influence perceptions of quality (Wang et al., 2025). Strategic
use of cultural symbols helps food product streamers to diversify their
product lines and develop distinct brands in a crowded market (Zheng
et al., 2023).
2.3 The SOR framework as theoretical
backbone
The Stimulus–Organism–Response (SOR) model, as developed by
Mehrabian and Russell (1974), forms the basis of the current study’s
theory. This framework suggests that stimuli from the environment
(S) trigger organismic states (O) that then elicit behavioral responses
(R). The SOR model has been widely applied in live streaming studies,
where stimuli such as interactivity, social presence, and atmospherics
influence cognitive and emotional states leading to purchase inten-
tions (Ming et al., 2021; Lo et al., 2022; Song et al., 2022). In other
e-commerce settings, organismic states are considered in terms of
perceived value, trust, and satisfaction, while the behavioral response
is defined as purchase intentions, purchase behavior, and post-pur-
chase behavior (Wu and Li, 2018). The applicability of this model to
different kinds of stimuli and mediating processes makes it ideal for
assessing the effects of cultural symbols on agricultural live streaming
(Zhang et al., 2022).
There are two unique features of the cultural-symbol stimulus
which make the development of an extended theory necessary. The
first is that cultural symbols are ambivalent stimuli; that is, they not
only communicate semantic information about origin and authentic-
ity but also demand cognitive attention in the process of repetition.
Existing SOR-based live streaming studies have focused on one route
for a positive response from the organism; that is, the inhibitory route
was left underspecified. Second, the psychological payoff from cultural
symbol exposure is unlikely to stay constant. According to the colla-
tive variable theory developed by Berlyne (1970), novelty-driven
arousal rises with the first exposure to the stimulus and then gradually
falls away as the stimulus becomes habituated, predicting a
non-monotonic stimulus–response surface that the standard linear
SOR cannot represent.
This paper builds upon the Stimulus-Organism-Response (SOR)
model by adding two positive organismic variables, perceived authen-
ticity and cultural identity, and one negative variable, psychological
saturation. It also adopts a curvilinear S-to-R association that repre-
sents the combined impact of the divergent paths. The dual-path cur-
vilinear expansion of SOR retains its simplicity but allows it to
effectively capture wear-out effects observed in repeated exposure
settings in traditional advertising literature (Pechmann and Stewart,
1988; Kronrod and Huber, 2019).
2.4 Dual-pathway mechanism: affordance
versus cognitive wear-out
Affordance pathway encompasses signaling theory (Spence, 1973)
and social identity theory (Tajfel and Turner, 2004). According to the
theory of signaling under conditions of asymmetric information, it is
argued that those costly-to-imitate observable features become cred-
ible signals, thus decreasing consumer ambiguity with regard to latent
attributes. Social identity theory argues that some self-defining ele-
ments come from group memberships, whereby cues about such
memberships become activated by the corresponding identities.
Cultural signs can also be used for both signaling purposes, which
include signaling origins and authenticity, as well as self-categorizing
as a member of culture, whereby the cultural symbols serve as identity
cues (Osmanova et al., 2023). Hence, the affordance pathway suggests
that exposure to symbolic cues leads to positive organismic states of
perceived authenticity and cultural identity, thus having an effect on
purchase intention positively.
The wear-out process incorporates the results from the body of
literature on advertising wear-out, which outlines a series of processes
from “wear-in, ” defined as the progressive build-up of effects through
initial exposures, to “wear-out, ” characterized by a decline in effect
through the excessive repetition beyond an optimal level of exposure
(Pechmann and Stewart, 1988). The repetitive processing of a consis-
tent message leads to a depletion of attention resources, develops
habituation, and, if the stimuli are highly exposed, induces reactance
against the marketer (Alwreikat and Rjoub, 2020; Kronrod and Huber,
2019; Wiedmann and Von Mettenheim, 2021). In the realm of digital
communication, branded-content overload and homogeneity are rec-
ognized as powerful antecedents to psychological saturation, which
refers to the cognitive condition that renders consumers resistant to
further persuasive messages (Zheng and Ling, 2021; Wang and Deng,
2022; Fernandes and Oliveira, 2024). Saturation thus constitutes the
inhibitory organismic state through which the wear-out pathway
dampens purchase intention.
However, each of these pathways works on different parts of the
cognitive processing spectrum. When the exposure level is low to
moderate, each subsequent symbolic feature still manages to reveal
useful information about its source and culture, giving an edge to
the affordance pathway. As the level of exposure increases further,
there are fewer gains in terms of information due to the redun -
dancy of features within the set of symbols, but there are rising
attention costs incurred nonlinearly; hence, the wear-out pathway
becomes more dominant than the other pathway, reversing the
slope of the net effect. The boundary conditions found in literature
relating to wear-out theory such as content variation and temporal
spacing (Becker and Gijsenberg, 2023 ; Van Ewijk et al., 2021 ; Guo

gains become negative depends on the consumption context
as well.
2.5 Hypothesis development
The streamer’s local dialect, traditional clothing, indigenous pro-
duction techniques, and geographical environment act as origin sig-
nals within the agricultural livestreaming context ( Spence, 1973).
Since consumers do not have the opportunity to verify agricultural
produce before purchasing them, these signals have a great impact on
consumers’ authenticity inferences. He and Wang (2017) have shown
that incorporating local culture into marketing communication
increases the consumers’ perception of authenticity for origin-related
goods, while Tian et al. (2020) reported the positive effects of cultural
identity signals in tourist settings with strong claims of origin.
Therefore:
H1: Regional cultural symbol exposure positively influences per-
ceived authenticity in agricultural product live streaming.
When authenticity is inferred, it results in positive evaluations of
the products and greater engagement in transactions. In terms of sig-
naling, an authentic source reduces the risk of the consumer obtaining
an inferior or fake product. When viewed from the point of view of
brand identification, authenticity increases symbolic congruence
between the consumer and the product, thus increasing purchase
intention ( Kumar and Kaushik, 2022 ). Fatma and Khan (2024)
showed that brand authenticity increases the willingness of consumers
to pay more for the product through brand identification. Perceived
authenticity, on the other hand, increases consumer-perceived value
across cultural contexts, as shown by Papadopoulou et al.
(2023). Hence:
H2: Perceived authenticity positively influences purchase inten-
tion in agricultural product live streaming.
These cultural cues not only help distinguish one’s cultural iden-
tity but also make the in-group visible to people who belong to the
culture, which leads to their self-categorization with the in-group. This
was proven by Osmanova et al. (2023) in their research on how brand
symbol cues trigger consumer brand identification through self-cate-
gorization processes. Therefore:
H3: Regional cultural symbol exposure positively influences cul-
tural identity in agricultural product live streaming.
When cultural identity is aroused, consumers have a propensity
toward products that support and reflect their cultural identity, which
fits well with in-group preference, according to social identity theory,
and identity-based choice in cultural consumption studies. According
to Zong et al. (2023), cultural identity acts as an impetus for purchas-
ing intentions for culturally significant products, and according to Li
X. et al. (2023), cultural motivation and product identification rein-
force each other to influence purchasing intention toward culturally
saturated products. Thus:
H4: Cultural identity positively influences purchase intention in
agricultural product live streaming.
Overexposure to culturally identical stimuli causes exhaustion of
the attentional capacity of consumers, despite the signaling and iden-
tity message being communicated at low levels of exposure through
each new symbolic stimulus. Live streaming is characterized by an
audiovisual environment that is saturated and thus ideal for triggering
fatigue arousal. This assertion is substantiated empirically in the con-
text of social media by Zheng and Ling (2021) and Fernandes and
Oliveira (2024). Therefore:
H5: Regional cultural symbol exposure positively influences psy-
chological saturation in agricultural product live streaming.
Once triggered, psychological saturation reduces susceptibility to
the marketing message that caused the arousal. Psychologically satu-
rated individuals commit less mental energy to process further mes-
sages, adopt avoidance strategies against the source, and possibly
express reactance against the persuasive message (Wang and Deng,
2022). Studies conducted in social media branded settings have estab-
lished a connection between fatigue and disengagement and weak-
ened behavioral intentions ( Fernandes and Oliveira, 2024 ). In an
agrarian live-streaming setting, this means that psychologically satu-
rated individuals will have reduced purchase intention despite the
positive signals that the symbols carry. Therefore:
H6: Psychological saturation negatively influences purchase
intention in agricultural product live streaming.
The composition of the affordance and wear-out pathways pre-
dicts an inverted U-shaped surface in the net stimulus–response link,
consistent with Berlyne’s (1970) optimal-arousal logic and the meta-
analytic evidence on advertising repetition ( Schmidt and Eisend,
2015). Therefore:
H7: Regional cultural symbol exposure exhibits an inverted
U-shaped relationship with purchase intention in agricultural
product live streaming, with purchase intention rising at low-to-
moderate exposure levels and declining at high exposure levels.

3 Methodology
3.1 Sample and data collection
The study focused on Chinese consumers who had logged on to
agricultural produce live streaming in the past 3 months. Such a cri-
terion was applied to ensure that the participants would be able to
provide relevant insights based on recent experiences with the phe-
nomenon being studied. The recruitment of the research participants
was implemented through convenience sampling combined with the
principles of snowball sampling.
The survey was conducted online through the Wenjuanxing
survey tool, which is very common in China and offers similar func-
tionality to that of Qualtrics. In addition to that, a pilot survey was
conducted for 30 respondents before the main survey to determine
the clarity and understanding of the items in the survey. The pilot
survey allowed for slight changes to the wordings of the items for

better understanding by the respondents. The main survey was con-
ducted for 4 weeks.
Several criteria were used for the purpose of data quality. These
include excluding the response form if it did not pass the attention
questions, took less than 3 min to complete, or was a straight line
response pattern. A total of 450 questionnaires were given to partici-
pants, but 398 were received. After going through quality control mea-
sures, 387 questionnaires were included in the study, giving a valid
response rate of 86.0%.
The sample size adequacy was evaluated using two procedures
recommended for PLS-SEM. Following Cohen’s (1992) power analy-
sis, with up to four predictors pointing at any endogenous construct,
a 5% significance level, a power of 0.80, and a minimum R2 of 0.10, the
required sample size is 113. Following Kock and Hadaya's (2018)
inverse square root method, with the smallest hypothesized path coef-
ficient of |β| = 0.19, a 5% significance level, and a power of 0.80, the
required sample size is 172. The achieved sample of 387 valid respon-
dents exceeds both thresholds.
3.2 Measurement
All the constructs in the study employed multi-item scales,
which were adapted from the existing literature of validated instru -
ments. There were minor wording modifications according to the
context of live streaming agricultural products. The four items mea -
suring regional cultural symbol exposure were adapted from the
cultural symbol perception scale of He and Wang (2017). Perceived
authenticity items were adapted from the brand authenticity scale
of Fatma and Khan (2024) . Cultural identity items were adapted
from Zong et al. (2023) . Psychological saturation items were
adapted from the social media fatigue scale of Zhang et al. (2021) ,
retaining the cognitive-exhaustion dimension most relevant to
repetitive cultural exposure. Purchase intention items were adapted
from Ming et al. (2021). A seven-point Likert scale, ranging from 1
(strongly disagree) to 7 (strongly agree) was used for all items. The
questionnaire was initially in English and then converted to Chinese
through the process of back-translation ( Brislin, 1970). Other con-
trol variables included are demographics such as gender, age, educa -
tion level, monthly income, and the frequency of viewing the live
stream. The measurement items for all constructs are presented in
Table 1.
3.3 Data analysis
Data analysis was conducted using SmartPLS 4.0 software,
employing partial least squares structural equation modeling (PLS-
SEM) to test the proposed hypotheses. The data analysis followed a
two-stage approach recommended by Anderson and Gerbing (1988).
The first stage assessed the measurement model by examining indica-
tor loadings (threshold > 0.70), internal consistency reliability includ-
ing Cronbach’s alpha and composite reliability (threshold > 0.70),
convergent validity using average variance extracted (threshold >
0.50) (Fornell and Larcker, 1981), and discriminant validity using the
Fornell-Larcker criterion and heterotrait-monotrait ratio (threshold
< 0.85) (Henseler et al., 2015). Common method bias was assessed
using Harman’s single-factor test (threshold < 50%) (Podsakoff et al.,
2003) and full collinearity VIF test (threshold < 3.3) ( Kock, 2015).
The second stage evaluated the structural model on the basis of path
coefficients, significance levels, and explained variables ( R2).
Bootstrapping with 5,000 resamples was employed to establish the
significance of the path coefficients.
R2 values of 0.25, 0.50, and 0.75 were set by Hair et al. (2014) to
describe weak, moderate, and substantial levels of explanatory power. To
test the inverted U-shape relationship proposed in H7, a quadratic form
for regional cultural symbol exposure was added to the model. To
address possible endogeneity associated with cross-sectional data, the
Gaussian copula method (Park and Gupta, 2012; Hult et al., 2018) was
used with SmartPLS 4 software. The instrumental-variable-free approach
relies on testing the correlation between the endogenous regressor and
structural errors by applying a copula function. The assumption regard-
ing the non-normal distribution of the focal regressor was confirmed
with the help of the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test. Then, the copula term
was added to the structural model predicting purchase intention, and its
significance was assessed via 5,000 bootstrap resamples. To examine
whether the structural estimates differ between low-frequency and high-
frequency viewers, the permutation-based PLS-MGA was conducted,
with measurement invariance assessed through the MICOM procedure
(Henseler et al., 2016) using 5,000 permutations.

4 Results
4.1 Sample description
The demographic characteristics of the 387 valid respondents are
summarized in Table 2. In terms of gender, 165 respondents (42.6%)
were male and 222 respondents (57.4%) were female, reflecting the
higher engagement of female consumers in live streaming e-commerce.
Regarding age distribution, the majority of respondents were aged 26–35
(41.3%), followed by those aged 18–25 (28.4%), 36–45 (19.6%), and over
45 (10.7%). In terms of education level, respondents with bachelor’s
degrees constituted the largest group (52.7%), followed by those with
junior college degrees (23.3%), postgraduate degrees (14.5%), and high
school or below (9.5%). Regarding monthly income, the distribution was
relatively balanced: below 3,000 CNY (15.8%), 3,000–5,999 CNY
(29.7%), 6,000–9,999 CNY (32.8%), and 10,000 CNY or above (21.7%).
In terms of the number of sessions spent watching live streaming of
agricultural products, 35.4% of respondents watched between 1 and 2
sessions a week, 27.4% watched between 3 and 4 sessions a week, 22.0%
watched between 5 and 6 sessions a week, while 15.2% watched daily.
4.2 Measurement model
The Common method bias was checked before testing the mea-
surement model. Harman’s single-factor test revealed the appearance
of five factors with eigenvalues above 1.0, with the first factor account-
ing for 32.17% of the total variance. The values for the variance infla-
tion factor ranged from 1.18 to 2.74, suggesting that CMB was not a
significant problem.
The measurement model was tested for reliability and validity. As
evident from Table 3, the values of the loadings of the indicators ranged
from 0.72 to 0.87. The values of Cronbach’s alpha and the composite
reliabilities ranged from 0.79 to 0.89 and 0.87 to 0.92, respectively. The
values of the average variance extracted ranged from 0.59 to 0.74.

psychological saturation (R2 = 0.32), and purchase intention (R2 = 0.51).
The R2 value of 0.51 for purchase intention suggested that the model had
moderate explanatory power for consumer purchase behavior.

4.4 Multi-group analysis
In order to test if there is any difference in the suggested model
across different conditions of media consumption, the analysis was
carried out using multi-groups by dividing the respondents into low
frequency users (1–2 sessions per week; n = 137) and high frequency
users (3+ sessions per week; n  = 250). Configural invariance was
found by design. Compositional invariance was also found since all
correlations of composite measures within each specific group
exceeded 0.95. Partial measurement invariance was established, per-
mitting meaningful comparison of group-specific path coefficients.
The findings, as displayed in Table 5, demonstrate a discernible dif-
ferential pattern. First, the wear-out effect is much more salient for fre-
quent users compared to infrequent users. More precisely, the relationship
between exposure to regional cultural symbols and psychological satura-
tion is much stronger for the former group (β_high = 0.65) than for the
latter group ( β_low = 0.41), with a permutation p-value of 0.003.
Furthermore, the relationship between psychological saturation and pur-
chase intention is more negative for the frequent group (β_high = −0.30)
than for the infrequent group (β_low = −0.18), with a permutation
p-value of 0.024. On the contrary, no discernible differences emerge
between groups for the affordance pathways. Permutation p-values of the
relationships between exposure to regional cultural symbols and per-
ceived authenticity, as well as cultural identity, along with their subse-
quent relationships to purchase intention, were above 0.10 in all cases.
The above relationship fits the theoretical expectation that the
consumption-induced cognitive overload phenomenon becomes even
stronger as one increases their consumption frequency; however, for
the affordance path, the influence remains relatively similar regardless
of varying consumption frequencies. This finding further defines the
scope of the inverted-U shaped curve where negative marginal utility
kicks in sooner for heavy consumers, supporting the contextual sen-
sitivity of the dual-pathway mechanism.
5 Discussion
This study investigated the effect of regional cultural symbols on
consumer purchase intention with respect to agricultural product live
streaming. The findings from the empirical test have revealed a series
of significant patterns for theoretical contributions and practical rec-
ommendations for agricultural product marketing.

The results demonstrated that regional cultural symbol exposure
significantly enhances both perceived authenticity (β = 0.49) and cul-
tural identity (β = 0.45). These findings align with prior research indi-
cating that traditional cultural symbols are closely related to
consumers’ cognition and willingness to buy (Zong et al., 2023). In the
context of live streaming within the agricultural product realm, cul-
tural elements such as regional dialect, regional traditions, and scen-
ery have strong authenticity cues signifying not only the authenticity
but also the origin of products. The strong positive link between expo-
sure to regional cultural symbols and perceived authenticity confirms
that consumers interpret cultural elements as indicators of product
quality and trustworthiness (He and Wang, 2017).
The findings support the mediating role of perceived authenticity
and cultural identity in shaping the purchase intention. Perceived
authenticity had a large positive impact on the purchase intention
(β = 0.35), and the cultural identity had a positive impact on the pur-
chase intention (β = 0.31). These results are consistent with existing
literature supporting the importance of perceived authenticity to con-
sumer engagement and purchase decisions ( Kumar and Kaushik,
2022), as well as the willingness of consumers who identify with the
regionally presented culture to purchase products aligned to their cul-
ture (Zong et al., 2023). The affordance pathway, operating through
perceived authenticity and cultural identity, explains how regional
cultural symbols influence a positive consumer response in the con-
text of agricultural live streaming.
The study also revealed a significant positive relationship between
regional cultural symbol exposure and psychological saturation
(β = 0.57), which subsequently negatively influenced purchase inten-
tion (β = −0.24). This finding empirically establishes the wear-out
pathway proposed in the dual-pathway framework, generalizing
fatigue effect theory from traditional advertising to cultural symbol
marketing. The effect of content overload and irrelevancy has been
found to influence the concept of social media fatigue (Fernandes and
Oliveira, 2024). If the regional cultural symbols are judged to be repet-
itive or formulaic by consumers, they will encounter mental and emo-
tional exhaustion and will be less receptive to marketing
communications. The role of psychological saturation as a negative
mediator defines the boundary conditions of the effectiveness of cul-
tural symbols and supports past findings on the determinants of social
media fatigue (Zheng and Ling, 2021).
The testing of an inverted U-shape relationship between regional
cultural symbol exposure and purchase intention (H7) is another
important theoretical contribution in this study. The finding indicates
that there is a significant positive linear component (β = 0.28) and a
negative quadratic component (β = −0.19), showing that purchase
intention increases at low levels and then diminishes at high levels.
The curvilinear trend observed in this study aligns with prior research
demonstrating that the relationship between advertising exposure and
affective response follows an inverted U-shaped pattern, where initial
exposures generate positive affect that eventually declines with over-
exposure (Schmidt and Eisend, 2015). The inverted U-shaped rela-
tionship suggests the existence of an optimal exposure level that
maximizes consumer purchase intention while avoiding the negative
consequences of overexposure (Kronrod and Huber, 2019).
It is the combination of the affordance pathway (through perceived
authenticity and cultural identity) and the wear-out pathway (through
psychological saturation) which helps to explain the complexity of con-
sumers’ behavior toward regional cultural symbols. The inverted
U-shaped pattern observed in the data is the empirical signature of this
dual-pathway composition, with the relative dominance of each path-
way shifting as exposure intensifies. The dual-pathway framework
extends the use of the SOR model in live streaming commerce research
as it demonstrates how a single stimulus is capable of producing both
affordance and inhibitory organismic responses (Ming et al., 2021).
The multi-group analysis further refines the boundary conditions
of the dual-pathway mechanism. The wear-out pathway intensifies
under heavy viewing, while the affordance pathways remain compa-
rably effective across viewing frequencies. This asymmetry suggests
that the inflection point of the inverted U-shaped surface is not a
fixed property of the stimulus but is contingent on the consumer’s
cumulative exposure history, extending Schmidt and Eisend’s (2015)
advertising repetition findings to non-commercial symbolic stimuli
in live streaming.
The model demonstrates strong explanatory power (R2 = 0.51 for
purchase intention), supporting the effectiveness of the proposed theo-
retical framework in explaining the major mechanisms associated with
the consumer response to regional cultural symbols in agricultural live
streaming marketing. The results also highlight the importance of con-
sidering possible negative effects resulting from the overuse of cultural
symbols. Live streaming agricultural content is a unique marketing
platform where the authenticity and emotional value of the cultural
items may play an important role in influencing consumer response,
provided that exposure does not exceed an optimal level.
6 Limitations and future research
First, although the Gaussian copula approach indicates that endo-
geneity is not an issue in the current study, there is a limit to establish-
ing causation due to the use of cross-sectional designs. In future
investigations, researchers may want to use panel data designs or
experimental designs involving laboratory manipulation of cultural
symbols to confirm the causality indicated by the theory-based dual-
pathway relationship.
Second, all variables were measured using self-report approaches.
Even though Harman’s one-factor test and VIF analysis of the full
collinearity have minimized the problem of common method vari-
ance, additional methods such as eye-tracking attention indices or
physiological arousal indicators can be included in future studies to
triangulate the saturation construct.
Third, the present sample was confined to Chinese audiences. Due
to the inherent context dependency of cultural identification, the
inflection point and pathway effects might vary from one cultural con-
text to another. Replication across cultures, especially in those that
lack strong traditions in geographic indication, will help assess the
generalizability of the inverted-U shaped pattern.
Fourth, this study considered regional cultural symbols as an
undifferentiated stimulus. There exist linguistic symbols (dialects),
material symbols (clothing, scenery), and procedural symbols
(method of production). Depending on their informational value and
speed of habituation, different categories of symbols can be consid-
ered as future directions for more granular mechanism studies.
Fifth, even though viewing frequency was used as one moderator
in the multi-group analysis, other relevant contextual factors could be
examined. Specifically, relevant factors include product type (search
products vs. experience products, perishable vs. shelf-stable products),
streamer type (farmer-streamer vs. professional host), platform

7 Conclusion
This study examines the influence of fatigue from regional cultural
symbols on consumer purchase intention in agricultural products live
streaming. The results reveal that exposure to regional cultural sym-
bols positively influences perceived authenticity and cultural identity,
which in turn enhance purchase intention. Exposure to regional cul-
tural symbols also positively influences psychological saturation,
which in turn reduces purchase intention. The results validate that
there is an inverted U-shape relationship between exposure to regional
cultural symbols and purchase intention. As exposure rises from low
to moderate levels, purchase intention increases; once exposure
crosses an optimal threshold, purchase intention declines. By integrat-
ing an affordance pathway and a wear-out pathway within a single
SOR framework, this research extends fatigue effect theory from tra-
ditional advertising to cultural symbol marketing, and refines the SOR
model by accommodating coexisting positive and inhibitory organis-
mic responses to a single stimulus. The findings suggest that marketers
should strategically deploy regional cultural symbols while avoiding
excessive repetition that triggers consumer fatigue.
