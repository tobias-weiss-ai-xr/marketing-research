   ABSTRACT

The aim of the study was to study the relationship between FOMO, impulsive buying tendency and
behavior, post-purchase dissonance, and shopping enjoyment. The main purpose of this research was
to understand how Fear of missing out creates an impact on the behavior of consumers and the role of
impulsive buying behavior in post-purchase dissonance. This study used quantitative research
methodology, and surveys were used to gather data. Data were gathered using convenience sampling.
A total of 300 questionnaires were distributed. SPSS was used for coding the data, and Smart PLS 4 for
measurement and structural equation modeling. The study's findings indicate that FOMO has an
indirect effect on post-purchase dissonance through impulsive purchasing behavior rather than a direct
one. When impulsive buying tendencies strongly predict impulsive buying behavior, post-purchase
dissonance starts to rise. Furthermore, data demonstrate how impulsive buying and impulsive buying
tendency influence the link between FOMO and post-purchase dissonance. The moderating effect of
shopping enjoyment has no effect on FOMO or impulsive buying. The results have made a great
contribution to the existing theory of customer behavior by putting some limelight on indirect pathways
through which FOMO affects post-purchase dissonance. With its emphasis on essential marketing
strategies, conscientious purchasing practices, and customer-friendly return policies, this study is
significant for marketers, consumers, and merchants.

Keywords: Fear of Missing Out, Online Shopping, Compulsive Buying, Impulsive Buying, Post-
Purchase Dissonance.

INTRODUCTION

Since everything has changed in today's world, the trade sector has also undergone significant change, more
importantly because of the growth of e-commerce. Consumer behavior is significantly influenced by several factors,
such as FOMO and post -purchase di ssonance. According to Abbasi (2017), impulsive buying is influenced by a
variety of factors, including culture, different economic cultures, individuality, timing, location, etc. When a
customer buys a product, their attitude can be influenced by both int ernal and external factors. Intrinsic factors
which stem from a person's unique qualities and attributes —have a significant impact on impulsive purchasing.

Although a lot of research has been done on how people perceive online shopping and industry trends, very few
studies have tried to look at the connection between impulsive buying and post -purchase dissonance. By
investigating post -purchase dissonance and the connection between imp ulsive buying and FOMO, this study will
add to the expanding corpus of theories and research. Our research will give consumers deep insights into why
they experience dissonance after making a purchase by looking at FOMO, impulsive buying, and other anteced ents
like post-purchase dissonance. This is of increasing interest to both academics and practitioners.  A condition known
as post -purchase dissonance occurs when a person's beliefs and behaviors conflict with  one another, leading to
tension and unease.

FOMO, or the fear of missing out on a perceived opportunity, drives people to make rash, impulsive purchases to
avoid feeling excluded or deprived. FOMO can encourage impulsive purchases. Customers' rapid, rarely
pleasurable reactions to outside stimuli lead to impulsive buying. A person's ability to make impulsive, unplanned
purchases with little to no concern for the repercussions is known as their impulse buying tendency. It is a tendency
of a person when he/she is not aware and does not want to focus on the outcome and quickly concludes buying
something, not keeping in view that you will regret it afterwards or not. It is also considered to be a purchase as
response to an abrupt requirement.

The factor that has been regarded as an individual characteristic is shopping enjoyment, which denotes the
propensity to find shopping more enjoyable and to have more fun while shopping than others. Post -Purchase
Dissonance is defined as a condition where a person's thoughts, specifically their beliefs and behaviors, are at odds
with one another, which leads to tension and unease. Impulsive Buying: It is the outcome of the consumer's prompt,
rarely pleasurable reaction to outside stimuli. FOMO: People who a re anxious about missing out on a perceived
opportunity are compelled to make rash, impulsive purchases to avoid feeling excluded or deprived. FOMO can
encourage impulsive purchases.

METHODOLOGY

The research design was quantitative in nature. The study collected cross -sectional data . The data were collected
using convenience sampling from 300 university students who had experience buying. Among the total
respondents, 80% of the data was gathered through structured questionnaires on a one-to-one basis, while 20% of
the data were gathered via online platforms through emails. A 5 -point Likert scale was used to collect data , where
1 indicated strongly disagree to 5 represented strongly agree. There were 22 questions in the questionnaire, and out
of 300 responses , 297 were complete responses and hence were used to evaluate the data. Online and paper -based
surveys were used to collect data. SPSS and Excel were used for coding. Demographics were examined using
descriptive analysis, and hypotheses were tested using PL S-SEM. PLS-SEM and SPSS were used to analyze the data.
The Measurement Model and the Structural Equation Model were analyzed. All the participants were informed
regarding the research and its application to academia. It was also confirmed by the participan ts that all the data
collected would remain confidential and their identity would not be disclosed.

Post-Purchase Dissonance: It is a condition where a person's thoughts, specifically their beliefs and behaviors, are
at odds with one another, which leads to tension and unease. Four Post -Purchase Dissonance items have been
extracted from Festinger's 1957 work. Impulsive Buying: It  is the outcome of the consumer's prompt, rarely
pleasurable reaction to outside stimuli. Seven items of impulsive purchasing have been extracted from
Madhavaram and Laverie  (2004).

FOMO: People who are anxious about missing out on a perceived opportunity are compelled to make rash,
impulsive purchases to avoid feeling excluded or deprived. FOMO can encourage impulsive purchases. Four
elements of FOMO have been extracted from (Good & Hyman, 2021; Hodkinson, 2016; Hodkinson, 2019).

Impulsive Buying Tendency:  It refers to a person's ability to make impulsive, unplanned purchases with little to
no concern for the repercussions. Rook and Fisher (1995) provided the five items of impulsive buying tendency.

Shopping Enjoyment:  It is the element that has been regarded as an individual trait that reflects the propensity to
enjoy shopping more and have more fun while doing it than others. Three things that make shopping enjoyable
have been lifted from Arnold and Reynolds (2003).

DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION

The findings indicate that post -purchase dissonance is directly influenced by mediators like impulsive buying
behaviors and tendencies, rather than FOMO. This study found that people with higher impulsive buying
tendencies significantly experience post -purchase dissonance. Moreover, the pleasure of shopping is not found to
be the strongest moderator of the association between FOMO and impulsive spending. There is no correlation
between impulsive inclinations and enjoyment level.

These results contribute to the study of behavior by influencing the role that impulsive buying plays in post -
purchase regret. In terms of understanding and reducing impulsive purchasing, the study has significant
implications for consumers, marketers, and  retailers. To get more results, future research can use more longitudinal
designs and include more moderating variables.

The following is the summary of the findings. The first hypothesis was rejected , as its p-value was not significant ;

The data shows a comprehensive understanding of FOMO by highlighting the lack of a direct link between FOMO

The study shows that impulsive buying greatly increases customers' regret and highlights the importance of
emotional control in purchasing behaviors. Impulsive purchases are characterized by unrestrained, emotion -driven
decision-making when objective evalu ation follows spontaneous gratification. This lack of reflection often leads to
post-purchase discord,  state of discontent or mental discomfort which occur when customers think back on  hasty
decisions taken by them and feel that a more careful assessment might have yielded better outcomes.

Impulsive tendencies are exacerbated by the encouragement to put instant emotional gratification ahead of careful
buying. Existed studies suggest that enjoyment could encourage impulsive buying. Our findings indicate that
shopping has no direct bearing on the association between impulsive buying and FOMO (Beatty & Ferrell, 1998).
This study demonstrates that, contrary to what was previously believed, the retail environment has less of an impact
on impulsive purchases driven by FOMO. The fear -induced desire to buy is so strong that it outweighs any
advantages of having a fun shopping experience, which is one reasonable  explanation for this result.

Customers are forced to make simultaneous purchasing decisions when they experience a sense of urgency brought
on by their fear of missing out. Shopping enjoyment becomes an option when a desired product or deal takes
precedence over the decision -making pr ocess. Furthermore, the lack of a direct correlation between the large
moderate variable of shopping satisfaction and immediate purchase behavior challenges the notion that a pleasant
shopping environment increases such behavior. It has been noted that imp ulsivity and satisfaction may be related
to the type of goods, the process by which FOMO is triggered, or individual FOMO contrasts. Future research could
look at the elements of shopping pleasure that could act as moderators.

To reduce post-purchase dissonance, the study highlights how important it is to understand impulsive buying and
FOMO (Fear of Missing Out). It counsels marketers to avoid misrepresenting their products and to provide positive
feedback to manage consumer expectations, encourage loyalty, and encourage repeat business. By employing
strategies like follow -up emails, satisfaction surveys, and customer service support, marketers can lessen post -
purchase dissonance. To make clients feel appreciated, they can also employ limitations like package discounts and
personalized recommendations. By producing videos, images, or videos and documenting customer feedback,
educational content about the product can help lessen post -purchase dissonance.

Because they lessen buyer dissonance, return policies are essential to the growth and expansion of businesses.
Customers can discover that making snap decisions isn't always a good idea and that they can make sensible
purchases. Customers can make better d ecisions about their needs if they postpone purchases for 24 to 48 hours. By
creating budgets and assessing themselves before making a purchase, consumers can prevent overspending and
impulsive purchases. Impulsivity can be decreased by making a shopping l ist and planning your purchases.
Frequent customers can offer unique product reviews, which can lessen regret after a purchase.
