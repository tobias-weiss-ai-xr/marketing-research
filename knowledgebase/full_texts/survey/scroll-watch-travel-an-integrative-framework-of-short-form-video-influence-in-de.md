Abstract
This study systematically reviews empirical research on short-form video platforms in destination marketing and place
branding to examine how these platforms shape tourists’ attitudes, electronic word-of-mouth, and behavioral intentions.
Following PRISMA 2020 guidelines, 33 empirical studies published between 2022 and 2025 were identified from Sco -
pus and Web of Science and synthesized through thematic analysis. Seven themes emerged: technology acceptance and
platform adoption; source credibility and influencer persona; content characteristics and cognitive-affective processing;
parasocial and relational mechanisms; content source and format contingencies; generational and individual-difference
moderators; and engagement dynamics in platform-native data. These themes are organized into a four-stage integrative
framework (platform entry, stimulus encoding, psychological processing, and behavioral response), that maps the sequen -
tial and contingent relationships among constructs drawn from Technology Acceptance Model (TAM)/Unified Theory of
Acceptance and Use of Technology 2 (UTAUT2), source credibility, Stimulus-Organism-Response (S-O-R), parasocial
interaction, and mental imagery theories. The framework’s primary contribution is connecting these theoretical constructs,
which prior studies have used separately, into a unified processing architecture, showing how they interact within a struc -
tured processing sequence rather than operating as isolated explanatory lenses, while identifying critical gaps: the absence
of observed tourist behavior, destination image as an outcome, and negative processing mechanisms. By integrating these
strands, the study provides a structured basis for future empirical testing and offers managerially relevant insights for
destination promotion in short-form video environments.

nant channels for digital communication and consumer
engagement, not only expanding existing social media

from text- and image-dominant media to video-first formats
has been consequential, not only in terms of visibility, but
also in how destination-related information is cognitively
and affectively processed by users, as evidenced by the role
of platforms such as TikTok and Douyin, which have been
credited with measurable surges in destination search popu-
larity and tourism revenue (Qiu et al. 2024). Furthermore,
and practitioners increasingly recognize short-form video as
a strategic tool for destination promotion (Luo et al. 2025).
Characterized by high interactivity, low barriers to partici -
pation, and a capacity for rapid dissemination, short-form
video marketing has rapidly become a consequential chan -
nel for destination promotion, one that destination manage-
ment organizations (DMOs) are actively seeking to leverage,
though its effective mechanisms are not yet fully understood
(Cao et al. 2021; Jiang et al. 2024), particularly in terms
of how multiple platform-specific factors interact to shape
tourist responses. For marketing scholars and DMOs alike,
understanding how these platforms shape tourist attitudes
and behavior is no longer a peripheral concern but a cen -
tral question for the field. The theoretical challenge posed
by this format is substantive, as it raises questions about
whether established models adequately capture how users
process continuously encountered, short-duration content.
The intersection of social media and tourism marketing
has attracted sustained scholarly attention over the past two
decades. Early reviews documented how Web 2.0 trans -
formed travelers from passive information consumers into
active content producers, who create, share, and evaluate
travel experiences online (Ketter and Avraham 2012; Leung
et al. 2013) thereby establishing the foundations for user-
driven information environments that current SVPs further
intensify. Within destination marketing specifically, digital
tools have reshaped both the supply side, where DMOs
deploy branding campaigns and manage stakeholder rela -
tionships (Daspit and Zavattaro 2014; Pike 2008; Sara -
niemi and Komppula 2017), and the demand side, where
consumer-generated content influences destination image
formation, electronic word-of-mouth (eWOM), and behav -
ioral intentions (Baber and Baber 2022; Dedeoglu et al.
2023). Place branding, understood as the coordinated effort
to develop and communicate a competitive identity for a
geographic locale (Daspit and Zavattaro 2014), has become
increasingly contingent upon the affordances of the plat -
forms through which destination narratives circulate.
SVPs differ from earlier social media formats in several
aspects, with theoretical implications. Unlike text-based
review platforms or photo-sharing applications, they com -
bine audiovisual storytelling, algorithmic personalization,
and participatory features (e.g., comments, duets, trend-
based challenges) within an interface designed for rapid
consumption and high engagement (Mohd Rizal et al. 2025;
Qiu et al. 2024). Research has shown that travel-related
short videos are typically staged and efficiently edited rep -
resentations of destinations, distinct from the perceived
authenticity emphasized in live-streaming formats (Qiu et
al. 2024). The role of social media influencers (SMIs) is also
distinctive: on SVPs, parasocial interaction and perceived
expertise appear to drive viewer attitudes more strongly
than the physical attractiveness or explicit trustworthiness
signals that dominate in other endorsement contexts (Qiu
et al. 2024). Content characteristics such as usefulness,
entertainment value, and ease of processing have been
identified as stimuli that shape trust and, subsequently,
behavioral responses through mechanisms consistent with
S-O-R frameworks, with consistent evidence across stud -
ies supporting these relationships (Luo et al. 2025). These
platform-specific properties suggest that findings derived
from research on Facebook, Instagram, or TripAdvisor can-
not be straightforwardly extrapolated to short-form video
environments.
Frameworks developed to explain tourist information
processing and attitude formation under earlier social media
conditions were largely built on the premise of deliberate,
search-driven content exposure (Leung et al. 2013), which
contrasts with the algorithmically curated, rapid-consump -
tion nature of short-form video environments. Short-form
video disrupts this premise because algorithmically curated
feeds now deliver content to unsolicited audiences; extreme
brevity forecloses the sustained elaboration that persua -
sion-based models presuppose; and the perceived authen -
ticity of UGC attenuates the psychological reactance that
typically diminishes the persuasive effect of official desti -
nation advertising (Cao et al. 2021). These affordances do
not simply accelerate existing mechanisms; they alter the
conditions under which attitudes, eWOM, and behavioral
intentions form (Jiang et al. 2024; Luo et al. 2025). Whether
established constructs retain explanatory purchase in this
environment remains an open question with both theoretical
and managerial stakes, and addressing it requires first situ -
ating this phenomenon within the relevant scholarly fields
and tracing how the literature has evolved to engage with it.
Studies on SVPs in destination marketing and place
branding have examined various components separately
such as technology acceptance (Wang et al. 2022a, b; Zhou
et al. 2023), message design (Cao et al. 2021; Yuan et al.
2024), influencer impact (Bou Aragones et al. 2025; Ibrahim
et al. 2024; Li and Sun 2024; Qiu et al. 2024), and the role
of UGC (An et al. 2025; Dedeoglu et al. 2023; Marti-Ochoa
et al. 2025; Tham et al. 2024). Although valuable insights
have emerged, the literature still lacks a unifying model that
can explain how platform features, content characteristics,
social dynamics, and user interactions converge to influence
tourism-related outcomes, as these dimensions are typically

examined in isolation rather than as interdependent mecha -

Against this background, the present study asks: How
and through which mechanisms do SVPs shape tourists’
attitudes, eWOM, and destination-related behavioral inten -
tions or actions? This question is deliberately broad enough
to encompass both the antecedent conditions (e.g., content
characteristics, platform affordances, influencer attributes)
and the mediating processes (e.g., trust formation, atti -
tude change, parasocial interaction) through which short-
form video exposure translates into destination-relevant
outcomes.
This systematic review contributes to the literature in
several specific ways with the aim of developing an integra-
tive understanding of how these mechanisms jointly oper -
ate within short-form video environments. First, it provides
a structured synthesis of 33 empirical studies identified
through Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA)
guided screening, encompassing the period from 2022 to
2025, a timeframe that captures the rapid rise of research on
this topic. Second, it maps the field’s theoretical landscape,
documenting the range of frameworks invoked and identify-
ing where theoretical integration is needed to move beyond
single-theory explanations toward more comprehensive
models, capable of capturing the interaction of multiple
mechanisms within platform-driven environments. Third,
the review identifies methodological patterns and limita -
tions, notably the predominance of cross-sectional survey
designs, the concentration of empirical evidence in Chinese
contexts, and the relative scarcity of experimental, longi -
tudinal, or qualitative approaches that constrain the causal
and contextual claims that can currently be made. Fourth, by
examining the dependent variables and mechanisms stud -
ied across the corpus, the review delineates which pathways
from short-form video exposure to behavioral outcomes are
empirically established, which remain contested, and where
the evidence is simply absent. In doing so, it addresses
gaps noted by Qiu et al. ( 2024) regarding the need for an
integrated theoretical understanding of how influencer and
platform characteristics jointly shape travel intention, and
it extends the scope of prior social media–tourism reviews
(Ketter and Avraham 2012; Leung et al. 2013) into the SVPs
field.
To address this gap, this study proposes an integrative
framework organized as a four-stage processing chain
(Platform Entry→Stimulus Encoding→Psychological
Processing→Behavioral Response) bounded by cross-
cutting moderating layers (individual differences and con -
textual contingencies). The framework maps how platform
adoption drivers, source credibility, content attributes, and
cognitive-affective-relational processing mechanisms con -
nect sequentially and contingently to shape visit intentions,
eWOM, and engagement behaviors. By organizing con -
structs from TAM/UTAUT2, source credibility, S-O-R,
parasocial interaction, and mental imagery theories into a
unified architecture, the framework identifies both empiri -
cally supported pathways and critical gaps requiring future
investigation.
Materials and methods
This systematic literature review followed the PRISMA
2020 guidelines, ensuring transparency and replicability in
the identification, screening, and inclusion of studies (Page
et al. 2021). The review protocol was developed before the
search and specified the research question, eligibility cri -
teria, search strategy, screening procedures, data extraction
codebook, and synthesis approach.
Two multidisciplinary databases, Scopus and Web of
Science (WoS), were searched on 2nd January 2026 using
the query string: [(tiktok* OR reel*) AND influenc* AND
marketing]. The string is deliberately broad regarding
terms related to tourism or destinations to avoid exclud -
ing relevant studies that use different wording in the field
of destination marketing and place branding. Additionally,
other terms, such as “YouTube Short*”, were pretested
but yielded no relevant results and were excluded from the
final search query. The authors decided not to predetermine
the initial search period to capture the first relevant stud -
ies independently of any fixed criteria. A filter was applied
to restrict results to articles published in journals, thereby
excluding conference proceedings, books, and book chap -
ters. This restriction ensured that all considered studies had
undergone formal peer review, which serves as a minimum
threshold for methodological rigor and reporting quality in
systematic reviews (Palmatier et al. 2018).
Studies were included if they met all of the following
criteria. First, the study had to address short-form video
usage and its relationship to consumer outcomes in the con-
text of destination marketing or place branding. Second, the
study had to present original empirical evidence derived
from quantitative, qualitative, or mixed-methods research
designs. Third, no geographic restrictions were imposed on
the empirical setting, though each study’s geographic con -
text was recorded for subsequent analysis. Fourth, the tem -
poral boundary encompassed all studies published up to and
including the end of 2025. Fifth, only articles published in
English were considered eligible.
The following categories of publications were excluded:
literature reviews, meta-analyses, purely theoretical or
conceptual articles lacking empirical data, and studies
that did not substantively address the relationship between
short-form video content and destination-related consumer

In the screening stage, two reviewers independently
assessed titles and abstracts against the eligibility criteria
in a double-blind manner. A last-round joint reading was
used to adjudicate 9 articles that had inconclusive decisions
in the independent assessment round, until consensus was
reached. The screening phase excluded 292 records, leaving
37 articles for full-text retrieval. Three full texts could not
be sourced, reducing the pool to 34 papers for eligibility
assessment.
In the eligibility stage, the full texts of the remaining
records were retrieved and assessed independently by two
reviewers against all inclusion and exclusion criteria. One
study initially retained during screening was subsequently
excluded after full-text reading because its empirical con -
text (Twitter/X network analysis) fell outside the review’s
focus on SVPs, leaving a final corpus of 33 articles. These
records were imported into EndNote, a reference manage -
ment tool.
For each included study, the authors extracted bib -
liographic details, research objectives, methodological
approach, key findings, limitations, and proposed future
research. Extraction was performed by the lead reviewer.

development of the integrative framework presented in
the Results and Discussion sections. A thematic synthesis

Results
This systematic review synthesized evidence from 33
empirical studies published between 2022 and 2025, as
shown in Table 1. The following sections present a descrip-
tive mapping of the corpus, considering bibliometric, meth-
odological, and theoretical dimensions, and a thematic
synthesis that identifies, organizes, and critically assesses
the thematic patterns emerging from the selected articles,
integrating findings across studies.
Descriptive mapping
Publication activity has surged recently, with most of the
included studies appearing in 2025 (n = 15), compared to
2024 (n = 11), 2023 (n = 4), and 2022 (n = 3), underscoring
the field’s rapid acceleration. The sharp inflection between
2023 and 2024 likely reflects TikTok’s rapid global user
growth during and after the COVID-19 pandemic. The
studies were scattered across 27 journals, indicating that no
single outlet has consolidated ownership of the topic. The
Journal of Vacation Marketing and the Journal of Hospitality
and Tourism Insights each published three studies; Current
Issues in Tourism and the Asia Pacific Journal of Marketing
and Logistics each published two. The disciplinary homes
are predominantly tourism and hospitality, supplemented
by outlets in psychology, information systems, communica-
tions, and general social science.
Methodologically, the modal approach is a quantitative
survey (n = 23; 69.7%), followed by computational analyses
of platform data (n = 4), experiments (n = 3), mixed-methods
(n = 2), and one qualitative study. The dominant methodology
is therefore a cross-sectional online survey of convenience-
sampled platform users. China is the most common empiri-
cal setting, providing the context for 16 studies (48.5%).
India, Indonesia, Malaysia, Portugal, and the United States
each account for 2, while Chile, Italy, Pakistan, Thailand,
and Vietnam each account for 1. At the continental level,
Asia accounts for the majority of empirical settings (n = 25;
75.8%), encompassing China, India, Indonesia, Malaysia,
Pakistan, Thailand, and Vietnam. Europe contributes three
studies (9.1%), confined to Portugal and Italy, while North
America is represented solely by two U.S.-based stud -
ies (6.1%) and South America by a single Chilean study
(3.0%). Two additional studies adopted global or geographi-
cally unrestricted samples (6.1%). Africa, Oceania, and the
Middle East are entirely absent from the empirical map.
Visit or travel intention (including visit intention, travel
intention, willingness to visit, revisit intention, and impul -
sive consumption intention) dominates the corpus, appearing
as a focal outcome in 18 studies. Attitude (toward the desti-
nation, the platform, or the influencer) serves as a terminal
dependent variable in 4 studies and as a mediating outcome
in 5 others. Behavioral and purchase intention constitute a
third cluster, while eWOM and sharing intention appear in
three studies. Engagement, operationalized through both
self-report and behavioral trace data, features in five stud -
ies, primarily those employing computational methods. The
concentration on visit intention reflects the field’s strong
orientation toward predicting pre-trip behavioral outcomes,
thereby offering a narrow view of tourist behavior that pri -
oritizes intention over observed action. Observed post-visit
behavior (verified visitation, spending, loyalty) has not been
measured in any study in the corpus, and destination image
does not appear as a standalone dependent variable.
Thematic synthesis
Seven main themes, articulated through fifteen sub-themes,
emerged from the substantive findings across the 33 included
studies. The themes are organized as parallel dimensions
rather than as a strict hierarchy, because they address dis -
tinct mechanisms through which SVPs shape tourist behav-
ior: platform-level technology acceptance, source-level
credibility, content-level processing, relational parasocial
dynamics, source-format contingencies, individual-differ -
ence moderators, and platform-native engagement patterns.
Although these dimensions interact in practice (content
attributes, for instance, are often filtered through credibil -
ity perceptions), the empirical literature has predominantly
examined them separately rather than as integrated systems,
thereby limiting the ability to capture how these mecha -
nisms operate jointly within platform environments, and the
thematic structure reflects this fragmentation.

Four studies uniformly confirm that parasocial and celeb-
rity relational constructs enhance travel intention through
affective mediating mechanisms (Han and Zhang 2023;
Pham and Phan 2025; Zhu et al. 2024, 2022). Celebrity
involvement increases travel intention through object-based
and existential authenticity (Zhu et al. 2022); celebrity
attachment operates through serial mediation via parasocial
interaction and enjoyment (Zhu et al. 2024); self-influencer
congruence works through identification with place and
emotional solidarity, with place knowledge negatively mod-
erating the congruence–identification link (Han and Zhang
2023); and shared identity with LGBTQ + influencers deep-
ens emotional bonds, particularly through telepresence and
sense of presence, translating into visit intention and place
social bonding (Pham and Phan 2025). All hypothesized
paths were supported in the first three studies, while Pham
and Phan ( 2025) found mixed results: telepresence and
social interaction predicted visit intention, but interaction
orientation and social presence did not, and the sexual ori -
entation moderation was supported for selected paths only.
Despite this consistency, all four studies are cross-sectional
surveys using single-stimulus designs, and three draw on
Chinese samples, limiting causal inference and cultural
generalizability.

Theme 7. Engagement Dynamics in Platform-Native
Data
This final theme draws exclusively from computational
studies examining engagement as a behavioral trace rather
than self-reported intention. Deng et al. ( 2025), analyzing
7770 TikTok videos using machine learning and SHapley
Additive exPlanations (SHAP) interpretability, found that
destination popularity and publisher authority jointly sur -
passed all physical video attributes in explaining engage -
ment variance. Luo and Li ( 2025), using expert-weighted
multi-criteria analysis (Picture Fuzzy Best–Worst Method),
found that development performance and financial perfor -
mance were the most important first-level dimensions, with
tourist destination development, financial benefits, and con-
tent quality emerging as the highest-weighted second-level
indicators for evaluating TikTok marketing performance
of tourist attractions. Jin et al. ( 2025), through BERTopic
modeling of 67,568 comments across three Chinese inter -
net-famous cities, documented temporal decay in audience
sentiment, revealing an engagement-erosion pattern that no
survey-based study has captured. Marti-Ochoa et al. (2025)
found that UGC commands greater engagement than official
brand content on TikTok, with nano-influencers outperform-
ing macro-influencers in engagement rate, consistent with
Theme 2’s evidence but derived from behavioral trace data.
The primary strength of these studies lies in their ecological
validity, as they capture behavioral patterns in real platform
environments rather than self-reported responses. However,
they are limited by the lack of causal inference and near-
exclusive focus on Chinese platforms.
Framework of short-form video influence in
destination marketing
In the proposed framework, the seven themes derived from
the literature do not operate separately, rather, they consti -
tute a sequential and interdependent architecture, in which
multiple mechanisms operate in a coordinated rather than
isolated manner. Theme 1 (technology acceptance) consti -
tutes a gateway: platform adoption logically precedes all
downstream effects. Once on the platform, viewers encoun-
ter content that varies along two parallel dimensions: source
characteristics (Theme 2) and content attributes (Theme 3),
which jointly shape stimulus encoding. Theme 4 (parasocial
mechanisms) represents a relational layer that likely deep -
ens over repeated exposure, temporally succeeding initial
content processing. Theme 5 (content source contingencies)
operates as a structural moderator, conditioning the effec -
tiveness of content–source combinations on destination
type and advertising framing. Theme 6 (individual differ -
ences) functions as a cross-cutting moderator at every stage.
Theme 7 (platform-native engagement) captures aggregate
behavioral traces outside the individual-level chain, includ-
ing temporal dynamics that individual-level themes do not
address.
Drawing on these inter-theme relationships, we propose
a framework, visually represented in Fig. 2, organized as a
four-stage processing chain: 1) Platform entry, 2) Stimulus
encoding, 3) Psychological processing, and 4) Behavioral
Response.
Stage 1: Platform entry
Perceived usefulness and hedonic motivation emerge as
the primary entry drivers, with consistent positive effects
across six studies (Liang et al. 2025; Sharma and Arora
2024; C. Wang et al. 2022a, b; Wang et al. 2022a, b; Wang
et al. 2025; Zhou et al. 2023). Habit was significant in both
UTAUT2 implementations (Wang et al. 2025; Zhou et al.
2023). Facilitating conditions were not significant in any of
these three studies, a pattern that may reflect ceiling effects
among digitally fluent populations, though this explanation
has not been directly tested. Social influence yielded mixed
results: significant in three studies (Ramos and Ramos 2025;
Wang et al. 2025; Zhou et al. 2023), but significant only
among business travelers in a fourth (Liang et al. 2025),

Stage 2: Stimulus encoding
Upon platform entry, the viewer encounters content
shaped by its source and its features. Trustworthiness and
expertise, whether tested as separate dimensions or within
composite credibility measures, were consistently positive
predictors of visit intention (Fong et al. 2024; Li and Sun
2024; Serrano-Malebrán et al. 2025; Shoukat et al. 2023),
while physical attractiveness yielded mixed results, non-
significant in isolation (Li and Sun 2024) but significant
within composite models (Serrano-Malebrán et al. 2025).
Content attributes including informativeness, entertain -
ment, and visual richness were broadly positive across
nine studies (Bai et al. 2023; Deng et al. 2025; Huang et
al. 2024; Mahasuweerachai and Suttikun 2025; Rashidin et

et al. 2024; Zhou et al. 2024), with exceptions for musical
effect (Bai et al. 2023) and content quality as a standalone
direct predictor (Huang et al. 2024). Content source type
(UGC, PGC, PUGC) moderates this stage: source–destina -
tion type matching was demonstrated experimentally (Li
and Tu 2024) and partially corroborated computationally
(Deng et al. 2025). A conceptually related but distinct form
of matching emerged from Gao et al. (2025), who found that
anthropomorphic (first-person narrated) content was more
effective for warm destinations than for competent destina -
tions, suggesting that narration style–destination stereotype
congruence also conditions stimulus encoding. Advertising
presence disrupted encoding by reducing processing fluency
in one experiment (Zhou et al. 2024).
Stage 3: Psychological processing
The framework identifies three processing pathways
documented in the corpus. The cognitive pathway, running
from processing fluency through mental imagery to atti -
tude, was confirmed in two independent studies using dif -
ferent theoretical lenses but structurally equivalent models
(Yuan et al. 2024; Zhou et al. 2024). The affective pathway

does not appear as a terminal dependent variable anywhere
in the corpus.
Discussion and conclusion
This review asked how and through which mechanisms
SVPs shape tourists’ attitudes, eWOM, and destination-
related behavioral intentions. The synthesis of 33 empiri -
cal studies reveals that the influence of short-form video on
tourist behavior is not a single mechanism but a multi-stage
process, involving the interaction of technological, content-
related, and relational factors across stages of user engage -
ment. Platform adoption is a necessary precondition, driven
primarily by perceived utility and hedonic motivation rather
than by infrastructure support. Once engaged, viewers pro -
cess content through two parallel channels: source credibil-
ity (where trustworthiness and expertise matter more than
physical attractiveness) and content attributes (where visual
richness and informativeness outweigh auditory features),
that jointly feed cognitive, affective, and relational process-
ing pathways, which operate in parallel rather than strictly
sequentially. These pathways converge on attitudinal and
intentional outcomes, with visit intention as the near-univer-
sal endpoint. The accumulated evidence is predominantly
positive in direction but narrow in scope, limiting the ability
to generalize beyond intention-based outcomes and specific
empirical contexts: it concentrates on pre-trip intentions,
relies on cross-sectional self-reports from convenience-
sampled Asian populations, and leaves purchase behavior,
post-visit behavior, destination image, and negative mecha-
nisms largely unexamined. The integrative framework pro -
posed in Sect. “Framework of short-form video influence
in destination marketing” organizes these findings into a
four-stage processing chain that maps the field’s current
knowledge while marking its principal blind spots, thereby
providing a structured basis for future empirical testing.
Theoretical contribution
The framework proposed in this review makes several con-
tributions to the theoretical understanding of the influence
of short-form video in destination marketing and place
branding. Its primary contribution lies in connecting con -
structs that the existing literature has treated as parallel but
unrelated. Studies in this corpus have drawn on TAM and
UTAUT2 (Liang et al. 2025; Wang et al. 2022a, b; Wang
et al. 2025; Zhou et al. 2023), source credibility theory
(Fong et al. 2024; Li and Sun 2024; Pham and Phan 2025;
Serrano-Malebrán et al. 2025; Shoukat et al. 2023), S-O-R
frameworks (Bai et al. 2023; De Yusa et al. 2025; Maha-
suweerachai and Suttikun 2025; Rashidin et al. 2025; Ser-
rano-Malebrán et al. 2025), mental imagery and information
processing theories (Yuan et al. 2024; Zhou et al. 2024), and
parasocial interaction theory (Pham and Phan 2025; Zhu
et al. 2024), but most studies have drawn on only one or
two of these traditions, thereby limiting the ability to cap -
ture how these mechanisms operate jointly within platform
environments, particularly under conditions of continuous
and algorithmically curated exposure. Notable exceptions
include Fong et al. (2024), who integrated TAM, social cog-
nitive theory, and the source credibility model, and Pham
and Phan ( 2025), who combined parasocial interaction,
source credibility, and telepresence theory within a single
model. Even these multi-theory studies, however, covered
only a subset of the theoretical landscape; none have linked
technology acceptance, content processing, relational
mechanisms, and platform-level dynamics within a unified
architecture. By arranging these theoretical inputs into a
sequential architecture, from platform entry through stim -
ulus encoding and psychological processing to behavioral
response, the framework makes explicit what has remained

implicit by clarifying how these mechanisms are temporally
and functionally related: that technology acceptance oper -
ates as a gateway condition for all subsequent persuasion
effects, and that source credibility and content processing
are parallel but interacting channels rather than competing
explanations.
A second contribution concerns the S-O-R framework.
The five S-O-R studies in the corpus (Bai et al. 2023; De
Yusa et al. 2025; Mahasuweerachai and Suttikun 2025;
Rashidin et al. 2025; Serrano-Malebrán et al. 2025) verify
the stimulus, organism, response chain, but have applied
it exclusively in its positive valence: favorable stimuli,
approach-oriented organism states, and behavioural inten -
tions (visit or purchase). Only one study introduced a dis -
ruptive stimulus: advertising intrusion, reducing processing
fluency (Zhou et al. 2024). Negative organism states such
as skepticism, destination fatigue, or cognitive overload
remain untested. This positive-valence asymmetry is not
inherent to the S-O-R model, which originally theorized that
approach and avoidance responses are symmetrical behav -
ioral outcomes (Mehrabian and Russell 1974), but rather
reflects how it has been applied in this domain.
Third, the review provides qualified support for the tech-
nology acceptance perspective. TAM’s core constructs were
significant across seven studies, and UTAUT2’s hedonic
motivation and habit were supported in all studies that tested
them. However, facilitating conditions were non-significant
in every UTAUT2 implementation (Liang et al. 2025; Wang
et al. 2025; Zhou et al. 2023), a pattern plausibly reflect -
ing ceiling effects among digitally fluent populations for
whom infrastructure support is no longer differentiating, a
context not anticipated when UTAUT2 was developed for
enterprise technology (Venkatesh et al. 2012). Additionally,
Fong et al. ( 2024) found that neither perceived usefulness
nor ease of use directly predicted visit intention; however,
self-efficacy fully mediated only the perceived usefulness–
visit intention path, while the ease of use–visit intention
path remained non-significant, even indirectly. This sug -
gests that perceived usefulness may function as an enabler
of confidence to act rather than as a direct antecedent of
behavioral intention, indicating a more indirect and context-
dependent role than traditionally assumed, whereas ease of
use may lack explanatory power altogether in destination-
specific contexts.
Fourth, regarding source credibility theory, trustworthi -
ness and expertise are robust predictors across five studies,
but attractiveness shows inconsistent effects: non-significant
in isolation (Li and Sun 2024) yet significant within compos-
ite models (Serrano-Malebrán et al. 2025). This pattern is
consistent with the Elaboration Likelihood Model (Petty and
Cacioppo 1986): attractiveness may function as a peripheral
cue effective only alongside substantive credibility signals.
The finding that micro- and/or nano-influencers outperform
macro- and/or mega-influencers (Bou Aragones et al. 2025;
Han and Zhang 2023; Marti-Ochoa et al. 2025) supports
this interpretation, with qualitative evidence suggesting that
macro-influencer self-commodification fragments destina -
tion image (Tham et al. 2024), whereas smaller creators
preserve destination centrality, as these influencers are per -
ceived as authentic and relatable, qualities associated with
central-route processing.
Fifth, the mediational evidence advances understand -
ing of how content effects translate into behavioral out -
comes. The convergence of (Yuan et al. 2024) and (Zhou
et al. 2024) on a serial mediation chain, from content play -
fulness or processing fluency through mental imagery to
attitude and intention, is notable for two reasons: the stud -
ies employed different theoretical lenses (mental imagery
theory and information processing theory, respectively) yet
arrived at structurally equivalent models, and one of the two
(Yuan et al. 2024) replicated the finding across a survey and
an experiment with AI-generated video stimuli. This cross-
theoretical convergence and partial replication constitute
the strongest mediational evidence in the corpus and sug -
gest that mental imagery may be a domain-specific mediator
in video-based destination marketing, warranting the same
systematic attention as constructs such as perceived risk
or satisfaction have received in more established tourism
research streams (Loureiro et al. 2022).
Finally, the framework distinguishes individual-level
processing from platform-level engagement dynamics.
Three platform-native computational studies (Deng et al.
2025; Jin et al. 2025; Marti-Ochoa et al. 2025), supple -
mented by a mixed-methods study (Gao et al. 2025) and
an expert-based evaluation (Luo and Li 2025), show that
engagement at scale is driven more by contextual factors
(destination popularity, publisher authority, content–audi -
ence alignment) than by audiovisual production quality,
suggesting that algorithmic curation introduces aggregate
patterns not reducible to individual processing, highlight -
ing the need to complement individual-level models with
platform-level perspectives. Jin et al. ( 2025) further docu -
mented temporal decay in audience sentiment across suc -
cessive viral city cycles, pointing toward a dynamic view of
destination marketing effectiveness that the dominant cross-
sectional paradigm cannot capture.
Managerial implications
Before outlining specific recommendations, it is important
to acknowledge a boundary in the evidence. The manage -
rial guidance that follows is derived predominantly from
studies that measure self-reported attitudes, intentions, and
engagement rather than observed tourist behavior, such

as verified visitation, booking conversion, or destination
spending. Only one study in the corpus (Luo and Li 2025)
provided performance-level metrics linking platform activ -
ity to offline outcomes. The recommendations should there-
fore be interpreted as empirically informed directions for
campaign design and content strategy, pending validation
through field experiments or longitudinal tracking that can
establish return on investment.
Managerial recommendations map directly onto the
four stages of the integrative framework. Stage 1 (Platform
Entry): indicates that first-contact effectiveness relies on the
combined weight of perceived usefulness, hedonic grati -
fication, and habitual engagement (Wang et al. 2022a, b;
Wang et al. 2025; Zhou et al. 2023). DMOs should therefore
invest in friction-free onboarding, fast loading, single-swipe
navigation, and vertical-first aspect ratios, while embedding
playful micro-features (such as polls, duets, and AR stick -
ers) that sustain the hedonic motivation shown to drive con-
tinued platform use. Given that facilitating conditions were
consistently non-significant across all UTAUT2 studies in
the corpus (Liang et al. 2025; Wang et al. 2025; Zhou et al.
2023), resources allocated to technical support infrastruc -
ture may yield diminishing returns relative to investments
in content entertainment value.
Stage 2 (Stimulus Encoding); high-definition, place-
specific visuals enhance destination familiarity and involve-
ment (Bai et al. 2023), and these effects are amplified when
paired with trusted messengers whose expertise or local
embeddedness align with the destination type. Campaign
briefs should specify production standards (color palette,
soundtrack, shot length) and endorser criteria (micro-influ -
encer credibility, resident identity) rather than relying solely
on follower counts; such alignment is especially critical for
sustainability narratives where authenticity outweighs reach
(Bou Aragones et al. 2025). The source–destination match-
ing effect documented in this review (Li and Tu 2024) offers
a particularly actionable guideline: UGC and PUGC should
be prioritized for hedonic destinations, PGC and PUGC
for utilitarian destinations, and PUGC as the most versa -
tile source across both types. Additionally, the finding that
advertising intrusion disrupts the processing chain (Zhou
et al. 2024) cautions against embedding overt endorsement
inserts within destination videos; marketers should favor
organic integration over interruptive placement.
Stage 3 (Psychological Processing): demonstrates that
processing fluency and playfulness stimulate vivid mental
imagery, which converts positive attitudes into visit inten -
tions (Yuan et al. 2024; Zhou et al. 2024). Marketers must
therefore guard cognitive ease: avoid mid-stream endorse -
ment inserts, keep captions concise, and synchronize audio
beats with visual cuts to reduce cognitive load. The rela -
tional pathway documented in this stage further suggests
that DMOs can amplify parasocial connection by featuring
creators whose identity aligns with the target audience’s val-
ues; a strategy shown to be effective for both micro-influ -
encers in sustainability contexts (Bou Aragones et al. 2025)
and LGBTQ + influencers reaching community-specific
audiences (Pham and Phan 2025).
When video fluency and relational connection are pre -
served, Stage 4 (Behavioral Response) indicates that atti -
tudinal gains cascade into eWOM, purchase intentions,
and engagement behaviors (Serrano-Malebrán et al. 2025),
with platform-level evaluation frameworks confirming that
propagation metrics and user perception are key perfor -
mance indicators for TikTok marketing (Luo and Li 2025).
Embedding trackable elements, such as geo-tagged offers,
QR codes, or promo links, within fluent videos enables attri-
bution and validates budget allocation.
The cross-cutting moderating layers identified in the
framework highlight two additional strategic consider -
ations. First, generational segmentation matters: Generation
Y exhibits stronger attitude-to-intention conversion than
Generation Z (Serrano-Malebrán et al. 2025), suggesting
that campaigns targeting Millennials can rely more heavily
on attitudinal persuasion, while those targeting Generation
Z may require more direct behavioral triggers such as chal-
lenges, hashtag participation, or gamified interactions. Sec-
ond, the platform-native engagement evidence (Deng et al.
2025; Jin et al. 2025; Marti-Ochoa et al. 2025) signals the
need for continuous, data-driven content optimization. UGC
outperforms brand posts in engagement metrics (Marti-
Ochoa et al. 2025), and destination popularity and publisher
authority are stronger predictors of engagement than audio-
visual production quality (Deng et al. 2025). DMOs should
therefore curate fan-video spotlights, provide downloadable
B-roll footage, and launch hashtag challenges to seed the
self-reinforcing UGC spiral while maintaining thematic
coherence (Tham et al. 2024). Since social interaction and
informativeness are key drivers of users’ positive attitudes
(Wang et al. 2022a, b) and performance metrics such as fol-
lower growth are critical for evaluation (Luo and Li 2025),
marketers should prioritize and monitor these engagement
signals. By refining content to maximize these specific
interactions, DMOs effectively feed the recommendation
engines that amplify visibility. These actions operationalize
the framework’s four-stage architecture, translating schol -
arly propositions into a practical playbook for digital desti -
nation marketing.
Limitations of the study
Several limitations of this review should be acknowledged.
The search was restricted to two databases, Scopus and WoS.
Although these are the most widely used multidisciplinary

indexes in social science and are recommended for sys -
tematic reviews in marketing scholarship (Palmatier et
al. 2018), they do not index all outlets. The exclusion of
conference proceedings and book chapters, while justified
by the need for a peer-review quality threshold, may have
omitted emerging work that has not yet been published in
journal form. The restriction to English-language publica -
tions likely introduced a linguistic bias. However, given
that 48.5% of empirical settings in the corpus are Chi -
nese, and that a substantial body of scholarship on Douyin
is published in Mandarin, the review may underrepresent
findings from the single most active research context. Simi-
larly, studies published in other languages may have been
excluded despite their relevance. The thematic synthesis is
inherently interpretive: the themes and sub-themes identi -
fied in this review emerged from the authors’ reading of the
corpus, and different reviewers might have drawn theme
boundaries differently.
Finally, the conclusions drawn from this review are con-
strained by limitations in the underlying evidence base.
The corpus is dominated by cross-sectional survey designs
(23 of 33 studies) relying on convenience samples (19 of
33) and self-reported Likert scales (27 of 33), which lim -
its causal inference and external validity. Only three studies
used experimental designs (Bou Aragones et al. 2025; Li
and Tu 2024; Zhou et al. 2024), and none employed longi -
tudinal panels, meaning that the temporal ordering of con -
structs, for instance, whether parasocial attachment drives
visit intention or the reverse, cannot be established from the
available evidence. The geographic concentration in China
(48.5% of studies), where all research operated within the
Douyin ecosystem, further constrains the transferability of
findings to TikTok’s international version, which differs
in algorithmic architecture, content moderation, and user
demographics. These methodological patterns in the cor -
pus do not invalidate the thematic findings reported here,
but they define the boundaries within which those findings
should be interpreted.
Future research agenda
The gaps and contradictions identified in this review point
toward a structured research agenda organized around four
priorities.
The first concerns under-researched themes: Visit inten -
tion dominates the corpus as the terminal outcome, mea -
sured through self-reported Likert scales in 27 of 33 studies.
No study has verified whether these stated intentions con -
vert into observed tourist behavior, such as actual book -
ings, verified visitation, or destination spending. The four
articles, including computational studies, that capture real
behavioral traces (Deng et al. 2025; Gao et al. 2025; Jin
et al. 2025; Marti-Ochoa et al. 2025), measure platform
engagement (likes, comments, shares), not tourist behav -
ior at destinations, though Luo and Li ( 2025) provided an
expert-based evaluation framework that uniquely incorpo -
rates offline performance metrics such as tourism revenue
and tourist growth. Post-visit outcomes such as satisfac -
tion, loyalty, and repeat visitation are similarly absent. Des-
tination image does not appear as a standalone dependent
variable anywhere in the corpus. Negative processing mech-
anisms (skepticism, cognitive overload, destination fatigue)
and the eWOM feedback loop from post-consumption shar-
ing back to content exposure remain entirely unexamined
despite their theoretical plausibility within the S-O-R and
parasocial frameworks already prevalent in the corpus.
The second priority involves contradictory findings:
Physical attractiveness yielded inconsistent effects across
studies, and the conditions under which perceived useful -
ness operates directly versus indirectly (through self-effi -
cacy) on visit intention remain unclear. These contradictions
likely reflect meaningful boundary conditions related to
processing mode, platform context, and audience character-
istics that targeted experimental designs could disentangle.
Third, methodological gaps constrain what the field can
conclude: The dominance of cross-sectional, convenience-
sampled surveys (23 of 33 studies) precludes causal infer -
ence and limits generalizability. Pre-registered experiments,
longitudinal panel designs tracking viewers from exposure
through booking to post-visit evaluation, and multi-platform
comparative studies (Douyin vs. TikTok international vs.
Reels) would substantially strengthen the evidentiary foun -
dation. The three processing pathways identified in Stage 3
of the framework (cognitive, affective, and relational) have
never been tested jointly in a single model; doing so would
reveal whether they complement, compete, or substitute for
one another.
Fourth, contextual extensions are needed. Africa, Ocea -
nia, and the Middle East are entirely absent from the empiri-
cal map, and Latin America is represented by a single study.
Cross-cultural replications would test whether the frame -
work’s relationships are universal or culturally bounded.
