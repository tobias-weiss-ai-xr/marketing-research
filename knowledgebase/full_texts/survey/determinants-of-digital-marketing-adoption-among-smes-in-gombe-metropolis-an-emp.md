ABSTRACT
Digital marketing adoption remains uneven among small and medium enterprises (SMEs) in Nigeria's northern
regions, despite its proven potential to enhance market reach and competitive advantage. This study examines the
combined influence of technological infrastructure, owner-manager digital literacy, and perceived market pressure
on the intensity of digital m arketing adoption among SMEs in Gombe Metropolis. Anchored in the Technology -
Organisation-Environment (TOE) framework and supplemented by Diffusion of Innovation theory, a quantitative
cross-sectional survey was administered to 312 SME owner -managers selec ted through stratified random
sampling. Data were analysed using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling (PLS -SEM). Results
indicate that all three predictors significantly and positively influence digital marketing adoption intensity, with
owner-manager digital literacy emerging as the strongest determinant (β = 0.41, p < 0.001), followed by perceived
market pressure (β = 0.29, p < 0.001) and technological infrastructure (β = 0.18, p = 0.021). The model explained
54% of the variance in adoption  intensity (R² = 0.54), demonstrating robust explanatory power. The findings
underscore that human capital development may yield greater adoption returns than infrastructure -only
interventions in resource-constrained contexts. The study contributes to TOE literature by contextualising digital
adoption dynamics in northern Nigeria and offers actionable insights for policymakers, SME support agencies,
and telecommunications providers seeking to foster inclusive digital transformation. Recommendations include
mobile-first digital literacy programmes, targeted SME data bundles, and peer -led mentorship clusters to
accelerate sustainable digital marketing uptake.
Keywords: digital marketing adoption, SMEs, technological infrastructure, digital literacy, market pressure.
1. INTRODUCTION

Digital marketing adoption intensity -the frequency and
strategic integration of social media marketing, email
campaigns, search engine optimisation (SEO), and paid
advertising-has emerged as a critical driver of
competitive advantage for small and medium enterprises
(SMEs) in emerging economies (Eze et al., 2023). In
Nigeria, where SMEs contribute over 48% of national
GDP and account for approximately 96% of businesses,
the ability to leverage digital channels directly influences
market reach, customer enga gement, and revenue
growth (Adepoju et al., 2024). Yet, despite the proven
benefits of digital marketing, adoption remains
inconsistent, particularly in northern regions like Gombe
Metropolis, where infrastructural deficits and capacity
gaps persist (Adamu & Ibrahim, 2023).
Stakeholders-including the Small and Medium
Enterprises Development Agency of Nigeria

these gaps by: (1) applying the Technology -
Organisation-Environment (TOE) framework to
simultaneously assess technological infrastructure,
owner-manager digital literacy, and perceived market
pressure; (2) employing Partial Least Squares Structural
Equation Modelling  (PLS-SEM) to test hypothesised
relationships with greater rigour; and (3) generating
context-specific insights for policymakers and SME
support organisations in Nigeria's underserved northern
regions.

2. LITERATURE REVIEW AND HYPOTHESES
DEVELOPMENT
2.1 Conceptual Review
Digital Marketing Adoption Intensity  refers to the
extent to which SMEs systematically deploy digital
channels- including social media, email marketing,
search engine optimisation (SEO), and paid advertising-
as core components of their ma rketing strategy
(Kingsnorth, 2019). Unlike binary adoption measures
(adopt vs. not adopt), intensity captures the frequency of
use, strategic integration, and resource allocation,
thereby offering a more nuanced understanding of digital
maturity (Adepoju et al., 2024).
Technological Infrastructure  encompasses the
reliability of internet connectivity, availability of
compatible digital devices, and access to user -friendly
online platforms (Adamu & Ibrahim, 2023). In Gombe
Metropolis, erratic power supply an d high data costs
frequently disrupt online operations, directly
constraining SMEs' ability to maintain a consistent
digital presence (Dataprojectng, 2024).
Owner-Manager Digital Literacy  reflects the
knowledge, self-efficacy, and prior experience of SME
leaders in using digital tools for business purposes
(Suroso et al., 2021). Even when adequate infrastructure
is available, low digital confidence among owner -
managers significantly dampens adoption intentions
(ResearchGate, 2023).
Perceived Market Pressure  captures the extent to
which SMEs feel compelled to adopt digital marketing
due to competitor activity, evolving customer
expectations, and industry -wide digital trends
(Iranmanesh et al., 2019). In Gombe's competitive retail
and service sectors, early-adopter SMEs often gain first-
mover advantages, intensifying pressure on laggards to
digitalise (Bruce et al., 2023).
2.2 Empirical Review
Technological Infrastructure → Digital Marketing
Adoption
Empirical studies consistently affirm that robust
technological infrastructure positively influences digital
marketing adoption. Adepoju et al. (2024), in a Lagos -
based study using PLS -SEM, found that perceived ease
of use (β = 0.156, p < 0.05) and perceived usefulness (β
= 0.153, p < 0.05) of digital tools significantly predicted
adoption intensity. Similarly, Adamu and Ibrahim
(2023) reported that SMEs in Adamawa State with stable
internet access were 3.2 times more likely to run regular
social media campaigns. However, these studies
predominantly focus on southern  Nigeria, where
infrastructure is relatively more developed. The
applicability of these findings to Gombe -characterised
by lower internet penetration (<30%) and frequent

Owner-Manager Digital Lite racy → Digital

Digital literacy among SME leaders emerges as a critical
enabler. Suroso et al. (2021) demonstrated that owner -

significantly higher self-efficacy and were more likely to
experiment with email marketing and SEO. In Gombe,
however, over 67% of SME owners report limited
computer knowledge, which directly hinders their
willingness to adopt digital tools (ResearchGate, 2023).
While studies like Eze et al. (2023) highlight literacy as
a barrier, few have operationalised it through multi -
dimensional indicators (training exposure, self -efficacy,
prior experience) within a structural model -creating a
methodological gap this study addresses.
Perceived Market Pressure → Digital Marketing
Adoption
Competitive and customer-driven pressures significantly
motivate digital adoption. Iranmanesh et al. (2019) found
that SMEs perceiving high competitor digital presence
were 2.7 times more likely to invest in paid advertising.
In Nigeria's retail s ector, Nwoko and Obi (2024)
observed that customer expectations for online ordering
and social media engagement directly influenced SMEs'
adoption of WhatsApp Business and Instagram Shops.
Yet, in Gombe's less digitally saturated market, the
salience of such pressures may differ. Existing literature
rarely examines how market pressure interacts with
infrastructural constraints-a contextual gap this research
fills.
While prior studies validate the individual relevance of
technological, organisational, and environmental factors,
three limitations persist: (1) limited focus on northern
Nigerian contexts like Gombe; (2) fragmented
examination of variables without testing their combined
effects; and (3) overreliance on cross -sectional surveys
without advanced mod elling techniques (Bruce et al.,
2023; Eze et al., 2023).
2.3 Theoretical Framework
This study is anchored on the Technology-Organisation-
Environment (TOE) framework (Tornatzky & Fleischer,
1990), which posits that technology adoption is shaped
by three contextual dimensions: technological readiness,
organisational capacity, and environmental pressures.
The TOE framework is particularly suited to this
research because it accommodates the multi -level
determinants of digital marketing adoption among
resource-constrained SMEs (Adepoju et al., 2024;
Maroufkhani et al., 2020).
To complement TOE, this study draws on the Diffusion
of Innovation (DOI) theory (Rogers, 2003), which
explains how perceived attributes of an innovation- such
as relative advantage, compati bility, complexity,
trialability, and observability - influence adoption
decisions. DOI is especially relevant for understanding
why SME owner-managers may embrace or resist digital
marketing based on their subjective assessment of its
benefits and ease of use.
The integration of TOE and DOI provides a robust
theoretical foundation for this study. Specifically, TOE
supplies the structural lens for categorising determinants
into technological, organisational, and environmental
factors, while DOI enriches the analysis by explaining
the perceptual mechanisms through which owner -
managers evaluate and decide to adopt digital marketing.
Together, these frameworks enable a comprehensive
examination of how infrastructural enablers (TOE -
Technology), owner -manager capa bilities (TOE -
Organisation / DOI -Adopter Characteristics), and
competitive dynamics (TOE-Environment) jointly shape
digital marketing adoption intensity among SMEs in
Gombe Metropolis.
2.4 Hypotheses Development
Based on the TOE framework and empirical evi dence,
the following hypotheses are proposed:
H₁: Technological infrastructure (internet reliability,
device access, platform availability) has a significant
positive effect on digital marketing adoption intensity
among SMEs in Gombe Metropolis.
H₂: Owner-manager digital literacy (training exposure,
self-efficacy, prior tech experience) has a significant
positive effect on digital marketing adoption intensity
among SMEs in Gombe Metropolis.
H₃: Perceived market pressure (competitor digital
presence, customer online expectations, industry trends)
has a significant positive effect on digital marketing
adoption intensity among SMEs in Gombe Metropolis.

3. METHODOLOGY
This study adopted a quantitative, cross-sectional survey
design to examine the determinants of digital marketing
adoption intensity among small and medium enterprises
(SMEs) in Gombe Metropolis, Nigeria. A stratified
random sampling technique was employed to select 350
SMEs across four key sectors - retail, services, agro -
processing, and hospitality - ensuring representation of
micro, small, and medium enterprises as defined by the
Small and Medium Enterprises Development Agency of
Nigeria (SMEDAN).
Data were collected using a structured questionnaire
administered to owner -managers of the sampled SMEs.
The questionnaire employed 5 -point Likert scales
(ranging from 1 = "Strongly Disagree" to 5 = "Strongly
Agree"), with items adapted from validated instruments
in Adepoju et al. (2024) and Eze et al. (2023). The
dependent variable- digital marketing adoption intensity-
was measured through four frequency-of-use indicators:
social media marketing, email campaigns, search engine
optimisation (SEO), and paid advertising. Independent
variables were operationalised as follows: technological
infrastructure (three items: internet reliability, device
access, platform  availability); owner -manager digital
literacy (three items: training exposure, self -efficacy,
prior technology experience); and perceived market
pressure (three items: competitor digital activity,
customer online expectations, industry trends).
Data analysis was performed using Partial Least Squares
Structural Equation Modelling (PLS -SEM) with
SmartPLS 4 software. This technique was selected for its
robustness in handling complex models with smaller
sample sizes and its suitability for exploratory research
(Hair et al., 2019). The measurement model was assessed
for reliability (Cronbach's alpha > 0.7, composite
reliability > 0.7) and validity (average variance extracted
> 0.5, HTMT < 0.85). Following confirmation of the
measurement model, the structural model was evaluated
using path coefficients, t -statistics (via bootstrapping
with 5,000 subsamples), coefficient of determination
(R²), and predictive relevance (Q²).
Ethical approval was obtained from the Gombe State
University Research Ethics Committee. Inf ormed
consent was secured from all participants prior to data
collection, and confidentiality of responses was
guaranteed throughout the research process.

4.4 Discussion of Findings
The findings affirm that technological infrastructure,
owner-manager digital literacy, and perceived market
pressure collectively shape digital marketing adoption
among SMEs in Gombe Metropolis, consistent with the
TOE framework (Tornatzky & Fleischer, 1990). Owner-
manager digital literacy exhibited the strongest
influence, corroborating Suroso et al. (2021) and Eze et
al. (2023), who argue that human capital often outweighs
technological enablers in resource -constrained settings.
In Gombe, where formal digital training remains scarce,
owner-managers with prior exposure to digital tools
demonstrated greater confidence in deploying social
media campaigns and email marketing -suggesting that
capacity-building interventions may yield higher returns
than infrastructure-only approaches.
Technological infrastructure, while significant, showed
a modest effect (β = 0.18), reflecting the nuanced reality
in northern Nigeria: even when devices and platforms are
available, erratic power supply and high data costs limit
consistent usage (Adamu & Ibrahim, 2023; CISD
Nigeria, 2026). This aligns with Dataprojectng (2024),
which noted that SMEs in Gombe often adopt
"intermittent digital strategies" -posting on social media
only when connectivity permits -rather than sustained
campaigns.
Perceived market pressure significantly motivated
adoption (β = 0.29), supporting Iranmanesh et al. (2019)
and Nwoko and Obi (2024). In Gombe's competitive
retail sector, SMEs observing rivals gaining customers
via WhatsApp Business or Instagram Sh ops reported
heightened urgency to digitise. However, this pressure
was moderated by infrastructural realities: as one

respondent noted, "I see competitors online, but when
my network fails during peak hours, I lose momentum."
This interaction underscores the need for integrated
policy responses that address both demand -side
incentives and supply-side enablers.
Contrary to studies in southern Nigeria (Adepoju et al.,
2024), sector and enterprise size were non -significant
controls, suggesting that in Gombe, digital adoption is
driven more by individual owner-manager attributes and
immediate market signals than by firm -level resources.
This contextual insight advances the TOE literature by
highlighting how environmental constraints can
attenuate organisational  determinants in underserved
regions.

5. CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS
This study empirically demonstrates that digital
marketing adoption intensity among SMEs in Gombe
Metropolis is significantly shaped by the interplay of
technological infrastructure, owner-manager digital
literacy, and perceived market pressure, with digital
literacy emerging as the most potent driver. While
infrastructural challenges persist, the strong effect of
human capital suggests that targeted capacity -building-
such as practical, mobile-first digital skills training-may
accelerate adoption more effectively than hardware -
centric interventions alone. Furthermore, the salience of
market pressure indicates that showcasing local success
stories and fostering peer learning networks could create
positive adoption cascades. These findings extend the
TOE framework by contextualising its application to
northern Nigeria's unique socio -technical landscape,
where infrastructural deficits interact dynamically with
organisational readiness and competitive dynamics.
Based on these insights, three actionable
recommendations emerge: First, SMEDAN and Gombe
State's Ministry of Commerce should co-design modular,
vernacular-language digital literacy programmes
focused on high -impact, low -bandwidth tool s
(WhatsApp Business, Facebook Lite) to enhance owner-
manager self -efficacy. Second, telecommunications
providers, in partnership with state agencies, should
prioritise "SME data bundles" with off -peak discounts
and reliability guarantees to mitigate infra structure-
related adoption barriers. Third, local business
associations should establish digital mentorship clusters
where early-adopter SMEs share practical strategies for
leveraging market pressure into sustainable digital
engagement. Future research cou ld longitudinally track
adoption trajectories post -intervention and explore the
moderating role of gender in digital literacy outcomes,
given the underrepresentation of female-owned SMEs in
this sample. Ultimately, unlocking digital marketing
potential in Gombe requires a synergistic approach that
empowers people, enables infrastructure, and amplifies
market signals -turning constraint into catalyst for
inclusive digital growth.
