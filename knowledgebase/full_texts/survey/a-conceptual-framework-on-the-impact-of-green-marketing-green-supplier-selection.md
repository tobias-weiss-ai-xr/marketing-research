Abstract
With the rise of global environmental issues, it has become imperative for
businesses to address environmental sensitivity at a strategic level. One key
concept highlighted in the literature in this context is green marketing strategies.
Green marketing is a marketing approach shaped by a sense of environmental
responsibility , aiming to develop and offer sustainable products. The growing
consumer demand for eco-friendly products has driven companies to pursue
green product innovation. However, the success of this process is closely linked
not only to the company’s internal dynamics but also to the actors within the
supply chain. At this point, selecting green suppliers becomes a key factor.
Identifying materials and packaging that minimize environmental impacts
during the early stages of product development requires close collaboration
with suppliers. Research indicates that green marketing strategies encourage
the selection of environmentally responsible suppliers and directly contribute to
green innovation processes. As a result, businesses gain a competitive advantage
while also enhancing their overall corporate performance. This study will
examine, within the literature, the effects of green marketing strategies on green
supplier selection and green product innovation, as well as the contributions
of green innovation to business performance and competitive advantage.

1. Introduction
Today , the growing environmental threats such as the rapid depletion of natural
resources, global climate change, environmental pollution, and biodiversity
loss are compelling businesses to integrate environmental sustainability into
their strategic decision-making. Environmental regulations enacted at both
the global and national levels, along with growing consumer environmental
awareness, are encouraging companies to reevaluate their traditional business
practices and adopt more environmentally friendly approaches. In light of these
developments, corporate environmental practices such as green marketing,
green innovation, and green supply chain management which have made
their way onto businesses’ agendas have become indispensable for companies
seeking a sustainable competitive advantage in today’s competitive landscape.
Green marketing is not merely about offering products that do not harm
the environment; it is a comprehensive strategy that involves minimizing
environmental impacts throughout the entire process from design and
production to packaging and distribution. The success of these strategies
largely depends on selecting business partners with environmental awareness
throughout the supply chain and developing innovative, eco-friendly products.
In this context, selecting green suppliers is a crucial step toward reducing
environmental impacts from the very first stages of the production process.
Suppliers’ compliance with environmental regulations is critical for businesses
to fulfill their environmental responsibilities. Additionally , these selections
help businesses improve resource efficiency , avoid legal penalties, and enhance
their credibility with consumers.
Furthermore, green innovation initiatives create added value for businesses
both economically and environmentally by facilitating the development of
eco-friendly products and processes. By adding an environmental dimension
to product and process innovation, these approaches not only enhance
environmental sensitivity but also enable businesses to expand their market
share, reach new customer segments, and strengthen their competitive
position. Today’s consumers prefer companies that engage in environmentally
responsible production, thereby transforming environmental innovation into
a tool for competitive advantage.
In light of all these developments, the primary objective of this study is
to evaluate the effects of green marketing strategies on businesses’ selection
of green suppliers and green innovation activities, and to analyze how these
strategies translate into business performance and competitive advantage.
A review of the relevant literature reveals that these relationships are mostly
addressed in a fragmented manner, and the holistic connections between

2. Conceptual Framework
2.1. Green Marketing
The concept of green marketing entered our lives in 1969. This concept was
introduced into the literature as a marketing technique related to ecological
factors and the “greening” of various aspects of traditional marketing (V an
Nguyen & Nguyen, 2016, p. 116). Developed in 1970, the concept of green
marketing emphasizes the design, development, pricing, and distribution of
products that do not harm the environment (Tabavar et al., 2021, p. 120).
Green marketing is defined as a process that identifies and meets consumer
and social-environmental needs profitably and sustainably (Chan, 2013, p.
443). The Business Dictionary , however, defines the concept as efforts to
shape consumer attitudes toward a brand. These adjustments are gradually
influenced by a company’s activities that alter the ecosystem’s characteristics
and indicate a level of concern for society . On the other hand, it can be
explained as the promotion of products that do not harm the environment
or offer environmental benefits (FuiY eng & Y azdanifard, 2015, p. 17). Green
marketing is defined as a company’s commitment to developing recyclable,
easily separable packaging, implementing initiatives to minimize pollution,
and offering eco-friendly products and services that enhance energy efficiency .
Emphasizing this concept can help minimize a product’s environmental impact
throughout its lifecycle, including material procurement, production, sales,
consumption, and disposal (Wu & Lin, 2016, p. 144).
In recent years, consumers have turned toward environmentalism.
Consequently , they expect companies to place as much special emphasis on
the environment as they do on the interests and needs of their stakeholders.
This is why green marketing is one of the hottest topics in today’s business
world. Many companies have begun to recognize the impact of green marketing
on their competitive positions. Traditional marketing focuses on consumer
demand but does not account for social welfare and environmental issues.
In the concept of green marketing, however, the environment is becoming
increasingly vital and significant for every aspect of life, from both the
customer’s and the producer’s perspectives. This issue is driving companies

International concerns regarding environmental sustainability and the
uncertainty of climate change are compelling all companies to confront the

strategy is a corporate marketing strategy that aims to meet customer needs
while minimizing environmental impacts. This strategy is successful because
consumers are willing to purchase products that are free of toxins and cause
minimal pollution and environmental impact (Sugandini et al., 2020, p. 81).
2.1.1. Green Marketing Mix
2.1.1.1.Product
The product encompasses the entire service package provided to buyers
during the trading process. A product’s value, packaging, purpose, and form
represent the most fundamental elements within the product mix (Geap et
al., 2018, p. 3). The product is what is being sold. It signifies more than just
a simple set of tangible features. It is characterised as a complex bundle of
benefits that meets customer needs (Ivy , 2008, p. 289). A product consists
of certain goods or services that a firm offers in the market. A product is
defined as anything—or any object— that can be presented to customers
to attract their attention, encourage purchase, or facilitate consumption,
and that fulfils certain desires or needs. It includes physical objects, services,
people, places, organizations, or ideas (Khan, 2014, p. 98). Within the
marketing mix, a product is defined as something introduced to the market

to be noticed, purchased, or used, and capable of addressing a need. A product
may include a physical object, a service, a place, an organization, or even an
idea (Pourdehghan, 2015, p. 49).
To increase a product’s value, “green” marketers make environmental claims
about the product’s attributes, the company’s policies, and its marketing or
production processes. A primary objective should be to lessen the negative
effects on society and the environment so that safer products may be made
available to buyers (Giantari & Sukaatmadja, 2021, p. 194). Produced without
harming the environment, “green” goods use eco-friendly technologies. More
people are buying eco-friendly goods because they care about the environment
(Sinambela et al., 2022, p. 157).
2.1.1.2.Price
Price is one of the most important concepts in the marketing mix. Scholars
describe it as the foundation of the elements that ensure market sustainability ,
increasing not only profits but also market share. Furthermore, price is not
only a primary competitive factor that affects a firm’s sales and profitability
ratios but also a marketing mix tool that adapts easily to ecological differences
(Išoraitė, 2016, p. 30). The amount a buyer must pay to acquire a product
is known as its price. Dynamic pricing is necessary to account for the many
variables that affect product prices, so prices can and do fluctuate quickly .
Important factors to consider when setting prices include product cost,
marketing approach, distribution costs, advertising expenditures, and market
price fluctuations (Singh, 2012, p. 42). In the marketing mix, pricing is king
because it determines how much money a company makes. Everything else,
including product, distribution, and promotion, is considered a variable
cost by the business. The price reflects the supply and demand for existing
products. Raw materials for green products are often expensive, and the cost
of purchasing these materials for production ultimately increases the product’s
price (Padhy & Vishnoi, 2015, p. 35).
Items with an emphasis on sustainability often have a “green price” tag.
Because of their higher environmental impact, some products may be more
expensive than competitors. Some buyers will pay more for a product if they
think it will improve their lives. Green product prices may be higher than
regular ones due to the costs associated with enforcing environmental policy
measures required by the company’s policies and other applicable programs.
According to Karanarathna et al. (2020, p. 130), one of the most crucial
elements of green marketing is green pricing. Market segment, manufacturing
costs, product/service diversity , sales volume, level of competition, and other

2.1.1.3.Distribution
The ease with which a product can be obtained by consumers is reflected
in the distribution channel, which is the route that the product takes to reach
those consumers. All three components of the marketing mix—product, price,
and promotion—use this component as a tool. Consumers would be unable to
purchase goods if they were not distributed. The term “distribution channel”
refers to the pathways that facilitate the exchange of products and services,
along with the corresponding monetary payments (Ahmed & Rahman, 2015,
p. 25). Distribution indicates where a product can be obtained. It may include
physical or virtual stores. For green companies to be established and maintain
competitive prices, product distribution must be conducted through acceptable
channels. A company’s specification of where and when a product will be made
available is a critical consideration for consumers (Mahmoud, 2019, p. 23).
Green distribution focuses on using distribution channels that handle
green products suitable for consumers, facilitating safe cycle operations and
distribution within environmental conditions and requirements (Hashem
& Al-Rifai, 2011, p. 94). Green distribution focuses on managing logistics
to reduce transportation emissions and, in fact, aims to reduce the carbon
footprint by targeting it (Singh et al., 2016, p. 699). Green distribution is
a process that aims to meet consumer needs by systematically managing the
company’s product distribution flow across all aspects. Indicators include
the company’s transportation, warehouses, inventory , customer orders, and
logistics activities. However, the green status indicator that is meaningful to
consumers is the condition of the store where the product is sold (Chairunnisa
et al., 2019, p. 323). Since the end goal of production is to generate revenue
for the business, it follows that the products must be conveniently located
for customers to buy . According to Tabit and Raewf (2018, p. 104), the
distribution network of a corporation is shaped by a series of entities, including
wholesalers, retailers, and distributors.
2.1.1.4.Promotion
Promotion is another key concept within the marketing mix. The
manager responsible for determining the level of marketing expenses for
promotional activities—such as sales promotions, advertising, public relations,

exhibitions, and demonstrations—is the one who makes these decisions.
Promotion activities generally complement personal selling, advertising,
and other promotional efforts. Promotion supports the merchant and sales
team in effectively introducing the product to consumers and encouraging
them to make a purchase. Promotion consists of various materials brought
together to achieve the company’s marketing objectives (Singh, 2012, p. 42).
Promotion is the communication process between the company and consumers
aimed at maintaining a strong reputation for products and services that will
create benefits for them during the purchasing process. Communication,
in the context of promotional activities targeting specific audiences, can be
either mass or individual, personal or impersonal. Additionally , in practice,
promotion is described as a communication system or a series of methods,
forms, and formats that convey information about a company’s activities
regarding products, services, and general consumers, as well as the broader
environment (Novak, 2011, p. 505).
Promotion is a sales technique related to communication that is essential
to the success of any marketing program. It helps potential customers become
aware of the many product and service options available. A successful product
or service holds no meaning unless its benefits are clearly communicated
to the target market, as seen at. A communication program is crucial in
marketing strategies because it plays three vital roles: providing necessary
information and advice, raising the target consumer’s awareness of a product’s
benefits, and periodically engaging the target audience (Khan, 2014, p. 101).
Promotions for green products help increase consumers’ awareness of green
concepts. This indirectly helps consumers find the specific green product
they are seeking. Encouraging the customer’s decision to purchase and where
to purchase are important because they foster interest in and the desire to
purchase green products. Green advertising plays a particularly significant
role when a company focuses on the natural environment and eco-friendly
products (Geap et al., 2018, p. 5).

to one definition, innovation is new learning resulting from a firm’s integrative
capabilities to generate new applications from existing knowledge (Troilo, 2014,
p. 38). Innovation generally refers to the development and/or implementation
of new ideas or behaviors. It can be perceived as a new idea, product, service,
market, operational structure, or system as a whole (Damanpour et al., 2009,
p. 652). Innovation encompasses any new application introduced to a company ,

including equipment, products, processes, policies, and projects (Arfi et al.,
2018, p. 210).
Through green innovation, firms can meet the demands of environmentally
conscious customers and achieve benefits such as increased efficiency , cost
reductions, and higher sales, thereby improving financial performance. Green
innovation can be defined as “innovation in technologies related to energy
efficiency , low pollution rates, waste recycling, eco-friendly product design,
and eco-friendly management, along with innovations a firm implements by
incorporating ecological products or processes” (Tariq et al., 2019, p. 287).
Green innovation refers to the development of environmentally friendly
products and processes through the adoption of organizational practices that
use fewer materials during product design, apply eco- design principles, reduce
emissions, and lower water and electricity consumption (Singh et al., 2020,
p. 3). There is uncertainty regarding the terminology for green innovation. In
the literature, researchers use the terms eco-innovation, green innovation, and
environmental innovation interchangeably . Additionally , there are numerous
definitions for the concept of green innovation. According to one definition,
green innovation is new products and processes that enhance customer and
corporate reputations while minimizing ecological impacts (Wang, 2019, p.
668). The theme of green innovation has seen increasing interest in academic
and policy circles in recent years, coinciding with the implementation of
transformative practices aimed at reducing or mitigating environmental harm
in many countries (Arfi et al., 2018, p. 210).
2.2.1.Green Product Innovation
To innovate is to carry out a process that incorporates novel ideas; this
could involve creating a new service, modifying an existing one, creating
a new product, or starting a new business. Improvements to a company’s
product or service are what we mean when we talk about product innovation;
improvements to the production or delivery of a product or service are what
we mean when we talk about process innovation. According to Wong (2012,
p. 470), the end goal of product innovation is to enhance product performance
to attract more customers and expand into new markets.
In product innovation, it is possible to leverage current technology and
evaluate existing techniques in various ways. Product innovation arises from
technological changes, evolving consumer demands, and increasing competition
(Yıldız & Seyhan, 2019, p. 1411). Green product innovation is one of the
most fundamental drivers of growth, ecological sustainability , and improved
quality of life. Green product innovation generally focuses on a product’s

greatest environmental impact—specifically , its use and disposal (Huang et
al., 2016, p. 1168). It is widely accepted that green product innovation refers
to new or modified products designed to reduce environmental impact. It
involves introducing and improving products using eco- friendly or recycled
materials, as this approach can reduce environmental impact at the input stage;
it also includes product modifications aimed at energy savings and pollution
prevention during use (Ma et al., 2018, p. 2).
2.2.2.Process Innovation
An innovative “green process” seeks to either increase efficiency by
decreasing energy use or increase value by repurposing waste. Reducing water
use, cutting emissions, increasing resource and energy efficiency , and switching
from fossil fuels to bioenergy are all part of green process innovation. Xie et al.
(2019, p. 699) state that this is how businesses seeking to innovate in green
technology can gain and maintain a competitive edge in the market. Improving
efficiency , adaptability , and output is what process innovation is all about.
Product or process value creation is the primary goal of innovation (Wong,
2012, p. 470). Improvements in environmental compliance, differentiation,
and financial performance are just a few of the ways green process innovation
can help industrial production. The manufacturing sector can acquire a green
reputation and first-mover benefits by implementing green process innovation
(Xie et al., 2015, p. 101).
Green process innovation encompasses any change or adjustment at any
stage of the production process—including material procurement, production,
or delivery—that reduces negative environmental impacts. Firms may modify
their operations to address only a few or many of these environmental
challenges. The extent to which they address or differently articulate various
sustainability elements through different green innovation performances,
the degree to which sustainability is at the core of innovation activities, and
the need for different strategies to achieve these goals (Albort-Morant et al.,
2018, p. 434).

emerged by enriching the traditional supply chain management framework
and integrating green activities into it. Green supply chain performance
focuses on the flow of materials, information, and services from suppliers to
the end customer via the manufacturer or logistics provider, while considering
environmental sustainability (Panja & Mondal, 2019, p. 435). Choosing

2.4. Performance
Operational performance describes the quantifiable aspect of a company’s
operational sequence, such as reliability , production cycles, and inventory
turnover. Operational performance also influences performance metrics related
to market share and customer satisfaction (Voss et al., 1997, p. 1048). The
role of operations varies depending on its position in the supply chain. To
rationalize why the total set of operational performance requirements of end
customers necessitates what each link in the supply chain does, starting from
the next link in the chain, an input/output analysis of each link is used to trace
the process back to the supply chain (Harland, 1997, p. 75).
Operational performance reflects the strategic dimensions in which a firm
chooses to compete. In other words, since the attainment of a competitive
advantage depends on effectively translating competitive priorities into
strategic capabilities, production capabilities and resources must be focused on
developing these competitive priorities (Yu et al., 2014, p. 685). A marketing
campaign’s success is measured by its ability to increase sales or customer
happiness, both of which are indicators of marketing performance. Proprietary
logistics are essential to sustain such a meteoric rise in sales volume, propelled
by efficient marketing. A crucial condition for marketing effectiveness is
logistics, which controls time, location, service quality , and product quantity
(Schramm-Klein & Morschett, 2006, p. 280). The following is a definition of
marketing accountability from the American Marketing Association: Ambler
and Roberts (2008, p.735) state that systematic management of marketing
resources and procedures is responsible for increasing the value of the business,
improving marketing efficiency , achieving quantitative returns on marketing
investment, and maintaining quality .

Conclusion
Today , businesses adopting strategic approaches shaped by sustainability
principles are not only a requirement for environmental responsibility but
also a key factor in securing a competitive advantage. In this context, the
green marketing strategies, green innovation practices, and green supplier
selection processes discussed here are evaluated as integral components of
a holistic framework aimed at enhancing environmental performance and
reinforcing organizational success. Reviews of the literature reveal that these
three core practices are complementary in nature and create strategic synergy
in businesses’ journey toward environmental sustainability .
Green marketing is a strategic approach in which businesses demonstrate
sensitivity to environmental issues by optimizing their products and services
to minimize environmental impacts throughout their lifecycles. The success of
this strategy largely depends on the development of eco-friendly products and
processes supported by innovative solutions. In this context, green innovation
enhances the effectiveness of green marketing strategies by offering multifaceted
benefits, such as reducing environmental impacts, improving energy and
resource efficiency , and developing new technologies—both products and
processes. In particular, green product and process innovations have been
observed to increase customer satisfaction, strengthen corporate reputation,
and enable market differentiation. However, the sustainable implementation

of green innovation within a business is only possible if actors in the supply
chain adopt environmental responsibilities. In this context, selecting green
suppliers plays a strategic role in a company’s interactions with its external
environment. Suppliers’ compliance with environmental standards ensures that
production processes are conducted in an eco-friendly manner and helps meet
regulatory expectations. Furthermore, sustainable partnerships established
with suppliers have a critical impact on businesses’ ability to achieve their
long-term performance goals.
In conclusion, to effectively implement green marketing strategies, it is
necessary to develop an integrated strategy that incorporates green innovation
and the selection of green suppliers. Evaluating these elements together not
only demonstrates an environmentally conscious corporate stance but also
yields tangible outcomes such as improved business performance, operational
efficiency , and a competitive advantage. The relatively limited coverage of
these three core elements in the literature enhances both the academic value
of future research and the potential for such studies to guide businesses on
their sustainability journeys. In this context, evaluating environmentally
focused strategic approaches within a broader framework and raising awareness
among practitioners are also critical requirements for achieving sustainable
development goals.
