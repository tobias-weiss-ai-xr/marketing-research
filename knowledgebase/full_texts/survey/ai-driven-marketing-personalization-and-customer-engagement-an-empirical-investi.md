Abstract
While AI -based personalization is prevalent in e -commerce, the
psychological processes underlying the transformation of these
experiences into engagement remain under-theorized. The current study
aims to examine a moderated-mediation model with perceived value and
customer trust as parallel mediators and privacy concerns as moderator.
We conducted a quantitative cross -sectional survey among 476 online
consumers in Indonesia, Malaysia and Thailand. The data were analyzed
using SmartPLS  4.0 using PLS -SEM, which includes measurement
invariance testing and power analysis.  The results confirm that AI -
driven personalization significantly increases engagement directly and
indirectly through perceived value (β=0.235) and trust (β=0.145).
Interestingly, privacy concerns largely mitigate the trust -building path
(β=−0.143). This research makes a novel contribution to the literature
by empirically validating the “personalization -privacy paradox” in the
emerging Southeast Asian market through a dua l-pathway mediation
lens. In practice, e -commerce operators should consider functional
utility (value) as the main driver of engagement, while trust is a long -
term relational outcome that needs transparent data governance.

Keywords: AI-Driven Personalization, Customer Engagement,
Customer Trust, Perceived Value, Privacy Concerns

INTRODUCTION
The deployment of artificial intelligence in digital marketing has transitioned from experimental
application to operational standard. Machine learning algorithms, collaborative filtering engines, natural
language processing pipelines, and predictive analy tics systems now orchestrate the personalization
strategies of major e-commerce platforms, streaming services, and digital retailers at scale (Kumar et al.,
2019; Huang & Rust, 2021) . In Southeast Asia, a region characterized by rapid e -commerce expansion
and diverse digital consumer cultures, AI -driven personalization has become a defining feature of

platforms such as Shopee, Lazada, and Tokopedia, each of which serves tens of millions of active users
(Google et al., 2023).
However, while the AI systems are technologically sophisticated, the academic literature has not
kept up with the commercial reality, leaving three substantive gaps. First, personalization and consumer
outcomes have been well studied in traditional web -based contexts (Tam & Ho, 2006 ; Aguirre et al.,
2015), but these studies largely ignore the unique ‘black -box’ nature of AI -driven algorithmic
personalization, which is fundamentally different from rule -based systems in its autonomy and opacity.
Second, although perceived value and customer trust have been s eparately implicated as mediators in
technology adoption literature  (McKnight et al., 2002 ; Gefen et al., 2003 ; Kim et al., 2007) , their
simultaneous operation as parallel mediators—representing the dual cognitive and affective pathways—
remains empirically under-tested in an AI marketing context. Third, the ‘personalization-privacy paradox’
has been conceptually developed (Awad & Krishnan, 2006; Aguirre et al., 2015), but its specific function
as a boundary condition reducing the trust -building mechanism has been empirically under -specified,
particularly in the evolving digital environment of Southeast Asia.
This study addresses these gaps through a moderated mediation model grounded in self -
determination theory Ryan & Deci, (2008)  the value -based adoption model (Kim et al., 2007) , and
relationship marketing theory (Morgan & Hunt, 1994) . The theoretical integration is motivated by the
multidimensional nature of customer engagement, which encompasses cognitive, emotional, and
behavioral investment (Brodie et al., 2011; Hollebeek et al., 2014), and by recognition that personalized
marketing activates multiple consumer psychological systems simultaneously. The study pursues three
research objectives: To examine the direct effect of AI -driven personalization on customer engagement
in Southeast Asian e -commerce settings; To investigate the simultaneous mediating roles of perceived
value and customer trust in the relationship between AI-driven personalization and customer engagement;
To assess the moderating effect of privacy concerns on the relationship between AI-driven personalization
and customer trust.
Beyond hypothesis testing, this study makes a methodological contribution by conducting
measurement invariance analysis across three national subsamples (Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand) before
conducting pooled structural analysis —a step rarely reported in cross-national consumer studies in this
domain. The empirical context of Southeast Asia is significant not only because of the region's e -
commerce growth trajectory but also because consumer attitudes toward data privacy and AI transparency
vary substantially across these markets, offering a meaningful context for examining boundary conditions
of AI personalization effectiveness. AI-driven personalization refers to the use of machine learning and
related computational techniques to tailor marketing communications, product recommendations, and
service experiences to individual consumers in real time, based on behavioral, transaction al, and
contextual data (Kumar et al., 2019; Tio et al., 2026). This capability distinguishes AI-driven approaches
from earlier rule-based personalization systems: rather than matching users to predetermined segments,
AI systems learn continuously from data and adapt recommendations dynamically, enabling a degree of
individual-level customization that was previously impractical (Huang & Rust, 2021).
The literature documents multiple mechanisms through which AI personalization operates in
digital marketing. Collaborative filtering systems generate product recommendations by identifying users
with similar behavioral histories (Lu et al., 2015) . Natural language processing enables the dynamic
personalization of marketing content, including email subject lines, chatbot interactions, and on -site
messaging (Xu et al., 2022) . Reinforcement learning algorithms optimize campaign timing and channel
selection in real time. Collectively, these techniques enable platforms to deploy personalization across all
stages of the consumer journey, from pre -purchase discovery and evaluation  through to post -purchase
retention and advocacy.
Empirical research on consumer responses to AI personalization has grown substantially since
2018. (Bart et al., 2005)  and subsequent studies established that online consumers respond
heterogeneously to personalization; some perceive it as a service enhancement while others experience
discomfort or perceived intrusiveness. More recently, research has documented that algor ithmic
mediation introduces new consumer concerns not captured by earlier personalization frameworks —
specifically around transparency, explainability, and the perceived fairness of algorithmic decision -
making (Starke et al., 2022; Shin et al., 2024)These concerns have motivated the integration of trust and
privacy constructs into models of AI consumer behavior, and they inform the theoretical architecture of
the present study.

Customer engagement is a multidimensional construct that encompasses the cognitive,
emotional, and behavioral investment that consumers make in their interactions with a brand (Brodie et
al., 2011) . In digital marketing contexts, engagement is operationalized through indicators such as
frequency and depth of platform interaction, content consumption, sharing behavior, user -generated
content production, and participation in brand communities (Van Doorn et al., 2010 ; Hollebeek et al.,
2014).
The theoretical link between personalization and engagement is well -established within self -
determination theory (Hutmacher & Appel, 2023; Orji et al., 2025) , which posits that experiences
satisfying an individual's needs for competence, autonomy, and relatedness produce greater intrinsic
motivation and behavioral investment. Personalized experiences —by delivering content and
recommendations that closely match  individual preferences —satisfy the need for competence (by
reducing information overload and enabling better decisions) and the need for relatedness (by creating a
sense of being understood by the brand). These psychological satisfactions, in turn, motiva te continued
and deepened engagement with the platform or brand (Hsieh & Chang, 2016)
Empirical support for the personalization -engagement relationship is available across multiple
digital contexts. Tam & Ho, (2006)demonstrated that personalized web content positively influenced user
information processing and decision outcomes. (Arora et al., 2008)  showed that personalization increased
customer responsiveness across promotional channels. More recent work specifically on AI -driven
personalization has documented engagement improvements across e-commerce and streaming platforms,
though effect sizes va ry substantially with construct operationalization and national context (Choi &
Chung, 2021; Xu et al., 2022) . The present study adds to this evidence base with primary data from
Southeast Asian consumers. Based on this theoretical and empirical foundation:
Hypothesis 1 (H1): AI -driven marketing personalization has a significant positive direct effect on
customer engagement.

Perceived value is defined as the consumer's overall assessment of the utility of a product or
service based on perceptions of what is received relative to what is given (Zeithaml, 1988). In digital
service contexts, perceived value encompasses functional benefits such as time savings, reduced search
costs, and improved decision quality, as well as hedonic benefits such as pleasurable experiences and the
satisfaction of discovery (Wang et al., 2004; Kim et al., 2007).
The value-based adoption model (Kim et al., 2007)  posits that perceived value is the primary
driver of technology adoption and sustained use behavior. Applied to AI -driven personalization, this
framework suggests that consumers engage more deeply with platforms that demonstrably reduce the
cognitive burden of product discovery and deliver recommendations aligned with their actual preferences.
When personalization is accurate and contextually appropriate, it converts data collection into tangible
consumer benefit, generating perceived value that motivates further engagement. Empirical evidence
supports this chain: (Wang et al., 2004)  found that perceived value mediated the relationship between
personalization and loyalty in a mobile commerce setting, while (Luo et al., 2019) demonstrated a similar
pathway in AI-assisted retail. The following hypothesis is accordingly proposed:
Hypothesis 2 (H2): Perceived value mediates the positive relationship between AI -driven marketing
personalization and customer engagement.

Customer trust refers to the willingness of a consumer to rely on an exchange partner in whom
they have confidence (Morgan & Hunt, 1994) . Trust involves beliefs about an exchange partner's
benevolence (genuine concern for the consumer's welfare), competence (ability to perform reliably), and
integrity (adherence to acceptable principles) (McKnight et al., 2002) . In e -commerce, trust has been
established as a critical antecedent of purchase intention, loyalty, and willingness to share personal data
(Gefen et al., 2003; Kim et al., 2007).
AI-driven personalization can build trust by demonstrating that a platform has accurately
understood the consumer's needs and has the competence to deliver relevant experiences reliably. When
recommendations are consistently accurate and contextually appropriate, consumers develop confidence
in the platform's capabilities and are more likely to interpret the personalization as beneficial rather than
intrusive (McKnight et al., 2002). This trust, once established, lowers perceived risk in consumer -brand
interactions and facilitates deeper engagement, as consumers become more willing to invest time,
attention, and personal information in the relationship (Morgan & Hunt, 1994 ; Kim et al., 2007 ).
Empirical evidence for trust as a mediator between technology features and consumer behavioral

outcomes is well -established in e -commerce research (Gefen et al., 2003; Pavlou & Fygenson, 2006) .
More recently,(Shin, 2020) and (Choi & Chung, 2021)  have extended this logic to algorithmic and AI -
mediated systems, showing that algorithm transparency and perceived fairness influence trust, which in
turn affects adoption and engagement behaviors. The following hypothesis is accordingly proposed:
Hypothesis 3 (H3): Customer trust mediates the positive relationship between AI -driven marketing
personalization and customer engagement.

Privacy concerns in digital marketing reflect the extent to which consumers are worried about
the collection, storage, use, and potential misuse of their personal data by organizations (Smith et al.,
2011). Privacy concerns operate within what (Aguirre et al., 2015) describe as the personalization-privacy
paradox: consumers simultaneously value personalized experiences and feel discomfort about the data
collection practices those experiences require. This paradox implies that the same AI personalization
capability may generate positive or negative consumer responses depending on individual differences in
privacy sensitivity.
The moderating effect of privacy concerns on the personalization -trust relationship is
theoretically grounded in attribution theory (Kelley, 1973)  and communication privacy management
theory (Petronio, 2002) . Consumers who experience high privacy concerns are likely to attribute
personalized experiences to surveillance rather than service —interpreting them as evidence of data
collection rather than as genuine responsiveness to their needs (Awad & Krishnan, 2006) . Under high
privacy concern, the competence and benevolence signals that would otherwise build trust become
contaminated by suspicion about the organization's motives, weakening the trust -building pathway.
Conversely, consumers with low privacy concerns a re more likely to interpret personalization
benevolently, allowing trust to develop through repeated positive personalization experiences. Empirical
evidence for this moderating mechanism is available from (Aguirre et al., 2015) , who documented that
the positive effect of information transparency on advertising effectiveness was attenuated by high
privacy concern, and from (Li et al., 2016) , who showed that privacy sensitivity moderated the
relationship between AI personalization and willingness to share data. The following hypothesis is
accordingly proposed:
Hypothesis 4 (H4): Privacy concerns negatively moderate the relationship between AI -driven
personalization and customer trust, such that the positive effect of personalization on trust is weaker when
privacy concerns are higher

The conceptual model integrates the four hypotheses into a moderated mediation framework. AI-
driven marketing personalization serves as the independent variable; customer engagement is the outcome
variable; perceived value and customer trust operate as parallel mediators; and privacy concerns moderate
the personalization-to-trust pathway. The theoretical architecture reflects the simultaneous activation of
value cognition and relational trust as distinct but co -occurring psychological responses to AI
personalization, with individual -level privacy sensitivity determining the degree to which the trust -
building mechanism is accessible. Figure 1 presents the proposed conceptual model.

RESEARCH METHOD
This study employs a quantitative, cross -sectional survey design. The research context is e -
commerce in Southeast Asia, specifically across Indonesia, Malaysia, and Thailand. These three markets
were selected for substantive and methodological reasons. Subs tantively, each market hosts large and
active e-commerce ecosystems in which AI-driven personalization is extensively deployed by platforms
including Shopee, Lazada, and Tokopedia (Google et al., 2023) . Collectively, Southeast Asia's e -
commerce sector is projected to reach USD 300 billion in gross merchandise value by 2025, with AI -
mediated consumer interaction playing an increasingly central role (Google et al., 2023) .
Methodologically, the selection of three national markets enables multi-group analysis to assess whether
model parameters are invariant across different socio -cultural and regulatory contexts, addressing a
limitation common to single-country digital marketing studies
The target population consisted of adults aged 18 years and above who had made at least one
purchase on a major e-commerce platform within the previous three months and who reported awareness
of having received personalized product recommendations during t hat period. A non -probability
convenience sampling procedure was employed, with respondents recruited through social media
platforms (Facebook, Instagram, Twitter), online consumer communities, and email invitation lists
maintained by consumer research pan els. Eligibility was confirmed through a screening question
requiring respondents to indicate whether they had recently noticed personalized recommendations on an
e-commerce platform. To reach this population, a non -probability convenience sampling technique was
employed. Respondents were recruited through social media platforms (Facebook, Instagram, Twitter),
online consumer communities, and email invitation lists. Eligibility was stri ctly confirmed through a
screening question requiring respondents to indicate whether they had recently noticed and interacted
with personalized recommendations on an e-commerce platform.
The minimum sample size was determined using the guidelines provided by Hair et al. (2019) for
PLS-SEM: with five constructs, the most complex structural path receiving five predictors, and a target
statistical power of 80% at an effect size of f² = 0.15, a minimum of 166 responses was required. The
obtained sample of 476 valid responses substantially exceeds this threshold. After removing 34
incomplete responses and 12 responses exhibiting invariant responding patterns (standard deviation < 0.5
across all items), the final analytic sample comprised 476 observations, distributed across Indonesia (n =
172, 36.1%), Malaysia (n = 160, 33.6%), and Thailand (n = 144, 30.3%). This diverse sample provides a
robust basis for analyzing AI-driven consumer behavior across the Southeast Asian market.
Data were collected via an online self-completion questionnaire over an eight-week period. The
questionnaire was originally developed in English and translated into Bahasa Indonesia, Malay, and Thai
using the back -translation procedure recommended by (Brislin, 1970) : each language version was
independently translated by a bilingual subject matter expert and then independently back -translated by
a second bilingual expert; discrepancies were resolved through discussion and consensus. A pilot study
was conducted with 40  respondents (approximately 13 -14 per country) prior to main data collection,
resulting in minor rewording of two items for conceptual equivalence.  The research instrument was a
structured questionnaire consisting of several sections. Each variable was measured using scales adapted
from previous studies (see Table 1) and translated using a back-translation procedure to ensure linguistic
validity.
All constructs were measured using multi -item reflective scales adapted from established
instruments. Responses were recorded on a seven-point Likert scale anchored at 1 (Strongly Disagree) to
7 (Strongly Agree). Table 1 presents the construct sources and item counts.  AI-driven marketing
personalization (ADMP) was measured using five items adapted from Tam & Ho, (2006) and updated to
reflect algorithmic personalization, with items capturing the perceived accuracy, relevance, and timeliness
of platform-generated recommendations. Perceived value (PV) was measured using four items adapted
from (Kim et al., 2007; Zeithaml, 1988) , capturing the utilitarian and overall value derived from
personalized platform interactions. Customer trust (CT) was measured using five items adapted from
(Gefen et al., 2003)  and (McKnight et al., 2002) , capturing beliefs about platform competence,
benevolence, and integrity. Customer engagement (CE) was measured using six items adapted from
(Hollebeek et al., 2014)  and (Brodie et al., 2011) , capturing cognitive, affective, and behavioral
engagement. Privacy concerns (PC) were measured using four items adapted from (Smith et al., 2011)
and (Dinev & Hart, 2006), capturing concern about data collection, data use, and the potential for privacy
violations.

Given the cross -sectional, single-source survey design, the potential for common method bias
(CMB) was systematically assessed using both procedural and statistical approaches, following the
recommendations of (Podsakoff et al., 2003) and (Kock, 2015). Procedurally, the questionnaire separated
predictor and outcome items into different sections with distinct instructions, employed reverse -coded
items across constructs, and assured respondents of anonymity and the absence of right -or-wrong
answers. Statistically, Harman's single-factor test was first conducted: an exploratory factor analysis with
all items loaded onto a single factor extracted 38.4% of the total variance, substantially below the 50%
threshold, providing preliminary evidence that CMB is n ot a dominant source of variance. A more
rigorous assessment was conducted using the common latent factor (CLF) method in SmartPLS 4.0, in
which a CLF was added to the structural model and the standardized path coefficients with and without
the CLF were compared. The maximum difference in path coefficients was 0.03, below the 0.2 threshold
recommended by (Kock, 2015) , indicating that CMB does not materially threaten the validity of the
structural model estimates.
Prior to conducting pooled structural analysis, measurement invariance across the three national
subsamples was assessed using partial invariance testing within the PLS-SEM framework, following the
procedure recommended by (Henseler et al., 2016) . Configural invariance was first established by
confirming that the same measurement model was applied in each subsample. Compositional invariance
was subsequently assessed by comparing composite scores across groups using permutation -based tests.
All con struct composites demonstrated compositional invariance (p > 0.05 for all permutation tests),
confirming that the constructs are measured equivalently across Indonesia, Malaysia, and Thailand and
that pooled analysis is methodologically justified.
The data were analyzed using partial least squares structural equation modeling (PLS-SEM) with
SmartPLS 4.0 (Ringle et al., 2022). PLS-SEM was selected because of its suitability for testing complex
models with multiple mediating and moderating paths, its appropriateness for non -normal data
distributions, and its established use in consumer behavior and digital marketing research (Hair et al.,

2019). The two -stage analytical approach recommended by (Hair et al., 2019)  was followed: the
measurement model was assessed before the structural model was examined. Mediation effects were
tested using bias -corrected bootstrapping with 5,000 resamples (Preacher & Hayes, 2008) . The
moderation effect of privacy concerns on the ADMP-to-CT path was assessed by constructing a product
indicator interaction term using the two -stage approach. Effect sizes were interpreted using Cohen's f²
guidelines (Hair et al., 2019).

Multicollinearity was assessed by examining inner variance inflation factor (VIF) values. All

We interpreted the fact that the indirect effect through perceived value (β = 0.235) was much
stronger than that of customer trust (β = 0.145) as very important to practitioners who intend to build
meaningful relationships with consumers via AI technology.  Our research model reflected mediating
effects that were different from those of (Tam & Ho, 2006)'s study on cognitive information processing,
and the superior predictive power of perceived value over customer trust in this AI -era implies that
consumers no longer care much about slow-rendered trust as the precursor to engagement but care about
AI-driven 'something in return now' (perceived value). This means that while trust is still necessary to
maintain relationships with customers, it has become secondary to quick and effective performance gains,

acting instead as a by-product that accumulates over time. This helps explain (Luo et al., 2019) (Ashfaq
et al., 2020; Chen et al., 2022) discovery that efficiency of AI service agents trumps initial concerns over
relationship formation in highly digitized contexts where everything happens in seconds.

Moderation Effect
The interaction effect between AI-driven personalization and privacy concerns on customer trust
was significant and negative (β = −0.143, t = 3.287, p = 0.001), supporting Hypothesis 4. A slope analysis
revealed that at low levels of privacy concern (one standard deviation below the mean), the effect of AI-
driven personalization on customer trust was strong (β = 0.630, p < 0.001). At high levels of privacy
concern (one standard deviation above the mean), the same effect was notably attenuated (β = 0.344, p <
0.001) but remained statistically significant, indicating that privacy concerns moderate the strength rather
than the direction of the personalization-trust relationship.

Model Fit and Predictive Relevance

of perceived value and trust merit investigation in future research. Q² values were positive for all

This study makes three substantive theoretical contributions to the literature on AI in digital
marketing, consumer behavior, and e-commerce.
First, the study advances understanding of how AI -driven personalization translates into
behavioral consumer outcomes by specifying and empirically testing a parallel mediation mechanism.
The simultaneous operation of perceived value and customer trust as mediators reveals that
personalization activates two functionally distinct psychological pathways: a cognitive -evaluative
pathway in which consumers assess the tangible utility of personalized experiences, and a relational -
affective pathway in which repeat ed accurate personalization builds confidence in the platform's
competence and benevolence. These pathways are complementary rather than substitutable, as evidenced
by the simultaneous significance of both indirect effects. This finding extends value -based adoption
theory (Kim et al., 2007)  and relationship marketing theory (Morgan & Hunt, 1994)  by demonstrating
their conjoint applicability in AI-mediated consumer contexts.
Second, the moderation finding refines the conceptualization of the personalization -privacy
paradox (Aguirre et al., 2015; Awad & Krishnan, 2006) . Rather than framing privacy concerns as a
categorical barrier, the slope analysis reveals a continuum: consumers with high privacy concerns still
develop trust, but at a substantially reduced rate. This suggests that the paradox operates as a dampening
mechanism rather than an absolute constraint, consistent with Communication Privacy Management
theory (Petronio, 2002), which holds that consumers manage privacy boundaries rather than abandoning
them entirely(Neubaum et al., 2023; Zhou & Tang, 2025)
Third, the measurement invariance analysis across Indonesia, Malaysia, and Thailand provides
methodological evidence that the theoretical constructs in this model operate equivalently across three
national e -commerce markets with distinct regulatory enviro nments and cultural compositions. This
establishes This establishes a firmer empirical basis than studies that pool multi-country samples without

literature. This confirms that the theoretical constructs in this model operate equivalently across markets
with distinct regulatory environments and cultural co mpositions, allowing for a  broader theoretical
generalization of AI personalization effectiveness in emerging economies.
Four practical implications arise for e -commerce operators and AI system designers. First, the
strong total effect of AI -driven personalization on customer engagement (β = 0.598) confirms that
investment in algorithmic personalization capability generates substantial returns in consumer behavioral
outcomes. Organizations should regard AI personalization as a strategic capability rather than a tactical
feature. Second, the mediation analysis indicates that the pathway through perceived value (β = 0.235) is
substantially stronger than the pathway through customer trust (β = 0.145). This suggests that the
immediate priority in personalization system design should be the relevance and accuracy of
recommendations—the conditions that generate perceived utility —with trust treated as a longer -term
relational outcome that accumulates with sustained positive personalization experience.
Third, the moderation finding has direct implications for privacy strategy. Platforms should not
treat privacy management as a regulatory compliance obligation separate from their personalization
strategy; the two are empirically linked. Implementing trans parent data practices, offering consumers
meaningful privacy controls, and clearly communicating how personalization data is used can mitigate
the dampening effect of privacy concerns on trust formation, thereby recovering engagement that would
otherwise be lost. Fourth, the regional variance in privacy concern levels —while not the primary focus
of this study —reinforces the value of market -specific personalization governance policies, as the
moderating effect of privacy concerns is likely to operate at diff erent magnitudes across the three
countries studied.
Several limitations should be acknowledged. The cross -sectional design precludes causal
inference and cannot capture how perceived value, customer trust, and engagement evolve over repeated
personalization interactions over time; longitudinal or experience-sampling designs are needed to address
this. The convenience sample, while adequate in size, limits the ability to make probabilistic claims about
the broader population of Southeast Asian e -commerce consumers. The operationalization of AI -driven
personalization as a consumer perceptual construct means that the variable captures perceived rather than
objectively measured AI personalization; future research employing platform -level trace data or
experiment-based manipulations would provide stronger construc t validity. Finally, the model explains
31.2% and 37.5% of the variance in customer trust and perceived value respectively, indicating that
additional antecedents of these mediating constructs remain to be identified.
CONCLUSION
This study has empirically examined the moderated mediation mechanisms through which AI -
driven marketing personalization influences customer engagement among online consumers in Southeast
Asia. The results confirm that perceived value and customer trust op erate as parallel psychological
intermediaries between personalization and engagement, and that privacy concerns attenuate the trust -
building pathway. Together, these findings offer a more precise theoretical account of why AI
personalization produces engagement, under what psychological conditions it does so most effectively,
and where its effectiveness is bounded.  Future research should pursue several directions. Longitudinal
panel designs are needed to distinguish between the immediate and cumulative effects of AI
personalization on trust and engagement. Experimental designs with platform -side manipulation of
personalization features —rather than self -reported perceptions —would address the construct validity
limitation noted above. Comparative cross-cultural analysis incorporating Hofstede's cultural dimensions
would help to disentangle the relative contributions of privacy regulation and cultural orientation to the
moderation effect observed here. Finally, the emergence of generative AI in marketing —including AI-
generated product descriptions, chatbots, and dynamic visual content —introduces new consumer
perception ch allenges around authenticity and transparency that extend beyond the algorithmic
personalization paradigm examined in this study and represent a productive frontier for future research.
