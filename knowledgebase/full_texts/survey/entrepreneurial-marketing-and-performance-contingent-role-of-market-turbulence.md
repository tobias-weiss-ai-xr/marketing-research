Abstract
In developing countries, entrepreneurial firms are increasingly advocating a shift from conventional marketing strate -
gies, paving the way for innovative approaches like entrepreneurial marketing. This paper investigates the moderating
role of market turbulence over the impact of entrepreneurial marketing over Performance of Entrepreneurial Firms.
We have developed the framework while taking the support of contingency perspective. We adopted a quantitative
research methodology and collected the data using survey technique from 384 entrepreneurial firms, and the manag-
ers and owners were contacted for the responses. The collected data, after ensuring the reliability and validity has been
evaluated using Smart PLS 3. Structural equation modelling has been applied to evaluate the research framework. The
findings revealed that six out of seven identified constructs of entrepreneurial marketing hold a significant impact over
performance of entrepreneurial Firms. Additionally, the moderating role of market turbulence has also found significant
which is the major significance of the study. The paper ends up by identifying the limitations faced while conducting
research and giving recommendations for future researchers.

1 Introduction
Various researchers have extensively defined Entrepreneurial Marketing (EM) in different ways [1 –3]. The term is fre -
quently linked with marketing operations conducted by entrepreneurial firms having limited resources, compelling them
to employ innovative and less complicated techniques [4 ]. Proactiveness, opportunity orientation, limited risk-taking,
innovativeness, consumer intensity, resource mobilization, and value generation are the seven elements of EM [5 –7].
Therefore, EM can be seen as a novel paradigm that successfully merge the key components of entrepreneurship and
marketing into one concept. EM evolves a strategic method which is utilized by entrepreneurial firms to conduct their
marketing operations in an economic and effective way [8 , 9].

2  Literature review
EM has given rise to multiple research streams, leading to diverse interpretations of the EM concept [40]. Among EM
studies, focusing on performance of entrepreneurial firms have led to the emergence of a large research stream [41,
42]. The conventional marketing approaches may hardly be fruitful or feasible for entrepreneurial firms [43] because of
limited resources which they fulfil through their network [44]. Another stream within the realm of EM concentrates on
the behavioural aspects of entrepreneurs [45].
2.1  Entrepreneurial marketing strategies
The basic goal of entrepreneurship as well as marketing is value creation, thus, EM can be termed as means of creating
value for the consumers [46]. Since neither the producer nor the consumer can independently develop consumer value,
this process is mutual [47]. In case the business is not selling something worthwhile, it will certainly lose the market [2 ].
Entrepreneurs have realized that consumers understand the significance of an exchange process to continue their rela-
tionship with the seller, so they prefer to identify consumer driven marketing strategy [10, 48]. Thus, consumer value is
what the consumer gets in return for the money they spend over the products [49]. Sustaining a market position involves
meeting consumer expectations, fostering repeated exchanges, and ensuring ongoing consumer satisfaction [22].
A traditional marketing strategy entails establishing marketing dominance and shaping perceived consumer value, to
ensure profitability [50]. This strategy is employed by entrepreneurs who aim to sustain current markets and deliver the
same consumer value. EM revolves around exploring new unexplored opportunities [30]. While entrepreneurs always
try to explore new markets while following the same conventional strategies and offer the same obsolete value strategy
in the new market [51]. EM strategy comes into play, only when the entrepreneurs introduce novelties and innovation
in their product or service which influence perceived value for their consumers in the existing as well as new markets
[52, 53].
EM strategy involves exceeding expectations of the consumer by exceptional consumer value expectation [54]. This
can be achieved by offering improved value in the new markets through continuous innovation and improvement in the
existing value [54, 55]. Entrepreneurs have the flexibility to adopt these strategies individually or by combining them.
Prior literature has identified many new ingredients of EM such as; focus on innovation [45, 50, 56], calculated risk-
taking [5, 57], focus on opportunity recognition [58], and flexible approaches to markets [56]. Due to varying attributes
by different scholars, several controversies have been observed in the existing literature regarding EM [30].
Moreover, innovation is viewed as a key factor in determining how well an entrepreneurial firm performs [59]. The
capacity of an entrepreneurial firm to consistently generate fresh innovative ideas can be transformed into new products,
new markets, new services, and using latest technological advancements [60]. By utilizing new or available resources in
a novel way, a focus on innovation may assist entrepreneurial firms in moving beyond the recognition of opportunity
[61]. Similarly, focus over the opportunity is underutilized market situation which acts as a source of potential profits
not only soon but also overall [39]. Opportunity recognition currently plays a crucial part in entrepreneurship research
as well as entrepreneurship theory.

2.2  Entrepreneurial marketing and performance of entrepreneurial firms
Literature linking EM and performance of entrepreneurial firms has identified EM as superior confirming avenue for
eliminating marketing practices of entrepreneurial firms having resource constraints [65] that are driven by several entre-
preneurial practices [66]. Studies indicate that EM can be applicable to firms of all sizes [14]. A constructive approach to
comprehending EM demands dissecting it into individual terms ’marketing’ and ’entrepreneurship’ independently [67].
Despite numerous attempts to formulate a universally accepted definition, none have been successful in defining
entrepreneurship in the perfect manner [68]. Entrepreneurship is the blend of innovativeness, risk taking, and proactiv-
ity in addition to assembling resources in a unique way to create consumer value in the products and services being
offered by the enterprise [69]. Researchers argued that entrepreneurship is all about seeking novel opportunities in a
challenging situation and effectively combining resources to generate profits [70– 72].
On the other hand, marketing is the process of generating, transmitting, presenting, and switching offers that ben-
efit the stakeholders [73]. The core marketing activities including product, place, price, and promotion, usually referred
as the marketing mix or 4Ps [74]. In past, marketing and entrepreneurship were considered two distinct fields, each
progressing independent of the other. However, literature revealed that there are three similarities between the defini-
tions of marketing and entrepreneurship [14, 71]. First, both fields emphasize the managerial process [75], second both
disciplines deal with unique combinations, marketing mix elements, and resources [40], finally, both of them rely heavily
on the idea of value creation [46, 51]. Thus, EM a relevantly recent theory, which emerged four decades ago, serves as a
bridge, connecting these two disciplines.
The demand for consumers is ever-increasing, and these traits affect the marketing sector [76]. The traditional market-
ing mix concept is deficient in both consumer orientation and consumer interactivity [77]. Five pivotal transformations
have emerged, necessitating a new direction for the field of marketing. These include the globalized and converging
economies of information, the frictionless and fragmented markets, the demanding consumers, their enhanced behav-
iours, and the adaptable organizations [61].
Considering the influential impact of marketing over performance of entrepreneurial firms, researchers proposed
several unique strategies to supersede the conventional one [78]. New non-conventional marketing strategies have
been developed due to globalization [79]. Hence, EM has reached a new paradigm which forces entrepreneurial firms
to rethink the marketing approaches that they should follow to succeed in dynamic situations [80].
EM is required in an unstable environment when conventional marketing practices are insufficient [1]. Markets these
days possess these traits, and most organizations currently in operation would benefit from and require the application
of EM. It must not be underestimated that EM can be used in several ways based on the level of marketing development.
Several researchers are now interested in touching the new topics like EM [3]. Although there are many publications
on this subject in the literature, only a few have looked at how EM affects performance of entrepreneurial firms in turbu-
lent markets [14, 56, 81–83]. Several studies that have employed EM strategies proposed are presented below. Similarly,
the current research revolves around EM and entrepreneurial firms to confirm the ways that how EM can assist them
in achieving their strategic objectives. The authors have incorporated risk management, pro-activeness, opportunity-
driven, innovativeness, consumer intensity, value creation, and leveraging resources to achieve competitive advantage.
Researchers have assessed that how EM and small firms are linked with their qualitative and quantitative outcomes [3]
and confirmed significant impact of EM on performance, however, the dimensions of EM taken by the authors vary from
country to country. Likewise, Lopes, et, al. [49] through the bibliometric analysis confirmed that EM affects performance
of entrepreneurial firms.
Khan, et, al. [84] investigated the moderating effect of EM for achieving performance of entrepreneurial firms in
Pakistan. The analysis of 102 entrepreneurial firms revealed that EM cannot be ignored because of its major role in

2.3  Moderating role of market turbulence
Merket turbulence is part of environmental factors, mainly revolving around risk and uncertainty, which requires proper
strategies to handle the turbulent environment. According to contingency theory, performance is determined by the fit
between a strategy and environment, not the plan itself. Turbulent market environments influence the allure, viability,
and unpredictability of collaborations by posing possibilities, restrictions, and risks [93]. Turbulent market environment is
a crucial component of external collaborations because it heightens the challenges associated with the firms’ processes
and the causal relation between strategy and firm performance [94]. Frequent and erratic changes in consumer demands
and product preferences, production and product technology, and the competitive environment are characteristics of
a turbulent market [95]. Businesses find it difficult to comprehend shifting consumer trends and develop new products
in volatile markets [96 ]. Because of this, businesses are extremely motivated to look for lucrative ideas from different
partners, which could lead to collaborations [97]. But the risk that comes with it also rises because companies that are
under pressure to find novel ideas for their own gain are more likely to become opportunistic [84].Market volatility is
frequently caused by fierce competition and erratic technology advancement timing [98]. Collaborations will focus more
on innovation as the market becomes more dynamic [99]. The secret to success is market and consumer driven innovation
which keeps the firms ahead of competitors because it is tough to track client demand and preferences [100]. Turbulent
market environment forces the enterprises, to create value for the consumers on continuous basis considering trends in
the market, because entrepreneurial firms are trend followers instead trend setters, thus, market turbulence may func -
tion as a facilitator for them to gain competitive advantage.
Second, in volatile markets, information needs will multiply since managers dealing with a great deal of uncertainty
need more knowledge to make wise judgments [101]. Effective information gathering and sharing facilitates decision-
making for all concerned, enabling them to identify opportunities in the market and develop winning strategies that
foster more productive teamwork [102]. Based on the above discussion we believe that market turbulence helps entrepre-
neurial firms to adopt differentiation strategy through EM, thereby incurring better performance. Therefore, in extremely
chaotic markets as opposed to stable markets, information competence is more likely to have an impact on collaborative
success. Thus, the following hypotheses are proposed considering the catalytic role of market turbulence.
H8 : Market turbulence significantly moderate the positive impact of proactiveness and performance of entrepreneurial
firms.
H9 Market turbulence significantly moderate the positive impact of value creation and performance of entrepreneurial
firms.
H10 Market turbulence significantly moderate the positive impact of innovativeness and performance of entrepreneurial
firms.
H11 Market turbulence significantly moderate the positive impact of opportunity oriented and performance of entre -
preneurial firms.
H12 Market turbulence significantly moderate the positive impact of customer intensity and performance of entrepre -
neurial firms.

2.4  Theoretical framework
In extremely volatile marketplaces, where rivals actively seek for novel concepts from a variety of sources, opportunist
risk and protective expenses rise [35]. Businesses may become overly defensive of their intellectual property and even
hesitate from collaborating with outside parties because of growing concerns about the security of their concepts and
procedures [3]. The current research therefore intends to identify the direct impact of seven identified EM factors while
catering market turbulence as moderator between EM dimensions and performance of entrepreneurial firms. It is impor-
tant to consider that entrepreneurial marketing serves as a major resource for the firms and only those factors that have
the capability to give competitive advantage can help a firm in achieving performance.
Thus, considering the proposition of Resource Based View (RBV) [103], the seven dimensions of entrepreneurial market-
ing are supported by RBV. RBV emphasizes that firms achieve superior performance by effectively utilizing their unique
resources and capabilities. Entrepreneurial marketing aligns with this theory as it leverages intangible resources like
proactivity, value creation, innovativeness, opportunity oriented, customer intensity, resource mobilizing, and limited
risk taking to achieve competitive advantage. SMEs often face resource constraints, and EM helps them maximize their
limited resources through innovative marketing strategies. RBV supports the relationship between the dimensions of
entrepreneurial marketing and performance of entrepreneurial firms by emphasizing how EM helps entrepreneurial
firms leverage and optimize their resources and capabilities.
Additionally, the support of Contingency theory helps to understand the moderating role of market turbulence
between entrepreneurial marketing and the performance of entrepreneurial firms by emphasizing that the effective -
ness of strategies, such as EM, depends on alignment with external environmental conditions [104]. Market turbulence,
characterized by rapid and unpredictable changes in customer preferences, competitor actions, and market dynamics,
plays a significant role in shaping this relationship [35]. Moreover, contingency theory posits that no single strategy is
universally effective; its success depends on how well it aligns with external factors [105, 106]. In highly turbulent mar-
kets, entrepreneurial marketing becomes more effective because it allows firms to adapt to changing customer needs
by quickly capitalizing on emerging opportunities [96]. Thus, based on the propositions of EM [51], considering the
limitations of the theory, the following framework has been proposed.

3  Methodology
The researchers used quantitative research methodology to assess the hypothesis. The information was gathered from
384 entrepreneurial firms [107] in three distinct Pakistani locations Karachi, Sialkot, and Lahore during February to
April 2024. The sample size is considered adequate as per the recommendations of Sekaran as well as Hair, et al. [108,
109] for the social sciences, majority of the scholars identified that 384 is the maximum sample size and majority of the
studies over entrepreneurial firms of SMEs have used the same sample size [11, 65, 110, 111].The purpose of choosing
these cities is that these are considered as the main hub of entrepreneurial firms in Pakistan and maximum proportion
of entrepreneurial firms in Pakistan belong to these cities [10, 11]. The question ‘‘Does your firm have a research and
development department or not’’ was set as a filter to determine whether the firm is entrepreneurial in nature or not.
Only those replies that indicated the enterprises had research and development departments were used in the analysis.

4  Conclusions, discussions, limitations, and recommendations
The purpose of this research was to evaluate the significance of EM on the performance of entrepreneurial firms operating
in the contextual settings of Pakistan. Remarkable findings from the analysis of primary data collected from the entre -
preneurial firms of Pakistan revealed that EM strategies like; proactiveness, value creation, innovativeness, opportunity
oriented, consumer intensity, and limited risk taking have favourable and statistically significant impacts on the overall
performance of entrepreneurial firms.
5  Discussions
EM has a favourable effect on overall performance of entrepreneurial firms, even though not all EM strategies have shown
a significant impact over performance like resource leverage being the part of EM has shown an insignificant impact.
As per the results mentioned in the analysis, majority of the EM strategies, whether used individually or in combination,
indeed exert an influence over performance of entrepreneurial firms. Specifically, EM strategies which include proac -
tiveness, value creation, innovativeness, opportunity oriented, and consumer intensity are particularly responsible for
gaining superior performance of entrepreneurial firms. There findings are in accordance with the prior literature where
the authors claimed that entrepreneurial marketing is important for the SMEs and entrepreneurial firms [25, 57, 84].
According to the data acquired, Pakistani entrepreneurial firms are typically very opportunity oriented, demonstrat -
ing a proactive approach for boosting their performance. The findings confirmed the conceptual frameworks that
were proposed by the researchers for empirical testing [7 , 14], hence, this study analyzed the same through empiri-
cal findings. Furthermore, these firms demonstrate a keen awareness of the strategic utility of resource mobilizing,
but this dimension has failed to cause a significant impact over performance. Perhaps the major reason behind its
insignificance is the lack of resources with the entrepreneurial firms which is argued by several researchers in the
past [8 , 55]. Moreover, in Pakistani entrepreneurial firms mostly face challenges in resource mobilizing because of
lack of support [44]. Additionally, they recognize the pivotal role of value creation as a central driver in augmenting
the overall performance of entrepreneurial firms which is also confirmed by the researchers in the past [100] because
the consumers of the entrepreneurial firms pay high value to the money that they are spending.
Regarding the market turbulence, the findings are surprising because market turbulence has significantly mod-
erated all the relationships positively. The findings are also consistent up to certain extent that moderating role of
market turbulence is significant, however, in many cases turbulence creates a negative impact, but as the study
sample was particularly focused over the entrepreneurial firms, thus, the market turbulence showed a positive role
because entrepreneurial firms are proactive in catering the changing scenarios [9 , 96]. Entrepreneurial firms which are
dynamic by nature and turbulent market creates challenges for the conventional businesses, however entrepreneurial
firms are dynamic and for such firms, turbulent environment creates more opportunities [80]. In entrepreneurial
firms, entrepreneurs know how to convert challenges into opportunities, that is why the moderating role of market
turbulence is found to have a significant moderating impact.
6  Theoretical contributions
The research findings are supposed to enrich the EM theory with the support of contingency theory. The moderating
role of market turbulence is confirmed in this research in strengthening the propositions of entrepreneurial marketing
theory and is in line with the prior studies where the moderating role of market turbulence has been analysed [28,
35]. EM practices allow the entrepreneurial firms to create new marketing tactics which are in line with the prevailing
market situations, thus, market turbulence instead of playing the role of a challenge act as a support [70]. In order to
cater the altering consumer demands, and changing dynamics of the market due to stiff competition, EM practices
help to be more adoptive as per the available resources and as per the market situation.

The most interesting finding is to have the positive impact of market turbulence, which is in contradiction with
several authors, as environmental turbulence [93], either it is market turbulence, of technological turbulence, it is
considered to act as a challenge and may influence performance negatively, but the current research highlighted
that market turbulence play a positive role for entrepreneurial firms, thus, strengthening the dynamic capabilities
perspectives as well as the entrepreneurial marketing theory with the support of contingency theory. The study also
supports contingency perspective that if a firm is capable of managing in the dynamic environment and has the
tendency covert challenges into opportunities, they outperform competition.
7  Managerial contributions
The research findings hold strong managerial contributions. The findings revealed that resource mobilization showed
an insignificant impact over performance, however, after the introduction of market turbulence, it becomes signifi-
cant. These findings guide the managers and the entrepreneurs to be adaptive in their strategies the findings guide
the managers to focus over the continuously changing market situations and be ready for converting challenges into
opportunities. Entrepreneurial marketing and entrepreneurship, when combined results in synergies, which boosts
the performance significantly. Entrepreneurs and managers need to focus over the consumers and must try to remain
one step ahead of their demands, in other words always try to exceed expectations of the consumers.
Market turbulence provides an opportunity to the entrepreneurs to be pioneer in change, to be innovative not only
in products and services but also in marketing techniques. Instead of focusing over heavy budgets for the marketing
campaigns, new innovative ways need to be derived for achieving better promotion of the entrepreneurial firm. The
findings identify that managers need to be proactive and must mobilize the resources to achieve performance. They
need to focus on value creation on continuous basis for the betterment of the firm.
8  Limitations
Despite the earnest efforts made in this study to advance both theoretical insights and practical applications, it is
crucial to know that certain limitations may potentially affect the robustness of the results. Firstly, the study relied
on subjective data collected from the owners and managers of entrepreneurial firms, the findings are based over the
perceptual measures of the respondents, unlike objective sources such as their financial statements or other internal
records [122]. Therefore, there is a potential concern regarding the accuracy of their responses. Secondly, due to the
unavailability of objective performance metrics, this study resorted to utilizing readily accessible measures like growth
in sales, improvement in profitability [123], improved market repute [124], and personal goals of the entrepreneur.
Hence, the findings may not portray the complete picture of performance of entrepreneurial firms since they are
solely driven from the perceptual measures of the entrepreneurs. Furthermore, this research collected data only from
the entrepreneurial firms in Pakistan, therefore, the findings may not be applicable to other geographic locations.
9  Directions for future
Despite the substantial contributions made by this study across various domains, it is essential to acknowledge
that no single study can provide all the answers in any given field of study. Considering the current circumstances,
it is prudent to recommend future avenues of research that can effectively delve into unexplored facets and propel
knowledge expansion within the field of EM. Firstly, the current study’s findings indicated that not all EM strategies
significantly affect the overall performance of entrepreneurial firms; hence, it is imperative to advocate for a further

Thirdly, the influence of EM strategies on the financial and non-financial performance of entrepreneurial firms
