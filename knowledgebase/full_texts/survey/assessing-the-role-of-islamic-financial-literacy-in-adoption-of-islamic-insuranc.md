This research assesses the key factors influencing the adoption of Takaful in Pakistan, with
particular emphasis on Islamic financial literacy , demographic factors, and contextual
factors. The study's primary objective is to evaluate how these variables shape individuals'
awareness of, and willingness to adopt, Takaful as a viable alternative to conventional
insurance. Grounded in the hypothesis that Islamic financial literacy plays a critical role in
promoting Takaful adoption, th e study also considers the moderating effects of
demographic factors  (such as age, income, education, and religious knowledge) and
contextual factors  (including access to information and marketing efforts) on this
relationship. A quantitative research design was employed, utilizing a survey methodology
to collect data from individuals with varying levels of exposure to Islamic finance and
Takaful. The survey instrument included carefully designed questions to measure Islamic
financial literacy, Takaful awareness, contextual factors, and demographic characteristics,
with responses analyzed on a Likert scale. A total of 132 respondents participated in the
study, and the data were analyzed using regression analysis  to identify significant
predictors of Takaful adoption. Reliability tests  (Cronbach's Alpha) were conducted to
assess the internal consistency of the measurement scales, and correlation tests  were
performed to evaluate the relationships between the variables. The conclusion drawn from
the study is that enhanced Islamic financial knowledge is positively correlated to Takaful
uptake. In particular, the result showed that respondents with higher level of Islamic
financial literacy were more likely to be aware  and have a higher intention to patronize
Takaful. Moreover, the available marketing influences, information awareness, and
regulations were seen promote the use of Takaful. These contextual influences were still
less compared to effects of Islamic financial literacy which mean marketing and awareness
are less without enough intensification of financial literacy. However, demographic factors
such as age, income and education level, as explained below, had a relatively smaller impact
on Takaful uptake. The a nalysis presented in this study is a rich source of practical
recommendations for policymakers, banking and financial institutions, as well as Takaful
players. Therefore, there are general recommendations related to the application of the
financial literacy in the sphere of Islamic finance: the policymakers are recommended to
include the information concerning Islamic financial literacy within the framework of the
educational programs at all grades along with providing the regular adult education
programs related to financial literacy.

Introduction
According to Standard and Poor’s Global, only 33% of the world’s population is financially literate. This lack of financial literacy is
even more worrying in Pakistan, which according to Brookings Institution the country stands 16th among the 26 poorest financially
literate nations in the world. Besides, less than a fifth of the population of the people in Pakistan have bank accounts whic h is
evident of their low level of financial literacy. For far beyond what can be often reduced to , financial literacy i s to have a wide
knowledge of the various products in the financial market and the necessary knowledge about them to be able to use them
successfully. A financially literate person is enabled to harness this knowledge in his or her favor in matters of finance with a view
of creating a financially secure future in the ever volatile economy.
Indeed, in times that are marked by growing uncertainty and unpredictability, including economic fluctuations, personal financial
shockers like job loss, illness or divorce, disruption is very much a reality. Within such a setting, it is possible to understand why
Islamic Insurance or Takaful is needed. The Takaful industry has expanded worldwide, and recent studies estimate its CAGR to be
around 14% supported by rising concern for Sharia-compliant Takaful products especially in the Middle East, South East Asia, and
selected African countries. The current leading countries in Takaful industry are Malaysia, Indonesia and some of the GCC countries
since have higher level of awareness, supported by proper regulation and sound base of Islamic finance.
Nevertheless, the situation in Pakistan seems completely different. However, up to today, the rate of uptake of Takaful in Bangladesh
remains low even though the country is a Muslim country. Therefore, the current depth of insurance, including Takaful in Pakistan
is shockingly below the per capita and even the world average remains at less 1% of GDP. Although there is increased interest  in
Shariah-compliant products, a relative ignorance of Takaful concepts and the advantages of using it has restrained its growth. Hanif
and Iqbal (2017) reveal that misconceptions exist even among those who are knowledgeable about Takaful, regarding the way its
operations as well as advantages. This is a general case of low financial literacy in Islamic finance, which hampers both individual
decision making and overall growth of the market for Islamic finance products.
On an international outlook, Takaful has potential to prove itself and demands of  the consumers from the Muslim as well non -
Muslim who are looking for cooperative and ethical financial system. For example, the country of Malaysia has targeted the Islamic
finance sector and has got highly developed and authoritative Takaful systems. On the other hand, the GCC countries especially
Saudi Arabia, and the UAE have incorporated Takaful under the umbrella of vision 2030 economic diversification agenda plans.
However, the Takaful market of Pakistan has not grown to the anticipated level due to structured impediments, low awareness
among consumers, and poor marketing strategies practiced by the financial sector of the country. Thus, the current need for
awareness-raising campaigns to improve the level of knowledge of Islamic finance, the populati on’s trust in Takaful companies,
and the indispensability of the product.
This issue needs to be resolved urgently to increase Takaful all the more to reach the actual goal of financial inclusion in the country
of Pakistan. The Financial Inclusion Insights  survey conducted in Pakistan shows that a meagre 21 percent of the grown -up
population has a fundamental understanding of financial-literacy limiting them to use of tools relevant to Islamic finance. Thus, the
existing conditions reveal a separation between the absence of difficulties in offering Islamic financial products on one hand, and
the lack of their use on the other, which evidences the problem of literacy. Takaful business across the continents has shown that
if awareness is created then growth can be attained, which can serve as role model to Pakistan. The promotion of Islamic Financial
Literacy can open up the potential of Takaful as a major avenue of financial risk management and economically empowering the
society, which is necessary for Pakistan.
Although, there is a gradual shift of focus on IF and greater acknowledgment of IF as an effective substitute of conventional financial
systems and structures (Iqbal & Mirakhor, 2011), however, the involvement of IFL in raising awareness about Takaful products and
services has not received adequate academic attention. Although several past works have found the link between financial literacy
and the usage of orthodox financial products, general financial literacy and its implications for Islamic financi al literacy remain
relatively uncharted, especially in Pakistan (Ahmed, 2018). Furthermore, more studies are needed to understand how different
demographic and contextual factors moderate or mediate with financial literacy to affect the use of Takaful. For instance, Bhatti &
Husin (2020) mentions that the impact of factors like information availability and culture as well as regulations towards Tak aful
penetration remains unknown. However, these gaps are unique to Takaful and this research seeks to fill the  identified gaps by
investigating how financial literacy, demographic factors, and contextual factors influence Takaful awareness and usage level. only

Although Bangladesh is an Islamic republic and the large number of people follows Islamic faith, th e level of Islamic finance is
remarkably low. Takaful is one of the main pillars of the Islamic financial system of which the Pakistan is a part of but the percentage
of people covered by any form of insurance is a meagre 4 % according to the State Bank of  Pakistan. Such a low rate of adoption,
therefore, evokes important questions about what may be causing such low uptake especially in a country where Islamic financial
instruments are culturally and religiously very relevant. This is on account of an unsatisfactory Islamic financial awareness means
that the common populace cannot discern different merits of Takaful. Further, insufficient marketing, low information access, and
regulatory constraints worsen the picture, which affects a significant number of individuals and does not protect them financially.
The current study aims to
 Assess the relationship between Islamic financial literacy and the adoption of Takaful among individuals and businesses
in Pakistan.
 Examine the role of contextual factors (access  to financial information, marketing efforts by Takaful providers, and
regulatory environment) in moderating the relationship between Islamic financial literacy and Takaful adoption.
 Examine the role of demographic factors (age, education level, income, and business sector) in moderating the relationship
between Islamic financial literacy and Takaful adoption.
The present study is significant for several reasons. Firstly, it aims to fill a gap by investigating the impact of Islamic f inancial
literacy on awareness and usage of Takaful. By so doing, it explains the reasons that hinder the adoption of Islamic insurance in
Pakistan with a predominantly Muslim population, indicating a great potential of growth due to increased demand of Shariah
compliant financial services. This study offers essential information on how lack of knowledge about Takaful and other financial
related matters affects the uptake of the product and it offers a positive addition to the theories on the behaviour and decisions in
the Islamic finance realm.
Second, the study advances the literature on financial inclusion and stability through a focus on Takaful as a means of guard ing
people and businesses against financial risks. Less than a fraction of a percent of the Pakistani population h as access to formal
banking an insurance services which makes adopting Takaful an important avenue for enhancing the standard of living. Risk sharing
is one of the ethical and realistic concepts in Islamic finance as Takaful system which makes it suitable to low income and informal
sectors. Through elaborating the relationship between Islamic finance knowledge and Takaful, the study pointed the way how to
improve the financial access in the Pakistani society and, thus, minimize economic risks among the population.
Thirdly, the implication of this research is highly significant for policymakers, financial institutions and Takaful provider s.
Therefore, with the understanding of the reasons why the public accepts or rejects Takaful; the study can guide the nece ssary
interventions required to increase Takaful acceptance amongst the public. As one example, financial literacy programs for different
age groups and domains of activities might solve the problem: rural residents, owners of small businesses, young speci alists, etc.
Awareness creation campaigns, therefore can respond in a big way using both the new applicances and the old artifacts to popularize
Takaful, demystify it and pass necessary information to the commons.
Furthermore, this study has the broader policy level relevance. It can help the policy makers at the regulatory bodies including State
Bank of Pakistan and Securities and Exchange Commission of Pakistan for framing the positive policies and environment to Islamic
finance. For example, rules requiring disclosure of information on Takaful products or offering incentives for Takaful providers to
penetrate under -served markets could improve trust and convenience. In addition, implications of this research may b enefit
financial institutions to develop better Takaful products based on understanding the customers from the consumer behavior
perspective that can fit the Pakistani market well.
Therefore, owing to promoting the development of Islamic finance in Pakist an and due to reflecting the current trends towards
legitimization of Islamic financial instruments as fair and effective counterparts of the conventional systems, this study is
universalistic and relevant. It makes a great emphasis on the relevance of mai nstream Islamic finance for other development
frameworks that address the issues related to the conducted financial sector development, enhancing the population’s access t o
Islamic financial services as well as supporting socioeconomic growth. Summing up it can be stated that this research will be able
to provide definite contributions regarding one of the most significant lacks in the field of Islamic finance, to introduce the notion
of financial inclusion and ensure the effective contribution to the sustainable development in Pakistan.

Literature review
Conventional financial education is similar to, but distinct from, Islamic financial education, in which people must understa nd
Islamic concepts of riba, gharar, mudarabah and musharakah. Rahim, Rashid, an d Hamed (2016) stated that Islamic financial
literacy is defined as the ability to use available financial literacy in a way that reflects Islamic culture. This brings in  a new
dimension of challenge to the scene as most people in these Islamic countries a re still to understand the fundamentals of Islamic
finance. For example, Ayub (2012) pointed out that only through educating clients on the basic tenets of Islamic finance including
qard al-hasan non-associative loans and risk sharing instruments is possible to gain trust and currency within Islamic based bank
arena. However, due to low level of information and knowledge sharing and transfer on these mechanisms much of it is met with
suspicion and misconception, as well as low patronage for products such as Takaful which fall under Islamic finance.
Takaful is an insurance system that holds the Islamic perspective of insurance and it reflects one sector/facets of the Islamic finance.
Takaful, on the other hand, has the attributes of insurance where the members contribute to finance the risk, share the losses, work
under the principle of cooperation and accept the status of mutual assistance and are Shia legal compliant. Investigation also reveals
that Knowledge and perception of Takaful are in proportion to t he level of literacy. Bhatti and Husin (2020) pointed out that the
level of understanding about financial products influence students’ attitudes toward Takaful as better and ethical compared t o
conventional insurance. The above finding goes in line with th e study conducted by Aziz et al. (2017), where it was revealed that
the higher level of IFL makes the respondent to understand the permissibility of Takaful product and the necessity of implementing
it into overall savings.
Also, Kehinde and Sharofiddin (2021) looked at the level of psychological awareness as it presents that the customers’ awareness
of Takaful’s risk sharing model enhances their trust more. Among the psychological factors, trust is one of the most critical for the
choice of financial prod ucts using Islamic finance. According to their study, awareness campaigns stressing on transparency,
concern and ethic nature of mutual helicopter cooperation could do a lot in improving acceptance of Takaful. In addition, analysing
the contingency between financial literacy and demographics, the study identified that factors like age, education attainment and
income significantly affect perceptions of Takaful in the same way. Low awareness about Takaful was the norm among too and
people categorized as younger and educated were found to be more responsive to Takaful which means that awareness should be
done in phases highlighting certain demography.
In addition to personal level characteristics, macro factors for decision making include marketing activities, legal frameworks, and
peoples’ attitude towards Takaful. Continuing, Ahmed and Maqsood (2018) highlighted on the aspect of regulatory policies to foster
an enabling environment of Takaful providers. In some parts of the globe where Islamic finance is not well developed, absence of
laid down rules and structures are some of the factors that may hinder its growth. Other approaches that target Culture and religion
also help in closing this knowledge gap to ensure that the customer target market has adequate k nowledge on the operation of
Takaful product in fulfilling their financial and religious aspirations.
Nevertheless, this study identifies several gaps in the literature with regards to the link between financial literacy and Ta kaful
adoption. Although much emphasis has been placed on the role of knowledge as a factor affecting consumers of Islamic finance,
very little research has focused on the role of demographic, contextual, and psychological factors. The awareness of Takaful is still
very low, and this would go a long way in explaining why Takaful has not been popular among people. A recent survey of Malaysian
consumers conducted by Hassan et al. (2018) shows that respondents have a very limited understanding of Takaful’s essential
functions. While 120 out of 503, respondents did not know anything about Takaful and 203 the remaining respondents did not
grasp the basic notion of Takaful being a risk sharing system. These findings indicate that even in comparator countries, lit tle
awareness of Islamic finance appears to exist. Yousop et al., (2020) conducted a study targeting microenterprises in Malaysia and
realized only a few of the business owners knew about Takaful products but the majority had good knowledge of conventional
insurance products. As such, these facts point up the difficulties seen for Takaful providers towards raising awareness and gaining
trust among potential consumers especially where suppliers target micro and/or new markets.
Areeba Khan et al. (2020) even observed that lack of financial literacy reduces peoples’ ability to differentiate between conventional
insurance and Takaful; such ignorance caused a sharp decline in the Takaful uptake. There is very limited empirical evidence on
Islamic financial literacy and Takaful especially in  the context of Pakistan, which in -turn has aggravated the problem of low
awareness levels. Similarly, Aslam, Shahzad, and Rehman (2020) recognized this research problem by pointing out that most people
in society have little knowledge about Takaful fundamentals. Such ignorance does not only slow down the use of Takaful, but it also
contributes to the continuous exclusion of certain populations in search of a Sharia -compliant financial product. Haron and
Shanmugam (2021) concluded that such a gap can only be closed through various measures with which we fully agree. Promoting

Research Methodology
This study employs a quantitative research methodology based on the research that readily investigates the major variables, which
are helpful for improving Takaful awareness and adoption in Pakistan. So, the relationships between the independent and
dependent variables could be measured and systematically analyzed to perform the objective examination of the hypotheses.
The study primarily focused on three independent variables: Demographic factors, Islamic Financial Literacy and contextual factors.
These variables were selected because they have some potential effect on individuals' awareness about, and willingness to adopt,
Takaful. The independent variables are Takaful Awareness and Adoption and the dependent variable for this research was measured
to determine the degree to which these independent variables affected an individual’s behavior towards adopting Takaful products.

The primary data collection tool designed was the survey questionnaire. A sample of 132 respondents familiar with financial
products with focus on Islamic finance and Takaful was surveyed. Specifically, sample size was chosen to achieve a large enough
sample size to make the statistical analysis reliable. The respondents were selected by convenience sampling. This sampling method
included people whom were exposed to some kinds of financial products included those who had opportunity to use or see some
kinds of Islamic finance and Takaful.
To measure Islamic financial literacy of the respondents in this study, a scale used by Al -Tamimi and Kalli (2009) in a study of
financial literacy in Islamic finance is adapted in this study. This scale contains items that exam respondents’ awareness of  the
concepts of Islamic finance, including the prohibition of riba, mudarabah, musharakah and the working of the I slamic financial
instruments. The level of Takaful awareness and Takaful adoption were operationalized by adapting the scale from Rashid and
Zubair (2016) which focused on the level of knowledge of Takaful products, the perceived benefits of such products, and the intent
to use Takaful products. This scale covers both perceptive and performative items like the recognition of the Takaful firms a nd
whether the respondents, or their families, have used Takaful services. Marketing stimuli included access to information, marketing
activities and regulatory environment and were assed from the model developed by Hassan et al. (2015). The scale aims at
determining the respondent awareness concerning Takaful, the extent to which they come across, notice marketing of Takaful and
their knowledge of the regulatory support for Islamic finance.
The data was then coded numerically to treat the Likert scale items as continuous data, a common practice in survey based research
to facilitate statistical analysis once the data was collected. Then, composite scores were calculated for each of the four sections of
the survey. Specifically, these scores were the scores of each respondent’s Islamic Financial Literacy, Demographic factors,
Contextual factors and Takaful Awareness and  Adoption. Each section averaged the responses in order to provide a more
comprehensive and reliable measure of each construct. A multiple regression analysis was used as the principal statistical method
for the data analysis. The selected technique to gau ge the effect of the independent variables (Islamic Financial Literacy,
Demographic Factors and Contextual Factors) on the dependent variable (Takaful Awareness and Adoption). Multiple regression is
used to examine the impact of the independent variables alone and (in combination) on the dependent variable holding other things
equal. Finally, the most important predictors of Takaful adoption were identified from the regression analysis, as well as the nature
of these relationships. Overall, the methodology was aimed to collect proper data on the factors influencing Takaful adoption in
Pakistan through a structured way, to the researcher, it is allowed to test the hypotheses and draw meaningful conclusions,
supported with statistical evidence.

Discussion
The results of the regression analysis provide important insights into the determinants of Takaful adoption in Pakistan with a
special reference to the roles of Islamic Financial Literacy, Demographic Factors, and Contextual Factors. That analysis points to
the important role of Islamic Financial Literacy in spurring both Takaful Awareness and propensity to Takaful Adoption. More
specifically, the significant and strong positive relationship between Islamic Financial Literacy and Takaful adoption indicates that
those with a better understanding of Islamic financial principles such as riba (usury), mudarabah (profit sharing) and musharakah
(partnership) are much more likely to view Takaful as the viable Sharia compliant alternative to conventional insurance.
The importance of this finding is especially pertinent in the context of Pakistan where, in general, there remains little awa reness
or understanding of Islamic finance within the general population. The sluggish wave of Takaful adoption is due to the reality that
Islamic products are available but Islamic finance principles are not well understood by the public. Such a situation calls f or
educational interventions aimed at improving Islamic financial literacy. The supporting evidence of this conclusion is derived from
correlation analysis which demonstrates strong positive relationship between financial literacy and Takaful adoption.
These findings strongly point to an important link between improving the financial literacy (especially those about the specifics of
Takaful) and increasing the adoption of Sharia compliant insurance products in Pakistan.
This Financial Education Islamic Financial Literacy – Takaful link is highlighting the role of financial education in ensuring
individuals make informed decisions. According to the study, the more a person knows about Islamic finance principles the mor e
confidence he is able to develop in his financial decisions that fit his religious values. Takaful campaigns and prog rammes are
disseminating knowledge on the meaning of Takaful and its benefits. Financial education initiatives can also help to build the trust
in the system, a huge barrier for many potential adopters who are skeptic about alternative financial products.
Furthermore, Across these factors, Contextual factors including the abilities to access information, marketing efforts, and regulatory
support were found to positively influence the adoption of Takaful, however, these are weaker compared to Islamic Financi al
Literacy. Results from the regression indicate that marketing campaigns and regulatory frameworks are important factors in
bringing about awareness of Takaful. But even though these contextual factors are important, they are of no help in increasin g
adoption unless they are accompanied by strong financial literacy initiatives. Without a strong base of knowledge, not even the best
marketing campaigns and regulatory support will have a huge effect on Takaful uptake.
This implies the need to evolve the Tak aful providers’ and policymakers’ and financial institutions’ ways of thinking beyond just
increasing accessibility of funding and marketing activities, which should rather involves educational strategies. Financial literacy
programs can be integrated into  marketing campaigns for Takaful to school the public on the advantages of Takaful, to cure
misconceptions and create trust in sharia allied fiscal merchandise.
Additionally, there is a positive relationship with Takaful adoption by Demographic Factors, s uch as age, income, education, and
religious knowledge, but they did not display statistically significant effect in the regression analysis. This shows that Eg yptian
Takaful compliant adoption behaviors have not been intuitively influenced by factors like income or education as Islamic Financial
Literacy and Contextual Factors are more powerful. It implies that people with a higher income level or more education will tend
to use financial products more often than those with lower education, but the evidence for such an association in this context was
not conclusively statistically significant.

Conclusion and implications
Conclusion
The study is a good contribution in understanding the factors affecting Takaful awareness and its usage in context of Pakistan with
particular emphasis to Islamic Financial Literacy, Demographic Factors and Contextual Factors.
This research ensures that I slamic Financial Literacy is the most powerful determinant of Takaful through the use of regression
analysis, reliability tests and correlation analysis. Islamic Financial Literacy is very essential in the development of indi viduals’
perception of Islamic financial products and their willingness to embrace Takaful as part of the Islamic financial system with
replacement for conventional insurance. Cronbach alpha coefficients produced by the reliability tests suggested high internal
consistency in the assessment of Islamic financial literacy, and thus the constructs used in the measurement of this variable were
dependable. Further, the correlation test also showed positive correlation result in Islamic financial literacy and Takaful adoption
which means that, the greater understanding of Shariah compliant financial concepts such as the working of Takaful the more they
are likely to appreciate and use Takaful product. This do point to why again and again the role of, and more so financial literacy as
a means of helping the understanding gap between the two concepts namely: awareness and adoption. This argument is more so
since Pakistan the study context is a predominantly Muslim country and hereby Takaful is inline with its religious beliefs.
Other Variables like Information Influence and Marketing Influence also came out as highly influencing Factors for Takaful Choice.
The correlation tests proved that those external factors have a positive relationship with the willingness of individuals to embrace
Takaful. This implies that Takaful providers, financial institutions, and governmental bodies must play central role in providing
proper information on Islamic insurance product. Therefore, the study points out that segmented communication strategies,
community enga gement activities, and information technology can improve clientele understanding and build confidence on
Takaful products. Such strategies can help to fill the knowledge deficit where there are barriers to information accessibility.
This study, therefore , holds wide policy implications on the future orientation of financial and Takaful institutions as well as
policymakers. Therefore, it is recommended that policymakers help couch Islamic financial literacy into education syllabi at a
national level and adult education to have most people dubbed adequately informed about the Islamic finance principles. Equally,
the regulatory authorities could encourage Takaful providers to support awareness creation efforts and provide favorable regulatory
environment and infrastructure to support Takaful business.
This could be tax credits, advertising subsidies, or partnership with organizations with a view to promoting the use of Takaful. To
the practitioners, therefore, this research implies that more efforts should be made in the marketing of Takaful where the unique
competitive overtures that differentiate it from conventional insurance can be well sold, demonstrated by the ethical soundness of
the concept, mutual sharing of risks as well as compliance with Shariah rules. For the players to promote the penetration of Takaful
solutions, the misconceptions regarding Takaful need to be clarified, and consumers informed about the overall benefits of Takaful
solutions. Potential areas of differentiation relate first to Takaful’s strong ethical foundations, as well as its potential to offer a
reassurance of financial security.

Implications
This study has important implications for academics, practitioners, policymakers, and other stakeholders who contribute to
enabling Islamic finance and Takaful adoption. Results for policymakers underpin the importance of improving Islamic financial
literacy as a central stimulus to Takaful uptake. The improvement in the public’s knowledge of Islamic principles and the
peculiarities of Takaful products will enable policymakers to set up an environment that would support Islamic finance
development. Achieving this means running nationwide financial literacy campaigns, incorporating the knowledge into curriculum
at educational institutions, and organizing partnerships with community organizations to disseminate the knowledge on Sharia
compliant financial products.
Additionally, the study indicates the influence of context factors, in particular the marketing efforts, information acce ss and the
regulatory environment, in affecting Takaful adoption. This implies that businesses situated within the Islamic finance terrain ought
to allocate resources in order of the strategic development of impactful communication and education projects. Diverse platforms
are available to takaful providers, which include social media campaigns, webinars and interactive mobile applications to appeal to
youth on tech and live the traditional media and community events to reach the old and rural men. Takaful products could also be
bridged by educational seminars, public service announcements and working with religious leaders in helping to bridge the
knowledge gap and build trust in Takaful products.
The study provides useful insights for practitioners such as Takaful providers and Islamic financial institutions in designing target
marketing and communication strategies. Now, these organizations should take efforts to simplify the presentation of Takaful' s
risk sharing and mutual cooperation principles, to mak e them meaningful to prospective customers. However, there is the
possibility of further quelling the public’s skepticism and increasing trust in the Takaful services by enhancing transparency with
regard to operations and governance.
