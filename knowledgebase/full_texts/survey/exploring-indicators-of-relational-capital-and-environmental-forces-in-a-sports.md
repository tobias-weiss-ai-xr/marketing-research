Abstract
Aim/Purpose: In a context where sports sponsorship has become an increasingly important strategic
tool for businesses, this study aim ed to explore and validate a set of relational capital and
environmental force indicators that influence the effectiveness of a sports sponsorship marketing
measurement model  in Thailand. The key problem addressed in this paper is the lack of a
comprehensive model that integrates relational capital and environmental dynamics in shaping
corporate strategies for sports sponsorship, which can limit both marketing effectiveness and return
on investment.

Introduction/Background: In today’s competitive global economy, businesses are increasingly
compelled not only to innovate , but also to develop sustainable and meaningful relationships with
their internal and external stakeholders. Sports sponsorship has evolved into more than a branding
tactic—it now serves a s a strategic relational and environmental mechanism that enables
organizations to project corporate values, engage diverse consumer bases, and cultivate long -term
brand loyalty. Sponsorship can enhance brand image, stimulate emotional engagement, and fost er
mutual trust between sponsors and their audiences. However, despite the rising global emphasis on
sports sponsorship, many Thai organizations continue to adopt inconsistent or ad -hoc sponsorship
strategies that lack a theoretical foundation or empirical validation. This often results in fragmented
collaboration, limited stakeholder impact, and suboptimal returns on investment. Recognizing these
challenges, this paper aimed to fill the gap by developing a conceptual model of marketing relational
capital and environmental forces, and testing its validity among a sample of marketing executives in
varied industry sectors engaged in sports sponsorship within Thailand.

Methodology: In t his study , a mixed -methods approach incorporating both qualitative and
quantitative research  was employed . Initially, in -depth interviews with nine experts were used to
extract critical indicators. The core research instrument was a structured questionnaire composed of
three parts: a) sponsor profiles, b) a 43 -item survey of 12 constructs of relational capital and
environment, and c) open-ended questions regarding sponsorship practices. The sample consisted of
645 marketing executives from eight industrial sectors in  Thailand who were selected through
stratified random sampling. Data analysis was conducted using SPSS 14.0 and AMOS 6.0, employing
Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA) and Partial Least Squares (PLS) techniques to examine the
reliability, validity, and fit of the proposed model.

Findings: The study confirmed that the relational capital and environmental forces model exhibited a
good fit with empirical data. Forty -three indicators were validated across eight core elements: three
from relational capital—moral and trustworthy appeal, emotional appeal, and logical reasoning—and
five from environmental forces —marketing capability, customer orientation, societal impact,
government support, and competitor invasion. Each factor demonstrated acceptable levels of
reliability and construct validity, with factor loadings above .40 and construct reliability scores
exceeding .70. The findings reveal ed that relational capital significantly contribute d to sponsorship
outcomes, particularly through emotional and moral appeals  that resonate with stakeholders.

Contribution/Impact on Society : This research contributes to the existing body of knowledge by
providing a validated framework for assessing relational capital and environmental factors that
influence sports sponsorship performance. The study offers insights into how businesses can
strategically align sponsorship decisions with relational and environmental capabilities to optimize
impact. Furthermore, it highlights the social valu e of ethical and emotionally resonant sponsorships,
demonstrating how relational capital can extend beyond brand promotion to societal development,
especially when aligned with national sporting initiatives and community engagement.

Recommendations: For practitioners, the study recommends adopting the proposed indicators as
strategic benchmarks for planning and evaluating sports sponsorship campaigns. Companies should
prioritize sponsorships that build emotional and moral connections with their target audiences while
remaining sensitive to cultural and market-specific factors. It is crucial to establish transparent, long-
term partnerships with sports entities and to invest in marketing capabilities that support relationship-
building. For researchers, furth er exploration into the dynamic interplay between sponsorship
effectiveness and emerging environmental pressures, such as digital transformation and shifting
consumer values, is advised.

Research Limitation s: Although the sample was drawn from a broad ran ge of industries, it was
geographically limited to Thailand, which may affect the generalizability of findings to other countries.
Additionally, the reliance on self -reported survey data may introduce response bias. The use of CFA
and PLS techniques, while  robust, requires subjective decisions in model construction and indicator
selection. The research also focused on business-to-sport relationships and did not capture consumer-
side perceptions or outcomes, which could provide a more holistic view of sponsorship effectiveness.

Future Research: Future studies should investigate the causal relationships between relational capital
and sponsorship outcomes across different cultural and industry contexts. Expanding the research to
include consumer behavior, sponsor-consumer-brand alignment, and digital engagement strategies
would offer valuable insights. Moreover, longitudinal studies examining the long -term impact of
relational and environmental strategies on sponsorship R eturn on Investment and brand equity  are
warranted. Further exploration into local and national sponsorship models and their respective
success factors can also contribute to developing a global framework for sports marketing.

Introduction
In the context of intense global competition, contemporary organizations must continuously refine
their business strategies and marketing objectives while adhering to ethical standards and principles
of good governance (S haulska et al., 2021). As innovation cycles shorten and consumer behavior
evolves rapidly, firms must adapt not only their products and services , but also their approaches to
stakeholder engagement and accountability. Ethical business practices are no longer optional; rather,
they are now fundamental to long -term sustainability, reputation management, and organizational
legitimacy. From a broader perspective, ethical climates and governance structures can also be
regarded as part of the environmental condit ions that shape trust, transparency, and credibility in
marketing activities.
In Thailand, the marketing ecosystem faces distinctive challenges, including declining consumer
confidence in both domestic and international markets, as well as growing expectations regarding
transparency and integrity in business practices. These challenges reveal a persistent gap between
market potential and effective execution. Addressing such issues requires strong commitment from
organizational leaders to cultivate ethical environments that emphasize responsibility not only toward

shareholders, but also toward employees, customers, and society at large. Prior research suggests that
ethical workplace atmospheres contribute significantly to operational efficiency, employee morale,
and brand integrity, thereby reinforcing sustainable marketing perf ormance (Sh aw, 2014). Thus,
marketing ethics grounded in sound corporate governance form an essential foundation for effective
sponsorship strategies and the development of relational capital in competitive economies.
Environmental forces represent critica l external conditions that shape organizational marketing
performance and sponsorship effectiveness. These forces encompass influences arising from
customers, government agencies, societal expectations, employees, competitive intensity, and
organizational marketing capability. Collectively, such environmental factors establish the institutional
and market context within which trust, legitimacy, and acceptance of sponsorship activities are
formed, thereby contributing to organizational efficiency and productivity (Shaw, 2014).
Within sports sponsorship, the effectiveness of relational capital is often contingent upon
prevailing environmental conditions. Supportive government policies, favorable regulatory
frameworks, and positive social perceptions of sports can enhance sponsorship outcomes, whereas
limited institutional support or heightened competitive pressures may constrain marketing
performance. Consequently, examining sports sponsorship solely through relational interactions may
provide an incomplete und erstanding of sponsorship effectiveness. Instead, sponsorship outcomes
should be viewed as emerging from the interaction between relational capital and broader
environmental forces (Cornwell & Kwon, 2020).
This perspective is particularly relevant in the T hai sports context, where sports organizations
frequently operate under close supervision and support from government agencies, and where
sponsorship activities are influenced by public funding structures, policy directions, and social
expectations. Integr ating environmental forces into the sports sponsorship marketing framework
therefore allows for a more accurate representation of real-world sponsorship dynamics in Thailand.
Sports sponsorship is closely aligned with market orientation, whereby business o bjectives are
integrated with customer values and societal trends. Prior research has identified several indicators
of effective sponsorship grounded in principles of good governance, including brand image
enhancement (Wakefield et al., 2020), mutual event  marketing that fosters brand –event synergy
(Wiedmann et al., 2018), and effective communication strategies that sustain consistent engagement
across multiple platforms (Cobbs et al., 2021). These elements contribute to increased enjoyment of
sporting even ts, stronger fan engagement, and broader recognition of sponsoring brands.
Furthermore, appropriate budget allocation and commercialization strategies are essential to ensure
that sponsorship activities generate both emotional and financial returns. Unlike  traditional
advertising, sports sponsorship enables brands to embed themselves within consumer lifestyles,
creating more authentic and memorable experiences. Strategic sponsorship has been shown not only
to yield measurable returns, but also to strengthen long-term relational ties with consumers (Cornwell
et al., 2016). Industry reports have further indicated that sports sponsorship represents one of the
most promising growth areas within the global sports sector.
Creating competitive advantage and fostering inter-organizational collaboration through relational
capital (RC) has become a strategic imperative in today’s knowledge -driven economy. Relational
capital encompasses networks, trust, loyalty, and shared understanding that sustain long -term
relationships between organizations and stakeholders. These intangible assets are developed through
responsiveness, reliability, and value co -creation, and have been widely recognized as a key
component of intellectual capital influencing organizational performance (Korsakiene et al., 2017). In
dynamic market environments, firms increasingly rely on relational knowledge embedded within
organizational processes to support collaboration and decision -making, particularly in sponsorship
partnerships. Customer enga gement initiatives, for example, enhance brand visibility and trust,
thereby strengthening relational capital (Calderón -Monge & Ramírez -Hurtado, 2022). Moreover,
relational capital facilitates knowledge sharing, innovation, and organizational learning, whi ch are
essential for sustaining long-term partnerships in sports sponsorship contexts (Xue et al., 2017).

Despite the growing importance of sports sponsorship as a strategic marketing tool, there remains
a significant research gap in understanding how rela tional capital and environmental forces jointly
shape sports sponsorship marketing outcomes. Addressing this gap is particularly important in
emerging and transitional markets such as Thailand, where sponsorship effectiveness is influenced by
both relational dynamics and complex environmental conditions. Accordingly, this study s ought to
explore and validate key indicators of relational capital and environmental forces within a sports
sponsorship marketing framework, providing a more comprehensive understan ding of sponsorship
dynamics in contemporary competitive environments.

Research Objective
To explore indicators of relational capital , moral and trustworthy appeal , emotional and logical
appeal, and environmental forces such as marketing capability, customer orientation, societal impact,
government support and competitor invasion in a sports sponsorship marketing measurement model.

Scope of the Study
This study focuse d on the development and validation of a sports sponsorship marketing
measurement model that integrates indicators of relational capital and environmental forces
influencing sports sponsorship in Thailand. The scope was limited to industrial business sectors that
are actively involved in or have experience with sports sponsorship. Specifically, data was collected
from 645 marketing executives across eight industries, including agro-food, consumer goods, finance,
industrial products, real estate and construction, natural resources, services, and te chnology. The
study emphasizes the identification of observable indicators from both relational and environmental
dimensions that contribute to effective sponsorship outcomes. The scope also included examining the
strategic implications of these indicators for marketing practices, stakeholder engagement, and brand
development. Geographically, the research was confined to the Thai context, which may reflect
localized business dynamics and sponsorship behaviors. Data collection was conducted from January
to May 2024, with data analysis completed within the same period. Therefore, the findings reflect the
prevailing marketing conditions and industry practices during that time.

Theoretical Framework
The theoretical framework of this study is grounded in the concepts of relational capital and
environmental forces , which are key constructs in  sports sponsorship marketing. Rather than
emphasizing normative or ethical prescriptions, the framework focused on organizing and explaining
the structural dimensions thro ugh which these constructs are conceptualized and measured within
the sponsorship context.
Relational capital is understood as a strategic intangible resource that emerges from sustained
interactions among stakeholders, including trust, cooperation, commit ment, and shared
understanding. Prior studies have identified relational capital as a critical component of intellectual
capital that facilitates collaboration, knowledge sharing, and long -term partnership development,
particularly in marketing and sponsorship activities (Xue et al., 2017). In sports sponsorship, relational
capital enables organizations to strengthen relationships with sports institutions, partners, and
consumers, thereby enhancing credibility and continuity in sponsorship engagements.
Environmental forces are conceptualized as external contextual conditions that shape the
environment in which sports sponsorship activities operate. These forces include government
involvement, societal expectations, competitive intensity, and organizational m arketing capability.
Such environmental conditions do not function as causal determinants in this study; rather, they
provide the contextual framework within which relational capital is developed and sponsorship
strategies are implemented. Prior research has suggested that marketing functions operate within
these environmental conditions, and organizational practices must be aligned with broader social and
institutional expectations (Walker & Hills, 2017).

Literature Review
Sports sponsorship has emerged as one of the most prominent and effective marketing approaches
in both the business and industrial sectors globally. According to the International Events Group
Sponsorship Report (202 0), approximately 69% of annual sponsors hip budgets are allocated
specifically to sports -related activities.  This significant proportion reflects not only corporate
marketing trends, but also a broader societal engagement with sports as a lifestyle and commercial
platform. In Thailand, the publi c sector plays an important role in fostering this environment. The
Sports Authority of Thailand (SAT), the main government body overseeing sports development,
allocates a substantial annual budget—approximately 3,061 million Baht—to support a wide range of
sports events, programs, and services. This institutional support provides critical infrastructure that
enables both public and private entities to engage in collaborative sponsorship, thereby creating a
well-established ecosystem for sports marketing and economic development.
Sports sponsorship focuses on marketing -oriented activities that enhance brand image, event
visibility, and communication support (Wakefield et al., 2020), as well as competition and marketing
opportunities (Cornwell et al., 2016).
Relational capital and environmental forces serve as the underlying mechanisms that connect
product brands with consumers through strategic sponsorship —often via celebrity endorsements or
high-profile sporting events. In the Thai context, the market is highly fragmented, with varying cultural
and regional dynamics across the country. This makes sponsorship -based marketing particularly
effective as it allows brands to establish direct emotional and symbolic connections with diverse
consumer groups. Integrated sponsorship strategies can not only elevate brand visibility, but they also
contribute to brand differentiation through creative use of media and interpersonal communication.
Crompton (2015) emphasized that sports sponsorship, when executed effectively, provides firms with

underscores the relevance of sports sponsorship as a marketing tool. Nonetheless, there remains a

The success of brand support through sports sponsorship depends heavily on clear marketing
objectives, effective segmentation, and content strategies that align with the values of both the brand
and its target audience. Bacon (2015 ) stressed that digital content design and data -driven sponsor
selection are critical for enhancing consumer perception and increasing return on investment.
However, in many cases, sponsors still struggle to establish a meaningful connection between the
sponsored event and their brand identity, often viewing sponsorship purely as a financial transaction
rather than as a relationship-building opportunity. Cultural context and consumer expectations play
significant roles in how sponsorship messages are received. Research by Cheung et al. (2019) showed
that consumers tend to rely on past positive experiences with brands when evaluating sponsorship
value, emphasizing the importance of consistency and authenticity in sponsor messaging. Conversely,
lack of transpa rency, sincerity, or mutual understanding can lead to contract breakdowns between
sports institutions (often governmental) and corporate sponsors (Jensen  & Cornwell, 2017). When
sponsorship is strategically aligned and mutually beneficial, however, it fosters long -term
relationships, improves brand recall, and builds consumer loyalty through repeated exposure across
communication channels (Yang & Ha, 2014).
Relational Capital (RC) has become increasingly vital in today’s knowledge -based and rapidly
evolving business environment. Firms seek to develop RC as a strategic asset that complements
technological advancement and drives competitive advantage (Kavida & Sivakoumar , 2009). RC
encompasses the accumulated value of relationships with customers, partners, and stakeholders, and
has been shown to directly influence marketing performance, sales potential, and even firm valuation
in capital markets (Dženopoljac et al., 2016 ; Sardo & Serrasqueiro, 2017). These relational assets,
though intangible, contribute to long -term value and reputation, which can significantly affect stock
market confidence and brand equity (Vishnu & Gupta, 2014). Meles et al. (2016) further argued that
RC plays a regulatory role in managing external forces, enabling firms to adapt, survive, and excel
under shifting market conditions. This study thus emphasises identifying key indicators of RC and
environmental forces relevant to the sports sponsorship context, especially for industries seeking to
measure and enhance their marketing effectiveness.
This study conceptualized relational capital through three primary forms of appeal: a) moral and
trustworthy, b) emotional, and c) logical. These appeals form the basis of consumer -brand
relationships in sponsorship contexts. First, the moral and trustworthy appeal establishes credibility
and authority, ensuring that sponsors are perceived as ethical, reliable, and aligned with public values
(Han & Laurent, 2023). This is particularly critical in the digital era, where consumers are highly
engaged and perceptive across online and offline media channels. Second, emotional appeal draws
on personal sentiments, cultural values, and belief systems to form deep psychological bonds between
brands and their audiences. Studies by Käpylä and Kennedy (2014) and Xie and Lou (2020) affirmed
that emotional resonance plays a key role in fostering brand loyalty and increasing sponsorship
effectiveness. Lastly, the logical reason appeal revolves around constructing rational, coherent
messaging that is aligned with consumer expectations and enhan ces decision -making processes
(Morrison, 2007; Tirkolaee et al., 2020). Shin et al. (2020) emphasized that a sound understanding of
relational dynamics is essential for brands aiming to cultivate enduring customer relationships through
sponsorship.

The measurement mod el was developed through a multi -stage procedure. Initially, 43 observed

Subsequently, the indicators were refined and reorganized into 12 first-order constructs based on

construct clarity and measurement precision while maintaining theoretical consistency. The 12 first -
order constructs were then specified as components of two higher -order latent constructs , namely
relational capital and environmental forces.
Confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) was conducted to validate both the first-order and second-order
measurement structures. This analysis enabled the assessment of factor loadings, construct reliability,
convergent validity, and overall model fit, thereby providing empirical support for the proposed
measurement framework.

Population and Sample
The study’s population comprised marketing executives from various industrial business sectors in
Thailand who w ere involved in or had experience with sports sponsorship. The sample size
determination followed the guidelines for Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA) using the Maximum
Likelihood Estimation technique. According to Hair et al. (2019), an appropriate sampl e size for CFA
should be at least 15 times the number of observed variables in the model.
In this research, a total of 43 observed variables were identified as part of the proposed relational
capital and environmental force measurement model. Based on this  calculation, the minimum
required sample size was 645 respondents (43 variables × 15 = 645). This sample size met the
recommended statistical threshold to ensure adequate power and model stability for CFA.
The sample was drawn from eight different industr ial sectors using stratified random sampling,
which ensured proportional representation across sectors. These sectors included agr iculture and
food, consumer products, finance, industrial products, real estate and construction, natural resources,
services, and technology. The stratification provided a comprehensive cross -sectional view of sports
sponsorship practices within the Thai industrial context, listed in the Stock Exchange of Thailand, thus
enhancing the generalizability and relevance of the findings as shown in Table 1.

Research Tools
The research instrument employed in this study was a structured questionnaire, with items
primarily adapted from established measurement scales used in previous studies (e.g., Bollen, 2014;
Esteghamati et al., 2010; Hair et al., 2019). Content validity was ensured through nine expert reviews
using the Item-Objective Congruence (IOC) Index, with all items achieving scores above .50. Reliability
was assessed using Cronbach’s alpha, with all constructs exceeding the reco mmended threshold of
.70, indicating high internal consistency. Construct validity was examined through Confirmatory Factor
Analysis (CFA), with results showing satisfactory factor loadings (λ > .40), Composite Reliability (CR >
.70), and Average Variance Extracted (AVE > .50). Discriminant validity was further confirmed by
comparing the square root of AVE for each construct with its inter-construct correlations.

Data Collection
The data collection process in this study began with the development of research instruments
based on in-depth interviews with 9 experts in the businesses of agro and goods services, real estate
and construction, technology, and one academician in the field of marketing and sports sponsorship.
Insights from these interviews were synthesized to construct a comprehensive questionnaire designed
to capture key dimensions of relational capital and environmental forces. The finalized instrument was
distributed to  645 marketing executives using a self -administered format. The questionnaire was
divided into three main parts. Part One gathered general information about the respondents,
including sponsor characteristics and types of sports sponsorships, using a checklist format. Part Two
consisted of 43 items measuring indicators of relationship marketing using a five -point Likert scale
ranging from 1 (no t practiced) to 5 (most frequent or important). Part three included open -ended
questions that solicited respondents’ perspectives and suggestions regarding relationship marketing
strategies in their business sector and their involvement in sports sponsorship activities. This mixed -
format structure ensured that both quantitative and qualitative data could be collected, o ffering a
well-rounded view of current practices and perceptions in the Thai sports sponsorship landscape.

Data Analysis
Reliability was assessed using Cronbach’s alpha coefficient , with a passing reliability value set at
.70 and above. Analysis was conducted by using SPSS version 14.0 and AMOS version 6.0 on sports
sponsorship and marketing relational capital with frequency descriptive analysis, skewness , and
kurtosis. To analyze the modeling of marketing relational capital in Thai industries, secondary order
confirmatory factor relationship analysis technique was performed by utilizing indices  such as
CMIN/DF, GFI, AGFI, NFI, CFI, RMR and RMSEA (Bollen, 2014). In summary, the eight variables of
marketing relational capital were tested using confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) and the Partial Least
Squares technique, with  standardized factor loading (λ) and coefficient of determination ( R2)
(Esteghamati et al., 2010).

Research Results
Confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) was  conducted to evaluate the validity of the proposed
measurement model comprising relational capital and environmental forces. The analysis followed a
hierarchical approach, beginning with the assessment of first -order constructs and subsequently
examining the second-order measurement structure.
At the first-order level, the 43 observed indicators were loaded onto their respective 12 constructs,
with standardized factor loadings exceeding the recommended threshold of .40. These results
indicated that the indicators adequately represented the underlying dimensions of sports sponsorship
marketing and demonstrated satisfactory indicator reliability.

Discussion of Findings
This study validated a model of relational capital (RC) and environmental forces that significantly
influences the effectiveness of sports sponsorship in Thailand. Emotional appeal and custo mer
orientation demonstrated the highest factor loadings, confirming their strong role in shaping
sponsorship success. Moral and trustworthy appeal, along with marketing capability, also emerged as
critical drivers, underscoring that both relational and en vironmental dimensions are essential for
achieving sustainable sponsorship outcomes. The model showed good fit indices, confirming the
robustness of the proposed framework.
The findings reinforce previous research highlighting the importance of emotional engagement in
sponsorship-linked marketing (Cornwell et al., 2016; Käpylä & Kennedy, 2014). They also confirm ed
Cornwell and Kwon (2020), who argued that sponsorship effectiveness is driven by relational ties
rather than transactional exchanges. Conversely,  our results partly challenge Crompton (2015), who
emphasized the risks of negative sponsorship outcomes; while risks exist, this study suggests that well-
managed relational capital indicators (trustworthiness, sincerity, and emotional appeal) can mitigate
these risks. Moreover, alignment with customer orientation supports Pitts and Zhang’s (2020)
argument that sponsorship effectiveness is maximized when brand strategies reflect consumer and
societal expectations.
This research extends the literature by integrating relational capital with environmental forces into
a unified sponsorship model. While RC has traditionally been studied as an intangible asset, our results
showed its interactive role with environmental factors such as competitor invasion and gover nment
support. This interaction contributes to a more comprehensive understanding of how sponsorship
strategies operate within complex, multi -stakeholder environments. The study thus adds to
intellectual capital theory by demonstrating that RC is not only a relational asset, but also a strategic
buffer and amplifier of environmental pressures.
For marketing executives, these findings provide clear guidance. Sponsorship strategies should
prioritize building emotional and moral connections with audiences while simultaneously investing in
marketing capability and customer orientation. Transparent, long -term partnerships with sports
organizations can enhance trust and credibility, while a strong customer orientation ensures that

dynamics such as competitor actions and policy changes, using the validated indicators as benchmarks
for decision-making. The study results raise awareness of a trial and reinforcement model to compare

response patterns, the fact is that organizers cannot expect to receive unlimited budgetary funds due
to the need for return on investment.
The study was limited to the Thai context, which may restrict generalizability to other countries
with different sponsorship ecosystems. Data were also self -reported by marketing executives, which
may have introduced potential bias. Future research should include consumer perspectives to capture
how sponsorship affects brand perception and loyalty more directly. Cross -cultural studies and
longitudinal designs are also recommended to test the stability of relational an d environmental
indicators over time and across markets.

Recommendations
Academic Recommendations
Marketing relational capital activities should be supported through moral, trustworthy, emotional,
and logical appeals of leading firms; this is crucial in relationship sports marketing. Another guideline
is to c onsider leading relational marketing indicators as drivers for uncertain and diverse
environments.

Future Research Recommendations
1. Develop a causal model of marketing relational capital global sports sponsorship success by
analyzing competitiveness and market opportunities, including market structure.
2. Pay attention to sports sponsorship, the application of market development, consumer
evaluation, and decision-making behavior.
3. Finally, a valid further study of sports sponsorship should focus on antecedents and successful
outcomes of events through levels of local and national sponsors’ analysis modeling approach.
