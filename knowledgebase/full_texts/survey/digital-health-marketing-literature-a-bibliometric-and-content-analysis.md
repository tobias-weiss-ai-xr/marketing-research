ABSTRACT
This study examines academic publications in the field of digital health marketing using bibliometric and content analysis to
identify current trends, research gaps, and future research directions. A comprehensive analysis was conducted on studies
published between 2000 and 2025, focusing on publication performance, citation structures, and thematic developments
within the literature. The findings indicate that research topics such as e-health, mobile health applications, and telemedicine
are particularly prominent in digital health marketing studies, and that scholarly output in this field increased rapidly following
the COVID-19 pandemic. The United States emerges as the leading country in terms of both publication volume and citation
impact, while China demonstrates high publication output but comparatively lower academic impact. The results of the study
suggest that artificial intelligence, data security, and innovative strategies aimed at enhancing user engagement are likely to
play a significant role in shaping future research directions in digital health marketing.
Keywords: Digital Health Marketing, E-Health, Telehealth, Mobile Health Applications, Artificial Intelligence in Healthcare.

INTRODUCTION
The digital transformation that began with the
widespread adoption of computers in business life
during the 1980s expanded further with the diffusion
of the internet in the early 2000s, affecting nearly
all areas of daily life, from banking and education to
shopping and communication. This transformation has
also altered the traditionally face-to-face nature of the
service sector, leading to the emergence of digital service
delivery models. The COVID-19 pandemic significantly
accelerated this process in healthcare, leading to the
rapid development and widespread adoption of various
e-health frameworks and digital health services (Pauzi
and Juhari, 2020; Schiliro, 2020, 2021; Park et al., 2022;
Wahab and Saad, 2022).
Digital health technologies encompass a wide
range of tools and systems, including telemedicine,
mobile health applications, wearable devices, artificial
intelligence (AI)-supported diagnostic systems, and
digital health platforms. These technologies facilitate
numerous healthcare processes, from routine patient
care to preventive health services. Wearable devices, in
particular, enable individuals to monitor and manage
key indicators of their own health status. By addressing
the priorities and preferences of major stakeholders
in the healthcare ecosystem—such as healthcare
providers, pharmaceutical companies, medical device
manufacturers, and patients—digital health technologies
have created new channels for service delivery. In parallel
with these developments, marketing communication
has become a critical component in promoting digital
health solutions and ensuring their adoption by target
audiences (Pauzi and Juhari, 2020; Schiliro, 2020; Park et
al., 2022).
The increasing digitalization of healthcare services
has inevitably transformed health marketing practices.
As patients and healthcare professionals become
more accustomed to digital technologies, a rapidly
expanding market has emerged, characterized by
continuous innovation and strong growth dynamics.
Within this market, marketing activities play a central
role not only in informing stakeholders about new
digital health products and services but also in shaping
user perceptions, encouraging adoption, and collecting

Despite the growing body of research on digital health

remains fragmented and lacks a comprehensive,
longitudinal mapping that systematically identifies

understanding of the field and to identify underexplored
areas that may guide future research.
Based on this identified gap, the present study aims
to examine the academic literature on digital health
marketing using bibliometric and content analysis
methods. Publications indexed in the Web of Science
database between 2000 and 2025 are analyzed to
map the evolution of research in this domain, identify
dominant themes and emerging topics, and assess the
contributions of leading countries, institutions, and
journals. In doing so, the study provides a structured
overview of how digital health technologies—such as
telemedicine, mobile health applications, digital health
platforms, health analytics, and artificial intelligence—
have been addressed within marketing-related research.
In line with this objective, the study addresses the
following research questions:
• What major trends and research patterns characterize
the existing academic literature on digital health
marketing?
• Which countries, institutions, and journals play a
leading role in shaping the field of digital health
marketing?
• What thematic areas and concepts have emerged as
dominant, niche, basic, or emerging within digital
health marketing research?
• In what ways has the COVID-19 pandemic influenced
the growth, orientation, and research priorities of
digital health marketing studies?
• What research gaps and future directions can be
identified to guide academics, practitioners, and
policymakers in this field?
The novelty and contribution of this study lie in
providing a comprehensive bibliometric and content-
based mapping of digital health marketing research over
a 25-year period. By integrating performance analysis
with science mapping techniques, the study offers an
evidence-based framework that enhances conceptual
clarity and supports future theoretical and practical
research in the field.
CONCEPTUAL FRAMEWORK
This section provides a conceptual background for the
study by outlining the main components and sub-fields
of digital health technologies commonly addressed in
the literature. Rather than proposing or testing specific
theories, the purpose of this section is to establish a shared
conceptual framework that facilitates the interpretation
of the bibliometric and content analysis results. The
concepts discussed in this section serve as reference
points for understanding the thematic structures,
keyword clusters, and research trends identified in the
subsequent analyses. In this respect, Section 2 supports
the analytical findings by contextualizing them within
the broader digital health and marketing landscape,
thereby enhancing the coherence and interpretability of
the study.
Definition and Market Size of Digital Health
Technologies
The term digital health technologies refers to delivering
healthcare services and products through digital
means, incorporating elements such as telemedicine,
mobile health applications, wearable technologies, AI-
based diagnostic tools, and digital health platforms
(Topol, 2019; Abernethy et al., 2022). Research findings
consistently demonstrate that these digital health tools
enhance clinical efficiency, minimize complications and
adverse incidents, alleviate patients’ social and financial
difficulties, and improve overall well-being (Abernethy
et al., 2022; Kainiemi et al., 2023). In addition, these
technologies enable the real-time remote monitoring,
identification, and transmission of physiological
parameters for patients receiving remote consultations,
making it possible both to provide online medical advice
for preventive measures and to respond promptly to vital
parameters in emergencies (Merchant et al., 2018; Barrett
et al., 2013; Abernethy et al., 2022).
With such benefits, digital health solutions that
meet the priorities of major healthcare stakeholders,
including providers, pharmaceutical industries, device
manufacturers, and patients, have advanced in line with

momentum of successive major technological advances
and cultural shifts, has led to a digital revolution in today’s
healthcare sector.
 Scope of Digital Health Technologies
Digital health products, which use technology to
help individuals improve their health and well-being,
cover a wide variety, ranging from consumer wearables
to ingestible sensors, from mobile health applications
developed by healthcare providers to artificial
intelligence, from robotic caregivers to electronic records.
According to a categorization proposed in a report by
the global consulting firm Deloitte on the digital health
sector, and widely accepted in the literature, the market
is divided into four sub-sectors, as shown in Figure 1
(Hampson et al., 2015).
Telemedicine: Conventional healthcare practices
often involved hospital visits after symptom onset,
followed by diagnostic procedures, treatment planning,
and interventions aimed at reducing or alleviating
symptoms. Through telemedicine, such care can now be
administered without in-person interaction, using the
remote transfer of clinical data between patients and
doctors (Kim and Jeong, 2020). Telemedicine represents
one of the most rapidly expanding branches of digital
health. By 2024, the market size had reached USD 161.64
billion, with projections indicating a 22.9% growth rate
that would expand the sector to USD 791.04 billion by
2032. This trajectory is reinforced by the necessity of
healthcare access in rural communities and the post-
pandemic demand for digital medical services (Fortune
Business Insights, 2025; Grand View Research, 2025).

Mobile Health Applications : Wearable devices
and their applications form part of this segment. Such
devices, which track activity, cardiovascular rate, sleep
cycles, and blood pressure, have become essential to
digital health delivery. Complementary mobile apps
enhance performance by improving information flow
and interaction. Efforts to optimize processes and lower
expenses in healthcare services are key drivers of mobile
technology development. Future advancements are
anticipated to evolve in tandem with artificial intelligence
and machine learning. The global market for mobile
health applications, estimated at USD 59.13 billion in
2023, is projected to grow to USD 160.0 billion by 2032
(Market Research Future, 2025).
Digital Health Platforms : Digital health platforms
provide integrated solutions that connect healthcare
providers and patients. Within this framework, the patient
records domain involves both storing digital information
and exchanging electronic medical data (Hampson et al.,
2015). Sources such as genetic profiles, personal health
data, electronic records, and national datasets generate
value for early disease detection, therapy development,
and improved efficiency in healthcare institutions.
With the emergence of services that utilize biometric
information, the medical sector is expected to expand
more rapidly in this field (Park, Song, and Lee, 2020). The
market, valued at USD 25 billion in 2022, is projected to
grow at a 20.1% annual rate between 2023 and 2030
(Frost & Sullivan, 2023).
Health Analytics : Including data mining, advanced
analytics, and visualization, this segment has grown in
significance for meeting the information requirements
of healthcare stakeholders. Well-structured analytics
hold potential to redefine healthcare organizations,
discover new value streams, and differentiate them
in the market (Hagan et al., 2019). As data sets grow in
size and complexity, alongside heightened competition,
regulation, and innovations such as precision medicine
and value-based healthcare, health analytics has become
a fundamental dimension of digital health. Its applications
include AI-assisted real-time imaging diagnostics,
treatment management tailored to diseases and genes,
and worldwide data integration and evaluation.
Recent studies indicate that a fifth segment has
emerged alongside the four traditional sub-sectors,
particularly after 2022, driven by expanding applications
of AI in medicine. Such technologies offer capabilities
that surpass human performance, much like their
transformative impact on science, the economy, politics,
transportation, and media (Shin, 2019).

patient monitoring, and clinical decision support systems.
Through machine learning and deep learning algorithms,
they enable the analysis of large datasets that form the
basis of health analytics and the extraction of meaningful
insights from these data. For example, AI-based imaging
systems can detect abnormalities in radiological images

RESEARCH AIM
The purpose of this research is to deliver an in-depth
evaluation of the scholarly literature on digital health
marketing using bibliometric and content analytical
approaches. The study aims to map the evolution
of research in this field, identify the main trends
and thematic structures, and highlight the leading
countries, institutions, and journals that contribute to
its development. By doing so, it examines how digital
health technologies such as telemedicine, mobile health
supported clinical decision support systems enable
physicians to make more informed decisions during
treatment processes. These technologies have significant
potential for personalizing healthcare services and early
disease diagnosis (Jiang et al., 2017; Topol, 2019; Esteva,
2021). The increasing deployment of AI, combined
with momentum from improvements in technological
infrastructure, has driven the healthcare AI market to
USD 14.92 billion by 2024. By 2030, it is expected to
rise to USD 164.16 billion, with a yearly growth rate
of 49.1% (Markets and Markets, 2024). This growth is
supported by advantages such as improved efficiency in
healthcare services, reduced error rates, and automated
clinical processes. Processing health analytics through
AI-supported systems enables healthcare institutions
to gain valuable insights in critical areas, such as early
disease diagnosis, prediction of clinical outcomes, and
patient risk analysis (He et al., 2019). Functions such as
real-time medical image analysis, integration of patient
records, and identification of epidemiological trends
are among the points where health analytics and AI
converge. Through the integration of these technologies,
decision-making processes in healthcare are accelerated,
and clinical effectiveness is enhanced.
A review of the health sciences literature reveals that
digital health applications and their sub-segments
are attracting significant interest, with continuously
increasing and diversifying areas of application. Market
analysis reports on digital health technologies also
demonstrate the sector’s rapid growth and present
forecasts that this trend will continue. In light of the
literature reviewed above, this study aims to reveal the
reflection of interest in these two areas on the marketing
literature. To determine this reflection, articles in the
field of digital health marketing included in the Web of
Science database were first examined using bibliometric
analysis to identify current trends and research areas.
Then, using content analysis, the main themes, research
topics, and future trends in digital health marketing were
identified. The methodology, data, and research process
used in this study are explained in detail below.

FINDINGS AND DISCUSSION
The 324 articles examined within the scope of this
study were published over a time span covering the
years 2000–2025. This section first presents the results of
the performance analysis and subsequently reports the
findings of the science mapping analysis. A total of 206
different sources (such as journals and books) contributed
to these publications, showing an annual growth rate of
6.2%, indicating a steady expansion of scholarly interest
in digital health marketing over time. On average, the
studies were 5.9 years old and received 19.88 citations per
article. Table 1 provides a general overview of the dataset
and summarizes the main descriptive characteristics of the
analyzed literature. As shown in Table 1, an examination
of document contents revealed that the studies used 840
Keywords Plus terms and 1,223 author-defined keywords.
A total of 1,566 different authors contributed to these
articles, with only 34 authors producing single-authored
works. The number of single-authored articles was 35,
and it was determined that an average of 5.09 authors
collaborated per article. However, no international co-
authorship was identified. These findings suggest that
the literature on digital health marketing is expanding
across a wide spectrum and has continued to grow in
recent years, with increasing momentum.

constitutes the first part of the bibliometric analysis, the

DISCUSSION
The present study provides a comprehensive
bibliometric and content-based overview of the academic
literature on digital health marketing, revealing both the
structural characteristics and thematic evolution of the
field over a 25-year period. The findings demonstrate
that digital health marketing has emerged as a rapidly
expanding research domain, with a particularly
pronounced acceleration following the COVID-19
pandemic. This pattern is consistent with the broader
digital health literature, which highlights the pandemic
as a critical turning point in the adoption, diffusion, and
commercialization of digital health technologies across
healthcare systems worldwide.
One of the most notable findings of the performance
analysis is the dominant role of the United States in
digital health marketing research. The United States leads
the field in terms of publication output, institutional
productivity, and citation impact. This dominance
aligns with existing research on digital health and
healthcare innovation, which frequently emphasizes
the country’s advanced technological infrastructure,
strong integration between academia and industry, and
early adoption of digital health solutions. In contrast,
although China exhibits a high volume of publications,
its comparatively lower citation impact suggests that
the international academic influence of its research in
digital health marketing has not yet reached a similar
level. This pattern mirrors observations in related areas of
healthcare innovation research, where rapid publication
growth does not always translate into proportional
scholarly impact.
The analysis also reveals that international research
collaboration in digital health marketing remains
relatively limited, with studies largely concentrated
within national boundaries. This finding contrasts with
the increasingly globalized nature of digital health
markets and technologies. The limited extent of cross-
country collaboration may restrict the exchange of
diverse perspectives and slow the development of
globally applicable marketing frameworks for digital
health solutions. From a theoretical standpoint, this
fragmentation suggests that the field is still in a
developmental phase, where national contexts strongly
shape research agendas and empirical focus.
The science mapping results further enrich the
understanding of the field by highlighting its thematic
structure. The prominence of telemedicine, mobile health
applications, and e-health as core themes is consistent
with prior research emphasizing these technologies as
foundational components of digital healthcare delivery.
Their central positioning within the thematic maps
indicates that these areas constitute the conceptual
backbone of digital health marketing research. At the
same time, the emergence of artificial intelligence–based
applications and health analytics as developing themes
suggests a gradual shift toward more data-driven and
personalized approaches to healthcare marketing.
Gamification strategies aimed at increasing user
engagement represent another noteworthy thematic
area identified in the analysis. The growing attention
to gamification aligns with existing studies in health
communication and behavioral science, which
underscore the importance of motivation, adherence,
and user experience in the successful adoption of
digital health solutions. However, the positioning of
gamification-related themes within the broader thematic
structure indicates that this line of research is still evolving
and has not yet reached full maturity within the digital
health marketing literature.
A significant contribution of this study lies in its
ability to identify research gaps that may not be readily
apparent in single-study analyses. In particular, the
relatively peripheral positioning of themes related to
data privacy, algorithmic bias, and ethical considerations
suggests that these issues remain underexplored from a
marketing perspective, despite their growing relevance
in digital health practice. This finding partially contrasts
with the broader digital health literature, where concerns
regarding data security and ethical governance are
increasingly prominent. The discrepancy suggests
a potential misalignment between technological
development and marketing-oriented academic
research.
Finally, although the primary focus of this study is global,
the findings also allow for contextual reflection on national
research landscapes. In light of this global analysis, the first
observation regarding Turkey is that academic interest in
the field is quite limited. In the dataset downloaded from
the WoS database, only two Turkish scholars, each with two
publications, were identified. Based on this observation,
a search was conducted in the national citation index,
TrDizin, using the same keywords on 07 May 2025, and
only two studies were found with these keywords. This
reveals a significant research gap in the Turkish academic
literature. While Turkish companies may not hold key
positions in this ecosystem, Turkey, with its large and aging
population, has substantial market potential.

IMPLICATIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS
Based on the findings of this bibliometric and content
analysis, several implications and recommendations can
be outlined for key stakeholders involved in digital health
marketing, including public authorities, practitioners,
and academics.
Implications for Public Authorities
For public authorities, it is recommended to prioritize
the development of internationally harmonized
standards in digital health marketing. In particular,
aligning data privacy regulations and ethical frameworks
across countries may facilitate the healthy and
sustainable growth of digital health markets. Such efforts
could help build trust among users and enhance the
global scalability of digital health solutions.
Implications for Practitioners
For practitioners operating in the digital health market,
testing artificial intelligence–supported personalized
marketing strategies and systematically measuring
the impact of gamification-based approaches on user
engagement are crucial. Understanding how different
user groups respond to these strategies can contribute
to more effective marketing practices and support long-
term market growth.
Implications for Academics
For academics, the findings highlight the value of
interdisciplinary research that integrates perspectives
from medicine, marketing, and data science. In addition,
longitudinal studies examining the long-term acceptance,
usage, and outcomes of digital health technologies are
Another significant potential for Turkey lies in the
major steps taken in digital transformation within the
Ministry of Health in the field of digital health. Following
this transformation, which gained momentum during
the pandemic, Turkey has become one of the leading
countries in digital health platforms and health analytics
with applications such as the e-Nabız system—containing
personal health data for nearly 74 million citizens—the
Centralized Physician Appointment System (MHRS) with

digital health transformation—especially e-Nabız and
MHRS—from the perspectives of healthcare providers
and patients, within the framework of the five themes

academic literature but also to the development of
roadmaps for healthcare providers.
This study advances the understanding of digital
health marketing as a distinct yet interdisciplinary
research field by situating its findings within the
broader digital health and healthcare innovation
literature. The discussion demonstrates that while
the field has achieved considerable growth and
thematic diversification, opportunities remain for
deeper theoretical integration, greater international
collaboration, and increased attention to emerging
ethical and societal dimensions.
LIMITATONS OF THE STUDY
Although this study aims to systematically map the
digital health marketing literature through bibliometric
and content analysis, it includes several limitations arising
from the research design. First, the dataset was limited
to publications obtained from the Web of Science (WoS)
Core Collection database on March 14, 2025. WoS was
preferred due to its coverage of high-quality academic
journals and its reliability for bibliometric analyses.
However, the exclusion of publications indexed in other
databases such as Scopus, EBSCO, or Google Scholar may
limit the comprehensive coverage of the entire body
of literature in the field. Therefore, the findings should
be interpreted with caution considering this scope
limitation.

CONCLUSION
This study offers a comprehensive, bibliometric,
and content-based overview of the digital health
marketing literature published between 2000 and
2025. By systematically mapping publication trends,
thematic structures, and research gaps, the study offers
an integrative perspective on the evolution and current
state of this emerging field. The findings demonstrate
that digital health marketing has experienced substantial
growth, particularly following the COVID-19 pandemic,
and is shaped by a concentration of academic influence
within a limited number of countries and institutions.
Beyond documenting these trends, the study
contributes to the literature by highlighting the
thematic foundations of digital health marketing and
identifying areas that remain underexplored, such as
ethical considerations, data privacy, and the marketing
implications of artificial intelligence–driven solutions.
By addressing a previously unexamined area through a
bibliometric lens, this research lays the groundwork for
future theoretical development and empirical inquiry,
thereby supporting more informed and globally inclusive
research agendas in digital health marketing.
Future research could expand the scope of this study by
including additional databases such as Scopus, EBSCO, or
Google Scholar to provide a more comprehensive view
of the digital health marketing literature. In addition,
future studies may consider using broader or alternative
keywords to capture different conceptualizations related
to digital health marketing. Further research could
also employ comparative bibliometric analyses across
databases or time periods to better understand the
evolution and emerging trends in this field.
