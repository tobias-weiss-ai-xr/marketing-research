ABSTRACT
This study examines the effect of green product practices on the performance of
food and beverage manufacturing firms in Kenya. The study was anchored on
the Natural Resource-Based View and the Stakeholder Theory. The theoretical
framework explains the rel ationship between green product practices and firm
performance. The target population was 192 food and beverage manufacturing
firms registered by the Kenya Association of Manufacturers. A census was
conducted since the target population was relatively smal l. A cross -sectional
survey design was adopted, and data were collected using structured
questionnaires from heads of marketing as the respondents in the respective
firms. The study response rate was 164 food and beverage manufacturing firms
in Kenya, repr esenting 85.4%. Data were analysed using descriptive and
inferential statistical techniques. The null hypotheses tested were rejected since
the p-value was less than the 5% significance level. The findings showed that
green product practices had a positive and significant effect on the financial and
non-financial performance of the firms. The study concluded that green product
practices are a significant determinant of financial and non -financial
performance. This signifies green product practices as eco -friendly initiatives
with economic gains. The study provides new empirical findings that can add
value to the body of knowledge. The study recommendations urge marketing
managers of listed firms to build a strong case for sustainability, where the green
products strategy is incorporated into the main business strategy of the firms for
financial and non-financial benefits associated with the green product strategy.
Further, the study recommends that policymakers provide incentives to support
the implementation of green product practices across the manufacturing sector.

INTRODUCTION
Environmental sustainability has become a
strategic priority for businesses worldwide due to
increasing environmental degradation, heightened
consumer environmental awareness, and growing
regulatory pressure. Consequently, firms are
increasingly integratin g green product practices
into their marketing strategies to demonstrate
commitment to environmental sustainability
while enhancing organisational competitiveness.
Green product practices, including the use of
clean eco -designs, recyclable materials, and
biodegradable packaging, have emerged as
important strategic initiatives that reduce
environmental impacts throughout a product's life
cycle while responding to the growing demand for
environmentally responsible products (Maziriri,
2020). The adoption of re cyclable materials and
biodegradable packaging minimises waste
generation and environmental pollution, making
them among the most effective approaches to
sustainable production (Larashati et al.,  2023).
Beyond environmental conservation, these
practices have become valuable strategic
resources capable of generating both financial and
non-financial benefits for firms (Eyasu et al.,
2026).
Globally, empirical evidence suggests that green
product practices contribute to improved firm
performance, although the magnitude of their
effect varies across industries and institutional
contexts. In the Southeast region of the United
States, firms that  integrated green product
practices into their marketing strategies
experienced enhanced consumer trust, stronger
brand recognition, and improved competitive
positioning, although financial gains such as
profitability and revenue growth depended on
industry characteristics and market maturity
(Babu et al., 2026; Fok et al., 2022). Similarly,
European firms increasingly use green product
practices as differentiation strategies to strengthen
market competitiveness. Businesses that
effectively communicate their commitment to
environmental sustainability through eco-friendly
products and packaging report improvements in
sales growth, profitability, and customer
acquisition while simultaneously strengthening
stakeholder relationships (Fontoura & Coelho,
2022; Tzanidis et al., 2024). Comparable findings
have been reported in Indonesia, where green
product practices significantly enhance firms'
non-financial performance, particularly customer
satisfaction and organisational reputation (Harini
et al., 2020). Likewise, firms in South Asia have
realised competitive advantages and improved
business performance by incorporating
environmentally sustainable product strategies
into their operations (Braik et al.,  2023).
Collectively, these studies demonstrate that
although green product practices are increasingly
recognised as valuable organisational resources,
their contribution to firm performance remains
context-dependent and is influenced by customer
preferences, organisational capabilities, firm size,
and resource availability (Islam et al., 2024).
Evidence from Africa presents a similarly
growing but less consistent body of knowledge. In
Nigeria, firms adopting green product practices
have reported improvements in both
organisational performance and environmental
sustainability (Orji et al.,  2025). Egyptian
manufacturing firms are increasingly
implementing green product strategies in line with

In the Kenyan context, environmental
sustainability has become increasingly important
among food and beverage manufacturing firms
due to growing consumer demand for
environmentally responsible products, regulatory
requirements, and increasing market competition.
Green product practices, including clean eco -
design, the use of recyclable or reusable materials,
biodegradable packaging, and environmental
branding, are progressively being recognised as
strategic resources capable of improving both
organisational competitiveness and long -term
sustainability. Green product practices integrate
environmental considerations throughout product
design, manufacturing, packaging, and disposal,
enabling firms to minimise environmental
impacts while responding to the prefer ences of
environmentally conscious consumers (Kwarto et
al., 2022). As the global green economy expands,
businesses increasingly leverage clean eco -
design, recyclable materials, and biodegradable
packaging to enhance their environmental
commitment while creating competitive
advantage (Kwarto et al. , 2022; Kumar et al.,
2021; Maziriri, 2020). Besides contributing to
environmental conservation, green product
practices are associated with financial benefits
such as increased profitability and sales growth,
as well as non -financial outcomes including
improved corporate reputation, stakeholder trust,
customer loyalty, and new customer acquisition
(Fernandes et al.,  2022; Leonidou et al.,  2020;
Rotman & Polonsky, 2024; Lee & Huang, 2021).
The food and beverage manufacturing subsector
is one of Kenya's most important manufacturing
industries, accounting for 22.13% of the Kenya
Association of Manufacturers (KAM) registered
membership, making it the country's largest
manufacturing subsector (K AM, 2025). The
manufacturing sector contributes approximately
70% of Kenya's industrial GDP, underscoring its
strategic role in employment creation,
industrialisation, and value addition (Kenya
Institute for Public Policy Research and Analysis,
2024). Desp ite this contribution, food and
beverage manufacturing firms continue to
experience considerable financial and operational
challenges that undermine competitiveness and
long-term growth. According to the Kenya
National Bureau of Statistics (KNBS, 2025),
many firms struggle to achieve sustainable
profitability and effectively compete with
multinational corporations. The adoption of green
marketing initiatives has largely emphasised
environmental compliance and operational
efficiency while placing relatively less emphasis
on improving profitability, sales revenue, and
market share (KAM, 2025; Mwove et al., 2024).
High production costs, intense competition, and
substantial investment requirements further
constrain firms' ability to invest in innovation and
sustainability initiatives necessary for improved
organisational performance (Wanjohi, 2022).
Beyond financial performance, non -financial
indicators such as customer loyalty, stakeholder
engagement, corporate image, and new customer
acquisition have become increasingly important
measures of business competitiveness and long -
term sustainability. Nevertheless, many food and
beverage manufacturing firms have yet to fully
exploit these strategic outcomes because of
limited technological capabilities, inadequate

product practices on both financial performance,
including profitability and sales revenue, and non-
financial performance, such as customer loyalty,
stakeholder trust, and new customer acquisition,
despite the growing recognition that these
dimensions jointly determine long -term
organisational success (Odhiambo, 2023;
Kimanthi, 2024). This knowledge gap constrains
managers' ab ility to make evidence -based
decisions regarding investments in
environmentally sustainable product strategies
while limiting policymakers' capacity to
formulate policies that effectively support green
manufacturing practices. Addressing this gap is
therefore essential for strengthening both
organisational competitiveness and
environmental sustainability within Kenya's food
and beverage manufacturing sector.
Consequently, this study aimed to examine the
effect of green product practices on the financial
and non -financial performance of food and
beverage manufacturing firms in Kenya, with the
following objectives;
• To establish the effect of green product
practices on the financial performance of food
and beverage manufacturing firms in Kenya.
• To assess the effect of green product practices
on the non-financial performance of food and
beverage manufacturing firms in Kenya.
The study hypotheses were;
H01: Green Product Practices have no significant
effect on the financial performance of food and
beverage manufacturing firms in Kenya.
H02: Green Product Practices have no significant
effect on non -financial performance of food and
beverage manufacturing firms in Kenya.
LITERATURE REVIEW
Theoretical Foundation
This study is anchored on the Natural Resource -
Based View (NRBV) and complemented by
Stakeholder Theory (ST) to explain the
relationship between green product practices and
the performance of food and beverage
manufacturing firms in Kenya. The NRBV posits
that environmentally oriented capabilities,
including clean eco -design, recyclable materials,
and biodegradable packaging, create sustainable
competitive advantage by enhancing cost
efficiency, reputation, and stakeholder
relationships (Hart, 1995; Hart & Dowell, 2011).
Complementarily, Stakeholder Theory
emphasises that creating value for customers,
regulators, suppliers, and society through
environmentally responsible practices strengthens
customer loyalty, corporate reputation, and
stakeholder trust, thereby improving financial and
non-financial performance (Freeman, 1984;
Freeman et al., 2010).

 Empirical Review
Green product practices are recognised as a key
dimension of green marketing that enhances firm
performance by improving environmental
sustainability, customer satisfaction, and
operational efficiency (Sarfraz et al.,  2022).
Green product development contributes to
environmental sustainability while enhancing
financial and non -financial performance through
cost reduction, improved profitability, customer
acquisition, and efficient resource utilisation
(Song et al., 2020; Ma et al., 2019; Larbi-Siaw et
al., 2022). Consequently, manufacturing firms
increasingly leverage green products to strengthen
customer perceptions, stakeholder relationships,
and competitive advantage (Lee & Huang, 2021).
Empirical evidence generally supports the
positive influence of green product practices on
firm performance, although findings remain
inconsistent across contexts. In the United States,
Fok et al.  (2022) found a significant positive
relationship between green product innovation
and firm performance among healthcare, retail,
and manufacturing firms. Similarly, Fontoura and
Coelho (2022) reported that green product
development significantly enhanced the long-term
performance of logistics firms in Portugal, while
Ma et al.  (2019) established that green product
innovation improved eco -performance, customer
acquisition, profitability, and resource efficiency
in Pakistan. Comparable findings were reported
by Afum et al . (2023), who found that green
product strategy positively influenced the
sustainability performance of SMEs in Ghana.
However, Cao et al. (2022) observed that green
product innovation positively affected economic
performance only during the initial and mature
stages of the product life cycle in Chinese
microenterprises. In contrast, Lewandowska et al.
(2022) reported a negative relationship between
green innovation practices and firm performance
in European Union enterprises. Likewise,
Andersén (2021) found no direct relationship
between green product initiatives and firm
performance among Swedish manuf acturing
firms. These mixed findings suggest that the
relationship between green product practices and
firm performance is context -specific and cannot
be generalised across industries and countries.
In Kenya, empirical evidence linking green
product practices to firm performance remains
limited and fragmented. Existing studies have
largely focused on green marketing strategies in
retail and supermarket settings (Onditi, 2024),
customer satisfaction in  the soft drink industry
(Macharia et al.,  2019), or green supply chain
management in manufacturing firms (Ngatia et
al., 2025). Although these studies report positive
outcomes associated with green practices, they do
not directly examine the effect of green product
practices on both financial and non -financial
performance, particularly within the food and
beverage manufactu ring sector. This contextual
and empirical gap justified the present study,
which sought to examine the effect of green
product practices on the performance of food and
beverage manufacturing firms in Kenya.

MATERIALS AND METHODS
This study adopted a cross -sectional research
design because data were collected at a single
point in time. Cross -sectional studies are
appropriate for examining relationships among
variables without requiring longitudinal
observation (Hunziker & Blankenagel, 2024). The
target population comprised all 192 registered
food and beverage manufacturing firms in Kenya
(KAM, 2025). Owing to the relatively small
population, a census approach was adopted. The
unit of analysis was the food and beverage
manufacturing firms, while the unit of observation
comprised marketing managers or heads of
marketing departments, who were selected
because of their responsibility for implementing
and evaluating green product practices and their
influence on firm performance.
Data were collected using a structured
questionnaire administered via the drop-and-pick-
later method, with assistance from three trained
research assistants. Green product practices were
measured using scale items adapted from Kwarto
et al. (2022), Wandosell et al. (2021), and Fok et
al. (2022), while financial and non -financial
performance were measured using items adopted
from Kilic et al. (2022) and Mittal et al. (2023),
respectively. All constructs were measured on a
five-point Likert scale (1 = Not at all to 5 = To a
very great extent ), which is appropriate for
assessing the degree of implementation of
organisational practices and performance
outcomes (Chang et al., 2022; Lee et al.,  2022;
Jaiswal et al., 2022; Kılıç et al., 2022). Data were
analysed using regression analysis after testing the
assumptions of linearity, normality, and
autocorrelation. Reliability was assessed using
Cronbach's alpha (Hair et al ., 2019), while
construct validity was established through
Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA) based on
Principal Component Analysis (PCA) with
varimax rotation after confirming data suitability
using the Kaiser –Meyer–Olkin (KMO) measure
and Bartlett's test of sphericity.

conducting EFA, the suitability of the data for
factor analysis was confirmed using the Kaiser -
