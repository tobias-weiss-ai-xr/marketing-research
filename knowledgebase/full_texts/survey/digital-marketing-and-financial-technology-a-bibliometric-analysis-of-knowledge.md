DIGITAL MARKETING AND FINANCIAL TECHNOLOGY: A BIBLIOMETRIC
ANALYSIS OF KNOWLEDGE STRUCTURE AND FUTURE RESEARCH TRENDS
(2017–2025)

In this study, we conducted a bibliographic analysis of scholarly articles  related to financial
technology and digital marketing in the Scopus database, focusing on documents publ ished between
2017 and 2025. This analysis aims to help researchers in this field identify current research trends and
compare existing research to pinpoint gaps and promising approaches, thus providing a  compass for
future research. Although 843 studies were initially retrieved, only 263 were selected for final analysis.
VOSviewer software was used to identify the most frequently used words and terms in abstracts, titles,
and keywords, and to analyze the studies in terms of co-authorship and cita tions. The results revealed
a growing interest among researchers in the field of financial technology and digital marketing,
particularly banking systems, reflecting the rapid pace of technological development in this area. In this
context, the introduction of artificial intelligence is expected to play a key  role in this transformation.
In terms of geographical distribution, China has the largest number of publica tions on this topic,
followed by the United Kingdom and India. Notably, Jordan ranks rela tively high, ahead of Ukraine,
Malaysia, and the United States. The study also found that most of the r esearch was published as
English-language articles in academic journals, particularly Resource Policy, I nternational Journal of
Bank Marketing, and International Review of Financial Analysis. Finally, this study acknowledges some
limitations, such as its relatively short timeframe (due to the novelt y of the topic) and its restriction to
English-language publications indexed in Scopus. The researchers believe this may ha ve overlooked
important insights available in other non-English databases and sources.
Keywords: Digital Marketing; Fintech; Banking Sector; bibliometric m apping analysis;
visualization.

Introduction.The modern world has witnessed a radical transformation (Syamsiah
Syamsiah, 2025), driven by digital acceleration which has reshaped the global economic
landscape. Within this context, financial technology (FinTech) has emerged as a pivotal force
(Chen, 2024), bringing about structural change in traditional financial and banking sectors (Mrs.
B.Veena, 2026). Indeed, it is no longer merely a complementary (Gomber et al., 2017) tool but
has become a strategic pillar and an integral part of the modern business environment (Barroso
& Laborda, 2022), playing a crucial role in accelerating the digital marketing of national
economies and enhancing their sustainability (Sadarsopewale & Kadam, 2026).
In this regard, the added value of emerging technologies—such as blockchain, artificial
intelligence, and cloud computing  lies in their remarkable ability to enhance operational
efficiency (Ahmed, 2026), improve the quality of customer services, and develop regulatory
frameworks. By leveraging big data analytics (Roy, 2022), FinTech companies have been able
to offer flexible, accurate, and more consumer-centric financing solutions (Unal & Aysan,
2022), boosting productivity and the ability to manage massive flows of financial data with
unparalleled precision. Building on this, digital platforms and application programming
interfaces (Zhang et al., 2021) have fostered a collaborative environment between traditional
financial institutions and startups, enabling the pooling of resources and the sharing of
technological innovations to deliver a seamless user experience.
However, this rapid development presents complex challenges related to cybersecurity
and digital fraud risks, making the development of modern regulatory and legislative
frameworks imperative to ensure transaction security and compliance (Ashwath Narayana
Gowda, 2025). From a theoretical standpoint, the adoption of financial technology (FinTech)
can be interpreted as a strategic resource that enhances the competitiveness of institutions.
Based on the Resource-Based View (RBV), FinTech systems are rare and valuable regulatory
assets that contribute to achieving a sustainable competitive advantage by enhancing
transparency and risk management. In line with Dynamic Capabilities Theory (Sampson, 2025),
this study further emphasizes that simply possessing technology is insufficient to achieve long-
term results; rather, it requires the ability of institutions to reshape their resources, develop their
business models, and innovate their decision-making processes to align with digital marketing
shifts. Therefore, this research framework adopts the view that FinTech represents the

Aim and Objectives.  This study included a bibliographic analysis using both
quantitative and qualitative indicators. Statistical analysis was used to identify patterns in the
distribution of research articles on a specific topic, focusing on particular themes and time
periods. The results of the analysis were presented visually to facilitate understanding.
The analysis interprets large amounts of data, allowing this type of research to benefit
from qualitative data analysis. Bibliographic data analysis employs a systematic and
reproducible process. This involves reviewing scholarly publications, including the use of
quantitative indicators for evaluation.
Articles are searched for, and trends and patterns within them are identified over time
(Talili et al., 2021). The analysis enables researchers to understand the literature and fa cilitate
knowledge construction by establishing diverse relationships between numbers, keywords, and
references. This study followed the bibliographic analysis methodology used by Zancanaro and
other researchers: (1) data collection, filtering, and standardization; and (2) analysis and
synthesis of the collected data (Zancanaro et al., 2013). Firstly, although definitions of digital
marketing and financial technology vary in the literature, this study utilized the most commo n
and frequently used keywords.
First, although definitions of digital marketing and financial technology vary across the
literature, this study employed the most common and frequently used keywords (Lal et al.,
2023), namely financial technology and digital marketing. It also used Scopus database search,
checking the title, abstract, keywords, and time period (2017–2025), and identifying the article
document type (Sanga and Aziakobono, 2023). We acknowledge its limitations, including its
narrow scope, which is restricted to English-language publications indexed within the scope
and may exclude important information from other non-English databases and sources (Zubilito
et al., 2023).
The study selection process for this research is illustrated in Figure 1.
The study sample was selected using the traditional literature review methodology, as
shown in PRISMA flowchart Figure 1. The initial search identified 843 records in the Scopus
database using the search term TITLE-ABS-KEY ("Fintech" and "Digital Marketing"). To
ensure data quality and consistency, the study's scope was limited to original articles published
in English in business, management, and economics journals between 2017 and 2025.
Other types of documents, such as books and conference proceedings, were excluded.
A final sample of 263 articles was filtered and subjected to bibliographic mapping and analysis
using VOSviewer software (Table 1).

4.1.W hat is the distribution of fintech and digital marketing research studies in terms of
year of publication, document type, language, and publication date in Science and Scopus from
2017-2025?
The time trajectory analysis shown in Figure 1 shows that academic interest in the
intersection of FinTech and digital marketing during the early part of the study period (2018 –
2021) was modest, with a relatively stable number of papers not exceeding 13 per year. The
exception was 2020, when 13 documents were released, likely due to the Coronavirus Disease
(COVID-19) pandemic. However, in the following year, the number of publications decreased
again to 7, suggesting that the field was still in its growth phase. Significant changes began in
2022, with the number of publications increasing more than four times from the previous year,
from 7 to 29, and continuing to increase to 35 in 2023. This turning point may reflect the
growing recognition within academia that FinTech and digital marketing are interconnected
and increasingly relevant areas of research, coinciding with the global expansion of digital
financial services, mobile banking, and AI-based marketing tools in the post-pandemic era.
However, the most interesting feature of this distribution ies the sharp increase observed in the
last two years of the study period, where the number of publications increased from 35 (2023)
to 55 (2024) and then almost doubled in 2025 to 111 documents, confirming the upward trend
in research and reinforcing the importance and timeliness of a standard literature analysis in
this specific period, in which this area was captured.

Conclusions and Directions for Further Research. This study employed
bibliographic mapping techniques to provide a comprehensive synthesis of the available
literature on fintech and digital marketing, based on predefined criteria. These criteria included
variables such as the annual number of publications, publishing platforms, search language,
geographic origins of engagement, affiliations, keyword usage, and the content of article titles
and abstracts. Furthermore, general patterns in the field of fintech and digital marketing were
explored. While this study is significant, certain limitations were identified that warrant further
in-depth investigation. These limitations included the exclusive use of specific keywords and
reliance solely on the Scopus database, which may have restricted the scope of our findings.

trends, academic studies, and literature related to fintech and digital marketing. This research
primarily aims to provide readers with information on current trends in keyword usage. The
study adopted an analytical framework that combines quantitative assessments with advanced
techniques to anticipate future trends in fintech and digital marketing research. This strategic
approach is expected to help bridge existing research gaps in this field. Research on the
intersection of financial technology and digital marketing is largely in line with the United
