Abstract
Digital marketing functions as a powerful changing force that affects rural areas in India because people gain access
to better internet service and low-cost smartphones and government programs that promote digital access. The research
study examines how digital marketing exists in rural areas of Balasore District Odisha through its current knowledge
base and the factors that enable its use and the difficulties that come with it and the effects that people think it has.
The researchers used a descriptive–quantitative research design to gather primary data from 300 respondents through
a structured Likert -scale questionnaire which contained 34 items that measured seven different constructs. The
researchers used a one -sample t-test to test the first hypothesis about awareness versus the neutral point and a one -
way ANOVA to test the second hypothesis about gender differences in awareness. The results demonstrate that all
construct means show a pattern of measurement that centers around the scale mid -point value of approximately 3.0
which demonstrates that people possess moderate understanding of the situation and showlimited adoption. The most
preferred platform for users is WhatsApp which has a market share of 21.7% while YouTube and Instagram follow
with respective market shares of 19.7% and 17.7%. The study retained both null hypotheses because people showed
no significant awareness change from the neutral point and no gender differences existed in awareness levels. The
study recommends targeted digital literacy  programmes, rural broadband improvements, and region -specific digital
marketing policies for Odisha.
Keywords: Digital Marketing, Rural India, Balasore, Odisha, Technology Adoption, TAM, Digital Literacy,
WhatsApp

1. Introduction
The marketing practices in rural India have undergone fundamental changes because people now have access to
inexpensive smartphones and affordable mobile internet. The number of rural internet subscribers in India reached
398.35 million on March 2024 while  95.15% of villages had access to 3G/4G mobile networks (Press Information
Bureau [PIB], 2024). The online presence of rural areas has increased by 28% since 2022 because 70% of rural
residents now use the internet (Misra, 2025). The digital revolution has  created new business opportunities for rural
businesses and farmers and entrepreneurs who want to use digital marketing tools to promote their products and
interact with customers.
Rural markets face ongoing structural problems because people lack digital skills and internet services are
unreliable and language differences exist and people distrust online payments (Gupta & Singh, 2021). The Technology
Acceptance Model (TAM) developed by Davis (1989) provides a strong theoretical framework that explains how two
factors perceived ease of use and perceived usefulness drive people to adopt digital marketing platforms. The Unified
Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT) develope d by Venkatesh et al. (2003) expanded the
Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) by adding social influence and facilitating conditions as essential factors.

The digital transition process which rural India experiences can be observed through the example of Balasore District
in Odisha. The internet subscriber base in Odisha will grow from 48.89 to 63 per 100 population while the state aims
to achieve 90% internet access by 2029 because there exists substantial connectivity and literacy differences among
different areas within the district. The state of Odisha maintained 1,237 villages without mobile network access as of
February 2026 according to the Odisha Digital Divide Report 2026. This research investigates how Balasore District
residents adopt digital marketing through its empirical research which helps fill existing research gaps on this topic at
the district level.
1.1 Objectives of the Study
• To assess the level of awareness of digital marketing among rural residents of Balasore District.
• To examine the usage patterns and preferred digital platforms in the study area.
• To identify factors enabling and challenging the adoption of digital marketing.
• To evaluate the perceived impact of digital marketing on business and income.
• To test two hypotheses relating awareness and gender to digital marketing adoption.

2. Literature Review
2.1 Digital Marketing and Rural Consumers
Digital marketing includes all marketing activities that use digital technologies to reach customers through social
media platforms and email services and search engines and mobile applications (Chaffey & Ellis -Chadwick, 2019).
The main platforms that people in rural India use to promote their businesses and search for products are WhatsApp
and YouTube and Facebook (Kumar & Sharma, 2022). The researchers discovered that 72% of rural Indian consumers
use smartphones frequently while 68% of them use social media platforms on a daily basis according to Sharma et al.
(2023). The researchers found that WhatsApp and YouTube have become the most popular platforms among rural
users because they offer easy-to-use features and provide content in local languages according to Patel and Deshmukh
(2023).
2.2 Technology Acceptance Model (TAM)
Davis (1989) developed the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) to study how users decide to accept information
technologies through his research which identified perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use as the main factors
that determine adoption intentions. The research conducted in rural Karnataka confirmed that perceived ease of use
stands as the most important element which affects digital technology adoption because people in rural areas face
difficulties with complex systems due to their limited d igital skills. Singh and Rao (2021) used TAM to study rural
areas of Odisha and found that government digital literacy programs help people understand technology better which
results in faster adoption of digital systems by people who previously lacked digital skills.
2.3 Awareness and Gender in Digital Marketing Adoption
Digital marketing platforms require users to first learn about their functions before they can use the platforms. Kumar
and Sharma (2022) established that awareness levels in rural India remain moderate, with many respondents limited
to passive exposure through social media feeds rather than active business usage. Research studies from the past have
shown that men use digital technology more than women in rural areas (Sharma et al., 2023). Recent evidence from
Odisha's Mission Shakti programme shows that women who gained access to mobile banking through self-help groups
achieved better digital literacy than before (Odisha Economic Survey, 2026). The study develops its second hypothesis
based on how gender roles are currently changing.
2.4 Challenges in Rural Digital Marketing Adoption

include both inadequate broadband services and electricity supply problems and (b) educational barriers which include

3. Research Methodology
3.1 Research Design
The research uses descriptive -quantitative methods to conduct its investigation. The researchers used a structured
questionnaire to collect primary data through cross -sectional research. The research explores rural digital marketing
patterns through its exploratory component while testing hypotheses through its analytical path.
3.2 Study Area
The Balasore (Baleshwar) District of Odisha India occupies a position on the northern coastal plain at Latitude 21°30'N
and Longitude 86°56'E. The district maintains its rural character with the majority of residents engaged in agricultural
work and small-scale trading activities. The researchers selected this area because it contains representative rural areas
and newly established mobile internet access and there exists limited research about digital marketing in this region.
3.3 Sample Size and Sampling Method
The researchers selected 300 respondents through the process of multi -stage random sampling. The research team
selected random rural blocks in Balasore District to conduct their study and then used systematic sampling to choose
one adult respondent from each household in the selected villages. The researchers used Cochran's (1977) formula to
determine a sample size of 300 for their study while using the formula n = Z²pq/e² = 1.96² × 0.5 × 0.5 / 0.05² to
calculate a sample size of 384 but set the sample size at 300 because of their budget limitations.
3.5 Hypotheses
The present study tests the following two hypotheses:
• H1₀: The mean awareness of digital marketing among rural respondents of Balasore District does not differ
significantly from the scale neutral point (3.0).
• H2₀: There is no significant difference in digital marketing awareness across gender groups (male, female,
and other) in Balasore District.

4.5 Hypothesis Testing
4.5.1 H1 – Awareness vs. Neutral Point (One-sample t-test)
The researchers utilized a one -sample t-test to test H1 in order to evaluate whether the composite awareness score
showed a significant difference from the scale neutral point which measured 3.0. The analysis yielded t(299) = 0.000,
p = 1.000. The researchers maintained null hypothesis H1₀ because p value exceeded 0.05. The mean awareness score
(M = 3.000, SD = 0.712) achieves exact equality to the neutral mid -point which demonstrates that rural respondents
from Balasore possess moderate digital marketing awareness that they express through neutral responses to awareness
statements. This finding matches the research of Kumar and Sharma (2022) who found that most rural consumers
maintain a transitional stage of awareness.
4.5.2 H2 – Gender and Awareness (One-way ANOVA)
The research used a one -way ANOVA test to evaluate H2 by comparing mean awareness scores for three gender
groups which included Male (n=98, M=3.01), Female (n=107, M=2.98), and Other (n=95, M=3.02). The result
produced F(2, 297) = 0.664, p = 0.516. The null hypothesis H2₀ remains valid because p value exceeds 0.05. The study
found no statistically significant difference in digital marketing awareness between different gender groups. The study
challenges the traditional belief that rural areas experience male-led digital growth (Sharma et al., 2023) which shows
how Odisha's Mission Shakti program helped women gain equal access to technology (Odisha Economic Survey,
2026).

5. Discussion
The study confirmed both null hypotheses, which demonstrated that residents of rural Balasore possess moderate
digital marketing knowledge that matches their demographic distribution. The Awareness mean value at scale neutral
point(M 3.000) indicates that district residents currently start to recognize digital marketing but still lack established
positive or negative views about it. The 'digital limbo' phase (Bhattacharya & Nair, 2022) exists because people obtain
smartphones but still lack complete digital literacy. The non-significant gender effect (H2) challenges the conventional
expectation of male-dominated rural digital adoption. Balasore research showed nearly equal gender representation in
participants who used mobile banking and digital marketing to ols through Mission Shakti SHGs (M_Male 3.01
M_Female 2.98 M_Other 3.02). The research proved that Jha et al. (2024) discovered government digital initiatives
advantages women who live in rural Odisha because they help decrease technology anxiety. WhatsApp's status as the
leading platform (21.7%) confirms IJSREM (2025) and Kumari and Pandey (2022) results which showed WhatsApp
Business functions as the easiest way for rural users to enter digital marketing because users only need to pay for data
which enables them to use vernacular support and voice-message features that eliminate literacy requirements. People
want government agencies to enhance their infrastructure (Q33 M 3.16) because they understand better connectivity
remains essential for BharatNet Phase II development plans (PIB 2024).

6. Conclusion
The research investigated how 300 rural participants from Balasore District Odisha adopted digital marketing through
two specific research the hypotheses. H1 testing showed that awareness exists at the mid-point since H1 testing shows
the awareness level r eaches (M = 3.000) which corresponds to his transitional adoption stage. H2 testing found no
significant gender differences in awareness (F = 0.664, p = 0.516), suggesting an emerging gender parity in digital
exposure attributable to government inclusion p rogrammes. The highest usage occurs through WhatsApp which
functions as the main platform and people use it most frequently while they express strong needs for government
infrastructure development (M = 3.16).
The present study requires focused solutions which include expanding BharatNet to provide better rural broadband
services and increasing digital literacy training through SHGs and schools and creating easy -to-use Odia-language
systems for e -commerce and go vernment websites. Future research should adopt longitudinal designs and
confirmatory factor analysis to track adoption trajectories post-BharatNet Phase II completion in Odisha.

7. Recommendations
• The district administration needs to make 4G and 5G network expansion their main focus for Digital Bharat
Nidhi project implementation in the underserved areas of Balasore.
• The training camps for WhatsApp Business should provide instruction in Odia language to farmers who work
in rural areas and to small business owners.
• Women who attend Mission Shakti SHG meetings need to receive digital literacy training because they
already possess basic knowledge through their existing gender-parity awareness.
• E-commerce platforms need to use simplified design elements in their local language interfaces to make their
services more accessible to rural customers while building their trust.
• The rural population of Balasore needs to receive cyber security knowledge through RBI SafePay and cyber
security awareness programs to combat the existing trust deficit which measures 2.86 based on Q25 M value.
• The researchers need to conduct longitudinal follow -up studies after BharatNet Phase II to measure
subsequent changes in system adoption.

8. Limitations of the Study
• The study design prevents researchers from establishing causal relationships because better results would
come from using longitudinal data to study adoption patterns.
• The testing process showed low Cronbach's alpha scores because the different measurement items across all
constructs showed poor consistency, which necessitated instrument development work.
• Respondents tend to answer Likert questions based on their actual preferences but their answers get
influenced by their desire to appear socially acceptable.
• The research used a sample from Balasore District which prevents results from being applicable to other
districts within Odisha.
• The research tested just two hypotheses while upcoming research needs to use multivariate analysis and
confirmatory analysis methods.
