ABSTRACT
Positioned within digital marketing and organizational learning perspectives, this study
addresses inconsistent findings regarding the direct effects of marketing activities and internal
capabilities on brand outcomes. The study examines whether social media marketing and learning
orientation enhance halal MSME brand performance and investigates the mediating role of brand
trust. A quantitative survey was conducted among halal-certified MSMEs, with data analyzed using
Structural Equation Modeling–Partial Least Squares (SEM-PLS). The results indicate that social
media marketing and learning orientation significantly influence brand trust and brand
performance. Brand trust exerts a strong direct effect on brand performance and partially mediates
the relationships between the two antecedent variables and brand performance. The analysis
demonstrates adequate explanatory and predictive power of the model, confirming that external
digital marketing efforts and internal learning capabilities operate synergistically through trust -
building mechanisms. These findings highlight that the effectiveness of social media strategies and
organizational learning does not merely translate into performance directly but is reinforced through
relational outcomes. The identification of brand trust as a mediating mechanism contributes to the
literature on halal MSMEs. It provides empirical evidence of how digital engagement and a learning-
oriented culture jointly strengthen sustainable brand performance.

INTRODUCTION
A considerable shift in marketing strategies among Micro, Small, and Medium
Enterprises (MSMEs) has emerged alongside the rapid expansion of digital technology.
Social media has evolved from a unidirectional promotional channel into an interactive
platform that supports long-term relationships between brands and consumers. Empirical
evidence by Lee and Park (2022) and Sohaib and Han (2023) shows that informative,
interactive, and consistent social media marketing activities enhance brand perceptions,
customer engagement, and loyalty. In the current digital environment, social media
marketing also serves as a strategic medium for building brand credibility, transparency,
and consumer trust. This is particularly relevant for halal MSMEs, where growing
awareness of halal compliance, product safety, and ethical standards makes halal
certification an important factor in brand evaluation. In Indonesia, halal certification
influences consumer trust and purchase intention (Nusran et al., 2023; Muntholip &
Setiawan, 2025).
From this perspective, Social Media Marketing (SMM) serves as a key strategic
medium through which firms communicate halal principles, emphasize production

relationships that suggest mediating variables (Ebrahim, 2020; Azhar et al., 2023;

Beyond external marketing factors, MSME brand success is also shaped by internal
organizational capabilities, particularly human resource quality. Learning orientation
reflects MSME actors’ commitment to continuous learning, openness to new knowledge,
and adaptability to market changes. Firms with strong learning orientation tend to
improve organizational responsiveness, innovation capability, and operationa l
consistency, strengthening organizational credibility and customer confidence (Calantone
et al., 2002; Schaffart & Malkows, 2024). Khan and Bashir (2020) found that learning
orientation enhances organizational credibility through adaptability and market
responsiveness, while Real et al. (2014) emphasized its role in building reputational
quality and customer trust. However, findings on its direct relationship with brand
performance remain inconsistent. Gomes and Wojahn (2017) found no significant direct
effect, while Xie and Zheng (2020) reported no direct impact on consumer -perceived
brand value. Similarly, Yuan et al. (2018) found no significant relationship in small
service firms. In halal-certified MSMEs, learning orientation is particularly relevant for
understanding halal standards, maintaining product consistency, and responding to
critical consumers. Thus, its influence on brand performance may operate through
relational mechanisms such as brand trust.
Brand trust has been widely acknowledged as a critical determinant of brand success
because it positively influences customer loyalty, repurchase intention, and long-term
relational outcomes (Chaudhuri & Holbrook, 2001; Delgado -Ballester & Munuera-
Alemán, 2005). From a relationship marketing perspective, trust functions as a strategic
asset that strengthens emotional attachment and long -term commitment between
consumers and brands. Recent studies by Kwon et al. (2021) further confirm that brand
trust plays an important mediating role in linking marketing activities and organizational
capabilities with consumer behavior outcomes. Tiep et al. (2023) demonstrated that brand
trust directly enhances brand performance through customer loyalty mechanisms, while
Huo et al. (2022) reported that trust strengthens brand equity and improves market
outcomes. In the context of digital marketing, Hafez (2021) and Gökerik (2024) also
highlighted that trust serves as an intermediary mechanism through which social media
engagement influences relational and behavioral outcomes.
Despite these insights, limited research has simultaneously integrated social media
marketing and learning orientation with brand trust as a mediating variable in explaining
halal MSME brand performance, particularly in local contexts such as Medan City.
Previous studies have predominantly examined these variables separately or focused on
large corporations rather than halal -certified MSMEs. Moreover, socio -cultural
characteristics and varying levels of digital literacy at the local level may influence
marketing effectiveness, consumer trust formation, and brand performance outcomes.
This study addresses the identified gaps by examining the mediating role of brand trust in
the relationship between social media marketing, learning orientation, and brand
performance among halal-certified MSMEs in Medan City.

Social Media Marketing and Learning Orientation on Brand Performance
Social media marketing can enhance brand performance by increasing brand visibility,
strengthening customer engagement, and supporting firms in developing stronger market
relationships. Social media adoption provides MSMEs with opportunities to
communicate their value propositions, respond to customers, and expand market reach
more efficiently. Faisal et al. (2018) found that strategic orientation influences MSME
performance through social media orientation, indicating that the effective use of social
media can contribute to improved organizational outcomes. Similarly, Odoom and
Mensah (2019) demonstrated that social media capabilities strengthen the relationship
between brand orientation and brand performance in SMEs. Oyewobi et al. (2021) further
showed that social media adoption contributes to business performance through
organizational learning capability. These findings suggest that the effective
implementation of social media marketing can strengthen brand visibility, market
engagement, and competitive outcomes, ultimately improving brand performance.
Learning orientation also contributes to brand performance by enabling organizations
to continuously acquire knowledge, adapt to environmental changes, and develop
innovative responses to market demands. Weerawardena et al. (2006) demonstrated that
organizational learning plays an important role in supporting innovation and brand
performance, particularly by enabling firms to respond effectively to industry conditions.
Similarly, Khan and Bashir (2020) found that a learning-oriented culture supports the
achievement of organizational performance goals through continuous learning and
adaptability. A strong learning orientation allows MSMEs to better understand customer
preferences, improve their offerings, and respond to changing competitive conditions.
Therefore, MSMEs with a stronger learning orientation are expected to achieve better
brand performance because continuous learning facilitates organizational adaptation and
the development of capabilities that support competitive outcomes.

Brand Trust on Brand Performance
Brand trust is an important relational asset that can strengthen brand performance by
reducing consumers’ perceived risk and encouraging favorable behavioral responses
toward a brand. According to Chaudhuri and Holbrook (2001), brand trust contributes to
brand performance through mechanisms such as brand loyalty, as consumers who trust a
brand are more likely to maintain their relationship with it and demonstrate favorable
responses. Similarly, Delgado-Ballester and Munuera-Alemán (2005) emphasize that
brand trust is an important component of brand equity because it strengthens consumers’
confidence in a brand and supports the development of long-term relationships. In the
context of SMEs, trust is particularly relevant because consumers may rely on perceived
brand credibility when evaluating products and making purchasing decisions. Kwon et al.
(2021) further demonstrate that factors contributing to brand trust are closely associated
with the development of brand loyalty, reinforcing its importance in strengthening brand-
related outcomes.
Empirical evidence also indicates that brand trust can translate marketing activities
and organizational capabilities into stronger performance outcomes. Ebrahim (2020)
found that trust plays an important role in explaining how social media marketing
contributes to brand equity and loyalty, indicating that consumer trust can strengthen the
outcomes generated by digital marketing activities. Likewise, Huo et al. (2022)
demonstrate the importance of brand trust and brand loyalty in translating corporate
social responsibility into favorable consumer responses. Tiep et al. (2023) further confirm
that brand trust contributes to SME performance through relational mechanisms
involving customer loyalty. Thus, when consumers perceive a brand as reliable, credible,
and trustworthy, they are more likely to maintain relationships with the brand, support
repeat purchasing, and generate positive responses that contribute to stronger brand
performance.

H5: Brand trust has a significant effect on brand performance.

Brand Trust as a Mediator
Brand trust can function as an important mediating mechanism through which social
media marketing contributes to brand performance. Social media marketing enables firms
to communicate with consumers, provide relevant information, and facilitate interactive
engagement. However, the influence of these activities on brand-related outcomes may
depend on the extent to which consumers perceive the brand as trustworthy. Ebrahim
(2020) emphasized that trust plays an important role in explaining how social media
marketing activities influence brand-related outcomes. Hafez (2021) demonstrated that
brand trust acts as an intermediary mechanism connecting social media marketing
activities with brand equity. More recent evidence also indicates that social media
marketing can strengthen brand trust, which subsequently contributes to favorable
consumer responses and stronger brand outcomes (Azhar et al., 2023; Gökerik, 2024).
Therefore, brand trust may explain how effective social media communication is
transformed into improved brand performance.
Brand trust may also mediate the relationship between learning orientation and brand
performance. A strong learning orientation encourages firms to continuously acquire
knowledge, remain open to new ideas, and adapt their practices to changing customer
expectations. These capabilities can strengthen the consistency and reliability of value
delivered to consumers, thereby fostering greater trust in the brand. Trust, in turn,
represents an important intangible asset that supports brand-related outcomes. Chaudhuri
and Holbrook (2001) found that brand trust contributes to brand performance through
customer loyalty, while Delgado-Ballester and Munuera-Alemán (2005) demonstrated its
importance in strengthening brand equity. In addition, Kwon et al. (2021) confirmed that
trust is an important determinant of brand loyalty and other favorable brand outcomes.
Thus, learning-oriented firms may improve brand performance not only directly but also
by cultivating stronger consumer trust.

RESEARCH METHODS
This study adopted a quantitative method with an explanatory design to investigate
the impact of social media marketing and learning orientation on brand performance,
with brand trust positioned as a mediating construct among halal-certified MSMEs in
Medan City. The sample consisted of 210 MSME entrepreneurs selected through
purposive sampling based on two criteria: possessing an active halal certification and
actively utilizing social media as a marketing channel. The number of respondents
satisfied the recommended criteria for PLS-SEM estimation and provided sufficient
observations for estimating the proposed structural model (Sarstedt et al., 2021).
Data were obtained through a structured questionnaire employing a five-point Likert
scale. The measurement items were adapted from previously validated instruments
covering social media marketing, measured through interaction and content quality (Xie
& Zheng, 2020). Learning orientation was measured through commitment to continuous
learning, shared organizational vision, and open-mindedness (Calantone et al., 2002).
Brand trust was measured through reliability and integrity (Chaudhuri & Holbrook,
2001). Brand performance was measured through market performance and competitive
position (Baker & Sinkula, 2009). To maintain conceptual consistency, the questionnaire
was translated and subsequently evaluated using a back-translation procedure to ensure
semantic accuracy.
Data analysis employed the PLS-SEM method supported by SmartPLS version 4
through a staged analytical approach. The measurement model (outer model) was initially
evaluated in terms of convergent validity, discriminant validity, and construct reliability.
Convergent validity was assessed using outer loadings and Average Variance Extracted
(AVE), with outer loading values of at least 0.70 and AVE values above 0.50 considered
acceptable. Discriminant validity was assessed using cross-loadings and the Fornell–
Larcker criterion, while construct reliability was evaluated using Cronbach’s Alpha and
Composite Reliability (CR), with values above 0.70 indicating acceptable internal
consistency (Hair et al., 2021). Following confirmation of adequate measurement quality,
the structural model (inner model) was assessed using R², adjusted R², and Q² to evaluate
explanatory power and predictive relevance. Hypothesis testing was conducted using
bootstrapping with 5,000 resampling iterations, with statistical significance determined by

DISCUSSION
The analysis demonstrates that SMM significantly and positively contributes to the
establishment of brand trust. This outcome is consistent with commitment–trust theory,
which suggests that consistent, transparent, and interactive communication fosters trust
within marketing relationships (Morgan & Hunt, 1994). In the digital environment, social
media platforms enable real-time engagement, openness, and reciprocal dialogue, thereby
reinforcing consumers’ perceptions of brand credibility. Prior empirical studies by Seo
and Park (2018) and Ebrahim (2020) similarly confirm that the quality of content and
interactive communication in social media contexts significantly contributes to the
development of consumer trust.
Learning orientation likewise demonstrates a positive and significant effect on brand
trust. This finding aligns with organizational learning theory, which highlights the
importance of continuous learning commitment, openness to new ideas, and a shared
strategic vision in strengthening organizational adaptability (Sinkula et al., 1997). Firms
that consistently engage in learning and proactively adjust to market dynamics are better
positioned to provide reliable value, thereby increasing customer trust. This outcome is
consistent with Real et al. (2014), who stated that a strong learning orientation contributes
to enhanced corporate reputation and perceived credibility.
Moreover, SMM demonstrates a direct and significant positive effect on brand
performance. Effective digital marketing activities enhance brand visibility, customer
engagement, and brand image, which in turn contribute to improved performance
outcomes. Prior studies by Oyewobi et al. (2023) demonstrate that coordinated social
media initiatives play a significant role in improving marketing performance and
reinforcing organizational competitiveness. Learning orientation also demonstrates a
significant positive effect on brand performance. Organizations that foster a robust
learning culture are generally more innovative and adaptive, enabling them to sustain
competitive advantage over time. This finding aligns with earlier research suggesting that
learning orientation fosters innovation and enhances overall organizational performance
(Hernández-Linares et al., 2021). Among all relationships examined, brand trust exhibits
the strongest direct effect on brand performance. Trust functions as a risk -reduction
mechanism and a crucial element in fostering customer loyalty (Chaudhuri & Holbrook,
2001). In the context of SMEs, trust constitutes a valuable intangible resource that
enhances customer retention and stimulates positive word-of-mouth, thereby supporting
overall brand performance. This finding reinforces the importance of relational factors in
determining how consumers respond to and maintain relationships with brands.
The mediation analysis further reveals that brand trust partially mediates the
relationship between SMM and brand performance. This indicates that SMM not only
influences brand performance directly but also enhances it indirectly through the
cultivation of trust. This finding by Morgan and Hunt (1994) reinforces the central role of
trust as a critical intervening variable within relational marketing frameworks. Similarly,
brand trust partially mediates the relationship between learning orientation and brand
performance. Organizations that are adaptive and learning-oriented improve performance
not solely through operational excellence but also by nurturing customer trust. This
suggests that organizational learning strengthens performance outcomes through
relational mechanisms grounded in trust (Real et al., 2014). These findings highlight

CONCLUSION
This research investigated the effects of SMM and learning orientation on brand
performance, incorporating brand trust as a mediating construct among halal-certified
SMEs. The findings derived from SEM-PLS analysis confirm that all hypothesized
relationships receive empirical support. Both SMM and learning orientation demonstrate
positive and significant effects on brand trust as well as on brand performance.
Furthermore, brand trust demonstrates a strong direct effect on brand performance and
partially mediates the relationships between SMM and learning orientation and brand
performance. These findings highlight that effective digital marketing, when reinforced
by a strong organizational learning culture, forms a critical foundation for cultivating
customer trust and sustaining brand performance over time.
From a managerial perspective, SME practitioners are encouraged to integrate
interactive, responsive, and value-driven social media marketing strategies with the
development of an adaptive learning culture capable of responding to market changes.
Theoretically, this study emphasizes the central role of brand trust as a mediating
mechanism in digitally driven brand performance models. Future research may extend
the analysis to other sectors and geographic areas to enhance the generalizability of the
findings, employ longitudinal designs to capture the dynamic interactions among
constructs over time, and incorporate variables such as customer engagement, innovation
capability, or brand image to provide a more nuanced understanding of how brand
performance is cultivated in the digital ecosystem.
