Abstract

Research background: Recent years have introduced a tremendous growth in  the application
of artificial intelligence (AI). Many organizations  use this technological development to en-
hance operations and facilitate daily tasks. Influe ncer marketing is a field where the use of AI
becomes inevitable.
Purpose of the article: The purpose of this paper is to analyze the current  state of research on
AI in influencer marketing and provide the core guidelines for further exploration of this field.
Methods: Bibliometric analysis of scientific literature is p erformed based on an article selec-
tion procedure encompassing steps indicated in the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic
Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) methodology. The  Scopus database was chosen, as it
has the relatively widest coverage of the Social Sciences. The search using keywords “artificial
intelligence” or “AI” and “influencer marketing” found 79 documents.

Literature review

Influencer marketing is a marketing strategy in whi ch companies rely
on influential figures and opinion leaders to encou rage consumers to pur-
chase their products or services (Allal-Chérif et al ., 2024). Influencers are
individuals who have garnered substantial following s on one or more so-
cial media platforms (Harambašić & Škare, 2024) while playing a mediating
role in spreading the messages from organizations t o individuals (Uz-
unoğlu & Misci Kip, 2014). On social media, influen cers share their opin-
ions about products, services, and brands every day  (Harrigan et al ., 2021).
This enables the establishment of authentic relatio nships, humanization of
brands, and creation of trust across audiences (Khr aiwish & Alsharif, 2024).
Sands et al . (2022b) emphasize that despite the possibilities for brands to
connect with consumers ensured by influencer market ing, it is also related
to risks of transgression and damage to brand reputation.
Han and Balabanis (2023) argue that influencer mark eting draws upon
several key theories from social sciences to explai n its effectiveness. These
theories include source credibility (SCT), persuasi on knowledge (PKT),
congruity (CT), parasocial interaction (PSIT), and similarity-attraction
(SAT). SCT stands for the main source characteristi cs, for example, exper-
tise, trustworthiness, likeability, similarity, and  familiarity (Martensen et
al ., 2018), to explain the influencers’ features nece ssary for convincing their
followers. As there is a general requirement for so cial media influencers to

disclose any commercial relationship (Boerman, 2020 ), PKT relies on con-
sumer awareness and vulnerability regarding influen cer messages. Analyz-
ing influencer–follower, influencer–product, and fo llower–product congru-
encies, CT deals with communication and persuasion contexts (Belanche et
al ., 2021). Moreover, parasocial interaction with an influencer, explored by
PSIT, was found to influence consumer engagement an d stickiness signifi-
cantly (Melnychuk et al ., 2024). SAT in the context of influencer marketin g
suggests that people are more likely to be drawn to  and influenced by indi-
viduals who share similar characteristics, attitude s, and values with them
(Han & Balabanis, 2023).
Mura and Stehlíková (2025) emphasize that each indu strial revolution
results in the disappearance of certain professions  and the emergence of
new ones. Harambašić and Škare (2024) state that us ing Natural Language
Processing and Sentiment Analysis tools, AI provide s a strong impact on
powering algorithms to recognize the authenticity o f content provided by
influencers and better audience targeting. Being a cutting-edge frontier in
influencer marketing, since 2015, AI offers a novel  approach and different
tools like robots, chatbots, etc., used for leverag ing social media data
(Bansal et al ., 2024). As AI is constantly evolving in contempor ary society
and becoming an omniscient theme (Dabija & Vătămăne scu, 2023), the
recent trend in influencer marketing is that AI-pow ered digital avatars
called “virtual influencers” are replacing human in fluencers (Allal-Chérif et
al ., 2024). While virtual influencers are still relat ively new, their potential to
captivate audiences and drive brand engagement is u ndeniable, opening
a fascinating new chapter in the evolution of influ encer marketing (Bansal
et al., 2024). The main quality of AI-generated influence rs is the absence of
human flaws: they might even seem more authentic th an humans (Allal-
Chérif et al ., 2024). Moreover, similar to humans, AI influence rs can evoke
parasocial relationships and credibility perception s, which further trans-
form into social capital within a culturally rich, collectivist setting (Omeish
et al ., 2025).
The latter shift from human-based to AI-based influ encer marketing has
also encouraged a paradigm shift. Scientists starte d searching for new theo-
retical backgrounds, theories, and models to explai n the phenomenon.
Drawing on the theory of mind perception (TMP), res earchers determined
that consumers tend to distrust virtual influencers  more than human ones
due to their lower perceived experience (Liu et al ., 2025). Under the Com-
puters Are Social Actors (CASA) framework, scholars  explained how fol-

lowers tend to treat virtual influencers as social actors and apply social
norms to them, even when aware they are not real pe ople (Yu et al ., 2024).
Moreover, virtual influencers do not face real-life experiences and emotions
like humans; the phenomenon, when virtual influence rs look and act simi-
lar but not equal to humans, followers experience a n unexplained discom-

Despite the topicality of leveraging AI influencers , the empirical
knowledge about how consumers respond to them and h ow brands might
best leverage AI influencers, or how and why consum ers react to them, is
still limited (Sands et al ., 2022a).

Research methods

To understand and organize previous findings, diffe rent quantitative and
qualitative approaches to literature review can be applied (Eger & Žižka,
2024). To synthesize the state of knowledge in the selected field for the
identification of the tendencies and perspectives, it is suggested to provide
systematic literature reviews (Page et al ., 2021) where bibliometrics em-
ploys quantitative methods to analyze and evaluate scientific research and
its effects (Ogutu et al ., 2023). Based on the systematic analysis of scien tific
literature, this review aims to establish guideline s for further research in
the field of AI in influencer marketing. As recomme nded by scholars (Al-
sharif et al ., 2024), the article selection procedure encompass ed steps indi-
cated in Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic R eviews and Meta-
Analyses (PRISMA-2020) methodology, which is a stan dardized approach
that enables ensuring the transparency, reliability , completeness, and quali-
ty of the analysis (Kovač et al ., 2024), and supports the identification of
trends, research gaps, and attractive areas in the domain (Page et al ., 2021).

To answer the research questions, the keywords refl ecting the chosen
field of study were determined: “artificial intelli gence” or “AI” and “influ-
encer marketing”. Further, the database was selecte d. The major typical
databases, representing citation indexes for genera l-purpose scientific liter-
ature, encompassing articles, conference proceeding s, and books, are Sco-
pus (relatively superior in coverage of the Social Sciences) and Web of Sci-
ence (advancing in research in Natural Sciences and  Engineering) (Kum-
pulainen & Seppänen, 2022). Kovač et al . (2024) emphasize that, together
with a comprehensive dataset, Scopus offers reliabl e tools enabling the
analysis of citation patterns, and its interface fa cilitates an accurate analysis
of data. The authors decided to explore the Scopus database as having rela-
tively widest coverage of the Social Sciences (Pile lienė & Jucevičius, 2023).
According to PRISMA-2020 protocol, there are three phases of document
selection for the analysis: identification of relev ant documents in the select-
ed database, screening the identified documents for  eligibility (language,
subject area, accessibility, publication stage, etc .), and inclusion (selecting
the final set of documents for the analysis) (Page et al ., 2021). In the identifi-
cation stage, the initial search was provided on th e 1st of April 2025 and
resulted in 79 documents: 75 published and 4 articl es in-press. While
screening the documents, it was determined that all  the documents were
published in the English language, which made them understandable for
researchers worldwide. Considering the document typ e, 46 articles, 12 con-
ference papers, 14 book chapters, 4 reviews, 2 conf erence reviews, and 1
book were identified. The detailed distribution of documents based on the

Conclusions

After performing a comprehensive literature review of the papers pub-
lished on the Scopus database on AI in influencer m arketing, several con-
clusions can be provided.
The review revealed that, being a relatively new re search direction, the
field is rapidly evolving and attracting new resear chers. The emerging field
of interest has attracted researchers from 40 count ries so far. The countries
leading (both by the number of documents and the nu mber of citations) in
the development of the field of AI in influencer marketing were found to be
the United States, Australia, and the United Kingdo m. India and China,
despite being in the top 5 based on the number of p ublications, do not at-
tract a substantial number of citations to be consi dered as leading the de-
velopment of the field.

Out of 40 contributing countries, ten (Libya, Sri L anka, Taiwan, Den-
mark, Portugal, Egypt, Macao, Mexico, New Zealand, and the United Arab
Emirates) have not been cited yet. However, the doc uments by these coun-
tries (except New Zealand, with a conference paper published in 2016)
were published only in 2024-2025; therefore, it is highly believable that the
citations are on the way. Moreover, as the field is  newly emerging, the pos-
sibilities of becoming one of the leading authors a re high. Therefore, schol-
ars publishing in the field of AI in influencer mar keting are highly encour-
aged not only to publish but also to disseminate their findings.
The keyword analysis revealed that the field still needs clarification in
terms of terminology. Mainly, the terms ‘AI influen cer’, ‘AI-generated in-
fluencer’, ‘computer-generated influencer’, ‘digita l influencer’ and ‘virtual
influencer’ are used to describe the same phenomeno n. On the other hand,
the terms ‘digital influencer’, and ‘virtual influe ncer’ are also used to name
the influencer in general. Such different understan ding and usage of the
terms violate the pure evolution of the domain.
The author keyword analysis highlighted 5 emerging research trends
that could guide future researchers in developing t he domain. The main
further directions would be: (1) AI influencer feat ure determination and
analysis; (2) consumer behavioral reactions towards  the AI influencers; (3)
platforms, medias and networks used by AI influence rs; (4) marketing
strategies to be used by AI influencers; and (5) su itability and appropriate-
ness of research methods in AI influencer marketing . The identified future
research trends would also serve as a checklist for  managers to be consid-
ered while choosing to apply the AI influencer as a  part of their marketing
strategies: determination of the most suitable plat form and choosing ap-
propriate features of the influencer would set a ba ckground for strategy
selection, and lead to the desired (forecasted) consumer reactions.
Several limitations can be named to characterize th is review. Firstly, the
research was limited to the information found on th e Scopus database;
therefore, documents and citations found on other d atabases were not
acknowledged. Secondly, only the documents encompas sing the deter-
mined keywords (‘artificial-intelligence’ OR ‘AI’ A ND ‘influencer-
marketing’) in their Title, Abstract or Keywords we re considered for the
review. However, the field-related documents could have been published
without using some of the selected keywords.
