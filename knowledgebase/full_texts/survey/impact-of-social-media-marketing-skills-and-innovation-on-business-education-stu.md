Abstract
The rapid proliferation of social media platforms has fundamentally transformed commercial
landscapes worldwide, compelling educational institutions to re-evaluate their curricular offerings.
This study empirically investigates the impact of social media marketing (SMM) skills acquisition
and digital innovation exposure on academic performance, entrepreneurial int ent, and career
readiness among business education students in Nigerian tertiary institutions. Employing a
quantitative cross-sectional survey design, primary data were collected from 384 purposively and
randomly sampled undergraduate students from eight u niversities and polytechnics across the
South-East, South -West, and North -Central geopolitical zones of Nigeria. Structured
questionnaires adapted from validated instruments (Cronbach's alpha = 0.87) were administered,
and data were analysed using descript ive statistics, Pearson product -moment correlation, and
multiple linear regression. Findings reveal that SMM skills acquisition exerts a statistically
significant positive effect on students' entrepreneurial intent (β = 0.612, p < 0.001) and career
readiness (β = 0.548, p < 0.001). Exposure to digital innovation tools similarly demonstrates a
strong positive relationship with academic performance (r = 0.693, p < 0.05). Regression analysis
indicates that SMM skills and innovation together explain 68.4% of va riance in overall graduate
employability outcomes (R² = 0.684, F (2, 381) = 412.3, p < 0.001). Additionally, 74.2% of
respondents reported that SMM coursework had increased their confidence in starting a digital
business. The study recommends the urgent integration of structured SMM and digital innovation
modules into formal business education curricula across Nigerian tertiary institutions, supported
by public-private partnerships with technology firms.

environment for digital entrepreneurship and marketing innovation (NCC, 2025). Despite these
opportunities, there exists a critical skills deficit among Nigerian university graduates in the domain of
social media marketing and digital innovation. Employers consistently report that fresh graduates lack
practical competencies in content strategy, analytics interpretation, paid digital a dvertising, and
customer relationship management via social platforms (Adeyemi & Lawal, 2024). This skills gap has

management knowledge and practical commercial application, is uniquely positioned to address this
deficit.
Existing literature has explored various dimensions of digital competence in higher education (Prensky,
2001; Redecker, 2017; Eze et al., 2020), yet there remains a conspicuous scarcity of empirical studies
that specifically examine the effect of structured social media marketing skills instruction and digital
innovation exposure on measurable student outcomes in sub-Saharan Africa. Most extant studies focus
on either developed Western contexts or broad ICT adoption, failing to capture the nuanced interplay
between SMM pedagogy, entrepreneurial motivation, academic performance, and career readiness in
the Nigerian business education context.

Objectives of the Study
This study therefore seeks to:
(1) examine the extent to which SMM skills acquisition affects entrepreneurial intent among
Nigerian business education students;
(2) assess the relationship between digital innovation exposure and academic performance;
(3) determine the combined predictive power of  SMM skills and innovation on graduate
employability outcomes; and
(4) ascertain the proportion of students who perceive SMM training as enhancing their confidence
to start digital businesses.

Literature Review
Social media marketing is broadly defined as the utilisation of social networking platforms to promote
products, services, or brands through content creation, community engagement, paid advertising, and
data-driven optimization strategies (Tuten & Solomon, 2017). Within educational setting s, SMM has
emerged as both a subject of study and a pedagogical tool that enhances student engagement and real -
world learning (Manca, 2020). Integrating SMM instruction into business education curricula has been
shown to foster critical thinking, data lite racy, creativity, and entrepreneurial thinking among students
(Aswani et al., 2018).
In Nigeria, the adoption of social media marketing instruction within tertiary curricula has been uneven
and largely informal. Okoye and Nwosu (2022) observed that while m ajority of business education
students in southeast Nigeria actively use social media for personal communication, fewer than 30%
had received formal instruction in leveraging these platforms for marketing or entrepreneurial purposes.

This disconnection between students' social media habits and structured skills acquisition represents a
critical pedagogical gap with significant economic consequences. Innovation in the digital context
encompasses the deployment of new technologies, processes, or business mode ls to create, deliver, or
capture value in ways previously not possible (Tidd & Bessant, 2018). For business education, digital
innovation exposure encompasses students' interaction with e -commerce platforms, AI -driven
marketing tools, data analytics dashb oards, digital payment systems, virtual marketplaces, and social
media automation technologies. Studies consistently affirm that students exposed to digital innovation
ecosystems demonstrate higher levels of academic motivation, entrepreneurial self -efficacy, and
preparedness for the labour market (Nkosi & Lekena, 2023; Ukachi, 2021).
The National Universities Commission (NUC) 2023 Benchmark Minimum Academic Standards
(BMAS) for Business Education in Nigeria acknowledges the importance of digital competenci es but
stops short of mandating specific SMM or digital innovation modules. This regulatory ambivalence has
contributed to inconsistent implementation across institutions (NUC, 2023).
Entrepreneurial intent refers to an individual's cognitive predispositio n to establish a new business
venture in the foreseeable future (Bird, 1988; Ajzen, 1991). In the Nigerian context, where formal
employment opportunities are scarce, entrepreneurial intent among graduates is both a psychological
and economic imperative. Re search by Igwe et al. (2023) demonstrates that digital skills training
significantly elevates entrepreneurial intent scores among Nigerian undergraduates, suggesting that
structured digital education can serve as a catalyst for self -employment. Career read iness
conceptualized as the degree to which graduates possess the competencies demanded by contemporary
employers has become a central measure of higher education effectiveness (NACE, 2022). The World
Economic Forum (2023) identifies digital literacy, crea tivity, and complex problem-solving as the top
competencies employers seek, all of which are cultivated through SMM training and digital innovation
exposure.

Theoretical Framework
This study is anchored on two complementary theoretical frameworks. First, the Technology
Acceptance Model (TAM) (Davis, 1989) posits that users' behavioral intentions to use a technology are
determined by their perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use. Within this study, students who
perceive social media marketing skills as useful and accessible are more likely to internalize and apply
these skills, translating into higher entrepreneurial intent and career readiness. Second, the Human
Capital Theory (Becker, 1964) argues that investments in education and training increase individuals'
productive capacities and economic returns. Applied to this study, the acquisition of SMM skills and
digital innovation competencies represents a form of human capital investment that directly enhances
students' market value, entrepreneurial potential, and employability outcomes.

Methodology
This study adopted a quantitative cross -sectional survey research design. The target population
comprised all final -year (300 and 400 level) undergraduate students enrolled in Business Education,
Marketing, and Entrepreneurship programmes across Nigerian Federal and State Universities and
Polytechnics. The research focused on final -year students because they have accumulated sufficient
academic exposure to meaningfully assess the relevance of social media m arketing and digital
innovation within their educational experience. A multi-stage sampling technique was employed. In the
first stage, eight institutions were purposively selected to ensure geographic representation across three

geopolitical zones: South -East (University of Nigeria Nsukka, Enugu State University of Science and
Technology), South -West (Lagos State University, Obafemi Awolowo University, Yaba College of
Technology), and North -Central (University of Abuja, Federal Polytechnic Nasarawa, Benue State
University). In the second stage, stratified random sampling was used to select student participants from
each institution.
The sample size was determined using Yamane's (1967) formula: n = N / (1 + Ne²), where N = the
accessible population estimate of 4,820 final-year students, e = margin of error (0.05). This yielded a
minimum sample of 370. To compensate for attrition and non -response, 420 questionnaires were
distributed, with 384 returned usable, representing a 91.4% response rate.
Data were collected using a self-administered structured questionnaire titled "Social Media Marketing
Skills, Innovation and Business Education Survey (SMMSIBE -26)". The instrument comprised five
sections: (A) demographic characteristics; (B) Social Media Marketing Skill s Acquisition Scale
(SMMSAS) 18 items; (C) Digital Innovation Exposure Scale (DIES) 14 items; (D) Entrepreneurial
Intent Scale (EIS) 12 items adapted from Liñán and Chen (2009); and (E) Graduate Employability and
Career Readiness Scale (GECRS) 16 items ada pted from the NACE Career Readiness Competency
Framework (2022).
All items were measured on a 5 -point Likert scale ranging from 1 (Strongly Disagree) to 5 (Strongly
Agree). The instrument was validated through face and content validity review by five academic experts
in Business Education and Marketing. Pilot testing on 40 students at the Federal University Oye -Ekiti
yielded a Cronbach's alpha coefficient of 0.87 for the entire instrument (subscales ranged from 0.81 to
0.89), confirming high internal consistency reliability.
Quantitative data were analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics Version 27. Descriptive statistics
(frequencies, percentages, means, and standard deviations) were used to profile respondents and
summarize key variables. Pearson product -moment correlation (r) was employed to assess bivariate
relationships between digital innovation exposure and academic performance. Multiple linear
regression analysis was conducted to determine the combined predictive effect of SMM skills and
innovation exposure on graduate employability outcomes. The level of statistical significance was set
at p < 0.05.

Discussion of Findings
The results of this study present compelling empirical evidence that social media marketing skills and
digital innovation exposure constitute significant determinants of business education students'
entrepreneurial trajectories and career outcomes in Nigeria. The composite mean SMM score of 3.15
(Moderate) acr oss all respondents indicates a baseline competence driven largely by organic social
media use rather than formal instruction — a finding consistent with Okoye and Nwosu (2022) who
similarly documented high social media usage but low formal marketing skill s among Nigerian
undergraduates.
The strong correlation between digital innovation exposure and academic performance (r = 0.693, p <
0.01) aligns with Ukachi (2021) and Nkosi and Lekena (2023), who documented analogous
relationships in South African and Ni gerian higher education settings. The mechanism appears to be

motivational: exposure to innovation ecosystems increases students' intrinsic motivation, hands -on
problem-solving skills, and engagement with coursework, thereby lifting academic outcomes. The
regression findings demonstrating SMM skills as the stronger predictor of employability (β = 0.548)
compared to innovation exposure (β = 0.469) suggest that employer -valued skills in social media
marketing may be more immediately instrumental than broader innovation exposure alone. This has
practical implications for curriculum design: while both dimensions are valuable, institutions
constrained by limited resources should prioritize practical SMM skills training as the higher -yield
intervention.
The finding that 74.2% of students report increased confidence in starting a digital business following
SMM training is particularly consequential in the Nigerian economic context. With graduate
unemployment hovering above 42%, cultivating entrepreneurial self -efficacy through digital skills
education offers a viable, scalable pathway to self -employment and value creation. This corroborates
Igwe et al. (2023) and the broader global literature on digital entrepreneurship education (Nambisan,
2017). The institutional c omparison (Table 8) provides perhaps the most policy -relevant finding:
institutions that have formally integrated SMM courses into their curricula consistently outperform
peers on every measured outcome. This natural experiment suggests that curriculum pol icy decisions
have tangible, measurable impacts on student outcomes  a finding with direct implications for NUC
policy formulation.

Conclusion
This empirical study has established that social media marketing skills acquisition and digital
innovation exposure exert strong, statistically significant, and practically meaningful positive effects on
the entrepreneurial intent, academic performance, and career readiness of business education students
in Nigeria. The study contributes original empirical evidence to a domain previously under-researched
in sub-Saharan African educational contexts, and provides a robust statistical foundation (R² = 0.684)
for policy advocacy. The findings firmly establish that SMM and digital innovation education are not
optional enrichments but essential competencies that determine graduate outcomes in the 21st -century
Nigerian economy.

Recommendations
1. The National Universities Commission (NUC) and National Board for Technical Education
(NBTE) should revise the Benchmark Minimum Academic Standards (BMAS) to mandate the
inclusion of at least one dedicated Social Media Marketing and Digital Innovation course in
all Business Education, Marketing, and Entrepreneurship undergraduate programmes.
2. Tertiary institutions should priorities continuous professional development for faculty in SMM
and digital tools, through partnerships with platforms such as Meta Blueprint, Google Digital
Garage, and HubSpot Academy, to ensure instructors can deliver current, industry -relevant
content.
3. State and federal governments should facilitate formal partnerships between universities and
technology companies (e.g., Meta, Google, Flutterwave, Paystack) to sponsor digital skills
bootcamps, donate hardware and software resources, and provide industry m entorship for
students.
4. Institutions lacking dedicated innovation labs should establish social media marketing studios
and digital entrepreneurship hubs, equipped with professional -grade analytics tools, content
creation equipment, and e-commerce simulation environments.
