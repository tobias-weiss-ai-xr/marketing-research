Abstract
This systematic review examines the influence of technological infrastructure and perceived organizational usefulness
on digital marketing adoption in Ethiopian banks, applying PRISMA methodology to synthesize peer-reviewed research
from 2020 to 2025. Findings reveal that reliable internet connectivity, platform reliability, adequate technological infra-
structure (including mobile banking platforms, digital tools like social media and e-mail marketing), and network robust-
ness are essential but insufficient conditions for adoption. Managerial perceptions of usefulness such as performance
expectancy, return on investment (ROI), and strategic alignment are critical mediators that determine whether digital
marketing initiatives are embraced. Additionally, digital marketing enhances operational speed, customer satisfaction,
and marketing efficiency, with social media platforms (Facebook, Telegram, Twitter, YouTube) playing a vital role in cus-
tomer engagement and organizational communication. The review also highlights the importance of digital literacy
training for both bank employees and customers to maximize digital marketing benefits. Government support and tel-
ecom infrastructure development emerge as significant enablers in fostering digital adoption. Future research priorities
include investigating the interplay between technological, organizational, and environmental factors within the Ethio-
pian context and assessing the long-term impacts of digital marketing on competitive advantage and financial perfor-
mance of banks. Study DOIs and key references are provided to ensure reproducibility and further scholarly inquiry.
Keywords Digital marketing adoption, Technological infrastructure, Ethiopian banks, Organizational usefulness
Introduction
Digital transformation is reshaping how banks engage
customers: Digital marketing channels (web, social
media, e-mail, mobile push, and app-based messaging)
are now central to customer acquisition, cross-selling,
and service retention [1]. In developing countries, the
pace and character of digital uptake depend strongly
on technological infrastructure (broadband and mobile
coverage, data centers, reliable electricity) and organiza -
tional readiness (strategy, systems, skills). Recent empiri -
cal work in Ethiopia confirms this dual dependency:
Nationally representative analyses find that mobile own -
ership, ATM/debit card access and internet connectivity
are significant enablers of digital payment and e-banking
uptake [2], while bank-level studies report that system
reliability, responsiveness, and security predict user satis-
faction and continued use [3].
At the organizational level, perceived usefulness
defined as management’s belief that digital marketing will
improve performance, customer reach, or profitability is
consistently associated with higher adoption intent and
resource allocation for digital initiatives [4, 5]. Ethiopia
is actively pursuing a national digital strategy (Digital
Ethiopia [6]) and has attracted development finance to

Research questions
1. What effect do technological infrastructure and per -
ceived usefulness (organization perspective) have on
digital marketing adoption in Ethiopian banks?
2. Which specific infrastructure elements matter most
for adoption?
3. How does organizational perceived usefulness shape
resource allocation and implementation of digital
marketing?
4. What barriers and opportunities arise from these
technological factors?
The following objectives address these research
questions.
Research objectives
Main objective: Systematically review and synthesize evi -
dence on how technological infrastructure and perceived
usefulness (organization perspective) affect digital mar -
keting adoption in Ethiopian banks.
Specific objectives:
1. Assess how infrastructural elements (connectivity,
system reliability, power/data centers) facilitate or
hinder adoption.
2. Evaluate how organizational perceived usefulness
(performance expectancy, ROI perception, strategic
alignment) influences adoption decisions.
3. Identify practical barriers and opportunities for
accelerating digital marketing adoption in the Ethio -
pian banking sector.
Methodology
Inclusion and exclusion criteria
Inclusion: empirical, peer-reviewed articles and high-
quality open access studies focused on Ethiopia (or Ethi -
opia-specific analyses within regional studies), published
2020–2025, addressing technological infrastructure,
perceived usefulness (organization/management per -
spective), e-banking/digital banking or digital marketing
adoption and English language only.
The choice of a 5-year timeline for a Systematic Lit -
erature Review (SLR) is often made to balance the need
for up-to-date information with the volume of litera -
ture, capturing recent developments without being over -
whelmed by a massive amount of data. A 5-year window
can reflect the most current practices and scientific
knowledge, which is crucial for some topics, especially in
rapidly evolving fields like digital Marketing.
Exclusion: non-peer-reviewed blogs or purely opinion
pieces; studies before 2020 (except foundational TAM/
TAM-extensions for theoretical framing); studies not
relevant to banking/financial services or not addressing
technological/organizational factors.

Summary of results
The included studies collectively highlight that tech -
nological factors play a vital role in digital marketing
adoption and digital banking services within Ethiopian
banks and related sectors. Teka [4] and Tiwari [1] found
that perceived usefulness and facilitating infrastructure
conditions such as reliable internet and security are key
determinants of customer adoption of electronic bank -
ing (e-banking) in Ethiopia. Ayinaddis et  al. [3] empha -
sized that service quality elements like system reliability
and responsiveness strongly affect customer satisfaction
and loyalty. Andaregie et al. [2] identified infrastructural
enablers like mobile phone ownership, internet access,
and ATM/debit card availability as national determinants
of digital payment adoption. Hordofa [7] discussed how
digital transformation indicators and payment penetra -
tion are linked to financial stability in emerging markets
including Ethiopia. Kwateng et al. (2019) reported on the
significance of performance expectancy (perceived use -
fulness) in mobile banking acceptance.
Broader technological readiness, adoption costs, and
awareness were underlined by Faiz [10], Gupta & Rani
[11], and Bogár et al. [12] as important in digital market -
ing adoption in firms and microenterprises. Advance -
ments in AI, blockchain, and data-driven personalization
were discussed by [13] and [14] in enhancing digital
marketing effectiveness and automation in banking strat -
egies. Cost, technological readiness, and perceived use -
fulness were echoed as key drivers by Mogollón-Claudett
et al. [15] in SMEs, while [16] and [17] emphasized digital
payment fintech marketing strategies focusing on secu -
rity, service quality, and user convenience.
Thematic synthesis and discussion
The synthesis of these 16 studies reveals three over -
arching themes shaping digital marketing adoption in
Ethiopian banks: technological infrastructure, perceived
usefulness and managerial attitudes, and emerging digital
technologies and strategies.
Technological infrastructure is consistently identified
as foundational. Reliable internet connectivity, robust
platforms, mobile ownership, access to digital payment
infrastructure, and system responsiveness emerge as
critical enablers for customers and banks to participate
in digital marketing and financial services [1–4]. Without
these infrastructural preconditions, banks’ digital mar -
keting initiatives cannot be effectively deployed or scaled.
National digital readiness and telecom support are cru -
cial for creating an enabling ecosystem.
Perceived usefulness is the dominant psychological
and managerial precursor to adoption decisions. Per -
formance expectancy, strategic fit, ROI considerations,
and perceived benefits strongly influence managerial
willingness to invest and customers’ motivation to engage
(Kwateng et al., 2019; [10],Mogollón-Claudett et al., [17]).
This mediates the relationship between infrastructure
availability and actual usage. Studies demonstrate that
banks’ belief in technology’s ability to improve opera -
tional speed, marketing efficiency, customer satisfaction,
and loyalty drives adoption of digital marketing tools [3,
11, 13].
Emerging technologies and advanced digital marketing
strategies play an increasingly important role. The inte -
gration of AI, machine learning, blockchain, and auto -
mation enables banks to personalize marketing, improve
customer interaction through chatbots, and enhance
security-focused fintech marketing approaches, which
helps build trust and competitive advantage [14, 16,
20],Amnas et al., [17]). These new digital tools stimulate
adoption by addressing customers’ security concerns and
convenience needs, particularly in financial services.
Training and skill development for both employees and
customers to enhance digital literacy is highlighted as a
necessary complement to these efforts. Without adequate
skills and trust, adoption remains limited despite tech -
nological availability [11, 12]. Furthermore, government
policies and institutional initiatives supporting network
improvements and fintech innovation catalyze wider dig -
ital marketing uptake [7].
Future research should explore the dynamic interac -
tions between these technological, organizational, and
environmental factors in Ethiopia’s banking context to
develop tailored adoption frameworks. Longitudinal
studies investigating how digital marketing adoption
impacts banks’ financial performance, competitiveness,
and customer retention over time will enrich under -
standing and guide policy interventions.
Discussion
This synthesis confirms a two-stage model for digital
marketing adoption in Ethiopian banks. In stage one,
technological infrastructure (both public broadband, data
centers, mobile coverage and private bank servers, secure
platforms, backup power) establishes the necessary oper -
ational capacity. Andaregie et al. [2] and national reports
show that Ethiopia’s recent investments and the Digital
Ethiopia 2025 strategy have begun to expand this capac -
ity, but gaps remain, especially in rural areas and in sys -
tem redundancy (World Bank,GSMA).
In stage two, organizational perceived usefulness deter-
mines whether banks actually adopt and scale digital
marketing. Perceived usefulness is shaped by perfor -
mance expectations (cost savings, cross-sell lift, customer
lifetime value), risk perceptions (security, fraud), and
strategic alignment. Teka [4] and UTAUT2-based
studies indicate that banks with leaders who see clear

performance benefits are more likely to invest in analyt -
ics, CRM integration, and digital campaigns,by contrast,
banks that view digital channels as risky or low-ROI hesi-

The adoption of digital marketing in Ethiopian banks is
fundamentally shaped by technological infrastructure
and managerial perceptions, as substantiated by the
recent work of Feyisa Girma and Tibebe Beshah [20] on
ICT adoption for strategic communication in selected
Addis Ababa banks. Their study emphasizes that ICT
infrastructure—encompassing reliable networks, digital
platforms, and communication technologies—is a criti -
cal enabler of strategic communication and marketing
adoption within banks. This finding aligns closely with
the systemic importance of technological infrastructure
highlighted in the reviewed studies [1, 2, 4].
Feyisa Girma and Tibebe Beshah [9] further reveal that
managerial perceptions, particularly beliefs in the useful -
ness and strategic benefits of ICT, serve as psychological
antecedents mediating the relationship between infra -
structure availability and actual adoption. This echoes the
synthesis theme that perceived usefulness—in terms of
performance expectancy, ROI, and strategic alignment—
strongly influences managerial willingness to invest in
digital marketing initiatives and shapes organizational
resource allocation ( [10],Mogollón-Claudett et al., [15]).
The findings underscore that without managerial buy-
in, even robust technological infrastructure may fail to
translate into effective adoption, reinforcing the neces -
sity of complementary organizational change efforts. The
incorporation of advanced technologies such as AI and
blockchain [14, 16, 20] further necessitates that leader -
ship understands and champions these innovations to
leverage their potential for personalized marketing and
improved security.
Moreover, the study’s Ethiopian context adds speci -
ficity to the broader discourse by drawing attention to
infrastructural challenges such as network reliability and
digital literacy gaps—issues echoed in the reviewed lit -
erature [7, 11, 12]. These factors highlight the contextual
barriers that banks must navigate to foster widespread
digital marketing adoption.
Hence, the interplay between ICT infrastructural
readiness and managerial perceptions forms a dual-
track foundation for digital marketing adoption that
must be addressed holistically. Banks are encouraged to
pair investments in digital infrastructure with manage -
rial training and clear business case development aimed
at elucidating operational benefits. Policymakers should
concurrently work to enhance national digital ecosystems
and establish supportive regulatory frameworks.
In conclusion, Feyisa Girma and Tibebe Beshah’s
[9] study significantly enriches the systematic review’s
discourse by providing empirical evidence from Ethio -
pian banks that confirms and contextualizes the impor -
tance of technological and managerial factors in digital
marketing adoption.
Implications for practice
The synthesis of technological infrastructure, perceived
usefulness, and emerging digital technologies in Ethio -
pian banks offers several important implications for
stakeholders aiming to foster effective digital marketing
adoption.
Integrated Infrastructure and Organizational Strat -
egy: Banks must approach infrastructure upgrades such
as reliable internet connectivity, platform robustness,
mobile banking access, and backup power not as stan -
dalone projects but intertwined with organizational
change efforts. This includes developing and present -
ing clear, data-backed business cases to senior leader -
ship that demonstrate the performance expectancy, ROI,
and strategic alignment of digital marketing initiatives.
Pilot digital marketing campaigns with measurable KPIs
are necessary to provide tangible evidence of benefits,
thereby elevating managerial attitudes toward sustained
investment.
Digital Literacy and Skills Development: Both employee
and customer digital literacy are critical to bridging the
gap between technological availability and actual adop -
tion. Training programs should be designed to enhance
skills in utilizing digital marketing tools, analytics, cus -
tomer relationship management integrations, and emerg-
ing technologies such as AI-driven chatbots. This human
capital investment is essential to build trust, reduce
resistance, and maximize operational speed and market -
ing efficiency.
Leveraging Emerging Digital Technologies: Adoption
efforts must actively incorporate advanced technolo -
gies like artificial intelligence, blockchain, and automa -
tion. These innovations enable personalized marketing,
improved customer interactions, and enhanced security
measures—addressing key barriers of trust and conveni -
ence for customers. Banks that strategically integrate
these technologies can gain competitive advantage and
stimulate higher engagement and loyalty.
Supportive Policy and Ecosystem Development: Regula -
tors and development partners should prioritize expand -
ing national digital infrastructure, including broadband
capacity and data center availability, to reduce infrastruc-
tural bottlenecks that constrain adoption beyond urban
centers. Clear and harmonized data governance, cyber -
security frameworks, and fintech innovation policies
are essential to mitigate legal risks and encourage bank
investments in digital marketing platforms and strategies.

Continuous Monitoring and Feedback Mechanisms:
Establishing mechanisms for ongoing assessment of

Research gaps
The thematic synthesis of 16 studies reveals critical gaps
in understanding the interplay between technological
infrastructure, perceived organizational usefulness, and
emerging digital technologies in driving digital marketing
adoption specifically within Ethiopian banks.
Infrastructure-Outcome Linkage Gap: While studies
consistently identify infrastructural enablers like reliable
internet, platform robustness, and mobile access as foun -
dational [4],Feyisa Girma & Tibebe Beshah, [9]; [2], no
Ethiopia-specific research employs longitudinal designs
to trace how targeted infrastructure investments—such
as broadband expansion or backup power systems—
translate into measurable digital marketing outcomes like
customer acquisition rates, conversion metrics, or ROI
over multi-year periods. Cross-sectional analyses domi -
nate, leaving causal pathways and temporal dynamics
unexamined.
Organizational Perceived Usefulness Gap: Existing lit -
erature heavily emphasizes customer-level perceived
usefulness (Kwateng et  al., 2019; [10]) but lacks empiri -
cal investigations into managerial and organizational-
level perceptions of digital marketing tools’ performance
expectancy, strategic fit, and resource allocation justifica-
tion within Ethiopian banking contexts [3, 11]. This omis-
sion hinders understanding of internal decision-making
barriers that mediate infrastructure availability and
adoption.
Emerging Technologies Integration Gap: The synthesis
highlights AI, blockchain, and automation’s potential for
personalized marketing [14, 16, 20], yet no studies quan -
titatively assess their adoption barriers, implementation
challenges, or competitive impacts relative to traditional
digital tools in Ethiopia’s resource-constrained banking
environment, particularly amid regulatory and digital lit -
eracy constraints [7, 12].
Contextual and Multi-Level Analysis Gap: Research
remains predominantly urban-focused (Addis Ababa-
centric, as in Feyisa Girma & Tibebe Beshah, [9]) and
siloed by theme, neglecting multi-level analyses that
integrate technological, organizational, environmental,
and policy factors within Ethiopia’s unique fintech eco -
system. Mixed-methods studies examining rural–urban
disparities, regulatory influences, and public–private
partnership effects are absent.
These gaps underscore the need for a comprehensive
framework that longitudinally integrates these thematic
dimensions to guide evidence-based digital marketing
strategies tailored to Ethiopian banks. (see the generated
image above).
Conclusion
The systematic review indicates that technological infra -
structure and organizational perceived usefulness are
key factors influencing the adoption of digital market -
ing in Ethiopian banks. While robust infrastructure pro -
vides the necessary operational capabilities, the ultimate
decision to adopt digital marketing strongly depends on
managerial perceptions of usefulness, including perfor -
mance expectations and strategic alignment. However,
these findings should be understood within the context
of diverse infrastructural challenges, managerial readi -
ness variations, and evolving technological landscapes in
Ethiopia. Efforts to enhance digital marketing adoption
should therefore combine investments in technological
platforms, national digital connectivity expansion, and
building strong internal business cases to support lead -
ership buy-in. Future initiatives could also benefit from
integrating emerging technologies and addressing organ -
izational change management to improve customer reach
and satisfaction.
Limitations of the study
Temporal Scope Limitations: The review focuses on stud -
ies published within a five-year period (2020–2025),
which may limit insights from earlier foundational
research and result in overlooking longer-term trends in
digital marketing adoption.
Study Selection and Coverage: The synthesis includes
16 peer-reviewed studies, which, although carefully
selected, may not fully capture all relevant empirical
investigations, especially unpublished works, non-Eng -
lish sources, or studies outside major databases.
Contextual Generalizability: Findings mostly reflect
the urban and institutional settings of Ethiopian banks,
potentially limiting applicability to rural or smaller finan-
cial institutions, or other developing country contexts
with differing infrastructural and organizational realities.
Methodological Diversity: Variations in methodologies
across the included studies, ranging from quantitative
surveys to qualitative analyses, introduce heterogeneity
that complicates direct comparison and generalization of
results.

Future scope for research
Longitudinal and Intervention Studies: There is a criti -
cal need for longitudinal research that tracks digital
marketing adoption over time across Ethiopian banks.
Such studies should measure the effects of infrastruc -
tural upgrades (e.g., platform enhancements, network
expansion), changes in managerial perceptions of digital
marketing’s usefulness, and corresponding marketing
outcomes such as customer engagement, sales conver -
sion, and profitability. Intervention studies implement -
ing specific digital technologies or managerial training
programs would provide valuable causal evidence on the
best practices to accelerate adoption and effectiveness.
Managerial and Organizational Perspectives of Per -
ceived Usefulness: Future research should deepen the
exploration of perceived usefulness from the managerial
and organizational viewpoint, moving beyond customer-
centric models. Investigations can explore how perfor -
mance expectancy, return on investment calculations,
and strategic fit influence leadership decision-making
and resource allocation for digital marketing initiatives in
banks. Qualitative and mixed methods approaches would
be especially useful to uncover nuanced organizational
dynamics, barriers, and facilitators.
Impact of Emerging Digital Technologies: As emerg -
ing technologies such as AI-driven personalization,
blockchain security, and marketing automation gain
prominence, future studies should assess how their inte -
gration influences adoption processes and marketing
effectiveness. Research can focus on barriers to adoption
of specific advanced tools as well as their competitive
impact on Ethiopian banks relative to regional and global
benchmarks.
Environmental and Policy Context: The role of external
contextual factors such as government digital infrastruc -
ture policies, fintech regulatory frameworks, and eco -
system support mechanisms requires further empirical
scrutiny. Studies examining how regulatory clarity, digital
literacy programs, and public–private partnerships foster
or impede digital marketing adoption will provide insight
for policy interventions tailored to Ethiopia’s unique
banking and technological landscape.
Customer Outcomes and Financial Performance:
Expanded research is needed to systematically link digi -
tal marketing adoption with ultimate organizational
performance indicators including customer satisfaction,
retention, market share growth, and financial returns in
Ethiopian banks. Multi-level analyses incorporating cus -
tomer behavioral data, sales metrics, and managerial per -
ceptions will enable a comprehensive understanding of
the value generated by digital marketing investments.
