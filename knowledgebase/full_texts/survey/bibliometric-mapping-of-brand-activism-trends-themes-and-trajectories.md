# Bibliometric mapping of brand activism: trends, themes, and trajectories

RESEARCH
International Review on Public and Nonprofit Marketing (2026) 23:449–477
https://doi.org/10.1007/s12208-026-00473-0
Abstract
The concept of brand activism has recently emerged as a distinct phenomenon,
garnering increasing scholarly interest. However, research in this area is still in
its infancy, characterized by fragmented studies and a lack of cohesive theoreti -
cal frameworks. This review aims to analyze the prevailing research patterns in
brand activism by classifying the essential attributes of publications. Using speci -
fied search criteria, the Scopus database was used to find, extract, select, and re -
view relevant publications from 1984 to 2025. In the search, 259 publications were
selected for this study after application of the exclusion and inclusion criteria. This
bibliometric analysis is based on performance analysis and science mapping. The
results reveal the most influential papers, authors, journals, and nations in this study
area. The findings suggest that brand activism research is multidisciplinary, en -
compassing business ethics, psychology, public policy, management, and market -
ing. The study identified six thematic clusters: Corporate Political Advocacy and
Institutional Change; Consumer Ethical Responses and Brand-Directed Activism;
Societal Impact of Brand Activism; Market–Society Tensions in Brand Activism;
Moral Emotional Responses to Brand Activism; Authenticity, Credibility, and Com-
petitive Outcomes of Brand Advocacy. These themes highlight their applications,
ranging from directing corporate political advocacy and stakeholder alignment on
socio-political issues to enhancing authenticity, credibility, and the competitive out -
comes of brand activism. The implications of this research emphasize brand activ -
ism as a strategic approach that links corporate values to societal requirements to
create social change in an increasingly polarized marketplace. This study is novel
in presenting a comprehensive bibliometric and science-mapping of brand activism
research to date, providing key takeaways, outlining future research directions, and
motivating scholars to explore this phenomenon further.

1 Introduction
Brand activism is gaining popularity as a key marketing approach (Moorman, 2020).
The term “brand activism” was coined by Sarkar & Kotler (2018) to describe brands’
efforts to support social, political, economic, or environmental reforms to attract pro-
gressive consumers. This strategy involves a company openly expressing its stance
on a controversial sociopolitical issue, demonstrating a proactive and distinctive
commitment to addressing pressing social concerns (Mirzaei et al., 2022; Vredenburg
et al., 2020). Brand activism distinguishes itself from conventional corporate social
responsibility (CSR) initiatives and cause-related marketing by engaging in unique
ways with contested sociopolitical causes to convey and uphold brand values (Pöyry
& Laaksonen, 2022). Thus, brand activism is a deliberate and values-based approach
(Vredenburg et al., 2020). In this approach, brands actively take a public stance on
contentious sociopolitical issues, with the dual objective of driving social change and
achieving economic objectives (Hydock et al., 2020). As brand activism continues to
gain prominence, scholars have focused on its repercussions for the brand, encom -
passing both economic and financial outcomes (Bhagwat et al., 2020; Hydock et
al., 2020). They also investigate how consumers respond to brand activism, offering
an important perspective on the complex effects of this phenomenon (Mukherjee &
Althuizen, 2020). Previous studies found that brand activism is likely to affect and
activate those who oppose the cause the brand promotes rather than those who agree
with it (Mukherjee & Althuizen, 2020; Pöyry & Laaksonen, 2022). Table 1 presents

More importantly, undertaking a systematic literature review of brand activism in

Pimentel et al., 2024; Cammarota et al., 2023). (Luna-Amador et al., 2025) focused
on authors’ co-citations and keyword co-occurrences but did not provide a holistic
picture of the research field. The authors have also taken the time period 1989–2023.
(Pimentel et al., 2024) focused on the brand activism concept from a firm’s perspec-
tive, while (Cammarota et al., 2023) concentrated on understanding the factors influ-
encing stakeholder responses to brand activism (Table 1).
In this light, the use of bibliometric analysis—a quantitative evaluation and map -
ping of the productivity, impact, and content of research in a specific field- emerges
as an appropriate approach (Kumar et al. 2023, 2024, 2025; Basu et al. 2023a, b; Lim
et al. 2022; Kumar and Ahmed 2023; Kumar 2016; Jibril & Kumar 2026; Yadav et
al. 2026). This methodology helps pinpoint publication and citation trends, major
contributors, and profound themes, thereby informing both academic and practical
use in the field. This study aims to present the progress of research on brand activism.
Our research objectives are the following:
RO1: To explore the publication and citation trends of brand activism.
RO2: To identify the major contributors to brand activism.
RO3: To highlight the key themes and propose future avenues for brand activism.
To fulfil these objectives, we first conduct a performance analysis to assess the
publication and citation trends, including those of the major contributors in this
research area. We then employ science mapping to visualize the intellectual structure
of this research domain. In the process, we unravel the prevalent themes and suggest
potential directions for future research in this field. The findings of this review serve
multiple stakeholders, including emerging and established scholars, practitioners,
and policymakers, by providing key information on publication and citation trends,
major contributors, and prominent themes in this field of research. Additionally, our
study identifies crucial knowledge gaps and future research opportunities, thereby
guiding practitioners and policymakers in this field.
The remainder of this article is organized as follows: Sect.  2 outlines the method-
ology, Sect.  3 presents the performance analysis, Sect.  4 delivers the science map-
ping based on keyword co-occurrence and bibliographic coupling to unravel trends
and themes, and Sect.  5 concludes the article with key takeaways, implications, and
suggestions for future research.

conference reviews, retracted articles, notes, brief surveys, and book chapters. This
resulted in 342 publications. Subsequently, this number was decreased by applying
a inclusion criteria based on language (“English”), document type (“article”), source
type (“journal”), and subject area (“business, management and accounting”) in line
with the recommendations of past review guides (Kraus et al., 2022; Paul et al.,
2021). The search, conducted on 13 December 2025, yielded a final sample of 259
articles for this study.
2.2 Corpus analysis
The corpus of Brand activism research is analysed using bibliometric analysis. Biblio-
metric analysis entails two significant components: performance analysis and science
mapping (Donthu et al., 2021; Mukherjee et al., 2022). We examine the publication
and citation trends, as well as the major contributors, of Brand activism research for
performance analysis. This includes identifying the most influential articles and the
most prolific and impactful journals, authors, countries, and institutions. Two widely
used methods in scientific mapping are bibliographic coupling and keyword co-
occurrence analysis (Donthu et al., 2021; Kumar et al., 2022). For science mapping,
we explore the major themes using bibliographic coupling, a clustering method that
groups citing articles based on citation similarity, specifically in the context of brand
activism research. On the other hand, keyword co-occurrence analysis discerns the
substance of articles by using authors’ keywords (Comerio & Strozzi, 2019). These
analyses were conducted using VOSviewer software (Van Eck & Waltman, 2017).
3 Findings
The primary purpose of this analysis is to identify trends and interests in BA research,
as well as research objectives, which are explicitly outlined and explained by high -
lighting the key findings. This review presents the publication trend (RO1) and the
top journals, articles, authors, countries, and institutions (RO2) in BA research,
which scholars and practitioners can rely on to understand the field’s performance
and sources of knowledge. The findings also presented the key themes generated
from the corpus of the papers(RO3).

3.1.1 Productivity (publication) trend of brand activism research
The year-wise productivity (publication) trend of brand activism research is depicted
in Fig. 2. The figure indicates that brand activism research has a history of around
four decades, dating back to 1984. However, there was no publication activity in
some years (1985, 1986, 1987, 1992–2002, 2004–2006, 2008, 2010, 2012–2013, and
2017). Though it is fascinating to witness the progression of brand activism research
across the years. Figure 2 clearly depicts this change, categorising the publishing
trends into two separate stages. Prior to 2021, in the first phase, the quantity of pub -
lications was quite limited, usually below 10 per year. These observations could be
attributed to various factors. First, the term brand activism was formally coined in
2018 and took time (1984–2020) to gain acceptance among business management
scholars. However, a notable change occurred in the second phase, with publications
frequently reaching double-digit numbers. The second phase accounted for 88.80%
of the total publications. The increase in publications during the latter period may
be due to several factors, including the publication of special issues by well-known
journals focused on brand activism research (e.g., the Journal of Public Policy &
Marketing, 2020). This is also well justified by the highest-cited paper, “Brands Tak-
ing a Stand: Authentic Brand Activism or Woke Washing?”  This qualitative-based

Cluster 1: Corporate political-advocacy and institutional change  The theme of this
cluster examines how business leadership influences society, advocates for issues
such as marriage equality, and promotes environmental sustainability goals. The
publications in this cluster explore the reasons driving CEO activism and corporate
engagement in social and political issues, as well as their impact on stakeholders and
company performance. The most cited publications in this cluster demonstrate how
CEOs manage activism while considering the perspectives of shareholders, workers,
consumers, and the broader community (Table 7). Notably, (Wettstein & Baur 2016)
provide an analysis of corporate involvement in political advocacy, using marriage
equality as a case study. This may draw attention to the underlying motivations driv-
ing these advocacy endeavours, as well as the potential consequences for corporate
image and societal influence. The other studies in this cluster examine how corpora-
tions manage institutional norms and constraints while engaging in corporate activ -
ism, considering the broader implications for corporate responsibility and the shift
towards sustainable practices (Eilert & Nappier Cherup, 2020; Delmas et al., 2019).
Cluster 2: Consumer ethical responses and brand-directed activism The theme of this
cluster encapsulates the essence of the research papers, focusing on the intersection
of brand ethics, consumer activism, and social consciousness across contexts such
as marketing strategies, corporate branding, and celebrity endorsements. Thus, this
theme sheds light on the complexities of brand-consumer dynamics, highlighting the
importance of ethical integrity, authenticity, and social responsibility in brand man -
agement and consumer engagement. This can also be seen in the most-cited publica-
tions in this cluster, which showcase the utility of brand activism for understanding
how brands experience adverse reactions due to perceived ethical failures (Table 7).
Noteworthily, Romani et al.(2015) examine instances where brands participate in
unethical behaviour or do not conform to customer ideals, resulting in adverse reac -
tions and an attitude against the brand, whereas (Sobande 2019) investigates the
occurrence known as “woke-washing,” which occurs when brands exploit social jus-
tice concerns for promotional objectives without demonstrating an authentic dedica-
tion to effecting social transformation. (Palazzo & Basu 2007) explore the ethical
issues regarding business branding activities. The study examines how customers
assess a brand’s genuineness, honesty, and commitment to social well-being, and
the resulting impact on the brand’s image and customer loyalty. (Popp et al., 2016)
examine professional football-related social media anti-brand networks. The study
presents how fans criticize football companies, teams, and players online and how
such anti-brand activism affects brand perception and fan engagement. Finally, (Pre-
ece 2015) analyses the phenomenon of celebrity branding by focusing on the case of
Ai Weiwei. The study presents how Ai Weiwei strategically develops his personal
brand by utilizing his creative pursuits, activism, and public image to connect with
audiences and promote social concerns.

5 Conclusion
The purpose of this study is to conduct a systematic literature review of the evolution
of brand activism as a research field, employing bibliometric and science-mapping
techniques. According to our results, brand activism has evolved from an emerging
and fragmented field to a mature multidisciplinary field of research which is rapidly
expanding. There is marked increase in publications, especially from 2020 onwards
due to the increasing social polarization and social pressure on companies to deal
with socio-political issues.
With more companies getting involved in social issues, this research confirms that
there are various ties between brand activism and several fields of public and non-
profit marketing such as social value creation, advocacy communication and stake -
holder engagement. Prior studies also demonstrated that social issue awareness is
increasingly raised by organisations with the aim of influencing public discourse and
realising the public good (Ferreira & Alves, 2026; Levit & Cismaru, 2020). This sug-
gests that brand activism should not be viewed solely as a market strategy but also
as a form of quasi-public engagement by private actors.The following sections will
begin by thoroughly examining the key outputs, which offer an invaluable under -
standing of the field’s development. Following this, attention will be directed towards
the intrinsic constraints of the current investigation and possible avenues for future
research. This discourse lays the groundwork for the final statements, highlighting
the substantial advancements this research has made in the field of brand activism.
5.1 Key takeaways and implications
Several key takeaways emerge from using multiple bibliometric analysis methods.
Examining the performance of brand activism research reveals a rise in publications
since 2015, characterised by varying growth rates. However, there has been a nota -
ble acceleration in publication numbers from the 2020s onwards, driven mainly by
greater acceptance among business management scholars and by theoretical advance-
ments. The field has expanded geographically through academic research originating
in the United States, the United Kingdom, and Australia. Research conducted in these
countries focuses on brand activism across political and social dimensions, includ -
ing racial justice, gender inclusion, political ideology, CEO activism, and corporate
advocacy. Research conducted in European and Asian settings shows different priori-
ties, as these studies focus on ethical legitimacy, stakeholder trust, and environmental
activism, demonstrating how different regions use distinct methods to study brand
activism.
The field of brand activism research has advanced, with research findings now
published in leading academic journals. The Journal of Public Policy & Marketing,
Journal of Business Research, and Journal of Business Ethics are among the leading
scholarly journals that drive research on authenticity, stakeholder alignment, moral
legitimacy, and the market effects of corporate social and political activities.
The paper by (Vredenburg et al., 2020) is the most cited work on brands tak -
ing a stand, specifically on authentic brand activism, with 537 citations. The Jour -
nal of Public Policy and Marketing is the most impactful, with 759 citations. The

5.1.1 Contributions of the study
Theoretical contributions The research makes multiple essential theoretical contri -
butions by consolidating scattered knowledge, deepening conceptual understanding,
and integrating current academic findings in the field of brand activism.
First, this research expands brand activism theory by identifying its multidimen -
sionality and differentiating between social, political, ethical, and environmental types
of brand activism. The study identifies six thematic clusters that show how authentic-
ity, moral legitimacy, and stakeholder alignment operate differently across settings.
This thematic arrangement extends previous conceptualizations that regarded brand
activism mainly as an extension of corporate social responsibility or cause-related
marketing. This facilitates the advancement of literature from definitional debates to
mechanism-based explanations of brand activism outcomes.
Second, by mapping intellectual connections across clusters, this study underpins
the centrality of authenticity, moral legitimacy, and stakeholder alignment in relat -
ing corporate activities to consumer and societal responses. This aligns with calls
for theoretical consolidation in emerging marketing domains, highlighting the role
of synthesis in theory-building (Mukherjee et al., 2022; Lim et al., 2022; Kumar &
Jibril, 2025).

approach to synthesizing fragmented literatures in emerging research domains.

perceived marginalization of consumers affect their likelihood of taking visible
vs. silent action?

function of trust in human-to-human (H2H) marketing. It emphasizes the signifi -
cance of genuine brand activism, which promotes social change and enhances brand

5.3 Limitations
Despite the aforementioned contributions from science mapping and performance
analysis in brand activism research, this study remains constrained in several ways.
Firstly, this study is confined to bibliometric observations of brand activism research.
Although our review was appropriately justified by the standards for conducting a
bibliometric review, it is vital to acknowledge that alternative review methods may
reveal more nuanced details. One potential approach is to conduct distinct supplemen-
tary reviews, such as content analysis, that delve more deeply into the BA research
streams or main thematic clusters. These reviews would offer a more detailed explo-
ration to expand the field’s overall understanding. As an alternative, future review
studies on the subject could conduct a content analysis of the theories in combination
with ST research or the methods employed in brand activism research. These recom-
mendations align with those of (Lim et al., 2022), who noted that conducting mul -
tiple review studies in the field is feasible as long as they provide fresh perspectives
on the current state of development and future directions of the field. Second, this
research does not make new theoretical or empirical contributions to brand activism,
but also advances existing knowledge. As a result, potential scholars should engage
in conceptual and empirical research on brand activism, drawing on the themes and
trends identified in this study. Third, this analysis only includes research published in
