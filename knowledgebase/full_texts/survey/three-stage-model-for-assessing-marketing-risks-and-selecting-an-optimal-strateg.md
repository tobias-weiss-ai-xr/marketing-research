Three-Stage Model for Assessing Marketing Risks and Selecting an Optimal Strategy
The aim of the article is to provide a theoretical and methodological substantiation, together
with a practical elaboration, of a comprehensive three-stage model for  assessing marketing risks and
selecting an optimal strategy for an enterprise operating under conditions of high volatility,
informational asymmetry and stochastic uncertainty of the marketing envi ronment. Traditional
deterministic and purely intuitive approaches to strategic marketing planning have proven insufficiently
reliable under such conditions, which creates the need for a hybrid risk-ma nagement methodology
capable of formalizing uncertainty, evaluating alternative strategies quantitatively and verifying their
stability before implementation. The article seeks to integrate qualita tive identification of marketing
uncertainty with quantitative decision-making tools drawn from game theory and simulation modelling,
thereby bridging the gap between conceptual risk classification and applied stra tegic decision support.
Analysis results. The study systematizes the principal types of marketing uncertainty according
to the elements of the extended marketing mix (4P/7P — product, price, place, promotion, people,
process, physical evidence), which allows the sources of marketing risk  to be traced to specific
managerial decision areas rather than treated as an undifferentiate d aggregate. The key factors
generating marketing risk are identified, including demand volatility , competitive reactions, pricing

qualitatively and quantitatively across the marketing-mix elements , producing a payoff matrix that
reflects possible states of the environment ("states of Nature") and the corresponding strategic
alternatives. A t the second stage, the model applies game-theoretic optimiz ation: decisions are
evaluated as games against "Nature" using the classical criteria of W ald (maximin, extreme pessimism),
Savage (minimax regret), Laplace (principle of insufficient reason) and Hurwicz (weighted optimism–
pessimism index), while competitive interactions are additionally modelled as antagonistic (zero-sum)
games with rival firms, capturing the strategic interdependence absent from s ingle-agent decision
criteria. Combining these criteria enables decision-makers to compare strategies under different risk
attitudes and information conditions rather than relying on a single, potentiall y biased criterion. At the
third stage, the strategies selected through the game-theoretic procedure undergo stochastic verification
by means of the Monte Carlo method, which simulates a large number of possible market scenarios and
allows the probability of critical or catastrophic losses associated wi th each strategy to be estimated.
This final stage transforms the deterministic outputs of the game-theoreti c analysis into probabilistic
risk profiles, giving managers a measure of downside risk exposure rather than  a single point estimate
of expected payoff. The integration of the three stages produces a coherent decision-support algorithm
that moves from structured uncertainty identification, through multi-cr iteria strategic choice, to
probabilistic resilience testing.
Conclusions and directions for further research . The proposed three-stage model
demonstrates that marketing-risk management can be substantially strengthened by combining
classification of uncertainty across the marketing mix, multi-criteria game-theoretic decision rules and
Monte Carlo–based stochastic verification into a single, internally consistent procedure. This approach
reduces reliance on subjective, intuitive judgment, increases the transp arency and reproducibility of
strategic marketing decisions, and provides enterprises with a quantitati ve basis for assessing the
resilience of chosen strategies to adverse market developments. The model is particularly relevant for
enterprises operating in volatile, competitively intense or informat ionally asymmetric markets, where
the cost of an unverified strategic error can be substantial. Directions f or further research include the
empirical testing and calibration of the model using longitudinal data from enter prises in different
industries and market conditions; the development of dedicated software t ools to automate the game-
theoretic evaluation and Monte Carlo simulation stages, thereby making the  methodology accessible
for routine managerial use; the extension of the antagonistic-game component to dynamic, repeated and
multi-agent settings that capture sequential and adaptive competitor behavi or; and the incorporation
of Bayesian updating mechanisms that would allow the model's probability e stimates to be refined as
new market information becomes available, further enhancing its practical applicability in real-time
strategic marketing management.
