ABSTRACT
Kelud pineapple is a leading horticultural commodity cultivated on the slopes of Mount Kelud in Kediri
City. However, its penetration into urban markets remains limited. This study aims to identify consumer
characteristics and analyze the factors influencing purchase intention toward Kelud pineapple in Kediri
City. A quantitative approach with a cross-sectional survey design was employed with 200 respondents
selected using purposive sampling. Data were analyzed using Structural Equation Modeling –Partial
Least Squares (SEM-PLS) based on an Extended Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) model. The results
show that attitude toward behavior, subjective norm, perceived behavioral control, concern for local
products, and marketing promotion significantly influence pur chase intention toward Kelud pineapple,
with an R² value of 0.561. Attitude toward behavior is the most dominant factor (  = 0.287), followed by
marketing promotion (  = 0.241). These findings provide strategic implications for developing the
marketing of leading local agricultural commodities.

Keywords: Kelud pineapple; purchase intention; Theory of Planned Behavior

INTRODUCTION
Indonesia’s horticultural sector, particularly local fruit commodities, has substantial economic
potential that has not yet been fully optimized. One of the leading commodities with distinctive
geographical characteristics is Kelud pineapple, a pineapple cultivated on the slopes of Mount Kelud in
Kediri City, East Java. Kelud pineapple is known for its sweet taste, crisp texture, and relatively high
water content compared with pineapple varieties from other regions. Nevertheless, the market
penetration of Kelud pineapple in urban areas such as Kediri City continues to face various challenges,
particularly those associated with increasingly complex consumer  behavior and preferences. A
comprehensive understanding of the factors driving consumers’ purchase intention toward this local
product is therefore crucial for developing the horticultural agribusiness value chain in the region (Putri
et al., 2024).
Consumer behavior in purchasing fresh fruit is influenced by various dimensions, ranging from
product attributes such as taste, freshness, and physical appearance to external factors such as price,
availability, and promotion. In the context of local produ cts, geographical identity and consumers’
emotional proximity to a product can also shape a distinctive perception of value. Previous studies have
shown that consumers in urban areas tend to consider visual quality, health benefits, and price
affordability as major determinants of fresh -fruit purchasing decisions (Mahmud et al., 2024) . At the
same time, increasing public awareness of local -product consumption as a form of support for farmers
and national food security also influences the purchasing orientation of contemporary urban consumers

The Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB), developed by Ajzen, has been empirically demonstrated to
explain consumer behavioral intentions across various food -purchasing contexts. TPB posits that
behavioral intention is shaped by three main constructs: attitude toward behavior, subjective norm, and
perceived behavioral control. Various studies have extended the TPB framework by incorporating
relevant contextual variables, such as environmental concern, perceived quality, price promotion, and
local sociocultural factors, to improve the model’s predictive power for purchase intention toward specific
products (Ayu et al., 2024). An extended TPB approach is therefore considered appropriate for examining
the complexity of consumer behavior toward geographically distinctive local fruit products such as Kelud
pineapple.
Previous studies on fruit -consumption behavior in Indonesia have predominantly focused on
imported products, organic products, or general horticultural commodities, whereas specific studies on
purchase intention toward local fruits with particular geograph ical advantages remain limited. This
research gap provides an important foundation for the present study. The absence of studies specifically
examining the determinants of consumer purchase intention toward Kelud pineapple in Kediri City, both
from the TPB  perspective and in relation to local marketing factors, creates a significant research
opportunity. The novelty of this study lies in integrating local -product promotion and perceived
geographical advantage as additional constructs within the TPB model to  explain purchase intention
toward Kelud pineapple, an approach that has not previously been applied in the context of horticultural
agribusiness in Kediri City (Cao & Miao, 2021; Utami et al., 2023).
Based on the background and research gap described above, the research questions are: (1) What
are the characteristics of Kelud pineapple consumers in Kediri City? (2) What factors significantly
influence consumers’ purchase intention toward Kelud pineappl e in Kediri City? (3) To what extent do
attitude, subjective norm, perceived behavioral control, concern for local products, and promotion
influence purchase intention toward Kelud pineapple in Kediri City? Accordingly, this study aims to: (1)
identify the  characteristics of Kelud pineapple consumers in Kediri City; (2) analyze the factors
influencing consumers’ purchase intention toward Kelud pineapple in Kediri City; and (3) measure the
magnitude of the influence of each variable in the extended TPB model  on purchase intention toward
Kelud pineapple in Kediri City.
This study is expected to provide theoretical benefits by enriching the literature on consumer
behavior toward local horticultural products in Indonesia through an extended TPB approach. Practically,
the findings can serve as a strategic reference for farm ers, business actors, and market managers in
designing more effective marketing programs to increase the market penetration and sales of Kelud
pineapple in urban markets, while also contributing to the strengthening of leading local agribusiness
commodities.

MATERIALS AND METHODS
This study employed a quantitative approach with a cross -sectional survey design to analyze the
factors influencing consumers’ purchase intention toward Kelud pineapple in Kediri City. The
quantitative approach was selected because it enables relationships  among variables to be measured
systematically and objectively and allows findings to be generalized to a broader population (Sugiyono,
2022). The study was conducted in Kediri City, East Java, because the city is the nearest urban area to
the Kelud pineapple production center on the slopes of Mount Kelud. Consumers in this area therefore
have relatively high accessibility to and familiarity with the commodity. Data was collected from March
through April 2025 via online and offline questionnaires using Google Forms, based on the criteria of
respondents who had previously purchased Kelud pineapples.
The study population consisted of adult consumers aged at least 17 years who resided in Kediri
City, had purchased or consumed Kelud pineapple, and were involved in household food -purchasing
decisions. Sampling was conducted using purposive sampling, invol ving 200 respondents, based on
sample adequacy considerations for Structural Equation Modeling –Partial Least Squares (SEM -PLS)
analysis. The research instrument was a structured questionnaire developed based on the extended
Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) framework. The exogenous varia bles comprised attitude toward
behavior, subjective norm, perceived behavioral control, concern for local products, and marketing
promotion. The endogenous variable was purchase intention toward Kelud pineapple. All statement items
were measured using a five-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree).
Instrument validity and reliability were evaluated through the outer model by examining factor

Characteristics of Kelud Pineapple Consumers in Kediri City

Descriptive analysis of the 200 respondents revealed a relatively homogeneous yet informative
demographic profile. The majority of respondents were female (81%), with the dominant age group being
45–60 years (45%); 87% were married, and 49% worked as house wives. These findings indicate that
women, particularly housewives, occupy a central position in household food -purchasing decisions,
including decisions regarding fresh fruit commodities such as Kelud pineapple. The dominant role of
women in food -purchasing decisions is consistent with domestic consumption patterns commonly
observed in urban Javanese communities, where women act as primary decision -makers in managing
household food needs (Sirait et al., 2025).
In terms of income, 60% of respondents earned less than IDR 4,000,000 per month. This condition
reflects a lower-middle-income consumer segment that is relatively sensitive to price and product-value
considerations. Nevertheless, the respondents’ high inte rest in Kelud pineapple indicates that
geographical attachment and pride in local products can transcend purchasing -power constraints. This
finding is consistent with Sunaryanti et al. (2025), who emphasized that consumer preferences for local
agricultural products are not determined solely by economic factors but are also influenced by the
functional value and novelty value embedded in the products.

Factors Influencing Consumers Purchase Intention towards Kelud Pineapple

Based on the results of the SEM-PLS inner-model evaluation, all five research hypotheses (H1–H5)
were confirmed as significant at p < 0.01, with an R² value of 0.561. This means that the extended Theory
of Planned Behavior (TPB) model explains 56.1% of the  variation in purchase intention toward Kelud
pineapple in Kediri City. First, attitude toward behavior was found to be the most dominant factor, with
a path coefficient of β = 0.287. Consumers with positive evaluations of the quality, taste, and benefits of
Kelud pineapple tend to have higher purchase intentions. This positive attitude may be formed through
previous consumption experiences, information received by consumers, and perceptions of the advantages
of local products compared with similar imported fruits. This finding is consistent with Sirait et al. (2025),
who examined suboptimal fruit in Bogor City and found that attitude toward behavior was the factor
most strongly associated with consumers’ purchase intention toward fruit products, indicating that the
more positive an individual’s attitude toward a product, the greater the likelihood of purchase intention.
Second, marketing promotion had the second-largest effect, with β = 0.241. Effective promotional
exposure, whether through price discounts or product-information campaigns, was found to significantly
encourage consumers’ purchase intention. In the digital era, promotional activities are no longer limited
to conventional media. Prasetyo & Ahmadi (2024) demonstrated that social media marketing significantly
affects consumers’ purchase intention, with brand trust acting as a mediating variable that strengthens
this relationship. Therefore, Kelud pineapple promotion strategies should integrate digital chann els to
reach a broader consumer segment in Kediri City.
Third, concern for local products had a significant effect, with β = 0.198. Consumers with a high
level of awareness of the importance of supporting local agricultural products tend to be more motivated
to purchase Kelud pineapple as a form of contribution to farmer empowerment and regional food security.
This finding reinforces the argument of Sunaryanti et al. (2025) that functional and social consumption
values significantly influence consumers’ purchasing orientation toward Indonesian local agricultural
products.
Fourth, perceived behavioral control had a significant effect, with β = 0.176. The ease of obtaining
Kelud pineapple in traditional and modern markets in Kediri City, combined with its relatively affordable
price, makes consumers feel that they have substa ntial control over their purchasing decisions. Good
product accessibility directly strengthens perceived behavioral control and ultimately increases purchase
intention Sirait et al. (2025). Fifth, subjective norm had a significant effect despite having the smallest
coefficient (β = 0.134). Recommendations and experiences shared by close social contacts, such as family
members, neighbors, or social groups, remain relevant in shaping purchase  intention toward Kelud

Magnitudde of the Effects of Variables on Purchase Intention towards Kelud Pineapple

Overall, the variables’ effects on purchase intention toward Kelud pineapple, from the strongest to
the weakest, are as follows: attitude toward behavior (β = 0.287) > marketing promotion (β = 0.241) >
concern for local products (β = 0.198) > perceived beh avioral control (β = 0.176) > subjective norm (β =
0.134). This pattern confirms that consumers’ internal cognitive -affective factor, namely attitude,
remains the primary determinant of purchase intention toward local food products. This finding is
consistent with various studies of food consumer behavior using the TPB framework, in which attitude
consistently outperforms other variables in predicting behavioral intention (Sirait et al., 2025; Sunaryanti
et al., 2025).
On the other hand, the magnitude of the effect of marketing promotion (β = 0.241) confirms that
well-planned marketing interventions can meaningfully influence consumer behavior. Purba et al. (2023)
support this finding by demonstrating that consumer engagement facilitated through social media
marketing strategies significantly increases purchase intention. Therefore, strengthening digital -based
promotional strategies and local -product education shoul d be a priority for business actors and
agribusiness stakeholders involved in Kelud pineapple in Kediri City.

CONCLUSION
This study identified that consumers’ purchase intention toward Kelud pineapple in Kediri City is
significantly influenced by five variables in the extended Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) model:
attitude toward behavior, subjective norm, perceived behavioral control, concern for local products, and
marketing promotion. All hypotheses were confirmed, with an R² value of 0.561, indicating adequate
predictive power of the model. Attitude toward behavior was the strongest determinant (β = 0.287),
followed by marketing promotion (β = 0.241) and concern for local products (β = 0.198). These findings
provide a strategic basis for farmers, business actors, and the Kediri local government to design more
targeted marketing programs for Kelud pineapple. Positive consumer attitudes can be fostered through
educational initiatives emphasizing product quality and nutritional value. Digital promotion using social
media platforms should be intensified to reach broader consumer segments. In addition, strengthening
the geographical identity of Kelud pineapple as a leading local product should be supported through
official certification and labeling. Future research should expand the geographical scope, incorporate
mediating variables such as product trust and perceived quality, and explore demographic segmentation
in greater depth to generate more precise and sustainable marketing strategies.
