1. Introduction

The development of digital technology has fundamentally revolutionized the global marketing landscape . As a strategic
adaptation to the era of digital transformation, digital marketing has become a crucial element in building relationships
between companies and consumers. With increasing internet penetration, the growth of social media, and the use of mob ile
devices, consumer behavior has shifted significantly from passive to active online search, comparison, and purchasing decisions
(Kannan & Li, 2017). This change is forcing companies to abandon traditional one-way communication approaches and shift to
more interactive, data-driven, and automated strategies through digital channels (Brodie et al., 2013).
The urgency of strengthening digital marketing strategies has become increasingly apparent following the COVID -19
pandemic, which has accelerated technol ogy adoption across nearly all sectors, including business and marketing ( Dwivedi et
al., 2020). Consumers now demand speed, convenience, and personalized experiences in every interaction with brands.
Therefore, companies need to understand the latest tren ds and strategic approaches proven effective in maximizing the
potential of digital marketing as a means of maintaining customer loyalty and increasing competitiveness. In this context, th e
scientific literature plays a crucial role as a source of knowledge and a foundation for evidence-based decision-making.
Although much research has been conducted in the field of digital marketing, the rapidly changing dynamics of
technology and market behavior require regular knowledge updates. While some previous studies focused primarily on tactical
aspects such as social media or email marketing, the development of technologies such as artificial intelligence, big data, a nd
omnichannel retailing has led to the emergence of new complexities that require deeper study ( Chaffey & Chadwick, 2019 ).
Therefore, it is crucial to remap the latest trends, findings, and methodological approaches used in cutting -edge research.
This study aims to systematically review scientific articles on digital marketing published in highly repu table journals
between 2024 and 2025. Using a systematic literature review (SLR) approach, this research seeks to identify dominant themes
in digital

Abstract The rapid development of digital technology has driven a transformation in global marketing practices, creating a
need for a deeper understanding of research directions and approaches in digital marketing. This study aims to examine
methodological trends a nd patterns in the scientific literature related to digital marketing published between 2024 and
2025. Using a systematic literature review approach based on the PRISMA protocol, 46 articles from the Scopus database
were analyzed in depth. The articles were selected from Q1 and Q2 journals in the Business, Management, and Accounting
category, with a focus on open access publications in English. The findings indicate that the dominant research topics
include the adoption of digital marketing by small and med ium enterprises, the use of advanced technologies such as
artificial intelligence, big data analytics, and metaverse, and the influence of digital marketing on business performance
and consumer behavior. In terms of methodology, quantitative approaches sti ll dominate, particularly with the use of
structural equation modeling, followed by qualitative and mixed methods. Furthermore, bibliometric analysis and machine
learning-based computational techniques have been widely adopted in recent studies. This study  not only presents
thematic and methodological mapping but also identifies literature gaps and future research opportunities. The results
emphasize the importance of ethical considerations in the use of artificial intelligence and big data, as well as the need for
further research on the integration of omnichannel strategies in the digital era. These findings are expected to provide
conceptual contributions to the development of digital marketing theory and serve as a practical reference for decision -
makers and industry players in developing adaptive and sustainable technology -based marketing strategies.

2. Methodology

This literature review uses the PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses) approach
as the primary guideline for systematically selecting and synthesizing scientific articles related to digital marketing (Paul  &
Criado, 2020). The PRISMA provides a robust framework to ensure that each stage —from identification to final selection —is
conducted in a transparent, structured, and replicable manner. This approach is crucial for ensuring that the review results
truly reflect relevant, high-quality, and scientifically sound literature (Snyder, 2019).
The primary data source for this study comes from the Scopus database, which is selected for its extensive, curated
scientific database and its coverage of highly reputable journals. The sear ch focused on articles published between 2024 and
2025, considering that this period reflects the most recent developments in the field of digital marketing. This limited timeframe
was designed to capture the rapidly changing dynamics of digital technology  and marketing strategies, particularly in the
context of digital transformation and increasingly digitized consumer behavior.
The search strategy was developed by combining relevant and specific primary keywords. The primary keyword used
was "digital mark eting," while also considering equivalents and synonyms such as "online marketing," "internet marketing,"
and "social media marketing" to broaden the scope of the search results. The search process was further limited to the
document type "article" and the  subject area "Business, Management, and Accounting" to ensure that the articles retrieved
were relevant to the focus of the study. To strengthen the validity and accessibility of the analysis, only articles written in
English and available as open access were included. Furthermore, only articles from journals ranked in Scopus Q1 and Q2 were
considered in the final selection stage.
The selection process was conducted in a step-by-step and systematic manner, following the PRISMA structure (Tranfield
et al., 2003). The initial search resulted in 1,955 articles. After applying the exclusion criteria to 1,016 articles from conference
proceedings, books, articles in press, and other nonjournal publications, 939 articles were selected for the next screening stage.
A selection based on the scientific subject "Business, Management, and Accounting" narrowed the results to 468 articles. This
was then further narrowed down on the basis of the document type "article" and the keyword "digital marketing," leaving 199
articles. These articles were then filtered on the basis of their English availability and open  access, resulting in 79 articles. A
final selection on the basis of Q1 and Q2 journal quality yielded 46 scientific articles, which served as the basis for analy sis in
this study.
The analytical techniques used in this study included content analysis and bibliometric mapping. Content analysis was
used to examine the methodological approaches employed in the selected studies. Bibliometric mapping was conducted via
software such as VOSviewer to map keyword trends that play a central role in the development of knowledge related to digital
marketing.
Through rigorous identification procedures, systematic selection, and comprehensive multidimensional analysis, this
study is expected to make a significant scientific contribution to understanding the direction, focus, and dominant
methodological approaches in current digital marketing research as illustrated in the PRISMA flow diagram (Figure 1).

Quantitative methods predominate, often using surveys to collect data from respondents such as business owners, marketing
professionals, consumers, or students. Structural equation modeling (SEM) and its variations, such as PLS-SEM and CB-SEM, are

Qualitative methods, including case  studies and in -depth interviews, provide contextual insights and in -depth
exploration of digital marketing phenomena. Mixed methods (combining quantitative and qualitative methods) are increasingly
common for triangulation and a more comprehensive understanding.
In addition to traditional research methods, this literature makes significant use of computational data analysis
techniques. These include natural language processing (NLP) and text mining to analyze content from social media or annual
reports; s entiment analysis to gauge public opinion; and machine learning models such as random forest models (RFMs),
generative adversarial networks (GANs), visual transformers (ViTs), and graph neural networks (GNNs) for big data analysis,
prediction, and visual p rocessing. Multiple correspondence analysis (MCA) is also used to visually represent relationships
between variables. Overall, the literature on digital marketing is a dynamic field characterized by rapid growth, broad
geographic coverage, and the use of diverse methodologies to address complex research questions.
On the basis of existing sources, several key themes and theoretical dimensions emerge as the core of digital marketing
research. Much research has focused on the factors influencing SMEs' and non profits' adoption of digital marketing, with
studies exploring the challenges and strategies for implementing digital marketing practices effectively (Checchinato et al.,
2024; Tiago & Veríssimo, 2014). Closely related to adoption is the theme of digital m arketing capabilities, encompassing the
skills, knowledge, infrastructure, and competencies that organizations need to succeed in digital marketing. Digital
infrastructure, a skilled workforce, and digital marketing capabilities are identified as key eleme nts (Gensler & Rangaswamy,
2025).
The relationship between digital marketing and various measures of business performance represents a central theme
in the literature. This includes its impact on sales, firm performance (often measured by TobinQ), and tourism marketing
performance, as well as competitive advantage and overall business performance. Research highlights the critical role of digital
marketing in the survival of SMEs (Setegn & Japee, 2025). Complementing this performance focus, studies explo re how digital
marketing influences consumer behavior, including purchase intentions, decision -making processes, customer engagement,
trust, satisfaction, and loyalty. The importance of understanding consumer behavior in the digital environment is emphasiz ed
(Hollebeek & Macky, 2019).
Research also examines various digital marketing channels and tools, such as social media (Facebook, Instagram, TikTok,
etc.), websites, search engines, mobile apps, eWOM (electronic word -of-mouth), and different digital marke ting mixes (e.g.,
the 8 Ps) (Dwivedi et al., 2021). A significant trend is the integration and impact of advanced technologies in digital marke ting,
particularly artificial intelligence (AI) in branding (AI-branding), AI for visual analysis and content creation, AI for prediction (e.g.,
random forest models), and ethical concerns related to bias and privacy in AI -based digital marketing.  Generative AI (such as
large language models and image generation) is a rapidly growing subarea (Davenport et al., 2020) .
Big Data (BD) and Big Data Analytics (BDA) are used to understand consumers, improve decision -making, and support
sustainable digital marketing (Wedel & Kannan, 2016). Additionally, augmented reality (AR), virtual reality (VR), and the
metaverse are exp lored to create immersive customer experiences and competitive advantage, with avatar marketing
emerging as a related concept. Although still in its infancy, the potential of quantum computing (QC) for market research in
digital ecosystems is beginning to be explored (Sáez-Ortuño et al., 2024).
Research is often applied to specific contexts such as SMEs, tourism (tourism SMEs, historical sites, etc.), education
(universities, music courses), banking, and creative industries (e.g., batik) (Setegn & Japee, 20 25). Various fundamental and
specifically developed theories are used, which are often adapted for the digital environment. These include the resource -
based view (RBV), social identity theory (SIT), the technology acceptance model (TAM) and its variations (UTAUT, UTAUT2),
eTailQ, experiential marketing, theory of perceived value, and newer frameworks such as SPICE (for the Metaverse), T -EESST
(for sustainable digital marketing), and specific models for analyzing AI bias or AI branding (CBBE x Data/Technolog y) (Verhoef
et al., 2015; Kannan & Li, 2017).
The literature analysis reveals clear trends in digital marketing research theory, methodology, and findings. In terms of
theoretical development, there has been a shift from the application of traditional stan d-alone theories to the integration and
adaptation of theories to explain complex digital phenomena. Theories such as TAM, UTAUT, and RBV are routinely used as the
foundation for digital marketing studies. At the same time, new theoretical frameworks and m odels are being developed to
explain emerging digital technologies and practices (e.g., AI, the Metaverse, sustainable marketing in big data). There is an
increasing focus on understanding the role of mediating and moderating variables in uncovering indirect or context-dependent
causal relationships in digital marketing (Laila et al., 2024).
Regarding methodological approaches, the dominance of quantitative methods with the increasing use of multivariate
analysis techniques such as SEM/PLS -SEM is a major tr end. However, there is growing recognition of the value of qualitative
and mixed methods to provide richer insights and contextual understanding. More rigorous and structured methodologies,
including detailed coding procedures in qualitative content analys is and rigorous reliability/validity testing in quantitative
research, are becoming standard. The use of computational analytics tools to process and analyze large, unstructured digital

In terms of research outcomes, studies consistently find positive evidence of the impact of digital marketing (and its

While providing a single conceptual map that encompasses all the nuances of diverse sources is difficult, the conceptual
landscape of digital marketing research, as seen from these sources, can be described through several interconnected
dimensions. At the heart of this landscape is digital marketing, which is understood as a set of digital strate gies, capabilities,
channels, and content used by organizations (SMEs, large enterprises, NPOs, etc.) to interact with and influence
consumers/users. Advanced technologies, such as AI, big data, AR/VR, metaverse, and quantum computing, are increasingly
becoming transformative forces that influence how digital marketing is practiced and how effective it is.
The key relationships explored in the literature reveal multiple layers of influence and interaction. Digital marketing
directly influences consumer behavior (e.g., purchase intent, engagement, trust, loyalty) and organizational performance (e.g.,
sales, profits, competitive advantage) (de Amorim et al., 2022). Digital marketing capabilities (including infrastructure and skills)
are prerequisites and enabl ers for successful digital marketing implementation and its impact (Almestarihi et al., 2024).
Advanced technologies influence digital marketing by enabling new practices (e.g., generative marketing, big data analytics)
and increasing effectiveness (e.g., through personalization, better predictions). These technologies can also influence consumer
behavior directly or indirectly (e.g., through immersive experiences in the Metaverse, interactions with AI chatbots) (Reed e t
al., 2025). Furthermore, consumer be havior, such as eWOM or social media interactions, can also influence organizational
performance and contribute to value cocreation (Sang et al., 2024).
To better understand this complex relationship, research regularly integrates mediating and moderating variables.
Mediators explain why or how digital marketing (or its components) influences outcomes (e.g., dynamic information
capabilities, public relations, and user interaction), while moderators explain under what conditions the influence of digita l
marketing (or its components) is stronger or weaker (e.g., consumer trust in brands, credibility, confidentiality, promotions).
There are also important cross -cutting themes that permeate the literature, including a focus on SMEs as a distinct target
audience or research subject, with specific needs and challenges. Sustainability dimensions in digital marketing practices have
gained prominence, alongside ethical and bias considerations in the use of digital marketing, particularly related to AI and data
privacy. The application of digital marketing in specific sectoral contexts (e.g., tourism, education, food and beverage) further
enriches this conceptual landscape.
This conceptual landscape suggests that digital marketing research is no longer simply about the use of simple digital
tools but is an increasingly complex field involving dynamic interactions between organizations, consumers, advanced
technologies, and contextual factors, explored via a variety of theoretical and methodological approaches.

4. Critical Discussion and Research Agenda

Studies by Espinoza and Piña -García (2024), Mukhopadhyay et al. (2024), Lima et al. (2024), and Cuijten and Wongpun
(2024) exemplify this atheoretical approach, conducting empirical investigations without grounding their analyses in
established theoretical frameworks. This atheoretical tendency severely limits theoretical contribution and generalizability,  as
without explicit theoretical frameworks, studies cannot adequately explain why relationships exist, thereby r educing their
contribution to cumulative knowledge development (Whetten, 1989).
Even studies employing established theories demonstrate significant limitations. The repeated application of Resource -
Based View (RBV) by Shams et al. (2024) and Mardatillah et  al. (2024) shows theoretical oversimplification, applying RBV
without accounting for the digital environment's rapidly evolving, platform-mediated nature. Despite examining AI, metaverse,
and augmented reality applications, reviewed studies conspicuously lack engagement with contemporary theories of
algorithmic governance (Gillespie, 2014), platform ecosystems (Choudary, 2016), or human -AI interaction (Daugherty, 2018).
Saura et al. (2024) examination of AI ethics  in digital marketing, while addressing a critical topic, employs only Multiple
Correspondence Analysis without theoretical grounding in ethics frameworks or algorithmic accountability literature. Similarly,
Alsoud et al. (2024) metaverse research and Kusumawati et al. (2024) avatar marketing study lack theoretical engagement with
virtual identity theories or presence theory, which are  essential for understanding user behavior in immersive digital
environments.

Methodologically, the field exhibits overwhelming dependence on cross -sectional survey designs, with 22 out of 30
studies employing this approach. While cross -sectional designs offer efficiency, they fundamentally constrain causal inference
and cannot capture the dynamic, evolutionary nature of digital marketing adoption and effectiveness (Maxwell & Cole, 2007).
Studies examining digital transformation, SME digital marketing adoption (Laila et al., 2024; Lima et al., 2024) and
technology implementation would benefit substantially from longitudinal des igns that track adoption trajectories, learning
curves, and performance evolution over time (Vandenberg, 2010). The digital marketing environment's rapid evolution —
particularly regarding AI, social media algorithms, and platform policies —renders findings from cross-sectional studies quickly
obsolete. Eight studies employ Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM) as their primary analytical
technique, reflecting concerning methodological homogeneity. Studies frequently fail to justify PLS -SEM selection over
covariance-based SEM, and employ PLS -SEM for mediation and moderation analyses without addressing recent critiques
regarding its limitations in estimating indirect effects.
Sampling limitations pervade the reviewed studies. Lima et al. ( 2024) recognize low SME accessibility due to pandemic
data collection but do not discuss potential non-response bias or sampling frame adequacy. The mega-influencers study (2024)
relies on 140 undergraduate students, raising serious concerns about external  validity and the generalizability of findings to
actual consumer populations with diverse demographic characteristics and purchasing power. Convenience sampling
dominates the reviewed studies without adequate justification or discussion of potential selection biases. The digital marketing
capabilities study (Shams et al., 2024) with N=302 across seven sectors (retail, IT, logistics, telecommunications, textiles,
automotive, petrochemicals) likely suffers from inadequate subsample sizes for meaningful cross-sector comparisons. The field
also demonstrates insufficient methodological pluralism, with only three studies employing bibliometric analysis while
experimental designs, ethnographic approaches, and computational methods remain largely absent.
Contextually, the reviewed studies exhibit pronounced geographic concentration, with substantial representation from
developing economies (Indonesia: 4 studies; Jordan: 4 studies; India, Thailand, Peru, Ethiopia, Vietnam, UAE: 1-2 studies each),
but limited engageme nt with cross -cultural theoretical frameworks (Mooij & Hofstede, 2010). Studies fail to theorize how
cultural dimensions —such as individualism -collectivism, uncertainty avoidance, or power distance —moderate digital
marketing effectiveness. Institutional context variations, particularly regarding digital infrastructure, regulatory environments,
and consumer protection frameworks, remain unexamined despite their known influence on digital marketing adoption and
effectiveness. Eleven studies examine SMEs, yet demonstrate insufficient theoretical engagement with SME -specific
characteristics that fundamentally differentiate them from large enterprises. Research treats SMEs as homogeneous entities
without considering resource munificence variations, organizational  learning capacity differences, or strategic orientation
heterogeneity.
Despite examining specific platforms (Facebook, Instagram, TikTok in the new ventures study, 2024; Opensooq app in
Aboalganam et al., 2024), research fails to engage with platform stud ies literature or consider platform affordances as
theoretical constructs. The effectiveness study identifies correlations between platforms (Instagram-TikTok r=0.16, p<0.01) but
does not theorize why certain platforms exhibit synergies or substitution effects, nor does it consider algorithmic mediation of
marketing content visibility. Studies demonstrate inconsistent conceptualization of "digital marketing" as a construct —some
treat it as unidimensional, others as multidimensional without clear theoretical  justification for dimension selection. This
conceptual ambiguity impedes construct validity and cross -study comparability. Outcome variables demonstrate concerning
heterogeneity and measurement quality issues, with studies examining "firm performance" wit hout distinguishing between
financial performance, marketing performance, or operational performance.
Multiple studies propose mediation and moderation mechanisms without adequate theoretical justification for
mediator/moderator selection. The tourism marketing study Setegn and Japee (2025) proposes public relations as a mediator
between promotional elements and marketing performance but does not explain why public relations specifically mediates this
relationship rather than alternative mechani sms. Studies frequently test mediation models without establishing temporal
precedence of the mediator, a critical requirement for causal mediation inference that cross -sectional designs cannot satisfy
(Hayes, 2013). Purchase intention serves as the primary outcome in six studies, yet purchase intention-behavior gaps are well-
documented, particularly in digital contexts where privacy concerns and information overload create intention -action
discrepancies.
Based on these critical gaps, several priority resea rch directions emerge. First, the field urgently requires development
of platform -mediated marketing theories that explicitly account for algorithmic curation, network effects, and platform
governance (Choudary, 2016). Research should investigate how platf orm algorithmic changes create marketing effectiveness
volatility, what theoretical mechanisms explain platform complementarity and substitution effects in multichannel strategies,
and how platform dependency alters SME competitive advantage sustainability . Second, the field must move beyond
technology acceptance models toward theories of algorithmic agency, human -AI collaboration, and algorithmic literacy
(Daugherty, 2018). Critical questions include: Under what conditions do consumers prefer AI versus hum an marketing
interactions? How does AI transparency affect marketing message credibility? What theoretical mechanisms explain consumer
algorithmic aversion in marketing contexts? Research should  examine how generative AI transforms content creation

Third, the field requires substantial methodological transformation. Longitudinal studies trac king digital marketing
adoption trajectories in SMEs over 2 -5 years are essential for establishing temporal precedence in mediation models and
understanding co-evolutionary dynamics between technology, organizational capabilities, and market outcomes (Vandenberg,
2010). Researchers should employ explanatory sequential designs combining surveys with in-depth case studies to understand
how SMEs without formal marketing departments develop digital capabilities, why certain digital marketing strategies succeed
or fail in specific contexts, and how organizational culture facilitates or inhibits digital transformation (Creswell and Clark, 2017).
The field should incorporate digital trace data analysis examining actual consumer behaviors on platforms rather than se lf-
reported measures, A/B testing and field experiments to establish causal effects of digital marketing interventions, and natural
experiments leveraging platform algorithm changes or policy shifts as exogenous shocks.
Fourth, multi-country comparative studies are needed to examine how cultural dimensions moderate digital marketing
effectiveness, whether Western -developed digital marketing theories generalize to collectivist, high power -distance cultures,
and how institutional environments condition digital marketing practices (Mooij and Hofstede, 2010). Research should develop
sector-specific theories explaining how service intangibility and simultaneity alter digital marketing effectiveness in pure service
contexts versus goods contexts, whether experient ial products versus utilitarian products exhibit different digital marketing
ROI patterns, and how heritage product authenticity perceptions interact with digital marketing in traditional craft industri es.
Fifth, investigation of post-cookie digital marketing strategies is critical, including effectiveness of contextual versus behavioral
targeting in privacy-constrained environments, first-party data collection strategies and their impact on customer relationships,
and trade-offs between personalization benefits and privacy concerns in consumer decision-making.
In conclusion, digital marketing research, while rapidly expanding, suffers from significant theoretical deficiencies (60%
atheoretical), methodological homogeneity (cross -sectional surveys, PLS -SEM d ominance), and insufficient contextual
theorization. The proposed research agenda prioritizes platform -mediated marketing theory development, AI -human
interaction theorization, longitudinal mixed -methods designs, cross -cultural investigation, and emerging phenomenon
examination. Addressing these gaps requires methodological pluralism, theoretical sophistication, and contextual sensitivity —
moving digital marketing scholarship from description toward explanation and prediction ( Gregor, 20 06). Without  these
fundamental improvements, the field risks continued fragmentation and limited theoretical progression, constraining both
academic knowledge advancement and practical guidance for digital marketing practitioners facing rapidly evolving
technological and competitive landscapes.

5. Final Considerations

The findings and directions of this research provide in -depth insights highly relevant to business practitioners and
substantially contribute to the academic world of  digital marketing. For organizations, this research emphasizes the need to
strategically adopt and integrate digital marketing to improve performance. Key findings identify the effectiveness of specif ic
tools and platforms, such as social media (Facebook,  Instagram), websites, and YouTube, in creating new markets and
enhancing online brand image. The importance of content delivery timing in B2B marketing to drive engagement is also
highlighted.
The implementation of new technologies shows significant poten tial. Augmented reality (AR) provides unique added
value and strengthens customer relationships. Big data support personalization, whereas generative AI enhances content
productivity. Chatbots could revolutionize digital banking services. However, the rese arch also reveals challenges, such as the
specific need to develop digital marketing capabilities in MSMEs and the importance of adoption factors in nonprofit
organizations. In addition to the supply chain and repositioning, digital marketing supports the sustainability of cooperatives.
Academic contributions include the use of advanced methodologies such as bibliometric analysis and literature synthesis
to map trends. The study introduces the concept of "inspirational value" and analyzes the role of customer value cocreation. A
multicriteria decision-making approach offers a platform evaluation tool, whereas an analysis of influencer marketing and the
importance of confidentiality in the financial sector enhances understanding. The research also identifies future areas, such as
influencer research beyond credibility, website quality evaluation, and the impact of quantum computing on market research.
These findings provide a strong foundation for effective practice and pave the way for further academic invest igations.
