Abstract
The performance of small and medium-scale enterprises (SMEs) in Nasarawa State, North
Central Nigeria, has remained persistently below potential despite the rapid proliferation of
digital technologies across the Nigerian business landscape. While digital payment and digital
marketing technologies have been widely promoted as drivers of enterprise growth, empirical
evidence on their specific effects on SME performance within Nasarawa State context remains
limited. This study examines the effect of digital technology adoption, through digital payment
technology adoption and digital marketing technology adoption, on the performance of SMEs in
Nasarawa State, North Central Nigeria. A cross-sectional survey research design was adopted,
with a sample of 386 SME owners/managers selected from a population of 10,728 registered
SMEs using a multi-stage sampling technique. Data were collected through a structured
questionnaire adapted from previously validated instruments, and analysis was conducted using
Partial Least Square Structural Equation Modelling (PLS-SEM) with SmartPLS 3.0. The study
concludes that SMEs operating within a digitally enabled business environment are measurably
more productive, profitable and competitive, and that strengthening digital payment
infrastructure and digital marketing capabilities in Nasarawa State is a development priority
with direct implications for job creation, rural enterprise growth and economic diversification.
Recommendations include expanding digital payment infrastructure to rural areas, introducing
digital literacy and marketing training programmes for SME owners, and creating incentives for
the adoption of integrated digital business solutions.

Introduction
The performance of small and medium-scale enterprises (SMEs) has remained a central concern
of economists, policy makers and development practitioners across the globe, given the sector's
role in employment generation, innovation, poverty reduction and gross domestic product
contribution. Globally, SMEs account for about ninety percent of all businesses, over seventy
percent of employment, and approximately fifty percent of GDP in both developed and
developing economies (World Bank, 2023). In advanced economies of Europe, North America
and parts of Asia, SMEs operate within digitally enabled ecosystems that provide seamless

In the developing world, and particularly across sub-Saharan Africa, SME performance tells a

broadereconomicdiversificationagendaofsuccessiveNigerian administrations.
Within Nigeria, Nasarawa State, located in the North Central geopolitical zone, has witnessed a
steady proliferation of SMEs across sectors such as agriculture, commerce, manufacturing and
services (Abdullahi, 2024). Despite this proliferation, SMEs in Nasarawa State continue to
record poor performance outcomes, characterised by low sales volumes, limited market reach,
high business failure rates and low profitability, particularly in the face of economic shocks,
currency volatilityand infrastructural deficits (Yakubu, 2023). A recurring explanationadvanced
by development economists for this state of affairs is the low adoption of digital technologies
that are supposed to expand market access, streamline paymentsystems and enhanceoperational
efficiencyforSMEs.
Digital technology adoption,encompassing the useof digitalpayment systems,digital marketing
platforms, e-commerce tools and other information and communication technology solutions,
determines two critical outcomes for SMEs: the efficiency of financial transactions through
digitalpaymenttechnology adoption,thatis,whetherenterprises canreceiveandmakepayments
through electronic channels such as mobile money, point-of-sale terminals, bank transfers and
online payment gateways; and the effectiveness of market reach and customer engagement
through digital marketing technology adoption, that is, whether enterprises can utilise social
media, email marketing, search engine optimisation and other digital marketing tools to attract
customers, build brands and expand market share (Ibrahim et al., 2024; Ehizokhale, 2025). In
Nasarawa State, reports from SME associations and business development service providers
have persistently linked low adoption of digital payment systems, occasioned by poor network
infrastructure, limited access to digital devices and low financial literacy, to cash-flow
inefficiencies and lost sales opportunities (Usman et al., 2025). Similarly, digital marketing
technology adoption has been undermined by limited digital skills, lack of access to affordable
data and insufficient awareness of the strategic value of digital platforms for business growth
(Abahetal.,2026).
Despitegovernmentinterventions such as the NationalDigital Economy Policy and Strategy and
periodicdigitalliteracyprogrammesbytheSmall andMediumEnterprisesDevelopmentAgency
of Nigeria (SMEDAN), SMEs in Nasarawa State continue to underperform, with many owners
reporting recurrent losses traceable directly to inability to accept digital payments, low visibility
in online marketplaces and lack of effective digital marketing campaigns (Eze & Okon, 2024).

LiteratureReview
DigitalTechnologyAdoption
Digitaltechnologyadoptionreferstotheprocessbywhichenterprisesintegrateandutilisedigital
tools, platforms and systems in their business operations, including digital payment systems,
digital marketing platforms, e-commerce solutions, cloud computing and other information and
communication technology applications (Ibrahim et al., 2024). It is a multidimensionalconstruct
that encompasses both the decision to adopt a technology and the depth and breadth of its
utilisation in business processes (Ehizokhale, 2025). Digital technology adoption has been
identified as a critical driver of enterprise productivity, market expansion and competitiveness,
particularly in developing economies where traditional infrastructure constraints can be partially
overcome through digital solutions (Usman et al., 2025). Gichohi et al. (2024) emphasised that
effective digital technology adoption requires not only access to devices and networks but also
digital literacy, perceived usefulness and organisational readiness. The study sees digital
technology adoption asthe extentto whichSMEs integrate andutilisedigital toolsand platforms
intheirpayment,marketing,operationalandcustomerengagementprocesses.
DigitalPaymentTechnologyAdoption
Digital payment technology adoption denotes the extent to which enterprises utilise electronic
payment systems and platforms for receiving and making payments, including mobile money
transfers, point-of-sale terminals, bank transfers, online payment gateways and digital wallets
(Usman et al., 2025). It is a function of the availability of digital payment infrastructure, the
perceivedease ofuseandusefulnessofdigital payment systems, the costoftransactions, andthe
level of digital financial literacy among business owners and their customers (Ibrahim et al.,
2024). Adeyemo and Onikoyi (2021) identified digital payment adoption as a critical enabler of
enterprise growth, noting that enterprises with diversified payment options record higher sales
volumes and improved cash-flow management. Digital payment technology adoption also
represents the capacity of enterprises to participate in the formal financial system and to benefit
from the efficiency gains associated with electronic transactions (Ogunleye & Adebayo, 2020).
This study defined digital payment technology adoption as the extent to which SMEs utilise
electronicpaymentsystemsforreceiving andmakingpaymentsintheirbusinessoperations.

DigitalMarketingTechnologyAdoption
Digital marketing technology adoption refers to the extent to which enterprises utilise digital
platforms and tools for marketing their products and services, including social media marketing
(Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, TikTok, X), email marketing, search engine optimisation,
content marketing and online advertising (Ehizokhale, 2025). It encompasses the frequency of
digital marketing activities, the range of platforms utilised, the integration of digital marketing
with business strategy, and the effectiveness of digital marketing campaigns in reaching target
customers (Ibrahim et al., 2024). Digital marketing technology adoption also encompasses the
useofanalytics toolsto measurecampaign effectiveness,optimisecontentandimprovecustomer
engagement (Nasir et al., 2026). This study views digital marketing technology adoption as the
extent to which SMEs utilise digital platforms and tools for promoting their products, engaging
customersandexpandingmarketreach.
PerformanceofSMEs
PerformanceofSMEsisamultidimensionalconstructthatcapturestheextenttowhichsmalland
medium-scale enterprises achieve their financial, operational and market objectives. It is
commonlymeasuredusingindicatorssuchassalesgrowth,profitabilityandreturnoninvestment,
market share and market expansion, customer base growth, operational efficiency and enterprise
survival (Eze & Okon, 2024; Yakubu, 2023). Abdullahi (2024) assessed SME performance
through productivitymeasures and reported differences in performance across sectors and states.
SMEperformancemay alsobedefinedin terms ofthe enterprise's capacityto generateeconomic
returns while efficiently using production resources and adapting to changing market conditions.
Usman et al. (2025) assessed SME performance through sales volume, profitability and
operational efficiency measures, while Eze and Okon (2024) measured performance through
revenue, customer base and market expansion. This perspective therefore treats SME
performance as a multidimensional outcome involving financial growth, market expansion,
operational efficiency and business sustainability rather than a single financial measure. This
study sees performance of SMEs as the extent to which small and medium-scale enterprises
achieve desired sales growth, profitability, market expansion and operational efficiency
outcomes.
DigitalPaymentTechnologyAdoptionandSMEPerformance
Usman et al. (2025) investigated the effect of FinTech on MSMEs' performance in Jigawa State,
Nigeria, using a cross-sectional survey design. The study covered MSMEs selected through a
multi-stage sampling technique. A structured questionnaire capturing digital payment methods,
alternativefinancing platforms and financial inclusion was administered,and datawere analysed
using PLS-SEM. Findings revealed thatFinTech adoption, particularly digital payment methods,
had a positive and significant effect on MSME performance, with financial inclusion mediating
the relationship. Although the study offered strong regional evidence, its focus on a single state
in North West Nigeria limited the generalisability of findings to other geopolitical zones with
different economic and infrastructural contexts, suggesting the need for context-specific
empiricalvalidationinNorthCentralNigeria.
Ibrahim et al. (2024) investigated the influence of online payment integration on SMEs'
performance in Osogbo Metropolis, Osun State, Nigeria, using a cross-sectional survey design.

DigitalMarketingTechnologyAdoptionandSMEPerformance
Abah et al. (2026) investigated the effect of digital marketing strategies on SME performance in
Karu Local Government Area of Nasarawa State, North Central Nigeria, using a cross-sectional
survey design. The study covered 412 SME owners selected through a multi-stage sampling
technique. A structured questionnaire capturing email marketing, mobile marketing and business
performance was administered, and data were analysed using multiple regression methods.
Findings revealed that email marketing significantly enhanced SME performance through cost-
effective and personalised communication strategies, fostering customer relationships and
increasing engagement, while mobile marketing was statistically insignificant. Although the
study provided strong state-level evidence on digital marketing, its focus on email and mobile
marketing alone limited the generalisability of findings to other digital marketing technologies
suchassocialmedia andsearch engineoptimisation.
Nasir et al. (2026) examined the effect of social media platforms usage on the performance of
SMEs in Katsina State, Nigeria, using a descriptive cross-sectional survey design anchored on
the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM). The study covered 381 SME owners selected
through random sampling. A structured questionnaire assessed the use of Facebook, Instagram,
TikTok, WhatsApp and X (Twitter) and their effects on SME performance, with data analysed
using PLS-SEM. Findings revealed that major social media platforms had positive and
statistically significant effects on SME performance, enhancing sales growth, customer
engagement, brand visibility and overall business competitiveness. WhatsApp and Facebook
demonstrated the strongest influence. Despite its methodological rigour, the study's North West
Nigerian setting differs institutionally from the North Central context, suggesting the need for
context-specificempiricalvalidation,whichthepresentstudyseekstoprovide.
Ehizokhale (2025) investigated the role of digital marketing in boosting oil palm MSMEs'
profitability in Edo State, Nigeria, using a survey research design anchored on the Technology
Acceptance Model (TAM). The study covered oil palm MSMEs selected through random
sampling. A structured questionnaire assessed online marketing campaigns, email marketing and
social media ads, with data analysed using regression analysis. Findings revealed a statistically
significant relationship between digital marketing technology adoption and the profitability of
MSMEs, and between online marketing campaigns and productivity. Despite its methodological
rigour, the study's focus on a single agricultural sub-sector(oil palm) limited the generalisability
of findings to SMEs across other sectors, suggesting the need for broader sectoral coverage,
whichthepresentstudyseekstoprovide.

TechnologyAcceptanceModel(TAM)andResource-BasedView(RBV)Theory
This study is anchored on the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), originally propounded by
Davis (1989), and the Resource-Based View (RBV) theory, originally propounded by Barney
(1991). The TAM posits that an individual's acceptance and use of a technology are determined
bytwo primary factors: perceived usefulness,whichisthedegree towhich apersonbelieves that
using aparticular system wouldenhancetheirjob performance, andperceived easeofuse,which
is the degree to which a person believes that using a particular system would be free of effort.
Applied to SMEs in Nasarawa State, the adoption of digital payment and digital marketing
technologiesis contingenton SME owners' perceptionsoftheusefulnessofthesetechnologies in
improvingsales,profitabilityandmarketreach, aswellastheeasewithwhichthesetechnologies
can be integrated into their daily business operations (Abah et al., 2026; Ehizokhale, 2025). The
RBV complements the TAM by positing that the sustained performance and competitive
advantage of an enterprise depend fundamentally on its abilityto acquire, control and effectively
deploy valuable, rare, inimitable and non-substitutable resources (Barney, 1991). Applied to
SMEs, digital technologies constitute critical strategic resources; an enterprise's performance is
therefore contingent on the adoption of digital payment technologies that enhance transaction
efficiency and financial inclusion, and on the adoption of digital marketing technologies that
expand market reach and customer engagement (Nasir et al., 2026; Ibrahim et al., 2024). The
integration of TAM and RBV is considered most appropriate for this study because it directly
links the adoption and utilisation of critical digital resources to enterprise-level performance
outcomes, providing a coherent theoretical lens through which the relationship between digital
payment technology adoption, digital marketing technology adoption and the performance of
SMEsinNasarawaStatecanbeunderstoodandempiricallytested.

data on digital payment technology adoption, digital marketing technology adoption and SME
performance from a representative sample of SME owners/managers at a single point in time.

State, North Central Nigeria. According to the Small and Medium Enterprises Development

From a Technology Acceptance Model perspective, the positive effects of digital payment and
digital marketing technology adoption on SME performance can be explained through the
perceived usefulness and ease of use mechanisms central to the theory: SME owners who
perceive digital payment systems as useful for improving sales and cash-flow management, and
digital marketing platforms as useful for reaching customers and expanding markets, are more
likely to adopt these technologies and integrate them into theirbusiness operations (Davis, 1989;
Abah et al., 2026). From a Resource-Based View perspective, SMEs that adopt digital payment
and digital marketing technologies are effectively acquiring and deploying strategic resources
that are valuable, rare, inimitable and non-substitutable, thereby positioning themselves for
superior performance relative to non-adopting competitors (Barney, 1991; Nasir et al., 2026). In
Nasarawa State, where digital infrastructure and digital literacy remain unevenly distributed
(Usman et al., 2025), the performance dividend of digital technology adoption is likely to be
even larger than the beta coefficients captured in this study's cross-sectional measurement, as
longitudinal tracking across multiple business cycles would likely reveal a larger cumulative
effect.
Conclusion
This study examined the effect of digital technology adoption, operationalised through digital
payment technology adoption and digital marketing technology adoption, on the performance of
SMEs in NasarawaState,NorthCentralNigeria.AnchoredintheTechnologyAcceptanceModel
and the Resource-Based View, and drawing on PLS-SEM analysis of 342 usable responses, the
study found that digital paymenttechnology adoption and digital marketing technology adoption
both have significant positive effects on SME performance. Together, these two dimensions of
digital technology adoption explain 58.2 percent of the variance in enterprise performance, a
finding that underscores the critical importance of digital technology adoption as a structural
determinantofSMEoutcomesinthestate.
The study makes important contributions to the digital entrepreneurship, SME development and
technology adoption literatures by providing quantitative PLS-SEM evidence on the digital
technology adoption performance nexus specific to Nasarawa State, North Central Nigeria. It
alsoextends theapplicationoftheTechnology Acceptance Model and theResource-Based View
to theSME sector by demonstrating that thetheories' core propositions that perceived usefulness
drives technology adoption, and that control of valuable digital resources drives performance
hold for digital payment and digital marketing technologies as strategic inputs. The study
concludes that SMEs operating within a digitally enabled business environment are measurably
more productive, profitable and competitive, and that strengthening digital payment
infrastructure and digital marketing capabilities in Nasarawa State is therefore a development
priority with direct implications for job creation, rural enterprise growth and economic
diversification.
