ABSTRACT

Background and Objective: In the backdrop of increasing sensitivity towards the environment,
encouraging responsible consumption is an important challenge worldwide. The current study
intends to establish the relationship between the digital marketing (DM) of sustainable products
and sustainable consumption and measure the moderating effect of environmental concern.
Aligned with the theoretical foundations of consumer behavior and marketing effectiveness of
digital technologies. The study aims to examine different characteristics o f digital markeint
and effectiveness, influencers, and quality of DM content can trigger consumers into adopting
environmentally sustainable consumption practices.

Methodology: The study employs a quantitative methodology where structured questionnaires
are administered to 500 digital consumers in China exposed to sustainability marketing
communications. The survey instrument applied standardized and validated scales. Thus, the
survey questions were developed on a 5-point Likert scale to measure the respondents' attitudes
towards the tested variables. Therefore, data analysis used Structural Equation Modeling (SEM)
with Partial Least Squares (PLS), which enables analysis of direct and mediating effects within
the model. The measurement model examined the in ternal consistency of the constructs and
their convergent and discriminant validity. The structural model estimated path coefficients to
test the hypotheses. A mediation analysis was conducted to test whether environmental
awareness significantly mediated the relationship between digital marketing strategies and
sustainable consumption behavior.

Results: Based on the research, it is possible to conclude that digital marketing communication
strategies such as content relevance, content interest, and message association impact and
modify sustainable consumption behavior and that environmentally oriented awa reness is a
mediating factor in this process. For the same reasons, it is expected that those e -marketing
initiatives that delivered relevant, exciting, and credible content were more effective at
persuading consumers to change their habits and embrace mor e sustainable behaviors. In
addition, consumers with a higher level of environmental sensitivity indicated a stronger
response to the promotion campaigns, underlining the significance of increasing people's
environmental sensitization as a component of digital promotion strategies.

Conclusion: The current research enhances the knowledge of digital marketing and
sustainability to comprehend how brands can shift consumer behavior towards eco-friendliness
using digital technologies. The findings also hold useful managerial implications for markete rs
aspiring to integrate their roadmap with the spirit of the SDGs by encouraging a more
sustainable fan practice.

Keywords: digital marketing, sustainable consumer practices, environmental awareness,
behavioral change, Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).

1 INTRODUCTION

Digital marketing, as a relatively new stream, has become a major
driving factor for consumers regarding and promoting sustainable practices. As
the intensity of environmental problems and the pressure to meet the SDGs
grows, it becomes important to compre hend how digital marketing can act as

Today, social networks, websites, and even smartphone applications are vivid
examples of marketers' opportunities to influence consumers globally. These
platforms provide an excellent chance for companies to create awareness
regarding environmental concerns and solicit support for changes in sustainable
practices, which means that digital marketing is now central to contributing to
the solution of sustainability issues (Chaffey & Ellis-Chadwick, 2019).
Eco-friendly consumerism, sustainable consumer behavior refers to using
consumer practices that cause the least harm to the environment and social
and economic systems (Young et al ., 2009) . Technological or Post -Modern

marketing techniques to assume centrality in changing the behavior of
consumers by offering information, promoting awareness, and embracing it

digital marketing communication and the impact of sustainable consumer

3.1 DATA AND METHODOLOGY

3.1.1 Study Design

This study uses a survey research approach to determine the impact of
digital marketing on sustainable consumer actions and the moderating part of
environmental consciousness. The current study analyzes the current state of
digital marketing initiatives to evaluate their impact on promoting sustainable
consumption practices and esta blish environmental consciousness's role in
moderating those relations. The study involves primary cross -sectional data
obtained from online respondents in China via structured questionnaires from
consumers.

3.1.2 Targeted Population, Sample Size, and Sampling Technique

The target population for this study includes digital consumer in China
who has at any time come across a digital marketing campaign that advocates
for sustainability. The use of digital consumers guarantees that those who will
respond to the study have firsthand experience with sustainable digital
marketing as needed for the study. A convenient sample of 500 respondents is
administered for the study. This number is deduced based on guidelines for
performing SEM analysis, which clearly s tates that the minimum number of
responses per indicator should be 10 (Hair, Risher, Sarstedt, & Ringle, 2019) .
Thus, a sample of 500 students is large enough for research of this type due to
the multi-factorial model of the study. To increase external validity, the cross-
sectional sample includes respondents of different ages, genders, income
levels, and education levels. Therefore, the research applies stratified random
sampling to include all the demographic segments of digital consumers and
select consumers randomly. The  population is segmented across different
groups by relevant variables like age, gender, income, educational level, etc.
Systematic sampling is then taken within each stratum so that every important
population sub-group will be included in the study.

3.3 DATA ANALYSIS

The preliminary data cleaning was done to check if the data was
complete and had no missing values and outliers. If there was missing or
inconsistent data in a case, the case was dropped, or data was imputed where
needed. Besides, normality assumptions were tested to see if SEM data
distribution met the assumption of the Shen et al. (2012) technique.

3.3.1 Structural Equation Modeling (SEM)

This study used the Structural Equation Modelling – Partial Least Squares
(PLS) method as the key data analysis technique. Due to its applicability to
models with hypothetical factors and to cope with non-normal data, which most
behavioral investigations deal w ith, PLS -SEM was selected. The choice of
quantitative methods arises from the necessity of measuring the connection of
variables and hypothesis verification. Data analysis was carried out using the
structural equation modeling method by applying the Partia l Least Square
technique because of the method's capability to handle complex research
models that incorporate latent variables and test direct and mediating effects.
SmartPLS software was utilized to analyze the data. SEM makes it possible to
provide both evidence of the direct impact of digital marketing variables on
sustainable consumption behavior and the indirect impact through the variable
environmental awareness. The analysis was carried out in two stages:

3.3.2 Measurement Model Assessment

• Internal consistency was assessed using Cronbach's Alpha and Composite
Reliability (CR). 0.7 or higher indicates acceptable reliability (Fornell &
Larcker, 2018);
• Convergent validity was examined using Average Variance Extracted
(AVE), where values above 0.5 indicate good convergent validity.
Discriminant validity was tested using the Fornell -Larcker criterion,
ensuring the constructs were distinct.

• The relationships between the variables (hypotheses) were tested by

PLS-SEM did not provide traditional goodness -of-fit indices like
covariance-based SEM (CB-SEM), but model fit was assessed using criteria such
as Standardized Root Mean Square Residual (SRMR), where values below 0.08
indicate a good fit (Henseler et al., 2014).

Respondents provided informed consent before completing the survey, and they
could withdraw from the study at any time.

marketing strategies to maximize their impact on consumer behavior.
In order to create cohesive sustainability campaigns that align with
national and global environmental goals (like SDGs), governments s hould
