Abstract: The purpose of this study is to review and analyze how Korean municipal governments
implement local promotion marketing strategies and activities using various media. The study also
shows how these municipalities can enhance promotion effectiveness based on the findings of pr ior
studies. The results of the study have been sixfold: First, over the past thirty years, there has been
limitless competition for survival among local governments, and these governments have increasingly
used more and more advanced techniques, more dive rse media, and more wide -ranging platforms.
Second, to achieve policy publicity, it is important to form favorable public opinions since they can
improve the awareness and favorability of the policy and PR content. Third, cross -media strategies
include soc ial media platforms, outdoor advertising strategies, and traditional TV broadcasting that
disseminate effective promotions. Fourth, adopting a variety of new media video techniques and
technologies to use these techniques tends to increase attention and en hance users' immersion.
Therefore, careful consideration of the characters used is required. Fifth, because YouTube primarily
involves short -form content, the developer's effort to strengthen usefulness, increase entertainment,
and satisfy basic viewer needs is essential. This study's sixth and final result is that using social media
proved effective in enhancing the fidelity and reality of suggested content from the perspective of the
relationship between the organization and the public and strengthening i nteractive traits of content
from the perspective of tourism and the intention to visit.

Keywords: Local Government PR, Promotion, Media, Video Content, Social Media

1. Introduction
Since 1995, 8 democratically elected terms of Korean local government have been actively carrying
out all-round media PR and marketing strategies by producing various media content. These various
types of media content help promote local government policie s and municipal administrations,
enhance the value of their regional brands, and promote tourism, festivals, and special products. This
is in line with the main trends of international video promotion by local governments, such as tourism,
investment attraction and global business collaboration, MICE (Meeting, Incentive Tour, Convention,
and Exhibition) promotion, and the strengthening of national and city branding. Such global trends
include the selection and concentration of cost reduction and maximization of effectiveness in the face
of high inflation and the active use of mobile media. Besides traditional media such as TV ,
newspapers, radio, magazines, and outdoor advertisements, local governments focus on developing
integrated marketing communication in cluding the active utilization of promotional videos, slogans,
and characters. They also use various social media such as YouTube, Facebook, Instagram, and
TikTok, as well as forms of smart media such as digital signage and mobile devices.

The main fields of local government public relations research can be broadly categorized as follows:
(1) Historic and developmental aspects of governmental PRs; (2) Promotions of gover nmental policy.
(3) Cross-media strategies and their effects; (4) Government ads; (5) Promotions using YouTube; (6)
PRs using various social media.

2.1.1 Emergence of the Need for PR Strategies for Local Governments
The Korean local government system, which actually began in 1995, introduced management
mindsets and techniques into the administrative system. The original goals of this system were to
enhance financial independence, to collect the personal and public opinions of voting eligible
residents, and to stimulate the competition system among local governments. The need for public
relations strategies for local governments led to the need to attra ct and implement profitable projects
for the income increase of residents and financial independence of local governments. The key
elements of urban marketing are placement marketing, city identity establishment, and city brand
value enhancement[1].

2.1.2 Characteristics of Early Local Government Promotion in the late 1990
As a means of creating a regional image for the establishment of urban identity, many local
governments actively implemented event projects that made use of the cultural specificity of their
region. Event projects included those with themes such as nature, culture, tourism, as well as
international conferences and competitions. For example, ‘the Gwangju Biennale,’ ‘the Chuncheon
International Puppet Festival,’ ‘the Jeju Island Village Fe stival,’ ‘the Cheongju Printing and
Publication Fair,’ ‘the Icheon Pottery Festival,’ and ‘the Boryeong Mud Festival.’
In addition to promoting the region through local festivals, local governments have also been
actively tried to promote the image and cha racteristics of the region to audiences and viewers through
direct support to produce movies and TV dramas. This is an attempt to apply corporate CI (Corporate
Identity) work through mass media, and use PPL (Product Placement) techniques in public relation s
for public administration and local governance. The local governments have also begun to support the
production of popular entertainment such as movies and dramas by providing set locations that are
also for local attractions and unique places as filming locations. This supports the project by providing
a portion of the production costs, and by constructing and supplying sets for filming[1]. This is like the
case of providing filming locations using regional famous landmarks and using infrastructure to
promote and earn rental income, providing convenience to video content creators, and seeking
regional promotion and tourism revenue in return[2].

2.1.3 PR Strategies for City Brand Enhancement in 2000s
In the early 2000s, integrated marketing communication (IMC) techniques were introduced to
enhance the values of regional or municipal brands. Each local government started developing and
using its own printed or spoken slogans and visual mascots using various digital media communication
means and platforms. R epresentative examples include ‘I Seoul U’ in Seoul, ‘Dynamic Busan’ in
Busan, and ‘Colorful Daegu’ in Daegu. Since the early 2000s, the IMC techniques have been widely
used as a tool for a variety of purposes by governments or organizations throughout the  world due to
the emergence of Social Network Service (SNS). Finally, as the use of SNS online platforms became
easier and more ubiquitous and the need to communicate with residents continued to grow, it spurred
municipal Korean government departments to i ntroduce social media for a promotional tool to
improve the efficiency of civil service and the image of the government[3].

2.1.4 PR Strategies Using Advertising and Visualized Media in 2010s
In the 2010s, advertising and video content strategies began to  spread throughout most local
governments. Local governments' use of visualized and synesthetic content also increased rapidly
during this era and attempts to advertise local government events and special products by user

2.2 Promotion of Local Government Policy
Policy promotion involves grasping the opinions of the public to then reflect  those opinions during
the policy generation process and finally make effective policy decisions. It refers to communicating a
thread or intention at the right time, to the right people, and in the most effective way so that it can be
understood and to lead to a deeper understanding and participation. According to existing theories and
previous research cases on that evaluated the effectiveness of public relations, policy and PR
evaluations have been primarily organized into two dimensions: (1) the evaluati on of outputs relative
to inputs in planning and execution; (2) the evaluation of effects or outcomes on cognitive -emotional-
attitude-behavior changes of among recipients.
The most widely known theories of IMC effectiveness measurement can be applied to policy
promotion effectiveness measurement modeling. The main research results and conclusions presented
in this study were based on the research results of MacKenzie, Lutz, and Belch’s structural equation
model[6]. The factors influencing the public's intention to support government policy were as follows:
the perception of the policy publicity, the favorability of the policy publicity, the perception of the
policy itself, th e favorability of the policy itself, with a final factor being the dependent variable of
policy support (intention) [Fig.1][7].
Policy favorability has a positive effect on policy support intention. Furthermore, the most
influential factor affecting policy favorability and support intention is not awareness of the policy PR,
but rather the favorability of the policy itself. Policy PR favorability indirectly rather than directly
influences policy advocacy intention through the formation of policy PR favorabi lity policy
favorability. This model illustrates the public’s lack of support for a policy is not caused by ignorance
but rather because they simply do not like the policies themselves and/or the promotion of said
policies. It suggests that, unlike product  advertising where raising awareness leads to product
purchases, it is more realistic and feasible to increase the favorability of the policy itself with a smaller

2.3 Cross-Media Strategies
In an era of multi-channeled multi-media, there are myriad forms of content, business, government,
and other types of organizations. Each of these all carry out cross -media strategies of persuasive
communication activities that are directed at consumers, t he public, and citizens at large. As a core
strategy of IMC, organizations, companies, and governments diversify their contact points with the
public to induce exposure, cognition, interest. They strive to generate significant attitudinal, and
behavioral c hanges with promotional messages. They develop and utilize content suitable for the
characteristics of the type of media being used and effectively build brand identity (BI) by combining
each medium in a complementary manner, while being cognizant of stren gths and weaknesses of each
medium.
Representative studies on the PR effects of local governments throughout cross -media types
provided this study with the following insights.
(1) TV has been an influential and popular visual media, with its realistic ima ges, lively expression
and dissemination power, but its viewership and influence are now decreasing and being replaced with
mobile and non-real-time TV viewing behavior[8].
(2) Newspapers and magazines (print media) are now rarely produced physically in th e form of
paper and are mainly used online in portals, and webzines on the web through the Internet. Magazines
are easily target consumers and advertisers according to the characteristics of public tastes. Policy
promotion on printed materials directly ope rated by local governments has a positive effect on the
perception of fairness and transparency of local governments, however, it does not have a significant
effect on the perception of the performance of local government organizations[9].
(3) Although the  value and influence of radio as a type of public relations media for local
government is decreasing; it still retains the advantages of providing strong consumer targeting and
reaching a fixed audience who can listen while engaging in other activities. Al so, advertising costs for
radio are very low[10].
(4) With the rapid rise of new forms of media (the Internet, mobile, social media, etc.), has come a
simultaneous remarkable increase in advertising, public relations, and marketing activities using these
new media. Furthermore, these new media can be controlled easily by their audience because they are
personalized and interactive. Since the amount of information carried by these new media is
overwhelmingly large, the probability of avoiding uninteresting messages and ads is high. Providing
incentives tends to lead to increased user interest in the form of clicks[8].
(5) Recently, public relations activities using new media have been frequently attempted by
recruiting social media PR steering groups (supporters), local creators, celebrities, and influencers. By
focusing on characteristics of interactive communication through dialogue on social media (Facebook,

2.4 Government Advertising
Municipal advertising objectives include developing and increasing: (1) A favorable attitude and
perception of local governm ents; (2) The intention to participate in various festivals and events
planned by local governments; (3) The intention to visit tourist attractions and attractions, and (4) A
revitalization of the local economy and prevention of population extinction. This  was done through
relocation of companies and individuals under the condition of providing various benefits and
incentives[12].
Some important findings of prior studies on the effectiveness of government ads are as follows:
(1) The key to the advertising e ffect depends on how naturally recipients pay attention to the
advertisement messages. After this is achieved, the process of cognition - emotion - attitude change -
action (visit, purchase, word of mouth, etc.) may happen. In this process, the degree of u nderstanding
and immersion in the advertising message after exposure is the key influence on the process leading to
an actual effect[13].
(2) In order to maximize the understanding and immersion effect of the advertising message,
various attempts are made to achieve this using audio -visual stimuli, such as using numerous types of
camerawork, sound, subtitles, fonts, copywriting, editing, and special effects. In addition, to enhance
the credibility, familiarity, entertainment, and usefulness of advertising m essages, city brand elements
(logos, characters, symbols/attractions, advantages and attractions, current events, etc.), characters
(heads of local governments, residents, etc.), and storytelling elements are developed and utilized[14].
(3) A study analyzed municipal ads in KTX trains, which were widely used to enhance the visual
effect of ads using visual media such as TV and outdoor ads. The local government ads in the trains
tended to use variety of camera angles by using drones, long shots, full shots, knee shots, waist shots,
and close-ups. They also strived to achieve a sense of dynamism through frequent scene transitions
and movements. They frequently displayed subtitles in a large variety of places. If a government had a
relatively large budget, it additionally used 3D animation and motion graphics. In video ads for
tourism purposes, extreme long shots and full shots for overall view were frequent, mainly using
drones. There were also infrequent transitions and movements, while plac ing subtitles on the bottom
of the screen. The tourism promotional videos tended to use slow zoom and time -lapse
cinematography to enhance the sense of presence. Furthermore, in local festivals and event ads,
techniques such as drone shots, close -ups, slides, panning, multi angle shots, and eye line changes,
were used. To capture various aspects of the public enjoying and experiencing the festival, handheld
cinematography emphasized realism, while panning and tilting pan at eye level to control the audience
perspective. Primarily, the latter part of the video was an introduction of the cast expressed with
images and CG by using a variety of frequent subtitles[12].
(4) A series of a content analyses and experimental studies on the effect of local government a ds
model shows that the frequency of and exposure time to a head of local government were higher than
in other model types. In that case, the method of using an appealing variety of policies and special
products within one advertisement was frequent. Local government leaders are more highly preferred,
and credible when compared to the expert model, and this is especially the case when promoting
specialty products. On the other hand, when presented solely for the purpose of promoting the venue,
ads featuring professional models are more preferred than ads featuring group leaders[14].

2.5 Promotions Using Y ouT ube
As of 2023, YouTube holds the highest percentage of the market share among online video services
(OTT) with 71.0%, followed by Netflix (35.7%), TVING (9.1%), Coupang Play (6.3%), and Wavve
(5.9%). More than 90% of internet users in Korea use YouTube as their primary source of online video
watching, regardless of their age or gender leaving a major disparity with the usage levels of other
video service options like Netflix and Instagram[15][16] . This is because YouTube allows general
media users to quickly a nd easily search for video content that contains the information, knowledge,
and entertainment you want. This medium also has the strength of not being limited by time
constraints. Also, it is effective across a wide range of genres including entertainment , education, and
information type content (e.g., how -to-videos) with each achieving its own purpose. Most YouTube
content generated by local government is categorized into policy promotion, tourism, B -grade content
(low-budget, parody, puns, kitsch, buzzwo rds, etc.), web drama, and music videos. Characters
appearing in this content are mostly ambassadors (famous celebrities, influencers, local supporters),
heads of local government, and civil servants[17].
Prior research on user response as it relates to co ntent characteristics and their effects show that the
enhancing factors for YouTube user engagement, satisfaction, and subscription include usability,
entertainment (playfulness), and the satisfaction of basic needs. The users recognize the influence of
the channel and content, and decide whether to subscribe or not, referring to the number o f likes,
views, and subscribers [18]. Among the characteristics of YouTube's content, planning, specificity,
usability, update cycle, entertainment value, live action, a nd the YouTuber's speech, friendliness,
sincerity, of communication with its users were identified as the most important usage factors[19].
Another content analysis study on the effectiveness of YouTube content channels among 9 local
governments in Korea,  analyzes the operation status of 9 municipal YouTube channels. This Study
examine the characteristic trends of 135 popular videos (the top 15 views by each local government)
and the characteristics of the format and content adding 8 metropolitan local gov ernments (Seoul,
Busan, Incheon, Daegu, Gwangju, Daejeon, Ulsan, and Sejong) and Chungju. These were chosen as
they occupy the largest portion of numbers of local government channels. The study investigated and
analyzed the relationship between the charact eristic trends of each video and the quantitative level of
viewer responses, such as the number of views, likes, and comments for each. The most frequently
produced and uploaded videos on the channels were for ‘policy promotion,’ ‘music and entertainment,’
‘city brand promotion,’ and ‘travel and events (festivals),’ respectively[20].

2.6 Promotions Using Social Media
Compared to traditional mass media, with today’s social media services (SNS), anyone can freely
create, share, and distribute information and  entertainment, and it is now easier to form relatively
horizontal human relationships. Governments are also actively adopting and using social media as a
key publicity medium along each stage of policy formation, policy decision -making, policy
implementation, evaluation, and feedback. SNS has now emerged as the best alternative for improving
a citizen's acceptance and participation in the implementation process of civil service, policy
promotion, and image improvement for municipal governments[3].
The key factors of government PR when using SNS are accessibility, speed, and relationship
development. The public has an accurate understanding of government PR practitioners and is aware
that there is a difference in perception between them and other government PR practitioners, but
government PR practitioners are not aware of the differences in perception among the public.

3. Method
This study used a meta -analysis of recent empirical studies to explore the main factors used for
enhancing strategies and effectiveness in the video content promotion of local governments. To collect
the literature for this analysis, a representative academ ic search engine addressed as ‘DBpia’
(https://www.dbpia.co.kr/) was used applying the key words of ‘visual media, PR content, and local
government.’ Also, the search set of the period of publication was collected from a fifteen -year period
from 2008 to 2023 because there had been very few studies on this topic before 2008.
As the result of the search, 72 articles were found, and 52 of them were selected for analysis. The
other 20 studies were excluded because their topics or their theoretical backgrounds were not highly
relevant to the purpose of this study. The studies that are excluded from the analysis are those about
pre-existing videos rather than pre -planned promotional videos, and cases where the strategy or
effectiveness of video promotions is not the central theme. All the findings in these selected articles
were analyzed according to the main topics of this study and organized accordingly.

4.2 Factors for Policy Support
To strengthen support for policy publicity, it is crucial to generate favorability toward the policy
itself, and to achieve this, it is necessary to improve the awareness of the policy promotion content as
well as greater favorable awareness of the policy itself. Individual citizen support for a policy comes
from the awareness and favorability of the policy itself, but it is also related t o the awareness and
favorability of the policy promotion itself, so it is necessary for the policy promotion content to be
widely accessible to citizens, and for it to induce efficient feedback by ensuring that the content is
truthful and interactive. To reach this end, consideration should be always made for the following: the
material, the pattern (format) of promotional content, the involvement of experts, the securing of killer
content, and the selection of useful media. A two-way public relations program for enhancing goodwill
with components such as newspaper planning articles, broadcast product placement, and online issue
management is more effective than a one-way public relations program to raise awareness.

local government ads need to carefully consider their use of characters such as the head of the local
government. Therefore, it is necessary to consider the content aspect of government ads and their
effectiveness in terms of audience cognition, emotions, attitudes, and behaviors. In particular, the
effectiveness of specific advertising models, such as group leaders, celebrities, influencers, ordinary
citizens, and foreigners, varies greatly depending on  the purpose of advertising and the type of
advertisement. The strategies therefore should be prepared to utilize them in the right place. Most
relevant studies suggest that the advertising model of local governments should be used strategically
and variably according to their purpose and appeal method.

while satisfying basic user needs, and operating within a short form. In the current h eyday of
YouTube, local governments now scramble to open channels and focus on improving quantitative
indicators such as the number of videos, subscribers, likes, and comments, but in addition to these
basic indicators, it is necessary to establish a new s trategy to strengthen the qualitative aspects of
individual content. These qualitative factors include usefulness, playfulness, attention, immersiveness,
friendliness, communicativeness, and reliability leading to the production of what the media industry
terms ‘killer content.’

4.6 Effective Social Media Use
When using social media, it is most effective to enhance the fidelity of the content and reality of the
topic by considering; the perspective of the relationship between the organization and the public, the
perspective of the tourists and their intention t o visit, and the importance of generating content that
strengthens through word-of-mouth, that is informational, reliable, entertaining, that is interactive, and
that is effective. Social media content has been empirically proven to be superior in competit iveness
compared to other media due to the ease of acquiring information and entertainment. It is also due to
the diversity of discussions and the ripple effect among users. Social media are rich and satisfying in
terms of culture, tourism, and events.
Awareness, understanding, and satisfaction among citizens with local government becomes higher if
social media promotion is generally at a high level and if the main motivation for using social media is
to ‘easily learn useful information.’ This includes muni cipal administration news and major policy
progress. Relatively high satisfaction was shown in the 'provision of useful information in daily life',
'amount of information', the 'collection of opinions', and the relatively low satisfaction in the 'prompt
response to citizens' needs'. Social media content provided by local governments is generally rated as
beneficial, while fidelity is low [24]. The content of publicity provided is insufficient compared to the
demands on the economy, the need for jobs, the imp ortance of health and welfare. It is shown that
promotion in the field of culture and tourism was excessive compared to the demand of users. In
addition, local government promotion using SNS proved to have significant effects in terms of ‘policy
awareness and understanding,’ ‘government-citizen relationship development,’ ‘policy understanding
and support,’ and ‘policy support and willingness to participate.’ However, there were many
unsatisfactory responses given when seeking information on the real lives o f citizens in terms of
economy, jobs, health, welfare, and providing local status when responding quickly and sincerely to
their demands.
Most of the empirical study on the effect of SNS marketing communication on brand image,
attitude, and loyalty of trav el destinations showed that municipal SNS marketing communication
consisted of 5 components: a ‘participatory event,’ a ‘viral moment,’ an ‘information provision,’ a
‘sales promotion,’ and an ‘advertising campaign.’ Also, establishing ‘brand image,’ ‘brand  attitude,’
and ‘brand loyalty’ are all required for successful campaigns. It was found that a local governments'
SNS 'word of mouth' and 'information provision' affect the 'brand image' of travel destinations.
Furthermore, SNS 'word of mouth', 'informatio n provision' and 'advertisement' affect 'brand attitude'.
SNS 'word of mouth', 'information provision' and 'sales promotion' were even found to affect 'brand
loyalty'. In addition, 'brand image' has a significant effect on 'brand attitude' and 'brand loyal ty', and
'brand attitude' has a significant effect on 'brand loyalty'[25].

round media PR strategies and activities to survive and to break away from the phenomenon of

centralization and regional extinction. They have done this through the promotion of tourism, festivals,
special products, and settlement conditions. This study explored how promotional videos and content
should be planned and produced, and how local government public relations departments can achieve
their goals in the areas of PR techniques, media and platform utilization, message content, and
production methods.
As a result of extracting and combining important fi ndings from previous empirical studies related
to the development process of local government PR and the effectiveness of PR strategies, various
implications and utilization plans for PR strategies were derived. In an environment where it is
difficult to e xpect the continuity and breadth of the publicity effect, this study tried to provide
consistent prior research findings, while excluding irrelevant results. The goal of this process was to
give PR practitioners insights into predictable effects among a wi de range of subjects. The
implications and practical applicability derived from the results are difficult to apply in small local
governments with low budgets, so in these cases, it is necessary to narrow the scope and reduce the
scale of each PR project.
What differentiates this study from others in a similar vein is that it helps to explore the
characteristics of PR according to changes in trends in the development of local government public
relations in Korea from a relatively broad perspective. Another differentiation is that it concentrates on
reliable results that have been commonly validated by prior studies and throughout reviewed literature.
Therefore, the items explored in this study are significant in that they can be applied to form a
comprehensive perspective rather than fragmented among the matters to be considered when
establishing and implementing a PR practice plan.
The limitation of this study is that it mainly relied on literature and prior research data because
publicity plans and practices of local governments have been very diverse and have been accompanied
by unpredictable changes according to media trends. However, there have not been many cases of
systematic and empirical measurement or confirmation of their effectiveness, and their re search
methods and results have not been consistent enough. It is considered inappropriate, in terms of
generalizability, to extract a specific case at a specific point in time and analyze that single case
empirically. In addition, due to the limited numbe r of cases of relevant prior studies, a full -fledged
meta-study was not available. Therefore, follow -up study should be proposed to clarify and further
analyze the relationship among content format, user response, PR favorability, and the goal
achievement potential of PRs. Also, comparative case studies and more detailed longitudinal studies
on strategies and effects of PR can be proposed to find more systematic insights as well as empirical
bases to achieve more effective PR practices.
