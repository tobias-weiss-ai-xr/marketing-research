Abstract
This study aims to examine Generation Z’s
behavioral intentions regarding the use of a
mental health app. This study adopts the
technology acceptance model (TAM) and
signaling theory to analyze the factors that
influence Gen Z’s propensity to adopt digital
mental health solutions in the context of parent -
child relationship issues that affect their mental
health. The research investigates the
relationships between perceived usefulness,
perceived ease of use, brand competence, attitude
toward using a mental health app, and behavioral
intention to use these digital services. A
quantitative cross-sectional study was conducted
with 311 Generation Z participants (aged 18 -25)
in Jakarta and surrounding urban areas who
reported parent -child relationship issues
affecting their mental health. Data were collected
through online questionnaires and analyzed
using Structural Equation Modeling with Partial
Least Squares (SEM -PLS) in SmartPLS. The
measurement model was evaluated for validity
and reliability, followed by stru ctural model
testing for hypothesis testing and goodness of fit
analysis. The findings reveal that perceived
usefulness and attitude directly influence
behavioral intention to use a mental health app.

development. The World Health Organization (WHO) underscores its importance across
all nations (World Health Organization, 2025), and this mental well-being is in line with
the common agenda from the United Nations in achieving Sustainable Development Goal
3: Good Health and Well -Being (United Nations, n.d.). Mental health disorders, like
physical illnesses, impose enormous burdens on both individuals and societies, yet they
often remain stigmatized and under-treated.
Within this global landscape, Generation Z (born 1995 –2009) is experiencing
unique mental health pressures, especially in Indonesia. One prominent driver is strained

2 LITERATURE REVIEW

2.1 Technology acceptance model

Originally developed by Davis in 1985, the technology acceptance model (TAM)
explains how users come to accept and use technology. The model emphasizes outcome
variables such as behavioral intention and actual system usage, alongside core
motivational cons tructs—namely perceived usefulness (PU), perceived ease of use
(PEOU), and attitude toward technology (Davis, 1989). TAM suggests that these factors
are directly linked to users’ attitudes and intentions, where a more favorable attitude is
expected to lead  to a stronger intention to use a system, ultimately influencing actual
usage behavior. Its widespread application in both academic and practical domains is
largely due to its conceptual simplicity and explanatory strength. Over time, TAM has
become the fo undation for numerous model extensions in the field of technology
adoption, offering valuable insights into the psychological and cognitive drivers behind
users’ decisions to embrace or reject technological tools. According to Marangunić and
Granić (2015), PU and PEOU have consistently been identified as key predictors, either

2.2 Signaling theory

Signaling theory, first introduced by Spence (1973), was developed to explain
information asymmetry between organizations and individuals —originally within the
context of job markets. The theory offers a robust framework for understanding
behavioral dynamics in scenarios where two parties hold unequal access to information.
Typically, one party, referred to as the sender, must decide whether to send a signal and
determine the form it should take. The other party, known as the receiver, is responsible
for interpreting the signal and making decisions based on its perceived meaning. Spence’s
seminal work demonstrated how job seekers could reduce information asymmetries by
using signals —such as educational qualifications —to communicate their value to
potential e mployers. These signals serve as indicators of quality, helping employers

categorized. Ultimately, such perceptions shape consumer attitudes and trust toward the

2.3 Hypothesis development

2.3.1 Relationships between perceived usefulness, attitude, and behavioral intention to
use a mental health app

Perceived usefulness —a central construct in the technology acceptance model
(TAM)—refers to the degree to which an individual believes that using a specific
technology will enhance their performance or quality of life (Davis, 1989). It reflects
users’ expe ctations that the technology will be instrumental in helping them achieve
desired outcomes more efficiently than alternative methods (Triberti et al ., 2016).
Alongside perceived ease of use, perceived usefulness significantly shapes user attitudes
and behavioral intentions toward adopting new technologies (Lu et al., 2005).
Attitude, in this context, denotes an individual’s overall evaluative judgment of a
technology—whether they view it positively or negatively (Edison & Geissler, 2003).
Research shows that perceived usefulness influences users’ attitudes by shaping their
cognitive evaluations of a system’s benefits. For instance, if users believe that Riliv’s
digital mental health services can meaningfully support their emotional well -being, they
are likely to develop a favorable attitude toward using the app. Empirical stud ies have
consistently demonstrated a positive association between perceived usefulness and
attitude, affirming the predictive strength of this relationship in a range of domains,

guidance, and intuitive functionality, they are more likely to regard it as easy t o use.

These judgments shape consumer attitudes toward the brand and contribute to brand trust
(Cuddy et al., 2007; Eisend & Stokburger-Sauer, 2013; Xue et al., 2020).

3 METHODOLOGY

This study employed a quantitative, cross-sectional design to explore Generation
Z’s behavioral intentions toward adopting digital mental health app, using Riliv as the
case context. The study is grounded in the technology acceptance model (TAM), theory
of planned behavior (TPB), and signaling theory, with a specific focus on perceived
usefulness, perceived ease of use, brand competence, attitude, and behavioral intention.
The study focuses on Indonesian Generation Z individuals aged 18–25 in Jakarta
and nearby urban areas, specifically those reporting mental health challenges tied to
parent–child relationship issues. Jakarta’s urban environment—marked by technological
sophistication—provides an ideal context to examine behavior related to digital mental
health app adoption. More importantly, young urban residents, especially those
navigating familial tensions, are at elevated risk for mental health issues. Large -sample
national data indicate that urban Indonesian youth aged 15–24 display a higher prevalence
of common mental disorders (CMDs) than rural peers, with depression affecting
approximately 6.1% of urban youth, compared to lower rates in non -urban areas
(Khairunnisa et al., 2024; Suryaputri et al., 2021). Meanwhile, community-based research
in Jakarta identified psychological distress and depression rates of 12.6% among
adolescents aged 16 –18, with in -school youth reporting elevated symptoms of social
anxiety, depression, and suicidality (Pham et al., 2024). Crucially, family dynamics —
particularly parent –child conflict, poor communication, parental mental health, and
disrupted parenting —are consistently implicated in Indonesian youth mental health
studies. Poor parent –child relationships have been li nked to increased anxiety,
depression, and emotional difficulty among adolescents and young adults (Rahmandani
et al., 2020; Suryaputri et al., 2021; Yani et al., 2025). In Jakarta, adolescents reporting
weaker family satisfaction or unhealthy parenting patterns were significantly more likely
to exhibit stress, depression, and emotional behavior problems (Eka et al., 2025).
A purposive sampling method was used to ensure alignment with the study’s aims.
Screening questions were used to verify age eligibility and relevant experiences with
mental health and parental relationships. In addition, respondents were required to
confirm awareness of the Riliv app, ensuring that their responses to the perceived
usefulness, perceived ease of use, brand competence, attitude, and behavioral intention

of fit (GoF) evaluation: evaluated R square, Q square, SRMR, GoF index, PLS predict,

5 CONCLUSIONS AND IMPLICATIONS

5.1 Conclusions

This study explored Generation Z’s behavioral intentions to use a mental health
app, with a specific case study of the Riliv app in Indonesia. Grounded in the technology
acceptance model (TAM) and signaling theory, the study employed a quantitative method
with a structured online questionnaire and SEM -PLS to analyze the proposed research
model. The study found out that the measurement model exhibited strong reliability and
validity. All constructs met recommended criteria for composite reliability, AVE, and
discriminant validity. Perceived usefulness emerged as a critical determinant, influencing
both attitude and behavioral intention. Attitude played a strong mediating role, while
perceived ease of use only indirectly contributed through its effect on usefu lness.
Unexpectedly, brand competence negatively affected behavioral intention, signaling
potential concerns about the financial concerns. The model fit was strong (GoF index =
0.447; SRMR = 0.071), with moderate predictive power, justifying the robustness  of the
theoretical model.

5.2 Implications

This study provides several theoretical contributions. First, it extends TAM by
integrating brand competence from signaling theory—rare in mental health app literature.
Second, offers empirical support for attitude as a mediator, reinforcing its role in shaping
digital health behavior. Finally, this study suggests potential for nonlinear relationships,
especially regarding branding cues and usability perceptions among Generation Z in
Indonesia.

For the practical implications, this study can suggest several practical strategies.

benefits, as usefulness —rather than ease of use —is a stronger motivator. Marketing

transparency, and emotional relevance. UI/UX designers should optimize usability but

5.3 Limitations and future research directions

This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, the cross -
sectional design captures behavioral intention at a single point in time and cannot
establish causal relationships among the constructs; a longitudinal design would allow
stronger causal inference and would permit tracking whether stated intention translates
into actual use of Riliv. Second, the purposive, non-probability sampling strategy and the
concentration of respondents in Jakarta and its surrounding urban areas limit the
generalizability of the findings to Gen Z populations in other regions of Indonesia or other
countries; probability -based sampling across a broader range of provinces would
strengthen external validity. Third, future research could also explore additional
psychological factors (e.g., stigma, self -efficacy, privacy concerns) and test the model
across other digital mental health platforms and cultural contexts.
