Abstract
Research aims: This study aims to systematically map the intellectual structure,
research trends, and thematic evolution of Fear of Missing Out (FoMO ) and
compulsive buying in live streaming commerce through a PRISMA -based
bibliometric review, while examining ethical implications of FoMO-driven marketing
practices from the perspective of Islamic business ethics.
Design/Methodology/Approach: This study employs a PRISMA-guided systematic
literature review combined with bibliometric analysis of Scopus-indexed journal
articles published between 2020 and 2025. Data from Google Scholar were
analyzed using VOSviewer to identify dominant themes, influential studies, and
research clusters through co-occurrence and network mapping. Research findings:
The findings reveal convergence between FoMO, live streaming features, and
social media marketing in driving compulsive consum ption, while ethical
implications remain underexplored. From an Islamic business ethics perspective,
FoMO-driven practices conflict with principles of moderation, rationality, and
consumer well-being, underscoring the need for ethically grounded strategies.
Theoretical Contribution/Originality: This study advances digital marketing
literature by integrating PRISMA-based bibliometric mapping with Islamic business
ethics. It extends FoMO and compulsive buying theory by introducing an ethical
lens that challenges performance-driven perspectives, linking emotional
consumption mechanisms with principles of moderation, rational decision-making,
and consumer well-being.
Practitioners/Policy Implications: Practitioners and policymakers should limit
FoMO-based marketing strategies and promote ethical, transparent, and
consumer-protective regulations to ensure sustainable practices.
Research Limitations/Implications: This study relies on Scopus -indexed
bibliometric analysis, limiting contextual depth; future studies should adopt
empirical and cross-cultural approaches.
Keyword: Live Streaming Commerce; Fear of Missing Out (FoMO); Compulsive
Buying; Bibliometric Analysis; Islamic Business Ethics

A. Introduction
The rapid advancement of digital technology has transformed the
business landscape, particularly through the expansion of e-commerce,
which reinforces consumerism and reshapes purchasing behavior. This
digitalization not only broadens market reach but also intensifies
compensatory and compulsive buying tendencies, especially in online
contexts (Rahim et al., 2022). Compulsive buying also known as shopping
addiction or oniomania is a behavioral addiction characterized by an
uncontrollable urge to shop excessively (Adamczyk, 2024; Thomas et al.,
2024). Although it may provide temporary emotional relief, it often leads to
psychological distress, financial strain, and social disruption (Brunelle &
Grossman, 2022; Adamczyk et al., 2020).
The rise of social media platforms has further amplified these
tendencies. Initially designed to facilitate social interaction, platforms such
as Facebook and Instagram have evolved into powerful marketing
environments that actively encourage consumption through interactive and
emotionally engaging content (Sharif et al., 2021; Acerit et al., 2022).
Textual, visual, and video-based promotional formats stimulate sensory and
emotional responses, increasing consumers’ impulsive and compulsive
purchasing behaviors, particularly among younger generations who
integrate social media into daily life (Nanda et al., 2023; Ismail & Siddiqui,
2025).
Within this context, Fear of Missing Out (FoMO) has emerged as a key
psychological driver of digital consumption. FoMO refers to the anxiety of
being excluded from others’ experiences, prompting continuous
engagement with digital platforms (Elhai et al., 2 016). This condition
increases susceptibility to social media –based marketing, stimulates
artificial consumption needs, and reinforces compulsive buying as a coping
mechanism for social anxiety (Hussain et al., 2023; Pérez de Albéniz -
Garrote et al., 2021). It is also associated with digital fatigue, anxiety, and
depression (Dhir et al., 2018; Fang et al., 2020).
While prior studies have identified factors such as materialism, self-
esteem, and traumatic experiences as antecedents of compulsive buying
(Adamczyk, 2024; Adamczyk et al., 2020), existing research largely focuses
on conventional online shopping or clinic al perspectives, with limited
attention to the role of social media marketing in shaping compulsive buying
behavior in contemporary digital environments (Vasiliu, 2022).
Consequently, the interrelationship between FoMO, social media
marketing, and compulsive buying remains underexplored. Beyond
behavioral explanations, FoMO-driven compulsive buying also raises

marketing continues to prioritize engagement and technological adoption,
while ethical evaluations of negative behavioral outcomes such as
compulsive buying remain marginal (Bashar et al., 2023, 2024).

live streaming commerce. By systematically examining how live-streaming

B. Methodology
To analyze the development and predict future trends within a
particular field of study, various literature review techniques can be
employed depending on the research objectives. One of the most effective
approaches is the Systematic Literature Review (SLR) combined with
bibliometric analysis. This method is designed to identify, analyze, evaluate,
and synthesize findings from a wide range of existing studies. In practice,
the SLR emphasizes the methodological approaches and theoretical
frameworks adopted across the reviewed literature (Paul et al., 2021).
In contrast, to achieve deeper empirical insight, researchers may
employ meta-analysis, a quantitative statistical technique that integrates
results from multiple independent studies investigating a similar problem.
Through this process, meta-analysis enables the estimation of cumulative
effects and the determination of overall relational patterns, thereby providing
robust and data-driven conclusions (Bashar et al., 2024; Hassan & Raza
Rabbani, 2022).
This study adopts a bibliometric research design as its core
methodological framework. The purpose of this approach is to uncover
dominant research trends in the domain of compulsive buying, particularly
by examining its relationship with social media and the Fear of Missing Out
(FoMO) phenomenon through network analysis techniques. The
bibliometric method proves highly effective as it allows researchers to
process large volumes of data, thereby enabling the visualization and
statistical prediction of the evolutionary trajectory of research within a given
discipline (Bashar et al., 2021).
The VOSviewer software application was employed to conduct co-
citation and co-occurrence analyses. This tool enables the visualization of
bibliometric data in the form of network maps composed of multiple clusters.
Through such visualization, researchers can easily identify core themes and
emerging subthemes within a particular field of study (Eck & Waltman,
2023).

The Google Scholar database was utilized to search for and retrieve
relevant articles concerning compulsive buying behavior. The search
process employed a combination of relevant keywords namely,
“Compulsive buying”, “Social media marketing”, “Fear of Missing Out
(FoMO)”, and “Live streaming” combined with Boolean operators
(AND/OR). The primary search string used was:
“Compulsive buying” AND “Social media marketing” OR “Fear  of
Missing Out (FoMO)” OR “Live streaming”.
The inclusion criteria limited the selection to publications from 2020–
2025, written in English, and categorized as either research articles or
review papers that specifically discuss compulsive consumer buying
behavior. The initial search in Google Scholar yielded 920 publications with
a total of 13,319 citations. These results were carefully screened and
filtered. The researcher excluded book chapters, conference papers,
research reports, and other types of literature that did not meet the inclusion
criteria (Ramadhi et al., 2024). Publications written in languages other than
English were also excluded, as were those not indexed in Scopus.
Subsequently, the titles and abstracts of the remaining articles were
meticulously reviewed to ensure that their primary focus was on social
media as a marketing instrument.

The review process was structured following the PRISMA protocol, as
illustrated in Figure 3. The initial literature search was conducted using the
Google Scholar database, as described in the previous section. After the
removal of duplicate records, the total number of publications decreased
significantly. Subsequently, through the application of inclusion criteria and
the limitation to studies examining the relationships among “Compulsive
buying”, “Social media marketing”, “Fear of Missing Out (FoMO)”, and “Live
streaming” indexed in Harzing, a total of 920 eligible studies were identified
for analysis.
This research employed a bibliometric analysis combined with a
systematic review approach, grounded in the PRISMA 2020 framework
(Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses) as
developed by Page et al. (2021). The PRISMA model ensures that the
analytical process is conducted transparently, systematically, and
reproducibly, from the identification stage through to the inclusion of articles.
This approach was implemented to maintain methodological validity and to
provide a comprehensive mapping of research trends in the fields of
management, business, and marketing, particularly concerning topics such
as compulsive buying, social media marketing, fear of missing out (FoMO),
and live streaming commerce.
The first stage, identification, involved collecting data from two primary
sources. The first source consisted of RIS-formatted files exported from
Mendeley, containing literature search results from Google Scholar covering
publications from 2020 to 2025. The second source was a Scopus-indexed
journal list (as of August 2025) in spreadsheet format, used to verify journal
indexation and reputation. The literature search utilized a combination of
keywords including “compulsive buying”, “social media marketing”, “fear of
missing out (FoMO)”, and “live streaming”.
From the initial search, 920 articles were retrieved. After the removal
of duplicates using reference management software (Mendeley and Zotero),
803 unique articles remained and were subsequently screened for further
evaluation.
The next stage was screening, which aimed to assess the preliminary
relevance of the articles based on their titles and abstracts. This process
was conducted to ensure that only studies truly aligned with the research
context were retained for further analysis.
Based on the screening results, 15 non-English articles and 85
thematically irrelevant studies were excluded from the dataset.
Consequently, 703 articles remained and were deemed eligible for the

Discussion
How Does the Fear of Missing Out (FoMO) Phenomenon Affect
Compulsive Buying in the Context of Digital Marketing?
The phenomenon of Fear of Missing Out (FoMO) has increasingly
been recognized as a significant psychological factor influencing digital
consumption behavior. FoMO reflects a form of social anxiety driven by the
fear of being excluded from experiences enjoyed by others and, in digital
marketing contexts, acts as a key trigger of compulsive buying. Compulsive
buying itself is defined as a pathological consumption pattern driven by
emotional impulses rather than actual needs (Adamczyk et al., 2020). In
digital environments, continuous exposure to online content intensifies
social pressure, envy, and the need for validation, strengthening impulsive
purchasing behavior (Ahamed et al., 2021). Emotional experience,
perceived value, and self-regulation capacity further determine how FoMO
translates into compulsive consumption (Dang et al., 2025; Anisimova et al.,
2025).
Beyond emotional drivers, digital marketing strategies amplify FoMO
through brand-related content that stimulates pleasure, arousal, and
consumer engagement (Shah & Ali, 2023). This effect is reinforced by
personality traits, habitual digital behavior, and hedonic values, which
strengthen brand attachment and encourage repetitive consumption (Ali et
al., 2022; Lervik-Olsen et al., 2024; Panjaitan et al., 2025). In the post-
pandemic context, uncertainty and herd behavior further position FoMO as
a catalyst for excessive online consumption (Gupta & Mukherjee, 2022). In

How does social media marketing shape or intensify Fear of Missing
Out (FoMO) ?
Across the corpus, social media marketing emerges not as a static set
of tactics but as an always-on socio-digital environment that continuously
orchestrates moments of potential exclusion. Campaigns translate time
pressure, social proof, and visibility cues into everyday micro-events—such
as disappearing stories, limited drops, countdowns, and live streams—so
that inaction or absence becomes psychologically costly. In this sense,
marketing does not merely respond to existing Fear of Missing Out (FoMO)
but constructs an environment where FoMO becomes adaptive for
maintaining social relevance (Lee et al., 2021; Jin & Ryu, 2020).
Three dominant mechanisms emerge from the literature. First, social
comparison is structurally embedded in social media platforms, where
curated content and visible engagement metrics intensify emotional tension
and motivate participation, purchasing, or sharing (Dinh et al., 2023; Ye &
Ching, 2023). Second, parasocial interaction and community engagement
personalize marketing messages through live chats, influencer interaction,
and collective viewing, turning marketing into shared social experiences
where absence implies exclusion (Dinh & Lee, 2024). Third, scarcity and
ephemerality are strategically used through limited-time offers, flash sales,

How does social media marketing through live streaming influence
compulsive buying in the context of digital marketing ?
Eligible studies show that social media marketing through live

immersive, and emotionally driven environment. Marketing operates as a
real-time engagement process where immediacy, visibility, and emotional

emotional identification with marketing messages (Ye & Ching, 2023; Xia et
al., 2024). Real-time interactions—through comments, likes, and chats—

collective social activity (Dinh et al., 2023; Lin et al., 2023).
These dynamics are intensified by emotional contagion, social
presence, and mechanisms such as limited-time offers and countdowns,
which heighten urgency and FoMO, encouraging impulsive and compulsive

How does Islamic business ethics principles frame and evaluate
FoMO-driven compulsive buying behavior in digital marketing
contexts?
Existing empirical studies consistently conceptualize compulsive
buying as a behavioral addiction rooted in emotional dysregulation, negative
affect, and failures in self-regulation (Adamczyk, 2024; Adamczyk et al.,
2020; Thomas et al., 2024). It is therefore understood not merely as a
consumption preference but as a maladaptive coping mechanism
associated with psychological distress, impaired social functioning, and
financial instability (Brunelle & Grossman, 2022). In digital contexts, social
stressors and emotionally charged environments further intensify these
tendencies (Adamczyk, 2024). From an Islamic business ethics perspective,
such patterns reflect violations of ḥifẓ al -ʿaql (protection of rational
judgment) and ḥifẓ al-māl (protection of wealth), as emotionally driven
consumption undermines cognitive autonomy and financial accountability.
Similarly, FoMO is grounded in social comparison, anxiety, and the
compulsive need for continuous digital engagement (Elhai et al., 2016; Dhir
et al., 2018). It increases susceptibility to marketing stimuli, fostering
artificial needs and compensatory consumption (Hussain et al., 2023; Pérez
de Albéniz-Garrote et al., 2021). While prior research emphasizes
psychological perspectives, an Islamic ethical lens highlights FoMO as a
moral risk linked to excessive dependence on social validation and material
symbolism, conflicting with wasatiyyah (moderation) and qanāʿah
(contentment). Thus, compulsive buying becomes not only a behavioral
outcome but also an ethically consequential phenomenon shaped by affect-
driven marketing. Furthermore, bibliometric evidence shows that social
media marketing research prioritizes engagement and persuasion while
overlooking negative externalities such as compulsive buying (Bashar et al.,
2023, 2024).
From an Islamic ethical standpoint, this reflects a tension between
performance-oriented marketing and moral accountability, raising concerns
about firms’ responsibility in deploying FoMO-inducing strategies without
regard for consumer well-being.

D. Conclusion
This study provides a comprehensive overview of the evolving
research landscape on Fear of Missing Out (FoMO) and compulsive buying
in live streaming commerce using a PRISMA-guided systematic review and
bibliometric analysis. The findings reveal a strong convergence between
FoMO, social media marketing, and live streaming features—such as real-
time interaction and scarcity cues as key drivers of emotionally driven
consumption, while highlighting that existing research predominantly
emphasizes engagement, persuasion effectiveness, and technological
adoption, with limited attention to negative behavioral outcomes.
Methodologically, this study contributes by mapping the intellectual
structure, key themes, and emerging trends through bibliometric analysis,
enabling the identification of research clusters, thematic evolution, and
underexplored areas in a structured and replicable manner.
From theoretical and ethical perspectives, the findings indicate a shift
from individual-level explanations of compulsive buying toward a systemic
understanding that integrates psychological, social, and technological
dimensions within affect-driven digital ecosystems. Through the lens of
Islamic business ethics, the study further suggests that marketing practices
exploiting emotional vulnerability and social pressure may conflict with
principles of moderation, rationality, and consumer well-being, offering a
complementary normative framework without rejecting existing theories.
Based on these insights, future research should expand empirical
investigations on negative behavioral outcomes, examine the role of
emerging technologies and algorithmic personalization, and develop ethical
marketing frameworks that balance commercial objectives with consumer
protection in digital marketplaces.
