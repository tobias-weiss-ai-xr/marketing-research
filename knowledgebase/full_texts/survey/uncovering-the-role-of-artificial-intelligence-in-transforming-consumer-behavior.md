ABSTRACT: Digital technologies have become an essential component of contemporary
business ecosystems, and digital transformation continues to reshape modern marketing
strategies. In this environment, Artificial Intelligence (AI) has emerged as a key technological
driver, significantly influencing how consumers search for information, evaluate alternatives,
and make purchasing decisions. As AI -based applications increasingly mediate consumer –
brand interactions, understanding their effects on consumer beha vior and purchase intention
has become an important research topic. This study conducts a bibliometric analysis to examine
the evolution of academic research on AI, consumer behavior, and purchase intention over the
past 33 years. Using the keywords “Artif icial intelligence,” “digital transformation,”
“consumer behavior,” “marketing,” and “purchase intention,” data were collected from the
Scopus and Web of Science databases, resulting in a final sample of 45 peer-reviewed articles.
The analysis focuses on publication trends, country contributions, and leading journals in the
field. The findings reveal a clear increase in research interest after 2018, reflecting the growing
use of AI in digital marketing. Overall, the results indicate that AI positively influ ences
consumer attitudes, engagement, and purchase intention, highlighting its strategic importance
and future research potential in AI-enabled marketing.
Keywords: Digital transformation, Artificial intelligence, Consumer behavior, Purchase
intention, Bibliometric analysis

1. Introduction
The rapid growth of science and technology has led to many significant discoveries
and innovations. However, AI is often seen as the leader in these developments
(Mariani et al., 2022: 758). Since the 1950s, AI has changed dramatically. Research
shows that using AI technology makes it much easier for consumers to make
decisions. It does this by lowering search costs , speeding up decision -making,
offering a wide variety of options, and reducing the impacts of sales organizations
(Huang and Rust, 2021: 35). A I, capable of human -like intelligence and solving
complex problems, has become widely adopted in many areas (Lee and Choi, 2016:
57). Algorithms are carefully designed to help computers follow instructions and
solve challenges. The use of AI has proven beneficial for both marketers and
consumers in the 21st century. It offers a fresh approach to problem -solving and
creates new solutions through a machine learning process that uses language.
Consumers benefit from AI as much as marketers do. While many of their clients
use AI, the company makes money by optimizing the use of financial and time
resources to meet consumer needs (Kim and Kim, 2017: 73).
The field of AI has evolved to a level where it has transformed the interface of
functions into an interface that is easy to use for users and focused on consumers.
As a result, consumers are ready to receive support from  AI in the stages of
purchasing, selecting, researching goods.
AI algorithms play an important role in helping marketers reduce costs and improve
consumer profitability (Yin and Qiu, 2021: 59). Today, AI is a key part of how
consumers make decisions, especially regarding their buying and consumption
habits. Consumers embrace AI because it helps simplify their lives and supports
their efforts to live sustainably. As a new tool of the 21st century, AI has become
essential and user-friendly for consumers. It allows them to use technology to their
benefit while also saving time and cutting costs. The profitability of AI in many
areas has led to its wide acceptance and use among consumers.
In recent years, the growing use of AI in marketing has changed how b usinesses
operate and how consumers expect to interact online. As companies start using
smart systems for customizing experiences, predicting trends, and creating content
automatically, the lines between human and machine decision -making are
becoming less clear. This shift highlights the importance of understanding how AI
technologies influence marketing practices and how consumers react in
complicated digital environments.

2. Review of literature
2.1. AI applications and marketing
AI is a part of computer science that allows machines to understand and imitat e
human communication and behavior. This technology has allowed for the creation
of smart machines that can think, respond, and perform actions similar to humans,
based on the data provided (Toorajipour et al., 2021: 509).
Marketers can use AI to better un derstand consumer behavior and improve their
ability to categorize consumers and guide them to the next step in their journey,
providing an optimal experience. By carefully examining consumer data, marketers
can avoid wasting resources on ineffective effor ts and focus on what consumers
truly want.
In particular, delivering results for consumer needs, ensuring proper product
production and service delivery, managing real -time pricing demand changes,
designing websites according to needs with AI applications, and making consumer
purchasing processes more flexible and easier are some of the areas where
marketing can make an impact.
AI-driven technologies have also redefined the way firms manage customer
experience by enabling seamless, omnichannel interactions and reducing friction

2.2. The impact of AI on consumer behavior and purchase intention
The consumer's attitude and approach toward buying a specific product or service,
along with their willingness to pay, is known as the intention to buy. Research
shows that AI boosts consumer confidence and intent regarding certain products
and services. Businesses widely use AI, enabling consumers t o visualize products
in a unique way through augmented reality applications. This helps them make
informed choices when they shop. Companies have adopted different AI -powered
technologies to offer consumers the best personalized options. (Pantano et al., 2017:
85; Reinartz et al., 2019: 358). The technology provided by AI helps consumers
clearly understand their buying preferences. Past studies have shown that the goal
of AI is to create programs that can solve problems like humans do, improving the
decision-making skills necessary for purchasing intent. (Qian and Xu, 2019: 68).
Additionally, research indicates that online platforms using AI offer a safer and
risk-free shopping experience for consumers (Haenlein et al., 2019). AI is a user -
friendly technology that helps consumers make buying decisions while benefiting
from various products or services. (Yoo, 2010: 92). The importance and
effectiveness of AI come from its ability to provide consumers with a large amount

AI applications can affect how consumers think and feel by changing how they

on how transparent, fair, and understandable the algorithm's outputs are. When
consumers see that an AI system works clearly and without bias, they tend to view
its suggestions more positively. This leads to greater acceptance of automated
advice and boosts the likelihood of making a purchase. This effect is particularly
strong in situations where consumers are overwhelmed with information or feel
uncertain. AI helps reduce the mental effort needed to evaluate options and builds
confidence in the decision results. Thus, trust plays a key role in how AI influences

consumers’ attitudes t oward AI -supported systems are strongly shaped by
algorithmic transparency, explainability, and ethical considerations. Consumers are
more likely to trust AI -generated recommendations when decision processes are

generative AI technologies in digital commerce has intensified discussions
regarding authenticity, privacy, and consumer autonomy. These developments
indicate that consumer trust in AI environments depends not only on technological
efficiency but also on ethical and transparent interaction design. AI -supported
personalization significantly affects consumers’ purchase intentions in digital

transparency has emerged as a critical factor shaping consumer trust toward AI -
supported online platforms. Transp arent and explainable AI systems positively
influence consumers’ perceptions of fairness, reliability, and trust in digital
purchasing processes (Maharjan, 2024).

experiential aspe cts of consumer behavior by personalizing interactions and
improving perceived service quality. Huang and Rust (2021: 38) argue that AI
enhances customer experience by increasing responsiveness, enabling highly
personalized communication, and optimizing se rvice encounters across digital

3. Methodology
The method of this study includes a three -stage process that involves selecting
bibliometric databases, identifying relevant keywords, and classifying studies by
year, country, and journal. In the first stage, m ajor indexing platforms were
examined to make sure high-quality academic work was included. Scopus and Web
of Science were chosen because they offer extensive coverage and are reliable for
bibliometric research. These databases are widely recognized in bib liometric
research due to their high -quality indexing standards, multidisciplinary coverage,
and reliability in mapping scientific production. The combined use of Scopus and
Web of Science also helped improve data robustness and minimize database-related
bias in the identification of relevant publications. The second stage defined a clear
set of keywords related to AI, consumer behavior, and purchase intention. This
helped retrieve studies that reflect the intellectual structure of the field directly. In
the final stage, the collected publications were analyzed across various areas,
including trends over time, geographical contributions, and patterns in journal
distribution. This analysis allowed for a complete assessment of how scholarly
interest in AI -driven consumer behavior has changed over time. This detailed
approach provides a clear and organized base for mapping the conceptual growth
of the field and identifying new research clusters.

3.2. The process of analysing data
After the initial database search, the retrieved records were manually screened
based on titles, abstracts, and keywords. Duplicate studies appearing in both
databases were identified and removed. The remaining publications were then
evaluated according to relevance and thematic consistency wit h the objectives of
the study. Although the initial search produced a large number of records, the
screening process focused specifically on studies directly examining the
relationship between artificial intelligence, consumer behavior, marketing, and
purchase intention. Due to the multidisciplinary and highly heterogeneous nature
of AI -related literature, studies focusing  solely on technical AI development,
engineering applications, or unrelated digital systems were excluded from the
analysis. Therefore, the final dataset was intentionally narrowed to highly relevant
and thematically consistent publications in order to improve conceptual focus,
thematic coherence, and analytical reliability. The study aimed to analyze keywords
related to AI, consumer behavi or, and purchase intention using a structured
bibliometric approach. After collecting publications from the chosen databases, the
dataset was cleaned by removing duplicates, irrelevant studies, and non -peer-
reviewed documents through the screening process illustrated in the PRISMA -
based flow diagram to ensure the reliability and thematic consistency of the results.
The remaining records were then examined by publication year, which helped
identify trends over time and track the growth of academic interest i n the field.
Additionally, contributions from different countries were evaluated to show the
geographical spread of scientific work and find out which regions have been most
active in researching AI-driven consumer behavior.

only observable purchasing outcomes but also underlying cognitive and behavioral
processes, such as information search, perceived risk , decision confidence, and

The second cluster focuses on AI and augmented reality, emphasizing their role in

measure campaign effectiveness more accurately. Overall, this cluster reflects the
growing strategic importance of AI in managing brand –consumer interactions and

The bibliometric analysis revealed four major thematic clusters within the literature

are more willing to shop online, especially when using AI -supported technology.

for consumers. This is important for both businesses and researchers. It can also

6. Limitations and future research directions
Although this study provides valuable insights into the academic landscape of AI,
consumer behavior, and purchase int ention, several limitations should be noted.
First, the analysis relies only on peer-reviewed articles from selected bibliographic
databases. This may omit relevant conference proceedings, book chapters, and non-
indexed publications that also contribute to  the field. Consequently, the scope of
the literature may not fully represent all scholarly work. Second, the bibliometric
approach depends on keywords defined by authors and indexers. Variations in
terminology and inconsistent keyword use may have affecte d clustering results.
Third, the final sample consists of only 45 studies, reflecting the early stage of this
research area. However, the small number of publications also limits the range of
thematic patterns that can be identified through bibliometric ma pping. Finally, the
study does not perform a full qualitative analysis of each article, which could
provide deeper theoretical insights beyond keyword relationships. Recognizing
these limitations helps put the findings into context and highlights the need for
further research in this rapidly changing field. In addition, advanced bibliometric
techniques such as citation analysis, co -citation analysis, and co -authorship

promising areas exist for future research. First, scholars can broaden the
bibliometric scope by adding more databases or grey literature to gain a better
understanding of AI -driven consumer behavior research. Longitudinal studies
comparing research from before and after 2020 may also show how digital
transformation has changed theoretical and methodological approaches in the field.
Second, future studies could use qualitative, experimental, or mixed -method
research designs to investigate how  consumers interpret and assess their
interactions with AI technologies, focusing on trust, risk perception, and
algorithmic transparency. Third, comparative studies across industries, such as
retail, tourism, healthcare, or finance, could offer deeper ins ights into how AI
influences consumer decision-making in different market environments.
Further research should look into cultural and demographic differences. This will

outcomes across different consumer groups. As machine learning systems become
more integrated into digital platforms, studies that examine real user data, emotional
interactions, and personalized methods will be particularly useful. Researchers can
also investigate the ethical aspects of AI in marketing, such as fairness, privacy
issues, and consumer independence. Exploring these topics will help create

and sustainable digital market ing practices. In addition, the thematic clusters
