Abstract: This study aims to examine the role of Quick Response Code Indonesian Standard
(QRIS) adoption as a mediating mechanism linking financial literacy, customer trust, and
perceived ease of use to MSME digital transformation in ASEAN emerging economies. The
study addresses a critical gap by positioning QRIS not merely as a transactional tool but as a
strategic enabler of digital transformation and marketing performance. A cross -sectional
survey was conducted involving 600 MSME owners across Indonesia, Malaysia, and Brunei
Darussalam. Data were analyzed using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling
(PLS-SEM) with bootstrapping procedures (5,000 resamples) to assess both direct and indirect
effects. Measurement and structural models were evaluated using reliability, validity, and
predictive relevance criteria. The findings provide actionable insights for policymakers and
practitioners to enhance MSME digital transformation by strengthening financial literacy,
building digital trust, and improving system usability. Financial literacy had the strongest direct
effect on QRIS adoption (β=0.360, p<0.001), and QRIS adoption significantly explained
81.9% of the variance in MSME digital transformation (R²=0.819). QRIS adoption can be
leveraged as a scalable digital infrastructure to promote financial inclusion, customer
engagement, and marketing performance in emerging markets. This study contributes to the
Decision Sciences literature by integrating multiple theory into a unified framework and by
conceptualizing QRIS adoption as a mediating decision mechanism. It provides novel cross-
country empirical evidence on how digital pa yment systems drive MSME digital
transformation in ASEAN contexts.

INTRODUCTION
The digital payment ecosystems in emerging economies have experienced notable
growth through partnerships between fintech and traditional financial institutions (Behl et al.,
2022; de Luna et al., 2019a; Yan et al., 2021). Key advancements in mobile and QR code
payments have significantly transformed the digital financial landscape in Indonesia, Malaysia,
and Brunei Darussalam. These countries are focusing on strengthening their digital economy
to enhance financial inclusion and support micro, small, and medium -sized enterprises
(MSMEs) (P. J. Morgan, 2022a; Yan et al., 2021). This digitalization effort aligns with a
broader ICT-driven transformation designed to foster competitive growth and economic
inclusivity. The introduction of the Quick Response Code Indonesian Standard (QRIS) by Bank
Indonesia has simplified payments into a unified code, potentially reducing transaction
complexities and expanding market access (Almunawar et al., 2023a; Hamzah Muchtar et al.,
2024). Despite these advancements, adoption remains uneven, particularly among rural and
traditional MSMEs, due to a digital divide linked to inconsistencies in technological readiness,
financial literacy, and trust in digital systems. These challenges reflect broader issues seen in
other domains of digital adoption, highlighting the need for a supportive entrepreneurial
ecosystem to drive economic progress. The underutilization of QRIS among MSMEs,
primarily as a transactional tool rather than a strategic marketing asset, demonstrates a gap in
optimizing its benefits for customer acquisition and market expansion (Dwi Amperawati et al.,
2024; Jenkins, Duer, and Connors 2021; Tarrant and Huerta 2015).
This study integrates the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) and the Unified Theory
of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT) to analyze QRIS adoption behavior. TAM
emphasizes perceived ease of use and usefulness as key factors for user acceptance, while
UTAUT expands this by including performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social
influence, and facilitating conditions. These constructs are crucial for micro, small, and
medium enterprises (MSMEs), where QRIS adoption is shaped by trust, digital literacy, and
usability (Azizah et al., 2024; Purnama et al., 2026). Additionally, the research employs a
dynamic capabilities perspective, considering digital transformation as a strategic resource for
companies to adapt their skills in varying contexts. This dual-framework approach enhances
the understanding of QRIS adoption and its potential marketing benefits (Herdiyeni et al., 2025;
Setiawan et al., 2025). Despite increasing QRIS research, studies remain scattered, focusing on
consumer adoption, sustainability, and sector-specific contexts. Earlier investigations primarily
addressed direct behavioral intentions or continued usage, neglecting QRIS adoption's
mediating role between financial literacy, trust, and usability, and its implications for MSME
digital transformation and marketing outcomes. As result, empirical insights linking QRIS
adoption to organizational digitalization in MSMEs are limited.
Research indicates that consumer trust, perceived ease of use, and financial capability
significantly influence MSMEs' willingness to adopt these technologies  (Dwianto et al.,
2025a). However, many enterprises in less urbanized or economically disadvantaged areas
remain underserved in the digital realm, facing barriers like limited infrastructure and low
awareness. This situation underscores the need for re-evaluation of adoption strategies and their
implications for marketing performance and business development in the digital landscape.
Overall, addressing these disparities is crucial for leveraging digital payment technologies to
foster inclusive growth and enhance MSME competitiveness.
Digital Transformation 5.0 integrates AI, IoT, and automation into MSMEs to enhance
agility, innovation, and competitiveness. It significantly boosts operational efficiency,
customer engagement, and strategic decision-making (Muhammad et al., 2025; Setiawan et al.,
2025). Furthermore, employing digital technologies allows firms to adapt resources to
environmental changes, improving digital readiness and organizational agility (Helfat, 2011;
Teece et al., 1997). While, MSME owners enhance their understanding and adoption of digital

Interest in digital financial innovation is rising, yet there is insufficient empirical analysis

illiteracy may hinder positive outcomes. Research has shown that digital tools lack strategic
value without customer trust and usability, while others argue for the positive impact of digital
payment solutions on engagement and brand image. There is a notable research gap concerning
a comprehensive framework interlinking digital transformation, customer trust, financial
literacy, QRIS adoption, and marketing performance (Almunawar et al., 2022; Dwianto et al.,
2025b; Hamzah Muchtar et al., 2024). Additionally, existing research often overlooks QRIS as

financial literacy, trust, digital transformation, and marketing performance (Brenes et al., 2019;
Ferdous et al., 2024; Karim et al., 2022; Marshall et al., 2020) . It contributes to payment
literature by integrating three behavioral theories, comparing findings across three ASEAN
countries, and expanding focus from payment functions to marketing performance
enhancement.

indicate QRIS can enhance business performance, particularly in urban and me dium-sized
enterprises, yet findings on its effectiveness remain inconclusive due to factors like low trust

QRIS adoption, and marketing performance. Furthermore, existing literature often treats QRIS

current study uses a mediated-moderated model to explore QRIS's effects on the interplay
between financial literacy, trust, digital transformation, and marketing performance,
contributing to the payment literature by integrating behavioral theories and comparing three
ASEAN countries.

structured questionnaire to generate perception and behavior data from a vast population

Forms) facilitated wide geographic representation covering Indonesia, Malaysia and Brunei
Darussalam, and was consistent with the research interest in digital transformation ((Hutchins

Data
This study uses cross-sectional survey data collected from micro, small, and medium-
sized enterprises (MSMEs) in three ASEAN countries: Indonesia, Malaysia, and Brunei
Darussalam. The data were collected between January and June 2025 using a structured
questionnaire distributed electronically (e.g., via Google Forms). A total of 600 usable
responses were subsequently collected, comprising 400 from MSMEs in Indonesia, 150 from
MSMEs in Malaysia and 50 from MSMEs in Brunei Darussalam. MSME owners were used as
the unit of analysis as they make decisions regarding the adoption of digital technologies such
as QRIS.
As definitions of SMEs vary from one country to another, a harmonization approach was
applied to ensure comparability. Three ASEAN Member States use different forms of SME
classification: Indonesia uses a revenue-based classification; Malaysia uses a combination of
revenue and workforce criteria; and Brunei Darussalam applies a hybrid classification system.
This harmonization enables a cross-country analysis of SME digital transformation.
A two-stage proportional random sampling technique was used to collect the sample,
ensuring representativeness of companies from each country, sector, and size. Stratification
was conducted based on economic sectors (e.g., agriculture, manufacturing, services, ICT, and
e-commerce), as well as company classification by size (micro, small, and medium), as these
are the primary determinants of digital readiness (Radicic & Petković, 2023; Teece, 2018).

Variable Data Instruments
 Digitalization for MSMEs involves integrating digital tools for marketing, operations,
and customer engagement, framed by the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) and
incorporating UTAUT and Dynamic Capability perspectives. Indicators of technological
readiness are visible through digital process integration, online marketing, customer platforms,
and QRIS usage (Hamzah Muchtar et al., 2024). Employing a 5-point Likert scale enhances
validity by capturing detailed perceptions of these changes (Singh et al., 2024; Usman, Rianto,
and Aujirapongpan 2025). The importance of digital infrastructure and behavioral orientation
for post-pandemic transformation in Southeast Asia is also highlighted. Financial literacy
significantly affects MSMEs' ability to utilize fintech tools like QRIS, influencing budgeting,
digital planning, and risk awareness. Enhanced financial literacy leads to improved strategic
decisions regarding ROI and partner selection, particularly in underdeveloped nations, where
financial acumen mediates technology adoption and strengthens past digital adoption models
against financial competence.
Trust is crucial for MSMEs adopting digital payments like QRIS, as it reflects customer
confidence in the technology (Mayer et al. 1995). Trust cues in online selling environments
enhance this perception, especially in low-response regions like Southeast Asia (Novak &
Kovacikova, 2024; Arndt et al., 2022; Weygandt et al., 2019). Local technology trust
differences show how customer trust influences digital literacy and adoption readiness. The
ease of use of QRIS for MSMEs, informed by TAM and UTAUT models, hinges on factors
such as straightforward adoption, minimal training, and low technical skill requirements. Many
small businesses lack advanced computer systems, necessitating simple solutions. Usability is
vital for adoption, according to research, highlighting the variability of technological readiness
across ASEAN sectors, with some more equipped than others for technology integration.
The adoption of QRIS is explained by the operationalization of intention to behave and
continued intention to use, which are the perceived usefulness, the system compatibility, and
the customer pressure (Dwianto et al., 2025b; Jenkins et al., 2021) . UTAUT behavioral
intention and actual usage dimensions are included in the model (Venkatesh et al., 2003, 2008).
The adoption of QRIS in Indonesia, Malaysia, and Brunei is triggered by policy, infrastructure,
and digital culture (Qizam et al., 2025). A composite indicator allows us to further cross
segment sectors and the economy and inform targeted analysis for inclusive fintech policy
making (Sulistyani, 2024; Trianto et al., 2023). MSME captures organization wide digitization,
involved both operational tools e-commerce, cloud accounting, digital marketing, customer
relationship technology and financial system technology such as QRIS (Nguyen, Nguyen, and

Data Analysis
SmartPLS was utilized for Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-
SEM) to analyze relationships between constructs, suitable for complex models with multiple
latent variables (Chin, 1998; J. F. Hair et al., 2019, 2020, 2025). Given the ordinal data from a
five-point Likert scale, directional scores were treated as approximately continuous to avoid
information loss from categorization, a common practice in literature (J. F. Hair et al., 2020,
2025). PLS-SEM, a variance-based method, is robust to data distribution assumptions and
tolerates non-normality (Hair et al., 2022; Henseler et al., 2009). Diagnostic checks, including
Harman's single-factor test for common method variance and variance inflation factor (VIF)
for multicollinearity, confirmed reliability, with no significant bias or collinearity detected
(Podsakoff et al., 2003; J. F. Hair et al., 2025).
This study should be interpreted as descriptive and exploratory, based on cross-sectional
survey data rather than time series data. Consequently, diagnostic procedures such as unit root
tests (URT) and spurious regression tests are irrelevant in this context. Instead, standardized
SEM-based diagnostic procedures were employed to evaluate the validity and robustness of
the model (Sarstedt, Hair, et al., 2022). Overall, the analytical strategy adheres to recommended
best practices within PLS-SEM and is consistent with recent work in the literature on digital
transformation, as well as with research on technology adoption in emerging markets (J. F.
Hair et al., 2025; Sarstedt, Radomir, et al., 2022).

The study confirms the relevance of the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), Unified
Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT), and Dynamic Capabilities Theory in
understanding the adoption of QRIS by MSME owners. Both TAM and UTAUT illustrate that
MSME owners are inclined to adopt QRIS if deemed useful and easy to use, alongside
favorable conditions. Dynamic Capabilities Theory elucidates the adoption's capacity to induce
organizational changes like digital integration. The digital transformation in MSMEs is
complex and shaped by individual perceptions, institutional trust, and strategic capability
development rather than being solely behavioral or structural. A key finding indicates that
financial literacy significantly accelerates digital transformation, directly and indirectly
through QRIS adoption. MSME owners with enhanced financial literacy are more adept at
evaluating digital technologies and integrating them effectively. This corroborates previous
research identifying financial literacy as crucial for digital readiness and participation in
financial ecosystems, highlighting its role as both a strategic resource and personal
competence, thereby reducing uncertainty and improving digital decision-making quality.

The study reveals that QR technology significantly influences the digital transformation
of MSMEs, serving a broader purpose than merely functioning as a payment method. QRIS
adoption potentially catalyzes a deeper digital integration across various busine ss aspects,
including customer interaction, transaction recording, operational efficiency, and access to
organized financial systems. This aligns with previous research indicating that standardized
payment systems enhance MSME efficiency and their participation in digital markets. By
introducing MSMEs to digital systems and fostering transparency, QRIS facilitates broader
digital transformation initiatives, establishing a foundation for adopting complementary
technologies, especially pertinent in Indonesia and the ASEAN region, where policymakers
leverage digital payment standardization for financial inclusion and modernization.
Moreover, the study confirms a mediating role for QRIS adoption, which constitutes a
notable contribution. The mediation analysis indicates that while financial literacy, customer
trust, and perceived ease of use do not directly impact MSME digital transformation, they
influence it indirectly through QRIS adoption. This underscores QRIS as a medium that
translates behavioral and organizational conditions into transformation outcomes, suggesting
that digital transformation thrives when perceptions of capability, trust, and usability convert
into actual behaviors. This perspective aligns with ongoing literature around QRIS and fintech,
which posits that implementing digital payment solutions is a crucial initial step towards
achieving digital readiness and subsequent business transformation.
From a strategic marketing angle, QRIS adoption should be viewed as integral to the
customer-facing infrastructure of MSMEs. Implementing QRIS in an integrated manner
enhances transaction convenience, builds customer trust, improves service responsiveness, and
boosts brand perception in the digital realm. This research challenges earlier studies that
categorized QR-based payment systems strictly as operational tools, emphasizing their role
within the broader customer journey and suggesting that MSMEs leveraging QRIS can capture
the advantages of digital engagement effectively.  The findings suggest crucial policy
implications for ASEAN governments and regulators. Supporting MSME digital
transformation necessitates cohesive strategies across financial literacy enhancement,
bolstering digital trust, and designing user-friendly platforms. Recommendations include
advancing MSME financial capability through focused education, improving digital trust via
payment safety standards, and ensuring that digital platforms like QRIS are accessible and user-
friendly for smaller enterprises. Historical evidence supports that collaborative institutional
strategies can accelerate technology adoption among MSMEs. Therefore, the policy challenge
lies in ensuring that the drive for more adopters translates into meaningful transformative
outcomes for all in emerging economies, balancing initiatives that foster adoption with those
that guarantee impactful results.

CONCLUSION
The study empirically validates the adoption of QRIS as a strategic mechanism that links
financial literacy, customer trust, and perceived ease of use to the digital transformation of
MSMEs in ASEAN destinations in emerging economies. Based on a synthesis of TAM,
