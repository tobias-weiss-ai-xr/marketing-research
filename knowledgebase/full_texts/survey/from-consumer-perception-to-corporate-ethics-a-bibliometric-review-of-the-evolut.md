INTRODUCTION
The rapid development of digital technology has transformed the
landscape of global marketing communication, forcing companies to adapt to
various new communication tools and channels that are reshaping marketing
strategies (Ziółkowska , 2021). This phenomenon shows that digital marketing
strategies are no longer just promotional tools but rather a means to build trust
and a company's ethical reputation in the public's eyes. The rapid development
of digital technology has transformed the  global business landscape, affecting
how companies interact with consumers and market their products and services
(Farah et al., 2024).
One of the main impacts of the digital revolution is the emergence of social
media as a strategic platform for building relationships, trust, and consumer
loyalty, while also strengthening brand image in the digital era (Pamungkas  et
al., 2024). This phenomenon underscores that consumer trust has now become a
central element in modern marketing communication, grounded in ethical values
and transparent information. By incorporating ethical principles into their
communication strategies, businesses can enhance their brand reputation, build
customer trust, and contribute to society's overall improvement (Chong & Patwa,
2023).
In a competitive market filled with digital information, consumers now
evaluate not only the quality of products but also the integrity and ethics of a
company's communication. Brand trust has become a key factor influencing
purchasing decisions amidst rap idly changing consumer behavior  (Yusuf et al.,
2024). In a highly competitive business environment, every company must be
able to anticipate increasingly intense competition by developing effective
marketing communication strategies to attract consumers' attention
(Muhammad Reza Pahlevi & Aminah Swarnawati, 2024)
Although previous research has explained the relationship between
marketing communication and consumer trust, most studies remain limited to
practical contexts and have not integrated the dimensions of consumer
perception, marketing communication, and corp orate ethics into a single,
comprehensive conceptual framework (Lee & Jin, 2019; Yusuf et al., 2024).
Another limitation lies in the dominance of a descriptive quantitative approach,
without longitudinal or bibliometric analysis capable of tracing conceptu al
evolution comprehensively (Donthu et al., 2021; Zupic & Čater, 2015). Therefore,
this study offers novelty by using a bibliometric approach to map the relationship
between marketing communication and consumer trust from an ethical and
social responsibility perspective (Chong & Patwa, 2023; van Eck & Waltman,
2010). This approach makes a theoretical contribution by showing how
marketing communication and trust have evolved from marketing instruments
into ethical mechanisms that shape public perceptions of corporate integrity,
while also laying the foundation for a new , globally developed paradigm of
ethical, trust-based marketing communication.
To address this gap, this study conducts a bibliometric analysis of articles
indexed in Scopus and published between 1992 and 2025 to explore trends,
dominant themes, and theoretical contributions in producing a conceptual map

THEORETICAL REVIEW
Digital marketing communication refers to a company's efforts to convey
promotional messages through digital channels to influence consumer behavior.
Marketing communication is a means by which companies inform, persuade, and
remind consumers, both directl y and indirectly, about the products and brands
they sell (Kotler & Keller, n.d.). Digital marketing communication is the activity
of delivering messages to potential consumers through various digital media to
influence their knowledge, attitudes, and actions towards a product (Sutrisno et
al., 2023). In a modern context, ethical issues in the marketing mix (product, price,
place, promotion) play an important role in building the brand's relationship
with consumers (Lee & Jin, 2019).
Consumer trust in digital marketing is the customer's confidence in a
company's integrity, competence, and reliability when interacting online.
Consumer trust involves the "willingness of the company to rely on business
partners" based on competence, integ rity, honesty, and company policies
(Pahlevi & Swarnawati, 2024). Trust in digital transactions involves believing that
a company is honest, technically competent, and committed to consumer needs.
For example, integrity is demonstrated through honesty and keeping promises,
competence relates to the ability to meet needs, and benevolence involves caring
about the customer's interests. High consumer trust affects loyalty and long-term
repurchase intentions (Jauhari & Nuzil, 2025).

Research shows a positive relationship between effective digital marketing
communication strategies and consumer trust levels. Transparent, consistent,
and interactive communication strengthens perceptions of a company's integrity
and competence. Digital m arketing communication strategies that focus on
enhancing vendor reputation and website quality, ease of online store access,
value-added content, and maximizing word -of-mouth (WOM) promotions
successfully build customer trust (Ilyas et al., 2023).
The study mentioned that 'digital marketing communication strategies
begin by strengthening vendor reputation, website quality, accessibility, added
value, and leveraging word -of-mouth' to build trust. Other empirical research
confirms the positive effect of digital marketing on trust. (Yunus et al., 2022)
found that the Digital Marketing variable has a significant positive relationship
with Online Trust, meaning that consumers become more trusting when digital
strategies are implemented effectively. In other words, the more effective
communication through digital media (such as informative content, positive
eWOM, and responsive service), the higher the consumers' trust in the brand.
This trust then fosters loyalty, recommendations, and long -term improvements
in marketing performance.

METHODOLOGY
This study uses a descriptive bibliometric approach to map trends,
developments, and key themes in the scientific literature on marketing
communication and trust, which has evolved from a mere marketing instrument
into an ethical mechanism that shapes public perceptions of social responsibility
and corporate integrity. The bibliometric method is chosen for its strength in
providing a systematic and comprehensive overview of the production of
scientific knowledge over time, as well as in revealing the intell ectual structure
and evolving discourse within a research domain  (Donthu et al., 2021; Zupic &
Čater, 2015)
To ensure a rigorous data collection process, this study adopts the four-step
procedure proposed by  Page et al. (2021) , which includes identification,
screening, eligibility, and inclusion. In the identification stage, relevant keywords
were entered into the Scopus database using the search string: "Marketing
communication" and "trust". This search initially yielded 283 pu blications. The
screening stage involved selecting only the keywords Marketing
Communications, Trust, and Marketing, resulting in 104 documents that met the
criteria.
The eligibility stage focused on language refinement, retaining only articles
published in English to maintain analysis consistency. One document was
removed, keeping the total number of publications at 103. These publications
were then included in the fin al analysis. Bibliometric data were analyzed using
VOSviewer software, which allows for visual mapping of co -authorship
networks, co -occurring keywords, and thematic clusters. The resulting maps
provide insights into the field's intellectual structure, hig hlighting dominant
research themes and potential gaps for further study.

CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS
Research results show that studies on marketing communication and
consumer trust have advanced significantly over the past three decades. Research
trends have shifted from an initial focus on consumer behavior and perception to
the integration of ethical values, sustainability, and social responsibility into
marketing communication strategies. This shift marks the emergence of a new
paradigm of ethical trust -based marketing communication, where marketing
communication functions not only as a promotional tool but also as a strategic
mechanism to build trust, reputation, and ethical legitimacy of companies in the
digital era. From the keyword analysis using VOSviewer, six main clusters were
identified: Consumer Attitudes and Behavioral Trust Formation; Digital
Marketing Communication and Engagement; Brand Relationship and Loyalty
Building; Academic and Theoretical Foundations in Marketing Communication;
Ethical and Corporate Communication Strategies; Advertising Effectiveness and
Message Credibility.
Based on the research findings, it is recommended that companies and
marketing practitioners adopt an ethical trust -based marketing communication

digital marketing activities. Strengthening communication strategies based on

digital communication, and corporate ethics to address marketing challenges in
the era of technological transformation and a sustainable economy.

FURTHER STUDY
 Further research can empirically explore the application of ethical, trust -
based marketing communication across various industrial and cultural contexts.
Longitudinal studies are also needed to understand the dynamics of consumer
trust over the long term a nd its impact on brand loyalty and reputation.
Additionally, future research can expand the scope by integrating big data and
machine learning-based analytical approaches to map consumer sentiment and
perceptions of corporate ethical communication in digit al media. Thus, further
studies will enrich the academic literature and provide strategic guidance for
companies in building ethical, sustainable, and trust -oriented marketing
communication.
