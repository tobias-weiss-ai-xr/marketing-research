Abstract
This study examines the relationships among Market Sensing Capability
(MSC), Business Network (BN), Eco-Brand Resonance Capability (EBRC), and
Marketing Performance (MP) among handicraft SMEs in Indonesia and
Thailand. The study addresses a research gap concerning how market-oriented
and relational capabilities contribute to sustainability -oriented brand
resonance in the handicraft SME context. A quantitative cross-sectional design
was used with 398 handicraft SME managers, comprising 264 respondents
from Indonesia and 134 from Thailand. The structural model was estimated
using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM),
consistent with the reported measurement and structural outputs. The results
indicate significant positive relationships from MSC to EBRC (β = 0.535, p <
0.001), BN to EBRC (β = 0.290, p < 0.001), BN to MP (β = 0.605, p < 0.001), and
EBRC to MP (β = 0.190, p < 0.001). The reported R² for MP is 0.973. However,

Introduction
Consumers increasingly consider environmental responsibility when evaluating
products and brands, placing growing pressure on firms to demonstrate that
sustainability is reflected in their products, practices, and market communication. For
small and medium -sized enterprises (SMEs), however, translating environmental
responsibility into credible market value remains challenging because sustainability -
oriented initiatives must be developed alongside limited resources, changing
consumer expectations, and competitive market conditions (Wong et al. 1996). In this

context, eco -branding extends beyond the communication of environmental
attributes. It can represent an organizational capability through which firms identify
environmental expectations, develop meaningful brand associatio ns, and
communicate credible sustainability value to customers (Rizqiyana and Wahyono
2020). This issue is particularly relevant to handicraft SMEs because their products are
closely connected to local materials, craftsmanship, cultural identity, community
relationships, tourism, and increasingly digital channels. These characteristics create
opportunities for handicraft SMEs to integrate environmental and cultural values into
their brand propositions while simultaneously requiring them  to understand
changing market expectations.
Despite these opportunities, SMEs often face difficulties in developing
sustainability-oriented marketing capabilities. Limited financial and organizational
resources, information asymmetry, and restricted access to market knowledge  can
constrain their ability to identify changing customer preferences and translate market
information into appropriate marketing responses (DiBella et al. 2023) . Under such
conditions, the ability to sense market changes becomes particularly important.
Market Sensing Capability refers to the firm's ability to identify, interpret, and
respond to customer needs, market trends, competitor actions, and changes in
environmental expectations (Sulaiman 2025). Through effective market sensing, SMEs
can obtain the information necessary to align their products and brand propositions
with emerging sustainability expectations. However, market knowledge alone may
not be sufficient. SMEs may also depend on external relationships to obtain
information, knowledge, collabor ation opportunities, and complementary resources
that are not readily available within the firm (Akomea et al. 2022). Business Network
therefore represents an important relational resource involving suppliers, customers,
communities, institutions, tourism actors, and other business partners (Akomea et al.
2022; Keller 2013).
The relationship between market knowledge, relational resources, and
sustainability-oriented brand development requires a clearer conceptual distinction.
Eco-branding broadly concerns the development and com munication of
environmental attributes and associations surrounding a brand, whereas brand
resonance refers to the strength and quality of the relationship between customers and
a brand (Keller, 2013). In this study, Eco -Brand Resonance Capability (EBRC) i s
conceptualized differently from consumer-based brand resonance. EBRC is treated as
a firm -level organizational capability, namely the ability of a firm to develop and
strengthen meaningful cognitive and affective brand relationships around credible
environmental value propositions. This conceptualization positions EBRC as a
mechanism through which sustainability -oriented resources and market knowledge
can be translated into market -facing brand value. Accordingly, the study does not
treat EBRC as a consume r attitude but as an organizational capability that connects
internal and external resources with sustainability-oriented branding outcomes.
The theoretical foundation of the study integrates Dynamic Capability Theory,
Resource-Advantage Theory, and the re lational view. Dynamic Capability Theory

explains how firms sense changes, seize opportunities, and reconfigure resources and
routines in response to changing environments (Teece 2007). From this perspective,
Market Sensing Capability provides the informational foundation for identifying
environmental preferences and market  changes and subsequently developing
relevant sustainability -oriented brand propositions. Resource -Advantage Theory
emphasizes the role of heterogeneous resources and capabilities in creating
marketplace advantage (Hunt and Morgan 1995) . Complementing this perspective,
the relational view emphasizes that interorganizational relationships can provide
firms with knowledge, resources, and complementary capabilities that may not be
available internally (Dyer and Singh 1998) . Business Network can therefore be
understood as a relational resource that may contribute to the development of Eco -
Brand Resonance Capability while also supporting Marketing Performance. Thi s
integrated theoretical perspective allows the study to examine eco -branding not
merely as a communication activity but as an organizational capability shaped by
market intelligence and relational resources.
Although previous studies have examined green m arketing, brand resonance,
market sensing, and business networks, these streams have not sufficiently explained
how market sensing capability and business networks may jointly contribute to an
organizational Eco-Brand Resonance Capability and subsequently relate to marketing
performance among handicraft SMEs. This gap is particularly relevant in the context
of Indonesia and Thailand, where handicraft businesses operate at the intersection of
local cultural identity, traditional craftsmanship, community -based resources,
tourism, and changing sustainability expectations. The contribution of this study
therefore lies not simply in comparing two national contexts but in developing and
empirically examining a capability -based framework that connects market
intelligence and relational resources with an organizational eco -brand capability and
marketing performance. The study consequently examines four direct relationships:
Market Sensing Capability and Eco -Brand Resonance Capability, Business Network
and Eco-Brand Resonance Capability, Business Network and Marketing Performance,
and Eco -Brand Resonance Capability and Marketing Performance. Based on this
theoretical framework, the study proposes H1: Market Sensing Capability positively
affects Eco-Brand Resonance Capability; H2: Business Network positively affects Eco-
Brand Resonance Capability; H3: Business Network positively affects Marketing
Performance; and H4: Eco -Brand Resonance Capability positively affects Marketing
Performance. Market Sensing Capability is spe cified as an antecedent of Eco -Brand
Resonance Capability rather than as a mediating variable, consistent with the
structural model examined in the study.

Market Sensing Capability and Eco-Brand Resonance Capability
The positive relati onship between Market Sensing Capability and Eco -Brand
Resonance Capability supports the dynamic -capability logic of the proposed model.
SMEs that are better able to identify customer needs, market trends, competitor
actions, and changing environmental exp ectations appear better positioned to
translate market information into meaningful sustainability -oriented brand
associations (Neill et al. 2007) . This finding is consistent with previous research that
positions market sensing as an organizational capability for understanding marke t
dynamics and supporting adaptive responses (Day 1994) . In the repo rted model,
Market Sensing Capability is positioned as an antecedent of Eco -Brand Resonance
Capability rather than as a mediating variable. Moreover, because the study employs
a cross -sectional design, the observed relationship should be interpreted as an
association rather than as evidence of a temporal causal effect.
For handicraft SMEs, this relationship is particularly relevant because market
signals may concern not only price and product demand but also preferences for
materials, authenticity, local identity, sustainability claims, and the channels through
which environmental value is communicated. Market sensing can therefore provide
the informational foundation needed to develop eco -brand propositions that are
relevant and credible rather than merely symbolic (Mitręga 2023) . The ability to
recognize and interpret such market signals may help handicraft SMEs align their
environmental value propositions with evol ving customer expectations, providing a
theoretical explanation for the relationship between market knowledge and Eco -
Brand Resonance Capability within the dynamic -capability perspective  (Partanen,
Kohtamäki, et al. 2020).
Business Network also shows a positive relationship with Eco -Brand
Resonance Capability (β = 0.290). This finding is consistent with the relational view,
which emphasizes that interorganizational relationships can provide access to
information, knowledge, collaboration opportunities, and complementary resources
(Dyer and Singh 1998). For handicraft SMEs, relationships with suppliers, customers,
communities, government agencies, tourism actors, and other business partners may
help firms identify relevant sustainability practices, obtain knowledge and resources,
and communicate environmental value more credibly.
The relationship should not be interpreted to mean that business netw orks
automatically generate brand resonance. Rather, external relationships can provide
resources and knowledge that SMEs mobilize and transform into organizational

capabilities for developing meaningful environmental brand associations. This
interpretation addresses the theoretical gap by connecting relational resources with a

community relationships, cultural identity, and environmental values can form

From a managerial perspective, business network development should
therefore be understood as a capability -building activity rather than merely as a
promotional strategy. Relationships with market actors can expose SMEs to changing

relationships can provide access to standards, training, partnerships, knowledge, and
market opportunities (Naili and Nuryakin 2021). The reported evidence supports H2
in the pooled sample, while differences between Indonesia and Thailand remain an
empirical question that requires measurement -invariance assessment and formal
multi-group testing before any country-specific interpretation can be established.

Business Network and Marketing Performance
Business Network has the strongest reported direct relationship with Marketing
Performance (β = 0.605, t = 6.670, p < 0.001). This finding suggests that relational
resources may be particularly important for handicraft SMEs because networks can
support market access, information exchange, collaboration, distribution, and
resource mobilization. The result is consistent with the broader literature on business
relationships and SME capabilities (Mesquita and Lazzarini 2008).
The result also indicates that sustainability -oriented branding should not be
treated as an isolated communication activity. For small firms with limited internal
resources, external relationships can reduce information constraints and facilitate
access to complementary capabilities  (Lin and Lin 2016) . Networks may therefore
contribute to marketing performance directly while also strengthening EBRC,
producing a capability pathway in which relational resources support both ma rket
outcomes and sustainability-oriented brand development.
From a managerial perspective, network -building should focus on
relationships that generate actionable knowledge and facilitate market access rather
than on the number of contacts alone. Supplier s, customers, communities, tourism
actors, business associations, and support institutions can each provide different
forms of resources and opportunities. These relationships may help explain the
relatively large reported coefficient for the relationship between Business Network
and Marketing Performance. However, given the cross -sectional design of the study,
this finding should be interpreted as an association rather than evidence of a causal
relationship.

The theoretical contribution of this study lies in integrating Market Sensing
Capability, Business Network, and Eco -Brand Resonance Capability within a
capability-based framework. Market Sensing Capability represents a sensing
mechanism within the dynamic-capability perspective, enabling SMEs to identify and
interpret changes in customer preferences, market trends, and environmental
expectations. Business Network represents a relational resource through which SMEs
can access external knowledge, information, collaboration opportunities, and
complementary assets. Eco -Brand Resonance Capability represents a firm -level
capability through which sustainability -related knowledge and resources are
translated into mar ket-facing brand value  (Tiwasing 2021). This framework extends
the understanding of eco -branding beyond a communication -oriented practice by
conceptualizing it as an organizational capability shaped by market intelligence and
external relational resources. Such an interpretation is particularly relevant to
handicraft SMEs, where cultural identity, craftsmanship, local materials, community
relationships, and environmental considerations may collectively contribute to the
development of market value.
From a managerial  perspective, the findings highlight the importance of
systematically monitoring customer needs, competitor activities, market trends, and
changing environmental expectations. Handicraft SMEs should also develop
meaningful relationships with suppliers, cus tomers, communities, tourism actors,
business associations, and relevant support institutions because these relationships
can provide knowledge, market access, collaboration opportunities, and
complementary resources (Partanen, Kauppila, et al. 2020). At the same time, credible
environmental practices should be translated into consistent and evidence -based
brand communication so that sustainability claims are supported by act ual business
practices. For SME support institutions and policymakers, capability -building
programs should integrate market intelligence, business networking, digital
marketing, and sustainability communication rather than addressing these areas
independently (Okuwhere et al. 2026) . Because differences between Indonesia and
Thailand were not formally tested, the findings do not provide a sufficient basis for
prescribing diffe rent strategies for the two countries based on presumed cultural,

customer communication (Raji 2026).
Several limitations should also be acknowledged. The cross -sectional design
limits the ability of the study to establish temporal or causal relationship s among the
variables, and the reported relationships should therefore be interpreted as
associations within the observed sample. The study also relies on self -reported
responses from SME managers, which may introduce the possibility of common -
method bias.  In addition, the reported reliability statistics for three constructs are
relatively weak and require further verification through item -level re -estimation,
including examination of indicator coding and construct assignment (Rampaul 2025).
The available manuscript also does not report measurement-invariance assessment or
formal multi -group analysis, limiting the statistical basis for comparisons between
Indonesia and Thailand. Furthermore, because the study focuses specifically on
handicraft SMEs, the findings may have limited generalizability to SMEs operating in
other sectors or business contexts. Future research should therefore re -estimate the
measurement model using complete item -level data, further validate the Eco -Brand
Resonance Capability scale, assess potential common -method bias, and conduct
measurement-invariance testing followed by appropriate multi -group analysis.
Longitudinal or mixed -method research could provide additional insight into how
market sensing and relational resources develop and are transformed into
sustainability-oriented organizational capabilities over time. Future studies may also
incorporate control variables such as firm age, firm size, export orientation, digital
marketing intensity, and geographical location to provide a more comprehensive
specification of the factors associated with marketing performance., digital marketing
intensity, and location may also improve model specification.

Conclusion
This study examined the relationships among Market Sensing Capability, Business
Network, Eco -Brand Resonance Capability, and Marketing Performance among
handicraft SMEs in Indonesia and Thailand. Based on the reported pooled -sample
results, Market Sensing Capability and Business Network are positively associated
