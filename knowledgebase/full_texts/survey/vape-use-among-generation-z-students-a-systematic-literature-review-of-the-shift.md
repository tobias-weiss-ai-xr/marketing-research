Abstract
The rapid increase in electronic cigarette (vape) use among Generation Z has emerged as a significant
public health concern, driven by changing smoking behaviors, evolving risk perceptions, and the
widespread influence of digital media. This study aimed to systematically review  the current evidence
regarding vape use among Generation Z students, focusing on the shift in smoking behavior, risk
perception, and the influence of social media. A Systematic Literature Review (SLR) was conducted
following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020
guidelines. Literature searches were performed in Scopus, ScienceDirect, PubMed, SpringerLink, and
Google Scholar, yielding 500 records. After duplicate removal, title and abstract screening, and full -
text eligibility assessment, ten studies published between 2023 and 2026 met the inclusion criteria.
The findings indicate that vaping has become increasingly common among Generation Z,
accompanied by misconceptions regarding its health risks and reinforce d by social media exposure,
peer influence, and digital marketing. Environmental and psychosocial factors also contribute to
vaping initiation and continuation. This review concludes that comprehensive tobacco control
policies, evidence -based health commun ication, digital media literacy, and stricter regulation of
online vape promotion are essential to reduce vape use and prevent nicotine dependence among
Generation Z students.

Introduction
The use of tobacco products, including conventional cigarettes and electronic cigarettes (e -
cigarettes or vapes), remains one of the leading public health concerns worldwide. Adolescents and
young adults, particularly Generation Z, are among the most vulnerable populations to nicotine
initiation due to rapid technological advancemen t, peer influence, favorable risk perceptions, easy
product accessibility, and the widespread promotion of tobacco -related content across digital
platforms and social media. To monitor tobacco use among adolescents, the Global Youth Tobacco
Survey (GYTS), developed by the World Health Organization (WHO), serves as a standardized global
surveillance system for assessing tobacco-related behaviors among individuals aged 13 –15 years. The
survey supports the implementation of tobacco control policies through the  WHO Framework
Convention on Tobacco Control (WHO FCTC) and the MPOWER strategy, providing evidence for
the development and evaluation of national tobacco control programs [1].
In Indonesia, tobacco consumption continues to represent a major public health challenge.
According to the Statistics Indonesia (Badan Pusat Statistik/BPS), 28.68% of individuals aged 15

years and older reported smoking tobacco during the previous month in  2025. Several provinces
recorded smoking prevalences above the national average, including Lampung (34.25%), West Nusa

findings indicate that tobacco use remains hig hly prevalent despite ongoing tobacco control
initiatives, emphasizing the need for more comprehensive prevention and intervention strategies [2].
Recent evidence suggests that subnational tobacco control policies can significantly reduce
smoking among a dolescents in Indonesia. Kusuma and Megatsari demonstrated that comprehensive
district-level tobacco control measures were associated with substantial reductions in both current and
lifetime smoking prevalence among adolescent boys, highlighting the import ance of strengthening
tobacco control policies beyond the national level  [3]. However, alongside the persistent burden of
conventional cigarette smoking, the use of electronic cigarettes has increased considerably over the
past decade. A nationwide cross-sectional study reported that most Indonesian vape users were young
adult males living in urban areas, had been using e -cigarettes for more than three years, and a
considerable proportion continued to smoke conventional cigarettes concurrently, indicating  the
growing phenomenon of dual use and the need for evidence-based regulatory policies [4].
The increasing popularity of vaping is often driven by the perception that e -cigarettes are safer
than conventional cigarettes. Nevertheless, emerging evidence i ndicates that disposable e -cigarettes
contain multiple chemical compounds, including high concentrations of ethyl maltol and benzoic acid,
which may pose potential toxicological risks to users [4]. Furthermore, vaping has been associated with
adverse respiratory outcomes. Individuals who use nicotine or cannabis through vaping devices have
been reported to experience significantly higher odds of dyspnea and other respiratory symptoms
compared with non -users, suggesting that vaping should not be considered  a harmless alternative to
combustible tobacco [5].
Beyond physical health consequences, vaping has also been linked to psychological well -being.
Previous studies have demonstrated significant associations between e -cigarette use, nicotine
dependence, nonsuicidal self-injury, and suicidal behaviors among adolescents and young adults [6]. In
Indonesia, mental health problems among adolescents remain highly prevalent. A school -based study
conducted in Jambi reported considerable levels of psychological di stress, depression, self -harm,
internet addiction, and fear of missing out (FoMO), while also identifying the presence of cigarette
and vape use among adolescents. These findings suggest that vaping behavior should be understood
within a broader psychosocial context rather than solely as a tobacco-related behavior [7].
Behavioral determinants of vaping extend beyond individual characteristics. Social class
discrimination has been shown to increase the likelihood of combustible tobacco use, nicotine vaping ,
and dual use among adolescents, highlighting the influence of social inequalities on tobacco -related
behaviors [8]. Likewise, adverse childhood experiences have been consistently associated with greater
risks of tobacco initiation, nicotine dependence,  and e -cigarette use later in life  [9]. In addition,
exposure to tobacco imagery on streaming television platforms significantly increases adolescents'
intentions to smoke cigarettes and use e-cigarettes, emphasizing the influential role of digital media in
shaping health-related behaviors [10].
Easy access to vaping products further contributes to their widespread use among young
people. Following California's restriction on flavored tobacco sales, physical retail stores remained the
primary source of flavored e -cigarettes among college students, indicating that regulatory policies
require stronger enforcement to effectively reduce youth access to vaping products [11]. At the same
time, innovative digital interventions such as artificial intelligence (AI)-powered chatbots have
emerged as promising tools for smoking and vaping cessation by providing personalized education
and behavioral support, although further evidence regarding their long -term effectiveness is still
required [12].

Although previous  studies have examined the chemical composition of e -cigarettes, health
consequences, psychosocial determinants, media exposure, accessibility of vaping products, and
emerging digital cessation interventions, the existing evidence remains fragmented across  different
disciplines and populations. Moreover, limited evidence has systematically synthesized how the shift
in smoking behavior, risk perception, and social media influence interact to shape vaping behavior
among Generation Z university students. This knowledge gap limits the development of
comprehensive public health strategies and evidence -informed tobacco control policies targeting
young adults.
Therefore, this study aims to conduct a systematic literature review to synthesize current
evidence on vape use among Generation Z students, with particular emphasis on the shift in smoking
behavior, risk perception, and the influence of social media. The findings are expected to provide a
comprehensive understanding of the factors driving vape use and to info rm future research, health
promotion initiatives, and tobacco control policies targeting Generation Z.

Method
This study employed a Systematic Literature Review (SLR) following the Preferred Reporting
Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 guidelines. Relevant articles were
retrieved from Scopus, PubMed, ScienceDirect, SpringerLink, and Googl e Scholar using the
keywords vape, electronic cigarette , Generation Z , college students , smoking behavior , risk
perception, and social media, combined with the Boolean operators AND and OR. Eligible studies
were original research articles published in Engl ish between 2020 and 2026, focusing on vape use
among adolescents, university students, or Generation Z populations, and available in full text. The
study selection process  included the stages of identification, screening, eligibility, and inclusion in
accordance with the PRISMA 2020 guidelines, as illustrated in Figure 1. Data were extracted based
on the authors, publication year, study design, participant characteristics, variables, and key findings.
The extracted data were synthesized using a narrative s ynthesis approach by categorizing the findings
into three main themes: the shift in smoking behavior, risk perception, and the influence of social
media on vape use among Generation Z students.

Results
A comprehensive literature search was conducted using Scopus, PubMed, ScienceDirect,
SpringerLink, and Google Scholar to identify studies related to vape use among Generation Z
students. The article selection process followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews
and Meta -Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 guidelines (Figure 1). After removing duplicate records and
applying the predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria, 10 studies were included in this systematic
literature review.
The selected studies were conducted in several countries, including the United States,
Indonesia, Singapore, Australia, and China, and employed a variety of research designs, such as cross-
sectional studies, qualitative studies, content analyses, protocol studies, and multi -case studies. The
reviewed articles primarily examined vaping behavior, risk perception, social media influence,
product accessibility, health communication, psychosocial determinants, and regulatory issues related
to vape use among adolescents and young adults.
The synthesis of the selected studies revealed three major themes. First, the shift in smoking
behavior indicates a growing transition from conventional cigarette smoking to electronic cigarette
use, with dual u se remaining common among young people. Second, risk perception strongly
influences vaping behavior, as many Generation Z users perceive e -cigarettes as less harmful than
conventional cigarettes despite increasing evidence of potential health risks. Third,  social media
influence plays a substantial role in shaping attitudes and intentions toward vaping through exposure
to online content, influencers, digital marketing, and peer interactions. Collectively, these findings
demonstrate that vape use among Gener ation Z students is driven by the interaction of individual,
social, environmental, and digital factors. A summary of the characteristics and key findings of the
included studies is presented in Table 1.

Discussion
The findings of this systematic literature review demonstrate that vape use among Generation Z
students is influenced by a complex interaction of behavioral, psychosocial, environmental, and
digital factors. Three major themes emerged from the reviewed studies: the shift in smoking behavior,
risk perception, and the influence of social media. These findings support the concept that tobacco -
related behaviors among adolescents and young adults are no longer determined solely by in dividual
preferences but are increasingly shaped by social environments, digital communication, product
accessibility, and contemporary marketing strategies.
The first major finding concerns the shift in smoking behavior, in which conventional cigarette
smoking is increasingly replaced or accompanied by electronic cigarette use. Several studies reported
that many adolescents and young adults perceive vaping as a modern, socially acceptable, and less
harmful alternative to smoking, resulting in increased exp erimentation and dual use of cigarettes and
e-cigarettes [4,15]. This trend is consistent with evidence from Indonesia showing that comprehensive
tobacco control policies can reduce smoking initiation among adolescents, although vaping continues
to emerge as an alternative nicotine product  [3]. Furthermore, a global systematic review reported that
e-cigarette use is increasing particularly among young adults, indicating that vaping has become a
growing public health concern across different populations  [18]. These findings suggest that tobacco
control policies should address all nicotine products rather than focusing exclusively on conventional
cigarettes.
The second theme highlights the importance of risk perception in shaping vaping behavior.
Although awareness of vaping -related health risks has improved, misconceptions remain widespread.
Adolescents exposed to media coverage of EVALI generally recognized that vaping could be harmful,
yet many incorrectly believed that nicotine alone was responsible f or the disease, demonstrating gaps
in health communication [16]. Similar findings indicate that vaping is frequently perceived as safer than
combustible cigarettes despite evidence linking smoking and vaping with respiratory symptoms,
cardiovascular risk s, and other adverse health outcomes  [3,5]. The persistence of inaccurate risk
perceptions highlights the need for evidence -based health education that clearly communicates the
health consequences of e-cigarette use among young people.
The third theme em phasizes the substantial influence of social media and the surrounding
environment on vaping behavior. Online platforms, influencers, streaming media, and user -generated
content continuously expose adolescents to positive images of vaping, often portraying  it as
fashionable, harmless, and socially desirable  [10,19]. In addition, qualitative evidence from Singapore
demonstrated that peer influence and social acceptance reinforced an illegal vaping culture despite
strict regulatory policies  [14]. Environmental factors also contribute to vaping uptake, as higher
densities of vape retailers and greater exposure to e -cigarette advertising have been associated with
increased vaping among youth  [20,21]. These findings reinforce the importance of regul ating digital
marketing, limiting youth exposure to vaping advertisements, and strengthening policies governing
the retail availability of e-cigarette products.
The reviewed studies also suggest that psychosocial conditions play an important role in vaping
initiation and continuation. Adolescents with adverse childhood experiences were more likely to
engage in smoking and vaping behaviors, partly through impaired executive functioning, indicating
that vaping is closely related to broader mental and behavior al health issues  [13]. Likewise, effective
health communication depends not only on information dissemination but also on the credibility of
communicators and meaningful engagement with young people. Studies on strategic influencer

leadership and student  engagement demonstrate that trusted communicators and interactive
communication approaches are more effective in promoting healthy behaviors than relying solely on
message exposure [13].
Overall, the findings indicate that reducing vape use among Generation Z students requires
comprehensive interventions that integrate tobacco control policies, digital health communication,
media literacy, restrictions on e -cigarette marketing, and school - and university -based health
promotion programs. Addressing behavioral, environmental, and communication factors
simultaneously is likely to be more effective than interventions targeting individual behavior alone.
Future research should prioritize longitudinal and intervention studies to better understand the long -
term effects of vaping and to evaluate strategies for preventing nicotine use among Generation Z.

Conclusion
This systematic literature review concludes that vape use among Generation Z students is
influenced by the interaction of behavioral, psychosocial, environmental, and digital factors. The
reviewed evidence indicates a clear shift from conventional cigarette smoking to electronic cigarette
use, supported by changing perceptions that vaping is more socially acceptable and less harmful than
traditional smoking. However, these perceptions are often inconsistent with current scientific evidence
regarding the health risks associated with e-cigarette use.
The review also highlights the important role of social media in shaping vaping attitudes a nd
behaviors. Exposure to online marketing, influencers, peer -generated content, and digital
communication contributes to the normalization of vaping and increases its appeal among young
people. Consequently, vaping behavior should not be viewed solely as an individual choice but as the
result of broader social and communication environments that influence decision -making among
Generation Z.
Overall, this review suggests that effective strategies to reduce vape use should combine
comprehensive tobacco contr ol policies with digital health communication, media literacy education,
and stronger regulation of online marketing and youth access to e -cigarette products. These integrated
approaches are expected to improve risk awareness, discourage vaping initiation,  and support
healthier behaviors among adolescents and young adults. Future studies should further evaluate the
effectiveness of these interventions through longitudinal and experimental research across different
populations and settings.
