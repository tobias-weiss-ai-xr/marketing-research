ABSTRACT
Entrepreneurial innovation is increasingly recognised as a key strategic driver of
marketing performance among small and medium hotel enterprises (SMHEs),
particularly within highly competitive and dynamic hospitality environments.
Despite a growing body o f research, the literature remains fragmented and
provides inconsistent evidence regarding the effects of different innovation forms
on marketing -related outcomes. This study undertakes a systematic literature
review to synthesise empirical findings on the  relationship between
entrepreneurial innovation and marketing performance in SMHEs. Guided by the
Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta -Analyses
(PRISMA) framework, peer -reviewed journal articles published between 2000
and 2025 were retrieved from major academic databases, including Scopus, Web
of Science, ScienceDirect, and Emerald Insight. A total of 78 studies met the
inclusion criteria and were analysed using thematic synthesis. The findings
demonstrate that product, service, proces s, and digital innovations, when
embedded within an entrepreneurial orientation, significantly enhance marketing
performance through improved customer satisfaction, brand visibility, market
responsiveness, and competitive positioning. However, the review also identifies
persistent theoretical gaps, methodological limitations, and a pronounced
contextual bias towards developed economies. By integrating fragmented
insights across hospitality and entrepreneurship research, this study contributes
to theory deve lopment and proposes a conceptual framework linking
entrepreneurial innovation to marketing performance in SMHEs. The findings
further provide practical implications for hotel owner -managers and
policymakers and outline directions for future research.

INTRODUCTION
The hospitality industry operates within an
increasingly complex and competitive
environment characterised by rapid technological
advancement, intensified market competition, and
evolving customer expectations (Dang & Nguyen,
2023; Seyfi & Vo -thanh, 2026; Soliman et al.,
2025; Yolcu et al., 2025) . Within this context,
small and medium hotel enterprises (SMHEs)
represent a substantial segment of the hospitality
sector and play a critical role in employment
generation, destination development, and the
effective functioning of tourism value chains
(Thomas et al., 2011). Despite their economic
significance, SMHEs are frequently confronted
with persistent structural constraints, including
limited access to financial resources, weak brand
recognition, and underdeveloped marketing
capabilities, which coll ectively constrain their
ability to compete effectively with larger hotel
chains (Alonso et al., 2018). Consequently,
enhancing marketing performance remains a
central strategic challenge for these enterprises.
Entrepreneurial innovation has emerged as an
important strategic mechanism through which
SMHEs can mitigate resource limitations and
improve marketing outcomes (Afriyie et al., 2019;
Asad et al., 2023; Qadir et al., 2022; Sipos et al.,
2025). Entrepreneurial innovation refers to the
pursuit and implementation of novel ideas,
processes, services, or marketing practices driven
by entrepreneurial behaviours such as
innovativeness, proactiveness, and risk -taking
(Lumpkin and Dess, 1996; Schumpeter, 1934). In
hospitality contexts, such innovation may
manifest in various forms, including service
customisation, experiential and relationship -
based marketing, adoption of digital platforms,
sustainability-oriented initiatives, and creative
branding strategies (Hjalager, 2010). These forms
of innovation are particularly salient in service -
intensive industries, where customer experience
and perceived value constitute key sources of
competitive advantage.
Marketing performance, commonly reflected in
outcomes such as customer acquisition,
satisfaction, brand awareness, market share, and
sales growth, is especially sensitive to innovation
in hospitality settings (Hariandja, 2025; Hussain
et al., n.d.) Morgan et al., 2009). Innovative
service offerings and marketing practices enable
hotel enterprises to differentiate themselves,
strengthen customer relationships, and respond
proactively to dynamic market demands
(González et al., 2025; Hariandja, 2025; Victorino
et al., n.d.) . Although an expanding body of
empirical research has examined innovation and
entrepreneurship within the hospitality sector,
evidence explicitly linking entrepreneurial
innovation to marketing performance in SMHEs
remains fragmented and dispersed across multiple
disciplines and contexts. Existing review studies
have predominantly focused on tourism
innovation more broadly or on overall firm
performance, with comparatively limited
attention devoted to marketing-specific outcomes
and the distinctive charact eristics of small and
medium hotel enterprises (Dang & Nguyen,
2023). This fragmentation in the literature
constrains the development of an integrated
understanding of how entrepreneurial innovation
influences marketing performance in SMHEs and
limits the ability of both scholars and practitioners

LITERATURE REVIEW AND
THEORETICAL BACKGROUND
Entrepreneurial Innovation in Hotel
Enterprises
Entrepreneurial innovation is theoretically
grounded in Schumpeterian economics, which
conceptualises innovation as the primary engine
of competitive advantage through the creation of
new combinations of resources, processes, and
market offerings (Schumpet er, 1934). Within
small and medium hotel enterprises (SMHEs),
innovation is predominantly incremental rather
than radical, reflecting constraints associated with
limited financial resources, organisational scale,
and formal research and development capacit y
(Xuhua et al., 2018) . Rather than relying on
structured innovation systems, innovation in
SMHEs is typically driven by owner -managers’
entrepreneurial mindset and facilitated by close
and continuous interaction with customers, which
enables the rapid identification of emerging needs
and market opport unities (Ottenbacher &
Harrington, 2007). This proximity to customers
allows SMHEs to experiment with novel service
concepts, delivery processes, and marketing
practices in a flexible and adaptive manner.
Within the entrepreneurship literature, innovation
is frequently conceptualised through the
framework of entrepreneurial orientation (EO), a
multidimensional construct encompassing
innovativeness, proactiveness, and risk -taking
(Espinoza-samaniego et al., 2025; Hossain, 2026).
Innovativeness reflects a firm’s propensity to
support creativity and experimentation,
proactiveness denotes forward-looking behaviour
aimed at anticipating and exploiting emerging
market trends, and risk -taking captures the
willingness to commit resourc es to uncertain
initiatives (Lumpkin & Dess, 1996). In hospitality
contexts, EO plays a central role in shaping
innovation-related decisions and strategic
behaviour (Kant, 2025; Peters & Kallmuenzer,
2015). Empirical evidence indicates that hotel
enterprises exhibiting higher levels of EO are
more likely to adopt innovative practices,
differentiate their service offerings, and respond
proactively to dynamic market conditions
(Jogaratnam, 2017). Consequently, EO functions
not only as an antecedent of entrepreneurial
innovation but also as a critical mechanism
through which innovation contributes to improved
competitive positioning and performance in
SMHEs.

organisational innovation and creativity,
transforming novel ideas into economically viable
outcomes. Organisational entrepreneurship is
widely recognised as a critical mechanism
through which firms achieve co mpetitive
advantage and successfully implement innovative
ideas, particularly under conditions of uncertainty

Marketing Performance in Hospitality SMEs
Marketing performance refers to the extent to
which marketing activities effectively achieve
market-related objectives and contribute to
organisational success. In hotel enterprises,
marketing performance is inherently
multidimensional and is commonly asse ssed
using a combination of financial and non-financial
indicators(Arenhart et al., 2023; Management &
Fitri, 2024; Sampaio & Régio, 2025) . Financial
measures typically include occupancy rates, sales
growth, and revenue performance, while non -
financial indicators encompass customer
satisfaction, repeat visitation, brand equity, online
visibility, and perceived value (Katsikeas et al.,
2016). The integration of both categories of
indicators provides a more comprehensive
assessment of marketing effectiveness,
particularly for SMHEs, where long -term
relational outcomes are often as critical as short -
term financial returns.
Given the experiential and intangible nature of
hospitality services, marketing performance in
SMHEs is strongly influenced by service quality,
customer experience, and relational marketing
practices. Customers’ perceptions of service
encounters, emotional  engagement, and
relationship quality play a central role in shaping
satisfaction, loyalty, and positive word -of-mouth
communication. Entrepreneurial innovation
enhances these dimensions by enabling hotel
enterprises to customise service offerings,
introduce experiential and value -added services,
and engage customers through digital and
interactive platforms. Furthermore, the adoption
of innovative marketing practices such as social
media engagement, online reputation
management, and data -driven customer
interaction allows hotels to respond proactively to
changing consumer preferences and competitive
market dynamics, thereby strengthening
marketing performance (Sigala, 2018).
Theoretical Foundations: Resource -Based
View, Dynamic Capabilities, and
Entrepreneurial Orientation
Research examining innovation and performance
outcomes is anchored in three complementary
strategic perspectives: the Resource -Based View
(RBV), Dynamic Capabilities Theory, and
Entrepreneurial Orientation (EO).
The RBV posits that firms achieve sustained
competitive advantage by developing and
deploying resources that are valuable, rare,
inimitable, and non-substitutable (Barney, 1991).
In hospitality SMEs, such resources are
predominantly intangible and include brand
reputation, customer relationships, tacit market
knowledge, service routines, and employee
competencies. Entrepreneurial innovation
enhances the strategic value of these resources by

Extending the RBV, Dynamic Capabilities
Theory emphasises firms’ abilities to sense
opportunities, seize them, and reconfigure
resources in response to environmental change
(Teece, 2007). For SMHEs operating in highly
turbulent hospitality markets, dynamic
capabilities manifest through rapid service
adaptation, digital adoption, and continual
refinement of marketing routines. Entrepreneurial
innovation serves as a key mechanism through
which these dynamic capabilities are enacted,
enabling firms to remain r esponsive to evolving
customer preferences and technological
advancements (Teece, 2007; Sigala, 2018).
Complementing these perspectives, EO describes
the strategic posture that predisposes firms to
experiment, innovate, and engage in proactive
market behaviour. EO not only drives the
adoption of entrepreneurial innovation but also
conditions the extent to w hich innovation
translates into performance outcomes, acting as
both an antecedent and a moderator in the
innovation–performance relationship (Lumpkin
and Dess, 1996; Jogaratnam, 2017).
Conceptualising Entrepreneurial Innovation
in Hospitality SMEs
Empirical studies conceptualise entrepreneurial
innovation in hospitality SMEs as a
multidimensional construct comprising several
interrelated forms. These include service
innovation, such as new or improved guest
experiences, personalization, and customer co -
creation (Hjalager, 2010; Ottenbacher and
Harrington, 2007); process innovation, involving
operational changes that enhance efficiency,
consistency, and service quality ; marketing
innovation, encompassing novel promotional
strategies, branding initiati ves, and relationship -
based marketing practices; and digital and
technological innovation, including the adoption
of online booking systems, social media
platforms, mobile applications, customer
relationship management (CRM) systems, and
data analytics for targeted marketing (Sigala,
2018).
In SMHEs, innovation is typically owner -driven
and informal, with owner -managers leveraging
their proximity to customers to trial service
changes rapidly. This distinguishes innovation in
hospitality SMEs from the more formal, R&D -
driven innovation process es commonly observed
in larger hotel chains (Ottenbacher & Harrington,
2007).
Empirical Evidence on Entrepreneurial
Innovation and Marketing Performance
A growing body of empirical literature reports
predominantly positive associations between
entrepreneurial innovation and marketing
performance in hotel SMEs, although effect sizes
and boundary conditions vary. Service innovation
has been consistently link ed to higher customer
satisfaction and loyalty through enhanced
experiential value and personalisation (O’Cass
and Weerawardena, 2010; Morgan, Vorhies and
Mason, 2009). Marketing and digital
innovations—such as social media engagement,
content marketing, o nline travel agency
optimisation, and CRM adoption —have been
shown to improve brand visibility, online
reputation, and customer acquisition, often
generating rapid marketing returns for resource -
constrained SMHEs (Sigala, 2018).
Process innovations contribute indirectly to
marketing performance by reducing service
variability and enabling more consistent service
delivery, thereby supporting customer satisfaction
and repeat visitation (Hjalager, 2010).
Technological innovations tha t enhance booking
convenience and communication efficiency
further improve conversion rates and reduce
customer acquisition costs. Nevertheless, much of
the existing evidence remains cross-sectional and
geographically concentrated in developed
economies, l imiting causal inference and
contextual generalizability (Hjalager, 2010).

Mechanisms Linking Entrepreneurial
Innovation to Marketing Performance
The literature identifies two primary mechanisms
through which entrepreneurial innovation
influences marketing performance. First,
marketing capabilities function as a mediating
mechanism, whereby innovation investments
enhance capabilities such as digital  literacy,
content creation, targeted promotion, and CRM
processes, which subsequently translate into
improved customer acquisition, satisfaction, and
brand equity (Katsikeas et al., 2016; O’Cass and
Weerawardena, 2010). Second, customer
experience and per ceived value serve as key
pathways, as service and experiential innovations
shape emotional engagement, satisfaction,
loyalty, and positive word -of-mouth, critical
drivers of marketing performance in hospitality
contexts (Morgan et al., 2009).
Moderators, Contextual Contingencies
Several organisational and environmental factors
moderate the relationship between entrepreneurial
innovation and marketing performance. High
levels of EO amplify innovation effectiveness,
enabling SMEs to derive stronger marketing
returns. Environmental t urbulence further
conditions outcomes, with firms possessing
stronger dynamic capabilities better positioned to
capture innovation benefits in volatile markets
(Teece, 2007). Additionally, digital infrastructure
and human capital influence the effectivenes s of
digital innovations, particularly in developing
regions where technological constraints may limit
returns. Institutional and cultural factors,
including regulatory support, tourism policy, and
local norms, also shape the feasibility and payoff
of entrepreneurial innovation.
Despite substantial progress in this research
stream, significant gaps remain, particularly in
developing-country contexts. Empirical studies
from Africa and East Africa are limited, resulting
in an insufficient understanding of contextual
constraints such  as seasonality, infrastructure
limitations, and market segmentation in emerging
tourism destinations. Methodologically, the
dominance of cross-sectional designs underscores
the need for longitudinal, mixed -method, and
quasi-experimental studies to establi sh causal
relationships. Furthermore, validated measures of
marketing capabilities and EO tailored to
hospitality SMEs remain underdeveloped, and
micro-level qualitative studies examining owner -
managers’ innovation decision-making processes
are scarce.
METHODOLOGY
Review Design
This study adopts a systematic literature review
(SLR) methodology to ensure a transparent,
rigorous, and replicable synthesis of existing
empirical evidence. The review process was
conducted in accordance with the Preferred
Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and
Meta-Analyses (PRISMA)  guidelines, which
provide a structured framework for identifying,
screening, assessing, and selecting relevant
studies. Specifically, the review followed the four
sequential PRISMA stages of identification,
screening, eligibility assessment, and final
inclusion. Adhering to this protocol enhances
methodological robustness by minimising
selection bias and ensuring consistency in study
selection and reporting, thereby strengthening the
credibility and reproducibility of the review
findings. The adoption of P RISMA guidelines
enhances methodological transparency and
replicability by systematically documenting the
study identification, screening, eligibility, and
inclusion processes.

FINDINGS AND SYNTHESIS
The findings are presented in line with the review
objectives, focusing on (i) forms of
entrepreneurial innovation, (ii) impacts on
marketing performance, (iii) underlying
theoretical and methodological foundations, and
(iv) moderating and contextual factors.
Forms of Entrepreneurial Innovation in Small
and Medium Hotel Enterprises
The systematic review identifies four dominant
forms of entrepreneurial innovation adopted by
small and medium hotel enterprises (SMHEs),
reflecting the multifaceted nature of innovation in
hospitality contexts.
Service innovation  emerged as a central
dimension and includes personalised service
offerings, experiential hospitality products, and
customer co-creation practices. These innovations
are primarily aimed at enhancing customer
experience and perceived value by tailoring
services to individual preferences and fostering
active customer involvement in the service
delivery process.
Process innovation was also widely reported and
encompasses operational flexibility, lean service
delivery systems, and the implementation of
quality management practices. Such innovations
enable SMHEs to improve service efficiency and
consistency, thereby supporting reliab le service
provision and enhancing customer satisfaction.
Marketing innovation  represents a particularly
prominent category and includes the adoption of
digital marketing strategies, social media
engagement, branding initiatives, and relationship
marketing practices. These innovations facilitate
improved communication with customers,
strengthen brand visibility, and support long-term
relationship building in competitive hospitality
markets.
Finally, technological innovation  involves the
adoption of online booking platforms, mobile
applications, customer relationship management
systems, and data -driven marketing tools. These
technologies enhance service accessibility,
streamline customer interactions, and enable more
targeted and responsive marketing efforts.
Across the reviewed studies, marketing and
digital innovations demonstrated the strongest and
most consistent associations with improved
marketing performance, particularly in terms of
customer acquisition, brand visibility, and online
reputation. This finding underscores the strategic
importance of innovation in marketing and
technology for SMHEs seeking to enhance their
competitive position in increasingly digitalised
hospitality markets.
Impact of Entrepreneurial Innovation on
Marketing Performance
Across the reviewed studies, entrepreneurial
innovation exhibited a consistently positive
relationship with multiple dimensions of
marketing performance in small and medium
hotel enterprises (SMHEs). Specifically,
innovation was associated with enhanced
customer satisfaction and loyalty , as innovative
service designs and personalised offerings

Theoretical Foundations and Methodological
Approaches in Existing Research
The analysis of the reviewed studies reveals
considerable variation in both the theoretical
foundations and methodological approaches
employed to examine entrepreneurial innovation
and marketing performance in small and medium
hotel enterprises (SMHEs). Fr om a theoretical
perspective, the literature is predominantly
anchored in strategic management and
entrepreneurship frameworks, although the depth
and consistency of theoretical application vary
across studies.
A substantial proportion of studies draw on the
Resource-Based View (RBV)  to explain how
innovation-related resources and capabilities
contribute to superior marketing outcomes.
Within this stream, entrepreneurial innovation is
conceptualised as a strategic resource that
enhances intangible assets such as brand
reputation, serv ice quality, and customer
relationships, which in turn support improved
marketing performance. Complementing RBV,
several studies adopt a Dynamic Capabilities
perspective, emphasising firms’ abilities to sense
market opportunities, seize them through
innovative actions, and reconfigure marketing
resources in response to environmental change.
This theoretical lens is particularly prevalent in
studies examining digital and marketing
innovations in turbulent hospitality environments.
In addition, Entrepreneurial Orientation (EO)
theory is frequently employed either as a direct
antecedent of innovation or as a moderating
construct influencing the strength of the
innovation performance relationship. Studies
grounded in EO theory highlight the role of
innovativeness, proactiveness, and risk-taking in
shaping hotels’ propensity to adopt innovative
marketing practices and derive performance
benefits. However, despite the frequent use of
these frameworks, theoretical integration remains
limited, with relatively few studies explicitly
combining RBV, Dynamic Capabilities, and EO
into a coherent explanatory model.
Methodologically, the literature is dominated by
quantitative, cross-sectional survey designs, often
employing self -reported measures of innovation
and marketing performance collected from
owner-managers or senior hotel managers. These
studies typically rely on regression -based
techniques or structural equation modelling to test
hypothesised relat ionships. While such
approaches provide valuable insights into
associations between variables, their cross -
sectional nature limits the ability to establish
causal relationships. A smaller number of studies
adopt qualitative or mixed -method approaches ,
including case studies and interviews, to explore
innovation processes and contextual influences in
greater depth. These studies offer rich insights
into how entrepreneurial innovation is enacted
within SMHEs, but are often limited in
generalizability. L ongitudinal designs,
experimental studies, and multi -source data
approaches remain rare, representing a notable
methodological gap in the literature.
Overall, the findings indicate that while existing
research is underpinned by well -established
theoretical frameworks and employs robust
quantitative techniques, it is characterised by
theoretical fragmentation and methodological
concentration. Greater theoretical integration and
methodological diversification, particularly

Moderating and Contextual Factors
The synthesis of the reviewed studies indicates
that the relationship between entrepreneurial
innovation and marketing performance in small
and medium hotel enterprises (SMHEs) is
contingent upon several moderating and
contextual factors. Among the most co nsistently
identified moderators is owner-manager
entrepreneurial orientation, which shapes both the
adoption of innovative practices and the extent to
which such practices translate into improved
marketing outcomes. Studies suggest that SMHEs
led by owner -managers with strong
innovativeness, proactiveness, and risk -taking
orientations derive greater marketing benefits
from innovation initiatives, as these orientations
facilitate opportunity recognition and timely
strategic action.
Environmental turbulence and competitive
intensity also emerged as significant contextual
influences. In highly dynamic and competitive
markets, the effectiveness of entrepreneurial
innovation is amplified, as innovative marketing
and service practices enable hotels to differentiate
themselves and respond more rapidly to changing
customer preferences. Conversely, in more stable
environments, the marginal returns from
innovation appear less pronounced, indicating that
market conditions condition the strength  of the
innovation–performance relationship.
Access to digital infrastructure and technological
resources was identified as another critical
moderating factor. Studies report that the positive
effects of digital and marketing innovations on
performance are stronger in contexts where hotels
possess adequate technological infrastructure,
digital skills, and acc ess to online platforms. In
settings characterised by limited digital
connectivity or low technological capability, the
performance benefits of innovation are often
constrained.
Finally, institutional and cultural contexts  were
found to shape the feasibility and outcomes of
entrepreneurial innovation. Regulatory
frameworks, tourism policies, and cultural norms
influence the extent to which SMHEs can
experiment with and implement innovative
practices. Supportive institutiona l environments
facilitate innovation adoption and performance
gains, whereas regulatory barriers and weak
institutional support may inhibit innovation
effectiveness.
Notably, the review highlights a significant gap in
empirical evidence from developing economies,
where contextual constraints such as
infrastructural limitations, institutional voids, and
market informality may alter the dynamics of the
innovation–marketing performance relationship.
This underrepresentation limits the
generalizability of existing fi ndings and
underscores the need for context -sensitive
research in developing and emerging tourism
destinations.
DISCUSSION
This section discusses the findings in relation to
the study objectives and relevant theoretical
perspectives. First, the discussion addresses
dominant forms of entrepreneurial innovation in
SMHEs. Second, it examines how entrepreneurial
innovation influen ces marketing performance
outcomes. Third, the discussion considers the
theoretical and methodological foundations
underpinning existing research.
The findings of this systematic literature review
confirm that entrepreneurial innovation
constitutes a critical strategic capability for
enhancing marketing performance in small and
medium hotel enterprises (SMHEs). Consistent
with the Resource-Based View (RBV) ,
entrepreneurial innovation enables hotels to
develop and leverage valuable intangible
resources such as knowledge, creativity, service
routines, and customer relationships that are

From a Dynamic Capabilities  perspective, the

Despite these contributions, the review also

is characterised by fragmentation and a heavy

Overall, these findings underscore the need for
greater theoretical integration and methodological

context-sensitive and longitudinal investigations
that can advance understanding of entrepreneurial
innovation and marketing performance in
SMHEs.
Implications
Theoretical Implications
This systematic literature review contributes to
theory by integrating insights from
entrepreneurship, innovation, and marketing
performance research within the context of
hospitality small and medium enterprises (SMEs).
By synthesising fragmented empirica l findings,
the study advances a more coherent conceptual
understanding of entrepreneurial innovation as a
multidimensional construct encompassing
service, process, marketing, and digital
innovations. In doing so, it highlights the
importance of examining marketing performance
as a multifaceted outcome that extends beyond
financial indicators to include relational and
experiential dimensions such as customer
satisfaction, loyalty, and brand equity.
Furthermore, the review underscores the
relevance of combining the Resource -Based
View, Dynamic Capabilities Theory, and
Entrepreneurial Orientation perspectives to
explain how entrepreneurial innovation translates
into superior marketing performance. The
findings reveal opportunities for deeper
theoretical integration, particularly in
understanding the mediating role of marketing
capabilities and the moderating influence of
contextual factors. By identifying theoretical gaps
such as the limited examination  of developing -
economy contexts and the underutilisation of
longitudinal perspectives, this study provides a
foundation for future theory development in
hospitality entrepreneurship and marketing
research.

Future Research Directions
Building on the findings of this systematic
literature review, several avenues for future
research are identified. First, future studies should
employ longitudinal and mixed -method research
designs to capture the dynamic nature of
entrepreneurial innovation and its evolving effects
on marketing performance over time. Such
approaches would enable stronger causal
inference and provide deeper insights into the
processes through which innovation capabil ities
are developed, deployed, and sustained within
small and medium hotel enterprises.
Second, there is a pressing need to extend
empirical investigations to developing and
emerging economies, where institutional,
infrastructural, and market conditions differ
markedly from those in developed contexts.
Context-sensitive research conducted in these
settings would enhance the generalisability of
existing findings and contribute to a more
nuanced understanding of how entrepreneurial
innovation influences marketing performance
under varying environmental constraints.
Third, future research should examine
sustainability-oriented and green  innovations in
hospitality SMEs, given the growing importance
of environmental responsibility and sustainable
tourism practices. Investigating how such
innovations influence customer perceptions,
brand image, and marketing performance would
offer valuable insights in to the strategic role of
sustainability in hospitality marketing.
Finally, scholars are encouraged to integrate more
customer-centric performance metrics , such as
customer engagement, experiential value, online
reputation, and digital word -of-mouth, alongside
traditional financial indicators. Incorporating
these measures would provide a more
comprehensive assessment of marketing
performance and better reflect the experiential and
relational nature of hospitality services.
Conclusion
This systematic literature review demonstrates
that entrepreneurial innovation constitutes a
critical determinant of marketing performance in
small and medium hotel enterprises (SMHEs). By
synthesising fragmented empirical evidence
across hospitality, entr epreneurship, and
marketing research, the study provides a
comprehensive understanding of how innovation-
driven entrepreneurial activities enable SMHEs to
enhance customer value and strengthen market
positioning. By addressing theoretical,
methodological, and contextual gaps, this review
provides a robust foundation for future empirical
research and evidence -based decision-making in
hospitality SMEs.
