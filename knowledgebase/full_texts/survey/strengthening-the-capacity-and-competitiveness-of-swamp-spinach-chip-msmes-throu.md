Abstract

This community service program aimed to strengthen the marketing capacity of
a micro-enterprise producing swamp spinach (bayam sawah) chips, a traditional
snack whose promotion had relied on instant messaging applications and
occasional offline exhibitions. The  program employed a Contextual Action
Research approach, beginning with a reconnaissance phase of field observation
and in-depth interviews, followed by a planning and action-with-reflection cycle.
Four interventions were implemented: improvement of product visuals through a
smartphone-based photography session, development of an official business
website functioning as a product information hub and online ordering facility,
optimisation of the business Instagram account together with a targeted
Instagram A ds campaign, and knowledge transfer so that the artisans could
manage the digital assets independently. Within the scope and duration of the
program, evaluation relied on qualitative and process indicators rather than
quantitative impact measurement. The r esults show an observable shift in the
artisans' marketing practices and digital capacity, from a reliance on instant
messaging and offline exhibitions toward a more structured digital marketing
presence, and the emergence of a cross -generational division of digital roles
within the group. Changes in sales volume, turnover, and audience engagement
were not measured and therefore cannot be claimed. The program provides an
initial foundation for improving the economic position of local artisans and for
preserving local culinary heritage in the digital era, and offers an assistance
model that has the potential to be adapted for other traditional food MSMEs.

Keywords: MSMEs; Swamp Spinach Chips; Digital Marketing; Visual Branding;
Community Empowerment.

Introduction
Rural economic development plays a crucial role in supporting national
economic stability. One of the strategic economic institutions that can accelerate
this goal is the Village-Owned Enterprise (BUMDes) together with the business
units it manages partici patively (Yarni et al., 2023). The Government of
Indonesia, through the Ministry of Cooperatives and SMEs, continues to promote
digital transformation as a strategic instrument to strengthen the competitiveness
of MSMEs, given that this sector contributes a significant share of the domestic
economy (Kementerian Koperasi dan UKM RI, 2022). However, digital
transformation at the grassroots level, particularly in community -based rural
enterprises, is still progressing slowly due to a technology literacy gap and a lack
of sustained assistance. The optimal utilisation of digital technology has been
identified as a crucial pillar in strengthening the competitiveness and market
resilience of local micro -enterprises amid modern digital transformation
challenges (Prasetiyo et al., 2023). This condition is clearly reflected in the food
processing sector, where locally rooted commodities with strong economic
potential often remain confined to conventional marketing practices.
Swamp spinach (bayam sawah) chips are one such distinctive regional
flagship commodity, being processed from spinach grown abundantly in paddy
field areas into a crispy, economically valuable snack. Nevertheless, the micro -
enterprises that manage this prod uct are generally still run traditionally, with
limited production scale and marketing reach. The marketing strategy applied so
far has remained conventional, relying on direct transactions, word -of-mouth
recommendations, and limited use of instant messaging applications without any
systematic content management strategy (Faruq et al., 2024), resulting in low
product visibility in the digital marketplace and hindering penetration into wider
consumer segments (Farhan et al., 2025).
Previous studies by Hasan et al. (2023) emphasise that promotional
digitalisation is a key factor in introducing rural products efficiently and
extensively. In line with this, Kader et al. (2024) and Rabbil et al. (2023)
concluded that integrated digital literacy training and the use of e-commerce tools
can improve the competitiveness and turnover of small business actors.
Conversely, the absence of modern digital marketing strategies is often

Based on the above discussion, there is a clear gap between the potential of
local flagship products, such as swamp spinach chips, and the limited capacity of
their business actors to utilise digital marketing channels optimally. This gap
underlies the urg ency of the present community service program, which is
specifically aimed at designing and implementing an applicable digital marketing
intervention for the swamp spinach chips MSME. The intervention covers the
development of an official website as a prod uct information hub, as well as the
optimisation of visual content and targeted advertising through Instagram Ads.
The program is expected to contribute practically in the form of improved digital
capacity and market reach for the artisans, and academicall y in the form of a
digital marketing assistance model based on Contextual Action Research that can
be adapted for other traditional food MSMEs.
More specifically, this activity aims to: (1) identify the conditions and
marketing barriers faced by the swamp spinach chips MSME; (2) design and
implement an official website as a product information hub and an online
ordering facility; (3) optimise the visual content strategy and targeted advertising
through Instagram Ads in order to expand market reach and brand awareness;
and (4) transfer knowledge to the artisans so that they are able to manage the
digital assets independently after the community serv ice program ends. The
results section below is organised to follow these four objectives in sequence.

Method
a. Approach and Action Research Design
This community service program employed a Contextual Action Research
approach, adapted from the reflective cycle of planning, acting, observing, and

reflecting developed by Kemmis and McTaggart (2014). This approach was
selected because it positions the service team and the partner as co -participants,
so that the root problem is understood from within the local social and economic
context rather than d efined externally. By engaging local partners actively, this
community-based action approach allows the intervention to be adjusted to local
realities and supports behavioural and structural change that the group can sustain
independently (Reni & Prasetiyo , 2026). The design was organised into two
overarching phases: a reconnaissance phase preceding the cycle, and the action
research cycle proper.
b. Participants and Setting
The activity was carried out at the swamp spinach chips production centre,
a household -scale micro-enterprise whose product constitutes a local flagship
commodity. The head of the artisan group served as the key informant, while
other members of the group acted as supporting informants. The service team
was assisted by students of Institut Agama Islam (IAI) Ngawi, who acted as
facilitators during observation, training, and the production of digital assets.
c. Reconnaissance Phase
Prior to entering the planning, acting, observing, and reflecting cycle, a
reconnaissance phase was conducted through field observation and in -depth
interviews. This phase aimed to map the partner's actual production and
marketing conditions and to ensure that the resulting intervention plan was
grounded in accurate contextual data rather than assumption.
d. Action Research Cycle
Building on the reconnaissance findings, the action research cycle
consisted of two stages. The planning stage formulated the solution design,
covering the website structure, the social media content schedule, and the
promotional visual materials. The action and reflection stage covered the delivery
of training, the creation of digital assets, and a joint evaluation forum with the
partner to formulate a sustainability plan. The implementation strategy followed
a sequential workflow: mapping the partner's in itial condition, strengthening
product visuals, building the website, optimising social media, providing
assistance in independent digital content management, and closing with a
reflective evaluation together with the partner.

e. Data Collection and Analysis
Data were collected through three techniques: participatory observation of
the production process and marketing practices; in -depth semi -structured
interviews with the artisans to explore managerial constraints and marketing
barriers; and documentation stu dies of existing transaction records and
promotional materials. The data were analysed descriptively and qualitatively
using source triangulation, comparing the results of observation, interviews, and
documentation in order to obtain a consistent picture o f the partner's condition.
f. Evaluation Indicators
Given the scope and duration of the program, evaluation relied on
qualitative and process indicators rather than quantitative impact measurement.
The qualitative indicators comprised observable changes in the artisans' digital
understanding and skills, as expressed during the assistance sessions and the
reflection forum. The process indicators comprised the establishment of an active
official website, the operational status of an optimised business Instagram
account, and the implementation of a targeted Ins tagram Ads campaign. Sales
volume, turnover, and engagement metrics were not measured within the scope
of this program.

Results
a. Initial Marketing Conditions and Barriers
The first stage of the program began by building interactive communication
with the swamp spinach chips artisans. Based on observation and interviews with
the head of the artisan group, it was identified that the chip -processing process
still follows a tra ditional recipe and traditional production practices that the
artisans associate with the distinctive savoury flavour of the product (Maghfiroh
et al., 2023). The artisans regard this traditional method as the main identity of
their product and are relucta nt to alter it.

The reconnaissance phase identified that the main barrier does not lie in the
product itself but in the limited reach of its marketing. Distribution revolved
around the local area, while orders from outside the area arrived only through
instant messaging a pplications and were largely dependent on the occasional
institutional exhibitions the group was invited to. Product documentation
consisted only of simple packaging photographs, product information was
conveyed manually through direct conversation with pr ospective buyers, and
there was no content schedule or digital channel under the group's own control.
These findings answered the first objective of the program and became the basis
for designing a digital technology -based intervention, comprising the
development of a business website and the optimisation of social media
marketing features (Hartono, 2022).
b. Development of Website and Online Ordering Facility
As a response to the limited market reach, the service team designed a
responsive business website that is accessible to prospective consumers from
various regions (Sihotang et al., 2024). The website structure was developed with
user-friendly navigation, covering a home page, a product catalogue, an about us
page, and contact and location information integrated with a digital map, so that
prospective buyers can place orders without having to meet the artisans in person.
The website was intended to function not only as an information medium but also
as an instrument of business credibility in the eyes of new consumers and
wholesale partners (Muqorobin, 2024). As part of the product catalogue content,

c. Visual Branding and Instagram Ads Implementation
Because the website catalogue and the paid advertising materials both
depend on product imagery, the third objective began with improving the visual
aspect of the product. The previous documentation, which consisted only of
simple packaging photographs, wa s replaced through a photography session
using a smartphone camera and assisted by students in product styling (Putri et
al., 2024). The session applied basic techniques that require no additional
equipment or budget, namely natural lighting, a clean and consistent background,
and close -up angles that show the texture of the chips. The output was a
consistent set of product visuals together with a redesigned package label, as
shown in Figure 2.

The digital promotion component was implemented through Instagram to
support the dissemination of business -related content and increase the visibility
of the participants' products. The promotion was conducted using the participants'
Instagram business acc ounts. However, the specific account name, campaign
duration, and geographic targeting settings of the Instagram Ads campaign were
not systematically documented during the program. Therefore, these details are
not reported quantitatively in this study. The advertising activity was integrated
with the content management assistance provided during the program, enabling
participants to apply the promotional materials developed during the mentoring
process.
d. Capacity Transfer and Independent Digital Management
To address the fourth objective, the implementation team held hands -on
practice sessions with the artisans on how to upload content, respond to
prospective buyers' messages, and update product information on the website
independently. The assistance was ca rried out gradually through a guided -
practice approach whose intensity was progressively reduced as the artisans'
confidence increased.
During this process, a difference emerged in the speed of technology
adaptation across generations within the artisan group. Relatively younger
members tended to understand and practise digital content management more
quickly, while older -generation artisa ns, who hold the core expertise in the
production process and the traditional recipe, needed a longer adaptation period.

Discussion
The findings from the reconnaissance and planning stage indicate that a
digital intervention is difficult to separate from a participatory approach that
positions artisans as subjects, not merely objects, of assistance. The Contextual
Action Research approach adapted from Kemmis and McTaggart (2014) enabled
the service team to recognise that the root problem did not lie in production
practice but in limited marketing capacity, so that the intervention did not need
to disturb the traditional recipe that the artisans regard as their product identity

et al. (2025) that many local food MSMEs in Indonesia have viable products yet
develop slowly because of limited innovation in promotion and digital product
introduction.
The improvement of visual branding through a smartphone -based product
photography session functioned as an accessible entry point into the MSME's
digital marketing strategy. This is in line with Putri et al. (2024), who report that
product photography usin g a smartphone camera can raise visual appeal while
keeping the cost of content production low for MSME actors with limited
promotional budgets. Consistent visuals also provide a foundation for a more
professional brand identity, as emphasised by Annisa Fa rihatul (2025) in the
context of brand engagement strategy through social media. Within this program,
however, the effect of the improved visuals on consumer perception was not
measured, so this relationship is stated as a plausible mechanism rather than a
demonstrated outcome.
Building an official website as a digital storefront is consistent with the
direction suggested by Hartono (2022), namely that web -based marketing
optimisation can support the expansion of MSME market share. It also reflects
the digital marketing concept of Chaffey and Ellis-Chadwick (2019), in which a
website functions as a central instrument for understanding and serving customer
needs in a more measurable way. Beyond its informational function, a website
can also operate as an instrument of business cred ibility for new consumers and
wholesale partners, as noted by Sihotang et al. (2024) and Muqorobin (2024)
regarding landing pages and website -based information systems in village
product marketing. Complementing this digital credibility, formal product
legality such as halal certification remains an important agenda for traditional
food micro -enterprises in order to strengthen consumer confidence and widen
market opportunities (Prasetya et al., 2023); this element has not yet been
addressed by the present p rogram and is proposed as a follow -up step.
In terms of social media, the optimisation of the business Instagram account
and the implementation of Instagram Ads represent an attempt to reach
consumers more selectively than conventional promotion allows, through a
structured visual content strategy. This direction is consistent with the concept of
social media put forward by Kaplan and Haenlein (2010) concerning the role of

The implementation of independent digital content management assistance

of digital assets itself. In line with the principle of community empowerment, the
artisans' growing ability to operate the website and the business Instagram

remains with the partner rather than toward a ceremonial activity. This principle
of self-reliance through knowledge transfer aligns with participatory community
empowerment models, which emphasise active local engagement to support
long-term program sustainability (Dewi Susilo Reni & Eko Prasetiyo, 2025), and
with the argument that integrated rather than partial digit al literacy training and
digital marketing tools are more relevant to the competitiveness of small business
actors (Hasan et al., 2023; Kader et al., 2024; Rabbil et al., 2023). Whether this
capacity is retained after the program ends can only be verified through follow-
up observation.
Overall, the combination of website, social media, and targeted advertising
was designed to form a complementary digital marketing ecosystem, in which
the website serves as a credible information hub while Instagram functions as an
acquisition and interact ion channel with prospective consumers. This design
corresponds with Prasetyawati et al. (2025), who argue that an innovative and
integrated marketing strategy is central to developing local MSME products so
that they do not stagnate. In a broader framewor k, the activity also supports the
role of BUMDes as a driver of the village economy (Yarni et al., 2023) and

This finding also needs to be read within the broader context of the digital
divide in Indonesia. Although APJII (2024) data show high national internet

and sustained assistance for business groups with limited technological literacy.
The cross-generational gap in the speed of technology adaptation observed
during the assistance process points to the relevance of a cross -generational
collaborative strategy, in which younger members act as day -to-day digital
admins while senior artisans remain the guardians of product quality and
authenticity. Such a division of roles proved more realistic than expecting all
group members to master digital competence equally within a short time, and it
aligns with the principle of community empowerment th at emphasises
recognition of the diverse capacities of group members.
In practical terms, the activity indicates that household -scale traditional
food MSMEs can begin to utilise digital marketing without large investment,
provided that adequate technical assistance is available. The four -element
assistance model, namely impr oving product visuals, building a website,
optimising social media, and independent knowledge transfer, has the potential
to be adapted by students or other assisting institutions for similar MSMEs, given
that its stages are simple and do not require advan ced technical expertise. This
potential, however, still requires testing on partners with different characteristics
before it can be treated as a replicable model.
The activity also carries implications for village economic empowerment
policy, particularly in encouraging synergy between BUMDes, higher education
institutions, and micro -business groups. Village governments may consider
allocating part of their economic  empowerment budget to support sustained
digital marketing assistance, given the qualitative changes observed in the
marketing practices of local MSMEs through this program. Regular collaboration

Several limitations should be acknowledged. The evaluation in this
program relied on qualitative and process indicators, so changes in turnover,
market reach, and consumer loyalty could not be measured; the relatively short
duration of the assistance did not allow long-term impact to be observed; and the
focus on a single partner group limits the generalisation of these findings to other
traditional food MSMEs with different socio -economic characteristics. In
addition, the age and technology literacy gap am ong older -generation artisans
remains a potential obstacle to the independent management of digital assets after
the program ends. Thorough knowledge transfer and continued assistance are
therefore essential if the results of this intervention are to be in ternalised as
lasting capacity for the artisan group, and future activities would benefit from a
measurement instrument that records sales and engagement data before and after
the intervention.

Conclusion
The assistance provided to the swamp spinach chips MSME through a
digital marketing strategy produced an observable change in the marketing
practices of the artisan group. The four objectives of the program were addressed:
the initial marketing barriers we re identified as limited market reach rather than
production quality; an official website was developed as an information hub and
online ordering facility; product visuals were improved and a targeted Instagram
Ads campaign was implemented alongside an opt imised business Instagram
account; and knowledge transfer was carried out, resulting in a cross-generational
division of digital roles within the group. Within the scope and duration of this
program, evaluation relied on qualitative and process indicators,  so the effect of
the intervention on sales volume, turnover, and audience engagement has not
been measured and cannot yet be claimed.
For the sustainability of the program, it is recommended that: (1) the artisan
group receive continued assistance in reading and analysing the business
account's performance data, so that the impact of the intervention can be
measured empirically; (2) mark eting channels be expanded to marketplace
platforms in order to reach a wider consumer segment; (3) product legality be
strengthened through the processing of distribution permits and halal
