Abstract
Digital marketing has become increasingly important to the competitiveness and
sustainability of small -scale businesses in Nigeria. However, the existing literature remains
fragmented, with many studies treating the adoption of social media or other digital tools as
evidence of effective digital marketing without adequately explaining how digital activities
are converted into measurable business outcomes. This article present s an integrative review
of theoretical and empirical literature on the relationship between digital marketing and the
performance of small -scale businesses in Nigeria. The review draws primarily on studies
published between 2021 and July 2026, while incorp orating relevant foundational literature
on digital marketing, technology adoption and organisational capabilities. The evidence
indicates that social media marketing, e -commerce, online advertising, mobile marketing,
email marketing and digital customer e ngagement can improve market visibility, customer
acquisition, customer relationships, sales growth, competitiveness and organisational
resilience. Nevertheless, these benefits are conditional on digital literacy, content quality,
customer trust, analytica l capability, infrastructure reliability, payment systems and order -
fulfilment capacity. The article identifies significant methodological limitations in the
literature, including reliance on cross-sectional surveys, self-reported performance measures,
single-state samples and limited attention to the mechanisms linking digital adoption with
financial outcomes. To address these limitations, the article develops the Digital Marketing
Performance Conversion Framework, which explains business performance as th e outcome
of a sequential process involving digital access, strategic adoption, marketing conversion
capability, customer response and value realisation. The framework advances the literature
by distinguishing digital presence from performance -generating d igital capability and by
introducing conversion friction as a major explanation for variations in outcomes among
Nigerian small businesses. The article concludes with implications for business owners,
policymakers and future research.
Keywords: Digital marketing; small-scale businesses; SMEs; business performance; digital
capability; customer trust; e-commerce; Nigeria

1. Introduction
Small-scale businesses occupy an important position in Nigeria’s economic and
entrepreneurial landscape. They support employment creation, household income, innovation
and the distribution of goods and services across formal and informal markets. Despite their
importance, many small businesses oper ate under conditions characterised by limited
financial resources, weak infrastructure, restricted market access and intense competition.
These constraints have increased interest in digital marketing as a relatively flexible means
through which firms may reach customers, build relationships and compete beyond their
immediate geographical locations.
Digital marketing refers to the strategic use of internet -enabled technologies and digital
channels to create, communicate and deliver value to customers. It in cludes social media
marketing, search-engine marketing, online advertising, email marketing, mobile marketing,
content marketing, e -commerce, websites and customer relationship technologies. Unlike
traditional marketing, digital marketing permits more inte ractive communication, rapid
feedback, audience targeting and the collection of customer -related data. These features may
be particularly valuable to small businesses that lack the resources required for large -scale
advertising.
The growing use of platform s such as WhatsApp, Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and online
marketplaces has lowered some of the barriers associated with customer communication and
product promotion. For example, a World Bank survey found that WhatsApp was used by a
substantial proportion  of surveyed Nigerian micro, small and medium -sized enterprises to
market goods and services. However, access to a digital platform does not automatically
indicate that a firm possesses an effective digital marketing capability. A business may
maintain an active social media account without generating qualified customer enquiries,
completing online transactions or achieving sustainable improvements in profitability.
Recent Nigerian evidence demonstrates the need for a more nuanced understanding of the
digital marketing –performance relationship. Ayokunmi et al. (2026), using data from 370
small and medium -sized enterprises in Oyo State, found that e -commerce implementation
improved enterprise performance. Nevertheless, only informativeness and interactivity
among the examined dimensions of social media marketing significantly strengthened the
relationship. Personalisation, trendiness and electronic word of mouth did not produce
significant moderating effects. This finding indicates that digital marketing pract ices do not
create equal value merely because they are technologically enabled.
Similarly, evidence from South -West Nigeria suggests that digital transformation can
enhance customer experience and analytical capability, which subsequently contribute to
financial management and performance outcomes. Qualitative research has also shown that
digital communication technologies can strengthen the resilience of Nigerian small
businesses by facilitating collaboration, maintaining business relationships and enablin g
greater flexibility during periods of disruption. These findings indicate that digital marketing
may influence performance through several intermediate mechanisms rather than through a
simple direct relationship.
Despite the growing volume of research, three major gaps remain. First, much of the Nigerian
literature concentrates on whether businesses use digital tools rather than how effectively
those tools are integrated into marketing and operational processes. Second, studies

capabilities required to generate measurable business value.
2. Review Approach
This study adopted a structured integrative review approach. The integrative method was
considered appropriate because the literature contains quantitative studies, qualitative
investigations, conceptual contributions and studies employing different definitions of digital
marketing and enterprise performance. Rather than limiting the review t o a single research
design, the approach permitted the synthesis of complementary evidence and the
development of a theory-informed conceptual framework.
The review focused primarily on literature published between 2021 and July 2026. Earlier
studies were retained where they provided important theoretical foundations or established
concepts in digital marketing, technology adoption and strategic capability.  Searches were
conducted using combinations of terms including digital marketing , small-scale business ,
small business performance, SME performance, social media marketing, e-commerce, digital
capability, Nigeria, Africa and emerging economies . Relevant li terature was identified
through scholarly databases, publisher platforms and academic indexing services.
Studies were considered relevant where they examined at least one digital marketing practice
or closely related digital business capability and linked it to customer, market, operational,
financial or resilience outcomes. Nigerian studies received priority, while evidence from
other African and emerging economies was used to strengthen theoretical interpretation and
identify transferable mechanisms. Stud ies concerned solely with individual consumer
behaviour, without implications for small-business performance, were not prioritised.
The evidence was synthesised thematically around digital adoption, customer engagement,
market reach, transactional capabili ty, analytics, financial performance, resilience and
contextual constraints. As an integrative review, the study does not estimate a pooled effect
size. Its objective is explanatory and theory-building rather than meta-analytical.
3. Conceptualising Digital Marketing in Small-Scale Businesses
Digital marketing should not be reduced to the ownership of a social media account or the
occasional publication of promotional content. Such measures capture digital presence but
provide limited information about the strategic quality of a firm’s marketing activities.
Effective digital marketing involves the coordinated use of digital channels to identify
customers, communicate value, stimulate engagement, facilitate transactions and maintain
profitable relationships.

4. Theoretical Foundations
4.1 Resource-Based View
The Resource-Based View argues that differences in organisational performance arise partly
from differences in the resources and ca pabilities controlled by firms (Barney, 1991).
Resources can generate sustained competitive advantage where they are valuable, scarce,
difficult to imitate and not easily replaced.
Digital technologies are widely available and may therefore not constitute rare resources by
themselves. A social media account, website or online advertising platform can be accessed
by competing businesses. The strategic advantage lies in the capability to combine these tools
with customer knowledge, creative content, analytica l competence, rapid response systems
and effective service delivery. Accordingly, digital marketing capability may become a
valuable intangible resource when it is embedded within a firm’s routines and customer
relationships.

The Resource -Based View helps explain why firms using the same digital platform may
achieve different outcomes. One business may employ Instagram mainly to publish product
photographs, whereas another may integrate customer data, content experimentation,
response management, online pay ment and after -sales communication. Although both
businesses have adopted the same platform, their capability levels differ substantially.
4.2 Dynamic Capabilities Theory
Dynamic capabilities refer to the ability of a firm to identify opportunities, mobilise resources
and reconfigure its operations in response to environmental change (Teece, 2007). This
perspective is relevant because digital platforms, customer preferences and online
competition change rapidly.
Small businesses require the ability to ident ify emerging customer behaviours, assess the
effectiveness of digital channels and adjust their marketing strategies. Firms must also learn
from digital feedback and reorganise their processes where necessary. Matarazzo et al. (2021)
found that digital transformation can support customer value creation when firms develop the
capabilities required to integrate technologies into organisational processes. Digital tools
therefore become useful not simply through adoption but through continuous learning and
adaptation.
The dynamic capabilities perspective also explains why digital marketing may strengthen
resilience. During disruptions, businesses with established digital communication channels
may maintain relationships, identify alternative customers and adjust  their delivery or sales
processes more rapidly. Ashiru et al. (2023) found that emerging digital communication
technologies supported resilience among Nigerian SMEs through relationship -building,
collaboration, process diversification and supply-chain flexibility.
4.3 Technology–Organisation–Environment Framework
The Technology –Organisation–Environment framework developed by Tornatzky and
Fleischer (1990) explains technology adoption through three contextual dimensions.
The technological dimension includes  perceived usefulness, compatibility, accessibility and
complexity. The organisational dimension incorporates management support, employee
competence, available resources and strategic readiness. The environmental dimension
includes customer pressure, competition, infrastructure, regulation and external support.
The framework is particularly relevant to Nigeria because the effectiveness of digital
marketing may depend on conditions outside the direct control of an individual business.
Internet access, elect ricity, payment infrastructure, logistics and customers’ willingness to
transact online can influence the benefits obtained from digital investment. Recent research
combining the Technology –Organisation–Environment framework with the Resource -Based
View co nfirms that digital performance is shaped by both internal capability and external
conditions.

5. Digital Marketing and Small-Business Performance
5.1 Market Visibility and Customer Reach
One of the most immediate benefits of digital marketing is increased visibility. Traditional
marketing often restricts small businesses to customers within their physical environment.
Digital platforms  reduce some geographical limitations by enabling businesses to display
products, communicate services and interact with customers across wider markets.
Social media is particularly important because it combines content distribution with direct
communication. Customers may discover products through posts, recommendations, shared
content or paid advertisements. Businesses may also receive immediate responses that
provide information about customer needs.
Research among Nigerian SMEs generally reports a posit ive relationship between online
marketing and competitiveness. Awotunde et al. (2024) found that online marketing was
positively related to SME competitiveness and that information and communication
technology initiatives supported business growth. However , increased reach should not be
interpreted automatically as increased performance. Visibility is an intermediate outcome. Its
financial value depends on the relevance of the audience reached and the business’s ability to
convert attention into customer action.
5.2 Customer Engagement and Relationship Development
Digital marketing changes communication from a predominantly one -way process to a more
interactive relationship. Customers may ask questions, request additional information,
comment on products and  communicate their experiences. These interactions provide
opportunities for firms to improve customer knowledge and develop stronger relationships.
Interactivity appears to be particularly important in the Nigerian context. Ayokunmi et al.
(2026) found th at interactivity and informativeness were the social media dimensions that
significantly strengthened the relationship between e -commerce implementation and SME
performance. This suggests that useful information and meaningful communication may be
more commercially valuable than merely producing fashionable content.
Engagement should therefore be evaluated by its quality rather than only by visible social
media indicators. A high number of followers or reactions may not correspond with customer
acquisition or revenue. More meaningful indicators include enquiries, response rates, repeat
interactions, qualified leads, conversions and customer retention.

5.3 Customer Acquisition and Sales Growth
Digital marketing may support customer acquisition by improving di scoverability and
reducing communication barriers. Online advertisements can target audiences using
demographic, geographic and behavioural characteristics. Content marketing may attract
customers by providing useful information, while customer reviews and  recommendations
may reduce uncertainty.
Social commerce has further reduced the distinction between communication and selling. A
customer may discover a product, request information, negotiate, make payment and arrange
delivery through interconnected digital channels. This process is particularly relevant to small
businesses that operate without sophisticated websites.
The contribution of digital marketing to sales depends on the strength of the entire customer
journey. Marketing may generate enquiries without producing revenue where businesses
respond slowly, provide unclear information or lack dependable payment and delivery
arrangements. Consequently, sales growth should be regarded as the outcome of an integrated
process rather than the direct result of online exposure.
5.4 Customer Experience and Data-Based Decision-Making
Digital marketing technologies generate data that may improve decisions. Businesses can
examine content reach, audience engagement, customer enquiries, advertising results and
purchase behaviour. Such information may help managers identify effective products,
customer segments and promotional messages.
However, access to digital data does not necessarily produce useful knowledge. Firms require
analytical capability to interpret info rmation and translate it into decisions. This capability
may be difficult for very small businesses because of limited training, time and technical
resources.
Ebhota et al. (2024), using data from 825 SMEs in South -West Nigeria, found that digital
transformation enhanced customer experience and big -data analytical capability. These
factors contributed to improvements in budgeting processes and financial outcomes. The
study is important because it identifies customer experience and analytics as mechanisms
linking digitalisation with organisational performance.
5.5 E-Commerce and Transactional Performance
E-commerce expands digital marketing from communication to transaction. It enables
businesses to display products, process orders and exchange value through internet-enabled
systems. Effective integration of marketing and e -commerce can reduce the distance between
product awareness and purchase.
Ayokunmi et al. (2026) found a significant positive relationship between e -commerce
implementation and SME performan ce in Nigeria. Nevertheless, the benefits of e -commerce
depend on several complementary conditions. Customers must trust the seller and payment
system. Product information must be accurate, orders must be processed efficiently and
delivery must correspond with customer expectations.

The evidence therefore supports a shift from analysing digital marketing and e -commerce as

5.6 Organisational Resilience
Digital marketing can support resilience by enabling businesses to maintain relationships and
adapt during disruption. Online commun ication may help firms remain connected with
customers and suppliers when physical interaction becomes difficult. Digital platforms may
also facilitate alternative products, customer groups and methods of service delivery.
Ashiru et al. (2023), based on in terviews with 42 Nigerian SME operators, found that digital
communication technologies supported resilience through collaboration, relationship-building,
diversification and supply -chain flexibility. This finding broadens the meaning of
performance. The value of digital marketing should not be assessed solely through immediate
revenue. Business continuity, customer retention and adaptive capacity may also constitute
important outcomes.

7. Barriers and Boundary Conditions
7.1 Digital Literacy
The effectiveness of digital marketing depends partly on the knowledge and confidence of
business owners and employees. Basic platform use is different from the ability to plan
campaigns, develop content, analyse data and evaluate return on invest ment. Limited digital
literacy may lead firms to adopt technologies without using them strategically.
Digital competence includes the ability to select appropriate channels, understand customer
behaviour, manage online communication, protect information and interpret performance
data. Training should therefore extend beyond introductory platform usage to include
marketing strategy and commercial measurement.
7.2 Infrastructure and Connectivity
Digital business activities depend on electricity, internet av ailability, affordable data and
functional devices. Weak infrastructure may increase operating costs and interrupt customer
communication. The wider African digital environment also remains characterised by
considerable disparities in access, particularly between urban and rural communities.
Infrastructure should therefore be treated as a boundary condition rather than merely a
general challenge. The same digital strategy may generate different results across locations
because businesses and customers experience different levels of connectivity.
7.3 Customer Trust and Cybersecurity
Online fraud, misleading advertising and concerns about payment security may reduce
customers’ willingness to transact digitally. Small businesses may face additional credibility
challenges where they lack established brands or formal online identities.
Trust may be strengthened through transparent information, verifiable contact details, secure
payment options, genuine customer reviews and consistent fulfilment. Trust is not only an
antecedent of online purchase; it may also mediate the relationship between digital
engagement and commercial performance.
7.4 Payment and Logistics Systems
Digital marketing may create customer demand that a business cannot satisfy efficiently.
Payment failure, inventory shortages, high delivery costs and delayed fulfilment may
interrupt the conversion process.
This problem creates what this review describes as conversion friction: the loss of potential
business value between customer engagement and com pleted, profitable transactions.

Conversion friction explains why firms with similar levels of online visibility may achieve
different financial outcomes.
7.5 Financial and Human Resource Constraints
Although many digital platforms have low entry costs, ef fective digital marketing is not
necessarily free. Businesses may incur expenditure on content development, advertising,
internet access, software, training and staff time. Limited resources may result in inconsistent
activity or dependence on a single platform.
Resource limitations may also prevent small firms from separating marketing responsibilities
from daily operations. As a result, digital communication may be irregular and customer
responses delayed.
7.6 Platform Dependence
Dependence on a single di gital platform may improve short -term efficiency because the
business concentrates its resources and builds an audience in one location. However,
excessive dependence may increase vulnerability to algorithm changes, account restrictions,
advertising costs and changes in customer preferences.
This review refers to this tension as the platform-dependence paradox. Concentration may
initially improve visibility and efficiency, but excessive reliance may weaken long -term
resilience. Small businesses may therefor e benefit from a balanced digital portfolio that
combines social media with customer databases, direct communication channels and owned
digital assets where feasible.

adoption leads directly to business performance. This assumption overlooks the intermediate
capabilities and customer responses required to convert technology use int o commercial
value.

review conceptualises the relationship as a sequential and context-dependent process:
Digital access and resources → strategic digital marketing adoption → digital
marketing conversion capability → market and customer responses → value realisation

8.2 The Concept of Digital Marketing Conversion Capability
The central construct introduced in this review is digital marketing conversion capability .
It refers to a firm’s ability to integrate digital communication, customer intelligence, trust -
building, transactional systems and fulfilment processes so that online customer attention
produces measurable business value.
The construct consists of five interrelated dimensions:
 Content-value capability:  the ability to provide relevant, credible and persuasive
information.

 Interactive-response capability:  the ability to communicate promptly and

 Customer-analytics capability:  the ability to collect, interpret and apply digital

The framework argues that adoption breadth alone is insufficient. The commercial effect of
using multiple digital platforms depends on the firm’s capacity to integrate these dimensions.
9. Propositions for Future Empirical Testing
Based on the reviewed literature and the proposed framework, the following  propositions are
advanced:
Proposition 1: The breadth of digital marketing adoption is positively associated with small -
business performance, but the marginal benefit decreases where digital channels are not
strategically integrated.
Proposition 2:  Digital marketing conversion capability mediates the relationship between
digital marketing adoption and business performance.
Proposition 3:  Content informativeness and customer interactivity strengthen the
relationship between digital marketing adoption and positive market responses.
Proposition 4: Customer digital trust mediates the relationship between online engagement
and completed digital transactions.
Proposition 5:  Payment reliability and fulfilment capability positively moderate the
relationship between customer purchase intention and financial performance.
Proposition 6: Owner-manager digital literacy and customer -analytics capability strengthen
the indirect relationship between digital marketing adoption and performance.
Proposition 7:  Platform dependence has a curvilinear relationship with long -term business
resilience, such that moderate concentration may improve efficiency while excessive
dependence increases vulnerability.
Proposition 8:  Weak infrastructure and limited institutional support increase  conversion
friction and reduce the strength of the relationship between digital marketing adoption and
business performance.
10. Novelty and Contribution of the Review
The novelty of this article lies in five major contributions.
First, it distinguishes digital presence  from performance-generating digital capability .
Previous studies frequently measure adoption through the use of digital platforms. The
proposed framework argues that platform use is an input rather than a performance outcome.

Second, the re view introduces digital marketing conversion capability  as the central
mechanism linking digital adoption with business performance. This construct integrates
marketing communication with customer analytics, trust, transactions and fulfilment.
Third, the concept of conversion friction explains why increased online visibility may fail to
generate corresponding improvements in sales or profitability. Conversion friction arises
where weaknesses in trust, payment systems, customer response, inventory or logisti cs
interrupt the customer journey.
Fourth, the review introduces the platform-dependence paradox, which explains how reliance
on a single platform may improve short -term efficiency while increasing long -term
vulnerability.
Fifth, the Digital Marketing Performance Conversion Framework integrates the Resource -
Based View, Dynamic Capabilities Theory, the Technology –Organisation–Environment
framework and trust perspectives. The integration provides a stronger explanation of
performance than the direct adopt ion–performance models that dominate much of the
existing literature.
11. Managerial Implications
Small-business owners should move beyond evaluating digital marketing through follower
counts, reactions and content frequency. These indicators describe visi bility but do not
demonstrate financial value. Managers should establish measurable objectives linked to
customer acquisition, conversion, retention and profitability.
Businesses should monitor indicators such as:
 qualified customer enquiries;
 enquiry-to-purchase conversion rates;
 online customer acquisition costs;
 response time;
 completed online orders;
 average customer value;
 repeat-purchase rates;
 customer retention;
 revenue generated through digital channels;
 gross margin from digitally acquired sales.
Small businesses should also integrate marketing with operational processes. Promotional

where fulfilment systems are unreliable.
Content strategies should prioritise usefulness and credibility. Recent Nigerian evidence
suggests that informative and interactive practices may be more valuable than the simple
pursuit of trendi ness or visibility. Businesses should provide accurate descriptions,
transparent prices, evidence of product quality and clear information about delivery and
returns.

12. Policy Implications
Government agencies  and enterprise -support organisations should treat digital marketing
capability as part of broader small-business development rather than as a narrow social media
skill.
Training programmes should include:
 digital marketing strategy;
 customer segmentation;
 content development;
 digital advertising;
 customer analytics;
 online trust and cybersecurity;
 e-commerce management;
 digital payment systems;
 customer relationship management;
 logistics and fulfilment planning;
 measurement of digital return on investment.
Policy initiatives should also address infrastructure because enterprise -level training cannot
fully compensate for unreliable connectivity, high digital operating costs or weak logistics.
Support should be adapted to the needs of rural businesses, informal enterprises and firms
owned by digitally underserved entrepreneurs.
Financial interventions may include digitalisation grants, shared e -commerce facilities,
affordable software and partnerships with technology providers. However, technology
provision should be accompanied by capability development and performance monitoring.
13. Future Research Agenda
Future studies should adopt longitudinal designs capable of examining changes in digital
capability and performance over time. Longitudinal evidence would provide a stronger basis
for causal inference than the cross-sectional designs that currently dominate the literature.
Researchers should also incorporate objective performance measures. Self -reported
perceptions of growth may be combined with sales records, online order volumes, conversion
rates, customer retention, profit margins and digital advertising returns.
Greater geographical coverage is needed. Much of the available evidence concentrates on
individual states or regions. Comparative studies should examine differences across Nigeria’s
geopolitical zones and between urban and rural enterprises.
Future research should distinguish micro, small and medium -sized businesses. These
categories differ in financial resources, employee capacity, digital s ophistication and

Sector-specific research is also necessary. Digital strategies may operate differently in retail,

The proposed Digital Marketing Performance Conversion Framework should be empirically

Further studies should investigate the use of artificial intelligence in small -business digital

14. Conclusion
Digital marketing provides substantial opportunities for small -scale businesses in Nigeria to
improve market visibility, customer engagement, customer acquisition, sales, competitiveness
and resilience. Nevertheless, the evidence does not support the assumption that the adoption
of digital platforms automatically produce s superior performance. Digital marketing
generates business value when firms possess the capabilities required to create relevant
content, interact effectively with customers, interpret digital information, establish trust,
facilitate transactions and fulfil customer expectations.
The existing Nigerian literature has contributed important evidence but remains limited by
cross-sectional designs, geographically restricted samples, self -reported performance
measures and inadequate attention to the mechanisms connecting digital activity with
financial outcomes. These limitations create a need for a more integrated explanation of the
relationship.
The Digital Marketing Performance Conversion Framework developed in this review
responds to this need by distinguish ing digital access, strategic adoption, conversion
capability, customer response and value realisation. It further demonstrates that performance
outcomes are shaped by organisational capability and by environmental conditions such as
infrastructure, customer trust, payment reliability and logistics.
The central conclusion is that the strategic question facing Nigerian small businesses is no
longer simply whether to adopt digital marketing. The more important question is how to
develop the capabilities and s upporting systems required to convert digital presence into
sustainable business performance.
