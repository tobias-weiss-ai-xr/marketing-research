Abstract
The decline in government -funded MICE activities in Indonesia has encouraged hotels to
strengthen their business -to-consumer (B2C) market through digital marketing, particularly
social media. This study examines the effects of Technology Adoption Costs and Competitive
Pressure on Social Media Usage and their implications for Business Performance in the hotel
industry. A quantitative survey was conducted among 73 three - to five-star hotels in Batam
and Bintan, Indonesia. Data were collected from General Mana gers and analyzed using
Structural Equation Modeling–Partial Least Squares (SEM-PLS). The results indicate that both
Technology Adoption Costs and Competitive Pressure positively and significantly influence
Social Media Usage. Furthermore, Social Media Usa ge has a positive effect on Business
Performance and significantly mediates the relationships between Technology Adoption
Costs, Competitive Pressure, and Business Performance. These findings suggest that the
benefits of technology cost efficiency and comp etitive pressure are realized through the
effective use of social media rather than through direct effects on business performance. This
study contributes to the hospitality literature by providing empirical evidence of the strategic
role of social media i n improving hotel performance and offers practical insights for hotel
managers in developing effective digital marketing strategies to sustain competitiveness in a
rapidly changing business environment.

The rapid development of digital technologies has fundamentally transformed the way

Among various digital marketing platforms, social media has evolved beyond a

services, strengthen brand awareness, engage directly with customers, and build long -term

The growing need for social media adoption has prompted hotels worldwide to
integrate digital marketing into their business strategies. Previous studies have shown that
effective social media use contributes to customer engagement, brand loyalty, purchase
intentions, and organizational competitiveness, ultimately supporting organizational
performance (Ahmad et al., 2018; Qalati et al., 2021; Noviaristanti et al., 2023; Spita  et al.,

while reaching a wider market segment at a relatively lower cost than conventional marketing

In Indonesia, hotel revenues are primarily generated from room sales and Meetings,
Incentives, Conferences, and Exhibitions (MICE). For many years, corporate and government
institutions represented the most important customer segments because of the high
frequency of business meetings, seminars, training programs, and official events
(Kusumawardani et al., 2022; Maulana et al., 2025). Consequently, many hotels became
increasingly dependent on the continuity of MICE activities as a major source of operational
revenue.

This condition changed substantially in 2025 when the Indonesian government
introduced a national budget efficiency policy that resulted in the postponement and
cancellation of numerous government -funded MICE events. The policy significantly reduced
demand for hotel services, particularly among hotels that relied heavily on government -
related business, leading to declining occupancy rates and operational revenues (Putra et al.,
2026). As a result, hotel management was required to redesign its marketing stra tegies to
maintain organizational sustainability.

One of the most widely adopted responses was strengthening the business -to-
consumer (B2C) market by targeting domestic and international tourists. This strategic shift
accelerated the adoption of social media as a primary marketing channel because it offer s

Literature Review
Technology Adoption Costs and Social Media Usage
Technology Adoption Costs (TAC) refer to the financial resources required by an
organization to adopt and implement new technologies, including expenditures on software,
employee training, system maintenance, and other operational costs associated with
technology utilization. Within the context of organizational social media adoption, these costs
represent an important strategic consideration, as managers evaluate whether the expected
benefits justify the required investment (Qalati et al., 2021).

Previous studies have suggested that social media is a relatively cost -effective digital
marketing tool compared with traditional marketing channels. Pereira & Freitas (2023) argued
that social media enables organizations to conduct promotional and communication activities
at substantially lower costs, allowing firms to reach broader customer segments while
reducing marketing expenditures. Consequently, the affordability of social media has
encouraged many organizations to incorporate it into their marketing strategies.

Nevertheless, adopting social media is not entirely cost -free. Organizations must
invest in skilled human resources, continuous content development, and supporting

technological infrastructure. Therefore, firms are more likely to adopt social media when its
implementation costs are perceived to be relatively low compared with the long -term
strategic benefits it can generate (Chatterjee & Kar, 2020).

Empirical evidence regarding this relationship remains inconclusive. Pateli et al. (2020)
found that social media provides an affordable marketing platform for hotels and contributes
to improved business performance by expanding market reach and increasing sales. In
contrast, Fu et al. (2024) argued that successful social media adoption still requires substantial
organizational investment and strong support from top management to generate meaningful
business outcomes.

These inconsistent findings indicate that the relationship between Technology
Adoption Costs and Social Media Usage requires further investigation, particularly in the hotel
industry, where operational characteristics and marketing strategies differ from t hose of
other sectors.

Competitive Pressure and Social Media Usage
Competitive Pressure (CP) refers to the degree of pressure perceived by an
organization as a result of competition within its industry. As market competition intensifies,
organizations are increasingly encouraged to adopt innovations and digital technologi es to
maintain their competitive advantage (Abbasi et al., 2022). Within the Technology –
Organization–Environment (TOE) framework, competitive pressure represents an
environmental factor that motivates organizations to implement digital technologies in
response to changing market conditions and competitors’ strategic actions ( Pateli et al.,
2020).

In highly competitive industries, organizations are required to respond quickly to
changes in customer preferences and competitors’ marketing strategies. Previous studies
have consistently identified Competitive Pressure as a significant driver of social m edia
adoption. Ahmad et al. (2018) found that competitive pressure positively influences social
media usage because firms seek to avoid falling behind their competitors in digital marketing
activities. Similarly, Tajudeen et al. (2018) and Abbasi et al. (2 022) argued that increasing
competitive intensity encourages organizations to adopt social media as a strategic tool to
strengthen their market position and sustain competitive advantage.

Despite these findings, most previous studies have focused on small and medium -
sized enterprises or industries other than hospitality. Given that the hotel industry is highly
dependent on customer engagement, brand reputation, and digital communication, th e
influence of Competitive Pressure on Social Media Usage may differ from that observed in
other sectors. Therefore, further investigation is required to examine this relationship within
the context of the hotel industry.

Social Media Usage and Business Performance
Social Media Usage (SMU) has become an essential digital marketing strategy that
enables organizations to strengthen their competitive position in an increasingly dynamic
business environment. Through social media platforms, organizations can communicate more
effectively with customers, expand market reach, enhance brand visibility, and obtain real -

In the hotel industry, social media serves not only as a promotional channel but also
as a platform for developing long-term relationships with customers. Continuous interactions
through social media enable hotels to gather customer feedback, better understand customer
preferences, and improve service quality in response to changing market demands.
Consequently, effective social media usage is expected to enhance organizational
performance by increasing customer engagement, strengthening brand awareness, an d
improving both financial and non-financial performance.

Although previous studies have acknowledged the strategic value of social media, its
contribution to business performance may vary across industries and market environments.
Differences in competitive intensity, customer characteristics, and organizational capabilities
in managing social media can influence the extent to which social media improves
organizational outcomes. Therefore, further investigation is needed to examine the
relationship between Social Media Usage and Business Performance in the contex t of the
Indonesian hotel industry.

The Mediating Role of Social Media Usage
The Technology –Organization–Environment (TOE) framework suggests that
technological and environmental factors do not directly enhance organizational performance.
Instead, these factors encourage organizations to adopt appropriate technologies, which
subsequently generate business value. In this study, Social Media Usage (SMU) is
conceptualized as a mediating mechanism that explains how Technology Adoption Costs and
Competitive Pressure contribute to Business Performance.

By effectively utilizing social media, hotels can expand their market reach, strengthen
customer relationships, improve brand visibility, and obtain timely market information to
support managerial decision -making. Consequently, the advantages of lower tech nology
adoption costs and stronger competitive pressure are expected to translate into improved
business performance only when hotels actively integrate social media into their marketing
and customer engagement strategies. Accordingly, this study proposes the following
hypotheses:

H1: Technology Adoption Costs positively influence Social Media Usage.
H2:Competitive Pressure positively influences Social Media Usage.
H3: Social Media Usage positively influences Business Performance.
H4: Social Media Usage mediates the relationship between Technology Adoption Costs and
Business Performance.
H5: Social Media Usage mediates the relationship between Competitive Pressure and
Business Performance.

Research Methodology
 This study employed a quantitative research approach using a survey design to
examine the relationship between social media usage and hotel business performance
(Purwianti, 2021). The study was conducted among star -rated hotels located in Batam City

and Bintan Regency, Riau Islands Province, Indonesia. These two destinations were selected
because they serve as Indonesia's main international tourism gateways, sharing maritime
borders with Singapore and Malaysia, and therefore exhibit a relatively high level of
hospitality industry activity (Fahlevi et al., 2025).

The study population consisted of all three-star to five-star hotels operating in Batam
and Bintan. According to data published by Statistics Indonesia (BPS) in 2025, the population
comprised 90 hotels. The minimum sample size was determined using the Krejcie and Morgan
sample size table, resulting in a required sample of 73 hotels.

The unit of analysis was the hotel organization, while the respondents were the
General Managers (GMs) of the participating hotels. General Managers were selected
because they are the primary decision -makers responsible for organizational management,
including the adoption of social media as a marketing strategy, and possess comprehensive
knowledge of its impact on hotel business performance.
Primary data were collected through self -administered questionnaires distributed
directly to the respondents. The measurement instrument consisted of indicators
representing each research construct and was adapted from previously validated studies
(Tajudeen et al., 2018; Qalati et al., 2021; Fu et al., 2024). All measurement items were
assessed using a five-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree).

The collected data were analyzed using Structural Equation Modeling –Partial Least
Squares (SEM-PLS). The analysis consisted of two stages. First, the measurement model was
evaluated by assessing construct validity and reliability. Second, the structural mo del was
examined to test the proposed hypotheses and evaluate the relationships among the
research constructs.

(3 respondents; 4.1%), and a master's degree (1 respondent; 1.4%). This profile suggests that
the majority of respondents possessed an appropriate educational background to evaluate
organizational strategies and business performance.

Hypothesis Testing
After confirming the validity and reliability of the measurement model, the structural
model was evaluated to test the proposed hypotheses. Hypothesis testing was conducted
using the bootstrapping procedure in SEM -PLS by examining the path coefficients (β) , t -
statistics, and p-values. A hypothesis was considered supported when the t-statistic exceeded
1.96 and the p-value was below 0.05.

Discussion
H1: Technology Adoption Costs and Social Media Usage
The findings indicate that Technology Adoption Costs have a positive and significant
effect on Social Media Usage, suggesting that the lower the perceived cost of adopting social
media, the more likely hotels are to integrate it into their marketing activi ties. This finding is
consistent with previous studies, which identified cost efficiency as one of the primary drivers
of digital technology adoption within organizations (Pereira & Freitas, 2023; Chatterjee & Kar,
2020).

A notable finding of this study is that hotel managers perceive social media as a
considerably more cost -effective marketing channel than conventional promotional media.
With relatively low implementation costs, social media enables hotels to attract both
domestic and international tourists, enhance brand awareness, strengthen customer
communication, and expand their promotional reach. These advantages have become
particularly important following the decline in demand from the corporate and government
segments caused by the reduction of MICE activities. Consequently, social media has emerged
as a strategic marketing tool that enables hotels to diversify their customer base and reduce
their dependence on the corporate market.

H2: Competitive Pressure and Social Media Usage
The findings reveal that Competitive Pressure has a positive and significant effect on
Social Media Usage, indicating that the greater the level of competition within the hotel
industry, the more likely hotels are to adopt social media as a marketing strategy. This result
is consistent with previous studies, which reported that competitive pressure encourages
organizations to adopt social media to strengthen their competitive position and sustain
market competitiveness (Ahmad et al., 2018; Tajudeen et al., 2018; Abbasi et al., 2022).

This study further demonstrates that intense competition among hotels encourages
managers to make more strategic use of social media to gain a competitive advantage. In the
context of declining demand from the corporate and government segments, social media has
become an essential marketing channel for maintaining business sustainability by expanding
market reach, increasing hotel visibility, and attracting both domestic and international
tourists more effectively.

H3: Social Media Usage and Business Performance
The findings indicate that Social Media Usage has a positive and significant effect on
Business Performance, suggesting that more effective utilization of social media leads to
better business performance among hotels. This finding is consistent with previous studies by
Noviaristanti et al. (2023) and Spita et al. (2020), which reported that social media enhances
organizational performance by expanding marketing reach, strengthening customer
relationships, and improving firms' competitive advantage.

This study further demonstrates that hotel managers perceive social media as a
strategic resource that contributes to organizational performance. Rather than serving solely

become an important strategy for maintaining business performance and ensuring the long -

H4: The Mediating Role of Social Media Usage in the Relationship between Technology
Adoption Costs and Business Performance
The findings indicate that Social Media Usage positively and significantly mediates the
relationship between Technology Adoption Costs and Business Performance. This result
suggests that lower technology adoption costs do not directly improve hotel busines s
performance; instead, they encourage hotels to utilize social media more effectively, which
subsequently enhances organizational performance. This finding is consistent with previous
studies by Tajudeen et al. (2018), Abbasi et al. (2022), and Qalati  et al. (2021), which argued
that the organizational benefits of digital technologies are maximized when these
technologies are effectively integrated into business activities.

This study further demonstrates that cost efficiency alone is insufficient to improve
hotel business performance. The benefits of lower technology adoption costs can only be
realized when hotel managers effectively utilize social media as a strategic marke ting tool to
attract tourists, enhance brand awareness, and strengthen customer relationships.
Therefore, Social Media Usage serves as a mediating mechanism that transforms technology
cost efficiency into improved business performance, particularly as hote ls adapt to changes
in market structure following the decline in demand from the corporate and government
segments.

H5: The Mediating Role of Social Media Usage in the Relationship between Competitive
Pressure and Business Performance
The findings reveal that Social Media Usage positively and significantly mediates the
relationship between Competitive Pressure and Business Performance. This result indicates
that competitive pressure does not directly enhance hotel business performance. Instead, it
encourages hotel managers to adopt social media as a strategic marketing tool, which
subsequently contributes to improved organizational performance. This finding supports
previous studies by Tajudeen et al. (2018), Abbasi et al. (2022), and Qalati et al. (2021), which
emphasized that social media enables organizations to respond effectively to competitive
pressures while improving business performance.

This study further suggests that increasing market competition encourages hotel
managers to adopt a more adaptive approach to digital marketing by leveraging social media
to strengthen their competitive position. Through social media, hotels can enhance th eir
visibility, expand market reach, and build stronger relationships with potential customers.
These findings highlight that the effective use of social media is a key mechanism through
which hotels respond to competitive pressure and maintain business pe rformance,
particularly when competing for leisure tourists amid the decline in demand from the
corporate and government segments.

Conclusion
 This study demonstrates that Technology Adoption Costs and Competitive Pressure
are important antecedents of Social Media Usage in the hotel industry. Furthermore, Social
Media Usage was found to have a positive effect on Business Performance and to serve as a
significant mediating mechanism in the relationships between Technology Adoption Costs,
Competitive Pressure, and Business Performance. These findings indicate that the benefits of
technology cost efficiency and competitive pressure do not directly tr anslate into improved
hotel performance; instead, they are realized through the effective use of social media as a
digital marketing strategy.

From a theoretical perspective, this study contributes to the existing literature by
providing empirical evidence on the role of social media in enhancing hotel business
performance, a topic that remains relatively underexplored in the context of Indonesia 's
hospitality industry. The findings highlight that social media is not merely a marketing
communication tool but also a strategic mechanism through which technology cost efficiency
and competitive pressure are transformed into improved organizational performance.

From a managerial perspective, the findings provide practical insights for hotel
managers seeking to maintain business performance amid the Indonesian government's
budget efficiency policy and the resulting decline in demand from the corporate and
government segments. Hotels should optimize the use of social media to expand market
reach, strengthen brand awareness, enhance customer relationships, and attract both
domestic and international tourists, thereby supporting long -term business sustainability in
an increasingly competitive market.
