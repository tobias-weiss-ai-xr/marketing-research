The increasing adoption of green local beauty products in Indonesia reflects growing
consumer awareness of environmental sustainability and personal well -being. However,
maintaining consumers' repurchase intention remains a challenge due to various
psychological and behavioral factors. This study aims to examine the influence of
altruistic motivation and egoistic motivation on repurchase intention toward green local
beauty products through the mediating role of attitude. Drawing upon the Theory of
Planned B ehavior and the Stimulus -Organism-Response framework, a quantitative
approach was employed using survey data collected from 362 Indonesian female
consumers. The data were analyzed using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation
Modeling. The results indica te that both altruistic and egoistic motivations positively
influence attitude, which in turn significantly enhances consumers' repurchase intention.
Furthermore, attitude significantly mediates the relationships between both motivations
and repurchase intention. These findings suggest that both environmental concerns and
personal benefit considerations contribute to consumers' repurchase intention through
the formation of favorable attitudes toward green local beauty products. The study
contributes to the green consumption literature by providing empirical evidence on the
psychological mechanisms underlying repurchase intention and offers practical insights
for green beauty brands seeking to strengthen consumer retention through green
marketing strategies.

Contribution/ Originality: This study extends the green consumption literature by integrating the Theory of
Planned Behavior and the Stimulus -Organism-Response framework to explain consumers’ repurchase intention
toward green local beauty products. It demonstrates how altruistic and egoistic motivations influence repurchase
intention through attitude and provides empirical evidence from the Indonesian green beauty market.

1. INTRODUCTION
The growing adoption of green local beauty products in Indonesia reflects increasing consumer interest in
environmentally responsible consumption and sustainable beauty practices [1]. In the context of this study, green
local beauty products are defined as skincare and cosmetic products offered by Indonesian brands that explicitly
emphasize sustainability-oriented practices, including environmentally responsible ingredient sourcing, cruelty -free
manufacturing, green packaging, and environmental or social responsibility initiatives. Despite this positive trend,
maintaining consumers’ repurchase intention remains a considera ble challenge for green beauty brands. Compared
with conventional beauty products, green beauty products are often associated with higher prices, shorter shelf lives,

Indonesia has emerged as a promising market for green consumption, supported by rapid expansion of its beauty
industry, increasing environmental awareness, and growing consumer preference for locally produced beaut y
products. As one of Southeast Asia’s largest beauty markets, Indonesia provides a unique context for examining
consumers’ sustainable consumption behavior, particularly toward green local beauty products [1, 4, 5]. Despite
these favorable market conditions, consumers continue to encounter trade-offs between sustainability considerations
and product performance when making purchasing decisions [1, 3]. These characteristics make Indonesia a
particularly relevant context for investigating consumers’ repurchase intention toward green local beauty products.
Previous studies have identified altruistic and egoistic motivations as impor tant drivers of green consumption
behavior [6, 7]. Altruistic motivation reflects consumers' concerns for environmental protection, ethical
consumption, and social responsibility, whereas egoistic motivation emphasizes personal benefits such as healt h,
safety, beauty enhancement, and self-care [3, 6]. However, existing findings regarding the relative influence of these
motivations remain inconclusive. While some consumers may be motivated by environmental and ethical concerns,
others may prioritize personal benefits when evaluating green products. Such inconsistencies suggest that the
motivational mechanisms underlying consumer decisions toward green local beauty products require further
investigation, particularly in emerging markets where sustainable consumption practices are still evolving [3, 6].
In addition, attitude has consistently been recognized as one of the most important predictors of behavioral
intention within the green consumption literature. Nevertheless, a favorable attitude toward environmentally friendly
products does not necessarily translate into actual repurchase intention. Consumers may hold positive perceptions of
green beauty products while remaining reluctant to repurchase due to concerns related to product effectiveness,
affordability, or consumption priorities [1, 2].
This phenomenon reflects th e persistent attitude-behavior gap frequently observed in sustainable consumption
research. Furthermore, although previous studies have extensively examined green purchase intention across various
product categories, empirical evidence focusing specificall y on repurchase intention toward green local beauty
products remains limited, particularly in the Indonesian context [3, 4, 7]. Therefore, a deeper understanding of how
altruistic motivation and egoistic motivation shape consumers' attitudes and subsequently influence repurchase
intention is necessary to advance the understanding of sustainable consumer behavior and support the development
of effective strategies for green local beauty products.
Although previous studies have examine d the role of altruistic and egoistic motivations in influencing green
consumption behavior, existing evidence remains fragmented and largely concentrated on general green products,
organic food, recycled products, and environmentally friendly consumer goods [6, 7]. Comparatively little attention
has been devoted to green local beauty products, despite their distinctive characteristics associated with personal
appearance, health considerations, self-care, and environmental responsibility. Furthermore,  most prior studies have
focused on purchase intention rather than repurchase intention, resulting in a limited understanding of the factors
that encourage consumers to maintain long-term consumption of green local beauty products.
From a theoretical persp ective, previous research has primarily investigated the direct influence of motivations
on green consumption behavior, while the underlying psychological mechanisms through which these motivations
translate into repurchase intention remain insufficiently explored. In particular, the mediating role of attitude in
explaining how altruistic and egoistic motivations shape repurchase intention toward green local beauty products has
received limited empirical attention. Consequently, there is a need for further investigation to clarify the psychological
processes that drive sustainable consumption behavior in the context of green beauty products.

2. LITERATURE REVIEW
This study em ploys two key theories, including the Theory of Planned Behavior , which explains how beliefs
shape behavioral intention , and the Stimulus -Organism-Response framework , which explores the psychological
drivers of green behavior. The Theory of Planned Behavio r aims to explain human behavior [8, 9]. It asserts that
behavioral intention is influenced by three factors, including attitude, subjective norm, and perceived behavioral
control [10]. Besides, the Stimulus -Organism-Response framework explains how environmental focus influences
individual behavior [11, 12]. The theory posits that external stimuli affect an organism’s cognitive and emotional
state, which leads to a behavioral response that consists of three components, including stimulus (socio-psychological
characteristics, social environment, and economic influences), organism (internal processes like perception, emotion,
motivation, and attitude), and response (resulting behavior) [13]. The integration of the Theory of Planned Behavior
and the Stimulus -Organism-Response framework provides a comprehensive theoretical foundation for explaining
consumers’ repurchase intention toward green local beauty products. Accordingly, the Stimulus-Organism-Response
framework explains how psychological motivations influence consumers’ internal evaluations, whereas the Theory of
Planned Behavior explains how these evaluations are translated into behavioral intention. The integration of these
theories therefore provides a unified framework for understanding the psychological mechanism underlying
consumers’ repurchase intention toward green local beauty products [14].
Repurchase intention, in this study, serves as the dependent variable, which refers to an outcome of individual
attitude linked to consumer loyalty and retention. It also relates to consumers’ willingness to purchase a product
again based on satisfaction that meets or exceeds expectations. This retention indicates consumer satisfaction and
positive product evaluation, making it vital for brands as it drives loyalty, reduces purchase decision time, and lowers
costs [15, 16]. As a post-purchase process, repurchase intention includes willingness, planning, and commitment to
repurchase. It differs from actual repurchase behavior and aligns with the Theory of Planned Behavio r [17].
In the context of green local beauty products, repurchase intention is particularly important because these
products require continuous consumption and repeated purchasing decisions. Unlike one-time purchases, repurchase
intention reflects consumers’ long -term commitment to sustainable consumption practices and their continued
support for environmentally responsible brands. However, previous studies suggest that consumers do not always
maintain their intention to repurchase gree n products due to concerns regarding higher prices, perceived
effectiveness, and product performance trade-offs [18, 19]. Furthermore, existing research has focused predominantly
on green purchase intention rather than repurchase intention, resulting in limited understanding of the factors that
encourage sustained consumption of green local beauty products, particularly in emerging markets such as Indonesia
[3].

Altruistic motivation and egoistic motivation are the independent variables. Altruism refers to a selfless action
where individuals prioritize others’ well-being over their own. It involves a willingness to invest resources to benefit
others and is also termed self -transcendence. Within the Stimulus -Organism-Response framework, altruistic
motivation functions as the organism and is widely studied in marketing to understand the drivers of consumer
behavior. While egoism, also called self -enhancement, contrasts with altruism by focusing on power, achievemen t,
social status, and personal gain. It involves prioritizing personal goals and information even within group settings
and includes caution in consumption, personal buying considerations, and self -interest framed as responsible
consumer behavior. Egoistic motivation, like altruistic motivation, is conceptualized as the organism in the Stimulus-
Organism-Response framework that shapes consumers’ evaluations of green local beauty products. It is widely used
in consumer behavior studies to explain motives of actions [6, 14].
Within the context of green consumption, both altruistic and egoistic motivations have been identified as
important psychological drivers of consumer behavior. Consumers may choose green products because they wish to
contribute to environmental sustainability and social welfare, reflecting altruistic concerns, or because they perceive
personal benefits such as improved health, safety, and product quality, reflecting egoistic considerations [6, 7].
Nevertheless, previous findings remain inconsistent re garding which motivation exerts a stronger influence on
consumer behavior. While some studies emphasize the dominant role of environmental concern in shaping green
consumption, others report that self-oriented benefits are more influential in determining consumer decisions [6, 14].
These inconsistencies indicate that the relative contribution of altruistic and egoistic motivations toward repurchase
intention remains insufficiently understood, particularly in the context of green local beauty products.
Unlike other green product categories, such as recycled products or organic food, green beauty products possess
distinctive characteristics related to personal care and appearance enhancement. Because these products are applied
directly, consumers often evaluate them based on both their environmental attributes and their perceived safety and
effectiveness [3]. Consequently, the psychological processes underlying consumers’ evaluations and repurchase
intention toward green beauty products may differ from those associated with other green product categories [14].
These distinctive characteristics highlight the importance of examining altruistic and egoistic motivations specifically
within the context of green local beauty products.
Recent studies conducted in emerging Asian markets have  increasingly examined consumer behavior toward
green beauty products. A study by Nguyen-Viet and Nguyen [3] investigated consumers’ purchase decisions toward
green beauty care products in Vietnam and demonstrated the importance of psychological factors in shaping green
consumption. Similarly, Mutiara et al. [4]  reported that consumers’ environmental concerns and attitudes
significantly influence their intention to purchase green cosmetics in Indonesia. Evidence from other emerging Asian
markets, including a study by Andika et al. [20], further suggests that consumers’ evaluations of sustainable beauty
products are influenced by both environmental considerations and personal benefits. Collectively, these findings
demonstrate that psychological determinants of green beauty consumption have received growing attention across
emerging Asian markets. However, empirical evidence focusing specifically on consumers’ repurchase intention
toward green local beauty products remains limited, particularly within the Indonesian context.
Attitude serves as a mediating variable in this study. Attitude as the key predictor of intention in the Theory of
Planned Behavior, is defined as an individual’s positive or negative evaluation of a behavior. It is shaped by cognitive
assessments, beliefs, values, and emotions, and can be favorable or unfavorable, remaining stable but adaptable with
new information. Attitude includes four dimensions, namely willingness to choose, accuracy in selection, perce ived
importance, and usage based on personal choice. It is assessed by perceived benefits, meaningfulness, and favorability.
Attitude is widely used in marketing studies due to its strong influence on behavior [6, 17].
Although attitude is widely recognized as a significant predictor of behavioral intention within the Theory of
Planned Behavior, favorable attitud es do not always result in actual purchasing or repurchasing behavior. This
phenomenon, commonly referred to as the attitude –behavior gap, is frequently observed in sustainable consumption

This study proposes that altruistic and egoistic motivations serve as important  psychological drivers that shape
consumers’ attitudes toward green local beauty products. These attitudes subsequently influence consumers’
repurchase intention. Accordingly, attitude is conceptualized as a mediating mechanism through which motivational
factors are translated into behavioral intention.
According to the Stimulus-Organism-Response framework, motivations represent internal psychological states
that influence how consumers evaluate products and behaviors. In the context of green consumption, altruistic
motivation reflects environmental conce rn and ethical responsibility, whereas egoistic motivation emphasizes
personal benefits such as health, safety, and appearance enhancement [6]. Previous studies have reported that both
motivations positively influence consumers’ evaluations of green products because they satisfy either self -
transcendence or self -enhancement valu es [14, 21]. Therefore, consumers with stronger altruistic or egoistic
motivations are expected to develop more favorable attitudes toward green local beauty products. Thus, the proposed
Hypothesis 1 (H1) and Hypothesis 2 (H2) are:
H1: Altruistic motivation positively influences attitude toward green local beauty products.
H2: Egoistic motivation positively influences attitude toward green local beauty products.
According to the Theory of Planned Behavior, attitude serves as an immediate antecedent of behavioral intention
[8]. Therefore, altruistic motivation is expected to influence repurchase intention indirect ly through attitude.
Consumers with stronger altruistic motivation are more likely to evaluate green local beauty products favorably
because such products are perceived to align with their environmental and ethical values [6]. These favorable
evaluations subsequently increase their willingness to continue purchasing the products in the future. Previous
studies have reported that altruistic concerns positively influence green consumption behavior through favorable
consumer evaluations and attitudes [22, 23]. Therefore, attitude i s expected to mediate the relationship between
altruistic motivation and repurchase intention toward green local beauty products. Thus, the proposed Hypothesis 3
(H3) is:
H3: Attitude mediates the relationship between altruistic motivation and repurchase i ntention toward green local beauty
products.

personal benefits they provide [3, 24]. W hen consumers perceive that green beauty products satisfy these self -
oriented needs, they are more likely to develop favorable attitudes toward the products. According to the Theory of
Planned Behavior, such positive attitudes subsequently strengthen behav ioral intention, including repurchase
intention [8]. Prior studies have suggested that egoistic motivation can positively influence green consumption
behavior through consumers’ evaluations and attitudes toward green products [6, 21]. Therefore, attitude is expected
to mediate the relationship between egoistic motivation and repurchase intention toward green local beauty products.

The Theory of Planned Behavior posits that attitude is one of the most important determinants of behavioral

3. METHODOLOGY
This study employed a quantitative cross-sectional research design to examine the relationships among altruistic
motivation, egoistic motivation, attitude, and repurchase intention toward green local beauty products. Data were
collected through a structure d questionnaire administered to Indonesian female consumers. The study adopted a
deductive approach to test the proposed hypotheses [25].
The questionnaire items were adapted from established scales used in previous studies on green consumption
behavior. All constructs were measur ed using a Five -Point Likert Scale ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5
(strongly agree), except for the dependent variable , which used a Six -Point Likert Scale. The use of multiple scale
formats aimed to minimize response pattern bias and common method  bias associated with self-reported survey data
[26]. In addition, retaining the original scale formats fro m previously validated instruments helped preserve their
psychometric properties and construct validity [27]. Although the use of different Likert scale formats was intended
to reduce response pattern bias, mixed response scales may introduce slight variations in respondents’ interpretation
of the response categories. To ensure the robustness of the measurement model, indicator reliability, internal
consistency reliability, convergent validity, and discriminant validity were thoroughly evaluated before testing the
structural relationships [28].

Before data collection, the questionnaire was subjected to a validity and reliability assessment procedure to ensure
the clarity, readability, and face validity of the research instrument. The instrument was reviewed by academics,
industry practitioners, and con sumers familiar with the Indonesian beauty market. Based on their feedback, several
refinements were made, including translation into Bahasa Indonesia, clarification of instructions, correction of
typographical errors, and simplification of wording to improve respondent comprehension.
The target population comprised Indonesian female consumers aged 17 years and above who had previously
purchased and used green local beauty products. Female consumers were selected because they represent the
dominant segment of the Indonesian beauty market and account for most beauty product users [5, 31]. In addition,
previous studies have shown that women generally exhibit greater involvement in beauty product consum ption and
place greater emphasis on product quality, health, and environmental attributes when making purchasing decisions
[4]. Therefore, focusing on female consumers provides an appropriate context for examining repurchase intention
toward green local beauty products in Indonesia. A non -probability purposive sampling technique was employed to
ensure that respondents met the study criteria. The minimum sample size was determined using G*Power version
3.1 [32], which indicated a minimum requirement of 77 respondents.
Data were collected using a structured online questionnaire administered through Google Forms. An online
survey was considered appropriate because the target respondents comprised active female social media users residing
in various provinces across Indonesia. Before participation, respondents were informed about the purpose of the study,
the voluntary nature of participation, confidentiality of responses, and the estimated completion time. Informed
consent was obtained before respondents proceeded to the questionnaire.
To ensure respondent eligibility, screening questions were included at the beginning of the survey. Participants
were required to be female consumers aged 17 years and above, have pri or experience purchasing green local beauty

Because the data were collected using a self -administered questionnaire from a single source , common method
variance (CMV) may potentially bias the observed relationships among constructs [34, 35]. To statistically assess
its presence, a marker variable technique was employed from a previous related study [36]. The marker variable was
theoretically unrelated to the principal constructs included in the research model and was incorporated into the
questionnaire to estimate the potential influence of common method bias on the observed relationships [37].
The collected data were analyzed using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM) with
SmartPLS software. It was selected because of its suitability for prediction -oriented research, its ability to estimate
complex relationships among mul tiple constructs simultaneously, and its robustness in handling relatively small
sample sizes and non-normal data distributions [28].
The data analysis was conducted in two stages. First, the measurement mode l was evaluated to assess the
reliability and validity of the constructs. Internal consistency reliability was examined using composite reliability,
and convergent validity was assessed through average variance extracted. Discriminant validity was evaluated using
the heterotrait –monotrait ratio. Second, the structural model was assessed to examine the proposed relationships
among constructs. The evaluation included path coefficients, coefficient of determination (R 2), effect size (f 2), and
predictive relevance. Hypothesis testing was performed using the bootstrapping procedure with 5,000 resamples to
determine the significance of the proposed direct and indirect relationships. The mediating effects of attitude were
assessed through the examination of indirect effects generated by the bootstrapping procedure [28].

4. RESULTS
A total of 404 responses were collected through an online survey conducted between February 2025 and March
2025. Because the questionnaire was distributed through open online platforms and beauty communities, the total
number of individuals who received or viewed the survey invitation could not be determined. Consequently, a
conventional survey response rate could not be calcula ted. Following data -screening procedures, including the
removal of responses that did not satisfy the predefined screening criteria, failed quality checks, and suspicious
response patterns, 362 valid responses were retained for further analysis.

in West Kalimantan (n = 90), followed by Jakarta (n = 65), West Java (n = 50), East Java (n = 38), West Sumatra (n

5. DISCUSSION
The findings reveal that both altruistic motivation and egoistic motivation positively influence consumers'
attitudes toward green local beauty products. Altruistic motivation exhibited a stronger effect, suggesting that
environmental responsibility and et hical concerns play a more prominent role in shaping favorable attitudes than
personal benefits. Nevertheless, the significant influence of egoistic motivation indicates that consumers also value
self-oriented benefits, such as health, safety, and beauty e nhancement, when evaluating green local beauty products.
These findings are consistent with previous studies highlighting the importance of both environmental and personal
motivations in green consumption behavior [3, 6].  The results further demonstrate that attitude positively influences
repurchase intention and represents the strongest relationship in the model. This finding supports the Theory of
Planned Behavior, which suggests that favorable evaluations of behavior increase behavioral intention . Consumers
who perceive green local beauty products positively are more likely to continue purchasing them in the future. This
result is consistent with previous studies reporting a positive r elationship between attitude and green repurchase
intention [19, 40]. The mediation analysis indicates that attitude signific antly mediates the relationships between
altruistic motivation and repurchase intention as well as between egoistic motivation and repurchase intention. This
suggests that both environmental concerns and personal -benefit considerations influence repurchase  intention
indirectly through the formation of favorable attitudes. The findings support the integration of the Theory of Planned
Behavior and the Stimulus –Organism–Response framework, which subsequently drive repurchase intention toward
green local beauty products.

6. CONCLUSION
This study examined the influence of altruistic and egoistic motivations on repurchase intention toward green
local beauty products through the mediating role of attitude. Using data collected from Indonesian female consumers
and analyzed through Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM), the findings revealed that
both altruistic motivation and egoistic motivation positively influence attitude toward green local beauty products.
Furthermore, attitude was found to positively influence repurchase intention and emerged as the strongest predictor
in the model. The results also confirmed that attitude significantly mediates the relationships between altruistic
motivation and repurchase intention as well as between egoistic motivation and repurchase intention.
These findings suggest that consumers’ repurchase intention toward green local beauty products is shaped by
both environmental concerns and personal benefit considerations, which operate through the formation of a favorable
attitude. The study contributes to the green consumption literature by providing empirical evidence on the
simultaneous roles of altruistic and egoistic motivations in explaining repurchase intention within the context of
Indonesian green beauty products. In addition, the findings support the integration of the Theory of Planned Behavior
and the Stimulus–Organism–Response framework in understanding sustainable consumer behavior.
From a managerial perspective, Indonesian green local beauty brands should develop marketing strategies that
simultaneously emphasize altruistic and egoistic values. Environmental appeals should communicate sustainable
ingredient sourcing, green packaging, cruelty -free production, and support for local sustai nability initiatives.
Meanwhile, egoistic appeals should highlight product safety, skin health, effectiveness, and product quality. Because
consumers may differ in their primary purchase motivations, marketers are encouraged to tailor communication
strategies according to consumer segments. Furthermore, given the significant influence of attitude on repurchase
intention, brands should strengthen favorable consumer attitudes through transparent sustainability claims, credible
