Abstract
The current research performs a bibliometric analysis of Customer Relationship Management (CRM)
practices and th eir offspring, looking at their contributions to business performance and strategic
formation. Based on data obtained from the Scopus database, it provides an extensive view of the scholarly
environment, identifying primary trends, popular studies, and upc oming themes in CRM. The analysis
employs biblioshiny and VOSviewer, two advanced tools for data visualization and citation network
analysis, to map the structure of CRM research and trace its evolution across different dimensions. The
paper explores how C RM practices have influenced organizational performance, customer satisfaction,
and strategic growth over time, while also identifying the "descendants" of CRM, such as newer
methodologies and technologies that have evolved from traditional CRM approaches. Key findings reveal
significant clusters of research on CRM ’s impact on customer loyalty, digital transformation, and data -
driven marketing strategies. The study also highlights gaps in the literature, offering future research
directions for scholars and practitioners seeking to explore the next frontier in CRM practices. By
combining bibliometric techniques with visualization tools, this paper contributes to a comprehensive
understanding of CRM ’s role in shaping business outcomes and strategic decision -making in a rapidly
changing business environment.

Keywords: CRM Practices, Customer Loyalty, customer satisfaction, Business Outcomes.

INTRODUCTION
CRM is now a critical business strategy for organizations that aim to create customer satisfaction, loyalty,
and business performance. CRM best practices are concerned with the management of customer data to
ensure customized services and satisfaction (1). The effectiveness of CRM programs is, in turn, closely
related to organizational commitment and knowledge management, which lead to customer loyalty and
profitability (2). Moreover, the level of CRM systems also has a significant influence on customer
satisfaction, with information, system, and service quality being the most influential factors (3). Having a
high CRM orientation has also been found to enhance market effectiveness and profitability, highlighting
its strategic value in business performance (4). As the era of digital transformation picked pace, CRM also
went beyond its conventional means through t he use of sophisticated technologies like artificial
intelligence (AI), big data analytics, and cloud computing (5,6). Through these, data is captured and

banking sector has been found to increase customer satisfaction through overall customer experience (15).
The mediating effect of customer experience in the relationship underscores the need for E -CRM
integration.
Although there has been enough of bibliometric research on CRM, there are some gaps in current research.
Current research like that by Pe rez-Vega et al. (16, 17) Del Vecchio et al. (2022) concentrates on Social
CRM and Big Data but misses the overall effect of such emerging technologies as blockchain and IoT.
There are still geographical imbalances in CRM literature, with research like that  by (18) spanning
prominent geographies while excluding developing markets. Industry -specific CRM implementation is
not well-researched, with only meagre attention outside higher education (19) and the retail sector (20).
Moreover, research like that by (21) and (22) does not perform a thorough investigation of CRM's financial
and competitive effect. Although (23) studied the contribution of social media to Supply Chain 4.0,
integrating it with CRM for predictive analysis has limited research. In addition, sustainability of CRM
strategies, as emphasized by (24), needs additional bibliometric analysis of CRM practices and its
descendants impacting business outcomes. Bridging these knowledge gaps will contribute to greater
comprehension of CRM's changing function in business strategy and technological innovation.

METHODOLOGY
This study is based on collection of information obtained from past research on the trend of research in
customer relationship management between 2014-2025 obtained from Scopus database that will be put as
input to conduct bibliometric research. Bibliometric research is a type of quantitative approach that uses
statistical analysis of published literature to describe trends, performance, and  relationship in academic
writings (30, 46) (31, 47). It employs a range of indicators to capture productivity, quality, and connections
among publications, authors, and research areas (3 0,46). It is a very potent tool for research landscape
mapping as wel l as grasping conceptual, intellectual, and social structures of the scientific field. These
analyses commonly employ quantitative methods such as network analysis and thematic clustering for the
identification of key themes, trends, and influential works (32,48) (33,49). For an effective search strategy
to retrieve relevant Scopus-indexed articles, the title, abstract and key words will involve this search query,
TITLE-ABS-KEY (“Customer Relationship Management") AND TITLE -ABS-KEY (“customer
satisfaction") OR TITLE-ABS-KEY ("brand loyalty") OR TITLE-ABS-KEY ("sales performance") with
regard to publications for the period of 2014 to 2025. In this case, a focus on relevant and current time for
the analysis from the past ten years was established by filtering off irrelevant and obsoleted content with
the use of the latter condition. The years before 2014 have been excluded within the scope. To avoid
duplication and error, it was limited to its source in peer-reviewed journal articles in their final form. Only
English language was used. The wide search string is as shown in Table 2. Data extraction was conducted
on January 14 2025, yielding an initial corpus of 179 documents after applying the specified filters as
mentioned in figure-1. Following a thorough review, two duplicate records were identified and excluded,
resulting in a final dataset of 196 unique documents. The dataset was exported in CSV format for further
analysis. Various analytical tools were applied to analyze descriptive data and create visualiza tions. For
descriptive statistical analysis, Microsoft Excel was the employed tool, while advanced analyses like co -
keyword and co -citation analysis, and bibliographic coupling were done through VOSviewer (3 4, 50 )
(35,51). Also applied was the web -based Bi blioshiny application that is part of RStudio for applying
Bradford's Law, producing three -field plots, identifying the globally most -cited articles, and more. It
therefore covered all the methodologies comprehensively.

Bradford Law: Bradford's Law of Scattering is a bibliometric law that describes the pattern of articles
between journals in a given subject area (38,54). It postulates that few core journals contain most articles,
with numerous other journals contributing fewer (39,55). As stated by law, the number of journals in the
second and third zones is n and n ² more than that of the first zone, respectively (38,54). Journals are

loyalty-a move towards digitization and innovation. Customer satisfaction and loyalty  remain a common
priority area of effort, with a growing desire to explore digital strategies and integrate them within CRM

Bibliographic Coupling: Bibliographic coupling, a method for analyzing relationships between documents
based on shared references, has gained renewed interest in recent years (43,59). While it is often assumed
that bibliographically coupled papers are conceptually similar, recent research challenges this notion (44,
60). Bibliographic cou pling can be applied at various levels, including author -level analysis, to reveal
intellectual structures and research trends (45, 61). It complements other citation-based methods like co-
citation analysis, providing a more comprehensive view of a field's intellectual landscape (46, 62).
In this network, nodes (circles) represent individual documents, whereas connections between nodes (if
two nodes are connected by an edge, the thickness of the edge represents the degree of bibliometric
coupling, where thicker edges represent stronger connections). The nodes are clustered together, with each
one identified by a different color, indicating sets of documents that share common references within
particular research themes or subfields. Larger nodes correspond to documents found to be more relevant
and also have a higher degree of shared citations; proximity indicates relative strength of the bibliographic
relationship. In figure-8 the red cluster includes major nodes like Mullins (2015) and Rahimi (2017) and
shows this cluster reflects the dominant area of research. The blue cluster includes documents like Rather
(2018) and Liau (2014) representing the influence of another major subfield. The green cluster has
documents such as Khan (2022) and Kumar (2022). The refore, this would reflect recent literature.
Peripheral nodes, such as Abu-Shabab (2015), have fewer connecting nodes hence potentially represent a
niche or isolated area of study.

CONCLUSION
This research makes an exhaustive presentation of trends, contributions, and thematic developments
within CRM practices in the period of 2014 -2025 that reveal important trends and avenues to be further
examined. The high research period came in 2022, publishing 31 publications, but witnessed a sharp drop
in 2023 and, thereafter, did decently next year with a publication of 21 articles. These volatile trends
combined with declining citation averages may suggest a stagnation either in the conventional  CRM
themes or lack of innovation around emerging issues. India takes the lead in terms of contributions to
CRM research, accounting for 43.4% of the publications, followed by the U .S. and China, with the U.S.
producing the most impactful work, evidenced by 682 citations.
Key contributors such as Kumar P, Mokha AK, and Rodriguez M, as well as the journal itself, International
Journal of Electronic Customer Relationship Management, have contributed much to the development of
the field. Thematic clusters reve al a focus on core CRM elements such as customer satisfaction, loyalty,
and trust, while emerging themes focus on digitization and innovation, reflecting broader business and
technology trends. Foundational authors include Fornell C. and Parasuraman A cont inue to influence
research directions, bridging traditional CRM practices with interdisciplinary approaches. Although the
contributions are remarkable, the decline in publication and citation trends points to challenges such as
adapting to artificial intel ligence, big data, and sustainability. There are also regional disparities in
contributions that point to gaps in global collaboration and knowledge sharing. For relevance, CRM
research must embrace innovation, address shifts in consumer behavior, and expl ore new methodologies
to keep impacting business practices. Future areas of research for CRM would be in their adoption and
integration with the newer digital trends, such as social media, AI, and blockchain, in improving customer
engagement and brand loya lty. Cross-cultural studies can help to understand how flexible and effective
CRM practices are in diverse cultural and geographical contexts. Sector -specific research may focus on
