1. INTRODUCTION
In recent decades, marketing has undergone a substantial development both
in theory and practice. The exponential growth in technology, increasing
complexity in consumer behavior, rapid socio-cultural changes, global crisis,
pandemic, wars etc. Through these types of situations worldwide, scholars’
perspective of marketing consequently moved beyond traditional approaches
to a wider range of multidisciplinary perspective. To reflect the evolving nature
of markets, marketing today works with various areas such as psychology,
sociology, technology, health, religion, and even medicine.
The multidisciplinary nature of marketing intriguingly enables marketing
researchers to examine diverse topics, even those at first appear unrelated or
unconnected. Neuro-marketing (Lee et al., 2007), Quantum Marketing
(Rajamannar, 2021), Health Marketing (Crié & Chebat, 2013), Faith Marketing
(Hashmi, 2018) etc. As one of the topic emerging seemingly unconventional,
the intersection of marketing and astrology has started to attract scholarly
attention (Awasthi, 2025; Sobreira & Manosso, 2024; Pathak & Holani, 2020;
Kwak et al., 2014). Accordingly, this study examines AstroLogical Marketing
through a SPAR-4-SLR systematic review and proposes a future research
agenda.
The intersection of astrology and marketing is framed in this work as
AstroLogical Marketing. Astrology in general uses the position of stars and
planets to predict human affairs and earthly events (Allum, 2011, p. 2) and has
a remarkable growing market in contemporary consumer culture. According
to Market Research Future report (2026), market size of astrology as of 2024
was estimated at 14.3 billion dollars and is expected to grow to 27.15 billion
dollars by 2035. Even though this commercial area using astrology presents
a profitable perspective yet remain largely under researched area  for
marketing scholars.
Astrology has entered into various markets and is accessible through apps,
subscription services, merchandise, and through social media. This
commercialization of astrology operates a symbolic mechanism for marketing
and emphasizes the thrill of consumers as astrology helps through
personalization, identity-marking, and self-curation (Awasthi, 2025, pp. 10-
11). In other words, consumers link astrological signs as personality traits,
emotions, compatibility, and lifestyles, incorporating these meanings into self-
expression and consumption. Accordingly, within marketing perspective this
symbolic associations might influence consumer preferences or decision
processes, attachment toward products, brands, and experiences. In this
sense, astrology extends beyond a belief system and is increasingly
embedded in contemporary consumption culture and marketing.
The growing commercial visibility of astrology has also attracted attention in
marketing research but seen as conceptually dispersed and relatively limited.
The integration of astrology into marketing might be seen on topics such as
social media (Lopez et al., 2021), consumption related variables (Çatlı, 2021),
market segmentation (Mitchell & Haggett, 1997) etc. Even though there is a

2. LITERATURE REVIEW
2.1. The Intersection of Astrology and Marketing
The potential use of astrology in marketing has been an attractive subject for
almost three decades (Mitchell & Haggett, 1997) and even today continues to
attract attention (Awasthi, 2025; Limna et al., 2025). At this intersection, it is
seen that existing literature examines astrology from different marketing
perspectives and explores how various astrological topics are integrated into
consumer behavior, digital marketing, marketing communications, and related
fields. The following studies offer deeper und erstanding and a general
overview of the intersection between astrology and marketing.
Gülmez et al. (2011) found that among young consumers in terms of impulse
buying astrology signs of fire (Aries, Leo, and Sagittarius) exhibits stronger
tendencies. The study further offers implications for marketing by using
consumers’ birth dates to influence behavioral responses. Underlying reason
for this suggestion is that some individuals might be more susceptible to
astrological stimuli than others. In addition, the authors argue that astrological
characteristics could serve as a potential market segmentation variable to
better reach targeted consumers.
Jian Wang et al. (2012) proposed that consumers may turn to psychological
beliefs linked to fortune-telling practices such as astrology, palmistry, and

Kwak et al. (2014) conducted a study with college students and found that
individuals with odd-numbered zodiac signs (e.g., Aries and Gemini) and non-
water signs (e.g., Leo and Virgo) were positively associated with impulse
buying tendencies. Results also indicated that consumers born under water
signs such as Cancer and Scorpio were more inclined to rate services more
favorably than consumers associated with non-water signs. The results
suggest that astrological sign classifications could be linked to differences in
consumer choices and service evaluations.
Uslu Divanoğlu and Uslu (2019) examined that consumers’ birth dates and
their corresponding zodiac signs might affect buying behavior, indicating that
some individuals are more susceptible to astrological influences than others.
Their results show that reading daily horoscopes does not affect consumers’
shopping behavior, but their behavior changes in terms of multiple sign groups
(three-sign and four-sign). The authors also suggest that researchers could
gain new insights from exploring the use of segmentation variables based on
zodiac signs in their study of consumer preferences and behavioral
tendencies.
Lopez et al. (2021) demonstrated that individuals experiencing levels of stress
tend to frequently consume astrological information shared on social media
platforms to cope with stress and form psychological reassurance in digital
environments. In addition, they found that in social platforms astrological
content consumption is more dominant among females compared to males.
Çatlı (2021) examined whether consumers’ online purchase intentions are
effected by perceived value, perceived risk and trust factors based on
astrological signs. The findings indicate that the effect of perceived value,
perceived risk and trust on online purchase intention may vary according to
consumers’ astrological signs. Furthermore, it is observed that the elements
and characteristics associated with individuals' zodiac signs influence their
online purchase intentions to varying degrees. Therefore, demographic data
such as consumers' birth dates can provide businesses with additi onal
insights for interpreting purchasing tendencies and behavioral patterns. The
author also recommends incorporating astrological variables into marketing
research to better understand consumer behavior.
Limna et al. (2025) examine the concept of “Muketing” which originated in
Thailand and represents a fusion of Mutelu  (a modern trend for faith,
superstition, and spirituality) and marketing. This concept integrates different
spiritual elements such as astrology, numerology, color symbolism, sacred
rituals, and talismanic objects into marketing activities. Research findings
indicate that marketing contents featuring symbols of luck, sacred rituals, and
astrological themes significantly affect consumers’ attitude, trust,
engagement, and purchase intentions. In the discussion section, the authors

In summary, the general literature indicates that astrology is becoming
increasingly integrated into marketing. Existing studies demonstrate that
astrological meanings, symbols, and classifications might affect consumer
behaviors such as buying tendencies, brand perceptions, online consumption,
emotional engagement, and service evaluation. Furthermore, the
digitalization of astrology on social media platforms and its incorporation into
culturally embedded marketing strategies highlight its growing commercial
and symbolic significance in contemporary markets. Although interest is rising,
literature remains fragmented across various marketing domains and lacks a
unified conceptual perspective. Therefore, examining these studies
collectively within the context of “AstroLogical Marketing” offers a significant
opportunity to gain deeper insight into the scope, evolution, and emerging
directions of this field of research.
3. METHODOLOGY
3.1. Research Design
Astrology has gained market presence and visibility through mobile
applications, social media platforms, subscription-based services, and digital
content. Traditionally astrology is regarded as a cultural or belief system,
however it has gradually evolved into a monetized form of digital consumption.
This transformation is clearly evident in personalized horoscope readings,
astrological analyses, branded products, and spiritual counseling services.
The accelerating growth of the global astrology market further proves this
commercialization (Market Research Future, 2026). Consequently, astrology
has ceased to be a marginal element of consumer culture and has embedded
itself in contemporary markets through mobile applications (Awasthi, 2025),
subscription-based services for financial markets (Pandey et al., 2023),
branded merchandise & product design (Sobreira & Manosso, 2024) and
digital contents in e-commerce (Limna et al., 2025).
Astrology has also grown interest in marketing scholarship from multiple
perspectives including consumer behavior (Çatlı, 2021), digital engagement
(Limna et al., 2025; Lopez et al., 2021), segmentation (Mitchell & Haggett,
1997) and symbolic consumption (Awasthi, 2025). However, these studies are
spread across different marketing disciplines thus do not provide an integrated
view to explain how astrology works within marketing. While literature often
considers single relationships, developing a coherent understanding of the
overall structure of this intersection is recommended (Çatlı, 2021; Uslu
Divanoğlu & Uslu, 2019). This leads to a need to systematically examine
existing literature and how astrology is conceptually and methodologically
positioned within marketing research, as well as how it can be further
developed. Accordingly, this research addresses the following questions:

These questions are addressed in a systematic and transparent way through

methodological characteristics, and reported outcomes, enabling a structured
synthesis of the literature.
In the second stage, the dataset generated from the systematic review is
analyzed further using Social Network Analysis (SNA). Thematic and variable
networks are constructed with UCINET and NetDraw. These networks offer a
map in terms of the visualization and the structure of the connections between
the different components of the AstroLogical Marketing research and the
intellectual structure of the field.
Through this integrated approach, the study aims to go beyond a descriptive
review and to propose a structured mapping of the AstroLogical Marketing
domain, identifying its internal relations, gaps, and potential directions for
future research development.
3.2. Process of SPAR-4-SLR
The fragmented and emerging nature of astrology in marketing creates a need
for a structured approach of systematic review. In line, this research uses
SPAR-4-SLR protocol to define a systematic, transparent, and repeatable
process for identifying and analyzing literature at the intersection of astrology
and marketing. SPAR-4-SLR offers a rigorous framework to organize the
identification, screening, and selection of studies with a systematic approach.
SPAR-4-SLR is developed by Paul et al. (2021) and briefl y provides a
complete and structured protocol to perform systematic literature review.

screening process, 23 empirical studies met the inclusion criteria and
constituted the final dataset, while 35 documents were excluded for not
satisfying the predefined selection criteria. Regarding sample size in
systematic literature review, no strict lower-bound threshold was detected in
literature. However, it is observed that systematic literature review studies

al., 2019) also have used relatively small empirical sample size. Finally,
selected studies were prepared for further analysis through systematic coding.
This coded data set forms the basis of the next stage of network-based
analysis to identify structural relationships within astrological marketing.

4.2. Social Networks for Themes and Variables
This research addresses the structural connections within AstroLogical
Marketing and use a network-based approach to analyze and visualize the
extracted themes and variables. Accordingly, to examine the networks in the
first step two separate matrix is created for themes and variables. The first
scheme for matrices are rows represent studies and columns represent
themes and variables. Then, coded using a binary system. To generate the
co-occurrence signals that form the foundation of the network, each included
studies’ variable or theme was coded as 1 if present, otherwise 0. Coding was
conducted carefully across all 23 studies. Upon completing first step, two
separate matrices were produced for article-by-theme and article-by-variable.
In the second stage, matrices were transformed into square co-occurrence
matrices (themes*themes and variables*variables) using UCINET’s affiliations
(2-mode to 1-mode) with mode as columns. The resulting square matrices
served as inputs for network analysis. Finally, the network analysis was
applied in UCINET using centrality measures and visualized using NetDraw.
In social networks, aside from the frequency of items (the total count of
appearances of each theme or variable across the included studies) for
centrality measurement used three different measurements. Degree to reflect
if a node is central and has direct co-occurrence connections with others.
Closeness to represent how efficiently a node can reach or be reached by all
others in the network. Betweenness for the extent to which a node lies on the
shortest paths connecting other nodes (Freeman, 1978). In addition to degree,
closeness, and betweenness, eigenvector centrality was measured to identify
the most influential nodes. In last step, to visualize the network both matrices
separately put into Netdraw (Borgatti, 2002).

5. DISCUSSION & CONCLUSION
This research applied a systematic literature review and network -based
analysis to explore the intersection of astrology and marketing by framing it
with the concept of AstroLogical Marketing. The research was inspired by the
increasing commercial visibility of astrology in today’s consumer culture as
well as the relatively fragmented nature of existing literature. Prior research
has examined astrology in the context of consumer beha vior, digital
engagement, segmentation, and symbolic consumption. However, there was
a lack of an integrated perspective capable of explaining how these studies
collectively shape an emerging marketing domain. Therefore, this study
employed the SPAR-4-SLR protocol to systematically identify, screen and
analyze existing literature. Social network analysis were also used to visualize

The results show that AstroLogical Marketing has largely developed around a
narrow conceptual framework. Existing studies predominantly adopt a
consumer behavior perspective and focus on the visible astrological
components such as zodiac signs (Çatlı, 2021; Uslu Divanoğlu and Uslu,
2019), horoscopes (Sobreira & Manosso, 2024; Sharma & Bhandari, 2023),
and superstitious beliefs (Sierra et al. 2017; Jian Wang et al., 2012). Thematic
and variable-level networks also show that concepts like purchasing behavior,
attitude, social media engagement, belief, and zodiac signs are structurally
central in the literature. These findings align with previous research that
frames astrology as a belief system but also as a symbolic and identity related
mechanism that influences consumption patterns (Sierra et al., 2018),
attitudes (Limna et al., 2025) and even emotional aspects (Temcharoenkit &
Johnson, 2021). In this sense, the findings suggest that literature has mainly
interpreted astrology as a symbolic consumption phenomenon embedded in
digital and behavioral marketing processes. At the same time, results also
show that AstroLogical Marketing is still conceptually and methodologically
limited. The dominance of consumer behavior perspectives suggests that
several marketing disciplines are still understudied despite their potential
relevance to astrology -related consumption. Therefore,  research
opportunities for future appear in areas such as branding, strategic marketing,
entertainment marketing, promotion, and service marketing. Similarly, the
symbolic, emotional, and identity-oriented nature of astrology might help
future studies to explore further integration of astrological meanings into
various marketing topics. The increasing visibility and commercialization of
astrology in social media also helps content -based engagement for
AstroLogical Marketing to development further in digital environments.
This research contributes to marketing literature in several ways. First, it
conceptualizes AstroLogical Marketing as an emerging research domain that
integrates fragmented studies at the intersection of astrology and marketing.
Second, with Social Network Analysis this research reveals the intellectual
structure of the field through thematic and variable-level connections. Finally,
by offering a structured conceptual foundation and identifying dominant
research streams and gaps, it contributes to the future theoretical
development of AstroLogical Marketing.
This research also provides several practical implications for marketing
researchers and practitioners. The findings suggest the potential role of
astrology-related symbols, stories, and signs in consumer engagement
strategies, particularly in digital environments where personalization and self-
expression are valued. Marketers should pay attention to how astrology
themes are integrated into social media content, entertainment -focused
campaigns, and symbolic branding practices to better understand emergent
consumer interaction patterns. In addition, the results show that demographic
information (e.g., birth-related markers) can offer alternative perspectives for
segmentation and targeting strategies in some market situations. The study
