Abstrak
Live streaming commerce telah mengubah praktik perdagangan digital dengan menggabungkan video waktu
nyata, performa streamer, affordance platform, partisipasi audiens, dan fungsi pembelian langsung dalam satu
lingkungan platform. Namun, penelitian yang ada masih banyak memahami fenomena ini melalui kerangka
perilaku konsumen, terutama model Stimulus–Organism–Response, niat membeli, kepercayaan, pembelian
impulsif, nilai yang dirasakan, dan keterlibatan pelanggan. Artikel ini bertujuan untuk meninjau bagaimana studi
live streaming commerce mengonseptualisasikan audiens, mekanisme komunikatif, dan proses psikologis yang
membentuk respons audiens dalam ruang perdagangan digital waktu nyata. Penelitian ini menggunakan metode
tinjauan literatur sistematis dengan Scopus sebagai satu-satunya basis data. Pencarian menghasilkan 197 artikel
setelah penerapan filter yang telah ditentukan. Setelah penyaringan judul dan abstrak, 75 studi diprioritaskan untuk
penelusuran teks lengkap, dan 21 artikel teks lengkap dianalisis melalui sintesis tematik. Hasil kajian menunjukkan
bahwa penelitian live streaming commerce masih didominasi oleh model kuantitatif berbasis variabel dan luaran
pembelian. Namun, konsep komunikasi seperti interaktivitas, kehadiran sosial, interaksi paraso sial, flow,
penggunaan dan gratifikasi, metadiscourse interaksional, kelebihan informasi, dan keterlibatan memperlihatkan
bahwa audiens tidak hanya berperan sebagai konsumen yang merespons stimulus pemasaran. Audiens juga hadir
sebagai penonton, partisipan, subjek relasional, dan aktor komunikatif dalam interaksi platform waktu nyata.
Artikel ini menawarkan Audience Communication Psychology Framework untuk memahami live streaming
commerce sebagai lingkungan komunikasi termediasi yang dinamis, partisipatif, sosial, dan psikologis.

Abstract
Live streaming commerce has transformed digital commerce by integrating real -time video, streamer
performance, platform affordances, audience participation, and direct purchasing functions within a single
platform environment. However, existing studies have predominantly examined this phenomenon through
consumer behavior frameworks, particularly the Stimulus–Organism–Response model, purchase intention, trust,
impulsive buying, perceived value, and customer engagement. This article aims to review how live streaming
commerce studies conceptualize audiences, communicative mechanisms, and psychological processes that shape
audience responses in real-time digital commerce spaces. This study employed a systematic literature review using
Scopus as the sole database. The search identified 197 records after applying predefined filters. Following title
and abstract screening, 75 studies were prioritized for full-text retrieval, and 21 full-text articles were analyzed
through thematic synthesis. The findings show that live streaming commerce research remains dominated by
quantitative, variable-based models and purchase-related outcomes. Nevertheless, communication-oriented
concepts such as interactivity, social presence, parasocial interaction, flow, uses and gratifications, interactional
metadiscourse, information overload, and involvement indicate that audiences are not merely consumers
responding to marketing stimuli. They also operate as viewers, participants, relational subjects, and
communicative actors embedded in real-time platform interactions. This article proposes the Audience
Communication Psychology Framework to understand live streaming commerce as a dynamic mediated
communicative environment that involves platform structures, streamer performance, audience psychological
processing, and communicative-behavioral responses simultaneously.
Keywords: Audience Communication Psychology, Consumer Behavior, Live Streaming Commerce,

Live streaming commerce has emerged as
one of the most dynamic forms of digital
commerce because it integrates real-time video,
product demonstration, two -way

a single platform environment. Unlike
conventional e-commerce, which largely relies
on product images, written descriptions, and

and affective shopping experience. Audiences
do not merely view products; they can ask

This development indicates an important

and participatory model. In live streaming
commerce, products are not simply displayed;
they are narrated, demonstrated, and
persuasively framed through interactions
between streamers and audiences. Streamers
function as communicators, sellers,

figures. Audiences, in turn, are not merely
potential buyers but also viewers, commenters,
community members, and communicative
participants. Studies on live-stream shopping
show that users can interact with streamers and
other viewers in real time through chat,
comments, likes, and direct responses, making
the shopping experience more social than
traditional e-commerce (Ma, 2021). Recent
Indonesian studies in Lektur also show that
digital marketing and platform-based public
communication rely on narrative consistency,
visual storytelling, audience engagement, and
directive speech acts in live shoppi ng
interactions (Addina et al., 2026; Aryanie &
Zulfiningrum, 2026; Hasibuan et al., 2026).
Academic interest in live streaming
commerce has increased alongside studies on
purchase intention, impulsive buying, trust,
social presence, perceived usefulness,
perceived value, flow, customer engagement,
and continuous intention. Existing studies have
shown that live streaming commerce possesses
distinctive characteristics, including real-time
interaction, vivid product demonstration, social
presence, streamer credibility, parasocial
interaction, and platform affordances. For
example, research on presence in livestream
shopping demonstrates that consumers
experience both physical presence and social
presence, which influence purchase intention
through concentration, perceived control, and
enjoyment (Yin et al., 2023).
Despite these developments, the literature on
live streaming commerce remains strongly
shaped by consumer behavior frameworks.
Many studies employ the Stimulus-Organism-
Response model, Technology Acceptance
Model, Post-Acceptance Model, Innovation
Diffusion Theory, IT Affordance, and various
purchase intention models. Within this pattern,
platform features, streamer characteristics,
product cues, or interactivity are typically
positioned as stimuli; trust, enjoyment, flow,
perceived value, or perceived usefulness as
organismic states; and purchase intention,
impulsive buying, customer engagement, or
continuous intention as responses. For instance,
a study on impulsive buying in live streaming
commerce applies the S-O-R framework to
explain the relationships among attractiveness,
expertise, product usefulness, purchase
convenience, perceived enjoyment, perceived
usefulness, and the urge to buy impulsively (Lee
& Chen, 2021).
Consumer behavior frameworks have made
important contributions by empirically
explaining relationships among variables.
However, this approach also has limitations.
First, live streaming commerce audiences are
often reduced to consumers, customers, buyers,
or users responding to marketing stimuli.
Second, communicative dimensions such as
interactivity, parasocial interaction, social
presence, streamer performance, and
metadiscourse are often treated merely as
supporting variables leading to purchase
intention. Third, the synchronous, social,
emotional, fast-paced, and information -
intensive character of live streaming commerce
has not been fully conceptualized as a
communicative environment that shapes how
audiences experience, interpret, and respond to
commercial messages.
Several studies have begun to show that live
streaming commerce cannot be understood
solely through the logic of purchase behavior.
Kang et al. (2021) demonstrate that customers
in live streaming commerce do not only
purchase products; they also produce
comments, e-WOM, gift-giving, and online
reviews as forms of customer engagement

In addition, the Uses and Gratifications

satisfy needs for entertainment, social
interaction, self-expression, and media

The emergence of parasocial interaction and
information overload further strengthens the
need for a communicative reading of live
commerce. Streamers can build perceived
closeness through personal forms of address,
familiar language, live demonstrations, a nd
responses to audience comments. At the same
time, audiences face dense flows of
information, including product claims, rapidly
moving comments, promotions, discounts, time
urgency, and social pressure to purchase.
Research based on the Elaboration Likelihood
Model shows that information overload may
shift audience processing from careful
evaluation of product attributes toward reliance
on peripheral cues related to the streamer (Cao
et al., 2025).
Based on this gap, this article argues for a
reframing from imported consumer behavior
frameworks toward audience communication
psychology. This reframing does not reject
consumer behavior theory; rather, it extends it
by positioning live streaming commerc e
audiences as viewers, participants, relational
subjects, and communicative actors. From this
perspective, purchase intention or impulsive
buying is not merely a response to marketing
stimuli but an outcome of interactions among
platforms, streamers, messages, communities,
and audience psychological processing.
This article is guided by four interrelated
research questions. First, it examines which
theoretical frameworks dominate live streaming
commerce research. Second, it investigates how
audiences are conceptualized in existing live
streaming commerce studies. Third, it identifies
the communicative and psychological
mechanisms that shape audience responses in
live streaming commerce. Fourth, it considers
how live streaming commerce can be reframed
through the perspective of audience
communication psychology.

2. METHODS
This study employed a systematic literature
review design to map, evaluate, and synthesize
the development of live streaming commerce
research, particularly in relation to how
audiences are conceptualized within a literature
dominated by consumer behavior frameworks.
The review was guided by PRISMA principles
to ensure that the search, selection, screening,
and reporting processes were conducted
transparently and systematically (Page et al.,
2021). The primary aim of the review was not
to conduct a statistical meta-analysis, but rather
a thematic synthesis, because the included
studies varied in terms of theory, constructs,
national contexts, methods, and research
outcomes.
Scopus was used as the sole database for this
review. Scopus was selected because of its
broad coverage of reputable journals and
conference proceedings in communication,
business, management, psychology,
information systems, decision sciences, and
digital commerce. The use of a single database
was intended to maintain consistency in the
search protocol, avoid duplication across
databases, and ensure that the analyzed articles
were retrieved from indexed academic sources.
The search was conducted using the Advanced
Search feature in Scopus. The final search
strategy was developed after preliminary
attempts produced an overly broad corpus,
particularly when general terms such as social
commerce were used. Therefore, the search was
refined by requiring the main live streaming
commerce terms to appear in the TITLE field,
while supporting constructs were searched in
TITLE-ABS-KEY. Final Scopus search string:

TITLE (
  "live streaming commerce" OR "live commerce"
OR "live shopping" OR
  "livestream shopping" OR "live stream shopping"

string and filters produced 197 records. After
title-abstract screening, 32 records were
excluded because they did not meet the
inclusion criteria. Of the remaining records, 75
studies were prioritized for full-text retrieval,
and the final thematic synthesis included 21
full-text articles. Before addressing each

The included studies indicate that live
streaming commerce is a relatively new
research area. All included studies were
published between 2021 and 2025, even though
the search protocol covered the period from
2010 to 2025. Most studies were conducted in

test variable-based models.

3.1 Dominant Theoretical Frameworks in
Live Streaming Commerce Research
The first research question asks what
theoretical frameworks dominate live streaming
commerce research. The synthesis shows that
the Stimulus-Organism-Response framework is
the most prominent theoretical lens. In many
studies, platform features, streamer
characteristics, interactivity, social presence,
product cues, media richness, or source
credibility are positioned as stimuli. Trust,
perceived value, perceived risk, flow,
enjoyment, satisfaction, involvement, or
perceived usefulness are positioned as
organismic states, while the final responses
usually include purchase intention, impulsive
buying, continuous intention, or customer
engagement.
The dominance of S-O-R is evident in Lee
and Chen’s (2021) study on impulse buying
behavior, which applies S-O-R to explain the
relationships among attractiveness,
trustworthiness, expertise, product usefulness,
purchase convenience, product price, perceived
usefulness, perceived enjoyment, and the urge
to buy impulsively in live streaming commerce.
Beyond S-O-R, several studies combine
consumer behavior frameworks with TAM,
PAM, IDT, IT Affordance, dual appraisal
models, and Flow Theory. For example, Li and
Liu (2021) combine S-O-R and Innovation
Diffusion Theory to explain the influence of
commodity price, commodity quality, real-time
interaction, platform reputation, anchor
professionalism, perceived risk, perceived
shopping value, and purchase intention.
Nevertheless, the corpus also contains
theories that are more closely aligned with
communication and media studies, including
Uses and Gratifications, Parasocial Interaction,
Interactional Metadiscourse, the Elaboration
Likelihood Model, and Flow Theory. These
theories remain less dominant than S-O-R and
purchase-related models, but they provide the
conceptual basis for reading live streaming
commerce as an audience experience rather than
solely as a consumer response. Table 5 is
included to map these theoretical frameworks
and to clarify how each framework functions
analytically within the reviewed corpus.

3.2 Audience Conceptualization in Live
Streaming Commerce Studies
The second research question asks how
audiences are conceptualized in existing live
streaming commerce studies. The synthesis
shows that most studies still use the terms
consumer, customer, buyer, shopper, or user.
These terms indicate that the subject of live
streaming commerce is predominantly
understood through a consumption and
transaction orientation. In this model, audiences
are usually conceptualized as actors who
receive stimuli from platforms or streamers and
produce responses such as purchase intention,
impulsive buying, or continuous intention.
However, some studies have begun to
expand this position. In Ma’s (2021) study on
live-stream shopping intention, audiences are
understood as media users seeking
gratifications, including enjoyment, social
interaction, social presence, self-presentation,
and utility. This indicates a shift from audiences
as buyers toward audiences as active media
users seeking social and psychological
experiences. Similarly, studies on engagement
and interaction show that live commerce
audiences may also act as commenters,
reviewers, information disseminators, gift -
givers, and community participants.
Thus, the term consumer is not fully
adequate to explain the subject of live streaming
commerce. Audiences in live commerce are
viewers, participants, community members,
relational subjects, and communicative actors.
They do not merely react to messages; t hey
watch, interpret, ask questions, respond, build
closeness, and participate in shaping the
communicative atmosphere of the live room.
This result directly supports the need to
reposition the audience from a transactional
subject to a communicative actor embedded in
real-time platform interaction.

3.3 Communicative and Psychological
Mechanisms Shaping Audience Responses
The third research question asks what
communicative and psychological mechanisms
shape audience responses in live streaming
commerce. The most dominant communicative
mechanism is interactivity, which appears in
various forms, including real-time interaction,
two-way communication, responsiveness,
personalization, synchronicity, platform
interactivity, media richness, live chat, and
comments. Interactivity distinguishes live
streaming commerce from conventional e -
commerce because audiences can communicate
with streamers, read responses from other
viewers, receive direct answers, and experience
social dynamics synchronously.
At the platform level, interactivity functions
as communicative infrastructure. Kang et al.
(2021) show that customers in live streaming
commerce can interact in real time with
broadcasters and other customers, while
broadcasters function as information sources
and customers act as co-creators through online
reviews and comments. This indicates that
interactivity should not be understood merely as
a technical feature, but as a communicative
structure that enables the formation of trust,
social presence, par asocial attachment,
engagement, and audience participation. In a
similar context, Addina et al. (2026) show that
directive speech acts in TikTok live shopping
function to guide, request, offer, suggest,
advise, and recommend, thereby reinforcing the
role of host communication in persuasion and
audience engagement.
The reviewed studies also identify several
psychological mechanisms shaping audience
responses, including trust, perceived
enjoyment, flow, social presence, physical
presence, perceived usefulness, perceived
value, perceived risk, satisfaction, involvement,
arousal, pleasure, and information overload.
Presence is particularly important. Yin et al.
(2023) distinguish physical presence and social
presence in livestream shopping. Physical
presence refers to the feeling of being in a real
shopping environment, while social presence
refers to the feeling of being with others in a
digital interaction space. The study shows that
physical presence influences purchase intention

3.4 Reframing Live Streaming Commerce
through Audience Communication
Psychology
The fourth research question asks how live
streaming commerce can be reframed through
the perspective of audience communication
psychology. The synthesis shows that
communicative and psychological concepts are
already present in the literature, but they remain
dispersed and are often positioned as supporting
variables for purchase intention. Concepts such
as interactivity, social presence, parasocial
interaction, flow, information overload,
involvement, uses and gratifications, and
metadiscourse need to be integrated into a more
communication-centered framework.
In this reframing, platforms are understood
as communicative environments that provide
affordances, synchronicity, media richness, and
information density. Streamers are understood
as communicators, relational figures, and
persuasive actors. Audiences are understood as
viewers, participants, community members, and
communicative actors. Audience responses are
understood not only as purchase intention, but
also as engagement, watch int ention,
continuous intention, e-WOM, social sharing,
and communicative participation.

3.5 Theoretical Saturation Of Consumer
Behavior Frameworks
The review shows that live streaming
commerce research is characterized by what can
be described as theoretical saturation of
consumer behavior frameworks. Most studies
use relatively similar frameworks to explain
how stimuli from platforms, streamers,
products, or shopping environments influence
consumers’ psychological states and generate
behavioral responses such as purchase
intention, impulsive buying, continuous
intention, or customer engagement. S-O-R is
the most dominant model because it simplifies
live commerce into a sequence of stimulus,
organism, and response.
However, conceptual limitations emerge
when live streaming commerce is continuously
understood through the logic of purchase
intention or impulsive buying. Audiences are
primarily understood as consumers reacting to
stimuli, rather than as communicative subjects
who watch, interpret, ask questions, respond,
build relationships, and help shape the
interactional atmosphere. Consumer behavior
frameworks remain useful, but they are
insufficient for explaining the communicative
complexity of live commerce.

3.6 Communication Mechanisms Hidden
Inside Consumer Models
Although the literature is dominated by
consumer behavior frameworks, the review
shows that many communicative mechanisms
are already present within these models.
Interactivity, social presence, parasocial
interaction, two -way communication,
responsiveness, synchronicity, media richness,
source credibility, and interactional
metadiscourse appear repeatedly, but are often
treated as intervening variables leading to
purchase intention.
These concepts, however, are central to live
commerce communication. Interactivity is not
merely a technical feature that facilitates
transactions; it is a communicative condition
that enables audiences to ask questions, receive
responses, read comments, o bserve social
reactions, and feel involved in a live situation.
One contribution of this review is therefore to
reposition these communicative concepts from
the margins to the center of analysis.

3.7 Repositioning the Audience: From
Consumer to Communicative Actor
This review highlights the need to reposition
the subject of live streaming commerce from
consumer to communicative actor. Terms such
as consumer, customer, buyer, or user are not
incorrect, but they are too narrow if used as the
only way to understand live commerce subjects.
In practice, individuals in live streaming
commerce watch, comment, ask questions,
follow streamers, build closeness, compare
information, and help shape the live room
atmosphere.
This repositioning is important because the
term consumer emphasizes transaction, whereas
audience allows analysis of watching,
experiencing, interpreting, and participating.
Live commerce audiences are not passive
audiences in the traditional sense; they are
communicative audiences who can produce
comments, influence the flow of live sessions,
provide social signals to other audiences, and
participate in shaping product meanings within
the platform space.

3.8 Information Overload and the Limits
of Rational Processing
Live streaming commerce also transforms
the conditions under which audiences process
messages. In conventional e -commerce,
consumers relatively have time to read product
descriptions, compare prices, examine reviews,
and make decisions. In live commerce, t his
process occurs in a fast, social, emotional, and
information-dense environment.
Information overload demonstrates that
audiences do not always make decisions
through calm and systematic information
evaluation. They often make decisions under
conditions of social stimulation, emotion,
speed, and temporal pressure. Peripheral
processing in live commerce is therefore not
merely an individual choice; it is also shaped by
the communication architecture of platforms
and the persuasive performance of streamers.

3.9 Audience Communication Psychology
Framework
Based on the synthesis, this article proposes
the Audience Communication Psychology
Framework in Live Streaming Commerce. The
framework does not reject consumer behavior
frameworks, but extends them. Its focus is not
only on the relationship between stimulus and
purchase response, but also on how audiences
experience commercial communication
cognitively, affectively, socially, and
relationally.
The framework consists of four main
components: Platform Communication
Environment, Streamer Communicative
Performance, Audience Psychological
Processing, and Audience Communicative and
Behavioral Responses. The model should be
understood as a dynamic communicative loop
rather than a linear pathway. Audience
comments can influence streamer style;
streamer responses can strengthen social
presence; social presence can increase
engagement; audience engagement can
reinforce community atmosphere; and
community atmosphere can in turn shape
audience perception and decision-making.

3.10 Proposed Conceptual Framework
Based on the synthesis of 21 full -text
articles, this review proposes the Audience
Communication Psychology Framework in
Live Streaming Commerce. The framework
was developed to address the limitations of
consumer behavior approaches that dominate
live streaming commerce research. It
conceptualizes live streaming commerce as a
real-time mediated communicative
environment in which platforms, streamers,
audiences, products, messages, communities,
and transactional mechanisms intersect
simultaneously.
The first component is the Platform
Communication Environment. Live streaming
commerce platforms are not merely technical
spaces for displaying video and facilitating
transactions; they are communicative structures
that shape how audiences view, respond,
participate, and make decisions. Key elements
include real-time interaction, synchronicity,
media richness, live chat and comments,
platform affordances, and information density.
The second component is Streamer
Communicative Performance. In live streaming
commerce, streamers are not merely sellers but
communicators, performers, opinion leaders,
entertainers, and relational figures. Key
elements include source credibility, expertise,

4. CONCLUSION
This systematic literature review shows that
live streaming commerce research has
developed rapidly in recent years, particularly
between 2021 and 2025. Based on the synthesis
of 21 full-text articles, the review finds that live
streaming commerce research remains strongly
dominated by consumer behavior frameworks,
particularly S-O-R, purchase intention,
impulsive buying, trust, perceived value,
perceived usefulness, customer engagement,
and continuous intention. These frameworks
provide important insights into how stimuli
from platforms, streamers, products, and live
commerce environments influence consumers’
psychological states and behavioral responses.
However, this review also shows that live
streaming commerce cannot be adequately
understood merely as a new variation of e -
commerce or social commerce. It is a real-time
communicative space that brings together
platforms, streamers, audiences, products,
communities, and persuasive messages within a
synchronous, fast-paced, interactive, emotional,
and participatory digital environment.
Audiences do not only buy or intend to buy;
they watch, ask questions, comment, read
responses from other viewers, build closeness
with streamers, experience social and physical
presence, face information overload, and
participate in live community dynamics.
Based on these findings, this article proposes
the Audience Communication Psychology
Framework in Live Streaming Commerce. The
framework conceptualizes live streaming
commerce as a dynamic communicative loop
consisting of four components: Platform
Communication Environment, Streamer
Communicative Performance, Audience
Psychological Processing, and Audience
Communicative and Behavioral Responses.
This model extends consumer behavior
approaches by placing communication,
relationality, social presence, parasoc ial
closeness, affective experience, information
overload, and audience participation at the
center of live streaming commerce analysis.
