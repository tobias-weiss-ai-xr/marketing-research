ABSTRACT
Managementandcostaccountinghavebecomeessentialtoolsforsupportingstrategicdecision-making,efficient
resource utilization, and organizational performance in modern businesses. Beyond traditional functions such
as budgeting and financial reporting, contemporary accounting practices incorporate advanced techniques to
improve cost control, performance measurement, and competitive advantage in an increasingly globalized
and technology-driven environment. This study emphasizes the importance of accurate cost information in
planning,controlling,andevaluatingbusinessoperationswhileaddressingchallengesinimplementingeffective
accounting systems. Using the MOORA (Multi-Objective Optimization on the Basis of Ratio Analysis)
method, the research evaluates five alternatives (A1–A5) based on four criteria: Wear Resistance (P1), Load
Carrying Capacity (P2), Coefficient of Friction (P3), and Environmental Impact (P4). The findings reveal
that A3 secured the highest overall ranking, demonstrating superior performance across the selected criteria,
whereas A2 received the lowest ranking. The results confirm that the MOORA method provides a reliable
framework for corporate management and cost accounting decision-making by identifying the most effective
alternative for improved organizational performance.
1. INTRODUCTION
Management and cost accounting are essential components of a company’s financial strategy, providing critical insights for
decision-making, planning and control. These disciplines focus on the identification, measurement, analysis and reporting
of financial and non-financial information to help managers make informed decisions to achieve organizational goals
[1]. Unlike financial accounting, which is primarily concerned with historical data and external reporting, managerial
accounting emphasizes providing information that supports forward-looking, operational, and strategic decisions. Cost
accounting, a subset of management accounting, focuses on capturing the costs of producing goods or services. It involves
tracking, analyzing and reporting costs to provide insights into cost control, profitability and performance [2]. Cost
accounting helps in pricing a product, managing costs and improving cost efficiency, which ultimately contributes to better
financial performance. Decision Support: Both management and cost accounting provide essential data that help managers
make informed decisions about pricing, budgeting, investments, and resource allocation. Planning and Controlling: These
accounting procedures are instrumental in setting financial goals, monitoring progress and implementing appropriate
actionstoensureachievementoforganizationalgoals[3]. PerformanceEvaluation: Byanalyzingcostdataandperformance
metrics, management accounting helps evaluate the efficiency and effectiveness of various departments, processes and

2. MATERIALS AND METHODS
In this study, Multi-Objective Optimization by Ratio Analysis (MOORA) method was used to evaluate and rank various
alternatives based on key parameters related to management and cost accounting. The objective is to evaluate alternatives
on a number of criteria, including benefit and benefit parameters, to determine the most appropriate option. Evaluation
parameters Eight evaluation parameters were considered, which were divided into four beneficial parameters and four
ineffective parameters: Cost efficiency: measures the financial efficiency and cost-effectiveness of each alternative. The
normalized values are multiplied by their respective weights to obtain a weighted normalized result matrix. This measure
includes the importance of each criterion in the decision-making process. MOORA Method: The MOORA method was
used by calculating the ratio of benefit to futility parameters for each alternative. Evaluation parameters Weighted Rating:
The performance of each alternative is multiplied by the weights assigned to each parameter, resulting in a weighted score.
This hierarchy highlights the strengths and weaknesses of each alternative relative to the criteria. Analysis: The weighted
scoreswereanalyzedtodeterminetheoverallperformanceofeachalternative. Thisanalysisinvolvescalculatingevaluation
values and ranking the alternatives based on their scores. Ranking: This equal weighting (0.25 for each parameter) ensures
a balanced consideration of all factors. Weighted Score: The normalized data is multiplied by the corresponding weights to
produce a weighted score for each alternative across all parameters. This step highlights how each alternative performs
relative to the others when considering the overall importance of each criterion. MOORA Method Application: The
Multi-Objective Optimization by Ratio Analysis (MOORA) method was used to aggregate the weighted scores for each
alternative into an evaluation value. This method helps identify the best performing option by comparing the ratio of
benefits to non-benefits. Ranking: Based on the evaluation values obtained through MOORA method, the alternatives
are ranked from most favorable to most favorable. The ranking provides a clear indication of which alternative provides

3. CONCLUSION
This study, various management and cost accounting alternatives are systematically evaluated to determine the most
effective approach to improve organizational performance. The analysis considers important factors such as cost efficiency,
customersatisfaction,safetyandimplementationcomplexitywhichisessentialforeffectivedecisionmakinginmanagement
and cost accounting. The findings reveal that different approaches offer distinct advantages depending on an organization’s
specific needs and priorities. For example, lean accounting (Alternative 3) emerged as a better performance, better overall
balance, especially in reducing costs and streamlining processes to facilitate strong security and implementation. This
makes it a very useful choice for companies looking to increase efficiency and reduce waste. Target costing (alternative
5) demonstrated strong performance, particularly in areas such as cost efficiency and customer satisfaction, making it a
viable option for companies focused on meeting market-driven cost targets and customer demands. However, this can
present some challenges in implementation that organizations must manage carefully. On the other hand, traditional cost
accounting(alternative1)andactivity-basedcosting(ABC)(alternative2)werefoundtobelessfavorableoverall,primarily
due to their complexity and lack of adaptability to modern business environments. These methods may still be appropriate
for specific contexts, particularly where detailed cost monitoring is required, but they are generally outperformed by
contemporary approaches. Standard costing (alternative 4) provided a consistent performance but did not stand out in any
particular area, making it a reliable but not exceptional choice for companies looking for a straightforward and familiar
accounting system. In conclusion, this study underscores the importance of choosing a management and cost accounting
system that is consistent with the company’s strategic goals and operational needs. Although overall lean accounting and
target costing seem to be more effective, the choice of accounting system should be tailored to the specific context of the
company, considering its unique challenges and objectives. The use of a structured assessment method such as MOORA
proves invaluable in guiding such important decisions, ensuring that all relevant factors are considered in a balanced and
objective manner.
