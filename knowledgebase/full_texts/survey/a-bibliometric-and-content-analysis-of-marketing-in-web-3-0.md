Abstract
The transition to Web 3.0, characterized by blockchain technology, decentralization, and the metaverse,
presents a transformative paradigm for marketing strategies, yet the existing literature remains notably
fragmented. To address this gap, this study systematizes the intellectual structure of Web 3.0 marketing to
identify fundamental trends, key forces, and future research directions. Adhering to the PRISMA framework, a
comprehensive bibliometric and content analysis was conducted on 121 articles sourced from the Web of
Science Core Collection spanning 2009 to 2025, utilizing Bibliometrix, VOSviewer, and CitNetExplorer. The
ﬁndings reveal a rapid surge in academic interest beginning in 2021 and identify three core thematic clusters:
blockchain’s role in marketing strategies, ethical and trust issues in decentralized environments, and marketing
in the metaverse and digital technologies. Furthermore, the study synthesizes key theoretical foundations,
including the Theory of Planned Behavior, Social Exchange Theory, Stakeholder Theory, and MarTech Theory,
that are essential for interpreting these digital phenomena. By pinpointing critical research gaps such as cross-
platform behavioral patterns, demographic and cultural factors, and ecosystem governance, this paper
develops a cohesive conceptual framework and proposes a structured agenda to guide future scholarly
investigations and practical implementations in the Web 3.0 era.
Introduction
In the modern world of information technology and digital transformation, Web3 represents an evolutionary
step in the development of the internet (Almeida et al. 2013). Digital transformation has become popular in
recent years as it represents a path toward the integration of digital technologies into business processes. As a
consequence, companies have undertaken various initiatives to explore new technologies and to capitalize on
its related beneﬁts. Web3 represents a new paradigm in which blockchain technologies, decentralization, and
cryptocurrencies intertwine with traditional online spaces, unlocking special opportunities for various spheres.
At its core, Web3 (or Web 3.0) is a vision for a new, decentralized version of the internet. Instead of relying on a
few gigantic tech companies to host platforms and manage user data, Web3 is built on blockchain technology,
where data is distributed across networks of computers, and users retain control over their own information
and digital assets. Web3 isn't just one technology, it's a stack of different concepts working together to remove
the "middleman" from digital transactions and interactions.
The promise of Web3 is to shift the internet's economic model. In the current Web2 landscape, users provide
the content and the data, while platforms capture the vast majority of the ﬁnancial value through advertising.
Web3 aims to redistribute that value back to the creators and users through digital tokens, offering a more
equitable, though currently highly experimental and volatile, digital economy. Web 3.0 allows for interactive
campaigns in the metaverse. The Metaverse is a term used to describe a hypothetical virtual space where
people can interact with digital environments and other users in a shared online world. Companies can create
virtual showrooms where users can explore and purchase both digital and physical products. It enables
consumers to engage brands directly without the involvement of middlemen, so that one can own data, control
privacy, and digitally consent.
One of the key areas signiﬁcantly impacted by Web3 is marketing. Marketing in Web3 offers new opportunities
for brands and enterprises to interact with their audiences, grounded in the principles of decentralization,
transparency, and security based on blockchain technology (Zhang et al. 2022). The increased collection of

user data by major corporations to reﬁne advertising and improve the user experience raised notable privacy
concerns. Interaction and engagement on social platforms come at cost (Popescu et al., 2025). In Web 2.0,
user data is collected and subsequently marketed to other interested parties. In response to these concerns,
Web 3.0 began to take shape around 2020. Powered by blockchain technology and artiﬁcial intelligence, Web
3.0 aims to establish a more equitable internet in which users could maintain control over their personal data.
Web 3.0 is also called decentralized Web or Semantic Web.
Consumer behaviour in the real world may differ from that in the virtual world (Park and Lim 2023). In order to
monitize on this concept, companies need to understand these differences. The metaverse concept can
reshape consumer psychology, leading to an overhaul in conventional marketing strategies. With a virtual
presence, businesses strive to deliver an unparalleled consumer experience. The metaverse has thus become a
favoured concept following substantial investments by well- known brands. The metaverse experienced
signiﬁcant traction during the COVID- 19 pandemic and (Dwivedi et al. 2022). Hence, there is an increasing
research interest in reassessing the viability of the metaverse )Lim, Das, et al. 2025). The development of Web
3.0 technologies, including blockchain, the metaverse, and NFTs, has revolutionized marketing by introducing
innovative methods for consumer engagement and building brand loyalty (Popescu et al., 2025). By 2030, the
global Web 3.0 market is projected to reach $33.53 billion, with a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of
49.3% (Grand View Research, 2023). Despite this remarkable growth, a comprehensive understanding of the
fundamental trends driving marketing strategies within Web 3.0 remains elusive, necessitating systematic
exploration of the patterns and principles shaping this transformative era.
Despite the importance and potential that this new technology offers for marketing, research on the subject
remains underdeveloped (Wasiq et al. 2023). To the best of the researcher’s knowledge, no reviews were
undertaken on Web3 technology. We found numerous reviews on the metaverse (Wider et al., 2024; Popescu et
al., 2025; and Lim et al., 2024).
While existing literature offers valuable insights into speciﬁc aspects of Web3 marketing, these perspectives
often remain fragmented. Research on the virtual world began to gain momentum after the launch of the ﬁrst
virtual reality headset for gaming in the mid- 1990s (Steuer 1995). Barrera & Shah (2023) and Cheah & Shimul
(2023) examined the metaverse's implications for marketing, with a particular focus on how immersive virtual
spaces transform consumer interactions. This became increasingly relevant as over 30% of global brands are
expected to implement metaverse-based marketing strategies by 2030 (PricewaterhouseCoopers, 2022).
Building on this, Dwivedi et al. (2023) and Zhang et al. (2022) explore the metaverse's potential to revolutionize
consumer research through real-time virtual engagement.
Blockchain's marketing impact has received speciﬁc attention through speciﬁc applications. These
applications, growing at 60% annually (Brave Browser Report, 2023), emphasize transparency and relevance in
reshaping consumer-brand relationships. Boukis (2019) and Gleim & Stevens (2021) further examine
blockchain's implications for brand-consumer dynamics, though their focus remains primarily on isolated
applications. NFTs and blockchain technologies, as crucial drivers of Web3 marketing, have garnered
signiﬁcant scholarly attention. However, these studies lack a unifying framework connecting their ﬁndings to
broader marketing practices. Recent meta-analyses provide valuable perspective: Kukreja et al. (2023) employ
scientometric approaches to map key research trends, and Kumar et al. (2021), Marthews & Tucker (2023)
investigate technology's inﬂuence on marketing strategies and Wider (2024) who undertook a Metaverse

bibliometric review. These reviews highlight the ﬁeld's dynamic and interdisciplinary nature while revealing the

This fragmented research landscape presents a critical challenge. Despite detailed individual studies, a
comprehensive understanding of the patterns shaping Web3 marketing remains lacking. This study addresses
this gap through bibliometric analysis, identifying commonalities, trends, and key forces driving marketing in
the Web3 era. This paper aims to develop a cohesive framework that advances the ﬁeld and informs future
research directions through bibliographic and content analyses. Bibliographic analysis enables a review of
existing scientiﬁc works, articles, and research, providing an opportunity to gain a holistic understanding of the
current state of the topic under study and to identify the main directions of development for marketing in the
context of Web3.
The paper is organized as follows. In the next section, we introduce the theoretical review, methodology, and
data extraction process. Then, the results of the bibliometric analysis are presented, outlining the most
inﬂuential journals, articles, institutions, and authors. We also note the research clusters revealed and the
conceptual structure of the literature. After that, we identify theories through the content analysis, coupled with
the bibliographic techniques. Finally, we present conclusions and future research directions for studying
marketing in the Web3 metaverse.
Literature Review
Literature reviews allow researchers to enhance their understanding of prior work in their ﬁeld, enabling them
to identify research gaps and future research routes. To address fragmented perspectives and build a cohesive
understanding of marketing strategies within the Web3 ecosystem, it is essential to examine the theoretical
frameworks underpinning current research. Theories guide researchers in addressing research questions
effectively and consolidating constructs and variables by establishing meaningful and tested relationships.
Theoretical foundations provide a lens through which researchers and practitioners can interpret the
transformative impact of blockchain, NFTs, and the metaverse on marketing practices. This study identiﬁes key
theories - such as the Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB), Social Exchange Theory (SET), Stakeholder Theory,
and Martech theory, that serve as the basis for analyzing consumer behavior, loyalty programs, and
decentralized governance in Web3 contexts. This study conceptualizes the literature from a macro perspective
by exploring and examining the themes in the literature.

Bibliometric analysis represents a methodology for analyzing research outputs, and it is born from library and
information science ﬁelds. It allows scholars to objectively analyze a large corpus of studies using quantitative
techniques and it combines two overarching categories of analysis: performance and science mapping. By
establishing these theoretical underpinnings, we set a structured foundation for the subsequent bibliometric
analysis, enabling a more nuanced evaluation of research trends, gaps, and contributions. This approach
ensures that the analysis is not only systematic but also aligned with established frameworks, enhancing its
relevance and rigor in identifying future research directions.
Methodology
Researchers are increasingly using content analysis in literature reviews to evaluate existing knowledge and
gain a deeper understanding of intellectual structures (Gaur & Kumar, 2018). Objectivity and reliability are
considered essential traits for conducting high-quality contextual analysis (Dionysis et al., 2022; Lacy et al.,
2015). Bibliometric analysis, one of the most effective quantitative approaches, uncovers connections between
publications, authors, institutions, and countries (Klarin, 2024). By integrating such bibliometric mapping, we
Page 6/34

can visualize the most productive authors, institutions, and countries within a speciﬁc ﬁeld, enabling further
analysis and revealing trends in literature production over time (Jing et al., 2024). In this study, we apply
bibliographic coupling, citation analysis, co-citation analysis, and keyword co-occurrence, combined with
content analysis to investigate our research questions as implemented in most bibliometric reviews
(Castañeda et al. 2022).
The research methodology was based on the PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and
Meta-Analyses) methodology as the founding framework for the bibliometric analysis conducted (Paul et al.,
2021). It involves a structured and transparent approach to conducting and reporting the bibliometric
systematic review. Based on the PRISMA protocol to identify the relevant research to review, we created a
research protocol. Search keywords were used to identify the relevant literature by deﬁning a well- structured
query and combining representative keywords. To improve the quality of our research scheme, we engaged in
preliminary research to discover the search keywords more representative of the ﬁeld of analysis. First, we
created a research protocol that speciﬁed the time period covered (2009–2025) and the types of journals
included in this paper. Then the paper selection process began with the application of predeﬁned inclusion and
exclusion criteria to screen and select relevant studies, with discussion of the screening process and explicit
reasons for exclusion at each stage. Data extraction was then implemented using a standardized method to
ensure consistency, capturing key information. The data synthesis phase involved both narrative and
quantitative methods, adhering to established statistical guidelines for pooling results (Alon et al. (2018). This
rigorous approach ensured that the review is comprehensive, systematic, and transparent, thereby increasing
the reliability and impact of the research ﬁndings.
For data analysis and visualization, we used the Bibliometrix R package, VOSviewer (version 1.6.20), and
CitNetExplorer. Bibliometrix was used for performance analysis (e.g., most-cited authors, journals, and
institutions); VOSviewer enabled network visualization of co-citations, keyword co-occurrence mapping, and
coupling; and CitNetExplorer facilitated exploration of the citation network and cluster identiﬁcation. The basis
for our study is a collection of bibliographic data from the Web of Science (WoS) database. WoS is widely
accepted in academia and includes studies published in high-quality journals. Since the research topic is just
beginning to gain signiﬁcant attention in the scientiﬁc community, we chose this database because it contains
over 170 million records and includes journals that adhere to a rigorous peer-review process, ensuring the
inclusion of high-quality research. WoS offers a comprehensive collection of bibliographic data and references,
making it a valuable resource for analyzing scientiﬁc output and conducting bibliometric analyses. The WOS
database is well-known for its high quality and comprehensiveness, making it an excellent choice for
bibliometric research (Wider et al., 2024). It is the world’s most widely used and most reliable research
publication and citation database, providing comprehensive coverage of the world’s leading research.
We conducted a backwards search by referring to major keywords “marketing”, “Web3”, and “blockchain” to
avoid missing any crucial parts of the literature from the journal list derived from Clarivate. The “title- abstract-
keywords” and “title” search parameters were used to identify articles that align with our research aims. There
was no restriction on the year of the articles, as we wanted to maximise the understanding of the metaverse
research curve. The ﬁrst article we found was from 2009, hence, our review included articles from 2009 to
2025. Following Bretas & Alon (2021) and Alon et al. (2018), we implemented a detailed data search and
extraction process. Table 2 illustrates the detailed data search and extraction process.

For the screening process, the duplicate and redundant articles from the journal database were cross- checked

provided minimal insights on Web3 were excluded from consideration in this study.

Co-citation analysis involves measuring how other publications cite works together; that is, the more times the
documents are cited together, the closer their relationship is, and thus they are considered to be in the same
research ﬁeld (Klarin, 2024). “Cluster analysis and co-citation analysis are also crucial analysis elements in
bibliometric research” (Jing et al., 2024). Figure 2 shows the co-citation of our bibliography database with three
delimited clusters.
The red cluster includes studies that examine how blockchain technologies can transform traditional models
of interaction between brands and consumers. This cluster contains three streams: Blockchain and Consumer
Relationships, Blockchain and Supply Chains, and Systematic Review of Blockchain Technologies in Marketing.
This cluster carries a more technical focus. The cluster includes the most-cited sources: as the absolute leader
in citations, Saberi et al. (2019) explore the technical aspects of blockchain technology and highlight the
barriers and challenges we may face during its implementation and adaptation to new environments. Casino et
al. 2019; Risius & Spohrer, 2017; Tapscott & Tapscott, 2018; Yli-Huumo et al. 2016 approach blockchain from a
less practical and more research-focused perspective, presenting systematic and bibliographic reviews. The
remaining researchers begin integrating marketing into the blockchain context. They also speculate on how
blockchain could reshape marketing strategies and advertising campaigns (Boukis, 2019; Ertemel, 2018; Gleim
& Stevens, 2021; Parssinen et al., 2018; Rejeb et al., 2020).
The blue cluster focuses on trust-building mechanisms in blockchain-based marketing environments, with an
emphasis on customer trust and decentralized exchanges. This cluster serves as a kind of bridge between the
other two, as researchers explore the complexity of implementing Web3 technologies, its impact on building

how blockchain technology enables trust in digital exchanges through its transparency, tamper-proof nature,
and decentralized control. Blockchain can deﬁnitely facilitate ethical marketing practices by creating
transparent systems for tracking goods and verifying authenticity (Tan & Salo, 2023). In the co-citation cluster

To understand the underlying themes, we conducted a thorough examination of their objectives using a
thematic analysis to develop a contextual understanding and advance our knowledge of these concepts. The
authors used VOS viewer for clustering as it is one of the most widely adopted software for thematic analysis
(Lim, Kumar, & Donthu 2024). Cluster and thematic analyses were conducted using a co- occurrence network
with the author's keywords. The study conducted a keyword co-occurrence analysis to uncover the ﬁeld's
thematic structure. Keywords in academic papers are terms chosen and crafted by the authors, typically
serving as key components that encapsulate the essence of the paper's content (Kwon, 2018). The selected
keywords enable the emergence of speciﬁc concepts, related issues, and trends using the applied
methodology and bibliometric analysis tools (Strozzi et al., 2017).
The keyword co-occurrence analysis reveals three distinct clusters that represent important themes at the
intersection of blockchain technology, marketing, and emerging digital environments such as the metaverse

framework, and then extend it by incorporating more comprehensive dimensions of blockchain research. The
researchers observed that blockchain studies primarily focused on technological advancements; however, they
identiﬁed signiﬁcant research gaps in application-oriented studies, particularly in management and value
creation domains (Parssinen et al., 2018). Recent studies have explored more specialized research directions,

A new stream emerges as an offshoot of the original "introductory" stream. Among the most impactful studies

of co-citation and bibliographic coupling patterns, we conﬁrm the signiﬁcance of this cluster, as this research
direction has evolved to explore consumer behavior in the context of long-term blockchain implementation.
The next direction within the cluster represents a technical stream, focusing on the characteristics of
blockchain transparency. This stream initiates the formulation of key features that are fundamental to arguing
for the advantages of Web3 technologies in marketing. These essential characteristics comprise data privacy

transformative capacity to redeﬁne trust mechanisms within digital ecosystems, subsequently inﬂuencing
marketing strategies and economic exchanges in decentralized networks (Tan & Saraniemi, 2023a).
These insights laid the groundwork for a new research direction examining blockchain's role in CRM systems.
Treiblmaier & Petrozhitskaya (2023) suggest that blockchain can revolutionize customer loyalty programs by
offering consumers control over their loyalty points and enhancing transparency in rewards systems. This new
approach to loyalty programs empowers customers by eliminating intermediaries and enabling more direct and
ﬂexible exchanges between consumers and businesses (Treiblmaier, 2018). Through experimental research,

strategies for cryptocurrency trading websites. These studies make signiﬁcant contributions by empirically
testing theoretical propositions established by preceding researchers.

Conclusions
The metaverse based on Web3 technology provides an opportunity for businesses and society, it also poses
unique challenges. It may improve inclusivity and bring consumers together, but it may also polarize them by
fostering communities and exacerbating social disparities. It can make socializing easy by overcoming
geographical boundaries and simultaneously enable consumers to isolate themselves from the real world.
Understanding it better will help others understand its complexities.
The Web3 ecosystem introduces new ethical challenges, particularly in balancing transparency and privacy.
Blockchain’s transparency fosters trust but raises concerns about data privacy, requiring frameworks that
protect sensitive information while maintaining credibility. Researchers such as Tan and Salo (2023)
emphasize the importance of developing ethical marketing approaches that integrate transparency with
privacy safeguards. Exploring how these principles can be applied across industries and cultural contexts is
vital for creating responsible marketing practices. Emerging technologies like NFTs and the metaverse further
complicate the ethical landscape. These tools enable immersive consumer experiences but pose risks of
psychological manipulation and over-commercialization. Golf-Papez et al. (2022) highlight the need for ethical
guidelines to prevent exploitation in synthetic environments.
We maintain that research would beneﬁt from future investigations into the four categories we identiﬁed in our
analysis: forecasting customers’ behavior, the social exchange between customers, the challenges of
developing MarTech, and the role of stakeholders in the Web3 ecosystem. The literature review reveals several
critical directions for future research in consumer behavior within Web3 environments. Taylor (2022) identiﬁes
a signiﬁcant research gap concerning the psychological implications of immersive digital marketing,
particularly in metaverse environments. This gap becomes especially relevant for gaming-based metaverse
marketing, where consumer-advertiser interactions occur in virtual contexts. Building on Marikyan et al. (2022)
ﬁndings about response eﬃcacy and cybersecurity threats, future research should investigate how users'
Page 20/34

conﬁdence in blockchain services evolves over time and across different applications. Additionally, while
Alkhudary et al. (2023) emphasize trust's role in mitigating perceived risks, more research is needed to
understand how this trust-risk relationship varies across different Web3 platforms and user demographics.
Kumar et al. (2021) and Taylor (2022) highlight the critical need for investigating cross-platform behavioral
patterns in Web3 environments.
Web3 is about who owns the internet, while the Metaverse is about how we experience the internet. Web3 (or
Web 3.0) is a vision for the "backend" or infrastructure of the internet. The Metaverse is a vision for the
"frontend" or user interface of the internet. It refers to a network of immersive, interconnected, 3D virtual worlds
where people can socialize, work, play, and learn using digital avatars. For a Metaverse to be truly open and
persistent, users need to be able to own their digital goods (like avatar clothing, virtual real estate, or tools) and
move them seamlessly between different virtual worlds. Web3 provides the economic and ownership layer that
makes this possible. This research advances the ﬁeld of Web 3.0 marketing through a systematic bibliometric
and content analysis of 121 articles. By identifying three main research clusters: Blockchain's Role in
Marketing, Marketing in the Metaverse and Digital Technologies, and Ethical and Trust Issues in Marketing—
this study provides a comprehensive framework for understanding the evolution of marketing within
decentralized digital ecosystems. A key contribution is the integration of previously fragmented perspectives in
existing literature. The study synthesizes insights across various theoretical frameworks, including the Theory
of Planned Behavior, Social Exchange Theory, Stakeholder Theory, and Martech theory, offering a cohesive
understanding of the ﬁeld. Through advanced bibliometric techniques such as co-citation and bibliographic
coupling, this research reveals the evolution of marketing strategies and emerging trends like NFT-driven
consumer engagement and blockchain-based loyalty programs.
Furthermore, this study contributes to academic discourse by proposing a structured research agenda. It
identiﬁes critical gaps in the literature, including the psychological impact of immersive technologies, the long-
term effects of blockchain-enabled trust mechanisms, and the cultural aspects of Web 3.0 consumer behavior.
These insights provide valuable direction for both academic research and practical marketing implementation
in the Web 3.0 environment.
The emergence of Web 3.0 and the metaverse presents a paradigm shift that offers unprecedented
opportunities for businesses while simultaneously posing unique societal and ethical challenges. This study
advances the ﬁeld of Web 3.0 marketing by conducting a systematic bibliometric and content analysis of 121
articles, successfully synthesizing a historically fragmented body of literature. By identifying three primary
research clusters—Blockchain’s Role in Marketing, Marketing in the Metaverse and Digital Technologies, and
Ethical and Trust Issues in Marketing—this research provides a comprehensive structural framework for
understanding the evolution of decentralized digital ecosystems. Furthermore, the study grounds these
developments in established theoretical frameworks, including the Theory of Planned Behavior, Social
Exchange Theory, Stakeholder Theory, and MarTech Theory, offering a cohesive lens through which to analyze
consumer-brand interactions in this new era.
Despite the rapid projected growth of the Web 3.0 market, signiﬁcant research gaps remain that necessitate
targeted academic inquiry. This review identiﬁes four critical categories for future investigation: forecasting
consumer behavior, exploring the dynamics of social exchange, addressing the complexities of MarTech
development, and examining the roles of stakeholders within the Web 3.0 ecosystem. Future studies must
Page 21/34

investigate the psychological implications of immersive digital marketing, evaluate the cross-platform
transferability of behavioral intentions, and assess the long-term sustainability of emotional connections
fostered by virtual inﬂuencers and gamiﬁed loyalty programs. Additionally, there is a pressing need to
understand how cultural differences shape consumer perceptions of digital ownership and value co-creation.
Future studies should explore how behavioral intentions transfer across different Web3 platforms and examine
the evolution of user attitudes as they interact with multiple Web3 technologies simultaneously. Research
should also expand on Dionysiset al. (2022) framework by investigating the impact of subjective norms in
various Web3 contexts and the role of perceived behavioral control in adopting emerging Web3 technologies.
Furthermore, there's a pressing need to develop new theoretical frameworks tailored to Web3 environments,
given their unique characteristics and challenges. This could include investigating how traditional behavioral
theories adapt to decentralized, immersive, and blockchain-based contexts, and how integrating TPB
components (attitudes, subjective norms, and perceived behavioral control) manifests in cross-platform user
experiences.
A signiﬁcant portion of the literature we reviewed examines value creation, in which customers gain exclusive
rights to digital assets, fostering a sense of exclusivity and integrating these virtual items into their self-identity
(Bilgihan et al., 2024). Despite the growing body of research on NFTs, the speciﬁc impact of decentralized
branding and distribution strategies remains underexplored, as Hofstetter et al. (2022) suggest. Further
investigation is needed into how gamiﬁcation elements inﬂuence long-term brand loyalty, as indicated by Wang
et al. (2023). Additionally, a notable research gap exists in understanding the role of cultural differences in
shaping value co-creation processes. Cultural factors signiﬁcantly inﬂuence how users perceive digital
ownership, uniqueness, and social value, as well as their engagement with gamiﬁed systems.
Future studies should explore how cultural dimensions shape consumer expectations and interactions in Web3
environments, thereby providing a more nuanced understanding of global consumer behavior. By combining
elements of digital ownership, consumer psychology, and gamiﬁcation, the body of research advances our
understanding of how Web3 technologies reshape consumer-brand interactions.
Loyalty programs are a critical mechanism for social exchange in Web3, evolving into systems of reciprocal
value exchange between brands and consumers. Blockchain-powered programs offer beneﬁts such as
transparent product information and exclusive privileges, surpassing traditional models through enhanced
incentive structures and gamiﬁcation. However, signiﬁcant research gaps remain.One major gap lies in
understanding the long-term sustainability of emotional connections created through gamiﬁed and virtual
engagement mechanisms, such as avatars and virtual inﬂuencers (Meyer et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2023).
Further studies should assess the impact of these connections on customer retention and brand loyalty over
time.
Another key area is the effectiveness of blockchain-enabled reward structures compared to traditional loyalty
programs (Peres et al., 2023; Treiblmaier & Petrozhitskaya, 2023). Research is needed to explore how these
programs inﬂuence consumer behavior and whether they provide superior value in fostering loyalty. The
evolution of transactional rewards and emotional engagement as Web3 technologies mature also warrants
further investigation (Peng, 2021; Xie et al., 2023). This includes examining how the balance between these
elements shifts in response to technological advancements and changing consumer expectations.
Page 22/34

Demographic factors present an additional gap, with little understanding of how different cultural and market
contexts affect consumer responses to virtual relationship-building tools, such as avatars and inﬂuencers
(Ghali et al., 2024). Future research should explore how these tools can be optimized for diverse consumer
segments. Lastly, while blockchain's role in content validation and electronic word-of-mouth (eWOM) has been
explored, its long-term effectiveness in cross-platform dynamics remains unclear. Studies should address the
integration of eWOM across Web3 environments and the credibility challenges of blockchain-veriﬁed systems.
Marketing technology (Martech) in Web3 represents a transformative force reshaping traditional marketing
approaches. While blockchain technology offers potential to eliminate traditional marketing roles through
direct producer-consumer connections, marketing continues to adapt by integrating these technologies into
existing processes (Haynes & Hietanen, 2023). The evolution of Martech manifests across several
interconnected technological dimensions. At its foundation, Web3 Martech relies on blockchain infrastructure,
enabling peer-to-peer transactions while navigating practical limitations in scalability, energy consumption, and
token volatility. Parssinen et al. (2018) note that while blockchain promises to revolutionize online advertising,
widespread adoption awaits solutions to technical challenges. However, Dwivedi et al. (2022) emphasize its
current value in enhancing transparency and enabling secure consumer data sharing, particularly in loyalty
programs and the traceability of marketing efforts. The advancement of immersive technologies marks
another crucial evolution in Martech.
The complexity of modern MarTech reveals several critical research directions. Future studies should
investigate how marketing professionals can effectively integrate emerging software and hardware skill sets
into metaverse implementations, and examine new targeting approaches as consumer touchpoints expand
across digital economic exchanges, social relationships, and environmental interactions. Key research
trajectories include: the transformation of advertising execution and evaluation methods, the development of
comprehensive marketing parameters, the integration challenges of personalized recommendation systems,
and the advancement of engagement metric analysis in Web3 environments. This agenda emphasizes the
need to understand both individual technological components and their collective impact on marketing
approaches, providing crucial insights for strategy development in the evolving Web3 landscape.
Current research reveals a complex hierarchical ecosystem in Web3 marketing. Alkhudary et al. (2023) and
Sung et al. (2023) map key stakeholders, including creators, intermediaries, investors, speculators, and
consumers, in value creation processes. Lee et al. (2023) and Peng (2021) highlight how major technology
companies dominate decision-making power, with Google and Facebook controlling 60–70% of the American
advertising market. Liu et al. (2021) demonstrate how these companies exploit technical advantages to control
user data, creating unequal value chain positions. Dwivedi et al. (2023) and Ghali et al. (2024) emphasize the
importance of governance frameworks, and Antsipava et al. (2024) discuss coordination between regulators
and opinion leaders; signiﬁcant research gaps remain.
Future research in Web3 marketing ecosystems should focus on multiple critical dimensions. Academic
investigation is needed to understand the long-term implications of power disparities on smaller stakeholders
and to develop effective international governance structures. Scholars should examine how diverse
stakeholder interests can be integrated while maintaining a balance between technological innovation and
participant protection. Furthermore, research should address the development of sustainable value distribution
Page 23/34

mechanisms and explore effective approaches to cross-border regulatory coordination in Web3 marketing
environments.
Understanding the evolution of trust dynamics among stakeholder groups is another crucial area for
investigation, as it underpins the sustainable and equitable development of the Web3 marketing ecosystem.
Future research must focus on establishing practices that prioritize consumer well-being while leveraging the
potential of decentralized technologies, ensuring that trust and ethics remain at the forefront of Web3
marketing's evolution.
Finally, the ethical and structural realities of Web3 marketing demand rigorous scrutiny. While decentralized
technologies promise equitable value distribution, current ecosystems are still heavily inﬂuenced by the
dominance of major tech companies, highlighting a critical need for research into international governance
structures, cross-border regulatory coordination, and sustainable value distribution. Marketers must also
navigate the delicate balance between blockchain's inherent transparency and the necessity of consumer data
privacy, establishing ethical guidelines that protect users from psychological manipulation in synthetic
environments. Ultimately, by outlining these fundamental trends and proposing a structured research agenda,
this study equips both scholars and practitioners with the foundational insights required to navigate and shape
the future of marketing in the decentralized digital economy.
