Purpose. This study aims to systematically review and analyse scientific literature on sustain ability
in the MICE (Meetings, Incentives, Conferences, and Exhibitions) industry published between 2016
and 2025.
Results. Through a visualised analysis of 110 core documents, this study reveals a significant growth
trajectory in the field of MICE sustainability over the past decade, with a notable surge in publications
since 2019, indicating rapid expansion in both depth and breadth of research. The United States, the
United Kingdom, China, and Australia emerged as the most active and influential countries in terms
of publications and centrality, while the Journal of Sustainable Tourism led as the most prolific
journal with 25 articles, establishing itself as a key academic platform in this domain. Research
coverage spanned the environmental (34 art icles), social (39 articles), and economic (12 articles)
pillars of sustainability; however, a prevailing focus on single dimensions was observed, highlighting
a scarcity of integrated systemic research across all three. The evolution of research hotspots shifted
from earlier themes of management and policy towards more recent concerns such as participant
perceptions, green quality, and social innovation.
Scientific novelty. The novelty of this research primarily lies in its cross-dimensional comprehensive
analytical perspective. Unlike previous literature reviews that often confined themselves to
environmental sustainability, this study integrates the environmental, social, and economic pillars
into a unified bibliometric framework, offering a more holistic  and balanced knowledge map.
Furthermore, the research demonstrates significant foresight, with its time span extending to 2025,
capturing emerging trends in the post -pandemic era where digital transformation and sustainability
are deeply intertwined withi n the MICE industry. The study also explicitly identifies, for the first
time, the potential evolutionary paths and application values of nascent technologies such as Artificial
Intelligence and Big Data in driving green transitions within the industry, th ereby providing new
avenues for theoretical construction in this field.
Practical value. On a practical level, this research provides essential insights for stakeholders within
the MICE industry. Firstly, it provides an evidence -based management framework for event
organisers and policymakers, underscoring the imperative of reducing negative environmental
impacts through green certifications and carbon footprint management (e.g., the “three-hub model”).
Secondly, the study highlights that enhancing particip ants’ “green perceived quality” significantly
boosts their willingness to support sustainable initiatives, offering theoretical backing for corporate
brand marketing and social responsibility strategies. Lastly, the research encourages the industry to
actively adopt digital tools (such as carbon credit systems and social media engagement) to foster
pro-environmental behaviours among the public, thereby facilitating a substantive transition of MICE

1. Introduction
Tourism is one of the most important and fastest -growing economic activities
globally (De Bruyn et al., 2022). MICE tourism, which comprises meetings, incentives,
conferences, and exhibitions, has also become one of the fastest -growing and most
important sectors within the entire industry (Martín-Rojo & Gaspar-González, 2024).
However, with the growth of MICE events, environmental issues are also on the rise.
MICE events involve large -scale population movements and gatherings, which can
have negative impact s on resources such as air, water, and land, as well as on local
residents (Dickson & Arcodia, 2010). For example, there are a series of issues such as
carbon emissions from event participants, the disposal of booth construction materials,
and noise pollution.
Statistics show that in 2019, the greenhouse gas emissions from the B2B trade
show industry in the United States and Canada were 6.1 million metric tons, equivalent
to the combined annual carbon footprint of 395,000 people in the United States. The
average carbon emissions from participants in B2B trade show flights were 120
kilograms (265 pounds) of carbon dioxide (Trade Show Executive, 2022). In 2015, the
United Nations adopted the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development, which at its
core proposed 1 7 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) across three dimensions:
social, economic, and environmental (United Nations, 2015). The exhibition industry
has also gradually reali sed the need for sustainable development. The Status of
Sustainability in the Exhibition Industry, published by UFI, shows that 73% of
exhibitors said their companies are highly committed to sustainability and that all
departments are transitioning towards sustainable development (UFI, 2021). For
example, the “Heartwarming Amsterdam ” project at RAI Amsterdam saved nearly
1,500 kilograms of food waste during the event. Also, GL events used bio-waste sorting
to turn leftover food into waste for venue gardens (UFI, 2019).
Some research and practices have been carried out on the sustaina bility of the
MICE industry. For example, integrating sustainable actions and concepts into MICE
events, such as exhibitions (Chirieleison & Scrucca, 2017), sports events (Huang &
Chiu, 2024; Shi & Zhang, 2022), and festivals (Laing, 2017), investigating participants'
perceptions and feelings towards sustainability (Li et al., 2020), and the sustainable
exploration and practice of exhibition organisers (Pradnyani et al., 2024; Hamid et al.,
2012).
However, overall, research on sustainability in the context of MICE is still lacking
(An et al., 2021). Moreover, there are currently few articles focusing on the overall
development trends and future directions of sustainability in the MICE industry.
Therefore, this study reviews the current state of research on M ICE sustainability
through bibliometric analysis, summari sing the existing problems and development
trends in MICE sustainability research. This study will help stakeholders in the MICE
industry to promote sustainable development in the sector. This paper aims to find

aiming to reveal future research hotspots and developmental directions for both

2. Review of literature
2.1. Sustainability
The term “sustainability” is so extensive that it is difficult to define precisely.
Many studies have attempted to define it, with different perspectives often leading to
different definitions. The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (2024) defines
sustainability as “Everything that we need for our survival and well -being depends,
either directly or indirectly, on our natural environment. To pursue sustainability is to
create and maintain the conditions under which humans and nature can exist in
productive harmony to support present and future generations.” UCLA (2016) defines
sustainability as “the integration of environmental health, social equity and economic
vitality in order to create thriving, healthy, diverse and resilient communities for this
generation and generations to come. The practice of sustainability recognises how these
issues are interconnected and requires a systems approach and an acknowledgement of
complexity.” However, the most widely disseminated, recognised and used definition
of sustainability comes from the United Nations Brundtland Commission, which
defines it as “meeting the needs of the present without compromising the ability of
future generations to meet their own needs” (Butlin, 1989).
2.2. Sustainability in MICE industry
With the introduction of the 17 Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) by the
United Nations across three dimensions – social, economic, and environmental – more
and more individuals, organisations, companies, and institutions in the MICE industry
have joined the movement towards sustainability. In 2021, UFI launched the Topics
Transferred to the Net Zero Carbon Events (NZCE) initiative. This initiative aims to
achieve net-zero emissions in the MICE industry by 2050 through the establishment of
greenhouse gas emission methodologies and collaboration with partners, suppliers, and
clients to drive industry-wide sustainable development (JMIC, 2021).
Meanwhile, research on the sustainability of the MICE industry continues to

(1) Perceived Attributes of Sustainability and Willingness to Support in MICE
Events (Huang & Chiu, 2024; Zhang et al., 2021). For example, Li et al. (2020)

sustainability of exhibition visitors. Vila-Lopez and Küster-Boluda (2020) examined

quality of life.
(2) Relationships Among Social, Economic, and Environmental Sustainability in
MICE Events (Orefice & Nyarko, 2020; Yoo & Cho, 2021; Ruan et al., 2022). For
example, Cooper and Alderman (2020) demonstrated that while the cancellation of
some sports events may result in economic losses, it also prevents a significant amount
of greenhouse gas emissions. Sports events need to strike a balance between the
environment and the economy to play a greater role in society. There is also research
on how event organisers can create social and environmental benefits for communities
while maintaining business viability (Schulenkorf et al., 2018).
(3) Promoting MICE Industry Sustainability through New Technologies and
Theories (Chirieleison & Scrucca, 2017; Salgado -Barandela et al.,  2021). The 2022
Beijing Winter Olympics used new construction techniques to reduce the use of
building materials and employed a WeChat mini -program to promote sustainable
concepts (Shi & Zhang, 2022). Japan ’s “three-hub model” at the 2019 Rugby World
Cup measured the scope of transportation emission reductions, resulting in a reduction
of 14,126.9 tons of carbon dioxide emissions (Ito & Higham, 2023).
(4) Learning from Case Studies of MICE Events for Experience and Improvement
(VanWynsberghe, 2014; Guizzardi et al., 2017; Tsaur et al., 2015). For example, Chen
et al. (2022) compared the cases of major events held in Guangzhou and Birmingham
to discuss whether major events can bring sustainable development to cities.
2.3. Bibliometric research in the MICE industry
Bibliometrics primarily involves the application of basic or advanced statistical
techniques to examine previous research findings (Koseoglu et al., 2016), including
subjects, samples, and methods, to quantitatively estimate the communication s
published about a certain discipline through calculations (Serrano et al., 2019).
Bibliometric studies can help researchers understand the following issues about the
current research field (Zupic & Čater, 2014; Serrano et al., 2019): 1.  It can assess the
research and publication performance of individuals and institutions. For example,
which scholars are at the forefront of the field or which journals publish a large number
of scientific studies. 2. It can reveal the issues, structure, evolution, and research trends
in the current discipline.
Bibliometrics has been extensively applied in tourism literature for the evaluation
of journals and their authors (Koseoglu et al., 2016). The application of bibliometrics

3. Methodology
3.1. Research design
This study selects papers from the Web of Science Core Collection database (WoS)
as the data source. Web of Science is the world-recognised scientific paper database. It
indexes more than 12,000 highly regarded influential journals worldwide (Romanova,
2024). Moreover, for bibliometric analysis, it is considered the most appropriate database
(Ding, 2020). Based on the articles and papers in the Web of Science Core Collection
database, this study uses Boolean operators in combination with various indicators to
select a set of papers for analysis. The screening process is shown in Figure 1.

After removing duplicates from the search results, a total of 351 papers were obtained.
3.3. Inclusion and exclusion criteria
This study further screened the 351 papers obtained. By examining the article titles,
abstracts, and keywords, 241 irrelevant papers were excluded. For example, the
combination of the terms “green” and “Incentive” may retrieve articles related to the hotel
industry, which is not relevant to the MICE industry. Finally, 110 papers were obtained.
3.4. Bibliometric analysis
Bibliometric analysis was conducted using VOSviewer and CiteSpace 6.4.1. The
analysis was divided into two parts: performance analysis and science mapping. In the
performance analysis, the study selected authors, countries, and journals as indicators
to analyse the knowledge structure in the field of MICE industry sustainability. Co -
occurrence analysis of cited documents and keywords was then performed to describe
the current research hotspots. A timeline view was used to display research hotspots in
chronological order. Finally, an analysis of burst words and their cited documents was
conducted to reveal future research directions.

4.3. Discussion
This study analy sed the MICE industry in the context of sustainability using
VOSviewer, CiteSpace, and Scimago Graphica software to understand the trends in the
sustainable domain of the MICE industry. The rise of terms such as nature-based events,
behavioural intention, green perceived quality, and social innovation over the past five
years (see Figure 8) indicates that r esearch is moving towards enhancing the
environmental awareness and support for sustainability among MICE participants
through subjective norms and behavioural perceptions. The environmental knowledge
that participants gain from MICE activities will permeate into their daily lives, and in
the future, there should be continuous, strengthened, and educational promotion of
sustainable MICE activities to change people’s attitudes and behaviours. This requires
ongoing exploration and research into sustainable MICE (Wong et al., 2020). In terms
of authors and journals in the sustainable domain of the MICE industry, D. Getz,
J. Mair, and A. Collins are among the most cited authors, and the Journal of Sustainable
Tourism is the journal that has published the most ar ticles in this field (25 articles),
with Tourism Management Perspectives ranking second (7 articles). Among the
countries studying the sustainability of the MICE industry, there is a significant gap
between the top four countries and those ranked fifth and below (see Table 3). Australia,
ranked fourth, has published 20 articles, while Canada, ranked fifth, has published 7
articles. According to the intermediary centrality index, the United States, China, and
the United Kingdom have played important roles in cooperation and connections with
other countries. In terms of citation strength over the past two years, Japan ranks first.
Most of the papers published in Japan are dedicated to the social impacts brought about
by large -scale sports events. For example, Kinoshita et al. (2024) investigated the
impact of positive and negative events from the 2020 Tokyo Olympics on residents ’
happiness and found that governments and sports organi sations need to develop
strategies to enhance residents ’ happiness. This study has effectively conducted a

5. Conclusions
In this paper, we conducted a bibliometric review of research in the field of MICE
industry sustainability. This paper analysed relevant studies in this field over the past
decade and explained the current research hotspots. Using VOSviewer, CiteSpace, and
Scimago Graphica software, we reviewed the development trends in this field and
listed the most influential authors, journal s, and countries that have contributed to
MICE sustainability research worldwide. First, it provides researchers studying the
sustainability of the MICE industry with the main development trends in the industry.
Through this study, one can understand the c hanges in research hotspots in the field
over the past 10 years and identify possible future development directions. Second, this
paper describes the research findings and issues related to sustainability in the MICE
industry, obtaining very important results. In the clustering networks of keywords and
co-cited documents, it can be observed that the current research on environmental,
social, and economic sustainability in the MICE industry is not connected. Most studies
focus on only one aspect. However, in  reality, economic benefits are often related to
the environment, and social aspects are often involved in the relationship between the
economy and the environment. Participants in the MICE industry can use these
structures to enhance the interdependence and mutual reinforcement of the three pillars
of MICE industry sustainability, thereby better promoting the sustainability of the
MICE industry. Cooperation among countries on research topics is also relatively weak,
and the current research hotspots are sh ifting towards environment impacts, CO 2
emission, and carbon footprint.
The development trend of the MICE industry sustainability field is also related to
the development of digital technology. Nowadays, the application of digital technology
has penetrated all walks of life, but digital technologies such as the Internet of Things,
artificial intelligence, and big data still have great potential and room for exploration

6. Limitations and future research
This bibliometric analysis, while providing a comprehensive overview of
sustainability in the MICE industry, acknowledges several limitations inherent in its
methodology. Firstly, the study exclusively utilised the Web of Science Core Collection
(SSCI and SCI -EXPANDED) as its data source. This selective approach, while
ensuring data standardi sation and quality, may have inadvertently excluded relevant
publications indexed in other prominent databases such as Scopus and EBSC Ohost,
potentially leading to an incomplete representation of the global research landscape.
Secondly, despite efforts to construct a comprehensive keyword search strategy, the
dynamic nature of academic terminology suggests that alternative keyword
combinations might yield different sets of literature, indicating room for refinement in
future search protocols.
Building upon these limitations, several avenues for future research emerge.
Subsequent studies should endeavour to broaden their data collection by incorporating
multiple academic databases to achieve a more exhaustive coverage of the literature.
Furthermore, active engagement and communication with leading scholars in the
sustainable MICE field are crucial to gain deeper insights into emerging trends and to
collaboratively shape future research agendas, thereby minimi sing individual
researcher subjectivity. The identified potential for applying advanced technologies
such as Artificial Intelligence, Big Data, and Machine Learning to MICE sustainability
also presents a fertile ground for empirical and theoretical investigations, exploring
how these tools can facilitate more effective sustainable practices and decision-making
within the industry.
