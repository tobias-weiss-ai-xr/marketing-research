Abstract
In recent decades, marketing research has developed rapidly, yet studies
specifically focusing on marketing for children remain limited. According
to the Scopus database, children-oriented marketing is often overlooked,
despite its potential as a long -term strategy to build brand loyalty from
early childhood through adulthood. Many studies view marketing for
children as a crucial approach to sustain lifelong customer relationships.
This study aims to conduct a bibliometric analysis of marketing for
children by reviewing empirical studies published in Scopus, offering a
new perspective on this underexplored topic, particularly in Indonesia. A
systematic literature review (SLR) was employed, using titles, abstracts,
and keywords from Scopus within a defined ti meframe. VOSviewer
software was applied to map research trends, author collaborations, and
frequently used keywords. Results indicate that research on children ’s
marketing continues to evolve, with key themes including its impact on
children’s consumption behavior, the role of regulations, and
technological influences in shaping strategies. Prior studies highlight
children’s vulnerability to digital marketing, particularly their difficulty
distinguishing between advertising and organic content. This suggests a
need for further exploration of the long -term effects of marketing
practices on children and the effectiveness of regulatory measures across
countries. The findings provide practical insights for marketers,
policymakers, and academics in creating more responsible and ethical
marketing strategies in the digital age. Research on children ’s marketing
strategies remains scarce, especially in Indonesia. This study contributes
a fresh perspective to bridge existing gaps and encourages more
comprehensive exploration of the field.

Introduction
Marketing to children has become one of the most exciting and controversial areas in business and
marketing (Calvert, 2008; Desmond & Carveth, 2007 ; Lapierre et al., 2017). With the growing
influence of digital media and the increased accessibility of information, children are now more
exposed to advertising and promotions than ever before. Research by Nandita et al. (2025) shows
that children have significant spending power, not only for themselves but also for their families.
This makes them an attractive target for marketers. Marketing to children involves not only
products directly aimed at them but also strategies that  influence parents’ purchasing decisions.
Research by Senevirathna et al. (2022 ) shows that children often influence family purchasing
decisions, making them an important focus in marketing strategies. Therefore, marketers need to
understand children ’s behavior and preferences to design effective campaigns (Martensen &
Grønholdt, 2008).
Ethics is another important aspect of marketing to children (Jill Austin & Lynn Reed, 1999).
Many countries have implemented strict regulations on advertising aimed at children, given their

vulnerability to advertising influence (Calvert, 2008). Research by Lapierre et al. (2017) shows that
advertisements aimed at children often exploit their limited understanding of commercial purposes,
which can lead to unhealthy consumption behavior (Zhang , 2018). Therefore, marketers should
consider ethical aspects in their strategies (Nicholls & Cullen, 2004). In addition, advances in
technology and social media have changed the way children interact with brands. Research by
Núñez-Gómez et al. (2020) shows that children are now more engaged with brands through digital
platforms, allowing marketers to reach them in a more interactive and engaging way. However, this
also creates new challenges for child privacy and data protection (Montgomery et al., 2017). Child
marketing should also consider cultural and social differences. Research by Cook (2019) shows that
children’s preferences can vary significantly by cultural background. Therefore, marketers need to
conduct in -depth market research to understand the needs and wants of children in different
market segments (Smith et al., 2019).
In the context of globalization, marketing for children also faces challenges from
international brands entering local markets (Zhang, 2018). Research by Smith et al. (2019) shows
that international brands often have an advantage in resources and innovation, but they must also
adapt to local norms and values to succeed (Cairns et al., 2013). This creates an interesting dynamic
in marketing for children, where marketers must balance global and local strategies ( Senevirathna
et al., 2022 ). With these factors in mind, marketers should  develop a holistic and sustainable
approach to marketing for children. Research by Güler Yıldız et al. (2021) shows that an approach
that focuses on positive values and practices grounded in sustainability and social responsibility can
strengthen brand pe rceptions among children and their parents (Cook, 2019). Building on this
perspective, prior research further emphasizes that marketing to children extends beyond
immediate transactions to cultivating enduring relationships with young consumers (Núñez -
Gómez et al., 2020).
To advance understanding in this domain, the present article systematically examines key
dimensions of marketing to children, including children’s consumer behavior, effective marketing
strategies, ethical considerations, and the transformative role of technology. In doing so, it seeks
to generate theoretically grounded, managerially relevant insights for marketers, scholars, and
policymakers. Furthermore, by synthes izing prior research, this study identifies critical gaps and
delineates the evolving scope of the literature on marketing to children. Accordingly, the study is
guided by the following research questions:
RQ1. Is research on marketing for children still relevant to pursue in the future?
RQ2. What is the current distribution of research on marketing for children?
RQ3. What are the theoretical and practical implications from the perspective of future research?
A bibliometric analysis of all metadata related to marketing for children from the Scopus
database provides a comprehensive overview of the field over time.

Literature Review
Marketing to children refers to marketing strategies and communication practices designed to
influence children’s preferences, attitudes, and consumption behaviors toward products, brands,
or services. This form of marketing typically employs child -oriented messages, visual elements,
characters, and media channels that align with children ’s cognitive and emotional developmental
stages (Lapierre et al., 2017; Smith et al., 2019). Over the past decades, marketing directed at
children has become an attractive business strategy for many companies because children represent
both current consumer s and future loyal customers. As technology has evolved, approaches to
reaching children have changed significantly ( Naumovska et al., 2020). While marketing in earlier
periods relied heavily on traditional media such as television and print advertising, contemporary
marketing strategies increasingly utilize digital platforms, social media, and data-driven technologies
(Buchanan et al., 2018) . This transformation creates substantial opportunities for companies to
engage young consumers more effectively and interactively ( Nagyova, 2024 ). However, these
developments also raise important ethical, regulatory, and developmental concerns about children’s
vulnerability as consumers (Boyland et al., 2024).

Before the digital era, marketing to children largely relied on  traditional media channels
such as television, radio, and children’s magazines (Boyland et al., 2024). Television advertising, in
particular, became a primary tool for introducing products to young audiences, often embedded
within cartoon programs and family entertainment shows ( Evans, 2008). One main advantage of
this approach was its ability to reach a broad audience through a single, carefully designed
advertising campaign (Confos & Davis, 2016). Marketers also frequently used animated characters
and brand mascots to build  emotional connections with children and foster early brand loyalty
(Boyland et al., 2024). Other common strategies included sponsoring children ’s events, including
promotional gifts in product packaging, and advertising through toy catalogs . Products such as
snacks, toys, and children ’s clothing were commonly promoted with bright colors and popular
characters, which have proven effective in capturing children’s attention and interest (Güler Yıldız
et al., 2021).
Despite these advantages, traditional media also has several limitations, particularly in
evaluating campaign effectiveness. Measuring the direct impact of television or print
advertisements on children ’s purchasing decisions is considerably more difficult than tracking
engagement in digital environments (Raudeliūnienė et al., 2018). Unlike digital marketing, which
enables real -time monitoring of consumer interactions, traditional marketing channels provi de
limited data on consumer responses and behavioral outcomes ( Józefacki et al., 202 1). Moreover,
parental influence has historically mediated children ’s role in purchasing decisions , as parents
typically act as the final decision -makers in household consumption (Confos & Davis, 2016).
Consequently, although children may request specific products, parents generally retain ultimate
purchasing authority (Nicholls & Cullen, 2004).
As digital technologies rapidly evolve, companies now have greater opportunities to reach
children through more interactive and personalized marketing strategies (Smith et al., 2019). Social
media platforms, mobile applications, and streaming services such as YouTube Kids have become
important channels for reaching young audiences (Naumovska et al., 2020). One increasingly popular
approach is influencer marketing, in which companies collaborate with social media personalities
who have large child audiences (Núñez-Gómez et al., 2020). Children often follow YouTubers or
young celebrities on social media and tend to trust recommendations from figures they admire more
than traditional advertisements. Through this strategy, companies can establish stronger emotional
relationships with young consumers and enhance brand engagement (Evans, 2008).
Another emerging digital marketing strategy is gamification, which integrates game -like
elements into marketing activities to boost user engagement (Saleme et al., 2020). Gaming
applications and educational platforms increasingly introduce products to children in entertaining,
interactive ways (Boyland et al., 2024). For instance, several fast -food companies have developed
mobile games featuring branded characters and products, enabling children to interact with the
brand without perceiving the experience as traditional advertising ( Nagyova, 2024). Furthermore,
advances in artificial intelligence and data analytics enable companies to tailor marketing content
more precisely to children ’s preferences and behaviors  (Zhang, 2018). Algorithms can analyze
patterns in children’s digital activity and deliver personalized advertising messages based on their
media consumption habits (Smith et al., 2019).
Although digital marketing offers considerable opportunities, it also introduces new
challenges, particularly around protecting children’s privacy and online safety (Milkaite & Lievens,
2019). Historically, the primary concerns surrounding marketing to children included the difficulty
of measuring advertising effectiveness and the growing implementation of regulatory frameworks
governing child-targeted advertising (Harris & Jacobs, 2023). Since the 1990s, many countries have
introduced regulations that restrict the marketing of unhealthy foods to children and require greater
transparency in advertising practices targeting young audiences (Livingstone et al., 2019). These
regulations emerged in response to growing awareness of marketing ’s influence on children ’s
consumption patterns and health outcomes, particularly the promotion of high -sugar foods and
nutritionally poor products (Huang et al., 2024).
Today, marketing to children faces increasingly complex challenges (Andrews et al., 2020).
A key issue is protecting children’s privacy in digital marketing. In addition, children’s growing use

of social media increases the risk of exposure to inappropriate and manipulative content
(Pendergast, 2023). Children often do not recognize advertisements disguised as organic influencer
content, leaving them more vulnerable to commercial influence without understanding the motives
behind it (Finkelhor et al., 2021). Beyond regulatory challenges, companies also face pressure from
advocacy groups calling for more ethical and responsible marketing to children (Boyland et al.,
2024). Excessive or overly aggressive campaigns targeting children can trigger negative public
backlash, potentially damaging a brand’s image (Núñez-Gómez et al., 2020). Therefore, companies
need to adopt a more transparent strategy, ensuring advertising aimed at children aligns with their
educational values and well-being (Confos & Davis, 2016).
Marketing to children has changed significantly from the past to the present, with evolving
opportunities and challenges (Ekasasi, 2005; Harris & Jacobs, 2023; Józefacki et al., 2021; Lapierre
et al., 2017; Nagyova, 2024; Pendergast, 2023; Huang et al., 2024). Whereas marketing once relied
more on traditional media such as television and print, digitalization now offers more opportunities
to reach children in a more personalized and interactive way (Harris & Jacobs, 2023). However,
with the advent of new technologies, challenges such as privacy protection, strict regulations, and
ethical responsibility are becoming increasingly important (Montgomery et al., 2017). To meet these
challenges, companies must adapt their marketing strategies to be more responsible, transparent,
and in line with global regulatory developments (Nicholls & Cullen, 2004; Núñez -Gómez et al.,
2020). With a more ethical and innovative approach, marketing to children can remain an effective
business strategy without compromising children’s interests and well-being.

Research Methods
A systematic literature review (SLR) using a bibliometric approach statistically analyzes research
results based on metadata from prior studies (Bennett et al., 2024). Bibliometric analysis, conducted
both quantitatively and qualitatively in a transparent manner, supports knowledge dissemination
(Supardin et al., 2023 ). In this study, researchers used bibliometric (quantitative) and content
(qualitative) approaches to gain a thorough understanding of the existing literature (Secinaro &
Calandra, 2021). Bibliometric analysis was first introduced to help researchers iden tify and
understand research networks based on citations, keywords, or authors (Supardin et al., 2023).
As an initial step, researchers focus on keyword selection. Keyword selection can be done
with a macro (top-down) approach, ranging from broad search paths to general studies and topics.
Therefore, after considering gaps in previous research and the limited studies on  marketing for
children, this study includes marketing for children as a reference in the Article Title, Abstract, and
Keywords. This research analysis uses research metadata from the Scopus database  to help
researchers gain relevant knowledge in their field. This research uses the Scopus database because
it is comprehensive, reliable, and a primary reference for researchers worldwide . Scopus indexes
content only from reputable publishers to ensure the highest quality, with rigorous checks and
selection by an independent review board. Researchers also use the Scopus database for various
purposes, such as conducting literature reviews, identifying experts, and tracking research trends.
This study employed a systematic literature review combined with bibliometric analysis
to map the development of marketing for children research. The data were retrieved from the
Scopus database due to its extensive coverage of high -quality peer -reviewed p ublications. The
search used the keyword “marketing for children” in titles, abstracts, and keywords. The search
period covered publications from 2010 to 2025 to capture the evolution of research trends over
the past two decades. The initial search yielded  6,596 documents, which were then screened
against the inclusion and exclusion criteria. Only peer -reviewed journal articles written in English
and directly related to marketing for children were included. We excluded conference papers,
book chapters, and unrelated articles. After screening, we selected 545 articles for the final
analysis (see Figure 1). We conducted the bibliometric analysis using VOSviewer, which visualizes
keyword co -occurrence, author collaboration networks, and thematic clusters. This approach
provides insights into the intellectual structure and emerging research themes within the
marketing for children literature.

Figure 2 illustrates the trend in the number of research documents published each year
from 2010 to 2025 related to marketing for children. The early period (2010–2013) shows relatively
stable output, followed by a significant increase that peaks in 2016, indicating heightened scholarly
attention, potentially driven by evolving marketing practices and growing societal concern about
children as consumers. After a decline in 2017, publication levels recovered and remained relatively
stable between 2018 and 2020, suggesting sustained academic interest. A slight decrease in 2021 is
plausibly linked to COVID-19 disruptions that affected global research productivity.
A renewed upward trend emerges from 2022 to 2024, with publications reaching a second
peak in 2024, reflecting growing relevance, particularly amid  rapid expansion in digital marketing.
However, the sharp decline observed in 2025 should be interpreted with caution. Rather than
indicating a substantive drop in scholarly interest, this pattern is more likely attributable to
methodological limitations, such as incomplete indexing in the Scopus database at the time of data
extraction, publication time lags, and ongoing editorial processes. Such delays are common in
bibliometric analyses, where citation databases may not yet fully capture recently published or in -
press articles. Therefore, the apparent decline in 2025 does not undermine the overall trajectory,
which consistently demonstrates that marketing for children remains an active and evolving
research domain.

research could further examine parents’ role in teaching children to be more critical of marketing
strategies, especially in an increasingly complex digital age ( Nagyova, 2024). The blue cluster is

respond to education marketing, as well as how cultural factors influence their academic decisions
(Abdul Rasool et al., 2023).
Based on this analysis, several recommendations for future research in marketing to
children are offered. First, as artificial intelligence and ad personalization technologies continue to
evolve, more in-depth research on the impact of algorithm-based marketing on children is needed
(Mulligan et al., 2023; Nagyova, 2024). Many digital platforms use algorithms to serve personalized
content, including ads tailored to user preferences. However, children who are still in the cognitive
developmental stage may not yet be able to critically evaluate the information they receive (Calvert,
2008; Desmond & Carveth, 2007; Lapierre et al., 2017). Therefore, studying how children process
marketing information in the digital ecosystem is crucial to understanding its impact on their
consumption patterns (Senevirathna et al., 2022). Second, further research is needed on the link
between food marketing and children ’s health . Although regulations on food marketing for
children are becoming stricter in some countries (Núñez-Gómez et al., 2020), gaps remain, such as
how children respond to marketing campaigns for healthy versus unhealthy foods (Cairns et al.,
2013; Huang et al., 2024). In addition, research could compare the effectiveness of education-based
marketing campaigns with more persuasive and emotional campaigns (Milkaite & Lievens, 2019;
Zhang, 2018). Third, future research could examine how social value-based marketing influences
children. Sustainability and social responsibility are increasingly global concerns, and many
companies are adopting marketing strategies that emphasize these values. However, little research
has examined how children understand sustainability and how social value -based marketing can
influence their attitudes and behaviors as future consumers (Nicholls & Cullen, 2004). Fourth,
research examining how marketing affects children ’s education and academic aspirations is
increasingly relevant (Güler Yıldız et al., 2021). With increased competition in the education sector,
many academic institutions use marketing to attract students ( Abdul Rasool et al., 2023 ). Further
research is needed on how this marketing shapes children ’s expectations of education and how
they compare educational institutions based on the branding strategies used  (Confos & Davis,
2016). Fifth, cross -cultural research is needed to understand how children in different countries
respond to different marketing strategies ( Nandita et al. , 202 5). Globalization has led many
companies to implement uniform marketing strategies across countries, but children’s responses
can vary greatly depending on cultural and social factors (Lapierre et al., 2017). Comparative studies
examining how cultural factors shape children’s understanding of and responses to marketing can
provide greater insight into how companies can adapt their marketing strategies to be more
effective and ethical (Nicholls & Cullen, 2004).

Discussion
The bibliometric results show that research on marketing to children has grown steadily over the
past decade. This trend reflects growing academic concern about how marketing strategies
influence children ’s consumption behavior and long -term brand relationships. This growing

attention is also linked to the rapid development of digital media, which has significantly expanded
the channels through which children are exposed to marketing messages.
Compared with studies conducted in Western countries, research in developing regions
remains relatively limited. Most influential publications originate from North America and Europe,
indicating a geographical imbalance in the literature. This suggests tha t future studies should
explore the cultural and socioeconomic contexts of children ’s marketing in emerging markets,
particularly in Asia and Africa.
Furthermore, the keyword co -occurrence analysis highlights emerging themes such as
digital marketing, advertising literacy, and ethical marketing practices. These findings suggest that
the field is gradually shifting from traditional advertising studies toward broader discussions about
responsible marketing and consumer protection for children.

Conclusion and Implications
Marketing for children is a constantly evolving field as technology, culture, and consumption habits
change. Children are attractive marketing targets for many companies because they have purchasing
power and significantly influence family purchasing decisions. Therefore, understanding how
children respond to marketing strategies is essential to ensure marketing is effective, ethical, and
responsible. Marketing for children encompasses a wide range of techniques, from television
commercials and digital marketing to social media and experiential strategies such as event
sponsorship and influencer marketing. In recent decades, digital ma rketing has increasingly
dominated, leveraging algorithms and user data to serve personalized ads. While these strategies
can increase marketing effectiveness, children often lack the cognitive ability to distinguish ads
from organic content, which can affect their mindset and consumption behavior.
Marketing can affect children in both positive and negative ways. On the one hand,
marketing can educate children about social values, sustainability, and healthy lifestyles. Marketing
campaigns that promote healthy food, physical activity, or eco-friendly habits can help form better
habits from an early age. However, aggressive marketing of fast food, harmful products, or
consumptive content can negatively affect children ’s development. Therefore, regulations
governing marketing for children are very important.
The future of marketing for children should focus on balancing business interests with
protecting young consumers. Companies need to adopt a more responsible approach by tailoring
advertising messages to suit children ’s developmental stages and following stricter ethical
standards. In addition, more research is needed on how children interact with digital marketing and
how parents can help children understand marketing strategies. With a more ethical, evidence -
based app roach, marketing for children can be a more positive tool for shaping healthy and
responsible consumption habits in the future.
