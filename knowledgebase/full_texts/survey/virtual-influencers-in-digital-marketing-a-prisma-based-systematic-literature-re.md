Abstract
Virtual influencers (VIs) are AI-generated personas that are reshaping digital marketing, particularly through social
media. While marketing research on human influencers is extensive, systematic insights into VIs remain limited. This
study conducts a systematic literature review (SLR) of 73 peer-reviewed articles from 2016 to 2024 using the PRISMA
framework and multiple analytical tools (RStudio, VOSviewer). Key research questions address the evolution of VIs,
their comparative effectiveness with human influencers, and how perceived trust and authenticity impact engage-
ment. Through thematic, content, and comparative analyses, the study uncovers key trends, identifies research gaps,
and proposes a conceptual framework to guide future research in marketing and communication.

Introduction
Virtual influencers have become a major emerging trend
in the ever-evolving landscape of digital marketing, revo -
lutionizing the way brands connect with their audiences
[40]. The rise of "virtual influencers" as defined by Arsen -
yan and Mirowska [5], as "agents augmented with digital
avatars, designed to look human." Given that "anthro -
pomorphized representations of virtual agents, espe -
cially visually attractive ones, elicit social responses and
behavioral change in humans," Khan and Sutcliffe [38]
claim that these virtual influencers provide an intriguing
backdrop. With advancements in computer-generated
imagery (CGI) technology, virtual influencers are gain -
ing activity on social media platforms and have become
easier to create [47]. Businesses started collaborating
with virtual influencers in 2016 since the debut of “Lil
Miquela” created by a startup Brud in 2016 [20].
The public’s attention was drawn to the first virtual
influencers during this period, including Lil Miquela,
Shudu, Noonoouri, and Naina. However, the high level
of control over virtual influencers has raised ethical con -
cerns. A VI does not have its own mind, as it is com -
pletely controlled by its creators and raises debates about
its authenticity, credibility, and the ethical consequences
[52]. With the beginning of 2024, the application of vir -
tual influencers became more common with brands using
digital counterparts to formulate new kinds of advertis -
ing campaigns aimed at tech-driven consumers [48].

was valued at USD 4.60 billion and further anticipated

Methodology
Research design
This study presents a systematic literature review (SLR)
that explores the virtual influencers’ development and
impact from the perspective of virtual influencers in digi-
tal marketing, including the comparative effectiveness,
credibility, and consumer engagement. The review is per-
formed in accordance with the PRISMA 2020 statement
[50] and consists of four stages that is identification,
screening, eligibility, and inclusion.
This special issue explores the timely and rapidly
evolving phenomenon of virtual influence within con -
temporary marketing practices. The review period of
2016–2024 provides an apt framework for comprehend -
ing how VIs have evolved from emergence of Lil Miquela
and other notable “virtual” influencers and celebrities
who incorporate computer-generated characters into
their marketing strategies in order to create more engag -
ing and provocative influencer marketing campaigns [64].
Recent years have seen an explosion of studies on vir -
tual influencers from a marketing, communication,
branding, and consumer psychology perspective. This
paper aims to unite the dots of this growing field of study
by identifying the major themes that have been discussed
so far as well as relevant theoretical approaches. It will
compare the current studies with findings from human
influencer studies to gain deeper insights into this emerg-
ing field. Moreover, it will investigate how perceived trust
and authenticity as outcome variables of virtual influenc -
ers’ behavior affect human consumers’ behavior.
The synthesized findings will be used to create a con -
ceptual framework that will serve as starting point for a
future research agenda for this under-researched field.
Qualitative content analysis and specialized bibliomet -
ric tools were used for the analysis and to cluster study
to gain an overview of the current study’s trends. The
research questions were used to frame the study and the
theoretical foundation for the analysis framework.
Research questions
To direct and organize the systematic literature review,
clearly defined research questions were developed. These
research questions informed the literature search, selec -
tion, analysis and synthesis of included studies. The study
addresses the following research questions:

PRISMA framework
This study follows the PRISMA 2020 (Preferred Reporting
Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses) frame -
work to ensure a transparent and systematic literature
selection process. The PRISMA approach consists of four
key stages: identification, screening, eligibility, and inclu -
sion illustrated in Fig. 1.
During the identification phase, Scopus and Web of
Science databases were searched with predetermined
search strings and initially 188 articles were identified.
During the screening phase, the first round of filtering
was conducted according to the specified criteria, such as
the publication year (2016–2024), language (English), and
type of document (peer-reviewed articles), which led to
the elimination of 13 records and 175 articles were left to
undergo further screening. During the eligibility phase,
the rest of the articles were filtered using the titles and
abstracts and then full-text evaluation. In this process,
irrelevant studies were eliminated. A total of 73 studies
were then chosen to be analyzed in the inclusion stage.
Database selection
This systematic literature review follows the PRISMA
2020 guidelines to ensure a transparent and structured
selection process [50, 68]. Two major academic data -
bases, Scopus and Web of Science (WoS), were selected
for literature retrieval due to their extensive coverage of
high-quality, peer-reviewed journals across multiple dis -
ciplines [46].
Scopus was chosen because it ensures a wide coverage
of scientific literature and provides access to high-quality
journals across business, management, social sciences,
and technology-related fields, which are highly relevant
to the study of virtual influencers [1, 27]. This database
is widely recognized for its comprehensive coverage and
reliability in bibliometric and systematic review studies.
To enhance the robustness of the review, Web of Sci -
ence was a complementary database. The inclusion of
WoS allows for cross-validation of retrieved studies and
improves the comprehensiveness, reliability, and validity
of the literature selection process along with review find -
ings [25].

Inclusion and exclusion criteria
The criteria of inclusion and exclusion were defined in an
organized way to assure a consistent, transparent and rig-
orous inclusion of vital studies throughout the systematic
review process. These criteria guided the screening and
selection process in alignment with the research objec -
tives of the study, presented in Table 1.
Quality assessment of included studies
In order to ensure the reliability of findings of the system-
atic literature review, a quality assessment of the included
studies was conducted. The studies were evaluated based
on common criteria used in systematic reviews to meas -
ure methodological quality.
The assessment considered the following criteria: clar -
ity of research objectives, appropriateness of research
methodology, data collection and analysis rigor, (4) rel -
evance to the study themes, credibility of findings. Each
study was carefully reviewed against these criteria to
ensure that only high-quality and relevant studies were
included within this review. The studies that were utilized
in this review were published in peer-reviewed journals
and conference proceedings. The review of the included
studies ensures the validity of the research presented in
this paper.

Data extraction and analysis
Data from 73 studies, selected from an initial pool
of 188 papers, were extracted on publication details
(author, title, year, journal). The analysis focuses on key
areas such as (trustworthiness, virtual influencer cred -
ibility, authenticity, consumer engagement, and ethical
concerns). A thematic analysis was conducted to iden -
tify major findings, methodologies, target audiences,
and research gaps followed by a comparative and con -
tent analysis to uncover recurring themes, patterns,
and gaps in the literature.
Levels of analysis
The study’s data analysis was conducted at two lev -
els. The first level focused on the basic details of the
reviewed literature, while the second level involved a
deeper analysis to answer the study’s key research ques -
tions. Figure  2 presents a breakdown of each level of
analysis. The first level of analysis examined five key
aspects: publication year distribution, authorship pat -
terns, word cloud and frequency analysis, and the clas -
sification of articles by research sources. The final level
deployed comparative analysis, thematic and content
analysis to discover key themes related to the existing
literature related to virtual influencers.
Results
This section presents the results of the comprehen -
sive literature review in abstract. Unlike the discussion
of individual studies, the results are presented as inter -
related themes that highlight trends, relationships and
insights revealed by the research findings. The results are
organized under three main themes corresponding to
the research questions (RQ1–RQ3) to provide a coher -
ent overall perspective on virtual influencers in digital
marketing.
Evolution of virtual influencers
The analysis of Fig.  3 indicates that research on virtual
influencers remained limited during the early period
from 2016 to 2020, with minimal academic attention
and no publications identified in 2016 and 2017. During
this phase, existing studies primarily focused on broader
themes such as influencer marketing and social media,
as AI-driven influencers were still in their early stages of
development [47]. However, a noticeable shift emerged
after 2020, as researchers increasingly began to explore
virtual influencers in greater depth, particularly focus -
ing on themes such as authenticity and trust. The rapid
growth of digital technologies and artificial intelligence
has significantly contributed to the expansion of research
in this domain.

Comparative effectiveness
Based on research significance as shown in Table  2, 20
high-impact papers were identified for further in-depth
exploration. It is important to note that influencer mar -
keting has gained widespread consumer engagement
globally [6]. Notably, research in this field has expanded
significantly between 2018- 2024 (Fig.  5). The increasing
integration of artificial intelligence, cloud computing and
instant messaging has contributed to this growth, leading

This component of the study analyzes virtual and
human influencers in a systematic way based on impor -
tant criteria such customer engagement, brand percep -
tion, credibility, and trustworthiness shown in Tables  3

The literature indicates that while virtual influencers
are effective in generating engagement due to their nov -
elty and controlled messaging, their impact is strongly
influenced by consumer perceptions of authenticity and
relatability [16, 65]. These findings suggest that trust and
emotional connection act as key mediating factors in
determining the overall effectiveness of virtual influenc -
ers compared to human influencers.
It is also worth noting that there are reasons to believe
that people engage differently with virtual influencers
when compared to human influencers. This may have
roots in the uncanny valley, which suggests that things
which are nearly human but not identical can be slightly
creepy. Virtual influencers may suffer from a lack of emo-
tional connection with the audience because they are
perceived to be missing out on certain human elements
of vulnerability and reality. This could, in turn, cause their
audience to perceive them as less authentic and therefore
less relatable.

Trust and ethics
Table 4 indicates that human influencers like Virat Kohli,
Cristiano Ronaldo, and Kendall Jenner achieve remarka -
ble levels of engagement alongside comments and shares.
A Cristiano Ronaldo post promoting a health and well -
ness brand achieved 19. 5  K shares, 38. 1  K comments,
and 3. 25 million likes. His extensive influence and sub -
stantial impact on consumer behavior emerges from
statistical data. Human influencers develop strong emo -
tional bonds with audiences while maintaining perceived
authenticity which drives exceptional engagement rates
[19]. Their personal narratives, established credibility,
and direct interactions with followers foster trust and
drive meaningful consumer engagement.
In contrast, though the influencers have very large fans
base as given in Table  3, the level of interactions and
engagement are not as substantial as it could be. This is
apparent as virtual influencer Lil Miquela’s post garnered
293 K likes and 544 comments with 25 shares in the tech
and innovation category. This, although high, is lower
than that of human influencers as they are more real,
relatable, and authentic than virtual influencers [36].
Another consideration is the way in which authenticity
and brand perception are impacted. Virtual influencers
controlled by brands or creators have a difficulty gaining
the public’s trust as consumers are more likely to view
content created by virtual influencers as promotional
rather than authentic.
Impact of consumer perceptions of authenticity
and trustworthiness on engagement
Compared to artificial digital influencers, human influ -
encers are often given higher ratings for authenticity and
trust [16]. Models and celebrities like Kim Kardashian
and Kendall Jenner are perceived as sincere and genuine
on social media because they incorporate the different
aspects of their personalities and personal experiences
into their social media posts, and share various personal
connections and insights with their followers. Moreover,
by actively posting updates, and giving replies to follow -
ers’ messages and comments on their social networking
websites, such as Instagram, human influencers develop
trust with their followers online.
However, most people do not think of virtual influenc -
ers like Noonoouri or Shudu Gram as “real” or “trust -
worthy. ” Because while they may post frequently, their
online existence lacks depth, and much of their content
is branded marketing. As a result, virtual influencers
rarely gain the same level of trust and rapport with their
followers that human influencers have cultivated, largely
because human influencers are able to draw from expe -
riences and emotions that are rooted in personal reality
[43]. But that’s beginning to change as some virtual influ -
encers are embracing their artificial nature and increas -
ing the transparency of virtual influencer marketing.
In addition to the earlier findings, the sentiment of
the posts and comments further reveal insights into the
dynamics between human and virtual influencers. As
given in Table  4, the comments on a post by Cristiano
Ronaldo, one of the world’s best-known athletes, about
wellness and health received overwhelmingly positive
responses from followers. This type of engagement and
relatability translates into credibility with his human
influencers. Conversely, posts from virtual influencers
garner mostly neutral sentiments and occasionally nega -
tive reactions, mainly because virtual influencers fail to
evoke emotional connections with their consumers [10].

Rationale for model
The need for a conceptual model arises from the frag -
mented and emerging nature of research on virtual influ -
encers. While prior studies have examined individual
constructs such as trust, authenticity, and engagement,
an integrated framework that explains how these ele -
ments jointly influence consumer behavior is lacking.
A systematic review of 73 articles found five key fac -
tors as consistently influencing consumer response
to virtual influencers: Features of VI, Trustworthi -
ness, Authenticity, Consumer Engagement, and Brand
Perception. Also, several articles give evidence to the
Opinion Leadership as a mediating variable that boosts
the persuasive influence of influencers [10, 58]. The
model designed in this study is based on these insights
and addresses the way in which virtual influencers can
influence purchase intentions as a contribution to the
theoretical development and as a practical contribution
to the future research on influencer marketing.
Theoretical foundation
The conceptual model proposed in this study is
grounded in established marketing and communication
theories, particularly the source credibility theory [30],
opinion leadership theory [35], and parasocial interac -
tion theory [29]. These frameworks explain how digital
personalities such as virtual influencers can affect con -
sumer trust, authenticity, and behavioral intention.
• Source Credibility Theory suggests that a communi -
cator’s perceived trustworthiness and expertise can
significantly influence message acceptance and con -
sumer behavior.
• Opinion Leadership Theory highlights the role of
certain individuals or agents in shaping others’ atti -
tudes or purchasing decisions.
• Parasocial Interaction Theory helps explain the
emotional connection consumers may form with
VIs, influencing their engagement and authenticity
perceptions.
• The Unified Theory of Technology Adoption and Use
may facilitate the analysis of VI adoption as more
new scientific technologies comes into the play
[69].

Construct development
The constructs used in the conceptual model are drawn
from themes identified in the systematic literature review.
• Features of VI: Includes visual realism, human like -
ness, interactivity, and content quality. These features
contribute to how believable and relatable a virtual
influencer appears.
• Trustworthiness: The perceived honesty, transpar -
ency, and reliability of the VI, influenced by brand
disclosure and ethical representation [41].
• Authenticity: The perceived genuineness and emo -
tional consistency of the VI’s persona and storytelling
[19].
• Consumer Engagement: Encompasses behavioral
(likes, shares), emotional (affinity, interest), and cog -
nitive (attention, recall) aspects of user interaction
[39].
• Brand Perception: How the VI shapes the image of
the brand they endorse particularly its innovation,
credibility, and appeal to tech-savvy consumers [51].
• Opinion Leadership: Refers to the VI’s ability to influ-
ence follower attitudes and behaviors by being seen
as credible, trendsetting, or aspirational [14, 54].
• Purchase Intention: The likelihood that a consumer
will purchase a product or service promoted by the
VI [7, 58].
Conceptual model description
The conceptual model presented in Fig.  6 illustrates the
relationships between virtual influencers, consumer
perceptions, and marketing outcomes. The pathways
depicted in the model are supported by the existing lit -
erature, which indicates that characteristics of virtual
influencers, such as anthropomorphism and technologi -
cal sophistication, influence consumer perceptions of
credibility and authenticity [47].
These perceptions, in turn, influence consumer engage-
ment and behavioral responses, highlighting the medi -
ating role of trust in shaping the relationship between
virtual influencers and marketing effectiveness [16, 65].
Furthermore, ethical considerations such as transparency
and disclosure are shown to impact credibility, which
ultimately shapes consumer attitudes and brand out -
comes [28].
The proposed model further suggests that opinion
leadership defined as the influence that certain consum -
ers have over other consumers’ buying decisions which
mediates the relationship between audience engagement
with virtual influencers and audience engagement with a
brand. Because virtual influencers are fictional characters
created to simulated real human communication and
their influence which is determined by how credible,
trustworthy, and authentic they are perceived. As such,
virtual influencers with high perceived credibility, trust -
worthiness, and authenticity are more likely to generate
engagement with a brand.
Thematic analysis
Each publication was systematically coded based on
(title, author(s) and year, key findings, methodology, objec-
tive, and research gap). Citation counts were analyzed to
evaluate the impact of virtual influencers and their role
in marketing (Table  5). The thematic table provides a
structured analysis, highlighting their influence on social
media, marketing, and consumer behavior.
Several studies indicate that virtual influencers can
engage with their audience and influence the purchas -
ing decisions of their followers through the creation of
emotional connections with their audience members
[23]. However, several studies also show that consumers
still feel that the human influencers are more authentic
and relatable to their followers [16]. The virtual influenc -
ers study themes indicate that several different method -
ologies have been used in existing studies. These include
several that used mixed methods and others that used
experimental or qualitative methods to gather informa -
tion about virtual influencers and their impact on their
audiences [56, 57]. Despite the number of studies that
have been performed on the topic of virtual influenc -
ers, there are still research gaps within the area of study
of virtual influencers. Most studies have focused on the
more well-known virtual influencers, such as Lil Miquela,
while ignoring the virtual influencers that are lesser
known but still worthy of study [20].
Although the use of virtual influencers in market -
ing is expanding, little is known about their long-term
effectiveness [23, 48]. There is a need for more research
because consumer attitudes differ, with concerns includ -
ing ethics, transparency, and trust [42, 56, 57].

Discussion
Interpretation of key findings
Virtual Influencers are no longer something new in digital
marketing. Artificial Intelligence and Digital Technolo -
gies are rapidly evolving. The evolution of new technolo -
gies affects how Virtual Influencers emerge and grow
[4, 47]. Novelty and technological elements have drawn
huge attention, and many are now focusing on how vir -
tual influencers can build consumer trust, increase cred -
ibility, and enhance influence [16, 65]. Human influencers
continue to elicit strong emotions and connect with con -
sumers on a number of different levels which continue
to drive consumer behavior. The findings from this study
highlight that trust is one of the key elements that con -
tribute to the effectiveness of virtual influencers. The
results from this study also reveals how ethical concerns
relating to the level of transparency and disclosure virtual
influencers provide, can shape how consumers view vir -
tual influences and affect how acceptable they find them
[28]. This study provides new insights into these emerg -
ing phenomena. Furthermore, it highlights the market -
er’s need to embrace this innovative form of influencing
while addressing the ethical issues within digital market -
ing strategies.
Theoretical implications
The findings of this study contribute to several theoreti -
cal perspectives in the literature. First, the results sup -
port the relevance of the uncanny valley theory, which
explains the potential discomfort experienced by con -
sumers when interacting with highly realistic but not
fully human entities. This provides insight into why vir -
tual influencers may face challenges in establishing emo -
tional connections compared to human influencers.
Second, the study reinforces the importance of trust
and credibility theories in digital marketing, highlighting
that consumer perceptions of authenticity and transpar -
ency are key determinants of engagement. The role of
anthropomorphism is also evident, as humanlike charac -
teristics of virtual influencers influence how consumers
perceive and interact with them.
Overall, the study integrates technological, psychologi -
cal, and marketing perspectives, offering a comprehen -
sive understanding of virtual influencer effectiveness and
contributing to the growing body of research in this field.
Ethical implications
Virtual Influencers are increasingly adopted in the mar -
keting in order to tackle challenges such as lack of time,
resources and media coverage [56, 57]. One of the pri -
mary challenges in utilizing these influencers revolves
around ethical concerns and transparency [48]. The ques-
tion arises whether brands should explicitly state to their
consumers that virtual influencers are not real people.
This is crucial for brands to maintain consumer trust and
to act responsibly and ethically in their marketing efforts
[21].
Research gaps in the literature
Despite the growing body of research on virtual influenc -
ers, several important gaps remain in the existing litera -
ture. First, from a theoretical perspective, there is limited
integration of interdisciplinary frameworks that combine
insights from marketing, psychology, and artificial intel -
ligence. While concepts such as trust, authenticity, and
anthropomorphism have been examined, there is a need
for more comprehensive theoretical models that explain
the complex relationships between virtual influencers
and consumer behavior.
Second, methodological gaps are evident in the current
literature. A significant proportion of studies rely on con-
ceptual discussions or exploratory approaches, with lim -
ited empirical validation. There is a need for more robust
quantitative and experimental studies to examine causal
relationships, as well as longitudinal research to under -
stand the long-term impact of virtual influencers on con -
sumer engagement and brand outcomes.

Conclusions
Summary and contributions
The rapidly increasing interest in virtual influencers
within the field of digital marketing has drawn the atten -
tion of many researchers and business marketers. This
study presents a comprehensive systematic literature
review regarding virtual influencers in the context of
digital marketing from different angles, especially focus -
ing on potential influencing factors of their effective -
ness such as their evolutions, comparative analyses, and
impact factors of trust, authenticity, and ethics.
Virtual influencers have been becoming an extremely
important marketing phenomenon in recent years driven
by AI technology. Virtual influencers have significant
advantages over human influencers including the fea -
tures of scaling up, of being consistent and controllable.
Moreover, from the consumers’ perspectives, building
trust, authenticity, and credibility is the key factors to the
effectiveness of virtual influencers. In spite of their dis -
advantages in terms of poor emotional connection, they
have got more attention in the digital marketing scene
and became popular due to their novel characteristics.
This study contributes to the body of existing knowledge
by offering a comprehensive overview of current research
in virtual influencers and interlinking technological
advancements with psychological and marketing orien -
tations to develop a deeper conceptual understanding.
Virtual influencers can gain more positive influence on
consumer engagement through enhancing level of trust
and authenticity.
By highlighting potential advantages and disadvantages
of virtual influencers and discussing relevant implica -
tions, this study helps to offer practical advice for market-
ers on using virtual influencers in effective and appealing
ways. Virtual influencers have unique features should

Limitations
The study has some limitations. First, the included arti -
cles were published in English language and extracted
from databases which are major sources of scientific evi -
dence, including Scopus and Web of Science databases.
However, this approach may suffer from database cover -
age limitations, i.e., some relevant studies published in
other databases and in non-English language might have
been excluded. Second, the study was based on peer-
reviewed articles and conference papers that have been
published. Therefore, there is a risk of publication bias,
where studies that have not been published, such as “gray
literature, ” or industry reports have been excluded from
the study. Third, the study is based on systematic litera -
ture review methodology, i.e., it depends on the search
string used, the inclusion and exclusion criteria, and how
the researcher interprets the studies included in the study
during the screening and synthesis process. Finally, vir -
tual influencer technology and generative artificial intel -
ligence are rapidly developing fields, and the findings of
this study are a snapshot of the current research available
in the literature, and future research and developments
might not be captured in this study.
Future research directions
The following future research directions are directly
derived from the research gaps identified in Table  4.
Future research can build on this study in several spe -
cific and actionable ways. From a theoretical perspec -
tive, empirical studies are needed to examine consumer
perceptions, trust formation, and behavioral responses
to virtual influencers across different cultural, social,
and demographic contexts. In particular, future research
should develop and test integrated theoretical frame -
works that combine insights from marketing, psychology,
and artificial intelligence to better explain virtual influ -
encer effectiveness. Addressing methodological gaps,
future research should investigate the long-term effec -
tiveness of virtual influencers in comparison to human
influencers, particularly in relation to brand loyalty, rela -
tionship building, and sustained engagement. Longitu -
dinal and experimental research designs are especially
recommended to capture causal relationships and long-
term effects.
In terms of ethical gaps, there is a need for deeper
exploration of issues such as transparency, disclosure
practices, and the potential psychological and social
impact of interacting with artificial entities. Future stud -
ies should examine how ethical perceptions influence
trust and consumer acceptance of virtual influencers.
From a technological perspective, future studies should
examine the role of emerging generative artificial intelli -
gence technologies, such as ChatGPT and Midjourney, in
shaping the design, realism, and influence of virtual influ-
encers. This includes exploring how AI-driven person -
alization and content generation affect engagement and
authenticity perceptions. Finally, addressing contextual
gaps, future research may explore interdisciplinary per -
spectives and examine virtual influencers across differ -
ent platforms, industries, and cultural environments to
develop a more comprehensive understanding of virtual
influencer ecosystems.
