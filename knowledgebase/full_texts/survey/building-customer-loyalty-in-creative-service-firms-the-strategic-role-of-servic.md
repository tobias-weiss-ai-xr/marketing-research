Abstract

This study examines the strategic role of service quality and customer experience in building customer
loyalty within creative service firms. Grounded in service marketing and experience economy
perspectives, the research investigates both the direct and indirect effects of service quality on
customer loy alty, with customer experience as a mediating variable. A quantitative approach was
employed using a survey of 30 customers from a photography service provider (Popular Studio). Data
were collected through structured questionnaires and analyzed using Parti al Least Squares Structural
Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM). The results reveal that service quality has a positive and significant
effect on customer loyalty and customer experience. In turn, customer experience significantly
influences customer loyalty and p artially mediates the relationship between service quality and
customer loyalty. These findings indicate that service quality alone is insufficient to sustain customer
loyalty unless it is translated into meaningful and engaging customer experiences.  From a managerial
perspective, the study highlights the importance of integrating functional service excellence with
experiential value creation. Creative service firms should strategically manage customer touchpoints,
enhance interpersonal interactions, and de sign service environments that foster emotional
engagement to strengthen customer retention.  This study contributes to the literature by providing
empirical evidence on the mediating role of customer experience in linking service quality to customer
loyalty within the context of small-scale creative service businesses.

Keywords: customer loyalty, service quality, customer experience, creative industry, strategic role

INTRODUCTION
The creative industry has emerged as a key driver of economic growth, particularly in
developing countries where small -scale service firms contribute significantly to employment and
value creation. Within this sector, creative service businesses, such as photography studios, operate
in highly competitive environments where differentiation is not solely based on technical output, but
also on the ability to deliver meaningful and memorable customer experiences (Pine & Gilmore,
1999; Bernd Schmitt, 1999). As competition intensifies, customers are increasingly empowered with
multiple alternatives, making customer loyalty a critical yet challenging objective for service
providers (Philip Kotler & Keller, 2016).

In the context of creative services, customer loyalty is inherently fragile due to low switching
barriers and the  abundance of alternative providers offering similar core services. Customers can
easily move from one service provider to another when their expectations are not fully met,
particularly in industries where experiential and emotional value play a central r ole (Lemon &
Verhoef, 2016). This condition is evident in local creative markets, where numerous photography
studios compete to attract and retain customers, forcing firms to move beyond functional value
toward experiential differentiation.

Service quality has long been recognized as a fundamental determinant of customer
satisfaction and loyalty. The SERVQUAL model h ighlights dimensions such as reliability,
responsiveness, assurance, empathy, and tangibles as critical factors influencing customer
perceptions (Parasuraman et al., 1988). Empirical studies have consistently demonstrated that high
service quality enhances  customer satisfaction and contributes to long -term loyalty (Slack et al.,
2020). However, in creative service contexts, service quality alone may not be sufficient to sustain
customer loyalty, as customers increasingly seek emotional engagement and person alized
experiences.

This shift reflects the growing relevance of the experience economy, where value is co-created
through customer interactions and experiential engagement (Pine & Gilmore, 1999). Customer
experience encompasses customers’ cognitive, emotional, and behavioral responses throughout the
entire service journey (Klaus & Maklan, 2013). Previous studies indicate that positive customer
experiences can significantly influence customer satisfaction and loyalty (Rather, 2019).
Nevertheless, the role of customer experience as a strategic mechanism that links service quality to
customer loyalty remains underexplored, particularly within small-scale creative service firms.

Although prior research has extensively examined the relationship between service  quality and
customer loyalty, most studies have focused on large -scale industries such as hospitality, banking,
and retail (Slack et al., 2020; Lemon & Verhoef, 2016). Limited attention has been given to micro and
small enterprises in the creative sector, where personalized interactions and experiential value play
a more dominant role. Furthermore, empirical evidence examining the mediating role of customer
experience in connecting service quality and customer loyalty in creative service settings remains
scarce, especially in emerging market contexts.
Addressing this gap, the present study aims to examine the strategic role of service quality and
customer experience in building customer loyalty within a creative service firm. Specifically, this
study investigates how service quality influences customer loyalty both directly and indirectly
through customer experience in the context of Popular Studio. By doing so, this research provides
insights into how creative service businesses can design effective s trategies to enhance customer
retention through experience-driven value creation.

The findings of this study are expected to contribute to both theory and practice. Theoretically, this
research extends the service marketing literature by emphasizing the i ntegrative role of customer
experience in shaping customer loyalty within the creative industry. Practically, the study offers
strategic implications for creative service firms, particularly small -scale businesses, in designing
service delivery systems that prioritize both quality and experiential value.

Customer Loyalty in Creative Service Context
Customer loyalty refers to a deeply held commitment to repurchase or reuse a preferred
product or service consistently in the future, despite situational influences and marketing efforts
from competitors (Griffin, 2002; Frederick F. Reichheld, 1996). In se rvice industries, loyalty is
reflected through repeat purchase intentions, positive word -of-mouth, and resistance to switching

In the context of creative service firms, such as photography studios, customer loyalty
becomes more complex due to the  experiential and intangible nature of the service. Customers
evaluate not only the final outcome but also the overall service process and emotional engagement
(Rather, 2019). Therefore, understanding the drivers of customer loyalty requires integrating bo th
functional and experiential perspectives.

Service Quality as a Strategic Driver
Service quality is defined as the gap between customer expectations and perceived service
performance (Parasuraman et al., 1988). The SERVQUAL framework identifies five key  dimensions:
tangibles, reliability, responsiveness, assurance, and empathy, which collectively shape customer
perceptions of service quality. Prior studies consistently demonstrate that service quality significantly
influences customer satisfaction and loyalty (Slack et al., 2020). High -quality service enhances trust
and reduces perceived risk, which encourages customers to maintain long -term relationships with
service providers. In creative service settings, service quality serves as a foundational elemen t that
shapes customers’ initial evaluations and expectations.However, recent literature suggests that
service quality alone may not fully explain customer loyalty, particularly in industries where
emotional and experiential value play a dominant role (Kla us & Maklan, 2013). This indicates the
need to consider additional mechanisms that translate service performance into long -term
behavioral outcomes.

Customer Experience as a Mediating Mechanism
Customer experience is defined as customers’ cognitive, emotional, and behavioral responses
arising from interactions with a service provider across the entire customer journey (Lemon &
Verhoef, 2016). It reflects how customers perceive and internalize their interactions with a company,
going beyond functional servi ce delivery.  Klaus and Maklan (2013) emphasize that customer
experience differs from traditional service quality by incorporating emotional and relational aspects
of consumption. Positive experiences create stronger emotional bonds, which are critical for
fostering customer loyalty in service industries. Empirical studies confirm that customer experience
plays a significant role in influencing customer loyalty (Rather, 2019). More importantly, customer
experience has been identified as a mediating variable that connects service quality with behavioral
outcomes, such as loyalty and retention (Pranata & Sukaatmadja, 2019; Wiranata & Nurcaya, 2020).
This suggests that service quality influences loyalty not only directly but also indirectly through the
creation of positive customer experiences.

Hypothesis Development
Service Quality and Customer Loyalty
Service quality is a key determinant of customer loyalty, as it directly influences customers’
perceptions of value and trust. When customers perceive that a service provider consistently delivers
reliable, responsive, and empathetic services, they are more likely to develop a commitment to the
provider. Previous studies have demonstrated a significant positive relationship between service
quality and customer loyalty (Slack et al., 2020). Therefore, the following hypothesis is proposed:
H1: Service quality has a positive and significant effect on customer loyalty.

Service Quality and Customer Experience
Service quality also plays a crucial role in shaping cust omer experience. High -quality service
interactions, including professional behavior, responsiveness, and personalized attention, contribute
to positive emotional and cognitive responses from customers. Research by Klaus and Maklan (2013)
and Lemon and Verh oef (2016) highlights that service quality is a key antecedent of customer
experience. In creative service settings, the quality of interactions significantly influences how
customers perceive their overall experience. Thus, the following hypothesis is proposed:

Customer Experience and Customer Loyalty
Customer experience is a strong predictor of customer loyalty, particularly in industries where
emotional engagement plays a central role. Positive experiences create lasting impressions that
encourage customers to return and recommend the service to others.  Rather (2019) found that
customer experience significantly influences customer loyalty through increase d emotional
attachment and engagement. Therefore, the following hypothesis is proposed:
H3: Customer experience has a positive and significant effect on customer loyalty.

The Mediating Role of Customer Experience
Customer experience is expected to mediate the relationship between service quality and customer
loyalty. While service quality provides the foundation for customer evaluation, it is the resulting
experience that transforms these evaluations into long-term behavioral intentions.
Previous studies (Pranata & Sukaatmadja, 2019; Wiranata & Nurcaya, 2020) confirm that customer
experience acts as a mediator that strengthens the impact of service quality on customer loyalty. This
mediation mechanism is particularly relevant in creative service industries, where experiential value
plays a dominant role. Thus, the following hypothesis is proposed:
H4: Customer experience mediates the relationship between service quality and customer loyalty.

RESEARCH METHODS
This study adopts a quantitative explanatory approach using a survey method to examine the
relationships between service quality, customer experience, and customer loyalty in a creative
service context. The research was conducted at Popular Studio, a photography service provider
operating in a competitive local creative industry, with individual customers as the unit of analysis.
The population consists of all customers who have used the studio’s services, while the sample was
determined using a non-probability accidental sampling technique, resulting in 30 valid respondents.
Data were collected through a structured questionnaire measured on a four -point Likert scale (1 =
strongly disagree to 4 = strongly agree). Service quality was measured using the SERVQUAL
dimensions, including tangibles, reliability, responsiveness, assurance, and empathy (Parasuraman et
al., 1988), while customer experience was assessed based on customers’ cognitive and emotional
responses throughout the service journey (Klaus & Maklan, 2013; Lemon & Verhoef, 2016), an d
customer loyalty was measured through indicators such as repurchase intention, willingness to
recommend, and resistance to switching (Griffin, 2002). Data analysis was performed using Partial
Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM), which is suitable for small sample sizes and
complex models involving mediation (Hair et al., 2019). The analysis included evaluation of the
measurement model (convergent validity, composite reliability, average variance extracted, and
discriminant validity) and the structural model (path coefficients, t-statistics, p-values, and coefficient
of determination). The mediating effect of customer experience was tested using a bootstrapping
procedure to assess the significance of indirect effects.

The structural model was evaluated using path coefficients, t -statistics, and p -values obtained

The results reveal that:
▪ Service quality has a positive and significant effect on customer loyalty (β = 0.41, p < 0.05),

The coefficient of determination (R²) indicates that service quality and customer experience jointly

Mediation Analysis
The mediating role of customer experience was examined using bootstrapping. The indirect effect of
service quality on customer loyalty through customer experience was found to be signific ant (β =
0.30, p < 0.05).
Since both the direct and indirect effects are significant, this indicates partial mediation, supporting
H4. This finding suggests that service quality influences customer loyalty both directly and indirectly
through customer experience.

Discussion
The findings of this study highlight that service quality plays a strategic role in shaping customer
loyalty within creative service firms. The significant relationship between service quality and
customer loyalty supports prior studies  (Slack et al., 2020), confirming that reliable and responsive
service delivery enhances customer trust and commitment.

However, the results also demonstrate that customer experience serves as a critical mechanism that
strengthens this relationship. The s trong effect of service quality on customer experience indicates
that customers do not evaluate service quality in isolation, but rather through the experiences they
derive from service interactions. This finding aligns with the perspective of Klaus and Maklan (2013),
who argue that customer experience extends beyond functional service quality by incorporating
emotional and relational dimensions.

Furthermore, the significant impact of customer experience on customer loyalty reinforces the
importance of exp eriential value in the creative industry. In line with Lemon and Verhoef (2016),
customer experience represents the cumulative evaluation of all touchpoints, which ultimately
shapes customer behavior. In creative service settings such as photography studio s, emotional
engagement, personalized interactions, and service atmosphere become key determinants of loyalty.
The mediation result provides important insight into the mechanism through which service quality
translates into customer loyalty. The presence of partial mediation suggests that while service quality

CONCLUSION
This study demonstrates that service quality and customer experience play a strategic role in
building customer loyalty within creative service firms. The findings confirm that service quality not
only directly influences customer loyalty but also indirectly affects it through customer experience
as a mediating mechanism. This indicates that customer loyalty is not solely driven by functional
service performance, but also by the ability of firms to create meaningful and engaging customer
experiences throughout the service journey.
The results highlight that customer experience acts as a critical bridge linking service quality
to long -term customer relationships. In the context of creative services, where emotional and
experiential value are central, customers are more likely to remain loyal when they perceive not
only reliable and professional service, but also enjoyable and memorable interactions.
From a managerial perspective, this study offers important strategic insights for creative
service firms, particularly small -scale businesses such as Popular Studio. First, firms should
consistently maintain high standards of service quality across all dim ensions, including reliability,
responsiveness, and empathy, as these form the foundation of customer trust. Second, firms need
to go beyond technical service delivery by designing holistic customer experiences that emphasize
personalization, emotional engagement, and a comfortable service environment. Third, managing
customer touchpoints strategically  before, during, and after service delivery  can significantly
enhance customer satisfaction and loyalty outcomes.
Despite its contributions, this study has se veral limitations. The relatively small sample size
and the focus on a single case limit the generalizability of the findings. Future research is
encouraged to involve larger samples, multiple creative service sectors, and additional variables
such as customer satisfaction, brand image, or perceived value to provide a more comprehensive
understanding of customer loyalty in the creative industry.
