Abstract
Complexities in the twenty -first century business environment attributable to the dynamisms of
globalisation, technological advancement, and changing consumer lifestyles have resulted in
intense marketing competitiveness, compelling organisations to adopt strategic marketing
practices that shift attention from transactional a pproaches to relationship -oriented strategies
emphasising customer satisfaction, trust, and loyalty. This study examined strategic marketing
practices and customer retention in the Nigerian retail sector, using Nigeria Bottling Company
(NBC) Plc. as a case  study. Specifically, the study focused on product differentiation, branding
strategy, pricing strategy, Customer Relationship Management (CRM), and promotional
strategies. The study was anchored on Relationship Marketing Theory (RMT) and Expectancy -
Disconfirmation Theory (EDT) and utilised a cross -sectional survey design. Data were collected
from 55 management and Marketing/Sales Department staff of NBC Plc., Ibadan Plant, using a
structured questionnaire. Data were analysed using descriptive statistics an d Pearson Product
Moment Correlation. The findings indicated significant positive relationships between product
differentiation and customer retention (r = 0.732, p < 0.001), branding strategy and customer
retention (r = 0.684, p < 0.001), CRM and customer  retention (r = 0.751, p < 0.001), pricing
strategy and customer retention (r = 0.562, p < 0.001), and promotion strategy and customer
retention (r = 0.711, p < 0.001). CRM and product differentiation recorded the strongest
relationships with customer retention. The study concludes that strategic marketing practices are
important in strengthening customer retention and recommends sustained investment in customer
relationships, product differentiation, branding, pricing and promotional activities.

Keywords: Strategic Marketing Practices, Customer Retention, Customer Relationship
Management, Product Differentiation, Nigeria Bottling Company Plc.

1.0 Introduction
1.1 Background to the Study
The business environment of the 21st century is characterised by intense competition,
globalisation, technological advancement and changing customer needs. These developments have
made it necessary for organisations to adopt strategic marketing practices t hat enable them to
understand market opportunities, satisfy customers and maintain competitive advantage. Strategic
marketing involves the formulation and implementation of marketing activities that align an
organisation’s resources and capabilities with market opportunities and customer needs.
Customer retention has become an important concern for organisations because retaining existing
customers contributes to repeat purchases, customer loyalty and long -term business continuity.
Reichheld and Sasser (1990) observed that customer retention can contribute significantly to
business performance, while Zeithaml et al. (2022) emphasised the importance of delivering value
and maintaining relationships in sustaining customer patronage.
In a competitive market, organisations employ different strategic marketing practices to retain
customers. Product differentiation enables firms to distinguish their products from competing
alternatives through product features, quality, packaging, sizes, flavours and other attributes.
Branding also helps organisations create a d istinct identity and influence customers' perceptions
of their products. Adewale and Ayozie (2020) noted the importance of branding in building
customer preference and loyalty.  Customer Relationship Management (CRM) focuses on
developing and maintaining relationships with customers through effective communication,
service delivery, complaint handling and continuous engagement. Pricing strategy also influences
customers' perception of value and their purchasing decisions, while promotion creates awareness
and communicates the benefits of products to existing and potential customers. Kotler and Keller
(2016) emphasised the importance of integrating marketing activities in responding effectively to
customer needs. The Nigerian beverage industry operates within a highly competitive environment
where consumers have access to different brands and product alternatives. Changes in customer
preferences, purchasing power, technology and market competition have increased th e need for
firms to adopt effective marketing s trategies. Novatia Consulting (2024) and Kasmo (2024)
identified the changing nature of the Nigerian market and the need for businesses to respond to
evolving consumer expectations. Similarly, Academia (2024), Jot Form (2025) and Incentive
Smart (2023) highlight the importance of customer engagement, satisfaction and loyalty in
sustaining customer relationships.  Nigeria Bottling Company Plc. operates in this competitive
environment with a portfolio of products serving different consumer segments. The company has
continued to employ strategic marketing practices involving product differentiation, branding,
customer relationship management, pricing and promotion. These practices are expected to
influence customers' perceptions, purchasing behaviour a nd continued patronage. However, the
adoption of marketing practices does not automatically guarantee customer retention, particularly
in a market where consumers can switch between competing brands.

1.2 Statement of the Research Problem
The Nigerian beverage market has witnessed increasing competition, changing consumer
preferences and the availability of alternative products. These developments have created
challenges for organisations seeking to maintain customer loyalty and repeat purchases. Although
firms invest in product differentiation, branding, customer relationship management, pricing and
promotional activities, the extent to which these practices contribute to customer retention may
differ.

2.1.1 Strategic Marketing Practices
Strategic marketing practices refer to the planned activities and decisions adopted by an
organisation to identify market opportunities, satisfy customer needs and achieve organisational
objectives. Strategic marketing involves understanding the market env ironment, identifying
customer needs and developing appropriate strategies for creating value and achieving competitive
advantage. Varadarajan (2010) views strategic marketing as concerned with the formulation and
implementation of marketing strategies that enable firms to respond to market conditions and
achieve desired performance outcomes.
Kotler and Keller also emphasise the importance of understanding customer needs and delivering
superior value in the development of effective marketing strategies. In a competitive business
environment, strategic marketing requires organisations to coordin ate different marketing
activities rather than relying on a single marketing practice. Product differentiation, branding,
customer relationship management, pricing and promotion are therefore considered important
practices through which firms can respond to customer expectations and competition.
For the purpose of this study, strategic marketing practices are examined through product
differentiation, branding strategy, customer relationship management, pricing strategy and
promotion strategy.

2.1.2 Components of Strategic Marketing
Strategic marketing comprises several interrelated components that collectively shape how a firm
competes and delivers value to customers. The key components of strategic marketing activities
and functions include segmentation, targeting, positioning, bran ding strategy, product
differentiation, pricing strategy, distribution, and promotion. Consequently, it goes to say that
strategic marketi ng strategy encompasses product differentiation, branding, pricing strategies,
Customer Relationship Management (CRM), and promotional strategies. These elements
determine how customers perceive the firm and influence their decision to remain loyal. In oth er
words, each component influences customer perceptions, satisfaction, and loyalty, thereby
affecting customer retention.

2.1.2.1 Product Differentiation
Product differentiation refers to the process of creating meaningful distinctions between a firm’s
products and those of competitors. It is the process of creating unique product attributes that
distinguish a brand from competitors (Porter, 1985). In Fast -Moving Consumer Goods (FMCGs)
markets and sector, differentiation may take the form of taste variations, packaging, design,
product size variants, and nutritional content, value, or perceived quality.
Porter (1985) identifies differentiation as a key competitive strategy through which firms offer
unique attributes that customers value. Effective product differentiation reduces perceived
similarity among competing brands and lowers the likelihood of cust omer switching. In the
beverage industry, where functional differences are often minimal, symbolic and experiential
attributes such as brand image and packaging aesthetics play a crucial role in shaping customer
preference and retention.

customer perceptions and build emotional connections. It is a strategic initiative that develops and
nurtures perceptions, emotional associations, and customer expectations. Aaker (1991) argues that

2.1.2.3 Pricing Strategy
Pricing affects consumer value perception and buyer behaviour. Kotler and Armstrong (2018)
assert that value -based pricing is more effective than cost -based pricing in retaining customers.
Pricing strategy refers to the approach adopted by firms in setting  prices to reflect value, cover
costs, and achieve competi tive positioning. Kotler and Armstrong (2018) emphasise that pricing
is the only marketing mix element that generates revenue, while all others incur costs.
Consequently, pricing decisions have a direct impact on customer perceptions of fairness and
value. In price-sensitive markets such as Nigeria, value -based pricing strategies are particularly
effective in enhancing customer retention. When customers perceive prices as fair relative to
product benefits, they are more likely to remain loyal, even in the p resence of competing
alternatives.

2.1.2.4 Customer Relationship Management (CRM)
Customer Relationship Management (CRM) refers to technologies, systems, and practices used to
manage customer interactions. It refers to organisational strategies, processes,  and technologies
used to manage interactions with customers throughout the customer life cycle. Peppers and
Rogers (2011) describe Customer Relationship Management (CRM) as a customer -centric
approach aimed at understanding individual customer needs and d elivering personalised value.
That is, Customer Relationship Management (CRM) improves customer satisfaction through
personalisation, complaint handling, and loyalty programmes.  Customer Relationship
Management (CRM) practices include customer data managem ent, complaint handling, loyalty
programmes, personalised communication, and after -sales support. Effective Customer
Relationship Management (CRM) enhances customer satisfaction, trust, and commitment, which
are critical drivers of customer retention in competitive markets.

2.1.2.5 Promotional Strategies
Promotional strategies encompass marketing communication activities designed to inform,
persuade, and remind customers about products and brands. These activities include advertising,
sales promotion, sponsorship, digital marketing, influencer campaigns, a nd social media
engagement. Shimp (2017) notes that well-designed and effective promotional activities not only
stimulates interest, encourages trial and purchase but also reinforces brand positioning and
customer loyalty. In the FMCG sector, promotional strategies are often used to counter competitive
pressures and maintain brand visibility. However, excessive reliance on price promotions may
encourage switching behaviour if not supported by strong brand and relationship-building efforts.

2.1.3 Customer Retention
Customer retention refers to a firm’s ability to sustain long-term relationships with customers and
encourage repeat patronage over time. That is, the firm's ability to maintain long-term buyer-seller
relationships and reduce customer switching. According to Hallowell (1996), customer retention

2.1.3.1 Key Customer Retention Metrics
Customer retention can be measured through indicators that capture customer behaviour and
attitudes. Common metrics include repeat purchase rates, customer satisfaction scores, churn rates,
participation in loyalty programmes and customer feedback analysis. These measures enable firms
to evaluate the effectiveness of strategic marketing practices, identify areas requiring improvement
and support data-driven decision-making. Novatia Consulting (2024) highlights feedback analysis,
satisfaction analysis, retention metrics, tracking, churn analysis and participation rates as useful
measures of customer retention. Feedback analysis helps businesses understand customer needs
and preferences; satisfaction scores indicate how well customer needs are being met; retention
metrics meas ure the strength of customer relationships; tracking facilitates comparison with
industry standards; churn analysis identifies patterns of customer attrition; and participation rates
can provide an indication of customer engagement and loyalty. Customer segmentation can further
assist firms in tailoring strategies to groups according to their behaviours and preferences.

2.1.3.2 Future Trends in Customer Retention
Advancements in digital technology are reshaping customer retention strategies. Artificial
intelligence, big data analytics and machine learning enable firms to gain deeper insights into
customer behaviour and preferences, supporting personalised marketing , predictive analytics and
real-time engagement. Behavioural analytics is increasingly becoming a cornerstone of customer
retention because it enables businesses to analyse customer interactions and preferences in real
time. These technologies can help firms anticipate customer needs, proactively address
dissatisfaction and identify patterns that we re previously difficult to discern. Firms that leverage
such technologies are better positioned to deliver customised experiences that foster customer
satisfaction, loyalty and long-term relationships.

2.1.2.3 Challenges of Customer Retention in Nigeria
Customer retention in Nigeria is constrained by economic instability, inconsistent service quality
and misalignment between customer expectations and actual service delivery. Many firms struggle
to consistently meet customer expectations, resulting in diss atisfaction and switching behaviour.
Misaligned expectations and inconsistent service quality remain important challenges because
failure to deliver the expected experience can strain relationships, particularly with loyal
customers. High price sensitivity and aggressive competitive promotions further complicate
retention efforts. Firms therefore need to prioritise service quality, value delivery and relationship-
building strategies while seeking to meet or exceed customer expectations and consistently provide
quality service that encourages customers to return.

2.2 Theoretical Review
This section reviews theories relevant to the study and explains how they support the relationship
between strategic marketing practices and customer retention. The study will adopt the
Relationship Marketing Theory and Expectancy-Disconfirmation Theory (EDT), emphasising the
importance of meeting and exceeding customer expectations through strategic marketing to foster
customer satisfaction, loyalty and retention.

2.2.1 Relationship Marketing Theory
Relationship Marketing Theory (RMT) associated with Oliver (1980), explains customer
responses in terms of the comparison between expectations and actual performance. Customers
form expectations before or during consumption and subsequently compare their experiences with
those expectations. When performance meets or exceeds expectations, positive confirmation or
satisfaction may result; when performance falls below expectations, dissatisfaction may occur.
The theory is relevant  to customer retention because continued patronage may depend partly on
whether customers' experiences correspond with their expectations. Product quality and
differentiation, brand promises, customer service, price and promotional messages may all shape
customer expectations. When the actual experience is consistent with or exceeds expectations,
customers may be more willing to continue their relationship with the organisation.
The theory therefore complements Relationship Marketing Theory by explaining ho w customer
experiences and expectations may influence continued patronage.

2.5 Gaps in Literature
Despite extensive research on marketing strategies, several gaps remain. Few studies focus
specifically on Nigeria Bottling Company Plc., particularly within Oyo State. Existing studies also
often examine marketing variables in isolation rather than within an integrated framework. Limited
attention has further been given to moderating factors such as service quality within the Nigerian
beverage context.

seeks to address the identified gaps by examining the combined and individual relationships
between these strategic marketing practices and customer retention in Nigeria Bottling Company

3.0 Methodology
3.1 Study Area
The study was conducted in Ibadan, the capital city of Oyo State, Southwestern Nigeria. Ibadan is
one of the largest metropolitan cities in West Africa, with a population of over 3 million residents
across eleven Local Government Areas and diverse socioeco nomic, cultural and demographic
characteristics (Wikipedia; Wisdomlib, 2026). The city is a major commercial, industrial and
administrative centre and serves as an important market for Fast -Moving Consumer Goods
(FMCGs), including beverages. Its competitive business environment, diverse consumer segments
and active market interactions provide a suitable context for examining strategic marketing
practices and customer retention (Tamunoiselegha et al., 2024).
The city also offers research accessibility and a concentration of manufacturing firms, wholesalers,
retailers and distributors. The competitive pressures and resource constraints experienced by firms
operating in such an environment require sophisticated marketing strategies (Ayokunmi et al.,
2024; Ibojo et al., 2025), while the accessibility of respondents facilitates data collection (Ojelabi,
2025). Previous marketing research in Ibadan, including studies on digital marketing and customer
experience, further provides an academic research context (Carim, Ibr ahim, & Adedokun, 2024;
Ayokunmi, Abu-Seman, & Rashid, 2024). Nigeria Bottling Company (NBC) Plc., the authorised
bottling partner of the Coca-Cola system in Nigeria, has a significant operational and distribution
presence in Ibadan. Its products are widely distributed and consumed within the city and
surrounding communities, providing a suitable setting for examining strategic marketing practices
and customer retention. The competitive beverage environment, involving soft drinks, bottled
water, energy drinks and fruit juice manufacturers, further makes Ibadan appropriate for the study.
The choice of NBC Plc.'s Ibadan Plant was also informed by its historical significance and
established customer relationship. The plant was founded in 1961, ten years after the incorporation
of Nigeria Bottling Company as a franchise bottler of the Coca -Cola Company, and formally
opened in 1977. It presently has major distribution outlets in Oyo State, including Ibadan, Shaki,
Kishi, Ogbomosho, Oyo and Lanlate (NBC Plc.).

3.2 Research Design
This The study adopted a mixed survey method involving qualitative and descriptive survey
research design. The descriptive survey design enabled the systematic collection of data from a
defined population to describe existing conditions, re lationships and practices as they occur
naturally. The design was considered appropriate because the study seeks to examine how strategic
marketing practices influence customer retention within the Nigerian retail sector and to measure
perceptions and experiences relating to loyalty and retention.

3.3 Population of the Study
The population of the study comprised of Company Management Staff, Marketing/Sales
Managers, and Front-line Executives of the Marketing and Sales Department of Nigeria Bottling
Company (NBC) Plc., Ibadan. Based on the study’s focus, the company’s Managemen t staff and
Marketing and Sales Department team of the Nigeria Bottling Company (NBC) Plc.’s were
involved, because they are directly involved in, or affected by the company strategic marketing
practices and customer retention efforts.

3.4 Sampling Methods and Sample Size
A sample size of 55 respondents, consisting of 15 Management staff and 40 Marketing and Sales
Department staff, was selected for the study. This size is considered adequate for statistical
analysis and consistent with similar empirical studies in marketing and retail research (Ogunnaike,

3.5 Sources and Methods of Data Collection
The study utilised primary data obtained through the administration of structured questionnaires
to respondents. The researcher personally collected the primary data to ensure a high response rate
and to seek clarification where necessary. Questionnaires w ere distributed and retrieved within a
specified period, with liaison with the Human Resources Department (Corporate Affairs Unit) of
NBC Plc. to minimise non -response bias. Respondents were assured of confident iality and
anonymity to encourage honest and accurate responses.
Secondary information was obtained from relevant scholarly and academic journals, literature,
publications, company bulletins and annual reports, periodicals and books, including online
sources

3.7 Research Instruments
The primary instrument for data collection is a structured questionnaire designed by the researcher
based on the study objectives, research questions, and reviewed literature. The questionnaire is
divided into two sections. Section A is structured on demog raphic information of respondents,
while Section B is on the constructs of the independent and dependent variables. These are strategic
marketing practices (product differentiation, branding, customer relationship management,
pricing, and promotional strategies), which is the independent variable and customer retention,
which is also the dependent variable.  Responses are measured using a five -point Likert scale,
ranging from Strongly Agree (5) to Strongly Disagree (1). The Likert scale is chosen because it
will allow respondents to express varying degrees of agreement and facilitates quantitative
analysis.

3.8 Validity and Reliability of the Instruments
Validity refers to the extent to which the research instrument measures what it is intended to
measure. To ensure content and face validity, the questionnaire items were developed in line with
established literature on strategic marketing and customer retention. The instrument was reviewed
by experts in marketing and research method ology, whose suggestions were incorporated to
improve clarity, relevance, and coverage. This process ensures that the questionnaire adequately
captured all key constructs of the study.  Reliability refers to the consistency and stability of the
measurement instrument. To ensure reliability, a pilot test conducted using five respondents not
included in the final sample, using Cronbach’s Alpha coefficient gave reliability coefficients that
were above 0.70 which is considered acceptable, indicating internal con sistency among
questionnaire items. This conforms with the adapted similar empirical studies in marketing and
retail research of Ogunnaike, et al. (2022), Azad, et al. (2025), and Zhang, et al. (2024).

4.5 Discussion of Findings
The findings of the study revealed that strategic marketing practices have significant relationships
with customer retention in Nigeria Bottling Company Plc., Ibadan. The findings are discussed in
relation to the five specific hypotheses tested.
The first finding established a significant positive relationship between product differentiation and
customer retention (r = 0.732, p < 0.001). This indicates that product attributes such as variety,
packaging, product sizes, flavours and perceived unique ness contribute to customers' continued
patronage. The finding supports Porter (1985), who identified differentiation as a strategy through
which firms create unique attributes valued by customers. In the Nigerian beverage market, where
consumers have several alternatives, the ability of a company to provide distinguishable products
can strengthen customer preference and reduce switching behaviour.
The second finding showed a significant positive relationship between branding strategy and
customer retention (r = 0.684, p < 0.001). The result indicates that a strong and consistent brand
identity contributes to customer loyalty and continued patronage.  This finding is consistent with
Aaker (1991), who argued that brand awareness, perceived quality, brand associations and brand

loyalty contribute to brand equity. The strong branding perception observed in the study suggests
that trust, reliability and consistent brand communication can strengthen customers' attachment to
the brand.
The third finding revealed a significant positive relationship between Customer Relationship
Management (CRM) and customer retention (r = 0.751, p < 0.001). CRM recorded the strongest
correlation among the five strategic marketing practices. This suggests that effective customer
interaction, complaint handling, personalised communication, customer service and relationship
maintenance are important in sustaining customer relationships. The finding supports Berry (1983)
and the principles of Relationship Marketing Theory, which emphasise trust, commitment,
communication and continuous relationship development as important mechanisms for customer
loyalty and retention.  The fourth finding established a significant positive relationship between
pricing strategy and customer retention (r = 0.562, p < 0.001). This indicates that customers'
perceptions of price fairness, value for money, discounts and incentives are associated with thei r
continued patronage. The finding is consistent with Kotler and Armstrong (2018), who emphasise
the importance of value-based pricing and customers' perceptions of value in marketing decisions.
However, the comparatively lower correlation suggests that pr icing, although important, may
operate alongside other factors such as product quality, branding, relationships and product
differentiation in influencing retention. The fifth finding showed a significant positive relationship
between promotion strategy and customer retention (r = 0.711, p < 0.001). This demonstrates that
advertising, sponsorship, sales promotion, event marketing and other promotional activities can
strengthen brand visibility and encourage continued patronage. The finding agrees with Shimp
(2017), who noted that effective promotional activities can stimulate interest, encourage purchase
and reinforce brand positioning and loyalty. The result further suggests that sustained
communication with customers is important in a competitive FMCG envi ronment where brands
continuously compete for consumer attention.  Overall, the findings demonstrate that product
differentiation, branding, CRM, pricing and promotion are relevant dimensions of strategic
marketing in relation to customer retention. The results also reinforce the relevance of Relationship
Marketing Theory, particularly the importance of continuous engagement, trust and value creation
in developing long-term customer relationships.

4.6 Conclusion
This study examined the relationship between strategic marketing practices and customer retention
in Nigeria Bottling Company Plc., Ibadan. The study focused on product differentiation, branding
strategy, Customer Relationship Management, pricing strategy and promotion strategy.
The findings established significant positive relationships between each of the five strategic
marketing practices and customer retention. Product differentiation was associated with customer
retention through product variety, packaging, sizes, flavours an d other distinguishing  attributes.
Branding strategy was found to support customer loyalty through brand identity, trust and
consistent communication. CRM demonstrated the strongest relationship with customer retention,
highlighting the importance of customer engagement, servic e quality, complaint handling and
relationship maintenance. Pricing strategy also showed a significant relationship, indicating the
relevance of perceived value and price fairness, while promotion strategy demonstrated a strong
positive relationship through advertising, sponsorship, sales promotion and other communication
activities. Based on these findings, the study concludes that strategic marketing practices constitute
important mechanisms through which Nigeria Bottling Company Plc. can strengthen customer
relationships and encourage continued patronage in the competitive Nigerian beverage market.

4.7 Recommendations
Based on the findings of the study, the following recommendations are made:
1. Customer Relationship Management: Nigeria Bottling Company Plc. should continue to
strengthen its CRM practices through active customer engagement, effective complaint-resolution
mechanisms, personalised communication and customer loyalty initiatives. Continuous training
and development of customer-facing employees should also be encouraged.
2. Product Differentiation: The company should continue to improve its product portfolio
through product innovation, new flavours, packaging options, sizes and product -line
improvements that respond to changing consumer preferences and market segments.
3. Branding Strategy: The company should sustain consistent brand development and
communication across its marketing channels. Strong attention should be given to maintaining
brand trust, product quality and a consistent brand identity.

perceptions of value and fairness. Discounts, incentives and competitive pricing may be used
appropriately while ensuring that pricing decisions do not undermine product quality or brand
positioning.
5. Promotion Strategy: The company should sustain effective advertising, digital
communication, sponsorship, event marketing and sales promotion activities to maintain brand
visibility and strengthen customer engagement and loyalty.
6. Quality and Consumer Protection: Relevant regulatory bodies, particularly NAFDAC and
the FCCPC, should continue to ensure compliance with product -quality and fair -competition
standards, as these contribute to consumer confidence and sustainable market competition.
