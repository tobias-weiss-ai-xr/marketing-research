Abstract

The rapid development of digital technologies has accelerated the emergence of virtual tourism
and immersive experiences based on virtual reality. This study aims to identify the main research
trends and emerging directions in the field of virtual tourism through a bibliometric analysis of the
scientific literature. The analysis was conducted using articles indexed in the Scopus database during
the period 2015–2025, limited to the Business, Management and Accounting subject area.
Bibliographic data were processed with VOSviewer software using keyword co-occurrence analysis.
The findings reveal six major thematic clusters related to immersive experiences, tourist behavior,
destination marketing, technology acceptance, digitalization, and emerging virtual experiences. The
results highlight the growing importance of me taverse tourism and the commercialization of
immersive experiences, demonstrating the transformation of virtual tourism into a distinct segment
of the digital experience economy.

1. Introduction

The rapid development of digital technologies has significantly transformed the tourism industry,
generating new forms of interaction between tourists and destin ations. Among the most important
technological innovations, virtual reality (VR), augmented real ity (AR), and immersive
environments have contributed to the emergence of virtual tourism as an alternative and
complementary form of tourism consumption (Yung & Khoo-Lattimor e, 2019). By enabling users
to explore destinations through interactive and immersive experiences, virtual tourism has expanded
the traditional boundaries of travel and introduced new opportu nities for tourism organizations and
digital service providers.
The growing accessibility of immersive technologies and the increasing demand for personalized
digital experiences have accelerated the development of virtual  tourism. The COVID-19 pandemic
further reinforced this trend by restricting physical mobility and encouraging the adoption of virtual
experiences as substitutes for conventional travel (Guttentag, 2020). Consequently, virtual tours,
360-degree content, and VR-based applications evolved from prom otional tools into products
integrated within the digital experience economy.

2. Literature review

2.1. Virtual tourism and immersive experiences

The development of digital technologies has transformed the way  tourists interact with
destinations and consume tourism experiences. Virtual tourism h as emerged as an innovative form
of tourism consumption based on virtual reality (VR), augmented reality (AR), and immersive digital
environments. According to Yung and Khoo-Lattimore (2019), virt ual tourism refers to the use of
digital technologies that enable users to experience and explore destinations through interactive and
simulated environments. Unlike traditional promotional tools, i mmersive technologies allow users
to actively engage with digital content and experience a sense of presence within virtual spaces.
Immersion and presence are considered fundamental concepts in t he development of virtual
tourism experiences. Immersion refers to the degree to which users perceive the virtual environment
as realistic and engaging, while p resence describes the psychol ogical sensation of “being there”
within the digital environment (Flavián et al., 2019). These dimensions significantly influence users'
perceptions, satisfaction, and behavioral intentions toward virtual experiences.

2.2. Virtual tourism in the post-pandemic context

The COVID-19 pandemic accelerate d the adoption of digital techn ologies in tourism and
increased interest in virtual experiences. Travel restrictions and social distancing measures
encouraged destinations, museums, and tourism organizations to employ virtual tours and VR
applications to maintain communication with potential visitors (Guttentag, 2020). As a result, virtual
tourism evolved from a promotional instrument into an alternative form of tourism consumption.
The growing popularity of 360-degree videos, immersive applications, and digital storytelling has
contributed to the expansion of the experience economy in touri sm. Consumers increasingly seek
personalized and interactive experiences, while tourism organiz ations invest in technological
innovations to enhance customer engagement and destination attr activeness (Pine II & Gilmore,
2011).

2.3. Metaverse tourism and emerging digital ecosystems

Recent developments in immersive technologies have given rise t o the concept of metaverse
tourism, which refers to persistent and interactive virtual env ironments where users can participate
in tourism-related activities th rough digital avatars (Buhalis et al., 2019). These virtual ecosystems
extend the traditional role of tourism promotion and create opp ortunities for new forms of social
interaction and digital consumption.

3. Research methodology

This study adopts a bibliometric approach to investigate the in tellectual structure and emerging
research trends in the field of virtual tourism and immersive experiences. Bibliometric analysis is
widely used to evaluate scientif ic production, identify themati c patterns, and map relationships
among concepts within a specific research domain (Donthu et al., 2021).
The Scopus database was selected as the source of bibliographic data due to its extensive coverage
of high-quality scientific publications and its relevance for r esearch in business, management, and
tourism. The search strategy included the following keywords: “ virtual tourism”, “virtual reality
tourism”, “VR tourism”, and “immersive tourism”. The search query was defined as:
("virtual tourism" OR "virtual reality tourism" OR "VR tourism" OR "immersive tourism")
To ensure the relevance and cons istency of the dataset, several  inclusion criteria were applied.
Only journal articles published between 2015 and 2025 were cons idered. Furthermore, the analysis
was limited to documents written in English, classified as “Art icle”, with the publication stage
“Final”, and indexed within the subject area “Business, Management and Accounting”.
The bibliographic records were exported in CSV format and processed using VOSviewer software
(version 1.6.20). A keyword co-occurrence analysis was performed to identify the conceptual
structure of the field and the relationships among research themes. The “All keywords” option and
the “Full counting” method were employed. A minimum threshold of five occurrences per keyword
was established to exclude isolated concepts and enhance the re liability of the network. In addition,
a thesaurus file was used to eliminate duplicate terms and standardize similar keywords.
The visualization and clustering functions available in VOSview er enabled the identification of
the most influential concepts and the detection of major themat ic clusters. Furthermore, density
visualization was used to highlight the intensity of relationsh ips among keywords and to identify
emerging topics within the virtual tourism literature.
The methodological framework adopted in this study provides a comprehensive overview of the
evolution of research on virtual tourism and contributes to understanding the current state and future
directions of this rapidly expanding field.

5. Conclusions

The present study provides a comprehensive overview of the evol ution of research on virtual
tourism and immersive experiences through a bibliometric analysis of scientific publications indexed
in the Scopus database. The findings demonstrate that virtual t ourism has become one of the most
dynamic research areas within the digital transformation of the tourism industry.
The keyword co-occurrence analysis and the visualization genera ted using VOSviewer revealed
six major thematic clusters associated with immersive experienc es, tourist behavior, destination
marketing, technology acceptance, digitalization, and emerging virtual experiences. These results
indicate that the scientific literature has progressively shift ed from considering virtual tourism
primarily as a destination promotion tool to recognizing it as a distinct component of the digital
experience economy.
Furthermore, the analysis highlighted several emerging trends, including the development of
immersive experiences, the increasing relevance of metaverse to urism, the importance of consumer
behavior and technology acceptance , and the growing commerciali zation of VR-based tourism
services. These trends suggest that immersive technologies will  continue to reshape tourism
consumption patterns and create new opportunities for innovation and value creation.
