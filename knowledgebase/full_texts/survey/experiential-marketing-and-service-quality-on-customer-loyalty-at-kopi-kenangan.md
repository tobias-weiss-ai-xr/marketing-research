Abstract:
Background: Indonesia's coffee shop industry has grown rapidly due to shifting lifestyles,
transforming coffee consumption into a social and cultural practice. Kopi Kenangan,
a leading national chain, faces intense competition in sustaining customer loyalty in the
suburban market of Subang, West Java.
Purpose: This research examines the partial and simultaneous influence of experiential
marketing and service quality on customer loyalty at Kopi Kenangan Subang. The study is
scoped to a single suburban quick-service outlet.
Design/methodology/approach: An explanatory survey was conducted with 50 purposively
selected respondents who had purchased at least twice, meeting parametric regression
requirements. Experiential marketing was measured through five experiential dimensions;
service quality through five SERVQUAL dimensions. Multiple linear regression alongside
validity, reliability, and classical assumption tests was applied using SPSS v25.
Findings/Result: Both variables simultaneously and significantly affect loyalty, explaining
41.8% of its variance. Service quality significantly influences loyalty, while experiential
marketing shows no significant effect. Service quality (73.1%) and experiential marketing
(71.6%) are perceived positively, whereas loyalty remains moderate (66.3%).
Conclusion: Service quality dominates loyalty formation in this quick-service context.
Management should prioritize operational consistency and staff responsiveness over
atmospheric investments to strengthen long-term customer retention.
Originality/value (State of the art): This study reveals a service-quality paradox in
suburban quick-service coffee shops, challenging the universal applicability of experiential
marketing theory. While existing frameworks assume atmospheric differentiation drives
loyalty, empirical evidence demonstrates that functional service delivery carries greater
predictive weight. The findings contribute context-specific boundary conditions to loyalty
theory and provide actionable guidance for resource allocation strategies.

INTRODUCTION
Modern lifestyle transformations have fundamentally
shifted coffee consumption from a functional necessity
to a symbolic social practice. Coffee shops now
serve as multipurpose spaces for work, study, and
socialization, where atmospheric design and brand
identity significantly shape consumer engagement
(Rizal & Alam, 2025; Syilfa et al. 2023). This evolution
has triggered rapid expansion of modern coffee chains
across Indonesian suburban markets, intensifying
competition for customer retention. In Subang, West
Java, the proliferation of local and national coffee brands
has created a saturated environment where attracting
first-time buyers is increasingly easier than securing
repeat patronage. Understanding which operational and
marketing dimensions genuinely drive habitual loyalty
in this suburban quick-service context has become a
critical managerial challenge. Without clear empirical
guidance, coffee shop managers risk misallocating
resources toward atmospheric enhancements that may
not yield proportional loyalty returns. Identifying the
precise determinants of customer loyalty is therefore
essential for developing evidence-based retention
strategies that ensure sustainable growth in emerging
market coffee industries.
Extant literature firmly establishes experiential
marketing and service quality as foundational
antecedents of customer loyalty in service contexts.
Schmitt (1999) and Brakus et al. (2009) demonstrate
that multi-dimensional brand experiences (sense,
feel, think, act, relate) foster emotional attachment
and differentiation. Concurrently, Parasuraman et al.
(1988) prove that functional service excellence across
tangibles, reliability, responsiveness, assurance, and
empathy consistently predicts repeat patronage and
advocacy. Recent Indonesian studies confirm these
relationships in metropolitan settings (Dhisasmito &
Kumar, 2020; Risal et al. 2025), meta-analytic review
of fifteen years of loyalty research confirms that service
quality and brand experience represent the two dominant
loyalty antecedents across hospitality contexts, yet their
relative predictive weights vary considerably by service
format, market maturity, and customer demographics.
Despite this evidence, systematic integration of both
constructs within suburban quick-service coffee chains
remains notably absent. Theoretical frameworks often
assume experiential primacy (Pine & Gilmore, 1999;
Schmitt, 1999), presuming that atmospheric and
emotional engagement naturally translates into loyalty
across all service formats. However, preliminary
observations in emerging suburban markets suggest
that utilitarian motivations, visit frequency constraints,
and competitive parity may elevate functional service
reliability above experiential differentiation. This study
addresses the gap by testing an integrated experiential-
service framework specifically within Kopi Kenangan
Subang, a representative suburban quick-service outlet.
The novelty lies in empirically examining whether the
theoretical dominance of experiential marketing holds
in practice, or whether service quality fundamentals
carry greater explanatory power for loyalty formation
in contexts characterized by moderate dwell times
and high operational standardization. By isolating
this paradox, the research contributes context-specific
boundary conditions to loyalty theory and provides
actionable clarity for resource allocation in Indonesia’s
rapidly expanding suburban coffee sector.
This study employs a quantitative explanatory survey
design utilizing multiple linear regression analysis
to examine direct causal relationships between two
independent constructs and one dependent outcome.
Unlike structural equation modeling, which requires
larger samples for complex latent variable estimation
and indirect path testing, multiple linear regression is
statistically robust and methodologically appropriate
for a sample of 50 respondents when evaluating direct
predictor-outcome relationships (Hair et al. 2019). The
analytical approach aligns with the research objectives
by enabling clear isolation of partial (individual) and
simultaneous (combined) effects while maintaining
interpretability for managerial decision-making. Data
collection follows purposive sampling criteria to ensure
respondents possess adequate brand exposure, while
instrument validation through Pearson correlation
and Cronbach’s Alpha guarantees measurement
precision. Classical assumption testing (normality,
multicollinearity, homoscedasticity) precedes
hypothesis evaluation to ensure parametric validity.
This streamlined methodological pathway provides
a transparent, replicable, and statistically sound
procedure for testing loyalty determinants without
unnecessary model complexity.
This research aims to achieve four specific objectives.
First, to measure the implementation intensity of
experiential marketing across sensory, affective,
cognitive, behavioral, and relational dimensions at
Kopi Kenangan Subang. Second, to assess perceived
service quality levels across the five SERVQUAL

methodological transparency, and direct alignment
with the research objectives.
Based on theoretical foundations and prior empirical
evidence, three hypotheses were formulated for
empirical testing. H1 posits that experiential marketing
has a significant positive effect on customer loyalty,
grounded in Schmitt’s (1999) experiential marketing
theory and supported by Brakus et al. (2009). H2 states
that service quality significantly influences customer
loyalty, based on Parasuraman et al.’s (1988) SERVQUAL
framework and corroborated by Dhisasmito and Kumar
(2020). H3 proposes that experiential marketing and
service quality simultaneously affect customer loyalty,
drawing on integrated service-experience literature that
emphasizes multidimensional loyalty drivers in coffee
shop contexts (Dhisasmito & Kumar, 2020). These
hypotheses provide a clear directional framework for
isolating partial and combined effects.

METHODS
This research utilizes both primary and secondary data
to ensure comprehensive empirical analysis. Primary
data were collected directly from customers of Kopi
Kenangan Subang through structured questionnaires
measuring experiential marketing, service quality,
and customer loyalty. Secondary data were obtained
from peer-reviewed journals, industry reports, and
institutional publications to support contextual analysis
and theoretical grounding. Primary data capture direct
consumer perceptions and behavioral intentions at the
specific research location. Secondary data provide
market context, including coffee consumption trends,
competitive landscape analysis, and demographic
benchmarks. This dual-data approach ensures both
measurement precision and theoretical relevance for
hypothesis testing.
Data collection was conducted from August to
December 2024 at the Kopi Kenangan outlet located
on Jl. Otto Iskandardinata No. 25, Subang. Purposive
sampling was employed to select respondents based
on three specific criteria: (1) having purchased
Kopi Kenangan products at least twice, (2) being 17
years of age or older, and (3) voluntarily consenting
to participate. This sampling strategy ensures that
respondents possess sufficient brand experience to
provide valid and reliable assessments of service and
experiential dimensions. A total of 50 respondents
were recruited, which aligns with statistical guidelines
recommending a minimum of 10–15 observations per
predictor variable for multiple regression analysis (Hair
et al. 2019). The questionnaire was pre-tested with ten
customers to verify item clarity, comprehensibility,
and contextual relevance before full-scale deployment.
Responses were measured using a 7-point Likert scale

RESULTS
Respondent Characteristics
The demographic profile of respondents (n=50)
provides context for interpreting the findings. As
shown in Table 2, the majority of respondents were
female (78%), reflecting the gender demographic often
associated with social coffee consumption. Regarding
age distribution (Table 3), the dominant group was 20–
25 years old (54%), followed by those under 20 (34%),
indicating that the primary customer base consists of
Gen Z and young Millennials. Behavioral patterns
reveal that 54% of respondents visited the outlet only
1–2 times in the past three months (Table 4). This visit
frequency suggests that while customers perceive
the brand positively, most have not yet developed
habitual patronage, which underscores the importance
of identifying the precise drivers required to convert
occasional visitors into loyal regulars.

Instrument quality was assessed to ensure measurement

Descriptive Statistics Analysis
Descriptive statistics provide an overview of
respondents’ perceptions regarding the research
variables. As presented in Table 7, Service Quality
received the highest mean score (25.68), corresponding
to a 73.1% achievement level. This indicates that
respondents perceive Kopi Kenangan’s operational
delivery as strong. Experiential Marketing followed
closely with a 71.6% achievement level, suggesting that
the outlet’s atmosphere and engagement are moderately
appreciated. However, Customer Loyalty recorded
the lowest score (23.22; 66.3%). This gap suggests
that while customers evaluate service and experience
positively, these perceptions have not fully translated
into strong behavioral retention, highlighting the
necessity of understanding which specific dimensions
drive loyalty in this context.
Classical Assumption Tests
Prior to hypothesis testing, classical assumption
tests were conducted to verify the suitability of the
regression model. Normality tests indicated that
residuals were normally distributed based on histogram
and P-P plot analysis. Multicollinearity tests (Table 8)
showed Tolerance values of 0.594 (>0.10) and VIF
values of 1.684 (<10.00), confirming the absence
of high correlation between independent variables.
Additionally, heteroscedasticity analysis revealed
a random scatter of residuals around the zero axis,
satisfying the homoscedasticity assumption. These
diagnostics confirm that the regression model meets
the necessary statistical requirements for unbiased
estimation.
Multiple Linear Regression Analysis
The regression coefficients (Table 9) yield the predictive
equation: Y = 3.190 + 0.101X₁ + 0.681X₂. The
coefficient for Service Quality (0.681) is substantially
larger than that of Experiential Marketing (0.101),
indicating a marked disparity in predictive magnitude.
The standard error for Experiential Marketing (0.172)
relative to its coefficient (0.101) suggests that this
relationship is weak and unstable, while Service
Quality’s coefficient (0.681) substantially exceeds its
standard error (0.167), indicating a robust and reliable
relationship. The coefficient of determination (Table
10) reveals R²=0.418, meaning both predictors jointly
explain 41.8% of loyalty variance. The remaining
58.2% is attributable to factors outside the model’s
scope, such as price fairness, brand image, and digital

single dimension but rather from the cumulative impact

et al. (2025), who found that integrated service and
atmospheric elements collectively shape patronage
decisions in Indonesian F&B markets. However, the
moderate coefficient of determination (41.8%) reveals

necessary for market entry but insufficient for driving
habitual retention. Customers may appreciate pleasant
ambiance but do not use it as a discriminating factor

Theoretically, this study contributes to the literature by
revealing a service-quality paradox in suburban quick-
service contexts: while experiential marketing theory
emphasizes atmospheric differentiation, empirical
evidence demonstrates that functional service delivery
remains the non-negotiable core of loyalty formation.
This finding suggests the need for contingency theories
that specify boundary conditions — such as market
maturity and customer motivation — under which
functional factors dominate experiential factors. It
Fourth, the measurement approach may have
captured static atmospheric perceptions rather than
dynamic experiential co-creation. Experiential
marketing encompasses interactive engagement,
surprise moments, and emotional narrative arcs
that unfold across multiple touchpoints. If the
instrument emphasized environmental cues over
participatory or relational experiences, it may have
underestimated deeper experiential dimensions that
genuinely influence retention. These contextual factors
collectively explain why theoretical expectations did
not materialize empirically. The finding aligns with
Harris et al.’s (2023) reassessment, demonstrating that
experiential marketing’s influence is highly contingent
on service format, visit frequency, competitive parity,
and customer motivation. Theoretically, this suggests
the need for contingency models that specify boundary
conditions under which experiential versus functional
factors dominate loyalty formation. Practically, it
implies that Kopi Kenangan should view experiential
elements as competitive parity requirements rather
than primary loyalty investments in suburban quick-
service markets.
The Effect of Service Quality on Customer Loyalty
Service quality demonstrates a highly significant and
substantial positive effect on customer loyalty (t =
4.082, p = 0.000, B = 0.681), confirming its foundational
role in retention dynamics. This result strongly aligns
with SERVQUAL theory (Parasuraman et al. 1988)
and cross-industry empirical evidence (Rusydi et al.
2024; Nunkoo et al. 2020; Caruana, 2002; Abror et
al. 2019), establishing that reliability, responsiveness,
assurance, empathy, and tangibles consistently predict
behavioral loyalty. In coffee shop contexts specifically,
functional consistency — accurate orders, prompt
delivery, clean facilities, and courteous staff —
reduces perceived risk and builds transactional trust
during each service encounter. Unlike atmospherics
that create ambient impressions, service quality
manifests in specific, memorable moments of truth
that directly shape repeat intentions. The descriptive
data support this interpretation: respondents rated
service quality highest (73.1%), particularly valuing

Recommendations
To address the limitations and unexplained variance
in this study, future research should focus on the
following areas: Variable Expansion: Incorporate
additional predictors such as perceived value,
brand image, and digital engagement to explain
the remaining 58.2% variance in customer loyalty.
Mediating and Moderating Mechanisms: Examine
customer satisfaction as a mediator to understand the
psychological pathway from service quality to loyalty.
Additionally, investigate visit frequency as a moderator
to test whether experiential marketing becomes
significant among frequent patrons. Generalizability:
Extend the research to multi-city, multi-outlet designs
to determine whether the service-quality dominance
observed in Subang represents a general pattern or a
context-specific phenomenon. Employ larger samples
(200–300) for structural equation modeling to test
full mediation and moderation models. Longitudinal
Design: Employ longitudinal data collection to track
loyalty evolution over time and establish stronger
causal inferences regarding the impact of service
consistency on retention.
This study has several limitations that should be
considered when interpreting the findings. First,
the single-outlet sample (n=50) in Subang limits
generalizability to other Kopi Kenangan branches and
challenges the universal applicability of the experience
economy framework (Pine & Gilmore, 1999) in price-
sensitive, high-efficiency markets.
Managerial Implications
Based on the dominance of service quality, management
should prioritize operational excellence over
atmospheric enhancements. Practical recommendations
include: (1) implementing standardized operational
procedures (SOPs) to ensure consistency in product
quality and service speed; (2) investing in barista
training programs that emphasize responsiveness and
empathy; (3) establishing real-time feedback monitoring
systems to address service lapses immediately; and (4)
viewing experiential elements (e.g., interior design) as
competitive parity requirements rather than primary
loyalty drivers. Resources should be allocated to
maintain functional reliability first, with experiential
investments deployed only when service standards are
consistently met.
CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS
Conclusions
This study aimed to analyze the influence of experiential
marketing and service quality on customer loyalty at
Kopi Kenangan Subang. The conclusions drawn are
a direct response to the research purposes and are
supported by empirical evidence.
First, regarding implementation levels, experiential
marketing receives a moderately positive evaluation
(71.6%), while service quality receives the highest
evaluation (73.1%). Customer loyalty remains at a
moderate level (66.3%). This hierarchy of perception
indicates that while Kopi Kenangan Subang has
successfully delivered functional service reliability,
the gap between high service perception and moderate
loyalty suggests that positive evaluations alone have
not fully translated into habitual patronage.
Second, regarding the partial influence, experiential
marketing does not demonstrate a statistically significant
effect on customer loyalty (t = 0.589, p = 0.559).
This finding contradicts the foundational experiential
marketing theory proposed by Schmitt (1999) and
Pine & Gilmore (1999), which posits that sensory and
emotional engagement are primary loyalty drivers.
