menciptakan peluang baru bagi perusahaan untuk memengaruhi perilaku konsumen. Namun, bukti empiris
mengenai dampaknya terhadap hasil konsumen jangka panjang masih terfragmentasi dan menunjukkan
inkonsistensi teoretis. Penelitian ini bertujuan mensintesis literatur yang ada mengenai adopsi pemasaran digital
dan perilaku konsumen di n egara berkembang serta mengidentifikasi mekanisme yang memengaruhi konversi
keterlibatan digital menjadi nilai konsumen yang berkelanjutan. Penelitian dilakukan menggunakan Systematic
Literature Review (SLR) berdasarkan pedoman PRISMA 2020 dengan sampel akhir sebanyak 47 studi substantif
yang dipublikasikan selama periode 201 5–2025. Hasil penelitian menunjukkan bahwa adopsi pemasaran digital
dipengaruhi oleh faktor teknologi, sosial, dan institusional, termasuk persepsi kegunaan, pengaruh sosial,
kepercayaan, literasi digital, dan kesiapan infrastruktur. Meskipun pemasaran digi tal secara konsisten
meningkatkan keterlibatan konsumen dan niat pembelian, pengaruhnya terhadap loyalitas, advokasi, dan
komitmen jangka panjang masih bervariasi antar konteks. Studi ini mengidentifikasi adanya conversion gap antara
keterlibatan konsumen dan hasil konsumen yang berkelanjutan, yang menunjukkan keterbatasan teori berbasis
adopsi seperti Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) dan Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology
(UTAUT). Untuk mengatasi kesenjangan tersebut, penelitian ini mengusulkan Institutional Conversion
Framework yang mengintegrasikan dimensi teknologi, relasional, dan institusional dalam menjelaskan perilaku
konsumen pasca-adopsi. Temuan ini berkontribu si pada pengembangan teori serta memberikan implikasi praktis
bagi pemasar digital, penyedia platform, dan pembuat kebijakan di negara berkembang.

Abstract: Digital marketing adoption has expanded rapidly across emerging economies, creating new
opportunities for firms to influence consumer behavior. However, evidence regarding its impact on long -term
consumer outcomes remains fragmented and theoretically inconsistent. This study aims to synthesize the existing
literature on digital marketing adoption and consumer behavior in emerging economies while identifying key
mechanisms that shape the conversion of digital engagement into sustainable consumer value. A sys tematic
literature review (SLR) was conducted following the PRISMA 2020 guidelines, resulting in a final sample of 47
substantive studies published between 201 5 and 2025. The findings indicate that digital marketing adoption is
driven by technological, social, and institutional factors, including perceived usefulness, social influence, trust,
digital literacy, and infrastructural readiness. While digital marketin g consistently enhances consumer
engagement and purchase intention, its effects on loyalty, advo cacy, and long -term commitment remain
inconsistent across contexts. This review identifies a persistent conversion gap between consumer engagement
and sustainable consumer outcomes, highlighting limitations in adoption -centric theories such as TAM and
UTAUT. To address this gap, the study proposes an Institutional Conversion Framework that integrates
technological, relational, and institutional dimensions to explain post -adoption consumer behavior. The study

INTRODUCTION
The rapid expansion of digital technologies
has fundamentally reshaped marketing practices
worldwide. Over the past decade, firms have
increasingly adopted digital marketing tools —
including social media platforms, search engine
optimization, mobile marketi ng, and data -driven
analytics—to enhance customer engagement and
optimize market performance (Dwivedi et al.,
2021; Li et al., 2021 ). The acceleration of digital
transformation, particularly following the
COVID-19 pandemic, has intensified reliance on
digital channels as primary interfaces between
firms and consumers (Roreng et al., 2024 ;
Verhoef & Bijmolt, 2019 ). Consequently, digital
marketing adoption is no longer optional but
constitutes a strategic imperative for firms seeking
competitiveness in dynamic environments
(HALIK et al., 2023).
Emerging economies represent a particularly
compelling context for examining digital
marketing adoption. These markets are
characterized by rapid digital penetration,
youthful demographics, expanding middle
classes, and institutional transitions that
differentiate them from developed economies
(Chatterjee & Kumar Kar, 2020 ). However,
structural challenges —including digital
infrastructure disparities, regulatory constraints,
income inequality, and cultural heterogeneity —
may moderate how digital marketing initiatives
influence consumer behavior (J. P. Londong,
2024). Despite accelerated digital diffusion, the
mechanisms through which digital marketing
adoption shapes consumer outcomes in emerging
markets remain theoretically fragmented and
empirically inconsistent.
Recent empirical studies suggest that
digital marketing adoption influences various
consumer-related outcomes, including purchase
intention, brand loyalty, customer engagement,
perceived value, and trust (Alalwan, 2018 ;
Hidayat et al., 2021 ; Sahetapy et al., 2025 ;
Sharabati et al., 2024 ). Nevertheless, findings
often vary across contexts. For instance, while
some studies emphasize the dominant role of
perceived usefulness and interactivity in driving
online engagement (Cho & Jeon, 2023 ;
Hallikainen & Laukkanen, 2021; Roreng & Halik,
2025), others highlight the importance of cultural
values, social influence, open innovation, or
institutional trust in shaping digital
responsiveness (J. B. Halik & Halik, 2024 ). This
inconsistency raises a fundamental question: Are
existing theoretical frameworks —such as the
Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), Unified
Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology
(UTAUT), and the Theory of Planned Behavior
(TPB)—sufficient to explain consumer responses
to digital marketing in emerging economies, or do
contextual contingencies demand theoretical
refinement?
In addition, prior literature reviews have
primarily focused on digital marketing tools in
isolation (e.g., social media marketing, mobile
advertising) or examined digital transformation at
the firm level without systematically synthesizing
consumer-level consequences in emerging
markets (Aswani et al., 2018 ; De Canio et al.,
2021; Dwivedi et al., 2021 ; Sarker et al., 2025 ).
The absence of a comprehensive and critically
integrative review leaves several unresolved
issues: (1) Which determinants most strongly
drive digital marketing adoption in emerging
contexts? (2) How do these determinants translate
into measurable consume r outcomes? (3) What
theoretical and methodological gaps persist in the
literature?
More importantly, existing theoretical
frameworks remain largely adoption -centric.
Models such as TAM, UTAUT, and TPB have
been extensively employed to explain technology
acceptance and usage intentions, yet they provide
limited insight into how consumer e ngagement
evolves into long-term outcomes such as loyalty,
advocacy, and relationship commitment. As a
result, the literature offers an incomplete
explanation of post -adoption consumer behavior
in emerging economies, where institutional
conditions, digital  literacy, and governance
structures may significantly influence conversion
processes. This theoretical limitation highlights
the need for a more integrative perspective
capable of linking technology adoption, consumer
engagement, and sustainable consumer value.

economies are not peripheral digital markets but
central arenas of transformation. However, rapid
growth does not necessarily imply uniform
consumer responsiveness. Variations in
institutional maturity, digital lit eracy, and socio -

efforts.
Despite the rapid adoption of digital
marketing technologies across emerging
economies, evidence regarding their long -term
impact on consumer outcomes remains
fragmented. While many studies report positive
effects on consumer engagement, interaction, and
purchase intention, these outcomes do not
consistently translate into sustained loyalty,
advocacy, or long -term customer relationships.
This phenomenon suggests the existence of a
conversion gap between initial digital engagement
and enduring consumer commi tment. Existing
literature has largely focused on adoption and
usage intentions, leaving limited understanding of
how and under what conditions engagement is
converted into long -term consumer value in
emerging economy contexts.
Even with the rapid growth of digital
platforms in developing economies, there is a
deficiency in the literature regarding a critically
synthesized comprehension of how the adoption
of digital marketing affects consumers in these
settings. Current research  is scattered across
various theories, methods, and locations,
hindering the advancement of cumulative
knowledge. In the absence of systematic
integration and critical assessment, scholars and
practitioners may depend on generalized models
from developed m arkets that might not
sufficiently clarify consumer behavior in diverse
structural contexts. Consequently, a systematic
review and thorough synthesis are essential to
unify theoretical viewpoints, outline empirical
results, pinpoint discrepancies, and sugg est a
cohesive research plan suited for emerging
economies.
Accordingly, this review argues that the
primary theoretical challenge is not technology
adoption itself, but the conversion of engagement
into sustainable consumer outcomes. By
synthesizing evidence from emerging economies,
this study develops the concept  of a conversion
gap and proposes an institutional perspective for
understanding why digital marketing
effectiveness varies across contexts.

Research Problem Formulation
Based on the identified gaps, this study seeks to
address the following research questions:
1. What theoretical frameworks have been
employed to explain digital marketing
adoption and consumer behavior in emerging
economies?
2. What key technological, social, and
institutional factors drive digital marketing
adoption in emerging economies?
3. How do trust and consumer engagement
shape post -adoption outcomes such as
loyalty, advocacy, and purchase intention?
4. What evidence supports the existence of a
conversion gap between digital engagement
and long -term consumer outcomes in
emerging economies?
5. What theoretical, methodological, and
contextual gaps remain in the literature, and
how can future research advance knowledge
in this domain?

METHODS
Search Strategy and Study Selection
The initial review process identified 54
references relevant to the study objectives. After
classification, 47 studies were retained as
substantive sources for thematic synthesis, while
5 methodological references and 2 international
reports were used to s upport the review protocol
and contextual interpretation. The substantive
evidence base was dominated by Scopus-indexed
publications (n = 38), supplemented by a limited
number of additional studies that provided
contextual insights into emerging economy
settings.
A structured search strategy using Boolean
operators was employed to capture relevant
studies. The search string combined terms related
to digital marketing adoption, consumer behavior,
and emerging economy contexts. The primary
search query was formulated as follows:
("digital marketing adoption" OR "digital
marketing" OR "social media marketing" OR
"online marketing" OR "digital advertising" OR
"mobile marketing" OR "influencer marketing")
AND
("consumer behavior" OR "purchase intention"
OR "customer engagement" OR "brand loyalty"
OR "consumer trust" OR "customer satisfaction")
AND
("emerging economies" OR "developing
countries" OR "emerging markets" OR
"Southeast Asia" OR "South Asia" OR "Middle
East" OR "Africa" OR "Latin America")
The search was limited to journal articles
published in English between 2015 and 2025. This
period was selected to capture recent
developments associated with accelerated digital
transformation, increasing social media
penetration, and post -pandemic shifts in
consumer behavior.
The study selection process followed four
sequential stages: identification, screening,
eligibility assessment, and inclusion. Initially, all
records retrieved from the databases were
exported and screened for duplicate entries. After
duplicate removal, ti tles and abstracts were
evaluated to determine their relevance to the
review objectives. Studies were retained for full -
text assessment when they addressed digital
marketing adoption, consumer -level outcomes,
and emerging economy contexts.
To enhance methodological rigor, explicit
inclusion and exclusion criteria were applied.
Studies were included if they: (1) examined
digital marketing adoption or digital marketing
practices; (2) investigated consumer -related
outcomes such as engagement, trust, purchase
intention, loyalty, satisfaction, or advocacy; (3)
focused on emerging economies or developing
market contexts; and (4 ) were published in peer -
reviewed academic journals. Studies were
excluded if they: (1) focused exclusively on firm-
level p erformance without examining consumer
outcomes; (2) investigated developed economies
only; (3) lacked sufficient methodological
transparency; (4) were conference proceedings,
book chapters, editorials, dissertations, or non -
peer-reviewed publications; or ( 5) were
unavailable in full-text format.
A total of 54 references were ultimately
retained for this review, comprising 47
substantive studies, 5 methodological references,
and 2 international reports. The substantive
studies constituted the primary unit of analysis,
while methodological and insti tutional sources
were used to support the review protocol and
contextual interpretation.

flow diagram, ensuring transparency,

the review process.

Data Analysis and Thematic Synthesis
For data analysis, this study applies a
thematic and content -based synthesis approach,
complemented by descriptive publication
analysis. Thematic synthesis enables the
identification of recurring patterns, theoretical
frameworks, determinants, mediating or
moderating variables, and consumer outcomes
across studies (Naeem et al., 2023; Snyder, 2019).
Articles were coded systematically according to
key dimensions, including theoretical foundation,
research design, geographic context,
measurement constructs, and main findings. The
coding process was conducted iteratively by the
authors. Initial codes were generated from the
research objectives and refined through repeated
examination of the included studies. Similar
concepts were subsequently grouped into broader
themes, includi ng adoption drivers, trust and
engagement mechanisms, consumer outcomes,
and contextual contingencies. Discrepancies in
coding interpretation were resolved through
discussion and consensus among the authors to
enhance analytical consistency.
 Cross-study comparison was conducted to
detect convergences, divergences, and
underexplored areas. In addition, a critical
synthesis approach was employed to evaluate the
robustness of theoretical applications —such as
TAM, UTAUT, TPB, and relationship mar keting
theory—and to assess whether these frameworks
adequately capture contextual specificities in
emerging economies.
Descriptive statistics were used to present
publication trends, regional distribution, and
methodological patterns, while interpretive
analysis was conducted to develop an integrative
framework linking determinants of digital
marketing adoption with consum er-level
outcomes. This dual analytical strategy
strengthens both the descriptive and explanatory
contributions of the review. By combining
systematic screening procedures with critical
thematic synthesis, the study ensures
methodological rigor, conceptual  clarity, and
theoretical advancement in understanding the
consumer impact of digital marketing adoption in
emerging economies.

Quality Assessment
To ensure the credibility and relevance of the
included studies, a quality appraisal procedure
was conducted during the eligibility stage. Each
study was assessed based on four criteria: (1)
clarity of research objectives, (2) methodological
transparency, (3) appropriateness of data analysis
techniques, and (4) relevance to the review
objectives. Studies that lacked sufficient
methodological information or demonstrated
limited relevance to digital marketing adoption
and consumer outcomes in emerging economi es
were excluded during the screening process. The
quality assessment was not intended to rank
studies but to ensure that only methodologically
sound and relevant evidence was included in the
final synthesis.

Adoption Drivers of Digital Marketing in
Emerging Economies
The reviewed literature consistently
indicates that digital marketing adoption in
emerging economies is influenced by a
combination of technological, social, and
contextual factors. While traditional technology
acceptance frameworks such as the Technology
Acceptance Model (TAM) and the Unified
Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology
(UTAUT) remain relevant, the evidence suggests
that adoption decisions are shaped by broader
environmental conditions that extend beyond
perceptions of technology usefulness and ease of
use.
Across the reviewed studies, perceived
usefulness emerged as one of the most frequently
reported determinants of digital marketing
adoption (J. Halik et al., 2021 ; J. B. Halik et al.,
2026; Su et al., 2023). Consumers are more likely
to engage with digital marketing platforms when
they perceive clear benefits, such as greater
convenience, faster access to information,
personalized recommendations, and improved
purchasing efficiency. Similarly, perceived ease
of use facilitates adoption by reducing the
cognitive effort required to navigate digital
platforms, particularly among consumers with
limited technological experience  (Sarker et al.,
2025; Verhoef & Bijmolt, 2019).
Social influence also plays a significant
role in shaping adoption behavior within
emerging economies. In many collectivist
societies, consumers rely heavily on
recommendations from family members, peers,
online communities, and social media influencers
when evaluating digital products and services.
Consequently, social validation often accelerates
the acceptance of digital marketing channels and
reinforces consumer willingness to interact with
branded content (Cho & Jeon, 2023 ; Dwivedi et
al., 2021).
Beyond these traditional adoption drivers,
the review highlights the increasing importance of
trust-related factors  (Hallikainen & Laukkanen,
2021). Several studies report that consumers'
willingness to engage with digital marketing
activities depends heavily on their confidence in
online transactions, data security, platform
credibility, and information reliability  (Alalwan,
2018; Dash et al., 2025 ). This finding is
particularly pronounced in emerging economies,
where institutional protections, regulatory
enforcement, and consumer safeguards may vary
considerably across countries. As a result, trust
functions not merely as a supporting factor but as
a critical prerequisite for digital engagement.
The findings further reveal that digital
infrastructure and technological readiness
substantially influence adoption outcomes.
Access to reliable internet connectivity,
smartphone penetration, digital payment systems,
and platform accessibility determine the extent to
which consumers can participate in digital
marketplaces (Mergel et al., 2019 ; Pingali et al.,
2023). Studies conducted in Southeast Asia,
South Asia, and several African markets
consistently demonstrate that infrastructure
constraints may limit adoption even when
consumer attitudes toward digital marketing are
generally positive (Pingali et al., 2023; Purandare,
2024).
A comparative analysis of the reviewed
literature suggests that adoption drivers are not
uniformly distributed across contexts. In
relatively mature digital environments, such as
parts of Southeast Asia, perceived usefulness and
personalization tend to dom inate consumer
adoption decisions. In contrast, studies conducted
in less mature digital ecosystems frequently
identify trust, infrastructure quality, and digital

Trust, Consumer Engagement, and Post -
Adoption Outcomes
Beyond initial adoption, the literature
increasingly emphasizes the importance of trust
and consumer engagement as critical mechanisms
through which digital marketing influences
consumer behavior. While adoption -related
factors explain consumers' willingness to interact
with digital platforms, post -adoption outcomes
depend largely on the quality of the relationship
established between consumers and digital
marketing ecosystems (Dwivedi et al., 2021; Li et
al., 2021).
Trust emerged as one of the most frequently
cited determinants of successful post -adoption
outcomes across the reviewed studies. In digital
environments characterized by information
asymmetry, perceived risk, and limited physical
interaction, consumers oft en rely on trust as a
mechanism for reducing uncertainty ( Hallikainen
& Laukkanen, 2021 ; Hidayat et al., 2021 ). Trust
may be directed toward multiple entities,
including brands, digital platforms, online
vendors, influencers, and payment systems.
Consequently, trust serves as a foundational
element that shapes consumers' willingness to
engage with digital marketi ng content and
maintain long-term relationships with firms ( Lou
& Yuan, 2019; Hassan et al., 2025).
The review further reveals that consumer
engagement functions as a key intermediary
between digital marketing initiatives and
behavioral outcomes. Interactive content,
personalized recommendations, social media
communication, influencer marketing, and
gamification strategies have been shown to
stimulate cognitive, emotional, and behavioral
engagement ( Clement Addo et al., 2021 ; De
Canio et al., 2021 ; Voorveld et al., 2018 ).
Engaged consumers are generally more likely to
interact with brands, share content, participate in
online communities, and develop favorable
attitudes toward products and services
(Hollebeek, 2019; Winell et al., 2023).
However, the relationship between trust,
engagement, and consumer outcomes is not
always straightforward. While many studies
report positive associations between engagement
and purchase intention, evidence regarding long -
term outcomes such as loyalty, advo cacy, and
repurchase behavior remains less consistent (Dash
et al., 2025 ; Khan et al., 2024 ). Several studies
indicate that consumers may actively engage with
digital content without developing enduring
commitments to a particular brand. This
phenomenon is especially evident in highly
competitive digital marketplaces where
consumers can easily s witch between platforms,
brands, and sellers at relatively low cost
(Hallikainen & Laukkanen, 2021 ; Dash et al.,
2025).
Comparative evidence across emerging
economies further demonstrates that the
effectiveness of trust and engagement
mechanisms is highly context -dependent. In
markets with relatively mature digital ecosystems,
consumer engagement often translates more
effectively into satisfaction and loyalty due to
stronger institutional support, reliable payment
infrastructures, and higher levels of digital
confidence (Pingali et al., 2023; Bao et al., 2025).
Conversely, in environments characterized by
regulatory uncertainty, limited consumer
protection, or low digital literacy, engagement
may generate short -term interactions without
producing sustainable relational outcomes
(Chatterjee & Kumar Kar, 2020 ; Djimesah et al.,
2026).
Another recurring finding concerns the
growing role of personalized digital experiences.
Artificial intelligence -driven recommendations,
targeted advertising, and customized content have
been shown to enhance engagement and perceived
relevance ( Dwivedi et al., 2021 ; McKee et al.,
2025). Nevertheless, excessive personalization
may also trigger privacy concerns, perceptions of
surveillance, and resistance behaviors, potentially
weakening consumer trust ( McKee et al., 2025 ;
Hallikainen & Laukkanen, 2021 ). This suggests

The Conversion Gap in Emerging Economies
One of the most significant insights emerging
from this review is the identification of a
persistent conversion gap between digital
marketing engagement and long -term consumer
outcomes in emerging economies. While the
reviewed literature consistently demon strates
positive effects of digital marketing on consumer
attention, interaction, engagement, and purchase
intention, the evidence becomes considerably less
consistent when examining outcomes such as
loyalty, advocacy, retention, and sustained
relationship commitment ( Clement Addo et al.,
2021; Dash et al., 2025; Khan et al., 2024).
This finding reveals an important limitation
within the existing literature. Most studies
implicitly assume a linear progression from
digital marketing adoption to engagement and
ultimately to positive consumer outcomes.
Adoption-centric theories, includin g the
Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) and the
Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of
Technology (UTAUT), primarily focus on
explaining consumers’ willingness to adopt and
use digital technologies ( Dwivedi et al., 2021 ;
Sarker et al., 2025 ). However, these frameworks
provide limited insight into what occurs after
adoption, particularly regarding the mechanisms
that transform engagement into enduring
consumer relationships. As a result, the literature
remains heavily focused on technology
acceptance while paying comparatively less
attention to post-adoption relational dynamics.
The reviewed evidence suggests that
consumer engagement should not be viewed as a
direct proxy for loyalty. Across multiple emerging
economy contexts, consumers frequently interact
with digital marketing content, participate in
social media campaigns, foll ow influencers, and
engage with online platforms without necessarily
developing long -term commitment toward a
specific brand ( Voorveld et al., 2018 ; Winell et
al., 2023 ). In highly competitive digital
environments, engagement often reflects
temporary attention rather than durable relational
attachment. This distinction is particularly
important because engagement metrics are
frequently used by practitioners as indicators  of
marketing effectiveness, despite limited evidence
that such interactions consistently translate into
enduring consumer relationships.
The existence of this conversion gap can be
attributed to several contextual conditions that
characterize emerging economies. First,
institutional trust remains uneven across many
developing markets. Consumers may trust a
particular platform or digital cam paign
sufficiently to engage with content, yet remain
reluctant to establish long -term relationships due
to concerns regarding transaction security,
product authenticity, privacy protection, or
dispute resolution mechanisms ( Hallikainen &
Laukkanen, 2021 ; Bao et al., 2025 ). Second,
variations in digital literacy influence consumers’
ability to evaluate digital information and
navigate online transactions effectively
(Djimesah et al., 2026 ). Third, differences in
technological infrastructure and platform
reliability can affect the consistency of consumer
experiences ( International Telecommunication
Union, 2023 ; UNDP, 2024 ). Finally, regulatory
uncertainty and weak governance structures may
reduce confidence in digital ecosystems, limiting
the translation of engagement into loyalty and
advocacy outcomes ( Chatterjee & Kumar Kar,
2020).
Comparative evidence further suggests that
the magnitude of the conversion gap varies across
contexts. In relatively mature digital markets,
stronger institutional environments and more
developed digital infrastructures facilitate the
transformation of eng agement into loyalty and
advocacy (Pingali et al., 2023 ; Bao et al., 2025 ).
In contrast, markets characterized by institutional
fragility, infrastructure constraints, and lower
levels of consumer confidence often exhibit
weaker relationships between engagement and

long-term behavioral outcomes ( Djimesah et al.,
2026; Chatterjee & Kumar Kar, 2020 ). These
findings indicate that the effectiveness of digital
marketing cannot be fully understood without
considering the contextual conditions under
which consumer interactions occur.
The concept of the conversion gap therefore
provides a theoretical lens for explaining why
similar digital marketing strategies may generate
substantially different outcomes across emerging
economies. Rather than assuming that adoption
and engagement automatically lead to loyalty, this
review argues that the relationship is contingent
upon institutional and contextual factors that
influence consumers’ willingness to convert
short-term interactions into sustained
commitment. This perspective aligns with broader
calls for contextualizing digital marketing
theories beyond technologically deterministic
explanations and toward institutionally embedded
consumer behavior (Dwivedi et al., 2021; Verhoef
& Bijmolt, 2019).
Consequently, the findings suggest that the
primary challenge facing digital marketing in
emerging economies is no longer encouraging
adoption alone, but understanding the conditions
under which engagement can be successfully
transformed into long-term consumer value. This
perspective extends existing adoption -centric
theories by shifting attention from technology
acceptance toward post -adoption conversion
mechanisms. By introducing the conversion gap
as a distinct conceptual problem, this review
contributes a more nuanced explanation of digital
marketing effectiveness in emerging economies
and provides the foundation for the proposed
Institutional Conversion Framework developed in

DISCUSSION
Theoretical Implications: Beyond TAM and
UTAUT
The findings of this review offer important
theoretical implications for understanding digital
marketing effectiveness in emerging economies.
A dominant pattern across the reviewed studies is
the continued reliance on adoption -centric
theories, particularly the Technology Acceptance
Model (TAM) and the Unified Theory of
Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT).
While these frameworks have substantially
contributed to explaining consumers’ intentions to
adopt and use digital technologies, the present
synthesis suggests that their explanatory power
becomes increasingly limited when examining
post-adoption consumer outcomes ( Cho & Jeon,
2023; Sarker et al., 2025).
TAM and UTAUT primarily focus on the
antecedents of technology acceptance,
emphasizing constructs such as perceived
usefulness, perceived ease of use, performance
expectancy, effort expectancy, and social
influence (Cho & Jeon, 2023; Sarker et al., 2025).
These factors effectively explain why consumers
engage with digital platforms and respond to
digital marketing initiatives. However, the
reviewed evidence indicates that successful
adoption does not necessarily lead to enduring
consumer relationships. Consumers may actively
interact with digital content, participate in online
communities, and express purchase intentions
without developing sustained loyalty, advocacy,
or long -term commitment toward a brand
(Clement Addo et al., 2021 ; Dash et al., 2025 ;
Winell et al., 2023).
This inconsistency reveals an important
theoretical gap. Existing adoption models
implicitly assume a relatively linear relationship
between technology acceptance and favorable
behavioral outcomes. In contrast, the findings of
this review demonstrate that the relationship
between adoption and long -term consumer value
is neither automatic nor universally stable.
Instead, it is shaped by a complex set of contextual
conditions that extend beyond the scope of
conventional adoption theories ( Bao et al., 2025 ;
Djimesah et al., 2026; Pingali et al., 2023).
The concept of the conversion gap proposed
in this review provides a useful lens for addressing
this limitation. The findings suggest that the
critical challenge in emerging economies is not
merely encouraging consumers to adopt digital
platforms, but unde rstanding how engagement
can be transformed into sustainable relational
outcomes. While TAM and UTAUT explain the
initiation of digital interactions, they provide
limited insight into the mechanisms through
which engagement evolves into trust, loyalty,
advocacy, and long -term customer value
(Hallikainen & Laukkanen, 2021 ; Hassan et al.,
2025; Walean et al., 2025).
The review further indicates that institutional
conditions play a more significant role than is
typically acknowledged within traditional
adoption frameworks. Factors such as
institutional trust, digital literacy, regulatory
quality, platform governance, a nd technological
infrastructure repeatedly emerge as influential
determinants of post -adoption outcomes ( Bao et
al., 2025 ; Djimesah et al., 2026 ; International
Telecommunication Union, 2023 ; UNDP, 2024).
These contextual influences help explain why
similar digital marketing strategies often produce
substantially different results across emerging
economies, even when adoption levels appear
comparable. Consequently, consumer responses
cannot be fully unde rstood through individual -
level technology perceptions alone.
Another important implication concerns the
integration of technological and relational
perspectives. Existing studies frequently examine
technology adoption, customer engagement, trust,
and loyalty as separate research streams (Dwivedi

Institutional Conversion Framework for
Emerging Economies
Building upon the concept of the conversion
gap, this review proposes an Institutional
Conversion Framework (ICF) to explain how
digital marketing adoption is translated into long-
term consumer outcomes within emerging
economies. The framework responds to the
limitations of existing adoption-centric models by
emphasizing that digital marketing effectiveness
is not determined solely by technological
acceptance but also by the institutional and
contextual conditions that shape post -adoption
consumer behavior (Dwivedi et al., 2021 ; Sarker
et al., 2025).
The proposed framework conceptualizes
digital marketing effectiveness as a sequential yet
contingent process. The process begins with
digital marketing adoption, which is influenced by
traditional technological factors such as perceived
usefulness, perceived ease of use, interactivity,
personalization, and social influence ( Alalwan,
2018; Dwivedi et al., 2021 ; Li et al., 2021 ).
Consistent with TAM and UTAUT, these factors
encourage consumers to engage with digital
platforms and marketing content ( Cho & Jeon,
2023; Sarker et al., 2025).
However, adoption alone does not guarantee
meaningful consumer outcomes. Following
adoption, consumers enter an engagement stage
characterized by interactions with brands through
social media platforms, mobile applications,
influencer content, digital comm unities, and
personalized marketing initiatives (Clement Addo
et al., 2021; De Canio et al., 2021 ; Farivar et al.,
2023). At this stage, consumers may demonstrate
high levels of participation, interaction, and
purchase intention. Nevertheless, the reviewed
evidence indicates that engagement frequently
remains transactional rather than relational
(Winell et al., 2023; Dash et al., 2025).
The framework therefore introduces the
concept of an institutional conversion mechanism,
which represents the critical process through
which engagement is transformed into long -term
consumer value. This mechanism is shaped by
several contextual conditions that repeatedly
emerged across the reviewed studies.
First, institutional trust  influences
consumers’ confidence in digital transactions,
platform integrity, privacy protection, and dispute
resolution systems. Higher levels of institutional
trust increase the likelihood that consumers will
translate positive engagement experiences in to
sustained commitment and loyalty (Hallikainen &
Laukkanen, 2021; Hidayat et al., 2021; Hassan et
al., 2025).
Second, digital literacy  affects consumers’
ability to evaluate online information, recognize
credible content, manage digital risks, and utilize
digital services effectively. Consumers with
higher digital literacy are more likely to derive
value from digital interactions and mai ntain
longer-term relationships with brands ( Djimesah
et al., 2026; Bao et al., 2025).
Third, platform governance  plays an
important role in shaping perceived fairness,
transparency, accountability, and content
reliability. Effective governance mechanisms
reduce uncertainty and strengthen consumer

confidence in digital ecosystems ( McKee et al.,
2025; Vacca et al., 2021).
Fourth, market maturity  influences the
extent to which digital marketing initiatives can
generate sustainable outcomes. In more mature
digital markets, stronger infrastructures,
established payment systems, and greater
consumer familiarity with digital commerce
facilitate the co nversion of engagement into
loyalty and advocacy. In contrast, institutional
fragility and infrastructure constraints may
weaken this conversion process ( Pingali et al.,
2023; International Telecommunication Union,
2023; UNDP, 2024).
The framework proposes that long -term
consumer outcomes —including loyalty,
advocacy, retention, satisfaction, and relationship
commitment—are not direct consequences of
adoption. Instead, they emerge when engagement
successfully passes through the institut ional
conversion mechanism. This perspective explains
why similar digital marketing strategies often
produce divergent outcomes across emerging
economies despite comparable levels of adoption
and engagement (Dash et al., 2025; Walean et al.,
2025).
From a theoretical perspective, the
Institutional Conversion Framework extends
existing adoption theories by integrating
technological, relational, and institutional
dimensions into a unified explanatory model.
Rather than treating adoption as the endpoint  of
digital marketing effectiveness, the framework
positions adoption as the beginning of a broader
conversion process conditioned by contextual
factors. Consequently, the framework offers a
more comprehensive explanation of consumer
behavior in emerging e conomies where
institutional environments, digital capabilities,
and governance structures vary substantially
across contexts ( Chatterjee & Kumar Kar, 2020 ;
Dwivedi et al., 2021).
The framework further contributes to the
literature by shifting the focus of digital marketing
research from adoption success to conversion
effectiveness (see Figure 2). This shift highlights
the importance of understanding not only whether
consumers engage with digital technologies, but
also whether such engagement can be transformed
into sustainable consumer relationships. As
digital ecosystems continue to evolve acro ss
emerging markets, future research should
empirically test the proposed framework acros s
different institutional and cultural settings to
validate its explanatory power and identify
context-specific conversion mechanisms.

Managerial Implications
The findings of this review offer several
important managerial implications for firms,
digital marketers, platform providers, and
policymakers operating in emerging economies.
A central implication is that digital marketing
success should not be evaluated solely through
adoption and engagement metrics. While
indicators such as click -through rates, social
media interactions, impressions, and purchase
intentions remain important, they provide only a
partial assessment of marketing effectiveness.
Organizations should place greater emphasis on
long-term relational outcomes, including
customer loyalty, retention, advocacy, and
customer lifetime value (Hollebeek, 2019; Winell
et al., 2023).
For marketing practitioners, the findings
suggest that generating consumer engagement is
necessary but insufficient for achieving
sustainable competitive advantage. Many firms
invest heavily in digital campaigns that
successfully attract consumer attention but fail to
cultivate enduring customer relationships.
Consequently, marketers should move beyond
short-term engagement strategies and prioritize
trust-building initiatives, personalized value
creation, and consistent customer experiences
across digital t ouchpoints. Strengthening
consumer trust through transparent
communication, reliable service delivery, and
responsible data management may significantly
improve the conversion of engagement into
loyalty (Hallikainen & Laukkanen, 2021; Hassan
et al., 2025; Hidayat et al., 2021).
The review also highlights the importance of
context-sensitive marketing strategies. Consumer
responses to digital marketing are shaped by
institutional conditions, cultural norms, and levels
of digital maturity. Therefore, strategies that
perform effectively in developed markets may not
generate equivalent outcomes in emerging
economies. Managers should adapt digital
marketing initiatives to local conditions by
considering factors such as digital literacy,
consumer confidence, payment infrastructure, and
platform accessibility. Localization efforts are
particularly important in culturally diverse and
institutionally heterogeneous markets ( Chatterjee
& Kumar Kar, 2020 ; Bao et al., 2025 ; Pingali et
al., 2023).

Future Research Directions
The findings of this review highlight several
promising directions for future research. While
the literature on digital marketing adoption in
emerging economies has expanded considerably
over the past decade, important theoretical,
contextual, and methodol ogical gaps remain
unresolved (Dwivedi et al., 2021; Snyder, 2019).
First, future studies should move beyond
adoption-centric perspectives and examine the
mechanisms through which consumer
engagement is transformed into long -term
relational outcomes. The concept of the
conversion gap introduced in this review suggests
that adoption and engagement do not
automatically result in loyalty, advocacy, or
retention. Future research should therefore
investigate the processes, conditions, and
behavioral pathways that facilitate or inhibit this
conversion. Particular attention should be given to
identifying the institutional and contextual factors
that shape post -adoption consumer behavior

(Hallikainen & Laukkanen, 2021 ; Hassan et al.,
2025; Walean et al., 2025).
Second, further empirical validation of the
proposed Institutional Conversion Framework is
needed. Researchers should examine the relative
influence of institutional trust, digital literacy,
platform governance, and market maturity across
different emergin g economy contexts.
Comparative studies would be particularly
valuable in determining whether these factors
operate consistently across regions or whether
their effects vary according to specific cultural,
economic, and regulatory conditions ( Bao et al.,
2025; Djimesah et al., 2026; Pingali et al., 2023).
Third, greater emphasis should be placed on
cross-country and cross -regional comparisons.
The existing literature remains heavily
concentrated in selected emerging markets,
particularly Southeast Asia and India, while
evidence from Africa, Latin America, and smaller
developing economies remains comparatively
limited. Future studies should investigate how
differences in institutional environments, digital
infrastructures, and consumer cultures influence
digital marketing effectiveness. Such comparative
analyses would contribute to a more nuanced
understanding of contextual heterogeneity within
emerging economies ( Chatterjee & Kumar Kar,
2020; International Telecommunication Union,
2023; UNDP, 2024).
Fourth, future research should explore the
evolving role of artificial intelligence, algorithmic
personalization, and platform ecosystems in
shaping consumer outcomes. As AI -driven
recommendation systems, automated content
generation, and predictive market ing tools
become increasingly prevalent, researchers must
examine their effects on consumer trust, perceived
authenticity, privacy concerns, and long -term
relationship development. These technologies
may simultaneously enhance engagement and
create new forms of consumer resistance, making
them important areas for theoretical and empirical
investigation (Bhuiyan, 2024; McKee et al., 2025;
Sahetapy et al., 2025).
Fifth, methodological diversification is
needed to strengthen causal understanding. The
literature remains dominated by cross -sectional
survey designs, which limit the ability to capture
changes in consumer behavior over time. Future
studies should employ longitudinal, experimental,
mixed-method, and multi -level research designs
to better understand how digital marketing
relationships evolve throughout the consumer
journey. Such approaches would provide richer
insights into the dynamic nature of engagement,
trust formation, and loyalty development (Naeem
et al., 2023; Snyder, 2019).
Sixth, future research should integrate
perspectives from marketing, information
systems, consumer behavior, and institutional
theory to develop more comprehensive
explanatory models. The fragmented nature of
existing scholarship often isolates technologic al,
relational, and contextual factors rather than
examining their interactions. Multi -theoretical
frameworks may offer a more robust
understanding of digital marketing effectiveness
in complex emerging market environments
(Dwivedi et al., 2021; Li et al., 2021; Sarker et al.,
2025).
Finally, future studies should pay greater
attention to vulnerable consumer groups and
issues of digital inclusion. Differences in digital
literacy, socioeconomic status, age, and
geographic accessibility may create unequal
opportunities for participation in digital
marketplaces. Investigating how digital marketing
affects diverse consumer segments can contribute
to both theoretical advancement and more
inclusive digital development policies ( Djimesah
et al., 2026 ; International Telecommunication
Union, 2023; UNDP, 2024).
Collectively, these research directions
suggest that the next stage of digital marketing
scholarship should focus less on whether
consumers adopt digital technologies and more on
understanding how institutional, technological,
and relational conditions shape the conversion of
engagement into sustainable consumer value.
Advancing this agenda will be essential for
developing theories that more accurately reflect
the realities of consumer behavior in emerging
economies.

CONCLUSION
This systematic literature review examined
the impact of digital marketing adoption on
consumer behavior in emerging economies by
synthesizing evidence from 47 substantive studies
published between 2015 and 2025. The findings
indicate that digital marketin g adoption is
influenced by a combination of technological,
social, and institutional factors, including
perceived usefulness, ease of use, social
influence, trust, digital literacy, and
infrastructural readiness. While digital marketing
initiatives consis tently enhance consumer

From a practical perspective, the findings

traditional engagement metrics and prioritize
strategies that foster trust, transparency, consumer
confidence, and long -term relationship

sustainable digital marketing success depends not
only on attracting consumer attention but also on
creating institutional environments that support

remains concentrated in specific emerging
markets, while comparative and longitudinal
studies remain relatively scarce. Future research
should therefore empirically  test the proposed

different institutional, cultural, and technological
contexts. Further investigation into the role of
artificial intelligence, platform governance, and
digital inclusion is also necessary to adva nce

Overall, this review shifts the focus of digital
marketing scholarship beyond technology
adoption toward the broader challenge of
conversion effectiveness. By emphasizing the
institutional conditions that shape post -adoption
outcomes, the study contributes to a more nuanced
understanding of consumer behavior and provides
a foundation for future theoretical and empirical
developments in emerging economy research.
