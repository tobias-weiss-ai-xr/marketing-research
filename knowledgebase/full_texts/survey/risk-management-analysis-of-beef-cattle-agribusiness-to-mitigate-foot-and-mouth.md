Risk management analysis of beef cattle agribusiness
to mitigate foot-and-mouth disease outbreak: A case
study in Banyumas, Indonesia

Akhmad Sodiq*, Muhammad Arief Hidayat, Setya Agus Santosa and Novie Andri Setianto

Department of Animal Production, Faculty of Animal Science, Universitas Jenderal Soedirman, Purwokerto, Indonesia.

Received 13 April, 2026; Accepted 16 June, 2026

Foot-and-Mouth Disease (FMD) continues to pose a significant threat to smallholder beef cattle
agriculture in developing nations, however the incorporation of risk management principles into farm -
level FMD prevention remains understudied. This study examined the risk management capabilities of
beef cattle agribusiness in Banyumas Regency, Central Java, Indonesia, across four dimensions: risk
identification, risk evaluation, risk mitigation,  and adaptive ability, utilizing survey data from 29 farmers
affected during 2022 FMD outbreak. The purposive sampling was applied to reflect the diverse business
practices of smallholder beef cattle. Farmers experiencing management and maintenance involvement
in marketing samples were chosen to reflect the diversity of practices, experiences, and managemen t
systems among smallholder farmers. The data indicated that farmers did not vaccinate cattle before the
outbreak and utilized herbal treatment as the primary response to infection. Around two -thirds of the
farmers reported 1 to 2 cattle infected per farm, while the disease lasted for 15 to 21 days. While farmers
were able to recognize early symptoms, their response was poorly managed by selling the infected
cattle, which amplified the spread of disease across the market chain. This trend suggests that th e
susceptibility of beef cattle agriculture was largely due to systemic deficiencies in risk management
integration rather than solely biological causes . This research is among the first to integrate farmers
adaptive behaviors into a structured risk management framework providing policy -relevant insights for
bolstering sustainable FMD control in resource-limited smallholder contexts.

Key words: Agribusiness, beef cattle, Foot-and-Mouth Disease (FMD), risk management.

Foot-and-Mouth Disease (FMD) is an infectious animal
disease, with an exceptionally high transmission rate and
significant impact on cattle production systems. Caused
by Aphthovirus and the mode of FMD transmission
includes direct contact, aerosol, or indirect media, making
it a serious threat to cloven -hoofed animals (Permatasari
et al., 2024). The clinical signs of FMD include fever,
lesions on the  mouth and feet, low appetite, and growth
disorder, which, collectively, lead to poor productivity and
high cost of medication. From the economic  perspective,

trade barriers, hindered distribution, and increased costs
for disease control and a lengthy recovery period (Knight-
Jones and Rushton, 2013).
The impact of FMD is not only technical and biological
but also systemic within the structure of agribusiness.
Smallholder farmers are characterized by limited
resources, the constrain that which limit the ability to
response to risks of diseases outbreaks due to limited
capacity to manage risk, limited access to formal
veterinary care, and poor implementation of biosecurity
measures (Tuska-Szalay et al., 2023) . The beef cattle
industry in Indonesia serves as  the foundation of
livestock production in rural areas, experienced
numerous limitation s during the FMD outbreak in  2022,
extending beyond the realm of epidemiology to
agribusiness (Febrianto et al., 2025). Mitigations of
infectious animal diseases require integrated preventative
measures including, vaccination, surveillance, biosecurity,
and risk communication (Vora et al., 2023). This
approach is aligned with the risk management principles,
emphasizing risk identification, impact evaluation,
preventive mitigation, fast response, and sustainable
evaluation of the effectiveness of mitigation (Singh et al.,
2013).
Despite this, the implementation of risk management in
smallholder farming often faces structural and economic
challenges. Farmers’ response to disease outbreaks is
often react and oriented towards managing short -term
losses by selling their sick cattle or using alternative
medication, instead of implementing long -term,
preventive mitigation  (Nuvey et al., 2020) . The  pattern
has been recorded in several scenarios involving the
outbreak of livestock diseases in Southeast  Asia, where
the lack of resources and poor dissemination of
information complicated the implementation of
preventative measures (Sieng et al., 2022; Delabouglise
et al., 2020). Late detection and subsequent responses to
FMD cases contributed to escalating the endemic and
increasing the inevitable economic loss (Carpenter et al.,
2011). The situation highlight s the gap between the
recommended risk -based mitigation and farmers’ farm
management practice. Banyumas Regency is among the
beef cattle centers in Central Java Province that
struggles with an FMD outbreak, affecting the stability of
agribusiness and economy. In addition, stereotypes of
FMD affect how local people perceive FMD. Some
farmers regard FMD as a common disease that can be
healed naturally or by traditional medicine. Consequently,
they often disregard biosecurity or vaccinations until an
outbreak occurs, rather than as a preventive measure. It
shows that farmers have low awareness of the risky
spread of FMD and the importance of preventive ac tions
(Rajput et al., 2024). In this context, the consequences of
FMD extend beyond a pure epidemiological
phenomenon,  —rather,   it  constitute  systemic  risk  that

affects the entire agribusiness subsystem, including, the
supply of production input and maintenance to livestock
product and market access for animal products (Kappes
et al., 2023) . Accordingly, analysis of farmers’ adaptive
response and risk management capacity became
important for understanding the resilience capacity of
local agribusiness in mitigating the outbreak and its
associated impacts.
Studies reporting the dynamics of FMD centered on
virological characteristics, epidemiological pattern, and
macro-level economic impact asse ssments (Humphreys
et al., 2025; Li et al., 2021). These studies have
significant contributions to animal science; however, they
do not integrate the relationship between the dynamic
epidemiology of FMD, economic impacts and risk
management principle in agribusiness. In particular, the
interplay of the farmers’ decision-making process, the
broader market dynamics and institutional framework is
underexplored. Studies integrating the farmer’s response
within the broader agribusiness risk management
framework is still limited, especially in smallholder
agricultural systems in developing countries, where risk
management mechanisms are absent, weak and or
poorly enforced (Knight -Jones et al., 2017).  This study
aims to analyze the risk management strategies in  beef
cattle agribusiness post FMD outbreak in Banyumas
Regency applying a structural framework encompassing
risk identification, evaluation, mitigation, and adaptive
system capacity.
This study makes two major contributions. Firstly, it
moves beyond descripti ve documentation of farmer
adaptive actions by integrating risk management
framework including risk identification, evaluation,
mitigation, and adaptive system capacity. Secondly, it
advances integrated analysis of the interrelationship
between biological risks, economic risks, and institutional
response in common analytical framework. Conceptually
its contribution to the understanding of the linkages
between biological disease risk and the economic risk
associated with endemic diseases as well as the
institutional risk involved in disease management.

Conceptual framework

The present study employs a risk management approach,
which has been developed in accordance with the
guidelines provided by  an International Organization for
Standardization (ISO) 31000, and is modified for
application to agribusinesses (Ocampo and Cruz, 2025).
Risk management has been de scribed as an organized
system involving the identification, assessment, treatment,
and monitoring of risks to mitigate the negative impacts
on organizational goals (Studzinski and Pasteur, 2020).
The risk management approach is especially important in
the   context   of   agribusiness,    considering    that    the

operations of agricultural systems involve several types
of risks simultaneously.
The conceptual framework adopted in this research
comprises of four related processes. The first process
relates to risk identification which relates to the ability to
identify the onset of FMD through recognition of
symptoms, modes of transmission, as well as spatio -
temporal considerations associated with an outbreak.
Secondly, risk evaluation involves examining the degree
of damage caused by the disease, both at the individual
animal level and for the overall production process within
a farm.
Thirdly, risk mitigation which refers to the examination
of all preventative and biosecurity measures adopted by
farmers and institutions, such as traditional treatment,
clinical care, vaccination, and movement controls.
Fourthly, Adaptive capacity assessment refers to the
ability of farmers, farmer collectives, and government
institutions to learn from outbreaks and adopt a more
preventative approach.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study area and design

The design used for this study was an explorative cross -sectional
study that sought to analyze the risk management capability of
smallholder beef cattle agribusiness after the 2022 FMD cases. The
study was carried out in September 2024 in the sub districts of
Sokaraja and Kembaran, Banyumas Regency, purposively sampled
based on  FMD incidenc e. An exploratory cross -sectional study
design was used to analyze the risk management capability of
smallholder beef cattle agribusiness following the 2022 FMD
outbreak, with data collected using both structured and
unstructured questionnaires. The study was carried out in
September 2024 in the sub districts of Sokaraja and Ke mbaran,
Banyumas Regency, purposively sampled based on  FMD
incidence.

Sampling and respondents

A total of 29 beef cattle farmers  impacted by the FMD cases  were
selected through snowball sampling. Considering that there is a
need for identifying those affected by the outbreaks in these
subdistricts where farmers work informally and cannot be identified
from a central database, snowball sampling technique was found to
be the most suitable one.  Although the selected sample size is not
large enough to make  statistical generalizations about the results
obtained, it fits into the exploratory nature of this study, which
focuses more on the depth rather than breadth of investigation.
There is an element of recall bias in the last two years passed from
the outbreak period (June to September 2022) to the time of
conducting the survey (September 2024).
A total of 29 beef cattle farmers affected during the 2022 FMD

Data collection

Data was collected by questio nnaires, structured and semi -
structured interviews, field observation, and documentation. This
includes record of FMD cases, number of infected cattle, incubation
period, mitigation efforts, vaccination, quarantine, and farmers’
characteristics. The secondary data was obtained from relevant
institutions, including the a griculture office. The interview
instruments were designed around four steps of the risk
management process; questionnaires on outbreak detection and
symptom recognition risk identification ; questionnaires on illness,
duration, severity, and herd impact, questionnaires that addressed
risk evaluation; while questionnaires on treatment, vaccinations,
quarantining, and movement control addressed risk mitigation.

Data analysis

The data collected was subjected to descriptive statistics an alysis
within the conceptual risk management framework.  First, data was
extracted from the interview responses, observations, and
documentation, then reduced and grouped based on the
respondent categories and the research themes related to the FMD
mitigation. Then, all data were coded into categories and
subcategories based on four stages of risk management ; risk
identification, risk appraisal, risk mitigation, and risk monitoring.
Third, the data were subjected to descriptive analysis, counting the
frequency and percentage of each response to obtain the emerging
patterns of the farming condition, cattle trading activities, and the
respondents’ adaptive strategies in facing the FMD outbreak.
Fourth, the thema tic analysis was conducted to interpret the
meaning of each category and identify the themes emerging from
the findings in terms of beef cattle agribusiness. Next, data
triangulations to compare the results from the interviews and
observations was performed with those from the secondary data,
namely information from relevant institutions and their reports and
the government regulations. Lastly, t he findings were cross -
checked with the current literature on FMD control, in
agribusinesses risk management fram ework, and the small -scale
farming system. The approach enhance d the validity and reliability
of the descriptive analysis.

Risk evaluation: Disease duration, herd impact, and
economic consequences

Here, presents the results of the risk evaluation analysis,
focusing on disease duration, the impact of FMD on herd-
level health, and the associated economic c onsequences
within the study area . As shown  in Tab le 4, majority of
farmers (76%) reported that their cattle were sick for 15
to 21 days, while the others respondent indicated a
duration of 22  to 28 days. This indicated duration is
consistent with the characteristics of FMD which is
typically acute infection as its proliferation is influenced
by physiological condition, immune status and host
responses (Li et al., 2021). On the other hand, based on
the risk evaluation perspectives, 10 to 28 days after
infection is when biological risks develop into financial
risks, because the animals showed more clinical
symptoms and declining productivity, leading to
increased treatment costs and disrupted sale of beef
cattle (Bovine, 2014) . Production declined, while input
such as f eed and vaccination costs increase without
corresponding to output gains.
These lead to financial strain on the producer and
missed opportunities to participate in the trade (Karyani
et al., 2020). All the respondents (100%) in the current
study indicated different recovery times between cattle
receiving clinical treatment and traditional medicine, with
the clinical treatment perceived to result in faster
recovery. Despite this perception, farmers continued
administering traditional treatment alongside pre ventative
measures. The practice may be attributed to  experience
gained over the period of farming, furthermore, isolating,
monitoring, feed additives, treatment, and biosecurity
reduce the risks of transmission (Moyo et al., 2014).
Based on the durations of FMD in the farms, 55%
indicated that the outbreak lasted for 1  to 30 days, 31%
reported prolonged outbreak of 31  to 60 days, and 14%
experience the duration that extend s beyond 60+ days.
The extended farm -level duration suggests the failure on
the containment measures which proliferate transmission
and prolonging economic exposure and increasing the
probability of inter-farm spillover.

Risk mitigation: Trea tment, vaccination, quarantine,
and movement control

Risk mitigation refers to strategies put in place in order to
lower the possibility or consequences of recognized risk
(Hanthorn and Sanderson, 2025). Various risk mitigation
measures implemented in the study area such as
treatment, vaccination, quarantine and movement control
as preventive and corrective efforts that have been
undertaken both by farmers and organizations  were
analyzed here.
The results on the use of traditional medication to tr eat
FMD is shown in Table 7. The respondents (100%) in the
current study show that all farmers (100%) in Banyumas
Regency have used tradition medicines (herbs) for
treatment FMD. Universal use of herbal medicine as the
initial treatment choice is an important discovery which
shows the cultural integration of herbal medicine practice
and difficulties of accessing veterinarians in rural areas in
Indonesia (Herdiana et al., 2021) . In the current study
farmers have indicated that they have used mixtures of
tradition herbs as treatment for FMD. 34% of the
respondent reported to have used the combination of
turmeric, curcuma, coconut palm sugar, and salt 34%
used turmeric, curcuma, young coconut water, egg, and
salt, while 3 2% mixture turmeric, coconut palm sugar,
salt, and young coconut as treatment (Hanthorn and
Sanderson, 2025) . This innovative method may be
attributed to farmers’ capacity to effectively utilize the
available local resources. Traditional herbs remedies are
reported to contain anti -inflammatories and
immunostimulants properties, as well as electrolytes, and
minerals, which are perceived to boost stamina recovery,
appetite and reduce symptom severity ( Benzie and

movement across regions, to install disinfectant spots
along transportation routes, and to implement cross -

vigilance among farmers and actors in cattle trade
(Mabunda et al., 2026) . Similar approaches were
implemented by other Europea n countries through a
rapid campaign, monitoring of infected areas, and regular
updates of information to encourage farmers’ compliance
to disease prevention policies (Bradhurst et al., 2022) .

cattle restriction but also the government capacity in

however, it results in inconsequential where

compliance, livestock mobility in markets, a nd the

Movement control requires clear policy formulation while
coordination among animal health agencies, market
managers, and farmers’ representatives, an aspect that
appears to have been insufficient in the case of
Banyumas (McLaws et al., 2023).

Adaptive capacity: Health monitoring, institutional
support, and systemic learning

This se gment presents the final components of the risk
management analysis model, focusing on adaptive
capacity in Banyumas district. It examines health
monitoring, institution support and systematic learning as
key dimensions of the system ability to respond to
diseased risk, it analyse how agribusiness incorporates
lessons from previous outbreaks, to adapt management
strategies, and prepare for future threats. The learning
capacity of the agribusiness system through health
monitoring practices and instit utional interactions is also
expored. The results in Table 11 indicate that 59% of the
respondents conducted a daily check, 34% twice a week,
and 7% once per week.

The frequency of livestock health checks during the
outbreak of FMD varied from daily to weekly, likely
reflecting differences in farmers’ awareness and capacity
to detect early symptoms (Garner et al., 2021). All
farmers (100%) reported receiving monitoring support
from the animal husbandry service, highlighting outbreak
and providing guida nce in mitigating the effects of
disease, considering that farmers  have limited resources
to respond to outbreak.
The support strengthens the farmers’ ability to monitor
the condition of livestock health and expedites case
management at the farm level (Richeson et al., 2018). In
terms of adaptive capacity, the perception is that the
universal coverage of institutional surveillance is
recognized in this case study. In contrast, the information
campaign on vaccination and biosecurity measures
including movement restricting did not reach all farmers.
Institutional surveillance appears to have achieved
universal coverage. This suggests that, although the
institution framework can provide broader coverage, the
limitation exists as used selectively. Following the
outbreak, 72% of farmers reported increasing the
frequency of checkups, while 28% reported no change in
frequency.
This adaptive behavior may reflect adaptive learning as
farmers responded to the incursion by intensifying
surveillance efforts regardles s of prior risk management
training. Nevertheless, the flexibility in adaptive capability
on an individual farm level does not suggest
organizational resilience. A key consideration is whether
this practice is sustainable post the outbreak
(Delabouglise et al., 2020).

Toward an integrated risk management framework
for smallholder beef cattle agribusiness

It is evident from the analysis that there are some
recurring weaknesses throughout all four key points  of
the risk management cycle. The main problem is that
despite farmers having high adaptability to changes, their
capacity is poorly structured and isolated from the system
of institutions which could enhance their abilities. The
analysis indicate s the recurring weaknesses of  all four
phases of risk management. Although farmers indicated
strengths on adaptability, the capacity is weakly
structured and isolated from institutional system which
limits its effectiveness. During risk identification, farmers
were able to identify the symptoms, and it can be
attributed to experience. However, absence of early
warning system limited the ability to response promptly,
and that might have contributed to spread diseases within
and between farms.
During the risk evaluation phase, the finding indicated
that disease lasted for between 15 and 28 days and post-
outbreak transmission implies  that  biological  risks  were

In respect to risk reduction, the lack of vaccinations

of structured risk management systems within the
business environment. Improving risk management in
such scenarios necessitates moving from prescriptive
approaches to integration  by utilizing farmers’  adaptive
potentials and strengthening sustainable support and
preventive actions by related institutions.

Conclusion

The study analyzed the risk management capabilities of
the beef cattle agribusiness of the  smallholder sec tor in
Banyumas Regency during the outbreak of FMD in 2022
using a four-step analysis consisting of risk identification,
risk assessment, risk reduction, and adaptive capability.
Although farmers have experience in identifying
symptoms of the disease and have indicated adaptive
learning, lack of integration of risk management system
remains a limiting factor in response to outbreak of FMD.
The study identified; delayed detection, lack of
isolation, lack of pre -vaccination inadequate quarantine
measures, and  poor communication on movement
restriction are consider among the factors that  may  have

stakeholders could have benefitted from weak or

From the policies perspective, improving risk

requires a representative sample. Second, the two -year
period between the outbreaks and the data collection
process may have introduced recall problems among
respondents. Therefore, future studies are encouraged to
use probability sampling methods and investigate
changes in behavior over a long period of time.
