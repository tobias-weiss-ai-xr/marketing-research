ABSTRACT:
Green consumption is essential to achieving sustainable development, as it helps reduce carbon
emissions and supports global environmental governance. Social media has emerged as a
key instrument in shaping consumer attitudes and actions. Nevertheless, limited research  has
systematically mapped the knowledge structure, research hotspots, and evolutionary patterns of social
media's influence on green consumption behaviour. To fill this gap, this study provides a bibliometric
review of 334 publications (2016–2025) to map the evolving research on social media’s role in green
consumption. The field shows consistent growth, with themes shifting from early work on consumer
attitudes and purchase intentions toward more focused studies of social media mechanisms, gr een
marketing, and policy drivers. Building on these trends, the review identifies several meaningful paths
for further research. These include employing clearer theoretical  frameworks, conducting cross -
cultural comparisons, and examining the influence of AI-based tools on consumer behaviour. By
outlining the intellectual structure, collaborative networks, and emerging directions, this analysis offers
a consolidated foundation for both researchers and practitioners interested in advancing sustainable
consumption.

Keywords: green consumption behaviour, social media influence, bibliometrics, CiteSpace, Web of Science

1. Introduction

Global warming, melting glaciers, and rising sea levels are just a few of the
environmental problems that have been exacerbated by the rapid expansion of the world
economy. The environment and human society are seriously threatened by these problems
(C. Wang, 2020; Huang et al., 2021; P. Wang et al., 2023) . To deal with these pressing
issues, the concept of sustainable development has become extremely important. Within
this, green consumption has emerged as a key way for people to help protect the
environment through their daily choices. Buying and using pr oducts in a way that meets
personal requirements while also reducing environmental damage is known as “green
consumption,” which is driven by a feeling of responsibility to society and the environment
(Duong, 2022). Encouraging green consumption is therefore seen as a vital strategy to
lower carbon emissions and support global efforts to improve the environment (Wei et
al., 2024).
As consumption patterns turn greener, social media has reshaped the market and
transformed consumers’ attitudes and psychology (Xie & Madni, 2023). There are several

This study aims to better comprehend the research evolutions and hotspots by

studies. Among existing papers, only a few studies have examined the impact of social
media on green consumption behaviour. Xiao et al. (2023)  have discussed how social

increase in study interest in online platforms in the digital context. However, the evolution
of the study themes is not thoroughly analysed.  While Munaro et al. (2024) examined 52
articles from the perspectives of theories, methods and contexts, the study did not adopt
a more holistic view to investigate the linkages within the literature, such as collaborative
networks among institutions or authors. Therefore, prior studies lacked: (1) examining the
field’s dynamic evolution from a broader viewpoint; (2) grouping the most popular
research subjects in this area; and (3) comprehending the field’s evolution process and
trends from a broad perspective.
A quantitative and statistical method that examines research topics and lessens
subjectivity and prejudice is bibliometric analysis (Donthu et al., 2021) . It can rapidly
explain the features of literature, evaluate and comprehend the process of development,
and identify research hotspots across many fields (Luo et al., 2021). To guide and inspire
interested researchers and practitioners, to map the present and future trends in the
importance of social media in green consumption behaviour research, this study uses a
bibliometric analysis with CiteSpace. Our study will answer the following two questions:
• Q1: What are the main trends and thematic shifts in research on the role of social
media in green consumption from 2016 to 2025?
• Q2: What principal challenges has social media introduced in green consumption?
This study makes the following contributions by providing answers to these
questions. Firstly, it maps the key topics and shows how they have changed over time.
Secondly, it highlights knowledge gaps and future study areas. Thirdly, it tracks the
evolution of research concepts and predicts future developments in the area.  Lastly, it
offers practical insights to help policymakers, businesses, and social media platforms better
promote sustainable consumption.

2. Methods and data

2.1 Methods and software
Bibliometric analysis is a powerful method to systematically map how a research
field develops over time (Liu et al., 2022) . By using data analysis and visualisation tools,
this method can show the structure of knowledge in a field, pinpoint main research themes
and key authors, and highlight emerging topics (X. Chen & Liu, 2020; Carve et al., 2021).
Bibliometric analysis typically employs techniques like co -authorship, co-word, and co -
citation analysis (Donthu et al., 2021; Passas, 2024). CiteSpace focuses on identifying key

2.2 Data and process
The Web of Science (WoS) database is widely used in bibliometric research. Its
extensive coverage across disciplines makes it a primary source for such analyses (Zhu &
Liu, 2020) . For this study, data were retrieved from the WoS Core Collection. The
bibliometric search strategy can be described as follows: Keywords = (“social media” or
“social media influence”) and (“green consumption” or “green consumption behaviour”),
time span = 2016 –2025, document types = article or review article, and language =
English. The data were collected on August 4, 2025. Following the initial search, the
retrieved documents were screened for relevance. The final dataset comprised 334 articles
(319 research articles and 15 review articles).
Subsequently, CiteSpace was used to generate knowledge network maps from the
collected data. Main analyses are as follows: (1) Productivity and impact were assessed at
the level of countries, authors, and institutions using metrics such as publication count,
citation counts, and  activity periods. (2) Co -authorship network analysis is used to map
collaboration structures among authors, institutions, and nations to identify important
contributions. (3) Co-word analysis is applied to detect major research themes, track their
temporal evolution, and identify emerging trends. (4) Co-citation analysis is performed to
uncover foundational knowledge groups and pivotal publications that form the intellectual
core of the field. The detailed procedures are in Figure 1.

core themes (Forcael et al., 2020; Lima et al., 2021). Furthermore, since keywords evolve,

Accordingly, this paper employs analyses of keyword clustering, evolution, co-occurrence,
and burst detection.

“attitude” also became a hot topic. The “consumption” node size shows that it is more

purchase behaviour. For example, Joshi & Rahman (2016) discovered that among young,

culture influences consumers’ inclination to make environmentally friendly choices

and “media” rose in 2019, indicating a connection between social media and green

4.2.4 Analysis of keyword burst
Keyword burst detection identifies terms that experience a sharp, time -bound
increase in frequency. This method reveals emerging trends and research fronts by
analysing the intensity and duration of these bursts. This study focused on the top 25 terms
with the highest burst intensity, as shown in Figure 10.
The burst periods of keywords reveal clear shifts in research priorities across three
phases. In the early stage (2017 –2021), studies focused on basic concepts and efficacy
validation, with keywords such as “performance”, “efficacy,” and “perceived consume r
effectiveness” indicating a primary interest in whether and why social media interventions
were effective. During the transitional phase (2022–2023), attention turned toward specific
contexts and decision mechanisms, reflected in bursts of terms like “food consumption”,
“information”, “intention,” and “willingness to pay.” Since 2023, the field has entered a
stage of deeper theoretical integration. A cluster of keywords with high burst strength has
emerged simultaneously, including  “social media”,  “purchase
intention”, “trust”, “environmental concern,” and  “antecedents”. This sug gests research
no longer treats social media as a mere tool but deeply investigates its interaction with
complex factors like consumer trust, environmental attitudes, and brand communication,
aiming to build more comprehensive theoretical models.
Importantly, the keyword “environmental attitudes” first appeared in 2016 but
experienced its strongest citation burst from 2022 to 2023, indicating it as a fundamental
yet persistently relevant concept that recently gained heightened scholarly focus. Regarding
sustained attention, the keyword “industry” demonstrates the longest continuous burst
period, spanning from 2022 to 2025. This suggests that the role of industrial and
commercial actors is a mid-to-long-term subject of consistent investigation. Furthermore,
the concurrent burst of “PLS -SEM” further indicates methodological maturation, with
researchers increasingly adopting structural equation modelling to examine mediated and

cluster homogeneity and reliability; a Q -value > 0.5 is generally considered to denote a
reliable clustering structure (H. Sun et al., 2022).

subsequently assigned as cluster labels. Figure 12 presents the resulting co -citation

5. Discussion

In the field of the role of social media in green consumption behaviour, there have
been many changes and new research directions. This study identifies several key
challenges that social media poses in promoting green consumption.
1. Theoretical Integration and Cross -Cultural Validation. The keyword analysis
reveals a clear trend: research interest has shifted from general concepts like
“performance” to focused clusters on social media, purchase intention, and environmental

concern (with the strongest bursts from 2023 –2025). To better understand these
connections, future studies should use clearer and combined theories. For example, future
research could examine how social media shapes consumer attitudes toward green
products. Also, most current studies are done in only one country (Kwon & Ahn, 2021;
Sreen et al., 2021; Ling et al., 2024). It is recommended to compare results from different
cultures (like the US, Europe, and Asia) to see if the same findings apply everywhere.
2. The Impact of AI and Green Industry Transformation. The strong and ongoing
appearance of words like “industry” and “technology” shows that  new technology and
greener industries  are becoming key topics. Although some researchers have already
focused on green consumption and AI (K. Wang et al., 2023; Dong et al., 2025; Yang et
al., 2025). Future research should deeper inquiry. For example, how does  an AI product
recommendation (like on shopping websites) affect green purchases? Can  AI tools help
people understand their environmental impact? How do  virtual try -on or smart
labels power by AI change trust in green brands? At the same time, future research should
study how companies are becoming more eco -friendly and how this interacts with new
technologies like AI. This will help businesses and governments use  AI and green
policies together to encourage sustainable shopping.
3. Evolving Platform Dynamics and Content Formats. Our analysis reveals that
“Facebook” received attention as an early burst term (2018 -2021). However, the current
social media landscape has undergone significant changes. Short -video platforms
represented by TikTok have become mainstream, and content formats have shifted from
text-and-images to short videos (Ma & Hu, 2021). This transformation has been confirmed
to influence green consumption communication and behaviour (Lee et al., 2024; Zhong et
al., 2025). Future studies could deeply compare the differential effects of various platform
types (e.g., text -and-image social media, short -video social media) and content formats
(text-and-image, short video, live streaming) in disseminating green information, sha ping
environmental attitudes, and influencing purchase intentions. For example, how does the
immersive and emotional storytelling of short videos more effectively enhance consumers’
environmental resonance and perceived effectiveness? How do the strong int eractivity
(e.g., comments, reposts) and algorithmic recommendation mechanisms of short -video
platforms accelerate the community diffusion and identity construction of green lifestyles?
Are there differences in information credibility, communication depth,  and behavioural
conversion between the static text -and-image content of the Facebook era and the
dynamic short videos of the TikTok era?

6. Conclusions

This research, which is based on CiteSpace, carefully examines 334 articles on how
social media influence green consumption behaviour from 2016 to 2025 using
bibliometrics and knowledge mapping approaches. The results demonstrate the growing
interest in this topic and the clear pattern of knowledge evolution. The study found that
the relevant articles have increased significantly since 2022, indicating that this field is
becoming an increasingly active focus in sustainable consumption research. From the
perspective of research countries, China, the USA and India are the core countries in terms
of output of achievements. While China leads in publication output, the scale and intensity

are the absolute main body of research, while the participation of enterprises and
professional research institutions is relatively insufficient. In terms of resear ch topics,
“behaviour”, “consumption”, “planned behaviour theory,” and “attitude and sustainable
consumption” constitute a stable and core knowledge cluster, among which the discussion
of young consumers and the effect of digital platforms is particularly high. Notably, the
focus of research in this field presents a clear evolutionary path: from early reliance on
basic theories and a focus on general behaviour patterns, to a gradual shift towards more
contextual and platform -based discussions. Future resear ch could enhance cutting -edge
topics such as influencer marketing, AI recommendation mechanisms, and cross -cultural

universality of the conclusions by fusing multi -source data and combining qualitative
methods. Overall, this study methodically organises the field’s knowledge structure,
cooperation network, and development trend. It also offers a clear point of reference and
roadmap for future research, policy development, and corporate practice on how to
successfully encourage green consumption through social media.
