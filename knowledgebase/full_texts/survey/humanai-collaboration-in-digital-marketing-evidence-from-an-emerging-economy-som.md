Abstract: Artificial intelligence (AI) is reshaping digital marketing through automation,
customer analytics, personalization, and data-driven decision-making. However, empiri-
cal evidence on human–AI collaboration in emerging and fragile economies remains lim-
ited. This study examined the adoption of human–AI collaboration in digital marketing
among businesses in Somalia, identified key barriers to AI adoption, and assessed the
association between human–AI collaboration and customer engagement, marketing effi-
ciency, and business growth. The study used a positivist research approach and a quanti-
tative cross-sectional survey design. Data were collected from 86 business owners, mar-
keting managers, digital marketing specialists, and ICT/technology officers using purpos-
ive sampling. Data were analyzed using descriptive statistics, Spearman's rank correla-
tion, and multiple linear regression. Respondents reported frequent use of AI -assisted
content creation, customer analytics, marketing automation, social media management,
and AI-supported decision-making. The principal barriers were limited technical skills,
limited infrastructure, implementation costs, data privacy concerns, and organizational
resistance to change. Spearman's correlations indicated positive associations bet ween
human–AI collaboration and customer engagement (ρ = .684), marketing efficiency (ρ
=.725), and business growth (ρ = .658), all reported at p < .001. Regression models ex-
plained 50.7% of the variance in customer engagement, 54.9% in marketing efficienc y,
and 46.4% in business growth. Human–AI collaboration was positively associated with
each outcome after controlling for organizational size and industry sector. Because the
study is cross-sectional and based on a purposive sample, the findings should be inter-
preted as associations rather than causal effects. The results highlight the importance of
combining AI capabilities with human judgment, creativity, contextual knowledge, and
responsible data practices in Somalia's digital marketing environment.

1. INTRODUCTION
Digital technologies have transformed marketing by making it more data-driven, automated, and per-
sonalized. Artificial intelligence (AI) supports customer segmentation, predictive analytics, conversational
technologies, content creation, recommendation systems, and campaign optimization. Research in market-
ing suggests that AI can augment human decision-making by handling repetitive tasks and processing
large volumes of information, while human marketers contribute contextual judgment, creativity, ethical
reasoning, and strategic direction (Davenport et al., 2020; Huang & Rust, 2021; Petrescu & Krish en,
2023).

Digital marketing is particularly relevant in emerging economies because increased internet access,
smartphone use, social media adoption, and digital financial services create new channels for customer
interaction and business development. Somalia has experienced growth in mobile connectivity and digital
financial services, creating opportunities for businesses to engage customers through digital channels
(World Bank, 2023). These developments provide a context in which AI-enabled marketing tools may be
useful, but adoption may also be constrained by infrastructure, skills, cost, privacy, and governance chal-
lenges.
Human–AI collaboration refers in this study to the use of AI capabilities together with human expertise
in marketing activities. The collaboration perspective is important because AI a nd human intelligence
have different strengths. AI can support large-scale data processing, automation, and analytical tasks,
whereas human actors remain important for contextual interpretation, creativity, relationship management,
and ethical judgment (Huang & Rust, 2022; Petrescu & Krishen, 2023).
Evidence from developing and emerging markets indicates that AI adoption is uneven and shaped by
organizational capabilities, infrastructure, employee skills, financial resources, and concerns about data
privacy and responsible use. These issues may be especially relevant in fragile digital ecosystems, where
technological development can move faster than institutional capacity and regulatory arrangements.
Despite growing international interest in AI-enabled marketing, empirical evidence focused specifically
on human–AI collaboration in Somalia remains limited. This study therefore investigates AI-supported
marketing practices among businesses operating in Somalia, identifies barriers to adoption, and examines
the statistical associations between human–AI collaboration and three organizational outcomes: customer
engagement, marketing efficiency, and business growth.
2. RESEARCH OBJECTIVES
To examine the level of adoption of human–AI collaboration in digital marketing activities among
businesses in Somalia.
To identify the key challenges affecting the adoption of AI technologies in digital marketing among
businesses in Somalia.
To assess the association between human–AI collaboration and customer engagement, marketing effi-
ciency, and business growth in Somalia.
3. METHODS
3.1. Research Approach and Design
The study adopted a positivist research approach and a quantitative cross-sectional survey design. The
approach was selected because the study sought to measure reported AI adoption practices, perceived bar-
riers, and organizational outcomes using structured quantitative data. The cross-sectional design captured
respondents' perceptions and reported practices at a single point in time. Accordingly, the study examines
statistical associations rather than causal effects.
3.2. Study Population
The target population comprised business owners, marketing managers, digital marketing specialists,
and ICT/technology personnel involved in digital marketing activities within businesses operating in So-
malia. Participants were considered appropriate because their roles provide direct knowledge of digital
marketing practices and the use of AI-related tools. The study focused on businesses using digital channels
such as social media, websites, e-commerce platforms, and mobile applications.
3.3. Sample and Sampling Technique
A total of 86 respondents were selected using purposive sampling. Participants were selected because
of their involvement in digital marketing and familiarity with AI-related technologies. The sample com-
prised business owners, marketing managers, digital marketing specialists, and ICT/technology officers.

3.4. Data Collection Instrument
Data were collected using a structured questionnaire consisting primarily of closed-ended items meas-
ured on a five-point Likert scale: 1 = Strongly Disagree, 2 = Disagree, 3 = Neutral, 4 = Agree, and 5 =
Strongly Agree. The questionnaire contained four sections:
Section A: demographic and organizational information, including gender, age, organizational role, rel-
evant work experience, type of business, organizational size, and industry sector.
Section B: human–AI collaboration adoption, covering AI-assisted content creation, customer analytics,
marketing automation, social media management, and AI-supported decision-making.
Section C: barriers to AI adoption, including technical skills, implementation costs, data privacy, tech-
nological infrastructure, and organizational resistance to change.
Section D: perceived organizational outcomes associated with human–AI collaboration, focusing on
customer engagement, marketing efficiency, and business growth.
3.5. Measurement of Constructs
The study used multi-item constructs to measure human–AI collaboration and the three organizational
outcomes. Items were informed by the literature on AI in marketing and human–AI collaboration. The
measurement framework was aligned with the variables subsequently analyzed in the results.

3.6. Data Collection Procedure
Questionnaires were distributed electronically through email, WhatsApp, and web-based survey plat-
forms. Participants were informed of the purpose of the study, the voluntary nature of participation, and
the confidentiality of their responses. Completed questionnaires were collected electronically and prepared
for statistical analysis.
3.7. Data Analysis
Data were analyzed using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS). Descriptive statistics
included frequencies, percentages, means, and standard deviations. Spearman's rank-order correlation was
used to examine bivariate associations because the questionnaire responses were measured using Likert-
type scales. Multiple linear regression was used to examine the predictive association between human–AI
collaboration and each outcome while controlling for organizational size and industry sector. The statisti-
cal results are reported using coefficients, R², adjusted R², F-statistics, t-values, p-values, and confidence
intervals where available.
3.8. Validity and Reliability
Content validity was addressed by developing questionnaire items from the literature on AI and digital
marketing. Internal consistency reliability was assessed using Cronbach's alpha. The reported coefficients
were above the commonly used 0.70 threshold.

3.9. Ethical Considerations and Common-Method Procedures
Participation was voluntary, informed consent was obtained, confidentiality and anonymity were re-

The manuscript also reports a Harman single-factor test, with the first factor accounting for 38% of the
total variance. This is below the 50% heuristic used in the original manuscript. However, Harman's single-
factor test alone does not establish that common-method bias is absent; the result is therefore treated as an
indicator rather than definitive evidence that common-method bias is not present.

5. DISCUSSION
5.1. Interpretation of Findings
The results indicate that respondents in Somali businesses reported substantial use of AI-supported ac-
tivities in digital marketing, particularly content creation, customer analytics, social media management,
marketing automation, and decision support. This pattern is consistent with the broader marketing
literature, which describes AI as a tool for automation, analysis, personalization, and campaign support
(Davenport et al., 2020; Huang & Rust, 2021).
The findings also support the human–AI collaboration perspective. AI can perform high-volume analyt-
ical and repetitive tasks, while human marketers provide contextual interpretation, creativity, ethical
judgment, and strategic direction. This complementarity is central to collaborative AI frameworks in mar-
keting (Huang & Rust, 2022; Petrescu & Krishen, 2023).
The reported positive association between human–AI collaboration and customer engagement is con-
sistent with the role of AI in personalization, conversational interaction, and customer analytics. In prac-
tice, AI-supported responsiveness and analysis may help firms tailor digital interactions, although the pre-
sent study does not establish a causal mechanism.
The positive association with marketing efficiency is also consistent with the use of AI for automation,
data processing, targeting, and campaign support. The relatively high mean for marketing efficiency and
the regression result suggest that respondents perceived a relationship between collaborative AI use and
more efficient marketing operations.
The association with business growth should likewise be interpreted cautiously. AI-supported segmen-
tation, targeting, customer interaction, and automation may contribute to business performance, but
growth is influenced by many factors beyond marketing technology. Because the study used cross-
sectional self-reported data, the results show an empirical relationship rather than a demonstrated causal
effect.
The barriers identified by respondents—skills, infrastructure, implementation cost, privacy, and organi-
zational resistance—are particularly relevant to emerging and fragile digital environments. These co n-
straints suggest that AI adoption is not only a question of access to software; it also depends on digital ca-
pabilities, organizational readiness, reliable infrastructure, and responsible data practices.
5.2. Theoretical and Practical Implications
• The study adds empirical evidence from Somalia to the growing literature on human–AI collaboration
in marketing, a literature that has largely developed in more digitally mature settings.
• The findings suggest that human–AI collaboration should be considered as an augmentation approach
rather than an assumption that AI automatically replaces marketing professionals.
• For businesses, investment in staff AI literacy, data practices, and digital infrastructure should accom-
pany investment in AI tools.
• For policymakers and higher-education institutions, skills development and responsible AI practices
are relevant to strengthening the digital business ecosystem.

5.3. Limitations
• The sample consisted of 86 purposively selected respondents, so the findings should not be general-
ized to all businesses in Somalia without further evidence.
• The cross-sectional design does not establish temporal order or causality. The terms 'effect' and 'im-
pact' have therefore been replaced throughout this revised manuscript with 'association' where appro-
priate.
• The data were self-reported and collected through a common questionnaire, which may introduce
common-method and response biases. The reported Harman single-factor result should not be treated
as a definitive test of the absence of common-method bias.
• The analysis controls for organizational size and industry sector, but the submitted manuscript does
not provide detailed sampling distributions for these variables. Their coding and descriptive statistics
should be retained in the final dataset and reported if required by the journal.
CONCLUSION
This study examined human–AI collaboration in digital marketing among businesses in Somalia. Re-
spondents reported frequent use of AI for content creation, customer analytics, marketing automation, so-
cial media management, and decision support. The principal barriers were technical skills, infrastructure,
implementation costs, data privacy, and organizational resistance to change. Human–AI collaboration was
positively associated with customer engagement, marketing efficiency, and business growth in the report-
ed correlation and regression analyses. These findings support the relevance of combining AI capabilities
with human judgment and contextual expertise in emerging digital markets. However, the cross-sectional
design and purposive sample mean that the results should be interpreted as associations rather than causal
effects.
7. RECOMMENDATIONS
• Invest in practical AI and digital marketing skills for business owners, marketing professionals, and ICT
personnel.
• Improve access to reliable and affordable digital infrastructure and AI-enabled tools, particularly for
small and medium-sized businesses.
• Use AI to augment human judgment, creativity, and strategic decision-making rather than treating au-
tomation as a substitute for human oversight.
• Develop internal policies covering data privacy, transparency, human oversight, and responsible AI use
in marketing.
• Strengthen collaboration among government, universities, technology providers, and businesses to sup-
port AI skills development and responsible digital innovation.
SUGGESTIONS FOR FUTURE RESEARCH
• Use larger probability-based samples to improve representativeness and generalizability.
• Conduct comparative studies across Somalia and other African emerging markets.
• Examine organizational culture, employee AI readiness, trust in AI, customer perceptions, and respon-
sible-AI practices as additional explanatory variables.
• Where feasible, combine survey data with objective marketing indicators such as conversion rates, cus-
tomer retention, campaign costs, or engagement metrics.
