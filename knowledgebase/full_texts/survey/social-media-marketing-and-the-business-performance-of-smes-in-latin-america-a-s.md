Abstract
Introduction
Despite the growing body of research on social media marketing and
SMEs, a considerable gap remains in the integrated understanding of
the state of the art regarding the effect of social media marketing on
SMEs’ business performance.
Objective
This study aimed to synthesise the evidence on the association
between social media use and the business performance of SMEs in
Latin America by identifying patterns in scientific production and
collaboration, as well as the thematic and semantic evolution of the
field. It also sought to identify capabilities, innovation, entrepreneurial
orientation, gender, firm size and geographical context as explanatory
factors.
Methods
A quantitative approach was employed, combining scientometric and
bibliometric methods with a systematic literature review. Records
from Scopus, Web of Science, and Dimensions were analysed using a
structured search protocol, PICO criteria, and the PRISMA flow
diagram to select and synthesise the relevant studies.

The evidence converged into three main clusters. The first comprised

transformation, artificial intelligence, big data, data analytics, digital

Introduction
Over recent decades, the expansion of digital platforms has reshaped business communication models worldwide. Social
media have evolved from spaces for personal interaction into strategic channels through which firms of all sizes compete
for visibility, build brands and generate transactions. This shift is particularly evident in emerging markets, where mobile
penetration has broadened access to tools that were previously available mainly to large corporations with substantial
advertising budgets. In this context, social media marketing has gained both academic and managerial relevance, not
merely as a communication practice but as a factor with documented effects on measurable business outcomes. 1,2
Recent studies indicate that platforms such as Facebook, Instagram and TikTok enable organisations to disseminate
content rapidly, build brand awareness and segment audiences with precision, while also presenting challenges related to
loss of narrative control and difficulties in quantifying return on investment. 3
Academic output on this topic has accelerated since 2015, reflecting both the widespread adoption of social media and the
growing need to understand its effects on business competitiveness. 4
Small and medium-sized enterprises occupy a particularly important position in this phenomenon. They are key
economic actors in most economies, generating employment, stimulating local markets and absorbing shocks such as
the 2020 pandemic, while operating with resource constraints that limit their access to costly traditional media. 5,6
Social media therefore offer SMEs an accessible competitive lever, characterised by low entry costs, audience
segmentation and direct contact with end consumers. However, the available evidence on whether this lever translates
into improved business performance remains fragmented, inconsistent across contexts and methodologically heteroge-
neous. Some studies report that social media marketing enhances market expansion, customer engagement and
competitiveness.5,7
Other studies caution that adoption alone does not guarantee results in the absence of complementary organisational
capabilities, such as entrepreneurial orientation, communication quality and the technological readiness of staff. 8,9 This
lack of consensus is not incidental; rather, it reflects the extent to which outcomes depend on sectoral context, the
country’s level of digital development and each firm ’s internal capacity to implement coherent strategies.
Despite these advances, several gaps justify a systematic review. First, most studies rely on samples from a single sector or
region, limiting the transferability of their conclusions to other contexts. 10–12 Second, business performance is not
measured using uniform standards, which fragments the available knowledge and makes robust patterns difficult to
identify.13,14 Third, the bibliometric analysis by Das et al. 15 of 98 articles published between 2010 and 2024 identified a
geographical concentration of knowledge in North America and Europe, with comparatively limited contributions from
emerging markets where SMEs account for a greater share of economic activity.
This situation highlights an issue that the literature has not yet resolved clearly. Despite the growing volume of research
on social media marketing and SMEs, a substantial gap remains in the integrated understanding of the state of the art
concerning the effect of social media marketing on SMEs ’ business performance. Existing studies tend to focus on
specific geographical settings, including Indonesia, Saudi Arabia and Pakistan, and often use restricted samples and
cross-sectional designs that limit the generalisability of their findings. 16,17
In addition, business performance is measured differently across studies: some use sales indicators, others rely on
engagement or customer-satisfaction metrics, and others consider only financial variables. This variation complicates
comparisons and hinders the development of a cumulative body of knowledge. 18,19
Accordingly, the main objective of this study was to synthesise the evidence on the association between social media and
the business performance of SMEs in Latin America. The specific objectives were to identify patterns in scientific
production and collaboration, examine the thematic and semantic evolution of the field, and assess capabilities,
innovation, entrepreneurial orientation, gender, firm size and geographical context as explanatory factors.
A bibliometric analysis was conducted to examine the evolution of the literature, the most influential authors and journals,
fields of knowledge, levels of collaboration and the semantic development of the phenomenon under study. The review
also systematised the mediators, moderators and contextual heterogeneities underlying the relationship between social
media marketing (SMM) and business performance.

Methodology
The study adopted a quantitative approach that combined scientometric and bibliometric methods to characterise the
scientific production under study with a systematic literature review (SLR) to examine and synthesise the available
evidence in accordance with the search protocol. The PICO strategy was used to structure the information need by
defining the procedures and operations required to retrieve the information necessary to address the research question.
Against this background, the research question was: Which mediators and moderators explain the relationship between
social media marketing and business performance among Latin American SMEs? The PICO framework was defined as
follows: P: Latin American micro, small and medium-sized enterprises; I: social media use combined with marketing/
innovation capabilities; C: use without identified mediators; O: business performance and competitiveness.
The study adopted a non-experimental, longitudinal design 20,21 and used scientometric and bibliometric methods to
analyse the scientific production associated with the research topic. 22,23
For the bibliometric analysis, the dataset generated by the initial search for terms related to the research question
comprised 7,638 Scopus records and 103 Web of Science results. Of these, 47 were duplicates, resulting in a total of 7,694
records for the bibliometric analysis.

region of Latin America and published up to and including 2026. Articles outside Latin America were excluded. This

Scientific production on social media marketing and the business performance of SMEs in Latin America can be divided
into three distinct periods: a phase of near invisibility until 2010, gradual growth between 2011 and 2017, and rapid
expansion from 2018 to 2025.
During the early period from 1997 to 2010, publications were sporadic: one article in 1997, another in 2003 and three in
2007. This pattern is unsurprising given that Facebook did not become publicly available until 2006 and Twitter was
launched in the same year. Latin American SMEs also operated in contexts where connectivity was still more of a
privilege than an everyday resource. During this period, the literature tended to treat digital marketing as a phenomenon
associated with large corporations in developed economies, leaving regional small businesses largely outside the
analytical focus.
The second period, from 2011 to 2017, shows genuine but gradual growth, from 7 publications in 2011 to 102 in 2017.
During this period, social platforms became established as commercial channels and SMEs began to enter the academic
agenda, although they remained relatively peripheral. The 2008 financial crisis had redirected a substantial share of
research resources towards macroeconomic issues such as financial regulation, employment recovery and fiscal stability,
while the digital marketing of small firms was not a major funding priority. Vasudevan et al. 27 document how this post-
crisis reorientation pushed business-management issues towards the margins of the international academic agenda for
several years.
From 2018 onwards, the figures in the analysed corpus describe an unprecedented growth curve: 335 publications in
2020, 504 in 2021, 749 in 2022, 1,069 in 2023, 1,779 in 2024 and 2,117 in 2025. Three factors help explain this
expansion. First, the COVID-19 pandemic forced millions of Latin American small businesses to shift to digital channels
almost overnight, turning social media marketing from an option into a necessity. Second, mobile penetration in Latin
America exceeded 70% of the population by around 2022, bringing digital platforms within reach of previously
disconnected markets. Third, the consolidation of Instagram, TikTok and WhatsApp Business as direct-sales tools
generated a substantial volume of observable business practices that the academic community could no longer overlook
(Figure 2).

The second cluster includes terms such as digital transformation, artificial intelligence, big data, data analytics, digital
technologies, information systems and decision making. This cluster represents the most recent strand of the literature, in
which digital transformation is no longer treated as an aspiration but as a process that firms, including SMEs, must
actively manage. The co-occurrence of behavioral research with data analytics and information management indicates
that scholars are increasingly combining quantitative methods with behavioural approaches to understand how firms
make decisions in digital environments. For SMEs, which have historically operated with limited resources for data
analysis, this methodological convergence raises questions about the practical feasibility of adopting these tools.
The third cluster groups concepts such as corporate social responsibility, sustainability, innovation, digitization, business
development and technology adoption, together with the notable presence of COVID-19. The recurrence of the pandemic
within this cluster is not incidental; it marks a turning point in how small firms understood digitalisation. The presence of
China as a recurring term probably reflects the large volume of Asian literature on SMEs and digitalisation that
contributes to the global corpus, although its direct relevance to the Latin American context is debatable.
Overall, the literature reaches broad agreement that the integration of social media and marketing capabilities is positively
associated with the business performance and competitiveness of Latin American SMEs, as online presence and the use of
digital tools create favourable conditions for improving business development, sales and operational efficiency. 30 The
systematic literature review identified three main contribution blocks concerning the role of digital platforms in
organisational competitiveness. These blocks underline the importance of strategically integrating technologies and
provide a basis for future research and the design of business strategies.
The first block, termed “Social media as catalysts for market reach and customer acquisition ”, shows that studies
generally agree that active use of networks such as Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp contributes to reaching new
markets, expanding the customer base and strengthening customer loyalty in service and commercial sectors. 31
The second block, “Strategic integration of marketing and innovation capabilities ”, shows that the contributions are
grounded in the Resource-Based View (RBV), whereby social media use and online presence should be aligned with
robust marketing management to strengthen product innovation and competitive differentiation. 32

Discussion
This study contributes bibliometric and systematic-review evidence on the association between social media marketing
and the business performance of Latin American SMEs. The combined analysis of 7,694 documents and the final
selection of twelve primary studies reveal a clear pattern whereby digital presence and the strategic use of social platforms
are positively associated with market expansion, customer loyalty and business competitiveness.30,35,37 The convergence
of evidence into three thematic blocks - social media as catalysts for market reach, the strategic integration of marketing
and innovation capabilities, and structural and territorial factors in digital maturity - provides complementary perspec-
tives on the phenomenon under study.
Nevertheless, the systematic-review findings also reveal considerable heterogeneity and therefore require cautious
interpretation. The literature does not converge on a single explanatory model of the relationship between social media
and performance; instead, the evidence is conditional on specific mediators and moderators. Studies by Vilema-Escudero
et al.43 and Venancio et al.44 show that social media adoption alone does not guarantee performance improvements unless
it is accompanied by technological readiness and high-quality organisational communication, whereas Gonzalez et al. 36
identify digital-skills training as the most influential factor in the development of SMEs in the region, regardless of the
country analysed.
The analysis also reveals several conceptual tensions that warrant critical consideration. The first concerns technological
accessibility and organisational capacity. Although social media reduce barriers to entry into digital marketing, their
effectiveness depends on complementary capabilities - including entrepreneurial orientation, marketing management and
digital human capital - that are not equally available to all SMEs. 42 A second tension arises between the promise of the
universality of digital platforms and evidence that their impact varies according to firm size and managerial gender.
Seclen et al. 41 likewise report a more pronounced effect among female-led management in creative industries,
challenging the assumption of homogeneous benefits and highlighting the need for differentiated models tailored to
organisational profiles.
The relationship between geographical context and technology adoption introduces another source of variation. García-
Vidal et al.33 show that within-country geography moderates the adoption of artificial intelligence in marketing, revealing
gaps between managers ’ strategic perceptions and their organisations ’ actual capacity for implementation. This
discrepancy between intention and installed capacity tempers the initial optimism surrounding the democratising
potential of social media for SMEs in the region.
The theoretical contribution of the systematic review is consistent with the Resource-Based View, which provides a
relevant framework for explaining why social media use is not sufficient in itself; its effect on competitiveness becomes
evident when it is combined with the marketing and innovation capabilities developed by the firm. 32,39 The keyword
co-occurrence analysis supports this interpretation: a first thematic cluster centred on consumer behaviour and commerce
coexists with a second cluster focused on digital transformation, artificial intelligence and data analytics. This pattern
suggests that the field is moving from a primarily communication-oriented understanding of the phenomenon towards
one centred on the organisational capabilities required to exploit digital technologies effectively.
An issue that is frequently overlooked in the reviewed literature is the risk of reverse causality. Firms with stronger prior
performance may plausibly have more resources to invest in digital marketing, introducing a risk of endogeneity into the
cross-sectional studies that predominate in the analysed corpus. 16,17 The bibliometric analysis also reveals an inherent
limitation: Lotka ’s Law indicates that 81.5% of authors contributed only one article to the collection. This highly
fragmented productivity pattern suggests that the field remains in a consolidation phase, with limited systematic
accumulation of knowledge around stable authors or research streams.
The reviewed evidence therefore supports a conditional model in which more effective social media use is simultaneously
linked to marketing and innovation capabilities, technological and communication readiness among staff, and explicit
consideration of structural conditions such as firm size, managerial gender and geographical context when designing
digital strategies.
A key limitation of this review is the possible omission of relevant studies not indexed in Scopus, Web of Science or
Dimensions. This issue is particularly important in Latin America, where a substantial share of academic output is
published in regional journals with lower international visibility. In addition, the strict application of the PRISMA
protocol resulted in only 12 documents meeting the PICO question and being retained for full-text analysis. Although
these studies demonstrated thematic relevance, the limited evidence base constrains the extent to which broader
conclusions can be drawn at the global level.

Future research should prioritise quantitative empirical studies and longitudinal designs that estimate the relative
contribution of mediators such as capabilities, innovation and entrepreneurial orientation to the relationship between
social media and business performance. Research should also extend geographical coverage to Latin American countries
that are currently under-represented in the corpus in order to strengthen the external validity of the findings and support
the design of policies promoting SME digitalisation across the region.
Conclusions
The bibliometric analysis and systematic literature review address an important research gap concerning social media
marketing and the business performance of SMEs in Latin America. Although the first publications in the corpus date
from 1997, interest increased substantially from 2018 onwards, with an annual growth rate of 23.65%. The most
prominent authors identified include Al-Hawary from Jordan, Chatterjee from India and Vrontis from Cyprus, who
examine the phenomenon from different perspectives within the global literature.
The evidence indicates that this research field is in a stage of consolidation rather than full maturity. Semantic analysis
based on keyword coupling identifies closely related thematic clusters involving consumer behaviour, digital transfor-
mation, artificial intelligence, data analytics and corporate social responsibility, highlighting the theoretical and
conceptual relationship between the strategic use of digital platforms and SMEs ’ business performance.
The systematic literature review reveals broad agreement and strong empirical support for the proposition that online
presence, understood as a low-entry-cost channel, can improve market expansion, customer loyalty and competitiveness
when it is combined with complementary organisational capabilities. Three main contribution blocks emerge regarding
the role of social media marketing in the business performance of Latin American SMEs. Together, these blocks
underscore the importance of strategically integrating digital technologies and provide a foundation for future research
and the design of business strategies.
The three main blocks identified are: (i) social media as catalysts for market reach and customer acquisition, whereby
intensive use of platforms such as Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp supports entry into new markets and customer
loyalty in service and commercial sectors; (ii) strategic integration of marketing and innovation capabilities, which
contextualises social media use through the Resource-Based View and indicates that online presence strengthens
competitiveness only when combined with marketing management oriented towards product innovation; and
(iii) structural and territorial factors in digital maturity, which emphasise the need for digitalisation strategies to consider
firm size, managerial gender and geographical context as key explanatory factors in local technology adoption.
Overall, the scientific evidence indicates that social media marketing has been used effectively by Latin American SMEs
within a multidimensional context in which firm size, managerial gender and geographical context influence the
development of digital maturity and the capacity to translate competitiveness into business growth and sustainability.
This systematic review provides a solid basis for future longitudinal research and for policies that promote digitalisation,
particularly by strengthening digital-skills training and improving the use of SME resources.
