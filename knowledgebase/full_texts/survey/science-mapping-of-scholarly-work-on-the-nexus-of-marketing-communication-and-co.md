Introduction
Food waste is a complex, challenging, and intricate issue with significant negative environmental,
social, and economic impacts on communities globally (FAO, 2016). According to the latest global
assessment – the Food Waste Index Report 2024 from the UN Environment Programme (UNEP,
2024)– 1.05 billion tons of food waste was produced in the world in 2022, which is equivalent to
about 132 kilograms per person and nearly one -fifth (19 per cent) of all food available to
consumers across the household, food service, and retail sectors. Notably, the largest share of this
waste comes from households – some 60 per cent, or 631 million tons, versus 28 per cent from
food service and 12 per cent from retail. This consideration in the household is a critical starting
point for the present review. Unlike upstream food loss in the suppl y chain, which is primarily
technical and logistical in nature, food waste at the consumption stage is substantially behavioral,
arising from decisions about purchasing, storing, preparing, and discarding food.

At the consumption stage, the habits of ind ividual consumers, families, and restaurants greatly
influence food waste creation; however, this domain is still quite underexplored in cont rast to
supply-chain viewpoints (Carvalho et al., 2025; Vittuari et al., 2023). Tackling food waste at the
consumer level necessitates grasping the psychological and behavioral factors that inspire waste -
reducing behaviors, as well as pinpointing successful communication methods that can
consistently impact buying, cooking, and eating habits (Moreno et al., 2020). The convergence of
consumer habits, sustainability, and marketing communication has become a vital field for
research, policy formulation, and the design of practica l interventions (Aschemann-Witzel et al.,
2016; Calvo-Porral et al., 2017; Olavarria-Key et al., 2021; Reynolds et al., 2019). This is exactly
the behavioral locus that makes marketing communication a potentially influential lever for change
(Mooij, 2010; White et al., 2019).

The literature on consumer food waste consists of multiple, distinct, but interrelated streams of
communication research, each of which has its own theoretical extraction, methodological
conventions, and unresolved strains. Recent empirical research has shown that a highly promising
approach to tackling consumer-level food waste is to improve the effectiveness of messages and
communication strategies used to encourage food waste reduction behaviors (Zulu & Muposhi,
2025). Nonetheless, the research environment shows considerable diversity in the design and
results of marketing communication initiatives aimed at reducing food waste (Olavarria-Key et al.,
2021). Previous research (Vittuari et al., 2023 ; Wang et al., 2022 ; Zulu & Muposhi, 2025 )
highlighted the impact of food waste reduction message framing is affected by various
interconnected factors, such as variations in consumers' construal levels, features of message
design, incentives for behavior change, and the context of consumption (household, restaurant,
buffet, or other environments).  A key research finding indicates that consumers are more receptive
to specific, practical actions when paired with persuasive proof of the economic and ecological
advantages of alterin g their behavior (Abos et al., 2024 ). In contrast, the findings from recent
literature hold that some communication strategies aimed at decreasing food waste might  lead to
unexpected effects. Messages that assign blame to consumers for food waste have been shown to
backfire, leading to resistance against participating in food waste reduction efforts instead of
encouraging the adoption of waste -reducing practices (Zulu & Muposhi, 2025 ). This difference

highlights a significant limitation in theoretical understanding. There is no consensus on the most
effective way to integrate diverse message frames in a single intervention, and there is no
systematic evidence on the effectiveness of different communication approaches for different
consumer populations and cultural contexts.

While distinct empirical studies provide useful insights, a comprehensive framework that
emphasizes the intellectual structure behind research on consumer food waste and marketing
communication is missing. In particular, there is still a lack of clarit y regarding: (1) which
theoretical frameworks and concepts have been most fruitful in clarifying the effectiveness of
marketing communication in reducing food waste; (2) how prominent authors and research groups
have impacted the development of ideas in th is domain; (3) what trends and themes have
dominated the literature across different geographical locations, research methods and time
periods; and (4) which research questions and settings are still underexplored, thus presenting
significant opportunities  for future research. Systematic reviews improve objectivity and
replicability by employing transparent, a priori criteria in the review process (Mulrow, 1994 ).
Combining the bibliometric analysis (citation network, co -occurrence of keywords, trends in
publications, and the thematic evolution of concepts over time) with a systematic review approach
provides useful insights into the content and structure of a research field (Kraus et al., 2020). These
integrated methods increase transparency in data collection and synthesis, leading to objectivity
and reproducibility and hence provide researchers and practitioners with more comprehensive and
reliable evidence synthesis than conventional  narrative reviews, especially when used in
conjunction with evidence-based decision making (Zupic & Čater, 2015).

Goals and Scope of the Study
The prime goal of this systematic, bibliometric and descriptive analysis is to find the answer for a
main research question. "How has the research  on the association of  consumer-related
food waste and marketing communication evolved over time?". This dual approach strategy
directly supports the four research concerns that guide the objectives of the present study.

i. An understanding of research evolution over time to comprehend whether research in the
areas of marketing communication and consumer food waste has increased in parallel with
the urgency of real world circumstances, and to identify possible points of conve rgence
with major policy milestones such as SDG target 12.3.
ii. Knowledge about leading countries, authors, and journals to fill the geographic and
institutional concentration gap, providing an evidence base for assessing generalizability
concerns rather than relying on impression alone.
iii. Comprehension of the major research themes that can directly addresses the fragmentation
across the framing, norms, social marketing, corporate, digital, and retail communication
streams, providing a structural map of how these themes relate.
iv. Identification of emerging topics by mapping the conceptual and theoretical posterities of
the field is important to understand where the field is going, especially given the rapid rise
of digital and platform-based communication.

This study contributes in several ways. First of all, it offers a systematic and integrated bibliometric
synthesis of the literature on the nexus between marketing communication and consumer food
waste, providing a thorough and repeatable map of a topic that was previously only comprehended
through disjointed, narrative descriptions. Second, it critically summarizes the main
communication tactics that have been studied in earlier research, including message framing,
social norms, social marketing, corporate commu nication, digital communication, and retail
communication. Third, the study provides an evidence -based rather than an assumption -based
evaluation of generalizability and representativeness in the field by specifically mapping
geographic, institutional, and thematic concentration. Lastly, by identifying emerging topics and

the review process (Page et al., 2021). The three main stream flow of PRISMA is detailed in Figure

Record Screening and Inclusion
The data sample was restricted to include only the journal articles published in English in the fields
of “Business, Management and Accounting, Social Sciences, Psychology, Multidisciplinary, Arts
and Humanities”. This resulted in the shortlisting of 322 articles, which were  then evaluated for
exclusion criteria, which was the completion of records and  the deletion of duplicate entries . A
final of 322 articles were selected for bibliometric analysis after screening and shortlisting.

Findings and Discussion
To achieve the objective of analyzing growth, trends, and research evolution in the domain of food
waste and marketing communicati on nexus we conducted a  bibliometric review including
descriptive and thematic analysis of journal publications applying a combination of bibliometric
visualization and keyword content analysis through the Bibliometrix (Biblioshiny) application and
VOSviewer software (“Visualizing Scientifc Landscapes” version 1.6.18). Biblioshiny helped in
statistical analysis, trend analysis, country and researchers’ productivity, citations and thematic
evolution and mapping at the same time VOSviewer helped in net work visualizations (authors,
keywords, citations, collaborations).

Yearly Global Scientific Research Production
Interconnected research on the issues of consumer food waste and marketing communication
remained almost zero to very limited till 2010 (see Figure 2). This suggests that the role of
marketing communication in the food waste issue was not seriously considered until the topic had
been established as a policy priority. The adoption of SDG Target 12.3 (Responsible consumption
and production) in 2015 gave the field a policy target for the first time. Subsequent intensification
of output from 2020 onwards c orresponds with both the first  Food Waste Index Report (UNEP,
2021) and the disruption of household food provisioning during the Covid -19 pandemic, each of
which reasonably w ould have drawn the researchers’ and funders' attention toward consumer -
related dimensions of the problem.

Discussion
Findings of the bibliometric descriptive review present a coherent and internally consistent
research development in the field of consumer food waste and marketing communication

Journal of Accounting and Finance in Emerging Economies               Vol. 12, No 2, June 2026

344

interconnection research. The publication trends show that this is a very young field, and the
publication volume has outgrown the theoretical consolidation. The country and author findings
show that this growth has been geographically and institutionally narrow, concentrated in a small
number of high -income research systems and spread across a wide but shallow population of
contributors from adjacent disciplines (food science, social marketing, food -waste behavioural
research) that do not yet form a cohesive research com munity. The findings also suggest that
although marketing communication and food waste relevant work is being produced, it is mainly
being published in general interdisciplinary sustainability outlets rather than in the flagship outlets
of the marketing discipline, thus limiting its visibility and integration with general marketing and
communication theory.

The findings of the keyword based thematic map and thematic evolution come together from three
independent analytical angles to arrive at the same central conclusion: the communication-specific
mechanisms, especially message framing and social norms , are bibliometrically emergent rather
than established, meaning the field currently possesses less internal theoretical coherence around
its own communication mechanisms. The emerging topics evidence is the final piece of the puzzle
in terms of where the field appears to be heading (framing, norms, product innovation as upcycled
food, price promotions, and social marketing), and where it conspicuously has not yet come (digital
and social -media-specific communication, and research outside a narrow set of high -income
countries).

Collectively, this bibliometric evidence empirically supports (rather than just in narrative literature
review) the theoretical fragm entation, geographic concentration and methodological gaps this
study identified qualitatively, and directly justifies the role of the systematic literature review in
synthesizing a still-forming, still fragmented body of work into a coherent account of what is, and
is not yet, known about the role of marketing communication in consumer food waste.

1.1. Future Research Direction
This bibliometric evidence offers a more reliable map of the intellectual structure of the field than
any single narrative review could provide. Precisely , because it is derived from the aggregate
pattern of keyword co-occurrence, thematic density, and citation based clustering over the full set
of carefully extracted data . Several significant directions for furthering study at the n exus of
consumer food waste and marketing communication are indicated by the bibliometric evidence.
At f irst, the f indings press the need for future research that explicitly test s the consumers’
behavioral patterns around suboptimal and upcycled food choice, purchase, and waste. Secondly,
message framing and social norms were found to be emergent themes in the domain of food waste
related consumer behavior. Future marketing research should especially look into the interactions
between normative and su stainable communication and various message framings , including
social norms and price promotions, rather than treating them as separate research streams.

Thirdly, the bibliometric results show that research is heavily concentrated in high-income nations.
Future studies should focus on developing and emerging nations, where there are significant
differences in food systems, cultural norms, purchasing patterns, infrastructure, and
socioeconomic circumstances. Comparative cross -national research would incre ase the
generalizability of current theories and offer culturally appropriate communication tactics for
lowering food waste worldwide.

Lastly, Future studies should use a wider variety of methodological techniques because the topic
is still very young. Long-term behavioral change would be better understood, and causal inference
would be strengthened by experimental designs, longitudinal studies, field experiments, mixed -
methods research, and cross -cultural comparisons. The growing body of evidence may also be
strengthened by meta-analyses and theory-building reviews.

Managerial Implications
This bibliometric analysis concludes that c ommunication tactics for decreasing consumer food
waste are still in their infancy. Focus on price promotions, sustainability communication, message
framing, and social norms in promotions of food, especially upcycled and suboptimal  foods, are
major areas of managerial considerations.
Marketers, food producers, and retailers should create communication campaigns that employ
persuasive strategies backed by research, like messages that promote constructive and sustainable
social norms. The increasing market potential is suggested by the e mergence of upcycled foods
and product innovation. Managers should employ marketing communication to inform consumers
about the environmental safety and quality advantages of imperfect and upcycled food products.

The emergence of price promotions as a new theme implies that communication should be used in
conjunction with pricing strategies. Retailers can reinforce both economic and environmental
motivations for purchase by combining persuasive sustainability messages with discounts on
imperfect or suboptimal food products.

Research Limitations
The primary limitation of this study is the selection of data from a single source database (Scopus);
consequently, relevant studies indexed in other databases (e.g., Web of Science) may have been
missed. Inclusion criteria in the systematic reviews (e.g., search terms, langu age) sometimes
contribute to selection biases and limitations of missing some high-impact work from the domain.
