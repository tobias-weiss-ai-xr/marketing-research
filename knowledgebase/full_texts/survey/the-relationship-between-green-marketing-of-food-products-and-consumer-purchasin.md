INTRODUCTION
Sustainable development is a process through which all people are able to
improve the fulfillment of their needs. In recent years, due to growing global concerns
regarding environmental degradation, rapid and unfavorable climate change,
pollution of enviro nmental resources, and the excessive consumption of natural
resources, governments, international organizations, and consumers have paid
increasing attention to the concept of the sustainable development of natural
resources. Consequently, this issue has attracted widespread attention (zahedi, 2013).
In response to these concerns, manufacturing and service organizations have sought
to address this critical challenge and reduce the negative environmental and social
impacts of their economic activities by adopting effective management practices and
applying modern marketing approaches.
One of the most important approaches aimed at reducing environmental
pollution, conserving natural resources, and promoting sustainable developme nt is
the implementation of green marketing. In this regard, the design, production,
distribution, and promotion of green products that cause minimal harm to public
health and the environment are considered among the principal components of green
marketing. Moreover, green marketing is regarded as one of the most important tools
for organizations to establish a competitive environment and gain consumers' trust
in the use of green products (Eyasu, 2026) .Green marketing refers to a set of methods
and practices designed and implemented to reduce the harmful effects of products
and services on the environment, human health, and society, while simultaneously
encouraging sustainable consumption patterns. This process involves the use of
environmentally friendly raw materials, recyclable packaging, and the dissemination
of information emphasizing environmental values and the health -related
characteristics of green products.
Global studies indicate that consumers are increasingly aware o f the harmful
consequences associated with the use of certain products and are paying greater
attention to environmental issues. Accordingly, their purchasing behavior can have a
significant impact on environmental protection, public welfare, and sustainab le
development (Hassan, 2026) . Food products constitute a substantial part of

retailers and consumers (Kim,, 2019).

Research Problem
Green marketing involves the design, packaging, pricing, and promotion of
food products in accordance with environmental considerations, with the objective
of reducing their negative impacts on the environment, human health, and society .
In recent years, this approach has emerged as a scientific trend in marketing
management and has received considerable attention (Polonsky, 2011) . Despite its
global development and growing application, the concept of green marketing has not
yet been fully established in Kabul City in either practical or scientific terms.
Furthermore, limited comprehensive research has been conducted on its impact on
the purchasing behavior of food product consumers (Ottman, 2017). Previous studies
have shown that consumers do not base their purchasing decisions solely on product
price and quality. Factors such as ethical and environmental considerations may also
influence their purchasing decisions and behavior. In  particular, consumers'
perceptions of environmental issues are considered an important variable affecting
their purchasing decisions regarding food products. In addition, compliance with
environmental considerations and the development of green products c ontribute to
brand building and sales growth (P. Kotler 2016). Understanding the impact of green
marketing on consumer purchasing behavior in Kabul City can influence the
decisions and policies of retailers as well as the purchasing decisions of consumers.

Theoretical Background
Green marketing refers to a set of activities in which the processes of designing,
packaging, production, pricing, and promotion are implemented in accordance with
the principles of green  products. More specifically, incorporating environmental
considerations into the packaging of food products in a manner that enhances
recyclability, reduces the use of chemical materials, and provides information
regarding the origin of green products is regarded as one of the most important and
influential components of green marketing. This managerial approach aims to reduce
the negative environmental impacts of economic activities and is therefore
considered an indirect component of sustainable developm ent (Polonsky, 2011) .
Consumer purchasing behavior, in the green marketing literature, refers to a process
that includes the stages of need recognition, information search, evaluation of
available alternatives, purchase decision, and post-purchase behavior (P. Kotler 2016).
Environmental awareness, in essence, refers to consumers' understanding of the
harmful consequences of using certain products on the environment and has been
identified in empirical studies as an important variable influen cing consumers'
attitudes toward green products (Peattie, 2005). Furthermore, the Theory of Planned

LITERATURE REVIEW
Numerous studies on green marketing have been conducted worldwide. A
study by Peattie (2005) indicates that consumers' inclination toward green products
is increasing. However, due to factors such as price instability, lack of trust, or limited
access to green products, these prefe rences do not always translate into actual
purchasing behavior (Peattie, 2005) . Similarly, Ottman (2017), in a study on green
marketing, emphasized that the success of green marketing depends on the
transparency of environmental information and the enhancement of consumers' trust
in green products. In the absence of consumer trust, green marketing practices are
likely to have limited effectiveness. The study also found that, despite having positive
attitudes toward green product s, consumers are less willing to pay higher prices
because of income constraints (Ottman, 2017) . Consumer behavior has also been
examined by Ajzen (1991). According to the Theory of Planned Behavior, providing
information about  environmental impacts alone is not sufficient to influence
consumers' purchasing behavior (Ajzen,, 1991).
Although numerous studies have been conducted in the field of marketing
management within the country, research focusing  specifically on the green
marketing of food products and consumer purchasing behavior remains limited. In
practice, markets in Kabul City are still largely influenced by traditional factors, and
variables such as price, quality, and product availability may have a greater influence
on consumers' purchasing decisions. This indicates that green marketing in Kabul
City is still at an early stage of development and has not yet been systematically
integrated into the structure and practices of retail outlets. T herefore, the existing
gap between the global literature on green marketing and the local conditions of
Kabul City highlights the importance of investigating this phenomenon, as the
findings can provide practical value for policymakers and marketing practitioners.

Discussions

correlation, and multiple linear regression analyses generally indicate that the
conceptual model of the study possesses satisfactory validity and explanatory power.
Specifically, the Cronbach's alpha coefficients for all construct exceeded 0.70,
indicating that the questionnaire and the research instrument have acceptable
reliability and adequate internal consistency. Furthermore, the results of Spearman's
rank correlation demonstrate a positive and statistically significa nt relationship
between the green marketing of food products and consumer purchasing behavior.

promotion and advertising exhibit positive effects on consumer purchasing behavior.

proportion of the variation in consumer purchasing behavior. Moreover, the adjusted

reinforcing role in consumer purchasing behavior, whereas the economic factor,

CONCLUSIONS
The findings of this study on the green m arketing of food products are
consistent with the theoretical literature and empirical studies conducted at the
international level. The results indicate that the green marketing of food products
and its related variables have significantly influenced consumer purchasing behavior
in retail stores. In particular, the findings are consistent with those reported by
(Peattie, 2005; Ottman, 2017;  Kim et al. 2019). The results highlight the important
roles of environmental awareness, trust in green claims, and m arketing
communication in shaping consumer purchasing behavior in retail outlets, thereby
supporting the findings of previous studies. On the other hand, the results indicate
that the price of green products has a negative and statistically significant eff ect on
consumer purchasing behavior in retail stores in Kabul City. This finding is consistent
with the economic conditions of developing countries and reflects consumers' high
sensitivity to the prices of green products. Among the variables examined, the
credibility of green claims demonstrates the greatest explanatory power for consumer
purchasing behavior, emphasizing the importance that consumers attach to the
environmental claims made by companies and manufacturing organizations. These
findings further suggest that the use of green advertising alone is not sufficient to
ensure the success of green marketing. Rather, transparency of information and
honesty in environmental claims play a well -defined role in the acceptance of green
products by consumers. Overall, the findings indicate that the successful
implementation of green marketing in the food market of Kabul City requires a
combination of environmental awareness, trust -building and effective price
management. Authors have suggested further studies in other parts of Kabul City.
