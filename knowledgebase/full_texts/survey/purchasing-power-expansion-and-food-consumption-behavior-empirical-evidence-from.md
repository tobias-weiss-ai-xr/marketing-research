Abstract
This study investigates to what extent an increase in purchasing power affects low-income households’ food consumption
behavior, using Thailand’s State Welfare Card program as a quasi-experimental setting. We apply a Regression Discontinuity (RD)
design on the 2023 Thai Household Socio-Economic Survey to identify the impact of a regular increase in the ability to spend
at the eligibility threshold. The findings demonstrate a strategic reallocation of food expenditures toward fresh and raw food
categories. Beneficiaries prioritize at-home meal preparation by significantly expanding expenditures on raw ingredients,
specifically meat and seafood, core carbohydrates, and fresh vegetables. In contrast, there is no significant increase in
spending on prepared foods, dining out, or discretionary goods such as alcohol and sweets. These results offer critical
implications for retail marketing strategies targeting low -to-middle-income segments and provide empirical feedback for
policymakers regarding the food expenditure patterns of targeted social assistance. Although our empirical evidence is
derived from a cash transfer setting, these behavioral insights may provide useful insights into broader contexts of purchasing
power expansion such as standard salary increases or minimum wage adjustments.
Keywords: Purchasing power, Consumer behavior, Food consumption, Cash transfer
Introduction
How do low-income consumers spend when their
purchasing power increases? In our context, an increase in
purchasing power refers specifically to the regular receipt
of additional cash. While literature consistently demonstrates
that when more money is on the table, households
increase food spending, it leaves a fundamental consumer
behavior question unanswered: how does this increased
ability to pay  alter food consumption modes and food
category selection? Using a targeted cash transfer program
as an empirical setting  to observe expanded purchasing
power, this study moves beyond top-line food spending to
examine granular consumer choice by addressing two core
questions. First, to what extent does an expansion in
purchasing power alter household food consumption
modes? Second, how do consumers reallocate  this
incremental ability to purchase across fundamental food
categories?
We investigate the impact of purchasing power
expansion for low -income households using Thailand’s
flagship social protection initiative: the State Welfare Card
program, as a case. Rather than distributing pure unconstrained
cash, the program regularly provides a structured bundle
of consumption subsidies earmarked for non -
discretionary  expenses, including utilities and public
transit, as well as a traditional unconditional cash transfer .
Because these targeted allocations absorb a baseline share
of essential living costs, they effectively free up fluid
resources elsewhere in the  household budget. We select
Thailand as our empirical case not only for this institutional
framework but because the country provides an optimal
laboratory for examining food -specific retail dynamics. As
a robust agricultural economy, baseline food access is
highly convenient, charac terized by ubiquitous traditional
wet markets, modern retail channels, and the viability of
home-grown consumption. The market offers a vast array
of dietary choices with clearly demarcated price tiers.
Because fundamental food security is broadly attainable,
the food consumption shifts observed when purchasing
power expands are not merely survival -driven, but rather
strategic consumer choices.
Understanding the impact of purchasing power
on food consumption patterns and category selection is
critical for two main audiences. For businesses and
marketers, it illuminates how an expansion in purchasing
power affects consumer preferences across food product
categories and meal -preparation habits. Equipped with
these insights, retailers can proactively optimize product
assortments and tailor marketing strategies to capture
activated consumer demand. Importantly, although our
empirical evidence is derived from a cash transfer setting,
these findings may provide useful insights into how
financially constrained households respond to broader
contexts of purchasing power expansion, such as standard
salary increases or minimum wage adjustments. For
policymakers, it provides the empirical foundation needed to
optimize program design, particularly  regarding how
structured financial assistance translates into  actual retail

spending and meal habits. Also, it helps reveal potential
unintended consequences in food expenditure  allocation.
Our study leverages cross-sectional microdata from
the 2023 Thai Household Socio-Economic Survey, obtained
from the National Statistical Office of Thailand. To isolate
the impact of expanded purchasing power on consumer
choices, we employ a Regression Discontinuity (RD) design.
This quasi- experimental app roach is ideally suited to our
empirical setting because program eligibility is determined
by an exogenous threshold —an annual per capita income
of 100,000 Baht. Our key outcome variables capture both
meal-preparation habits (raw ingredients, semi -processed
items, and dining out) and granular retail spending across
the five fundamental dietary groups: meat and seafood,
rice and grains, vegetables, fruits, and milk products.
Our findings reveal a distinct shift in household
food consumption behavior at the eligibility threshold,
demonstrating  that consumers use expanded purchasing
power to reallocate their food expenditures. First, regarding
eating habits, households direct their newly freed cash
toward raw ingredients for home cooking. They
significantly expand their baseline grocery baskets
rather than  disproportionately shifting to dining out,
thereby avoiding built -in service premiums. Second,
unpacking their category selection reveals a clear hierarchy
of prioritization: consumers allocate the largest share of
their incremental spending to core structural categories,
driving substantial and statistically significant increases in
meat and seafood, alongside staple carbohydrates and
vegetables. Finally, this rise proves to be highly functional;
despite unconstrained budgets, households do not divert
incremental funds toward discretionary categories like alcohol
or sweets, indicating a deliberate consumer choice to shift
their food expenditure allocation toward fresh meal inputs.
Recent literature examining consumer behavior
presents a nuanced view of how purchasing power
increments affect retail spending and category -level food
consumption, using cash transfer programs as cases. Baker
et al. (2023) document rapid consumption increases following
an increase in purchasing power, with households directing
a substantial portion of these new funds toward food
spending. Furthermore, cash injections can drive significant
retail shifts; for example, Woo et al. (2022) demonstrate
that a shopping coupon program increased expenditures on
food  and beverages by 50% without impacting
entertainment and leisure. Examining dietary quality,
Hoddinott and Skoufias  (2004) provide evidence that
conditional financial assistance  in Mexico enabled
beneficiaries to acquire more calories from vegetable and
animal products. Similarly, a comprehensive review by
Bastagli et al. (2019) highlights that increases in purchasing
power consistently improve dietary diversity, even  when
evidence of changes in physical health outcomes remains
limited. Habimana et al. (2021) show that unconditional
financial assistance in Rwanda boosts recipients’ ability to
spend, resulting in a marginal increase in total and food
consumption while having a null effect on non-food spending.
However, consumption impacts are not universally expansive;
for instance, Vannavanit and Takahashi (2025) found no
statistically significant evidence of a rise in food spending
with a regular increase in purchasing power during the
COVID-19 pandemic.
Our study contributes to this strand of literature
by demonstrating that an expansion in purchasing power
fundamentally affects consumer choice across two distinct
dimensions: where they eat and what they consume.
Specifically, we provide empirical evidence on how an
increased ability to spend drives a strategic shift toward
home-preparation modes, while simultaneously enabling
households to shift their expenditures toward fresh and
raw food categories.

Literature Review
To provide empirical context for our analysis, this
study uses Thailand’s flagship social protection initiative —
the State Welfare Card Scheme (commonly referred to as
the Bat Khon Jon program)—as our case study for observing
an increase in purchasing power. Initially launched in 2017
to alleviate poverty through direct targeted assistance, the
Thai government initiated a comprehensive restructuring
and a second major round of national registration in 2022.
Registration was formalized through both online portals
managed by the Ministry of Finance and physical government
channels, including provincial treasury offices, district
administrative offices, and branches of the specialized
financial institutions. Following this restructuring, the program
achieved massive scale; in 2023, approximately 14.6 million
individuals received the welfare card, a figure that is six
times larger than the 2.39 million Thais officially classified
as living below the consumption -based poverty line in the
same year (Poonpolkul et al., 2024).
The current eligibility criteria span demographic,
institutional, and financial dimensions to rigorously identify
vulnerable populations. Demographically, an applicant must
hold Thai nationality, be at least 18 years of age, and not
belong to explicitly excluded institutional groups, such as
Buddhist monks, incarcerated individuals, active civil servants,
or government pensioners. Most critically for our empirical
strategy, the program imposes strict income and asset-based
thresholds. Applicants must be unemployed or have an
annual income not exceeding 100,000 Baht, with total liquid
financial assets (such as bank deposits or bonds) similarly
capped at 100,000 Baht. Crucially, these income threshold
are evaluated based on the average per capita levels within
the applicant’s family unit, providing the exogenous cutoff
utilized in our regression discontinuity design.
In addition to income and liquid asset constraints,
the state places strict limitations on physical wealth and

debt profiles. Applicants must not own real estate or land,
though exceptions are granted if the property falls strictly
below legally defined thresholds for residential or agricultural
use. Finally, to ensure the program targets individuals without
significant financial leverage, applicants are barred from
holding any active credit cards and cannot possess outstanding
formal loans that exceed established institutional limits.
Approved applicants receive comprehensive benefits,
including monthly cash transfers and consumption subsidies.
First, beneficiaries receive a monthly allowance of 300 Baht
designated for purchasing essential consumer goods,
educational materials, and agricultural inputs at designated
local shops (Thong Fah shops). Second, the scheme provides
an 80 Baht subsidy per person every three months to offset
cooking gas expenses. Third, transport mobility is supported
through a consolidated public transportation allowance of
750 Baht per person per month, applicable across multiple
public transit systems. Fourth, the program includes utility
relief consisting of a monthly subsidy of up to 315 Baht per
household for elec tricity and 100 Baht per household for
water consumption. Finally, the initiative integrates an
additional disability stipend of 200 Baht per month for
qualifying individuals. Cumulatively, for a household operating
near the 100,000 Baht eligibility threshold, this bundle of
full benefits constitutes a substantial and regular increase
in their overall purchasing power. By covering these baseline,
essential expenses, the program’s structured delivery
mechanically frees up cash elsewhere in the budget, driving
the strategic reallocation of food spending observed in our
analysis.

the necessary purchasing power to strategically restructure
their grocery baskets and meal-preparation habits.
Having established the substantial magnitude of
the purchasing power expansion, it is crucial to justify our
empirical focus on retail food behavior. As a fundamental
human necessity, food constitutes the absolute base of the
hierarchy of human needs, acti ng as the primary driver of
consumer behavior when resources are constrained (Maslow,
1943). For Thailand in particular, food constitutes the largest
component of a low-income household’s budget (National
Statistical Office, 2023).

Methodology
Data and key variables
This study utilizes cross- sectional microdata from
the 2023 Thai Household Socio -Economic Survey (SES), a
nationally representative dataset administered by the
National Statistical Office (NSO) of Thailand. The full survey
employs a comprehensive sampling framework covering
49,664 households nationwide. The SES provides highly
disaggregated data on household income, revenues,
and consumption expenditures across diverse product and
service categories.
In addition to granular consumption metrics, the
survey captures detailed information on household demographic

characteristics, socio-economic backgrounds, and financial
conditions. To ensure data quality and minimize measurement
error, the NSO collects these data through structured, face-
to-face interviews. Given its extensive coverage and granular
expenditure variables, this dataset provides an ideal empirical
setting to analyze the impacts of expanded purchasing power
on disaggregated consumer behavior.
To ensure empirical comparability between households
with baseline and expanded purchasing power, we apply
two targeted sample restrictions. First, we restrict our
analytical sample to households with an annual per capita
income of no more than 200,000 Baht. This restriction
focuses our sample  on a relatively homogeneous sub -
population surrounding the eligibility threshold of 100,000
Baht, maximizing local comparability. Second, we
systematically exclude households whose primary income
sources are derived from self -employment. Because per
capita income determines program eligibility, and informal
or entrepreneurial revenues are notoriously difficult to
verify accurately due to high volatility and reporting friction,
excluding these households minimizes potential
measurement error and ensures the precision of our
threshold estimations.
To evaluate how increased purchasing power affects
consumer spending patterns and category -level demand,
we  construct our dependent variables using two
complementary functional forms: monetary expenditure
levels and natural logarithmic transformations. When targeted
subsidies enhance consumer purchasing power, analyzing
expenditure levels in Thai Baht allows us to quantify the
absolute scale and monetary magnitude of category
change. Concurrently,  applying a natural logarithmic
transformation accounts for right-skewness in transaction
data and captures proportional  demand responsiveness,
reflecting the relative spending adjustments across distinct
retail categories.

discrepancies, we employ a reduced -form (Intent -to-

directly estimate the effect of expanding the policy
threshold regardless of actual take-up and will be discussed
more in the next section.

welfare benefits and 3.0% receive an elderly allowance.

across the primary consumption categories underscores the
distributional stability of the sample, validating the use of this
data for our localized threshold estimations.

Empirical strategy
The eligibility criteria, particularly the distinct income,
create discontinuities in eligibility. Consequently, it offers a
highly robust setting for implementing quasi-experimental
methods to isolate the causal impacts of purchasing power
expansion on subsequent consumer choices at the threshold.
To identify the causal effects of the additional purchasing
power on consumer behavior, this study employs a Regression
Discontinuity (RD) design. This non-experimental approach
is uniquely suited to our setting because eligibility is
determined, pre -specified institutional cutoffs. Following
Lee and Lemieux (2010), the internal validity of our RD
estimates rests on the fulfillment of three core assumptions:
(1) continuity of unobservable, (2) discontinuity in first -
stage relationship, and (3) the exclusion restriction.
The continuity assumption requires that all
unobserved factors affecting consumer choices vary
continuously across the eligibility threshold. Intuitively,
this assumption is satisfied if individuals have “imprecise
control” over their running variable near the cutoff,
rendering  treatment assignment as good as random
within a local neighborhood. Here, we selected per capita
annual income as our running variable because it is
relatively better at quantitatively capturing treatment
status than other continuous profile such as saving which
could be subject to household risk appetite and investment
preference.
To formally validate this assumption, we perform
a robust density test on the running variable to ensure
individuals cannot precisely manipulate their eligibility
status. Consistent with the continuity assumption, we find no
statistical evidence of discontinuous sorting at the 100,000
Baht threshold (see Appendix, Table A1). Furthermore,
sorting around the cutoff is structurally difficult in this
setting; eligibility is tied to pre -determined administrative
benchmarks —such as property ownership, active credit
cards, and bank deposit volumes—verified through rigorous

We further validate the assumption by performing a
falsification test on a comprehensive vector of predetermined
household characteristics. Table 2 presents the estimation
results across fundamental socio-economic and demographic
baseline dimensions. The point estimates reveal that crossing
the eligibility threshold has no statistically significant effect
on all dimensions with the exception of age, which is
included as a covariate in the formal analysis. The coefficients
remain statistically indistinguishable from zero, and the
optimal localized bandwidth choice behaves stably across the
independent metrics. This systemic absence of discontinuous
shifts at the threshold provides strong empirical evidence
that households do not strategically manipulate their standing
around the cutoff.
The age difference observed in Table 2 reflects a
life-cycle dynamic within the Thai labor market, specifically
given our sample restrictions. Because our analysis
systematically excludes households whose primary income
sources are derived from self-employment, the households
remaining in our sample consist of workers who frequently
exit the formal labor force via retirement as they reach
their mid-to-late 50s. This transition causes a systemic drop in
their reported per -capita income. Consequently, the
population falling just below the eligibility threshold is
structurally older compared to those just above the
threshold who are still actively earning in the formal
workforce. Therefore, this discontinuity does not indicate
manipulative sorting around the cutoff, but rather an
accurate reflection of the formal labor  force life -cycle.
Nevertheless, to ensure that this demographic variation does
not confound our estimates, we incorporate age—alongside
all other observed household characteristics —as control
covariates in all of our model specifications.
The second foundational assumption requires a
discontinuity in the probability of receiving the welfare
card at the eligibility cutoff. In field settings, a common
empirical challenge stems from measurement friction: self-
reported income captured in househ old surveys can
deviate from the official administrative records used by the
state to determine eligibility.
As documented in Table 3 and illustrated in Figure 3,
crossing the eligibility threshold induces a highly
significant, immediate jump of 57.2 percentage points in the
probability of program take -up at the cutoff. This robust
first-stage discontinuity confirms that the threshold holds
substantial operational bite in practice, validating the
plausibility and strength of our empirical framework.

The final assumption, exclusion restriction, dictates
that crossing the eligibility threshold affects consumer
behavior only through an increase in purchasing power or
the eligibility and receipt of the state welfare card. The
primary threat to this exclusion restriction would be a
confounding  policy that utilizes the exact same financial
cutoffs. However, a comprehensive review of Thai social policy
that could impact consumer purchasing power confirms
that this specific threshold is unique to the State Welfare
Card Scheme. Because other public safety nets in Thailand
target distinct demographic groups using independent
criteria tailored to specific so cial needs, the exclusion
restriction holds robustly in our empirical setting.
When implementing the RD framework, empirical
estimation introduces an operational tradeoff between
localized bias and statistical precision. Restricting the
analytical sample to observations in ultra -proximity to the
institutional threshold minimizes structural bias by comparing
highly identical households; however, this strategy dramatically
inflates variance if the localized sample size is constrained.
To systematically optimize this tradeoff, we adopt a data-
driven bandwidth selection procedure following Calonico
et al. (2014), which minimizes the Mean Squared Error (MSE)
criterion to establish optimal bandwidths above and below
the cutoff. Across all specification, the set of predetermined
covariate in Table 1 is included in the regression.
Our primary analysis strategically prioritizes the
reduced-form (Intent-to-Treat, or ITT) estimations. We lean
on the reduced-form specifications for three reasons: First,
as documented in our first -stage analysis, the observed
threshold features imperfect  compliance, with a take -up
jump of 57.2 percentage points. Running a full instrumental
variable specification divides the reduced -form impact by
this first-stage compliance rate, which mechanically scaling
up the point estimates and can inflate standard e rrors.
Interpreting the reduced form avoids this artificial inflation,
offering a more conservative and grounded estimate.

Second, from an institutional design and policymaking

impact of making the program available to the population.
Because policy executives can control program rules but
cannot enforce perfect compliance, the ITT effect represents
the realistic, operationally relevant metric for purchasing
power increase evaluation.
Third, the reduced form relies strictly on the random
assignment mechanism of the threshold itself. It strictly
does not require the stringent assumption that the threshold
only impacts household behavior through actual card
utilization, providing a robust causal benchmark.
Results and Discussion
1. Effect on Disaggregated Food Consumption and
Channels
To examine how expanded consumer purchasing
power affects consumer food choice and grocery spending
dynamics, we segment total food expenditures into two
distinct retail and consumption channels: at -home meal
preparation and food away from home (food servi ce and
commercial dining). Within the at -home domain, we
differentiate between raw ingredients (requiring domestic
food preparation labor) and prepared and processed foods
(ready -to-eat takeout items, instant noodles, and shelf -
stable packaged goods). This  classification allows us to
observe whether expanded consumer purchasing power
stimulates scratch cooking, convenience -driven packaged
goods demand, or out-of-home food service consumption.

5. Robustness check
To ensure the validity of our main findings and rule
out the presence of false positives, we subject the observed
shifts in consumer behavior to a series of rigorous sensitivity
tests. Importantly, to isolate coefficient sensitivity without
the confounding effect of fluctuating sample sizes, the
“Baseline Model” utilized throughout this robustness matrix
employs an optimal bandwidth constrained to maintain a
fixed and symmetric number of observations on both sides
of the threshold. This methodological choice differs deliberately
from the unconstrained, asymmetric MSE-optimal bandwidths
reported in the main estimation results.
Against this fixed baseline, we implement three
core robustness checks. First, we test for potential non-linear
distortions or overfitting by evaluating our models under
progressively higher polynomial orders, running estimations
up to a third -order specification (Cattaneo et al., 2019).
This ensures that the documented shifts in consumption reflect
robust behavioral adjustments around the threshold rather
than being disproportionately skewed by extreme or outlying
household observations. Second, we vary the underlying
statistical weighting scheme. We replace our primary triangular
data-weighting approach with two alternative standard
weighting profiles: the Epanechnikov and Uniform distribution
models. This variation ensures that our results do not depend
on an isolated mathematical assumption regarding how
close or far a household sits from the eligibility cutoff.

Conclusions
This study provides empirical evidence on how an
expansion in purchasing power affects the food consumption
modes and category selections of low -income households.
By exploiting the eligibility threshold of Thailand’s State Welfare
Card program through an RD design, we demonstrate that
when budget constraints are relaxed, households increase
their spending on at -home food preparation. Rather than
expanding expenditures on convenience foods or dining
out, beneficiaries direct their newly freed purchasing power
toward raw ingredient expenditures for home cooking, most
notably meat and seafood, alongside essential carbohydrates
and vegetables. Crucially, we find no evidence of budget
diversion into discretionary or temptation categories, such
as alcohol or sweets.
These findings have significant implications for both
business and public policy. For food retailers and marketers,
the increased purchasing power allocation toward home
preparation and fresh proteins signals a valuable opportunity
to optimize assortment and promotional strategies in lower-
income catchment areas. For policymakers, the results
provide evidence that structured cash flow injections can
yield positive changes in food spending patterns, enabling
financially constrained populations to independently increase
their purchases of fresh grocery inputs without the need for
strict paternalistic consumption controls.
While this study utilizes robust cross-sectional data,
future research could explore long term tracking to determine
if these spending shifts persist over time or normalize as the
novelty of expanded purchasing power diminishes. Furthermore,
because our methodology captures retail grocery expenditures
rather than actual physiological consumption, subsequent
studies employing direct nutritional assessments or precise
caloric intake tracking are needed to definitively evaluate
the dietary health impacts of such financial interventions.
Additionally, expanding this framework to investigate non-
food categories would provide a more holistic understanding
of consumer behavior under relaxed financial constraints.
