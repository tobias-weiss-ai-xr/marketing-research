Abstract. In the digital era, public media‑consumption habits have been fully transformed.
Traditional offline and print advertising fail to fit young consumers' fragmented, social and
visual content‑receiving patterns. Thanks to low‑creation barriers, rapid spread and high
user interactivity, short‑video platforms have turned into core carriers for modern brand
new‑media promotion. As a century‑old fast‑moving consumer‑goods brand, Coca‑Cola
maintains market competitiveness via consistent emotional marketing and standardized
brand‑image management. To keep pace with youth‑oriented market trends, it has
reallocated marketing budgets, cut spending on traditional ads and rolled out abundant
localized short‑video campaigns on Douyin, Xiaohongshu and WeChat Channels. Taking
Coca‑Cola's short‑video marketing practices between 2023 and 2025 as its research subject,
this paper draws on emotional‑marketing, UGC communication and brand‑communication
theories. Using literature review, typical case analysis and comparative research, it explores
how short‑video creation, hotspot operations and user‑interaction activities shape
brand‑communication effects. By analyzing public data from four representative campaigns,
it summarizes effective strategies and latent drawbacks in Coca‑Cola's new‑media
marketing. Departing from simplistic empirical assumptions, this study follows rigorous
academic logic and avoids unfounded causal links between short‑video marketing, sales
growth, and enhanced user loyalty. Based on objective data and public user feedback,
targeted optimization proposals are offered for refined short‑video marketing by classic
beverage brands. It provides practical references for traditional FMCG brands pursuing
youth‑oriented brand transformation and digital‑communication upgrading.

1. Introduction
1.1. Research background
With the continuous iteration of mobile internet technology, the public's daily information
acquisition methods have changed fundamentally. In the past two decades, commercial brand
promotion mainly depended on traditional channels such as television commercials, outdoor
billboards, street posters, and printed magazines. Such marketing modes belong to typical one-way

linear communication. Brands only output information unilaterally to the public, lacking effective
real-time interaction, user feedback collection, and secondary social dissemination channels [1]. At
present, short-video platforms have occupied the main leisure time of young consumer groups aged
18 to 30. Short, intuitive, and emotionally resonant video content is easier to capture public attention
and trigger spontaneous sharing and discussion among users.
The beverage fast-moving consumer goods industry presents a highly homogenized market
pattern. There is almost no substantial difference in product formula, taste, and basic functions
among mainstream beverage brands. In this competitive environment, product quality can no longer
form an absolute competitive advantage. Brand temperament, emotional connection with consumers,
public brand cognition, and youth discourse power have become the key dimensions affecting brand
market performance [2]. Many time-honored international beverage brands are facing the common
problem of aging brand image. Young consumers tend to define traditional classic brands as old-
fashioned and out of line with contemporary youth culture.
In order to eliminate the stereotype of aging brand image and continuously maintain market
popularity among young groups, numerous classic fast-moving consumer goods brands have begun
to lay out new-media marketing. As one of the most influential global beverage brands, Coca-Cola
has always taken emotional bonding and public warmth shaping as its core brand genes. In recent
years, the brand has significantly reduced its budget investment in traditional television and print
advertising, and focused on creating localized short-video content and interactive challenge
activities on Chinese mainstream short-video platforms. The continuous short-video marketing
layout of Coca-Cola has formed a complete set of new-media communication systems, which has
typical research significance for analyzing the brand communication value of short-video marketing.
1.2. Research significance
1.2.1. Theoretical significance
From the theoretical perspective, most existing academic studies separate short-video marketing
rules from brand localized operation cases. Many studies only elaborate on the general advantages
of new-media marketing or simply interpret the connotation of emotional marketing and UGC
theories, lacking targeted theoretical verification combined with real brand cases and public
operational data [3]. This research systematically combines emotional marketing theory, UGC user
creation communication theory, and multi-dimensional brand communication evaluation theory,
applies classic marketing theories to analyze the actual effect of Coca-Cola's localized short-video
campaigns, verifies the applicability and limitations of traditional marketing theories in the short-
video digital scenario, and makes up for the insufficient empirical research on new-media marketing
of international fast-moving consumer goods brands.
1.2.2. Practical significance
From the practical perspective, this research objectively sorts out the effective strategies and
potential hidden problems of Coca-Cola's short-video marketing through real platform data and
public user comments. Different from subjective empirical judgment, this study maintains neutral
and prudent academic thinking, does not exaggerate the positive effect of marketing behaviors, and
does not arbitrarily deny the value of problematic content. The research conclusions and targeted
optimization suggestions can provide operable reference for other traditional beverage and fast-
moving consumer goods brands, help brands avoid common new-media marketing problems such as

1.4. Research status
1.4.1. Foreign research status
Foreign scholars have formed a relatively complete research system in the fields of digital
marketing, short‑video communication and brand emotional shaping. In terms of short‑video
marketing effectiveness, scenario‑based emotional narrative in short videos can effectively narrow
the cognitive distance between brands and young consumers and improve public brand recognition
[1]. From the perspective of user spontaneous communication, UGC content generated by users'
independent creation has higher social credibility than official unilateral advertising content, and can
effectively expand the decentralized communication boundary of brands [2].
In terms of innovative new‑media marketing of classic brands, excessive pursuit of content
innovation and technological novelty may weaken the stable emotional connection accumulated by
classic brands for a long time [3]. Vernacular daily narrative and low‑threshold interactive
participation are the core keys for new‑media content to gain youth market popularity [4]. With the
development of digital technology, artificial intelligence has also been widely applied in content
creation and brand marketing iteration, providing new technical paths for refined short-video
operation [5]. The above foreign research provides solid theoretical support for this study, but most
foreign literature focuses on global universal rules and lacks targeted analysis combined with
Chinese localized social platforms and youth consumption characteristics.
1.4.2. Domestic research status
Domestic research on short-video marketing is abundant, most of which focuses on the general
promotion strategies of domestic emerging fast-moving consumer goods brands and the basic
advantages of new-media communication. Research related to Coca-Cola mostly focuses on the
brand's traditional offline marketing layout, public welfare marketing strategies, and global brand
positioning, while few studies focus on the latest localized short-video series campaigns and real
public operational data [6].

1.5. Research methods
This paper comprehensively adopts three academic research methods to ensure the objectivity,
systematicness, and rigor of the research process and conclusions:
1.5.1. Literature research method
This study sorts out domestic and foreign classic literature and latest research achievements related
to short-video marketing, brand communication effect evaluation, emotional marketing, and UGC
user-generated content communication. By clarifying the core connotation, applicable scenarios, and
theoretical limitations of each theory, it constructs a complete theoretical analysis framework, laying
a solid theoretical foundation for subsequent case analysis and problem discussion.
1.5.2. Case analysis method
This paper selects four typical localized short-video marketing campaigns launched by Coca-Cola
on Chinese mainstream platforms from 2023 to 2025 as specific research cases. All research data are
derived from official platform public statistics, official brand release information, and public user
interactive data. This study analyzes the content design logic, platform adaptation strategy, and
public communication performance of each case one by one, and summarizes the common rules and
individual problems of brand short-video marketing.
1.5.3. Comparative analysis method
By comparing the communication modes, content characteristics, audience interaction effects, and
communication boundaries of Coca-Cola's traditional advertising and modern short-video
marketing, this study further highlights the innovative advantages of new-media marketing in
adapting to the youth market, and objectively analyzes the inherent deficiencies of short-video
communication itself, to ensure the neutrality and comprehensiveness of the research perspective.
2. Related concepts and theoretical basis
2.1. Core concept definition
2.1.1. Short-video marketing
Short-video marketing refers to a digital brand promotion mode in which brands create short visual
content adapted to platform rules and youth aesthetic preferences based on social short-video
platforms, and realize brand information output, public exposure, and user interaction dissemination
through algorithmically accurate recommendation and user social sharing [6]. Different from

traditional one-way rigid advertising, short-video marketing has four core characteristics:
fragmentation of content viewing, visualization of expression form, two-way nature of user
interaction, and viral socialization of secondary dissemination. It is the mainstream new-media
marketing mode adapted to the digital information era.
2.1.2. Brand communication effect
Brand communication effect refers to the comprehensive public influence formed after brand
information is disseminated through specific channels, which is the core index to evaluate the
quality of brand marketing activities [7]. Based on the unified academic evaluation standard of new-
media brand communication, this study evaluates brand communication effect from four objective
dimensions: brand public exposure scale, user interactive participation activity, public brand image
cognition, and consumer emotional association degree. This evaluation system only judges objective
communication performance based on public data and user feedback, and does not make arbitrary
inference on sales conversion and loyalty changes without data support.
2.1.3. UGC (user generated content) communication
UGC communication refers to the decentralized social dissemination behavior in which ordinary
users independently create, edit, and publish relevant content based on brand themes and activity
rules under the guidance of official brand activities [8]. Different from official OGC, UGC content is
created from the perspective of ordinary users, with stronger daily sense, authenticity, and social
affinity, which can break the single communication perspective of official brands and form a multi-
dimensional brand public communication matrix.
2.2. Theoretical basis and practical application in this research
2.2.1. Emotional marketing theory
Emotional marketing theory points out that in the product homogenization market environment
where product functional differences are weakened, the core competition of brand marketing no
longer depends on product quality and functional advantages, but on the emotional resonance and
value identification established between brands and consumers through scene creation and
emotional output [6]. Consumers' memory and preference for brands mostly come from positive
emotional experiences in daily scenes rather than single product use feelings.
In this research, emotional marketing theory is directly used to analyze the content design logic
of Coca-Cola's daily life short videos and festival-themed videos. Coca-Cola abandons rigid product
function promotion and implants brand symbols into warm scenes such as family reunion, friend
gathering, and youth growth, so as to build a stable positive emotional association between the
public and the brand. This theory explains why emotion-oriented content creation has always been
the core strategy of Coca-Cola's short-video marketing, and also provides a theoretical basis for
analyzing the advantages and deficiencies of brand emotional content expression.
2.2.2. UGC user communication theory
UGC communication theory emphasizes that users are the main body of secondary communication
in social media platforms. Brand official activities are only the starting point of communication, and
user spontaneous creation and sharing are the key links to expand brand communication scope and

enthusiasm of mass users, and massive user-generated content can form continuous viral

2.2.3. Brand image shaping theory
Brand image is a dynamic public cognitive system gradually formed by the public through long-
term contact with brand information, activity participation, and emotional experience [7]. Classic
time-honored brands are prone to solidified aging brand cognition in the process of long-term
market operation. New-media short-video marketing can reshape the brand's public temperament
through trendy content creation, youth scene layout and interesting interactive forms, narrow the
cognitive gap between traditional brands and young consumers, and realize the iterative upgrading
of brand image.
This theory runs through the whole process of brand communication effect analysis. This study
analyzes how Coca-Cola optimizes its youthful, fashionable, and life-oriented brand image through
short-video content, and also explains why single and homogenized emotional content will restrict
the further upgrading of brand image.
2.3. Theoretical application limitations
In order to ensure academic rigor, this study clarifies the applicable boundaries of the above
theories. Traditional marketing theories are summarized based on the traditional media
communication environment. In the short-video digital scenario, the effect of theoretical application
will be restricted by platform algorithms, user aesthetic changes, and content innovation degree.
Therefore, this study does not mechanically apply theories, but combines the actual performance of
brand cases to make flexible analysis and objective judgment.
3. Marketing environment analysis of fast-moving consumer goods brands in the short-video
era
3.1. Platform advantages of short-video new-media communication
Short-video platforms have formed a complete digital communication ecosystem covering all age
groups, especially occupying the absolute dominant position in the youth consumer market aged 18
to 30. Different from the blind delivery mode of traditional advertising, short-video platforms rely
on big data algorithm recommendation technology to realize accurate push according to user age,
preference, browsing habits, and regional characteristics, which greatly improves the matching
degree between brand content and audience groups [9].
At the same time, short‑video content has strong social attributes. High‑quality brand content can
realize multi‑level secondary dissemination through user likes, comments and reposts, forming a
continuous viral communication effect. The low creation threshold and diverse content forms of

3.2. Inherent limitations of traditional marketing modes
Traditional brand marketing represented by television advertising, outdoor billboards, and print
media has obvious lag and limitations in adapting to the modern youth market. First, the cost of
traditional advertising production and delivery is high, and the investment cycle is long, which

understand public evaluation and emotional attitude towards marketing content. Third, the form of

contemporary young consumers, and easy to aggravate the aging solidification of brand public
image [1].

Most century-old fast-moving consumer goods brands face the common dilemma of aging public
cognition and single brand temperament. Young consumers generally believe that traditional classic
brands lack youth vitality and trend sense. Short-video marketing breaks the fixed form of
traditional brand promotion. Brands can integrate youth culture, daily life scenes, and social hot
topics into content creation, so that classic brands can get rid of the old-fashioned label, establish a
close connection with young groups, and maintain the long-term market vitality of brands in the
increasingly competitive consumer market.

4.5. Summary of Coca-Cola's short-video marketing core strategies
Based on the above four typical localized cases and theoretical analysis, Coca-Cola's short-video
new-media marketing has formed four stable and systematic core strategies adapting to the Chinese
market:
First, emotion-oriented content creation. The brand abandons hard-selling promotion logic, takes
daily warmth, festival reunion, and youth vitality as the core emotional themes, and builds brand
emotional barriers through scene-based emotional narrative.
Second, hot-node linkage marketing. Closely combine seasonal consumption hot spots,
traditional festival nodes, and youth campus hot rhythms to launch phased themed campaigns,
maintaining the long-term online activity of the brand.
Third, low-threshold UGC interactive activation. Relying on platform characteristics to launch
user creation challenges, mobilize mass users to participate in brand communication, and form
decentralized viral dissemination.
Fourth, multi-platform differentiated layout. Form targeted content styles for Douyin public
domain trend communication, Xiaohongshu creative life sharing, and WeChat private domain
emotional dissemination to adapt to different user group characteristics.

5.2. Existing objective deficiencies and potential problems (prudent academic expression, no
unsubstantiated negative judgment)
5.2.1. Partial content homogenization and single narrative logic
Most of Coca-Cola's emotional short-video content adopts similar daily warm scene creation modes.
The narrative framework of family reunion, friend gathering, and daily relaxation is relatively single
and repetitive. Long-term homogenized content creation is easy to cause user aesthetic fatigue,
gradually reduce the novelty and attraction of brand new-media content, and restrict the continuous
growth of brand topic heat and public attention.
5.2.2. Imbalanced proportion between emotional output and product display
In order to maintain warm emotional brand temperament, Coca-Cola's short-video content
excessively focuses on emotional narrative and scene atmosphere creation. Most videos do not
involve product characteristic display, new product experience introduction, and scenario-based
product promotion. The excessive separation between brand emotional communication and product
information output makes it difficult for new-media content to feed back product publicity, resulting
in limited comprehensive marketing value of short-video campaigns.

5.2.3. Shallow user participation and lack of long-term interactive mechanism
The brand's current UGC interactive activities are mostly short-term phased challenge campaigns.
User participation is mostly driven by activity rewards and fresh experience. Most users only

Limited by the openness of public data, this study can only evaluate the brand communication effect

video marketing and product sales growth, consumer purchase intention improvement, and user
loyalty enhancement. All conclusions in this study are limited to the public new-media
communication level, and no excessive causal inference is made.
6. Targeted optimization suggestions for Coca-Cola's short-video marketing
Based on the objective data of the four cases and the neutral analysis of advantages and deficiencies,
combined with emotional marketing, UGC communication, and brand image shaping theories, this
study puts forward four targeted and operable optimization suggestions for the brand's new-media
marketing:
6.1. Enrich content themes to alleviate homogenization
On the basis of retaining the classic warm emotional content that conforms to the brand gene, Coca-
Cola can further enrich the diversity of short-video content themes. The brand can appropriately add
environmental public welfare content, cross-border cooperative creation, youth trend culture

7. Conclusion
7.1. Research conclusion
This study takes four typical localized short-video marketing campaigns of Coca-Cola from 2023 to
2025 as the research object, takes emotional marketing theory, UGC communication theory, and
brand image shaping theory as the theoretical framework, and uses literature research, case analysis,
and comparative analysis to systematically explore the practical effect, strategic characteristics, and
existing deficiencies of classic beverage brand short-video marketing.
The research finds that short-video new-media marketing has obvious innovative advantages over
traditional one-way advertising in expanding brand public exposure, activating user public
interaction, and optimizing youthful brand image. Coca-Cola has realized efficient new-media brand
communication through stable emotional content creation, hot-node campaign operation and multi-
platform UGC interactive activation, effectively improving the public popularity and youthful
temperament of the century-old brand in the youth consumer market [1, 2].
At the same time, the brand's current short-video marketing still has objective deficiencies such
as partial content homogenization, insufficient integration of emotional communication and product
promotion, and shallow long-term user participation. Restricted by public data conditions, this study
cannot verify the direct impact of short-video marketing on product sales and consumer loyalty, and
all research conclusions are limited to the objective evaluation of new-media brand communication
effects.
In general, short-video marketing is an indispensable and effective digital transformation path for
traditional classic fast-moving consumer goods brands. Reasonable new-media content layout and
interactive operation can effectively help old brands break aging cognition and realize youthful
market transformation.

First, the data sources of this study are limited to public platform operational data and open user
feedback information. There is no offline questionnaire survey design and longitudinal sales data
verification, so the evaluation of marketing effect is limited to the macroscopic public
