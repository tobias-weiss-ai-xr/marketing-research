Abstract:
The competitive marketing environment has changed as businesses increasingly use big data analytics
(BDA), but there is limited theoretical understanding of how raw data translates into measurable
marketing results. The paper introduces a five -stage process model showing how BDA data streams
transform into sustainable marketing results through intelligence transformation as the mediating
component. The model developed from Resource-Based View (RBV), Dynamic Capabilities Theory, and
Data-Information-Knowledge-Wisdom (DIKW) hierarchy principles was used to conduct systematic
literature synthesis through peer -reviewed sources between 2018 and 2024. It was created through
structured literature synthesis, identifying 76 sources via PRISMA -based screening from Web  of
Science, Scopus, and Google Scholar. The model identifies intelligence transformation as the pivotal
mediating stage at which the BDA -performance relationship is most decisively shaped. The study
presents five research -based hypotheses that identify or ganizational absorptive capacity and data
governance maturity as crucial factors that define the study's limits. The study results provide a step-by-
step implementation guide and KPI framework to help marketing managers and chief data officers
achieve their strategic and sustainable marketing goals through BDA investments, while the research
presents a unified theoretical framework that shows how organized data sensing develops into sustained
competitive advantage.

Introduction
The digital market transformation has produced an exceptional volume of data which people generate
through their social media usage, e -commerce purchases, mobile app interactions, Internet of Things
(IoT) device operations and business system data processi ng. Organizations now function in what
researchers refer to as a data-intensive environment which presents them with information that exceeds
traditional marketing research methods to conduct their analysis (Fosso Wamba et al., 2019; Lim, Yap,
& Makkar, 2020). The business world has adopted big data analytics (BDA) as its primary management
and technology focus because it enables companies to transform their core informational assets into
market advantages (Bag et al., 2021; Gupta et al., 2020).
The BDA capabilities function as a core component of marketing operations which directly deals with
customer behavior through its research base. The existing research base shows empirical evidence that
BDA adoption leads to better marketing results which h elp businesses improve customer acquisition
and retention and brand equity and revenue growth (Arunachalam, Kumar, & Kawalek, 2018; Sheng et
al., 2019). The organizations which implement BDA capabilities achieve different marketing results
because the tech nology does not automatically lead to better marketing outcomes. The research
question requires investigation because it analyzes why certain firms achieve market leadership through
data investments while others fail to do so (Frizzo-Barker et al., 2020; Lim et al., 2020).
BDA research lags behind because existing studies present direct performance links between data
processing and marketing functions without explaining the organizational methods through which data
transforms into business intelligence (Amado et al., 2018; G upta et al., 2020). The intelligence
transformation framework remains under theoretical investigation despite its function as a core
component of BDA performance research, which transforms basic data into market-relevant knowledge.
Researchers have yet to create a process model which shows how organizational absorptive capacity
and data governance maturity and cross-functional knowledge sharing work together to enable effective
organizational transformation.

mediating element which functions through specific moderating factors. Researchers should advance
their work through five hypothesis -driven propositions which will facilitate future research work. The

construct which connects BDA with marketing performance measurement; (2) create a unified process

Big Data Analytics: Conceptualization and Marketing Dimensions
The academic literature on BDA in marketing has grown substantially over the past decade, transitioning
from early descriptive accounts of technological possibilities to increasingly rigorous empirical
investigations of performance consequences (Ozbayoglu, Gudelek, & Sezer, 2020; Goodell et al., 2021).
Amado et al. (2018) and Gupta et al. (2020) conducted systematic reviews which showed that
researchers focused on three primary themes: customer analytics and personalization, competitive
intelligence and mar keting mix optimization. The research demonstrates that organizations which
successfully implement analytics-driven insights in their marketing decisions achieve better results than
those who depend on instinctual judgments and conventional market research methods.

Theoretical Foundations
The study uses Dynamic Capabilities Theory (DCT) as its core basis because DCT states that
companies achieve long-term competitive advantages through their ability to detect environmental shifts
and to utilize newly available opportunities and to adjust th eir existing capabilities (Teece, Pisano, &
Shuen, 1997; Teece, 2007). The dynamic capabilities of organizations in BDA -marketing performance
relationships represent the processes through which businesses update market intelligence by tracking
real-time data and they use analytical results to modify their marketing approaches while they build
knowledge from their previous marketing efforts (Bag et al., 2021; Goodell et al., 2021).
The RBV (Barney, 1991) analysis of cognitive analytics establishes DCT as a framework which identifies
cognitive analytics as a permanent competitive advantage path -dependent capability that meets VRIN
requirements for achieving sustained competitive growt h because it helps businesses improve their
marketing decisions (Gupta et al., 2020). Multiple research studies in analytics and digital transformation
fields validate this theoretical framework. The study by Mikalef et al. (2021) showed that BDA capability
improved dynamic managerial capabilities and enhanced firm performance while Brewis et al. (2023)
found that strategic BDA implementation created a link between market orientation and competitive
advantage.
The DIKW hierarchy progresses from Data through Information and Knowledge to Wisdom which
provides an epistemological structure to describe the process that transforms BDA raw data into
marketing results (Rowley, 2007). Marketers assess the marketing poten tial of raw data through its
contextualization and pattern extraction because these processes create knowledge which marketers
use to develop strategic marketing plans (Fosso Wamba et al., 2019). The Information Processing
Theory established by Galbraith ( 1974) complements the existing framework by explaining how
organizations need to develop their processing abilities to handle large quantities of information which
directly applies to BDA governance and dissemination requirements.
Intelligence Transformation

organizational procedures which the organization uses to turn raw data into marketing knowledge that
supports better marketing decision -making. Contemporary m arketing and strategy research employs
various related terms such as market sensing (Day, 1994) market intelligence generation (Kohli &
Jaworski, 1990) and analytical capability (Gupta et al., 2020), but these terms fail to explain properly
how BDA transfo rms data into intelligence through multiple technologically advanced stages. The

Organizational Moderators of the BDA-Performance Relationship
The expanding research area studies how BDA produces performance results through organizational
and contextual elements which either enhance or limit BDA performance outcomes. The ability of a firm
to recognize and absorb and utilize external knowledge emerges as the key ability which multiple studies
identify as an essential moderating factor. The research by Lim et al. (2020) established that
organizations with strong absorptive capacity can better transform analytics content into their marketing
strategy, while Gupta et al. (2020) found that organizations which can absorb technology -based
knowledge will match their data quality with market performance results.
The BDA-performance relationship shows moderation through data governance maturity which defines
data management processes that include mechanisms for maintaining data quality and safeguarding
data and controlling data throughout its entire existence (Friz zo-Barker et al., 2020). The rate of
intelligence transformation depends on technological infrastructure quality which consists of cloud
computing capacity and integration middleware and real -time processing systems according to Fosso
Wamba et al. (2019) and Bag et al. (2021). The study presents three theoretical constructs which explain
the connections between BDA dimensions and intelligence transformation and its moderators and their
theoretical foundations in Figure 1, which shows how these constructs in teract with each other in the
proposed process model.

Research Methodology
The study establishes its original contribution through the identification of theoretical gaps, which
emerged from our complete examination of existing BDA -marketing literature. The existing research
shows direct links between capabilities and performance yet fails to demonstrate how organizations
transform these capabilities into results. The present study addresses this research gap through the
development of a conceptual framework which draws on various theoretical perspectives. The current
study follows  the established theory -building procedures of both information systems research and
marketing strategy research (Amado et al., 2018; Fosso Wamba et al., 2019).

The development of the conceptual model occurred through three distinct operational phases of work.
The first phase used systematic literature search methods to examine Web of Science and Scopus and
Google Scholar with the search terms 'big data analytics' and 'marketing performance' and 'intelligence
transformation' and 'dynamic capabilities' and 'data -driven marketing' while limiting results to peer -
reviewed articles published between 2018 and 2024. The literature obtained from the second phase was
synthesized by applying process-theory logic (Van de Ven, 1992) which recognizes temporal order and
causal pathways and defines the environmental factors that determine process results. The third phase
established five theoretical propositions which researchers can utilize to conduct future studies by
following the theory -building method that prior research validated in this field (Teece, 2007; Rowley,
2007).
The literature search process followed PRISMA-adapted reporting conventions as established by Moher
and other researchers in 2009. An initial retrieval across the three databases returned 2,314 records.
The title and abstract screening process decreased th e number of candidate articles to 263 full -text
articles after deduplication (n = 1,891 unique records) emerged from the deduplication process. The
study excluded 198 articles because they failed to demonstrate a BDA -marketing performance
relationship (n =  83) or they focused solely on technical content without marketing or organizational
effects (n = 64) or they lacked necessary theoretical support from strategic management or information
systems models (n = 31) or their publication sites had rankings lowe r than ABDC B or Scopus Q2
standard (n = 20). The remaining 65 articles together with 11 key pre -2018 foundational works that
researchers discovered through citation snowballing created the synthesis corpus which consisted of
76 total sources. The structur ed extraction template was used to code each source for BDA construct
conceptualization and intelligence transformation operationalization and marketing performance
outcome(s) and theoretical framework(s) and methodology and industrial and geographic conte xt. The
research team conducted coding of a random 20% subsample (n = 15 empirical articles) which a second
researcher evaluated independently leading to a result of Cohen's kappa κ = 0.79 for BDA dimension
coding and κ = 0.74 for theoretical framework coding which both surpassed the substantial agreement
threshold of 0.70 (Landis & Koch, 1977).
The paper introduces a methodological innovation by introducing a triangulated multi-theory proposition
derivation approach which separates it from earlier BDA -marketing research studies that based their
findings on a single theoretical framework. The five  model stages produced propositions through three
theoretical traditions which included RBV and Dynamic Capabilities Theory and the DIKW -Information
Processing nexus before they underwent testing to confirm cross -framework consistency. Propositions
received advancement with high confidence only when they received theoretical backing from at least
two of the three frameworks; those backed by a single framework were marked exploratory and received
priority for empirical research. The triangulation logic follows epistemological standards that Eisenhardt

The Five-Stage Conceptual Process Model
The first stage data acquisition and integration begins with systematic data collection which gathers raw
data from various internal customer relationship management (CRM) systems and enterprise resource
planning databases and social media platforms and digital advertising platforms and IoT sensors and
third-party market data providers. The primary task during this phase involves building an integrated
data architecture which organizations commonly represent as either a data lake or data warehouse
system that merges various data streams into one single data sto rage location which downstream
analysis functions can use (Fosso Wamba et al., 2019; Gupta et al., 2020). The RBV framework
identifies an organization’s data acquisition capabilities as the main source which provides
organizational advantages through informational resource development.
The second stage, data processing and governance, transforms raw information into proper datasets
which organizations can analyze through data cleaning and data deduplication and data normalization
and data enrichment processes. The stage works to establish data quality standards through which data
accuracy and data completeness and data consistency and data timeliness are evaluated into the first
dependable data transformation method (Frizzo -Barker et al., 2020; Fosso Wamba et al., 2019). The
data governance frameworks create the institutional data framework which establishes policies for data
ownership and data access control and data quality standards and regulatory compliance management
through the General Data Protection Regulation enforcement particularities.

processed data undergoes advanced analytical examination which includes machine learning and deep

from Stage 5 back to Stage 1 because academic research shows that advanced BDA -powered
marketing systems operate as continuous learning systems (Sheng et al., 2019; Lim et al., 2020).

framework establishes a theoretical connection which shows how organizations achieve better
performance through BDA since they transform data into useful knowledge (Rowley, 2007; Teece, 2007).
Bag et al. (2021) provide partial empirical support for Proposition 2 because their research shows that
analytics capability mediates the connection between BDA investment and marketing performance. The
mediating pathway shows that businesses which invest in BDA infrastructure but fail to establish
matching intelligence transformation capabilities will consistently underperfo rm their expected outcome
which has significant implications for practical purposes. A hypothetical vignette illustrates the contrast
between two data types and their transformation pathways. A financial services firm processing social
media sentiment data  (unstructured, high -variety BDA) must apply natural language processing and
topic modelling at Stage 3 to extract actionable signals about brand perception and competitor
positioning. The same company uses propensity modeling and churn prediction algorith ms to analyze
its transactional account data which falls into the structured, high -volume BDA category. Both parties
start with Stage 1 by processing basic BDA data yet their intelligence transformation path diverges after
reaching Stage 3 as they require different methods and tools and organizational capabilities which
demonstrates that P2 mediates through channels which depend on data type.
Proposition 3 (P3) addresses the moderating role of organizational absorptive capacity. The
organizations which produce high -quality market intelligence will achieve better marketing results only
when they develop the skills to absorb and utilize the acquired intelligence. The core concept of Cohen
and Levinthal (1990) explains how marketers must understand the value of analytical insights before
they can use them in their strategic marketing decisions (Lim et al., 2020). The hypothetical mid-market
consumer goods operator invested in cutting -edge predictive analytics platform which at Stage 3
produced weekly forecasts for upcoming demand and performance projections across product
categories. The marketing team will continue using intuitive campaign planning  because they lack data
literacy skills which results in intelligence outputs at Stage 4 staying dormant on dashboards without
affecting Stage 5 execution. The competitor who has a basic analytics platform yet demonstrates strong
absorptive capacity throug h data-literate marketing managers and a defined analytics review system
will achieve greater marketing success from inferior intelligence resources. This vignette directly
operationalizes P3’s moderation claim.

Discussion
The research demonstrates that organizations will derive different marketing performance advantages
from their BDA expenditures which leads to different results because of the existing empirical evidence
(Amado et al., 2018; Frizzo-Barker et al., 2020). Organizations with intelligence transformation as their
main mediating process will separate themselves from their competitors who rely primarily on data to
create knowledge. The main theoretical assertion explains how intelligence transformation enables BDA
to boost organizational performance which supports both Bag et al. (2021) and Sheng et al. (2019) about
how analytical capabilities influence performance and advanced analytical methods impact customer
relationship management success respectively.
The inclusion of organizational absorptive capacity as a moderator reflects a broader recognition in the
digital transformation literature that technology investments must be accompanied by organizational
capability development to realize their value (Brew is et al., 2023; Goodell et al., 2021). This finding
supports the argument that the competitive advantage from BDA is culturally and organizationally
conditioned rather than purely technical. Managers who invest in data -literacy programs, cross -
functional analytics governance structures and evidence -based decision cultures will amplify the
marketing returns from BDA investments beyond what the technology alone can deliver.

Summary
The created process model explains how big data analytics leads to sustainable marketing performance
through intelligence transformation. Theoretical foundations supported the identification of five
operational phases which included data acquisition and integration, d ata processing and governance,
intelligence transformation, strategic dissemination and marketing execution. Th e established five
research propositions direct future studies which will identify intelligence transformation as the crucial
asset when BDA systems create their strongest impact on performance results. The specific objectives
permitted the following outcomes: (1) the development of intelligence transformation as a separate
exists multiple stages which operate as a mediating construct between BDA and marketing performance;
(2) the creation of a unified five -stage process model which integrates the RBV Dynamic Capabilities
Theory the DIKW hierarchy Information Processing Theory and Organizational Learning Theory; (3) the
derivation of five propositio ns which establish theoretical connections between each stage and
organizational as well as marketing outcomes. The study successfully achieved its specific objectives
while making important contributions at practical managerial theoretical and social leve ls to the field of
data-driven marketing research. The research demonstrates that intelligence transformation serves as
the primary stage which creates value for enterprises when they implement BDA processes toward
performance enhancement because intellige nce transformation functions as the core value -creating
stage. Executives should allocate resources to develop analytical talent while establishing data literacy
programs and cross-functional knowledge-sharing programs and data governance systems. Executives
must comprehend that their enterprise needs to enhance BDA-based transformation and dissemination
capabilities to achieve maximum marketing performance returns. The model establishes its first stage -
specific operationalization framework for intelligence  transformation in marketing contexts which
organizations can use to evaluate their current maturity at each pipeline stage through the KPI structure
developed in the Discussion section. The research develops a process model which uses multiple
theoretical frameworks to define intelligence transformation as a unique conceptual element. The
academic literature lacks sufficient exploration of this combination of concepts because researchers
need to understand how analytics produce market impact instead of mer ely increasing data volume.
The social contribution of this research demonstrates how organizations achieve BDA -driven
sustainable marketing excellence through continuous improvement which requires ongoing investment
in both technical systems and human analytical talent.

Theoretical Implications and Further Research
The five-stage model propose d serves to enhance existing BDA and marketing theoretical research.
The model establishes intelligence transformation as a distinct mediating construct which enables
researchers to study BDA performance through different causal pathways that require separa te
empirical testing. The development of a unified process framework which combines these theories RBV,
Dynamic Capabilities Theory, the DIKW hierarchy, Information Processing Theory and Organizational
Learning Theory constitut es a theoretical achievement which has not been accomplished in this
research area.
The research extends BDA-marketing boundary condition theorizing through its inclusion of absorptive
capacity and data governance maturity as moderators which enable organizations to create analytical
value through their data, instead of being restricted b y technological requirements. The model shows
that intelligence-driven marketing and sustainable competitive advantage for sustainability scholarship
function as mutual benefits which create competitive advantage, when Stage 3 outputs fulfill
requirements for long-term value assessment and stakeholder value assessment alongside marketing
efficiency measurement. The paper presents its main restriction through its conceptual framework which
blocks any causal claims until researchers validate them through empirical studies. The five-stage model
creates a structured linear approach for processes while real -world applications o f the model include
multiple parallel operations, recursive processes and non -linear execution methods. Future research
should focus on how organizations use intelligence transformation to improve their marketing operations
through empirical studies that use longitudinal surveys, experimental methods or case studies. The
research will investigate how the intelligence transformation pathway w orks across different industries
by examining its operation in service and manufacturing contexts, B2B and B2C environments,
emerging and advanced markets, while also determining whether SME -scale implementations need a
different model which uses alternati ve stage sequencing methods. The proposed model boundary
conditions will be better understood through future research which investigates how sustainability goals
should be officially implemented in Stage 3 intelligence design processes.
