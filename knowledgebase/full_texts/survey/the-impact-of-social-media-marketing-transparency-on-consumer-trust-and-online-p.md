ABSTRACT
This study investigated the relationship between social media marketing transparency and consumer trust and online purchase intention in a sample of
respondentsfrom Guntur,Andhra Pradesh. This studyutilizedaquestionnaire data file. One header rowwasduplicatedin thequestionnaire data.Afterremoving
this row, 103 unique respondents remained. Each respondent completed questions regarding their profile, as well as 5-point Likert-style items assessing
transparency, trust and purchase intention. Descriptive statistics, reliability estimates (Cronbach's alpha), Pearson correlation coefficients and ordinary least
squares (OLS) regression models were utilized to analyze the data. Average ratings were 3.41/5 for perceived transparency, 3.39/5 forconsumer trust and3.37/5
for online purchase intention. Transparency was significantly correlated with trust (r = 0.81, p < .001) and purchase intention (r = 0.84, p < .001). Additionally,
trust wassignificantly correlated with purchase intention (r= 0.83, p <.001). While the relationships identified inthis study represent significant relationships in
the present sample, the survey methodology does not allow for determining causality or inferring that the relationships identified in this sample exist for all
consumers in Guntur. The findings of this study support the practical utility of providing consumers with accurate product information, clear offers, and
transparentpromotionalcommunications.

1. Introduction of the Study
Social media platforms have emerged as a significant source of product discovery, brand communication, review generation (customer reviews),
influencerpromotion,anddecisionmakingregardingpurchases madeviae-commerce. As consumers browse socialmedia,they will likelybeexposed
tosponsoredpostsbybrandsand/orinfluencers;videodemonstrationsofproducts;specialoffersanddiscounts;advertisements;and/orbrand-generated
content. Given that consumers are generally unable to examine products in person prior to purchasing them on-line, the level of transparency and
credibilityprovidedthroughdigitalchannelscangreatlyinfluencehowconsumersevaluateabrand.Inthecontextofthisresearchproject,socialmedia
marketing transparency refers to how clearly brands provide information about their promotional efforts including product features, pricing, discount
availability, andapplicableterms andconditions.Transparentcommunicationenables consumers toclearly identifywhatis beingpromoted,andunder
which circumstances. Conversely, lack of transparency regarding sponsorshipdisclosure or incomplete information about a particularoffer maycause
uncertainty among consumers. Consumer trust refers to the degree of confidence consumers have in a brand's honesty, reliability, and ability to fulfill
its promises. Online purchase intention refers to a survey participant's stated probability of considering, visiting, purchasing from or recommending a
brand based upon their experience with the brand through social media.Although these two concepts are interrelated, they are distinctly separate: a
statedintentionorpositiveperceptiondoes notnecessarilyleadtoanactualpurchase. ThisresearchprojectwasconductedinGuntur, AndhraPradesh.
Theresearcherutilizedtheavailablequestionnairedatatoinvestigatetherelationshipsandperceptionsamongthesevariables.

discounts, andterms).Additionally, the draft states thattrust and purchaseintentionhave been examined extensively inprior studies; however, there is
limited evidence regarding the combined effect of these two variables on the overall level of transparency in social media marketing.As a result, the
authors identified a need for additional evidence regarding this gap specifically for consumers in Guntur. In order to address this gap, the authors

5. Research Methodology
5.1 Research design
The researcher employed a quantitative, cross-sectional, descriptive-correlational research design. Data were collected from participants at a single
pointintimetoinvestigaterelationshipsamongvariablesratherthantoestablishcause-and-effectrelationships.
5.2 Sample design
The usable data set included 103 survey responses. The provided data file did not contain information about the sampling method employed (e.g.,
whether itwas random)and therefore, the sample is considered to bea convenience/voluntary response sample when interpretingresults. As such,the
resultscouldhave beeninfluencedbyparticipantself-selection biasandtherefore maynotaccurately reflect thecharacteristicsoftheentirepopulation
ofGuntur.
5.3 Data collection
The data set provided in this study was derived from a structured questionnaire that had been exported into a comma separated value (CSV) file. The
questionnaire contained questions regarding respondent characteristics, social media usage habits, ten transparency related factors, seven trust related
factorsamongconsumers,sixonlinepurchasingintentionrelatedfactorsandonegeneralquestionabouthowimportanttransparencyistotheconsumer.

10. Conclusion

anindividual'sintenttopurchaseonlineareallrelatedpositively.Themajorityofrespondentsindicatedmoderatepositiveviewsregardingsocialmedia
