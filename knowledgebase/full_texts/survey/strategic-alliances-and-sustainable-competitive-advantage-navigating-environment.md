ABSTRACT
Nigeria’s oil and gas sector is confronted with significant environmental turbulence with consequential disruptive
changes that practically vitiate the capacity of corporate organizations to maintain competitiveness. Navigating this
volatile business horizon requires firms in this sector to – as a matter of corporate strategy – resort to tactical alliances
and partnerships to acquire and boost adaptive resilience, thus provoking an intellectual debate on the empirical utility
of this strategic initiative. T his study examines the nexus between strategic alliances and sustainable competitive
advantage of quoted oil and gas firms in Rivers State, Nigeria. Strategic Alliance - the explanatory variable - as herein
conceptualized, trifurcates into three operational dimensions of Technology, Marketing, and Supply Chain.  Sustainable
Competitive Advantage  – the Criterion variable - is on the other hand operationalized as Innovation Capability  and
Operational Efficiency. The study which is domiciled in the Resource - Based View and Relational View theories, adopts
a cross-sectional survey research design. Spearman's rank correlation coefficient and partial correlation are utilized to
analyze data obtained from 265 management staff across the eight qualifying firms admi nistered with closed -ended
questionnaire. Results reveal that all the three alliances have significant and positive relationships with the two
measures of sustainable competitive advantage, with technology alliance  as the strongest predictor of innovation
capability and marketing alliance  as the strongest determinant of operational efficiency . The results also reveal
organizational agility as partially moderating the relationship between strategic alliances and sustainable competitive
advantage. The study concludes, therefore, that strategic alliances supported by organizational agility have s ignificant
influence on sustainable competitive advantage. In the light of the findings, it recommends greater investment in
technology partnerships, more effective col laborative marketing efforts, partnerships in the supply chain and agility -
enhancing organizational structures.
Keywords: Technology Alliance, Marketing Alliance, Supply Chain Alliance, Sustainable Competitive Advantage,

1 INTRODUCTION
In the present dynamic business environment, organizations have been forced to have collaborative strategic
approaches that will help to improve their long-term competitiveness and sustainability of their organization. However,
the world's globalization, t echnological progress, customer expectations and the environmental uncertainties have
made it difficult to only use internal resources to achieve competitiveness. For these reasons, strategic alliances have
emerged as a significant way in which companies g et access to complementary resources, exchange knowledge, and
reinforce their organizations' capabilities (Barney, 1991). Dyer and Singh (1998) suggest that collaborative
relationships can be used by organisations to develop relational advantages that are hard to replicate amongst
competitors. In recent years, Zupanc and Logožar (2025) have also emphasized the strategic alliances to enhance the
resilience of organizations, describing how they contribute to innovation, knowledge sharing, and the optimization  of
resources within the participating organizations.
Oil and gas companies quoted in Rivers State, Nigeria, operate in a highly competitive and uncertain business
environment as they deal with volatile oil prices, technological advancements, disruption in the supply chain and
growing regulatory requirements. All these challenges have increased the importance of collaborative solutions which
increase the adaptability and performance of organizations. Kotabe and Helsen (2022) pointed out that strategic
alliances provide companies with an added advantage to pool complementary resources and minimize risks associated
with operations while increasing market prospects. Likewise, Porter (1985) argued that organization achieve and
maintain a superior performance over a sustained period of time by continually making improvements in efficiency and
differentiation. These realities have given strategic alliance between oil and gas companies to sustain their presence in
the new energy market in Nigeria more relevance.
Technology alliances, marketing alliances and supply chain alliances are the typical expression of strategic alliances.
Technology alliances encourage organization's access to more advanced technologies, research expertise and
innovation capabilities, which enhance organizational learning (Lawson & Samson, 2001). Marketing alliances provide
an increased reach to customers, brand visibility, and market expansion by engaging in joint marketing activities
(Kotabe & Helsen, 2022). Similarly, in supply chain all iances, coordination between the supply chain partners and
suppliers helps to lower the operation costs and enhance the service delivery (Min et al., 2005). From the results, Yang
et al. (2015) concluded that these dimensions of the alliance are important to enhance firms' sustainable competitive
capabilities.
1.1 Statement of the Problem
The ability of the firm to continually innovate and function efficiently amidst changes in the environment is an indicator
of sustainable competitive advantage. The capability to innovate helps firms develop better products, services and
processes to meet evolving market needs (Calantone et al., 2002; Wang & Ahmed, 2004), and operational efficiency
helps improve productivity by effectively using resources and organizing business processes (Li et al., 2006; Flynn et
al., 2010). But the benefits can only be achieved in the context of organizational agility, defined as the ability to "sense"
changes in the environment and quickly re -align resources to meet new opportunities (Sharifi & Zhang, 1999). Thus,
the researchers Tallon and Pinsonneault (2011) and Ciampi  et al. (2021) highlighted that for the agile organizations,
they can get more benefit from strategic alliance compared to other types of organizations, thus the interaction between
strategic alliance and sustainable competitive advantage can be considered  as a moderator.
Although there are existing Nigerian studies that revealed that strategic alliances enhance the outcome of an
organisation, there are certain gaps that need to be identified. For instance, Horsfall and Wills (2021) focused on Supply
Chain Collaboration and  Sustainable Competitive Advantage of Energy Companies in Rivers State, Nigeria and Agabe
and Akhigbe (2024) studied Strategic Alliance capability and Competitive Advantage of Telcos in Rivers State. In the
same vein, Wokocha and Madukasi (2024) investigat ed strategic alliances and organizational growth of multinational
oil and gas companies while Onwuchekwa (2025) studied the supply chain strategies and the effectiveness of oil and
gas companies in Rivers State. While these studies contributed to understan ding of the collaborative strategies, they
mostly focused on the organizational growth, effectiveness and overall competitive advantage instead of sustainable
competitive advantage based on innovation capability and operational efficiency. In addition, the re was a lack of
integrated dimensions of technology alliance, marketing alliance, and supply chain alliance in operationalizing the
strategic alliances. Again, there is scarcity of empirical evidence on the effect of Strategic Alliance on Sustainable
Competitive Advantage (SCA) of quoted Oil and Gas Companies in Rivers state through the lens of Organisational Agility.
This study aims to fill these gaps by studying the effect of technology alliance, marketing alliance and supply chain
alliance on the innova tion capability and operational efficiency, taking the organizational agility as the moderating
variable.

• examine the nexus between technology alliance and innovation capability;
• investigate the nexus between marketing alliance and innovation capability;
• examine the nexus between supply chain alliance and innovation capability;
• examine the nexus between technology alliance and operational efficiency;
• investigate the nexus between marketing alliance and operational efficiency;

1.2 Research Questions

• Is there any significant relationship between marketing alliance and innovation capability of quoted oil and gas

• Does supply chain alliance  relate to significantly to innovation capability of quoted oil and gas firms in Rivers

2.1.1 Strategic Alliances
Strategic alliances are intentional and mutually beneficial strategic relationships that are voluntary and non -exclusive
between two or more independent organizations in which they share complementary resources, capabilities,
technologies or expertise to a ccomplish a common goal. In contrast to mergers and acquisitions, alliance partners
continue to operate as distinct entities and simply work together in research and development, technology transfer,
marketing, production, distribution and supply chain man agement. The main goal of strategic alliances is to do
something that is difficult or expensive for the alliance partners to do alone by sharing risks, costs, knowledge and
competencies (Ireland et al., 2002). Kotabe and Helsen (2022) claim that strategic alliances help companies enhance
their competitive position by acquiring new markets, technologies and organizational capabilities. Recent reviews also
highlight that strategic alliances have become important mechanisms of innovation, resilience and sustai nable
competitiveness of the organizations in very dynamic business environments.
Strategic alliances can be traced back to the realization that no one organization has all the resources and capabilities it
needs to be a successful competitor in today's more complex markets. Out of the resource -based view, alliances create
the opportunity for companies to gain access to valuable and complementary strategic resources that can contribute to
their organization's capabilities and competitive position (Barney, 1991). In a similar way, the inter -firm relationship-
oriented approach states that a competitive advantage can be found in the relation between firms, in the knowledge -
sharing routines, the complementary combination of resources, the governance and the trust between the firms (Dyer
& Singh, 1998). In addition to cost savings, current all iance literature shows that companies are using alliances to gain
increased speed in innovation, better digital transformation, and greater resilience of their supply chains, and to more
effectively cope with environmental uncertainty.

The nature of strategic alliances varies with the goals of the organizations. Alliances (technology, marketing, supply
chain) help firms to conduct joint research, technological development, innovation, marketing, and sharing of
distribution channels, prom otion expertise and customer knowledge; supply chain alliance helps firms to coordinate
the supplier, manufacturer, logistics firms, distributors and helps to enhance the operational performance (Min et al.,
2005). According to Yang et al. (2015), these al liance dimensions support to the learning and innovation capability of
the organizations by integrating and complementing resources and knowledge. As a result, strategic alliances are
increasingly recognized as strategic tools to obtain sustainable competi tive advantage, as well as improve innovation,
operational efficiency and organizational adaptability in industries where change is a constant occurrence, including
the oil and gas sector (Zupanc & Logožar, 2025; Kotabe & Helsen, 2022).
2.1.2 Sustainable Competitive Advantage
Sustainable competitive advantage is achieved when an organization is able to outperform other organizations with the
resources, capabilities, and competencies that are valuable, rare, difficult to imitate and well -organized. Temporary
competitive advantage can be easily reproduced by rivals, but sustainable competitive advantage allows firms to have
a competitive advantage over rivals even after changes in the business environment (Barney, 1991). According to Porter
(1985), organisations that are able to sustain competitive advantage will be cost leaders, differentiators or focusers that
create superior customer value. But, there is a growing focus in the strategic management literature that sustaining
competitive advantage is not only about positioning in the market but also the capacity of the organization to constantly
update and renew its strategic capabilities to align with changes in the environment (Grant, 2022). As a result,
sustainable competitive advantage is now a dynamic phenomenon in which organ izations developing resources,
learning and innovating new ways to operate, and carrying out operations effectively and efficiently are all key pieces
of the puzzle to long-term success (Teece, 2018; Barney, 2021).
Nowadays, organizations have understood that capabilities, which help them adapt, innovate and use resources
effectively, are the basis for sustainable competitive advantage, not just physical resources. In dynamic markets and a
rapidly changing technology , digital transformation and growing customer expectations are forcing companies to
constantly develop their products and services and transform their internal processes in order to stay competitive
(Teece, 2018). Wang and Ahmed (2004) asserted that the fi rm's organizational capabilities are the ability to allocate
resources effectively in order to accomplish strategic goals and Grant (2022) contended that firms remain competitive
by continuously renewing capabilities and strategic flexibility. Recent resea rch also shows that companies with more
developed innovations and efficient operational systems are more resilient, adaptable, and able to better perform on
the market than companies that mostly rely on tangible resources (Musa et al., 2024; Zupanc & Logož ar, 2025).
For this research, sustainable competitive advantage is defined as innovation capability and operation efficiency.
Innovation capability is defined as the ability of an organization to generate, adopt and commercialize new ideas,
technologies, products and  processes to create value and improve its competitive position (Lawson & Samson, 2001).
Further, Calantone et al. (2002) interpreted that companies that have high innovation capability are more responsive
about the impact of changes in customers' needs an d technological development. On the other hand, operational
efficiency is related with the organization's capability of optimizing resources, reducing operational costs, enhancing
quality and productivity along the value chain (Li et al., 2006). Flynn et al. (2010) proved that the operational efficiency
leads to better performance of the organization because of better coordination and integration of supply chain.
Therefore, companies that can enhance innovation capacity and operational efficiency together s tand a better chance
of acquiring sustainable competitive advantage in an industry with high competition like the Nigerian oil and gas
industry (Porter, 1985; Musa et al., 2024).
2.2 Theoretical Framework
2.2.1 Resource-Based View Theory
One of the most influential theories in the explanation of the achievement and maintenance of competitive advantage is
the resource-based view (RBV). This theory was first put forward by Wernerfelt (1984) and subsequently developed by
Barney (1991) who cla imed that the firm's internal resources and capabilities play an important role in determining
the firm's performance in addition to the market conditions. The theory states that a firm achieves sustainable
competitive advantage if it has resources that are valuable, rare, inimitable and non-substitutable (VRIN). These include
technological expertise, organizational knowledge, managerial capabilities, innovation competencies, and collaborative
relationships, which are difficult for other companies to obtain  or duplicate (Barney, 1991). Recent literature has a
greater focus on the fact that the strategic resources of firms have to be constantly developed and reconfigured to stay
competitive in dynamic business environments (Barney, 2021; Grant, 2022).
The resource-based view is very relevant to the current study as alliances are a way for the quoted oil and gas companies
to gain access to complementary resources and/or capabilities which may not be available in the firm. Technology
alliances can help to gain access to new or improved technologies and specific knowledge, marketing alliances can help

coordination and abilities (Kotabe & Helsen, 2022). These cooperative schemes increase innovation capability and

suggests that an agile organization enhances the firm's capacity to integrate, deploy, and exploit the resources it gains

resource-based view is very helpful in understanding the role of strategic alliances in attaining sustainable competitive

2.2.2 Relational View Theory
Dyer and Singh (1998) developed a theory of relational view that explains how organizations can achieve competitive
advantage not only by their resources but also by their interorganizational relationship. The theory suggests that
relational rents stems fr om collaborative partnerships, which are supernormal returns generated by the alliance
partners as they combine complementary resources, share knowledge, trust and coordinate through appropriate
governance structures. The relational view does not like the resource-based view is primarily interested in internal
organizational resources, but also considers that sustainable competitive advantage may also be found in strategic
relationships which cannot be easily imitated (Dyer & Singh, 1998). Later research ha s further supported this theory
and showed that collaborative networks contribute to organizational learning, innovation and operational excellence
in a more and more interconnected business world (Lavie, 2006; Kale & Singh, 2009).
Considering that the independent variable of this study is strategic alliance, which is inherently related to cooperative
relationships between organizations, the relational view theory is particularly suitable for this study. As for the forms
of alliances , technology alliances promote joint research and knowledge sharing, marketing alliances involve joint
marketing and value creation for customers, and supply chain alliances lead to better alliances among business partners
in terms of coordination, information sharing, and resource integration (Min et al., 2005; Kotabe & Helsen, 2022). From
these collaboration setups, companies gain more in innovation capability and operational efficiency than they would
otherwise be able to do on their own. Moreover, organizational agility helps companies quickly leverage the knowledge
and capabilities developed through strategic alliances to enhance the relational resources' role in sustainable
competitive advantage (Ciampi et al., 2021). Thus, the relational view theory o ffers a sound theoretical basis of the
relationships studied in this research on the quoted oil and gas companies in Rivers State, Nigeria (Yang et al., 2015;
Zupanc & Logožar, 2025).
2.3 Empirical Review
Okoh et al., (2025) studied the impact of strategic alliances on the performance of selected supermarkets in Nigeria. The
research design adopted was cross sectional survey research design, and the respondents selected were 10 leading
supermarkets with their head office in the cities of Lagos and Abuja. The researchers used stratified random sampling
and primary data were obtained by using a questionnaire with 5 Likert scale. The SPSS was used to perform descriptive
statistics, correlation analysis and mult iple regression analysis. The results from the regression indicated that
collaborative problem-solving positively and significantly influenced the organizational performance. The knowledge
sharing also had a significant and positive impact and the customer satisfaction positively and significantly affected the
organizational performance.
Tan and Adejuwon (2024) examined the impact of strategic alliances on the performance of Federal Inland Revenue
Service in Nigeria. The research was approached in a quantitative survey method and the primary data was collected
using online questionnaires f rom the senior staff of the Federal Inland Revenue Service in the South -West Zone. The
study involved 30 offices with the respondents selected by using purposive sampling technique with 279 respondents.
The predictor variable was strategic alliance and the organizational performance was measured in terms of tax revenue
generation, voluntary tax compliance and expansion of tax net. Simple percentages, means, standard deviations,
correlation coefficients, t-tests, regression analysis and analysis of variance were used to analyse the data. The findings
showed that strategic alliances positively and significantly affected the performance of Federal Inland Revenue Service.
The regression coefficient also showed that strategic alliance significantly predicted the organizational performance.
The study conducted by Al -Tabbaa and Zahoor (2024) explored the link between alliance management capability and
the internationalization of small and medium -sized enterprises (SMEs), focusing on the influence of incremental and
radical co-innovation. This research was based on the premise that even when firms enter alliances, it is not sufficient
to ensure better results unless they have the skills to coordinate alliance partners, manage the transfer of knowledge
within the alliance, address disagreements, and integrate alliance goals. The researchers tested a model that related
alliance management capability with two innovation pathways and then international expansion, with quantitative data
collected from small and medium -sized enterprises. The direct and  indirect relationships among the constructs were
estimated using the method of structural equation modeling. Results showed that the alliance management capability
positively reinforced incremental and radical co -innovation. Progressive developments of ol d products, technologies,
and processes through incremental co -innovation and creation of fundamental new products and processes through

Dubey et al. (2024) studied the role of alliance management capability and digital transformation conjointly enhance

Amah and Allagoa (2026) examined the relationship between strategic orientation and sustainable competitive
advantage in food and beverage companies in Rivers State, Nigeria. Sustainable competitive advantage , their
criterion variable, was assessed through three proxies - differentiation advantage, operational excellence, and
organizational responsiveness.  Employing a quantitative research design, data were collected from managers of
selected food and beverage firms using structured closed -ended questionnaire.  Kendall Rank Correlation and Partial
Correlation analyses were utilized by the researchers to test three hypothesized relationships. Their findings revealed
significant positive relationships between strategic orientation and the measures of sustainable competitive advantage.
Strategic orientation enhanced differentiation, operational excellence, and responsiveness. The study concluded that
strategic orientation substantially contributed to competitive sustainability in the dynamic food and beverage industry.
Based on the study’s findings, the authors recommended that firms foster long-term strategic vision to leverage the full
potential of strategic leadership in sustaining competitiveness.
A study by Amah and Diri (2025) investigated the relationship between strategic foresight and operational performance
of oil and gas firms in Rivers State. Strategic Foresight , the study’s predictor variable,  was opreationalized as
Environmenatal Scanning  and Strategic Choice – two critical dimensions of corporate strategy. On the other hand,
Organisational Agility and Timely Delivery was employed as proxies of Operational Performance, the criterion variable.
The population of the study consisted of top and middle level managers of the six major oil and gas companies in Rivers
State. Data obtained from all the 54 respondents in this category across the six firms studied were subjected to analysis
and a total of four hypotheses tested, using Spearman Rank Order Correlation. Results indicated that the two dimensions
of strategic foresight, namely Environmental Scanning  and Strategic Choice  both contributed significantly towards
achieving higher operational performance of oil and gas firms in Rivers State as measured by the two proxies employed
therein - Organisational Agility and Timely Delivery. The study concluded, therefore, that Strategic Foresight is a veritable
management tool for enhancing operational performance being – as revealed by the results - a strong predictor of the
latter

3 METHODOLOGY
The research design used was cross -sectional survey research. The design was suitable since the researcher was able
to collect quantitative data from the respondents at a specific time and analyse the correlation between strategic
alliances, sustainable competitive advantage and organisational agility without the manipulation of the study variables.
The design was deemed appropriate for research on management, specifically in relation to hypothesis testing and the
use of standardized questionnaires (Creswel l & Creswell, 2023; Saunders et al., 2023). The research was based on the
positivist philosophy. The objective of positivism is social phenomena can be measured objectively in terms of
observable facts and empirical evidence. The philosophy was for using s tructured questionnaires and statistical
procedures for testing hypotheses and establishing relationships among measurable variables (Creswell & Creswell,
2023; Saunders et al., 2023).
The study population consisted of 265 management staff of eight quoted oil and gas companies in Rivers State, Nigeria.
These companies comprised 11 Plc, Aradel Holdings Plc, Conoil Plc, Eterna Plc, MRS Oil Nigeria Plc, Oando Plc, Seplat
Energy Plc and Tota lEnergies Marketing Nigeria Plc. The number of people was taken from the Human Resource
Departments of each company in 2025 and included employees who had sufficient knowledge of alliance activities and
organizational performance. Due to smallness and acce ssibility of the population, the census sampling technique was
used. Therefore, there were 265 management employees as samples. The census approach removed sampling error due
to selecting a fraction of the population and increased the representativeness of  the results because all eligible
respondents were given an opportunity to participate (Sekaran & Bougie, 2020). The study was conducted through
primary data which was collected by using structured questionnaire technique from the respondents. The questionnaire
used closed-ended items on a five -point Likert scale with strongly disagree to strongly agree. The data collected were
of quantitative type since they indicated the respondents perception on the study variables.
Strategic alliances were used as the independent variable, and measured by technology alliance, marketing alliance, and
supply chain alliance. The dependent variable was sustainable competitive advantage and was measured by innovation
capability and operational efficiency. The moderating variable, organizational agility was measured by responsiveness,
adaptability and flexibility indicators based on literature. The content and face validity were determined by expert
assessment by the specialists in the fields of management and measurement. Cronbach's alpha coefficient was used for
instrument reliability, and only the constructs with reliability coefficients ≥the recommended level of 0.70 were used
(Hair et al., 2022). Descriptive statistics such as mean, sta ndard deviations, frequencies and percentages were used for
data analysis. The bivariate hypotheses were tested using Spearman's rank correlation coefficient, as the data were
ordinal, while the moderating effect of organizational agility was done at 0.05 level of significance using partial
correlation analysis (Field, 2024).

Using partial correlation analysis, the moderating effect of organizational agility on the relationship between strategic

agility had a correlation coefficient of 0.266 with sustainable competitive advantage. The probability value for both

with higher level strategic alliance capabilities also had higher level of organizational agility.
The relationship between strategic alliance and sustainable competitive advantage decreased slightly from 0.330 to

advantage was partially moderated by organizational agility, hence, it did not change the nature of the relationship but
made it more effective. Thus, more responsive, adaptable and flexible organizations were better able to transform
alliance resources and alliance capabilities into innovation capabili ty and operational efficiency.
5 DISCUSSION OF FINDINGS
Results from the Spearman's rank correlation analysis found that positive and statistically significant relationships
existed between strategic alliances and its two dimensions of sustainable competitive advantage, which are innovation
capability and opera tional efficiency. The results of the study showed that technology alliance (ρ = 0.536, p = 0.000),
marketing alliance (ρ = 0.416, p = 0.000), and supply chain alliance (ρ = 0.342, p = 0.000) all had significant positive
relationships with innovation capab ility. Likewise, technology alliance (ρ = 0.336, p = 0.000), marketing alliance (ρ =
0.568, p = 0.000) and supply chain alliance (ρ = 0.417, p = 0.000) had positive and significant relationship with
operational efficiency. These results suggest that improv ement in collaborative technological development, joint
marketing and supply chain partnership increased the innovation capability and operational efficiency in the quoted oil
and gas companies in Rivers State. The results agree with the resource-based view theory that organizations are able to
gain sustainable competitive advantage if they can access, combine, and deploy valuable strategic resources and
capabilities that competitors are unable to easily imitate (Barney, 1991; Grant, 2022).
Technology alliance has a positive association with innovation capability which indicates that firms with technology
alliance had more capability to introduce new improved products, processes and operational systems. Technology
partnerships provided access to complementary expertise, lowered the cost of innovation, and speeded up the learning
of technology, enhancing organizations' innovative capacity. This is in line with the suggestion of Lawson and Samson
(2001) that the ability to innovate can only be built up through learning in the organization and knowledge integration.
It also aligns with the results of Al -Tabbaa and Zahoor (2024) who found that the alliance management capability had
a significant positive impact on incremental and radical innovation  through collaborative knowledge creation and
sharing of resources between partner organisations. Additionally, the result is consistent with the evidence of Okafor et
al. (2026) which revealed that technology strategic alliance also had a positive and significant effect on the performance
of manufacturing firms in Ogun State, Nigeria, affirming the fact that technology strategic alliance is a way of enhancing
the capability of an organization and its competitiveness.
The results also showed the marketing alliance had positive and significant relationship with innovation capability and
operational efficiency. This relatively stronger relationship between marketing alliance and operational efficiency (ρ =
0.568) suggests that marketing alliance had strengthened the marketing intelligence, customer responsiveness, demand
forecasting and coordination of resources within the organization which in turn improved the operational efficiency.
Strategic Marketing Partnerships faci litated the exchange of customer data, enhanced distribution networks, and
boosted service delivery, contributing to overall organizational efficiency. This result aligns with Kotabe and Helsen
(2022) who posited that marketing alliances can lead to market  expansion and organizational effectiveness by
complementary competencies. The finding is also corroborated by the empirical evidence of Okafor et al., (2026) which
found that marketing strategic alliance significantly affected the performance of manufactu ring firms in Nigeria as
compared to technology strategic alliance, which had a lesser influence on the firm's performance. Likewise, Okoh et al.,
(2025) reported that co-operation with knowledge sharing, customer satisfaction, and problem-solving among selected
supermarkets in Nigeria positively and significantly affected the performance of the organization.
The positive and statistically significant relationships between supply chain alliance and innovation capability, as well
as supply chain alliance and operational efficiency were also identified. This result suggests that partnership with
suppliers, logist ics service providers, and other value -chain actors improved the coordination of processes, allowed
information exchange, minimized delays, and boosted organizational productivity. The positive relationship between
SC alliance and operational efficiency of  the supply chain indicates that effective supply chain management can help in
efficient use of resources and deliver better services. The result agrees with Li et al. (2006) and Flynn et al. (2010) who

proposed that supply chain integration ensures that the organizations operate efficiently and share information
effectively. The findings are also consistent with the research conducted by Dubey et al. (2024), who discovered that
alliance management capability enhanced digital transformation, supply chain agility, and organizational performance
by enabling companies to leverage their alliance resources to effectively respond to environmental disruptions. Their
research found that flexible operational capabi lity and effective knowledge integration enable the creation of more

The findings from the partial correlation revealed a moderating effect between strategic alliance and sustainable
competitive advantage by organizational agility. Based on the zero-order correlation, it can be seen that the correlation
value between strategic alliance and sustainable competitive advantage is r = 0.330, p = 0.000, which means there is a
positive correlation between the two variables. But once organizational agility was controlled for, the correlation
coefficient decreased slightly to 0.326, which means that organizational agility had some influence on the relationship
observed. While the reduction was moderate, it indicates that there was a certain moderating effect, which means the
agility of the organization improved the effectiveness of strategic alliances in the innovation capability and operational
efficiency. Organisations with higher responsiveness, flexibility and adaptability were thus more able to convert the
resources they acquired from the alliances into sustainable competitive advantage. This finding agrees with Sharifi and
Zhang (1999) who believe that organizational agility is a way for firms to adapt quickly to changes in the environment
through effective resource reconfiguration. It also corroborates the findings of Tallon and Pinsonneault (2011) who
claimed that agility enhances the ability to convert strategic resources into better performance of the organization. More
recently, Ciampi et al. (2021) stated that organizational agility plays a substantial role in strengthening t he efficacy of
collaboration, by boosting the ability of firms to adjust to technological and market adjustments. Thus, the present result
shows that the incorporation of high level of organizational agility is necessary to achieve higher SCA for the forma tion
of strategic alliance among the quoted oil and gas companies in Rivers State, Nigeria.
6 CONCLUSION
The study found that strategic alliances proved to have a positive effect on sustainable competitive advantage of the
quoted oil and gas companies in Rivers State, Nigeria. For the impact on innovation capability, technology alliance had
the highest impact, followed by marketing alliance and supply chain alliance. Marketing alliance had the highest impact
on operational efficiency, then supply chain alliance, and technology alliance, in turn. The study also found that
organizational agility partially medera ted the relationship between strategic alliances and sustainable competitive
advantage as it allowed firms to effectively utilize alliance resources and capabilities. Thus, ongoing investment in
strategic alliances is key to long-term innovation capability and operational efficiency, as is organizational agility.
Limitations of the Study
This study was carried out on quoted oil and gas companies in Rivers State, Nigeria; the results, therefore, cannot be
easily generalized across other sectors and geographical locations. The study also had a cross-sectional research design
that meant that it couldn’t possibly have examined how strategic alliances and sustainable competitive advantage
developed over time, but rather only at one specific point in time. In addition, primary data was collected using
structured questionnaire, and the results may  have been influenced by the opinions of the respondents. Although the
measures of ensuring confidentiality and anonymity of the respondents were implemented, there might still be
response bias. Also, the study emphasized technology alliance, marketing all iance, supply chain alliance, innovation
capability, operational efficiency and organizational agility; other organizational and environmental factors which
might affect sustainable competitive advantage outside the scope of the study were left out.
Recommendations
Based on the results, the following recommendations are made:
• The management of the Quoted Oil and Gas Companies in Rivers State should focus on enhancing investment
in the technology -based collaboration with research institutions, technology vendors, equipment
manufacturers and digital solution providers. These coll aborations should be centered on joint research and
development, digital oilfield technologies, automation, predictive maintenance, and knowledge transfer
programmes aimed at boosting the capabilities of the firms to develop innovative products, services, and
processes.
• Improved co -operative marketing with distributors, strategic customers and marketing partners should be
encouraged by the management of the oil and gas companies to enable them to share information about the
market, coordinate demand forecasting, jointly r un promotional programmes and jointly manage customer
relationship. These will help in the sharing of these activities, thereby reducing resource wastages and making
service delivery more efficient along the value chain.

• Management should have long -term contractual relationships with suppliers and service providers, and joint

should be established. This will improve coor dination, avert delays in procurement, help to control inventory

• Management needs to build organizational agility through flexible organizational structures, faster decision
