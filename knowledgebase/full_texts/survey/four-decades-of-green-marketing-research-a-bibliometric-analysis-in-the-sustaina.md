1. Introduction

Marketing strategies are crucial for a company's survival and for optimizing its profits. Effective marketing strategies
have favourable consequences for the brand reputation of the company, ensuring that the company remains memorable to
the consumer. Acco rding to a study conducted by Iswari in 2020, marketing communication strategies have a strong and
beneficial impact on Tokopedia's brand image, particularly in the realm of advertising. The study conducted by Prabowo et al.
(2021) further supports the not ion that both marketing strategy variables and the quality of service have an impact on
consumer purchasing decisions within the Indonesia National Service. A marketing strategy refers to the deliberate actions
undertaken by a company to promote awareness and recognition of its product or service among the general public (Morgan
et al., 2019). Essentially, a marketing strategy should prioritize inputs, environments, and outputs. The inputs typically co nsist
of resources, whereas the environment encompasses both internal and external factors. The outputs are usually represented
by profits and the overall performance of the company (Morgan et al., 2019).
The ideal orientation of marketing strategy planning encompasses strategic principles such as agility, indirect tactics,
focus, diverse targets, and asymmetrical competition (Paley, 2021). With the advancement of technology and evolving public
opinion, the marketing strategy model is undergoing changes during the production and distribution stages of a product or
service. Consequently, the marketing team must adjust to these changes. The adoption of digital marketing strategy tools can
facilitate more efficient and globally controlled marketing efforts (Katsikeas et al., 2019). Marketing strategies must not only
conform to contemporary technological tools but also adjust to the evolving social dynamics of society. One way to adapt is by
embracing favourable perceptions that receive significant public recognition, such as green marketing, which focuses on the
concept of sustainable business and incorporates environmental sustainability (Polonsky & Ottman, 1998).
Sustainability in marketing focusses on the process of developing products that satisfy the needs and desires of
consumers while simultaneously protecting the environment and the welfare of future generations (Pomering, 2017; Amoako
et al., 2022). The strategic planning process of the firm should be intentionally designed to incorporate sustainability business
strategies into every aspect, including the corporate vision, mission objectives, and tactical plans, as well as the marketin g
strategy (Amoako et al ., 2022). The marketing mix is a strategic marketing tool that directs marketing plans to accomplish
sustainable marketing objectives and generate value (Amoako et al., 2022). It is a marketing parameter that can be managed
and has a direct effect on the conscious decision-making of customers. The marketing mix is employed to influence marketing
Abstract  Businesses need to be able to adapt and respond effectively in order to stay up with the rapid and ongoing changes
that are occurring in both society and the natural environment. One alternative adaptation strategy is to adopt green
marketing tactics based on environmental sustainability ideals. As a result, the goal of this study is to provide a bibliometric
analysis of green marketing research conducted over the last four decades. The method used is bibliometric analysis, which
starts with exploration,  continues on to identifying search terms, then doing a search and  validating the list of papers
discovered, and finally conducting analysis in the Scopus database. According to the results of the bibliometric investigation,
there are two hundred books entirely dedicated to the subject of green marketing. All of the papers have an H-index of 70,
indicating that they have been referenced at least 70 times. The environmental, sustainable, business, marketing,
management, current service management, applied behavioral science, and social sciences are all linked to the main trend
of green marketing research. According to the results of bibliometric analysis, the most frequently covered themes in green
marketing research are those related to green marketing, marketing, consumers, effects, roles, green marketing strategies,
green marketing orientations, and perspectives on green marketing. Corporate social responsibility, application, influence,
green product, value, corporate image, consumer behavior, and mediating role were the keywords associated with green
marketing that garnered the least amount of research in the studies undertaken on the subject.

To address the challenges that arise during the implementation of green marketing, it is imperative to thoroughly
examine the subject in conjunction with the advancement of green marketing practices over time. Hence, the objective of this
study is to elucidate the resear ch on green marketing over a span of four decades through the utilization of bibliometric
analysis. The selection of four decades is based on the progression of the green economy from its initial focus on environmental
orientation in the 1980s to its curre nt emphasis on sustainability in the 2000s. The findings of this study provide valuable
insights for readers to gain knowledge about the implementation of a green economy in the fourth decade, as well as the
challenges that arise. This knowledge can help readers effectively mitigate these obstacles.

2. Methodology

This study utilizes bibliometric analysis, employing the publish or perish and VOSviewer  tools. The necessary data
consists of the journal and its accompanying papers, which can be acquired via publication or publication via the Scopus
database. The study encompasses the period  from 1983--2023, which spans four decades. Additional tools utilized for data
processing requirements include Microsoft Excel and Microsoft Word. Microsoft Excel facilitates data manipulation, including
data selection, classification, tabulation, and visua lization. Microsoft Word is designed for the purpose of articulat ing and
documenting research findings in scientific articles. This research is implemented through a series of systematic stages
to ensure the coherence and logicality of the research findings, as follows:

1. Explore topics related to marketing issues on Google Scholar. This aims to find topics that are relevant to current
problems. On the basis of the results of this exploration, the author found that the topic of green marketing is currently
receiving more attention.
2. Determine keywords for search, which consists of two keywords, namely, "green" and "marketing". The use of English
in these two keywords aims to obtain more global data and is not limited to research originating from just one region.
3. When searching for papers on the basis of keywords in the publish or perish instrument, the time span of the paper is
in accordance with the research provisions that have been determined. The paper database used is Scopus.
4. If a paper has been found, then the list of paper findings is checked to ensure that the title or topic of the paper findings
matches the topic of discussion. This data search and selection resulted in several document identities, such as year of
publication, language, journal, title, author, affiliation, keywords, document type, and abstract (Yu et al., 2020;
Digdowiseiso et al., 2024; Digdowiseiso et al., 2025; Sugiyanto et al., 2024). The findings are exported in CSV format for
bibliometric analysis in VOSviewer.
5. Bibliometric analysis was carried out in VOSviewer  with clustering, overlay and density analysis models. This
visualization aims to review the relevance between keywords on the basis of the topics raised in the research, as in the
research of Yu et al. (2020), Digdowiseiso et al. (2024), Digdowiseiso et al. (2025), and Sugiyanto et al. (2024).

3.3. Productivity of the title of the paper (Citation)

The research examined the frequency of citations in each paper as a measure of the prominence of paper titles. The
paper with the highest number of citations is the one that investigates the field of green marketing and applies Ajzen's theo ry
of behavioral planning. This paper demonstrates that Ajzen's theory of planned behavior offers a conceptual framework that
is suitable for research and theoretical analysis. The theory is tested under two distinct market conditions, as described by
Kalafatis et al. (1999).
The second-ranked paper originated from Lee's (2008) research findings on green marketing opportunities targeted at
young consumers. The author employs multiple regression analysis in this study, revealing that social influence is the primar y
determinant of environmentally conscious purchasing behavior among teenagers in Hong Kong. The fourth major predictor of
green marketing is concern for self-image in environmental protection and perceptions of environmental responsibility.
The third-ranked paper examines green marketing strategies (Cronin et al., 2010). The research demonstrates that an
ecologically conscious approach can encompass three key components: (1) eco-friendly innovation, (2) organizational greening,
and (3) environmentally friendly alliances. The primary approach to implementing a green marketing strategy involves creating
novel or inventive products that are environmentally sustainable. Organizations can achieve environmental sustainability by
implementing green practices, green procedures, and green strategies in their supply chain management. Furthermore, as part
of their commitment to environmental sustainability, companies can opt to employ alliances or partnerships to enhance their
eco-conscious practices.

3.6. Density model analysis

The density results from the data visualization show the luminosity of the colors in the highlighted phrases. This color
represents the level of significance of keywords in scientific literature. As brightness grows, so does the frequency with wh ich
the keyword is used as a subject in scientific journals. As the hue fades, the frequency with which the keyword appears in
scientific publications decreases, indicating that it is not a popular subject of study in many scientific journals. In Figure 3,
density analysis revealed that the topic of green marketing, marketing, consumer, effect, role, green marketing strategy, green
marketing orientation, and perspective  are several keywords that appear most frequently in green marketing research. The
keywords such as corporate social responsibility, application, influence, green product, value, corporate image, consumer
behaviour, and mediating role are the keywords that have been least studied in green marketing research.

Meanwhile, Choudhary & Gokarn (2013) asserted that sustainable marketing principles should be evident in product
creation, packaging, and promotion focused on environmental preservation. In this case, sustainable advertising necessitates
promotional messages that not only endorse items but also convey societal principles for the common good, with ramifications
for nature, the economy, and society, primarily aimed at fostering sustainable living (Prianti, 2012). If advertising channels
cause environmental degradation, the negative effects and consequences will outweigh the firm's economic gain from the sale
of associated items or services. The negative impact on the community will eventually hurt the firm. Unsustainable advertising
impacts not only the community or society, but also the companies that use it. Inadequate knowledge on promoting green
behaviors among customers is hindering the expansion of green marketplaces (Chekima et al., 2016). Environmental claims in
marketing are becoming relevant as corporations target environmentally conscious consumers (Ashok & Aswathanarayana,
2018). Carlson et al. (1993) found a lack of systematic examination of environmental advertising, despite extensive literature
on the topic.
Packaging and sustainability are rapidly being debated by industry participants and academics in relation to consumer
behaviour. The concept of "Green Packaging" is crucial for promoting sustainability in marketing (Amoako et al., 2022).
Consumers often make purchasing judgments based on product packaging. Unsatisfactory packaging can discourage customers
from purchasing a high-quality product. Many industry players consider this a crucial aspect for corporate success.
Unfortunately, its widespread use is negatively impacting consumers and society. This is due to the use of non-recyclable or
biodegradable packaging materials. In third-world economies, there is a lack of effort to properly dispose of and manage
garbage from discarded, used, and empty containers. Discarded packaging contributes significantly to trash in industrialized
economies and impacts the environment. It can serve as a starting point for corporations to use green marketing strategies
(Peattie & Charter, 1994).
Consumer behaviour is also closely related to purchasing intention. Grewal et al. (1998) defined purchasing intention as
the likelihood that customers will purchase a specific product. Chen (2013) found that environmental knowledge, attitudes,
concern, and social influence impact green purchase intentions. According to Mahmoud et al. (2017), a positive buy intention
can lead to a purchase action, while a negative intention can lead to a non-purchase. Consumers are increasingly conscious of
the impact of their consumption on the environment, leading to a desire to support sustainable products (Kumar et al., 2018).
Environmental protection is a daily concern that goes beyond fulfilling personal needs. According to Yadav et al. (2019), growing
customer awareness and preference for sustainable companies has influenced their purchasing decisions.

4. Conclusion

The bibliometric analysis revealed that there are a total of 200 papers dedicated to the topic of green marketing. The H-
index for all the papers is 70, indicating that there are a minimum of 70 papers that have received at least 70 citations each.
The title trend of green marketing research is associated with the environment, sustainability, business, marketing,
management, contemporary service management , applied behavioral science, and social sciences. According to bibliometric
analysis, keywords related to green marketing  marketing, consumer, effect, role, green marketing strategy, green marketing
