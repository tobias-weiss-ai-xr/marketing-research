Introduction: Inﬂuencer marketing has become an important strategy for
shaping consumer attitudes and purchase behavior, particularly among
Generation Z consumers who are highly active on social media platforms.
Although previous studies have examined inﬂuencer credibility and parasocial
interaction separately, limited research has integrated inﬂuencer
characteristics and content attributes within a uniﬁed framework, especially in
emerging market contexts. This study investigates how inﬂuencer-related
factors inﬂuence Generation Z consumers’ purchase intention in India.
Methods: A quantitative cross-sectional research design was adopted. Data
were collected from 370 Generation Z respondents from Tier-1 metropolitan
cities in India who actively engage with inﬂuencer content on social media.
Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) was employed to examine the
relationships among inﬂuencer expertise, inﬂuencer passion, content quality,
frequency of exposure, engagement, trust, perception, and purchase intention.
Results: The ﬁndings indicate that trust and perception signiﬁcantly inﬂuence
purchase intention and act as key mediating mechanisms. Inﬂuencer expertise
positively affects trust, while content quality positively affects perception.
Frequency of exposure negatively affects perception, suggesting diminishing
effectiveness of repeated inﬂuencer content. Engagement did not signiﬁcantly
inﬂuence purchase intention and did not function as a mediator in this context.
Discussion: This study contributes to the inﬂuencer marketing literature by
integrating Source Credibility Theory and Parasocial Interaction Theory within
a single structural model and by providing empirical evidence from an
emerging market context. The ﬁndings offer practical implications for
marketers targeting Generation Z consumers by emphasizing the importance
of inﬂuencer credibility, authenticity, and high-quality content over excessive
exposure strategies.

1 Introduction
The proliferation of social media platforms has signiﬁcantly
transformed the marketing landscape, enabling digital content
creators, commonly referred to as social media inIf_luencers, to
play a central role in shaping consumer attitudes and purchase
behavior. Despite the increasing adoption of inIf_luencer
marketing strategies, the mechanisms through which inIf_luencer
attributes and content characteristics translate into consumer
purchase intention remain insufﬁciently understood, particularly
among Generation Z consumers in emerging market contexts.
Generation Z, typically deﬁned as individuals born between
the mid-1990s and early 2010s, represents a digitally native
cohort characterized by a strong preference for authenticity,
peer-based recommendations, and interactive engagement
(Fromm and Read , 2018; Williams et al. , 2012). Social media
platforms such as Instagram and YouTube have become
dominant channels through which this cohort interacts with
brands and inIf_luencers. Prior research suggests that Generation
Z consumers are more likely to rely on inIf_luencers who
demonstrate relatability, authenticity, and consistent value
alignment, thereby enhancing the persuasive effectiveness of
inIf_luencer marketing ( Djafarova and Troﬁmenko , 2019).
However, the extent to which speciﬁc inIf_luencer attributes and
content-related factors jointly inIf_luence consumer decision-
making processes remains underexplored. The effectiveness of
inIf_luencer marketing can be explained through the integration
of key theoretical perspectives. Source Credibility Theory posits
that communicator characteristics, particularly expertise and
trustworthiness, inIf_luence audience attitudes and behavioral
responses ( Ohanian, 1990). In this context, inIf_luencer expertise
serves as a cognitive cue that enhances perceived reliability and
reduces uncertainty in decision-making.
In contrast, Parasocial Interaction Theory explains how
audiences develop perceived emotional relationships with media
ﬁgures, resulting in feelings of familiarity, attachment, and social
connection ( Horton and Wohl , 1956). These relationships are
particularly salient in social media environments, where
inIf_luencers are often perceived as relatable peers. Together, these
theories suggest that inIf_luencer attributes and content
characteristics shape psychological responses, including
engagement, trust, and perception, which subsequently inIf_luence
purchase intention. Although prior studies have examined
individual dimensions of inIf_luencer marketing, several research
gaps remain. First, existing research often treats inIf_luencers as a
homogeneous category and does not sufﬁciently distinguish
between speciﬁc inIf_luencer attributes, such as expertise and
passion, and content-related factors, such as content quality and
exposure frequency. Second, while psychological constructs such
as trust and engagement have been explored, limited studies
have examined the simultaneous mediating roles of engagement,
trust, and perception within a uniﬁed structural framework.
Third, empirical evidence focusing on Generation Z consumers
in the Indian context, particularly within Tier-1 metropolitan
cities characterized by high levels of digital engagement, remains
limited. Furthermore, lifestyle-oriented sectors such as fashion
and food, where visual appeal and emotional connection are
critical, have received relatively less attention in this context. To
address these gaps, this study develops and empirically tests a
theoretically grounded structural model that examines how
inIf_luencer characteristics (expertise and passion) and content
attributes (content quality and frequency of exposure) inIf_luence
Generation Z consumers’ purchase intention. The model further
investigates the mediating roles of engagement, trust, and
perception in explaining how inIf_luencer-related factors translate
into consumer behavior. Accordingly, the study addresses the
following research questions:
RQ1: How do inIf_luencer characteristics (expertise and
passion) and content attributes (content quality and exposure
frequency) inIf_luence Generation Z consumers’ purchase
intention?
RQ2: To what extent do engagement, trust, and perception
mediate the relationship between inIf_luencer marketing factors
and purchase intention?
This study adopts a quantitative cross-sectional research design
and employs Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) to examine
the relationships among the proposed constructs. By integrating
Source Credibility Theory and Parasocial Interaction Theory
within a uniﬁed framework, the study makes three key
contributions. First, it provides a more nuanced understanding
of inIf_luencer effectiveness by distinguishing between multiple
inIf_luencer attributes and content characteristics. Second, it
advances theoretical understanding by examining the differential
mediating roles of engagement, trust, and perception. Third, it
contributes empirical evidence from the Indian Generation
Z context, thereby extending the applicability of existing theories
to an emerging market setting. Overall, this study offers a
theoretically integrated and empirically grounded explanation of
how inIf_luencer marketing inIf_luences Generation Z consumers’
purchase intentions, with particular relevance to visually driven
sectors such as fashion.
2 Literature review
Understanding how Generation Z responds to inIf_luencer
marketing requires an integrated examination of persuasion
mechanisms and source-related cues within digital
environments. The Elaboration Likelihood Model (ELM) (Petty
and Cacioppo , 1986) provides a foundational framework for
explaining how individuals process persuasive messages through
either a central or peripheral route. In the context of social
media, where users are frequently exposed to large volumes of
content, Generation Z consumers are more likely to rely on
peripheral cues rather than engage in extensive cognitive
evaluation. These cues include inIf_luencer expertise, content
quality, and frequency of exposure, which function as heuristic
signals that shape attitudes and purchase intentions.
Building on this, Source Credibility Theory (SCT) emphasizes
that communicator characteristics, particularly expertise and
trustworthiness, play a critical role in inIf_luencing audience
responses ( Ohanian, 1990). InIf_luencer expertise serves as a
cognitive cue that enhances perceived reliability, while
trustworthiness reduces uncertainty in decision-making. Prior
research consistently demonstrates that higher levels of
perceived credibility lead to stronger consumer trust and more
favorable purchase intentions ( Sokolova and Keﬁ , 2020). Thus,

2.1 Inﬂuencer expertise and passion
InIf_luencer expertise and passion represent two distinct yet
complementary dimensions of inIf_luencer effectiveness. Expertise
refers to the perceived knowledge, competence, and experience
of the inIf_luencer within a speciﬁc domain ( Ohanian, 1990).
Within the ELM framework, expertise functions as a peripheral
cue that signals credibility and reduces perceived risk, thereby
enhancing trust and inIf_luencing purchase intention.
In contrast, inIf_luencer passion reIf_lects the emotional intensity
and enthusiasm expressed in content creation. Passion contributes
to perceived authenticity, which is particularly important for
Generation Z consumers. InIf_luencers who demonstrate genuine
enthusiasm are more likely to be perceived as intrinsically
motivated, thereby strengthening emotional connection and
encouraging engagement ( Audrezet et al. , 2020). While expertise
primarily operates through cognitive evaluation, passion
functions through affective mechanisms, highlighting the dual
role of logic and emotion in inIf_luencer persuasion.
2.2 Content quality and frequency of
exposure
Content-related attributes play a signiﬁcant role in shaping
consumer responses to inIf_luencer marketing. Content quality
refers to the perceived relevance, clarity, and aesthetic appeal of
inIf_luencer-generated content ( De Veirman et al. , 2017). High-
quality content enhances message processing and positively
inIf_luences consumer evaluations, particularly through the central
route of persuasion when users engage more deeply with content.
Frequency of exposure, grounded in the mere exposur e effect
(Zajonc, 1968), suggests that repeated exposure increases familiarity
and liking. However, in digital environments charac terized by
content saturation, excessive exposure may lead to diminishing
returns. For Generation Z consumers, frequent exposure may shift
from reinforcing familiarity to generating fatigue,  thereby
negatively affecting perceptions of authenticity and credibility.
2.3 Engagement, trust, and perception as
mediating mechanisms
Engagement, trust, and perception serve as key psychological
mechanisms that explain how inIf_luencer attributes translate into
consumer behavior.
Engagement refers to the degree of cognitive, emotional, and
behavioral interaction with inIf_luencer content, including actions
such as liking, commenting, and sharing ( Brodie et al. , 2013).
While engagement reIf_lects active interaction, prior research
suggests that such interactions do not always translate into
purchase-related outcomes, particularly among Generation
Z users who may engage with content at a superﬁcial level.
Trust represents the consumer’s belief in the reliability,
honesty, and credibility of the inIf_luencer ( Ohanian, 1990;
Sokolova and Keﬁ , 2020). Trust functions as a central
mechanism that reduces perceived risk and enhances the
persuasive impact of inIf_luencer recommendations. InIf_luencers
perceived as credible and transparent are more likely to
inIf_luence purchase intention through trust-based processes.
Perception, in this study, refers to the consumer’s evaluation
of inIf_luencer authenticity, credibility, and relevance. This
construct captures how followers interpret inIf_luencer content
and assess its alignment with their values and expectations.
From a Parasocial Interaction perspective, perceived authenticity
plays a critical role in shaping these evaluations, as inIf_luencers
who appear genuine and relatable are more likely to establish
meaningful connections with their audience ( Audrezet et al. ,
2020). Consequently, perception functions as a key mediator
linking content-related factors to consumer responses.
2.4 Purchase intention
Purchase intention reIf_lects an individual’s conscious
willingness to purchase a product or service based on available
information and perceived value ( Ajzen, 1991). In inIf_luencer
marketing contexts, purchase intention is shaped by both
cognitive evaluations (e.g., credibility and trust) and affective
responses (e.g., emotional connection and authenticity). Prior
studies consistently demonstrate that inIf_luencer credibility, trust,
and perceived authenticity positively inIf_luence purchase
intention, highlighting the importance of psychological
mediators in consumer decision-making.

4 Research methodology
4.1 Research design
This study employed a quantitative cross-sectional survey
design to examine the relationships between inIf_luenc er-related
factors and Generation Z consumers’ purchase intent ion.
A structured questionnaire was used to measure the latent
constructs included in the proposed model. The cros s-sectional
design enables the analysis of relationships among variables at
a single point in time; however, it does not permit
causal inference.
4.2 Sample and data collection
A purposive non-probability sampling technique was
employed to select respondents who actively engage with
inIf_luencer content on social media platforms such as Instagram
and YouTube. This approach is appropriate for targeting
digitally active Generation Z users, who constitute the primary
population of interest in inIf_luencer marketing research.
However, the use of purposive sampling introduces l imitations
related to external validity and generalizability. The sample is
restricted to respondents from Tier-1 metropolitan cities in India,
who are likely to exhibit higher levels of digital literacy and social
media engagement than the broader population. There fore, the
ﬁndings should be interpreted as context-speciﬁc an d may not be
generalizable to Generation Z consumers in rural ar eas, smaller
cities, or different cultural settings. While this sampling approach
enhances contextual relevance, it does not ensure
representativeness of the overall population. Futur e research may
adopt probability-based sampling techniques or incl ude more
diverse geographic segments to improve generalizability.
The ﬁnal sample comprised 370 usable responses, which is
considered adequate for the complexity of the proposed
structural model. In line with Hair et al. (2019) , SEM studies
with models of moderate complexity generally require a
minimum of 200 observations. Furthermore, applying the
commonly recommended ratio of at least ten observations per
estimated parameter, and considering that the model comprises
eight latent constructs measured by 24 observed indicators, the
sample size of 370 exceeds the minimum requirements for
reliable parameter estimation, thereby providing adequate
statistical power for the proposed structural model.
In addition to direct path estimation, indirect eff ects were
formally assessed to evaluate the proposed mediation relationships.
Bootstrapping procedures were employed using AMOS 2 4, with
5,000 bootstrap resamples generated and bias-corrected conﬁdence
intervals computed at the 95% conﬁdence level, cons istent with
contemporary recommendations for indirect effect es timation in
SEM-based research (Hair et al. , 2019; Preacher and Hayes, 2008).
A mediation effect is considered statistically sign iﬁcant when the
bootstrapped conﬁdence interval does not include zero.

6 Discussion of ﬁndings
This study provides important insights into how inIf_luencer
characteristics are associated with the purchase intentions of
Generation Z consumers in the Indian context. Consistent with
Source Credibility Theory ( Ohanian, 1990), the ﬁndings indicate
that inIf_luencer expertise inIf_luences purchase intention indirectly
through trust. For Indian Generation Z consumers, who are
often characterized by skepticism toward overt promotional
content, perceived expertise functions as a credibility cue that
reduces perceived risk and enhances conﬁdence in inIf_luencer
recommendation. This ﬁnding is particularly relevant in the
Indian digital retail environment, where concerns about
sponsored content may heighten the role of expertise as a trust-
building signal, and is consistent with prior research conducted
in Western contexts ( Casaló et al. , 2020), while extending its
applicability to an emerging market setting.
Content quality was also found to inIf_luence purchase
intention primarily through perception. High-quality, relevant,
and visually appealing content enhances consumers’ perceptions
of inIf_luencer authenticity and alignment with brand values. This
pattern may reIf_lect central-route processing, as described in the
Elaboration Likelihood Model ( Petty and Cacioppo , 1986),
where individuals rely on message quality when evaluating
persuasive communication. However, the non-signiﬁcant role of
engagement as a mediator is particularly noteworthy given that
prior studies conducted in Western contexts have generally
reported positive engagement effects on purchase intention. This
divergence may reIf_lect culturally speciﬁc patterns of social media
use among Indian Generation Z consumers, who may engage
with inIf_luencer content primarily for entertainment or
information rather than as a precursor to purchase behavior.
Additionally, platform-speciﬁc factors and the high volume of
inIf_luencer content in the Indian digital ecosystem may
contribute to engagement behaviors that are decoupled from
actual purchasing decisions.
The ﬁndings further indicate that frequency of expo sure
negatively affects perception and indirectly reduce s purchase
intention. This challenges the conventional assumpt ion that
increased exposure enhances persuasion. Instead, th e results
may indicate diminishing responsiveness to repeated  exposure,
potentially reIf_lecting content fatigue ( Reinikainen et al. , 2020),
particularly when excessive exposure reduces percei ved
authenticity. In contrast, inIf_luencer passion demons trated
only a marginal effect on engagement and no signiﬁc ant
relationship with purchase intention. This ﬁnding d iverges
from prior studies ( Lou and Yuan , 2019) and suggests that
overt expressions of enthusiasm may not consistentl y translate
into perceived credibility within this context. It is possible
that such expressions are perceived differently wit hin the
Indian cultural context, where audiences may exerci se greater
scrutiny toward emotionally expressive promotional
communication; however, this interpretation remains
speculative and warrants further empirical investig ation.
Overall, these ﬁndings advance theoretical understanding of
inIf_luencer marketing by demonstrating that different inIf_luencer
attributes operate through distinct psychological mechanisms.
The results further indicate that the effectiveness of inIf_luencer
marketing is context-dependent, shaped by the preferences and
behavioral tendencies of Generation Z consumers in
emerging markets.

6.2 Practical and managerial implications
For marketers, brand strategists, and content creators
operating in lifestyle-oriented sectors, the ﬁndings suggest that
strategic emphasis should be placed on domain expertise,
credibility, and content quality. Collaborations should prioritize
inIf_luencers who are not only popular but also perceived as
knowledgeable and authentic within their niche.
In addition, brands should carefully manage content
frequency. Rather than relying on volume, a strategically timed
and curated approach is more likely to sustain consumer interest
and trust. Overexposure may lead to disengagement, thereby
undermining campaign effectiveness. Maintaining a balance
between visibility and value is crucial for long-term impact.
The ﬁndings carry several speciﬁc practical implications for
marketers, inIf_luencer agencies, and content creators:
• Prioritize Expertise to Build Trust: Brands targeting Indian
Generation Z should collaborate with inIf_luencers who
demonstrate clear domain knowledge and professional
credibility, as trust emerged as a signiﬁcant and
inIf_luential mediator of purchase intention.
• Focus on Quality over Quantity: High-quality, relevant content
enhances consumer perception and indirectly strengt hens
purchase intention more effectively than frequent posting.
• Avoid Overexposure: Excessive posting frequency may
contribute to content fatigue and diminish brand impact;
