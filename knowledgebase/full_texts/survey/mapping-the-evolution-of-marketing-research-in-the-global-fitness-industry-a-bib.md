Abstract
This study aims to map the evolution of marketing research in the global fitness industry
through bibliometric and thematic analysis. Data were collected from Scopus,
ScienceDirect, and PubMed databases, covering the period from 2015 to 2025. A total of
2,462 articles were initially identified, with 1,171 articles meeting the inclusion criteria
for analysis, and the top 10 most cited studies selected for in -depth review. The analysis
was conducted using VOSviewer to examine publication trends, authorship patterns,
country contributions, research domains, and thematic developments. The results indicate
a significant and consistent increase in research productivity, growing from 30
publications in 2015 to 230 in 2025, with over 15,000 citations globally. The United
States, China, and the United Kingdom domina te scientific contributions, while leading
authors such as García -Fernández have played a central role in shaping the field. The
dominant research themes include service quality, customer satisfaction, loyalty, digital
fitness platforms, and c ustomer behavior. Furthermore, the findings highlight the
increasing integration of digital technologies, such as fitness applications and social
media, in marketing strategies. This study demonstrates that marketing research in the
fitness industry has evolved i nto a multidisciplinary and rapidly growing field. It also
identifies future research opportunities, particularly in digital transformation, customer
experience, and technology-driven service innovation within the global fitness sector
Keywords: fitness industry; sport marketing; bibliometric analysis; customer behavior;
consumer behavior.

Introduction

The development of the global fitness industry in the last two decades has shown
significant growth, driven by increasing public awareness of health, active lifestyle
trends, and advances in digital technology (Peng et al., 2025) . This transformation
requires industry players to adopt adaptive and innovative marketing strategies in order
to reach consumers effectively (Cioppi et al., 2023; Kraus et al., 2022). Marketing in the
fitness industry not only focuses on the promotion of physical facilities such as fitness
centers, but also includes technology-based services such as fitness apps, virtual training,
and wearable technology integrations (Soon Kim & Sung Lee, 2024) . Digital -based
marketing approaches have become increasingly important in the wake of the COVID-19
pandemic, which has forced many industry players to switch to online engagement
strategies to maintain consumer participation (Kholiilah & Syahchari, 2024).
Previous studies on marketing in the fitness industry have identified a variety of
key factors, ranging from intrinsic and extrinsic customer motivation, barriers to
participation, to the influence of brand image on loyalty  (Ntoumanis & Moller, 2025;
Viveiros et al., 2025) . However, as in a study by García-Fernández, Gálvez-Ruíz, et al.
(2018) and Cunningham (2013), existing research remains fragmented, often focusing on
specific geographic contexts, market segments, or isolated service attributes. A
comprehensive, large-scale mapping of the evolution of marketing research in the global
fitness industry is still lacking. While several narrative or systematic reviews exist in sport
marketing and physical activity promotion, no prior bibliometric study has systematically
synthesized the intellectual structure, thematic evolution, and research trends in fitness
industry marketing across global scientific databases over the last decade.
Bibliometric methods, particularly when supported by tools such as VOSviewer,
enable quantitative analysis of publication trends, collaboration networks, influential
authors, countries, institutions, and thematic clusters (Ding & Yang, 2022; Donthu et al.,
2021). These methods can uncover the research landscape, identify knowledge gaps, and
provide an evidence-based roadmap for future investigations. Given the rapid expansion
of fitness marketing literature and the growing integration of digital technologies, a
bibliometric review is both timely and necessary. Unlike traditional systematic reviews

This study is particularly relevant to the scope of journals specializing in sport

marketing dynamics in the fitness industry contributes directly to sport management
scholarship by bridging consumer behavior, service innovation, and health promotion.
Moreover, the rise of digital fitness platforms and wearable technologies aligns with
interdisciplinary interests in exercise science, public health, and information syst ems.
Thus, the findings of this bibliometric analysis will benefit academics, practitioners, and
policymakers seeking to design evidence-based marketing strategies that enhance fitness
participation and customer loyalty.
Based on this background, the formulation of this research problem includes: (1)
What is the trend of marketing research development in the fitness industry in global
scientific publications? (2) What are the most influential journals and publications based
on the number of citations on this topic? (3) What are the main themes and distribution
of countries in the development of marketing research in the fitness industry according to
bibliometric analysis and systematic review?
Accordingly, the objectives of this study are: (1) to map the evolution of
marketing research in the fitness industry through bibliometric analysis; (2) to identify
influential journals, key publications, and leading contributors; and (3) to uncover
dominant thematic clusters and propose future research agendas. By achieving these
objectives, this study provides a comprehensive reference for researchers, practitioners,
and policymakers to formulate sustainable marketing innovations in the global fitness
sector.

Eligibility criteria
The research selection was based on the following inclusion criteria: (1)
documents related to marketing development in the gym, fitness club, or fitness industry;
(2) publication in English; (3) document type: peer -reviewed articles or reviews; (4)
publication years: 2015 –2025. Studies were excluded if: (1) they are non -research
publications (such as conference proceedings, book chapters, or reports) or laboratory
studies involving animals; (2) duplicate publications; (3) do not have complete or
adequate m etadata; or (4) are limited to abstracts or conference presentations only.

Selection of studies and identification
At this stage, 2,462 articles were retrieved from the Scopus, ScienceDirect, and
PubMed databases. All records were exported in RIS format and imported into reference
management software (Mendeley) for duplicate detection. Duplicates were identified first
by DOI, then by titl e and author matching. Manual checking was performed to confirm
duplicates across databases because metadata formats differ. After removing 254
duplicate articles, 2,208 articles remained for further review. In the 2015-2025 screening
stage, 513 articles were identified and 1,695 articles advanced to full -text evaluation.
Furthermore, 524 articles were excluded due to conference abstracts, posters, or
unavailability of full texts, leaving 1,171 articles for comprehensive bibliometric analysis.
Of these 1,171 articles, all were analyzed using VOSviewer for co-authorship, co-citation,
and keyword co-occurrence. In addition, the top 10 most cited articles were selected for
an in-depth thematic review to illustrate the most impactful studies; however, the main
thematic analysis was conducted on the entire set of 1,171 articles using keyword
clustering in VOSviewer, not solely on the top 10. For standard operationalization, this
study followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses
(PRISMA) guidelines. The study selection process is illustrated in the flow diagram in
Figure 1.

VOSviewer analysis parameters
Bibliometric mapping was performed using VOSviewer version 1.6.19. For co -
authorship analysis at the author level, a minimum threshold of two documents per author
was applied with no minimum citation requirement to include emerging researchers. For
keyword co-occurrence analysis, a keyword was required to appear at least five times
across the dataset; from an initial set of 3,245 keywords, 287 met this threshold, and the
full counting method was used. Co -citation analysis of cited references employed a
minimum citation threshold of 20 per reference. Clustering was conducted using
VOSviewer’s default resolution parameter of 1.00 with a minimum cluster size of two.
The association strength method was chosen for normalizing co -occurrence data, while
fractional counting was applied for co -authorship to account for multi -authored papers.
Before analysis, author names, keywords, and affiliations were manually standardized;

Integration of multiple databases
Because PubMed, ScienceDirect, and Scopus have different metadata schemas,
the following steps were taken. First, all records were converted to a common RIS format
using Mendeley. Second, author names were standardized by last name and first initial to
avoid splitting due to different abbreviations. Third, affiliations were harmonized using
the author's listed primary affiliation at the time of publication. Fourth, keywords were
merged when identical in meaning but different in spelling (e.g., "digital fitness" and "e-
fitness"). Fifth, citations were sourced from Scopus for consistency, as Scopus provides
citation counts for most indexed documents; when a document was not in Scopus, citation
data were taken from Crossref via the VOSviewer enrichment function.

Discussion
Trends in Marketing Research Development in the Global Fitness Industry
The bibliometric results demonstrate a marked and consistent increase in
marketing research within the fitness industry over the past decade (2015 –2025). The
number of publications grew from 30 in 2015 to 230 in 2025 (Table 2), reflecting the
field’s emergence as a relevant and dynamic academic domain. This upward trajectory
aligns with the global expansion of the fitness market and the growing recognition of
marketing as a strategic lever for engaging consumers.
The growth is largely attributed to increasing public awareness of physical activity
and healthy lifestyles. A systematic review by  (Viveiros et al., 2025)  confirms that
participation in fitness is strongly influenced by both intrinsic and extrinsic psychological
motivations (Sadewa et al., 2025). Health awareness encourages consumers to engage in
fitness programs through both conventional centers and digital platforms  (Hu et al.,
2023). As demand for fitness services rises, industry players must adopt adaptive
marketing strategies that cater to a broader spectrum of consumer needs.
Digital technology has emerged as a key driver in this domain, reshaping how
consumers access fitness services.  Peng et al. (2025)  highlight that innovations such as
Unmanned Gyms and Smart Fitness Technology are transforming the modern fitness
industry, enabling more personalized and flexible offerings . (Soon Kim & Sung Lee,
2024) add that integrating digital technology in fitness activities increases user
engagement and strengthens loyalty, underscoring the importance of technology in
contemporary marketing strategies.
The COVID -19 pandemic acted as a significant accelerator, compelling many
gyms to pivot to digital-based services (Bohórquez et al., 2024). Research by  Kholiilah
& Syahchari (2024) in Indonesia demonstrates that the use of social media, technology
integration, and the physical environment experience significantly influence customer
engagement in fitness centers, shifting the focus from purely physical services to digital
engagement strategies. Several other studies have observed similar growth patterns. For
example, a bibliometric analysis of fitness apps revealed a sharp increase in publications
after 2015, mirroring the findings of this study  (Liu & Avello, 2021) . A broader
bibliometric analysis of health and sports promotion noted a steady increase in

Influential Journals, Authors, and Publications
The analysis of the most cited publications shows that research primarily focuses
on service quality, customer satisfaction, and loyalty. The top -cited study by (García-
Fernández, Gálvez-Ruíz, et al., 2018) on the effects of service convenience and perceived
quality on perceived value, satisfaction, and loyalty in low -cost fitness centers leads the
list (233 citations). This is consistent with a systematic review on fitness center service
quality, which concluded that positive perceptions of service quality influence customer
satisfaction, loyalty, and retention, essential for the sustainability of fitness businesses
(Ferreira-Barbosa et al., 2022). Another study on Spanish fitness centers further supports
this, finding that perceived quality and customer engagement directly influence perceived
value, satisfaction, and ultimately loyalty (Gálvez-Ruiz et al., 2023).
Consumer digital experience has become an increasingly dominant research topic,
as seen in the second most cited publication by (Kim 2022) which focuses on the influence
of digital attributes on the YouTube fitness platform on user flow experience, satisfaction,
and behavioral intentions during COVID-19. A separate systematic review on marketing
strategies for sports and fitness venues found  that personalization of service and
utilization of technology are key strategies for increasing perceived value and satisfaction
(Sadewa et al., 2025), aligning with Kim’s findings.
Another highly cited publication Foroughi et al. (2019) examines the relationship
between service process quality, outcome quality, satisfaction, and behavioral intentions
in Malaysian gyms. This aligns with the findings of (García-Pascual et al., 2020), which

experience, digital platforms, and loyalty, reflecting the unique service-oriented nature of
the fitness industry.
Main Themes and Country Distribution
The bibliometric analysis reveals that marketing research in the fitness industry is
dominated by the United States (251 documents, 7,151 citations), followed by China and
the United Kingdom, a pattern that aligns with other bibliometric studies in sport
management where the US, China, and European nations are consistently identified as
major contributors. The dominance of the United States is further corroborated by
analyses of research on the economic impact of sports, which identify the US, UK, and
China as the most active countries (Kudinska et al., 2025). However, a distinct regional
pattern emerges when examining the geography of sport management research more
broadly; while the US leads in volume, analyses of research settings reveal that the US
and the UK lead in research impact, whereas nations like C hina, despite high output,
exhibit lower citation influence (Kamenecka-Usova et al., 2025).
In addition to Western nations, research contributions from Asia  particularly
China, India, and Malaysia have increased significantly. This trend is driven by economic
growth and rising health awareness in the region, with researchers exploring marketing
strategies tailored to local market characteristics (Peng et al., 2025). Despite this growth,
the geographical distribution remains heavily concentrated in high -income nations,

Identification of Research Gaps and Future Research Directions
 Based on the findings, several research gaps and future directions can be
identified. While digital platforms (Cluster 5) are rapidly growing, research on how they
integrate with traditional service quality (Cluster 3) to create a cohesive customer
experience remains limited. Future studies could examine how fitness apps and wearable
devices can be used to enhance perceived service quality and loyalty in physical fitness
centers a hybrid model often referred to as “phygital” fitness. A 2025 study on online
home fitness identified synchronicity, coach reputation, and the home environment as key
triggers for retention, suggesting a promising area for future investigation (Langaro &
Werling, 2025). Although customer satisfaction and loyalty are well -studied, there is a
lack of longitudinal research on long -term engagement and retention, particularly in
digital fitness contexts. A systematic literature review on the continued usage of mobile
fitness applications concluded that sustained engagement is essential for leveraging
digital technologies to promote long-term health, underscoring the need for research into
the psychological and behavioral factors that lead to sustained participation in fitness
programs, especially those delivered online (M. Chen et al., 2025). With the rise of social
media influencers (Cluster 1), understanding how influencer credibility, authenticity, and
trust affect consumer purchase intentions and brand loyalty is crucial.
A 2025 study on social media influencers in the health and fitness sector found
that different influencer characteristics impact purchase intention through consumer
attitude as a mediator (Sambyal & Gopal, 2025). Future research could explore the role
of micro-influencers versus macro-influencers and the impact of influencer marketing on
different demographic segments. While the country distribution was analyzed, few
studies have systematically compared marketing  strategies across different cultural and

research on sustainability practices (Cluster 2) within the fitness industry, such as eco -

strategies could attract environmentally conscious consumers and enhance brand image.
Finally, the importance of community in fitness participation is highlighted by
reports that a large proportion of active consumers consider social interaction and
community to be the main reasons they join a fitness community. Future research should
expand on value co -creation theory (Cluster 6) by examining how online communities,
social features in fitness apps, and user -generated content facilitate co -creation and
enhance loyalty (Gaofu et al., 2025) . By addressing these gaps, future research can
contribute to a more comprehensive understanding of marketing dynamics in the fitness
industry, supporting the development of evidence -based strategies for enhancing
consumer engagement, satisfaction, and loyalty in an increasingly digital and competitive
global market.

growth in marketing research within the global fitness industry, from 30 to 230 annual

Kingdom dominate scientific output, while influential authors such as García‑Fernández

influence, brand management, service quality and loyalty, consumer behavior, digital
technologies and motivation, and value co‑creation. These findings confirm that fitness
marketing has evolved into a multidisciplinary, technology‑driven domain with a strong
focus on customer experience and digital engagement.
Despite limitations related to database coverage and language restrictions, this
study provides a comprehensive roadmap for researchers and practitioners. Future
research should explore the integration of digital and traditional service quality (phygital
fitness), long‑term consumer retention, influencer trust, cross‑country comparisons,
ethical AI personalization, and sustainability marketing. Addressing these gaps will
support evidence‑based strategies to enhance consumer engagement, satisfaction, and
loyalty in an increasingly competitive and digital fitness market.
Study Limitations
This study has several limitations. First, the search was limited to three databases
(Scopus, ScienceDirect, PubMed) and English‑language publications, potentially missing
relevant studies in other databases or languages. Second, citation‑based metrics fav or
older publications and may not fully capture recent impactful research. Third, despite
manual harmonization, metadata inconsistencies across databases (e.g., author names,
keywords) could slightly affect the accuracy of bibliometric maps. Fourth, the in ‑depth
thematic review focused only on the top 10 most cited articles; however, the primary
thematic analysis used keyword clustering on the full dataset (1,171 articles) to mitigate
this. Fifth, parameter choices in VOSviewer (e.g., threshold, resolution)  involve some
subjectivity, although sensitivity checks confirmed the robustness of main findings. Sixth,
grey literature (conference proceedings, industry reports) was excluded. Despite these
limitations, the study offers a valid and reproducible roadmap of marketing research in
the global fitness industry.
