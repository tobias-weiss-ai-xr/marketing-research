Abstract
The rapid expansion of IoT ecosystems is transforming the very nature of strategic marketing
management by shifting focus from product -centric transactions toward continuous, data -driven
value creation. This study develops a conceptual framework for managing smart technologies within
IoT ecosystems and to identify the key factors influencing marketing effectiveness in highly
connected digital environments. The research adopts a conceptual method ology based on literature
synthesis, comparative theoretical analysis, conceptual modelling, and an illustrative case study of
the Xiaomi smart ecosystem. Findings demonstrate that strategic marketing effectiveness
increasingly depends on the interaction o f three core capabilities: Data Intelligence, Personalization
Capability, and Ecosystem Integration. Building upon these dimensions, the study proposes the Smart
Marketing Efficiency (SME) framework and introduces Karaoke Capitalism Pressure as a moderating
factor reflecting the effects of technological imitation and market convergence. The proposed model
contributes to the literature by integrating Service-Dominant Logic, Servitization Theory, Ecosystem
Theory, and Smart Marketing concepts within a unified analytical structure. The study also provides

1. Introduction
Smart technologies’ rapid diffusion fundamentally transformed the nature of contemporary
markets, creating interconnected digital environments in which products, services, users, and
organizations continuously exchange information. This transformation was driven, to a significant
extent, by the development of Internet of Things (IoT), which enables physical objects to
communicate through embedded sensors, cloud infrastructures, and intelligent software systems. As
IoT technologies become increasingly integrated into business process es and consumer lifestyles,
firms face new challenges in designing effective strategic marketing approaches capable of generating
value within highly dynamic and data -intensive ecosystems (Mukhanbetali et al., 2026; Kulikov et
al., 2022 ). Traditional marke ting management models were developed for relatively linear value
chains characterized by one-directional communication between producers and consumers. However,
the emergence of IoT ecosystems altered these relationships by facilitating continuous interac tions
among multiple stakeholders, including technology providers, platform operators, device
manufacturers, software developers, and end users. In such environments, marketing can no longer
be limited to promoting individual products; instead, it must coordinate value creation across complex
networks of interconnected actors and technologies.
The growing adoption of smart technologies particularly affected electronic commerce,
service delivery, industrial production, healthcare, transportation, and smart c ity development.
Emerging capabilities enable firms to develop highly personalized marketing strategies, improve
customer experiences, and optimize decision -making processes. Empirical evidence suggests that
IoT-enabled marketing initiatives can significan tly improve organizational performance through
enhanced customer engagement, increased sales effectiveness, and higher returns on marketing
investments (Diaz et al., 2022; Dhanawade et al., 2024). At the same time, strategic utilization of
smart technologies introduces new managerial complexities. The value generated by IoT systems
increasingly depends on the ability of firms to transform raw data into actionable insights, ensure
interoperability across heterogeneous technological infrastructures, maintain cybersecurity and
consumer trust, and coordinate activities among multiple ecosystem participants (Tariq et al., 2020).
Consequently, strategic marketing management within IoT environments requires capabilities that
extend beyond conventional market segmen tation, promotion, and customer relationship
management. According to Pizło et al. (2022), the IoT means “digitization of physical objects by
supplementing them with smart digital functionalities thanks to the use of new technologies at various

As a result, there remains a lack of comprehensive conceptual frameworks explaining how

2. Theoretical Framework
2.1. Internet of Things Ecosystems as a New Marketing Environment
The Internet of Things evolved from a technological infrastructure enabling machine -to-
machine communication into a complex socio -technical ecosystem that connects physical devices,
digital platforms, organizations, and consumers through continuous data exchange. Unlike traditional
digital environments, IoT ecosystems are characterized by real -time connectivity, decentralized
interactions, and the integration of phys ical and virtual value creation processes (Bhardwaj et al.,
2025). Within such ecosystems, smart technologies function not as isolated products but rather as
interconnected components of broader service networks. Consequently, competitive advantage
increasingly depends not only on technological capabilities but also on the ability to coordinate
interactions among multiple ecosystem participants.
The ecosystem perspective suggests that value creation emerges from collaborative
relationships involving manufac turers, software developers, service providers, platform operators,
and end users (Liu et al., 2024). These interdependencies generate network effects whereby the value
of a technology increases as the number of connected devices and participants expands. Therefore,
organizations operating within IoT ecosystems must adopt strategic approaches that account for both
technological integration and stakeholder coordination. Moreover, continuous flow of real -time
information transforms market intelligence process es. Firms gain unprecedented opportunities to
observe consumer behavior, monitor product performance, predict future demand, and adapt
marketing activities dynamically (Malik, 2024). This shift represents a clear trend of transition from
periodic market an alysis toward continuous market sensing, where strategic decisions are
increasingly informed by live data streams generated within the ecosystem itself. As a result, IoT
ecosystems constitute a fundamentally new marketing environment characterized by
interconnectedness, data abundance, adaptive interactions, and collaborative value creation. These
features require the reconsideration of conventional marketing frameworks and necessitate the
development of ecosystem-oriented strategic marketing approaches.

2.2 Theoretical Foundations of Strategic Marketing Management in IoT Ecosystems
IoT ecosystems’ growing complexity actually became the impetus for emergence of several
theoretical perspectives explaining the ways how organizations create, deliver, and capt ure value in
highly interconnected digital environments. Strategic marketing management of smart technologies
cannot be adequately understood through traditional product -oriented frameworks alone, as IoT
ecosystems are characterized by continuous connectiv ity, real -time data generation, platform
interdependencies, and collaborative value creation. Consequently, a multidimensional theoretical
foundation is required to explain the mechanisms through which marketing strategies operate within
these ecosystems.

2.2.1 Service-Dominant Logic
One of the most influential perspectives informing IoT marketing is Service-Dominant (S-D)
Logic. This theory challenges the traditional goods-dominant view of marketing by arguing that value
is not embedded within products but is co-created through interactions between providers and users.
In IoT environments, connected devices serve primarily as platforms for delivering ongoing services
rather than as standalone products (Simoes, 2019). The value of a smart technology therefore emerges
through its continuous use, adaptation, and integration into consumers' daily activities. From a
marketing perspective, S-D Logic shifts strategic attention from transactional exchanges toward long-
term customer engagement and experience management. Organizations are required to continuously
enhance service quality, personalize interactions, and facilitate customer participation in value
creation processes. This perspective provides an important theoretical basis for understanding the
relational nature of marketing within IoT ecosystems.

2.2.2 Product-Service Systems and Servitization Theory
The transition from product ownership toward service consumption is further explained by
Product-Service System (PSS) theory and the concept of servitizat ion. These approaches describe
how firms transform traditional product -centric business models into service -oriented solutions that
deliver outcomes rather than physical goods. Within IoT ecosystems, connected technologies enable
organizations to offer sub scription-based, usage -based, and outcome -based services supported by
real-time monitoring and predictive analytics (Sharma & Jakhar, 2025). Smart devices become
components of broader service systems that continuously generate value throughout the customer
lifecycle. Consequently, marketing strategies increasingly focus on customer retention, subscription
management, and long -term relationship development rather than solely on customer acquisition

(Sanyal et al., 2023). The servitization perspective is part icularly relevant for understanding how
smart technologies create recurring revenue streams while simultaneously strengthening customer
loyalty and ecosystem engagement.

2.2.3 Data-Driven Value Co-Creation
The ability of IoT technologies to generate vast  quantities of real -time data led to the
emergence of data-driven value creation as a central theoretical concept. This perspective emphasizes
data as a strategic resource that enables organizations to personalize offerings, optimize customer
experiences, predict future needs, and continuously improve service performance (Wilson et al .,
2024). Unlike traditional marketing approaches that rely on periodic market research, data -driven
value creation supports continuous market sensing and adaptive decision -making. Organizations
enjoy direct insights into customer behavior, product usage patterns, and contextual consumption
environments, allowing marketing interventions to become increasingly precise and responsive
(Rosário & Raimundo, 2025). However, the effect iveness of data-driven marketing depends on the
establishment of consumer trust and the responsible management of personal information.
Consequently, privacy protection, transparency, and ethical data governance become integral
components of strategic marketing management within IoT ecosystems.

2.2.4 Ecosystem Theory and Network Effects
Ecosystem Theory provides another critical foundation for understanding marketing in
interconnected technological environments. According to this perspective, organizational success
depends not only on internal resources and capabilities but also on the firm's position within broader
networks of complementary actors and technologies (Theodorakopoulos et al., 2025). IoT products
rarely function independently. Ins tead, they derive value from integration with platforms,
applications, cloud infrastructures, and third -party services (Katuk et al., 2023). As the number of
connected users and devices increases, positive network effects enhance the overall attractiveness and
functionality of the ecosystem. Marketing strategies must therefore extend beyond individual product
promotion and include ecosystem positioning, alliance formation, platform governance, and
interoperability management. This theoretical perspective hi ghlights the strategic importance of
ecosystem orchestration as a mechanism for sustaining competitive advantage in digital markets.

2.2.5 B2B2C Marketing Perspective
The hybrid structure of many IoT markets also increased the relevance of the Business -to-
Business-to-Consumer (B2B2C) perspective. Unlike conventional marketing channels, IoT
ecosystems frequently involve multiple layers of interaction among technology providers,
distributors, service partners, and end users. The B2B2C perspective recognizes  that successful
commercialization of smart technologies requires organizations to simultaneously address the needs
of business partners and final consumers (Ardito et al., 2025). While intermediary organizations often
prioritize operational efficiency, co st reduction, and return on investment, end users focus on
usability, convenience, personalization, and trust. Strategic marketing management must therefore
balance these objectives through integrated value propositions that satisfy both stakeholder groups .
The increasing prevalence of platform -based ecosystems further reinforces the relevance of the
B2B2C approach, as customer value is frequently generated through coordinated interactions among
multiple market participants (Sharma, 2026). Taken together, t hese theoretical perspectives provide
a comprehensive foundation for understanding strategic marketing management in IoT ecosystems.
While each theory focuses on a different, particular, aspect of value creation, their integration
suggests that successful marketing strategies depend on the ability to combine service orientation,
data intelligence, ecosystem coordination, customer participation, and multi-stakeholder relationship
management. These insights form the basis for identifying the core strategic dimensions of marketing
management discussed in the following section.

2.3 Core Strategic Dimensions of Marketing Management in IoT Ecosystems
The t heoretical perspectives discussed above collectively suggest that effective marketing
management in IoT ecosystems extends beyond traditional activities related to promotion, market
segmentation, and customer acquisition. The interconnected nature of smart technologies, continuous
data generation, and ecosystem -based value creation require a broader strategic framework capable
of integrating technological, organizational, and relational dimensions. Based on the synthesis of
Service-Dominant Logic, Servitization Theory, Data-Driven Value Creation, Ecosystem Theory, and
the B2B2C perspective, five core strategic dimensions can be identified as fundamental determinants
of marketing effectiveness within IoT ecosystems.

2.3.1 Value Co-Creation
Value co -creation represents the central strategic principle underlying contemporary IoT
marketing. Unlike traditional business models in which value is embedded within products and
transferred to consumers through market transactions, IoT ecosystems facilitate continuous
interactions between organizations and users throughout the entire product -service lifecycle.
Connected devices generate real -time information that allows firms to adapt functionalities,
personalize experiences, and respond dynamically to evolving customer  needs. In this context,
customers become active participants in value creation rather than passive recipients of products and
services (Karpa et al., 2023; Borodin et al., 2023). Their interactions with connected technologies
continuously generate feedback and behavioral data that contribute to product improvement, service
enhancement, and innovation processes. Consequently, marketing management must focus on
designing mechanisms that encourage customer participation, strengthen engagement, and facilitate
long-term relationships capable of generating mutual value.

2.3.2 Data Intelligence
Data intelligence constitutes the informational foundation of strategic marketing management
in IoT ecosystems. Through sensors, connected devices, mobile applications, and cloud
infrastructures, organizations gain access to unprecedented volumes of real -time data describing
customer behavior, usage patterns, contextual conditions, and operational performance. The strategic
importance of data intelligence lies not in data  collection itself but in the ability to transform
information into actionable insights. Advanced analytics, machine learning algorithms, and predictive
modelling enable organizations to identify emerging market trends, anticipate customer preferences,
personalize offerings, and optimize marketing interventions. As a result, decision-making shifts from
retrospective analysis toward predictive and adaptive marketing management.
Moreover, data intelligence enhances organizational responsiveness by enabling co ntinuous
market sensing and rapid strategic adjustments. Firms capable of effectively leveraging data resources
are better positioned to identify opportunities, reduce uncertainty, and sustain competitive advantages
within rapidly evolving digital environments.

2.3.3 Servitization and Customer Lifecycle Management
The growing prevalence of connected technologies accelerated transition from product-centric
to service-oriented business models. Within IoT ecosystems, value is increasingly delivered through
continuous service provision rather than through one -time product sales. Smart devices function as
platforms for delivering ongoing services, software updates, predictive maintenance, remote
monitoring, and personalized experiences. This transformation place s customer lifecycle
management at the center of marketing strategy. Instead of focusing exclusively on acquisition,
organizations must maintain long-term customer relationships through continuous value delivery and
engagement. Subscription -based models, u sage-based pricing mechanisms, and outcome -oriented
services create recurring interactions that require ongoing communication and relationship
management. Servitization’ strategic significance, in fact, lies in its ability to transform customer
relationships from transactional exchanges into continuous partnerships, generating both stable
revenue streams and stronger customer loyalty.

2.3.4 Ecosystem Orchestration
IoT technologies derive much of their value from integration within broader networks of
devices, platforms, applications, and service providers. Consequently, strategic marketing
management increasingly depends on the organization's ability to coordinate relationships across

complex ecosystems involving multiple stakeholders. Ecosystem orchestrat ion encompasses
partnership development, platform governance, interoperability management, and the alignment of
complementary resources. Rather than competing solely through product features, organizations
compete through the strength and functionality of the ecosystems they help create and maintain.
Ecosystem orchestration effectiveness directly influences user adoption and customer
satisfaction. Consumers generally prefer solutions that seamlessly integrate with existing
technologies and provide access to  a broad range of complementary services. Therefore, marketing
strategies must communicate ecosystem compatibility, collaborative innovation, and network value
alongside traditional product attributes. Moreover, ecosystem orchestration contributes to the
generation of positive network effects, whereby the value of the ecosystem increases as more
participants, devices, and services become interconnected. Such effects create significant barriers to
entry and enhance long-term competitive positioning.

2.3.5 Trust and Security Governance
Trust represents one of the most critical determinants of consumer participation in IoT
ecosystems. The collection, transmission, and processing of large volumes of personal and behavioral
data expose organizations to challen ges related to privacy protection, cybersecurity, and regulatory
compliance. Consumers increasingly evaluate connected technologies not only on the basis of
functionality and convenience but also according to perceived levels of security and transparency.
Concerns regarding unauthorized data access, cyberattacks, surveillance, and misuse of personal
information may significantly reduce technology adoption and customer engagement. As a result,
trust and security governance must be viewed as strategic marketi ng capabilities rather than purely
technical responsibilities. Effective marketing management requires transparent communication
regarding data practices, clear privacy policies, responsible data governance mechanisms, and visible
commitments to cybersecur ity. Organizations that successfully establish trust are more likely to
encourage data sharing, strengthen customer loyalty, and facilitate long-term participation within the
ecosystem.

2.3.6 Integrative Perspective
Although each strategic dimension contributes independently to organizational performance,
their effectiveness ultimately depends on their interaction. Data intelligence supports personalized
value co -creation; servitization transforms value creation into long -term customer relationships;
ecosystem orchestration expands the scope of value generation through network collaboration; and

3. Methodology
The methodological strategy does not seek statistical generalization. Instead, it aims to provide
theoretical generalization through conceptual synthesis and model development, contributing to the
advancement of strategic marketing theory in the context of  digital ecosystems and smart
technologies. The study employs a conceptual research design aimed at developing a theoretical
framework for strategic marketing management of smart technologies within IoT ecosystems. Given
the rapidly evolving nature of IoT-enabled business environments and the fragmented state of existing
marketing literature, a theory -building approach was selected to integrate diverse theoretical
perspectives into a coherent analytical model. The methodological foundation of the study combines
four complementary approaches. First, integrative review and synthesis of academic literature were
conducted to identify the principal theoretical streams relevant to IoT marketing, including Service -
Dominant Logic, Servitization Theory, Data-Driven Value Creation, Ecosystem Theory, and B2B2C
marketing perspectives. Second, comparative conceptual analysis was employed to examine
relationships observed among these theoretical approaches and identify common strategic dimensions
underlying marketing activities in connected digital environments.
Third, a conceptual modelling approach was used to develop the Smart Marketing Efficiency
(SME) framework. The model was constructed through theoretical abstraction and integration of the
key dimensions identified i n the literature, namely Data Intelligence, Personalization Capability,
Ecosystem Integration, and Karaoke Capitalism Pressure. This approach enabled the formulation of
a conceptual efficiency function that explains how organizations transform technological capabilities
into differentiated customer value within IoT ecosystems.

The integration of Service -Dominant Logic, servitization  theory, ecosystem thinking, and
data-driven marketing suggests that strategic marketing management in Internet of Things
environments is evolving toward more integrated and adaptive paradigm. Beyond the identification
of individual strategic dimensions, recent developments in digital markets indicate the emergence of
what can be conceptualized as Smart Marketing Logic, where marketing becomes continuously
adaptive, data-responsive, and ecosystem-embedded.
In this context, Smart Marketing can be defined as a dynamic capability -based approach to
marketing management that leverages real -time data from connected technologies, predictive

analytics, and ecosystem interactions to deliver hyper-personalized, continuously evolving customer
value. Unlike traditional digital marketing, Smart Marketing is not campaign -based or transaction -

strategic dimensions form the architecture of Smart Marketing in IoT ecosystems: Value Co-Creation,

4.1.1 Smart Marketing and the Logic of Karaoke Capitalism
The contemporary IoT marketing landscape is increasingly characterized by what was
described as karaoke capitalism, a condition in which firms tend to replicate each other’s innovations,
producing standardized “cover versions” of technological and marketing solutions rather than
genuinely differentiated strategies. In the context of IoT, this phenomen on manifests in the rapid
imitation of smart features, such as sensor integration, mobile applications, and connected services,
often without strategic differentiation or meaningful customer value innovation. This creates a
structural tension between techn ological convergence and strategic originality. While IoT
technologies are widely accessible and easily replicable, competitive advantage increasingly depends
not on the mere adoption of smart features, but on the originality of data interpretation, custom er
relationship design, and ecosystem positioning. Within this framework, Smart Marketing becomes a
response to the copycat dynamics of karaoke capitalism by shifting strategic emphasis from “what
companies offer” to “how intelligently and uniquely they us e data to create value” (Cleveland &
Bartsch, 2019). This shift highlights that in IoT ecosystems, innovation is no longer primarily
product-based but relational, experiential, and data-driven.

4.1.2 Five Pillars of Smart Marketing in IoT Ecosystems
The Smart Marketing logic in IoT environments can be further operationalized through five
interconnected strategic mechanisms:
1. Continuous Listening. IoT devices function as always -on sensing systems that capture real -
time behavioral data, enabling firms to understand actual user behavior rather than relying on
self-reported preferences.

2. Predictive Personalization. Advanced analytics and machine learning allow organizations to
anticipate customer needs and deliver proactive, highly individualized offerings  before
explicit demand is expressed.
3. Servitization and Outcome Orientation. Value creation shifts from ownership of physical

4. Frictionless Engagement. IoT ecosystems reduce or eliminate transactional barriers by

5. Ecosystem Embeddedness. Smart products derive their value from integration into broader
technological ecosystems, where interoperability and network participation significantly
enhance functionality and customer value.

4.2 Toward a Strategic Logic of Smart Marketing Efficiency
The interaction between Smart Marketing and karaoke capitalism suggests that competitive
advantage in IoT ecosystems is no longer determined by technological adoption alone, but by the
efficiency with which firms convert data into differentiated customer  value. In environments where
technological features are easily replicated, strategic uniqueness emerges from the depth of customer
insight, the sophistication of personalization, and the strength of ecosystem relationships.
Accordingly, strategic marketing effectiveness in IoT ecosystems can be conceptualized as a function
of three interdependent variables: the intensity of data intelligence utilization, the degree of
personalization in value co -creation, and the level of ecosystem integration. This logic forms the
conceptual basis for the development of a formalized efficiency framework.

4.3 Conceptual Model and Smart Marketing Efficiency Function in IoT Ecosystems
Building on the identified strategic dimensions and competitive dynamics of karaoke
capitalism, we propose a conceptual model of Smart Marketing efficiency in Internet of Things
ecosystems. The model integrates the logic of continuous data acquisition, predictive personalization,
and ecosystem embeddedness into a unified analytical framework that explains how firms transform
technological capabilities into differentiated market performance. At the core of this model is the
assumption that in IoT ecosystems, strategic marketing effectiveness is determined not by the mere
availability of smart technologies, but by the efficiency with which organizations convert data flows
into personalized and ecosystem -aligned customer value. In highly imitative environments, where

technological features are easily replicated, competitive advantage is driven by th e originality and
precision of data interpretation and the ability to sustain meaningful customer relationships across
interconnected platforms.
The proposed Smart Marketing Efficiency (SME) function can be expressed as follows:

SME=f(DI×PC×EI)/KC    (1)

Thus, effectiveness of Smart Marketing depends on:

- how well a company manages data (DI)
- how well it personalizes (PC)
- how integrated it is into ecosystems (EI)
and decreases if:
- every market player around is copying each other (KC = karaoke capitalism)

Thus, SME model conceptualizes strategic marketing performance in IoT ecosystems as a
multiplicative and inverse relationship between key capabilities and environmental pressure.
Specifically, efficiency increases when firms simultaneously enh ance data intelligence,
personalization capability, and ecosystem integration, while it decreases under conditions of
intensified imitation dynamics associated with karaoke capitalism. The multiplicative structure
reflects the synergistic nature of digital  capabilities, where the combined effect of strategic
dimensions is greater than their isolated contributions. The inverse component captures the
diminishing impact of innovation when market actors rapidly replicate technological and marketing
solutions.

4.4 Xiaomi as an Illustrative Case of Smart Marketing Efficiency in IoT Ecosystems
The practical relevance of the proposed Smart Marketing Efficiency framework can be
illustrated through the development of the Xiaomi Smart Home Ecosystem. Xiaomi represents one
of the most successful examples of ecosystem-oriented strategic marketing in the Internet of Things
market. Rather than focusing exclusively on manufacturing individual smart devices, the company
developed a platform-centered ecosystem strategy that integrates numerous interconnected products
and services (Zhao & Yi, 2022). A distinctiv e feature of Xiaomi’s approach is its ecosystem
orchestration capability. The company established partnerships with more than one hundred
specialized technology firms responsible for developing complementary smart products, including
robotic vacuum cleaner s, wearable devices, smart lighting systems, air purifiers, and security
solutions. Instead of competing within every product category independently, Xiaomi positioned its
smartphone platform and Mi Home application as the central interface connecting the entire
ecosystem. From the perspective of the proposed model, Xiaomi demonstrates a high level of Data
Intelligence (DI) through continuous collection and analysis of user interaction data generated by
connected devices. These data enable the company to id entify behavioral patterns, optimize user
experiences, and improve ecosystem functionality through regular software updates.
The second component of the model, Personalization Capability (PC), is reflected in Xiaomi’s
ability to provide context -sensitive r ecommendations, customized automation scenarios, and
personalized service experiences. Through machine -learning-based analytics, the ecosystem adapts
to user preferences and consumption habits, thereby strengthening customer engagement. The third
dimension, Ecosystem Integration (EI), constitutes perhaps the strongest element of Xiaomi’s
strategy. The company’s competitive advantage derives not from individual devices but from the
interoperability of hundreds of connected products operating within a unified  technological
environment. Such integration increases switching costs and strengthens customer loyalty by
encouraging long-term ecosystem participation.
Importantly, Xiaomi also illustrates the relevance of Karaoke Capitalism Pressure (KC). The
global smart device market is characterized by rapid imitation of technological features and product
concepts. Xiaomi’s response has been to emphasize ecosystem coordination, customer experience,
and platform -based value creation rather than relying solely on produc t-level innovation. This
strategy supports the argument that sustainable competitive advantage in IoT ecosystems increasingly
depends on the ability to transform data and connectivity into differentiated customer value.
Overall, the Xiaomi case provides em pirical support for the conceptual assumptions of the
Smart Marketing Efficiency model and demonstrates how the interaction of data intelligence,

personalization capability, and ecosystem integration can contribute to superior strategic marketing
performance in highly competitive digital environments.

4.5 Discussion and Implications
The findings obtained suggest that strategic marketing management of smart technologies in
Internet of Things ecosystems requires a conceptual shift from product -centered marketing toward
ecosystem-centered value creation. While previous studies investigated individual dimensions of IoT
marketing, such as personalization, servitization, data analytics, or customer engagement (Haider et
al., 2025; Olalekan, 2021), the proposed Smart Marketing Efficiency framework demonstrates that
these dimensions operate as mutually reinforcing components of an integrated strategic system. The
results also support the assumptions of Service -Dominant Logic, according to which value is not
embedded in products but co -created through ongoing interactions among market participan ts
(Uzoka, 2020). In IoT environments, connected devices function as interfaces through which
organizations continuously exchange information with consumers and adapt service offerings based
on real -time feedback. Consequently, marketing effectiveness incr easingly depends on the firm's
ability to transform customer interactions into actionable intelligence and personalized value
propositions (Baykal, 2023). The proposed model extends this perspective by identifying Data
Intelligence and Personalization Capability as primary mechanisms through which value co-creation
is operationalized within digital ecosystems.
The findings also reinforce the central arguments of Servitization Theory. The analysis
indicates that competitive advantage of smart technologies no longer derives primarily from hardware
functionality but from the continuous services associated with connected products. This observation
is particularly evident in platform-based ecosystems where recurring interactions generate long-term
customer relati onships and stable revenue streams (Spremic et al ., 2020). The Smart Marketing
Efficiency framework, thus, aligns with contemporary research emphasizing the transition from
ownership-based consumption toward outcome-oriented and service-oriented value models.
Moreover, the study contributes to Ecosystem Theory by highlighting the strategic
significance of ecosystem integration as a distinct determinant of marketing performance. Existing
literature frequently recognizes interoperability and network effects a s important characteristics of
IoT environments; however, they are rarely incorporated into formal marketing frameworks
(Petrescu, 2019; Zhang & Watson, 2020). The present model suggests that Ecosystem Integration
should be treated not merely as a technolo gical feature but as a strategic marketing capability that
directly influences customer retention, switching costs, and perceived value. In this sense, firms

compete increasingly through the quality of their ecosystems rather than through isolated product
attributes.
A particularly important contribution of the presented study lies in incorporation of karaoke
capitalism perspective into IoT marketing analysis. Most existing studies implicitly assume that
technological innovation automatically generates sustainable competitive advantages (Alekya et al.,
2025; Kumar et al., 2021; Putra et al., 2023). However, the rapid diffusion of digital technologies and
the widespread adoption of similar smart features challenge this assumption. As connected
technologies be come increasingly standardized, firms face growing pressure from imitation and
market convergence. The concept of Karaoke Capitalism provides useful lens for understanding why
technological adoption alone is insufficient for differentiation in mature digit al markets. The
introduction of Karaoke Capitalism Pressure as a moderating factor actually expands current
theoretical discussions by acknowledging that marketing effectiveness depends not only on the
development of strategic capabilities but also on the firm's ability to maintain distinctiveness under
conditions of accelerated imitation. This perspective enables comprehending why organizations
possessing comparable technological resources often achieve significantly different market
outcomes. The determin ing factor is not the technology itself but the originality with which firms
convert data into customer experiences, ecosystem relationships, and service innovations.
From a broader perspective, the findings indicate that the future evolution of strategic
marketing management in IoT ecosystems will increasingly depend on technological, relational, and
ecosystem capabilities integration. The proposed Smart Marketing Efficiency framework suggests
that organizations should view data not merely as an operational resource but as a strategic asset that
supports continuous learning, adaptation, and innovation. Likewise, personalization should be
understood not as a marketing tactic but rather as a dynamic organizational capability embedded
within ecosystem relationships. The theoretical contribution of this study lies in the development of
an integrated framework that combines Service -Dominant Logic, Servitization Theory, Ecosystem
Theory, and Smart Marketing concepts within a single analytical structure. Unlike pre vious
approaches that examine these dimensions independently, the proposed model explains how Data
Intelligence, Personalization Capability, and Ecosystem Integration jointly influence strategic
marketing effectiveness while accounting for the constraining  effects of competitive imitation. This
integrative perspective contributes to the emerging literature on digital ecosystems by providing a
coherent explanation of how firms create and sustain value in highly connected environments.
The study also offers a  range of managerial implications. First, managers should prioritize
investments in data intelligence capabilities, as the ability to transform IoT -generated information

should focus on developing advanced personalization mechanisms capable of converting customer

ecosystem-oriented perspective by strengthening interoperability, strategic partnerships, and platform
integration rather than concentrating exclusively on product -level innovation. Finally, for firms it is
expedient to recognize strategic risks associated with karaoke capitalism and avoid competing solely
through technological features that can be rapidly imitated. Sustainable competitive advantage is
determined by uniqueness of customer experiences, ecosystem relationships, and data -driven value
creation processes.
Overall, the findings suggest that strategic marketing management in IoT ecosystems should
be interpreted as a process of orchestrating interconnected capabilities rather than managing
individual technologies. Firms that successfully integrate data intelligence, personalization capability,
and ecosystem integration while mitigating the effects of competitive imitation are more likely to
achieve sustainable Smart Marketing Efficiency and long-term market leadership.

5. Conclusions
The study contributes to scientific discourse within digital transformation of marketing
paradigms by developing integrated conceptual framework for the strategic marketing management
of smart technologies within IoT ecosystems. Drawing upon Service -Dominant Logic, Servitization
Theory, Ecosystem Theory, and data -driven marketing perspectives, the research conceptualizes
Smart Marketing as a dynamic capability -based system that continuously adapts to customer
behavior, ecosystem interactions, and real -time information flows. The principal theoretical
contribution of the study is the development of the Smart Marketing Efficiency (SME) framework.
The model demonstrates that marketing effectiveness in IoT ecosystems is determined by the
interaction of three str ategic capabilities - Data Intelligence, Personalization Capability, and
Ecosystem Integration. At the same time, the framework introduces Karaoke Capitalism Pressure as
a moderating factor reflecting the growing influence of imitation, standardization, an d competitive
convergence in digital markets. This perspective extends existing research by claiming that
sustainable differentiation is characterized with increasing dependence on the originality of data
utilization, ecosystem orchestration, and customer experience design rather than on technological
adoption alone.
The illustrative Xiaomi case further demonstrates that successful IoT marketing strategies are
built upon ecosystem -based value creation, platform integration, and continuous customer

competitive advantages will emerge from the ability to coordinate interconnected technological and
relational resources across digital ecosystems.
From a manageria l perspective, the study suggests that organizations should prioritize
investments in advanced data analytics, personalization capabilities, and ecosystem development
while simultaneously addressing the risks associated with technological imitation. Manage rs should
view data not merely as an operational resource but as a strategic asset capable of generating long -
term customer value and ecosystem competitiveness. Overall, the study argues that the future of
strategic marketing management lies in the transit ion from product -centric competition toward
intelligent ecosystem orchestration. In increasingly interconnected markets, firms that successfully
combine data intelligence, personalization, and ecosystem integration while maintaining strategic
distinctiveness will be best positioned to achieve sustainable Smart Marketing Efficiency and long -
term market leadership, and this perspective can become a broad field of further research.
The study, meanwhile, is subject to several limitations. As a conceptual investigation, it does

using quantitative and mixed -method approaches, examine industry -specific variations in Smart
Marketing implementation, and explore the role of emerging technologies such as artificial
intelligence, digital twins, and autonomous systems in shaping the next generation of IoT marketing
ecosystems.
