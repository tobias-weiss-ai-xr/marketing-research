Abstract:
This study investigates the impact of contemporary digital marketing
strategies on organizational sales performance within modern business
environments. The research focuses on three primary dimensions: Artificial
Intelligence (AI) and Big Data Analytics, Influencer Marketing, and Mobile
Marketing. Employing a mixed-methods research design, the study combines
quantitative survey data collected from 300 marketing and sales professionals
with qualitative insights derived from secondary case studies of selecte d
multinational organizations. Quantitative analysis utilized descriptive
statistics, Pearson correlation, and simple regression analyses to examine the
relationships between the identified marketing strategies and sales
performance outcomes. The findings indicate statistically significant positive
relationships between all three marketing strategies and organizational sales
performance. Among the examined variables, AI and Big Data Analytics
emerged as the strongest predictor of sales performance (β = 0.44 5, p <
0.001), followed by Influencer Marketing (β = 0.333, p < 0.001) and Mobile
Marketing (β = 0.248, p < 0.001). The qualitative analysis of case studies
from Amazon, Nike, and Starbucks provided contextual depth, illustrating
how technology -driven, cus tomer-centric digital marketing strategies are
operationalized within successful global organizations. Cross -case thematic
analysis revealed four recurring themes: data -driven personalization,
influencer-led trust -building, mobile -first customer engagement , and
customer-centric strategic orientation. The combined findings confirm that

Introduction:
Over the past decade, the rapid proliferation of digital technologies
has fundamentally transformed global marketing landscapes, compelling
organizations to reconceive how they create value, engage consumers, and
sustain commercial performance. Traditional  mass-communication
approaches—predicated on unidirectional broadcast channels such as
television, print, and radio —have steadily given way to interactive, data -
driven, and customer -centric frameworks better suited to an increasingly
connected and discerning consumer base (Kotler et al., 2021). The advent of
artificial intelligence (AI), big data analytics, social media ecosystems, and
ubiquitous mobile connectivity has not merely supplemented existing
marketing repertoires but has restructured the foundati onal logic of how
organizations pursue competitive advantage.
Within this evolving landscape, three contemporary marketing strategies have
attracted particular scholarly and managerial attention: AI and Big Data
Analytics—enabling hyper -personalized communication and predictive
customer insights; Influencer Marketing—leveraging trust, authenticity, and
social credibility to shape consumer attitudes and purchase intentions; and
Mobile Marketing —capitalizing on the ubiquity of smartphones to deliver
timely, context-sensitive, and transactionally seamless customer experiences.
Although each of these strategies has been explored independently in prior
literature, comparatively limited empirical attention has been directed toward
examining their collective impact on organizational sales performance within
a unified analytical framework (Sharabati et al., 2024).
This research accordingly addresses that gap by investigating the
relationships between these three contemporary marketing dimensions and
sales performance, defined as a multidimensional construct encompassing
revenue growth, market share, customer acquisi tion, and sales target
attainment (Behrman & Perreault, 1982). A mixed -methods design is
employed, combining quantitative survey data from 300 marketing and sales
professionals with qualitative case study evidence from multinational
organizations, to yield  both statistically grounded and contextually rich
insights. The study is guided by three hypotheses: H1 —AI and Big Data
Analytics positively influence sales performance; H2—Influencer Marketing
positively influences sales performance; and H3 —Mobile Market ing
positively influences sales performance.

I. Literature Review and Theoretical Framework
1. Modern Marketing Trends and Sales Performance
Contemporary marketing is characterized by a fundamental transition from
product-centric, broadcast -model communication to relationship -driven,
interactive, and analytically informed engagement strategies. This evolution
has been accelerated by the converg ence of digital infrastructure, behavioral
data availability, and consumer demand for personalized, frictionless
experiences (Chaffey & Ellis-Chadwick, 2019). Modern marketing no longer
functions as a peripheral support activity but constitutes a strategic capability
integral to value creation and organizational competitiveness.
Sales performance, the primary dependent variable in this study, is
conceptualized as a multidimensional outcome of organizational marketing
activity encompassing financial metrics —revenue growth, sales volume,
profitability—as well as non -financial indica tors such as customer
acquisition, retention, and brand loyalty (Grewal et al., 2020). The
relationship between modern marketing strategies and sales outcomes
operates through multiple pathways: by enhancing relevance and resonance
of marketing communicati on; by lowering informational and transactional
barriers in the customer journey; and by cultivating trust and emotional
engagement that sustains long-term purchasing relationships. Understanding
these pathways is especially pertinent as digital marketing investment
continues to scale globally while organizations remain challenged to establish
clear causal links between specific marketing activities and measurable
business performance.

2. AI and Big Data Analytics in Marketing
AI and big data analytics represent perhaps the most structurally
consequential of the contemporary marketing innovations. By enabling
organizations to collect, process, and interpret vast behavioral datasets in real
time, these technologies allow for the delivery of highly personalized product
recommendations, dynamic pricing strategies, automated customer
segmentation, and predictive campaign optimization (Davenport et al., 2020).
Retailers such as Amazon leverage recommendation engines that analyze
browsing histories, purchase records, and interaction data to generate
contextually relevant offers, substantially increasing conversion rates and
customer lifetime value. Research consistently demonstrates that AI-powered
personalization enhances perceived relevance, reduces decision friction, and
stimulates repurchase behavior —all critical antecedents of superior sales
performance (Wamba et al., 2017).
3. Influencer Marketing
Influencer marketing has evolved into a mainstream strategic instrument,
particularly effective in consumer-facing industries where peer endorsement,
trust, and social proof powerfully shape purchase intentions (Alalwan, 2020).
Unlike traditional celebrity advertising, influencer partnerships are grounded
in perceived relatability, content authenticity, and parasocial relationships
between digital creators and their audiences. The persuasive efficacy of
influencer-generated content is theoretically explaine d by social influence
theory and the Technology Acceptance Model, which posits that consumers
are more likely to adopt product -related attitudes and behaviors when they
perceive information as credible and relevant (Lou & Yuan, 2019). Empirical
evidence co nfirms that well -executed influencer campaigns significantly
elevate brand awareness, consumer trust, and purchase intention, translating
into measurable sales improvements across fashion, beauty, technology, and
food service sectors.
4. Mobile Marketing
The global proliferation of smartphones has positioned mobile marketing as
an indispensable component of the contemporary marketing mix. Mobile
channels—encompassing in -app advertising, SMS messaging, push
notifications, location -based services, and mobile -optimized e -commerce
interfaces—enable organizations to engage consumers at every stage of the
purchase journey with immediacy, personalization, and contextual precision
(Shankar et al., 2016). Research indicates that mobile marketing positively

communication is perceived as informative, timely, and trustworthy (Tsang

mobile-originated e-commerce transactions globally underscores the strategic
importance of mobile touchpoints in driving organizational sales performance
(Srivastva, 2024).
5. Theoretical Framework
The theoretical architecture of this study integrates four complementary
perspectives. The Stimulus -Organism-Response (S-O-R) model (Erensoy et
al., 2024) frames contemporary marketing stimuli —personalized AI
recommendations, influencer content, mobile not ifications—as external
inputs that activate internal cognitive and affective states, ultimately
culminating in observable consumer behaviors including purchase. The
Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) (Ajzen, 2020) explains how attitudes
toward marketing communication, subjective social norms cultivated through
influencer engagement, and perceived behavioral control —facilitated by
user-friendly mobile payment systems —jointly predict behavioral intention
and purchase behavior. The Technology Acceptance Model (T AM)
(Venkatesh & Davis, 2000) illuminates how perceived usefulness and ease of
use of AI -driven platforms and mobile applications determine consumer
adoption and engagement. Finally, the AIDA communication model provides
a sequential framework for understa nding how contemporary digital
marketing content guides consumers from initial attention, through interest
and desire, to actionable purchase decisions. Collectively, these theoretical
lenses offer a robust conceptual basis for examining how AI analytics,
influencer communication, and mobile engagement differentially and
collectively influence consumer behavior and organizational sales outcomes.

1. Research Design and Philosophy
This study adopts a mixed -methods research design grounded in the
pragmatic research paradigm, which supports methodological plurality in the
service of generating practically applicable knowledge about complex
organizational phenomena (Creswell & Clark, 2007). The quantitative strand
provides statistical evidence of the relationships between contemporary
marketing strategies and sales performance, while the qualitative strand —

2. Population, Sampling, and Participants
The target population comprised marketing and sales professionals employed
in digitally active organizations across the retail, technology, e -commerce,
services, and hospitality sectors. A purposive sampling strategy was
employed to ensure that selected re spondents possessed direct practical
experience with at least one of the study's focal marketing strategies: AI and
big data analytics, influencer marketing, or mobile marketing. Geographic
restrictions were not applied; participation was open to qualified
professionals across different national contexts, enhancing the diversity of
professional perspectives represented in the dataset. A total of 300 valid
responses were collected via an online survey administered through
professional networks and LinkedIn g roups. This sample size satisfies the
recommended ratio of approximately five to ten respondents per
measurement item, given the sixteen-item analytical instrument employed in
the study (Wamba et al., 2017).

Quantitative data were collected through a structured self -administered
questionnaire comprising five sections: demographic and professional
background; AI and Big Data Analytics (four items); Influencer Marketing
(four items); Mobile Marketing (four items ); and Sales Performance (four
items). All analytical items were measured on a five -point Likert scale
ranging from "Strongly Disagree" (1) to "Strongly Agree" (5). The
measurement scales for each construct were adapted from previously
validated instrument s, as detailed in Table N°1 below, ensuring content
validity and theoretical alignment.

5. Discussion and Theoretical Implications
The quantitative and qualitative findings of this study converge to support a
theoretically coherent and practically significant picture of how
contemporary digital marketing strategies contribute to organizational sales
performance. The primacy of AI and Big Data Analytics as a sales
performance predictor is consistent with the TAM framework: as AI -driven
platforms increase the perceived usefulness and personalization of marketing
communication, consumers engage more deeply and convert more efficiently
(Venkatesh & Davis, 2000). The case of Amazon operationalizes this
relationship compellingly, demonstrating that organizations with mature data
analytics capabilities can achieve sustained commercial advantage through
progressively refined customer intelligence.
The positive relationship between Influencer Marketing and sales
performance extends prior empirical findings by contextualizing the trust -
building mechanism within real organizational practice (Lou & Yuan, 2019;
Alalwan, 2020). The Nike case confirms that  influencer credibility operates
through TPB constructs —attitude formation and subjective norm
reinforcement—to reduce purchase uncertainty and foster brand loyalty. The
comparatively modest but still significant contribution of Mobile Marketing
to sales v ariance aligns with evidence that mobile engagement enhances
convenience and accessibility rather than acting as a primary persuasive
medium, consistent with the AIDA model's characterization of mobile
channels as particularly effective at the "action" sta ge of the consumer
decision process (Tsang et al., 2004).
The qualitative cross -case analysis additionally suggests that digital
marketing effectiveness is contingent upon industry context and
organizational digital maturity. Organizations operating in consumer -facing,
digitally advanced markets appear to realize  faster and clearer returns from

Conclusion
This study set out to examine the impact of three contemporary marketing
strategies—AI and Big Data Analytics, Influencer Marketing, and Mobile
Marketing—on organizational sales performance. Through a rigorous mixed-
methods design integrating quantitative survey analysis of 300 professionals
and qualitative case study evidence from Amazon, Nike, and Starbucks, the
investigation yielded several significant and mutually reinforcing findings.
Quantitatively, all three marketing strategies demonstrated statistically
significant positive relationships with sales performance. AI and Big Data
Analytics emerged as the most powerful predictor (β = 0.445), confirming the
centrality of data -driven pers onalization in contemporary marketing
effectiveness. Influencer Marketing (β = 0.333) and Mobile Marketing (β =
0.248) also contributed meaningfully to sales performance variance,
highlighting the complementary roles of trust-based digital endorsement and
mobile-facilitated convenience in the modern consumer journey. All three
research hypotheses (H1, H2, H3) were empirically supported.
Qualitatively, the cross -case thematic analysis revealed four convergent
strategic themes: data -driven personalization through AI analytics; trust -
building and credibility enhancement through influencer engagement; real -
time mobile customer interaction; and overarching customer-centric strategic
orientation. These themes illuminate the organizational mechanisms through
which digital marketing strategies translate into commercial performance,
providing actionable guidance for marketing practitioners in digit ally active
industries.
The study contributes theoretically by integrating the S-O-R, TPB, TAM, and
AIDA frameworks within a unified empirical examination of digital
marketing's performance implications, offering a holistic conceptual lens for
future scholarship. Practically, the  findings support increased organizational
investment in AI -powered marketing infrastructure, strategic influencer
partnership management, and mobile-first customer experience design as key
levers for sales performance improvement.
Several limitations warrant acknowledgment. The reliance on self -reported
survey data introduces potential social desirability and perception biases. The
cross-sectional design precludes causal inference and temporal understanding
of marketing performance dynamics. The sample, while professionally
diverse, is predominantly drawn from digitally advanced organizational
