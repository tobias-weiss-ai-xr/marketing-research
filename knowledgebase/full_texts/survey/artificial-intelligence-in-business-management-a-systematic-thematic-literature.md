ABSTRACT

Artificial Intelligence (AI) has become a transformative force in contemporary business administration,
enhancing strategic decision -making, operational efficiency, innovation, and organizational
competitiveness. However, research on AI remains fragmented across busin ess disciplines, limiting
understanding of the field’s intellectual structure, dominant themes, and future directions. This study
systematically synthesizes existing knowledge on AI applications in business administration, identifies
major thematic areas, highlights research gaps, and proposes a comprehensive future research agenda. A
Systematic Thematic Literature Review (STLR) was conducted following the Preferred Reporting Items
for Systematic Reviews and Meta -Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines. Peer -reviewed articles published
between 2015 and 2026 were retrieved from Scopus and Web of Science using predetermined inclusion
and exclusion criteria. Thematic synthesis was applied to identify patterns, conceptual developments, and
research trends. Eight major them es emerged: AI -driven decision -making, marketing and consumer
analytics, human resource management, financial management, supply chain and operations management,
ethical AI governance, human–AI collaboration, and strategic organizational transformation. The findings
indicate that AI has evolved from an operational automation tool into a strategic organizational capability
supporting digitalization, business performance, and sustainable competitive advantage. Key gaps concern
cross-functional AI integration,  Generative AI, Explainable AI, responsible governance, sustainability,
SME adoption, and empirical evidence from developing economies and diverse industry contexts.

Keywords: Artificial Intelligence; business administration; systematic thematic literature review; digital
transformation; strategic decision -making; AI Governance; Human –AI Collaboration; organizational
transformation.

1. INTRODUCTION

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is undoubtedly one of the most disruptive technological paradigms in
the 21st century and is currently changing the business landscape as we know it. AI has matured over the
last ten years from being a complimentary analytical tool to becoming a fundamental strategic driver for
organizational decisions, efficiency, customer engagement and innovation capacity. By leveraging
advanced AI capabilities like machine learning, natural language processing, deep learning, and predictive
analytics, organizations can now process large volumes of data, gain actionable insights, and improve the
quality of managerial decisions at an unprecedented speed and accuracy (Brynjolfsson & Mcafee, 2017 ).
AI is becoming increasingly vital in the context of volatility, uncertainty, complexity, and ambiguity
(VUCA), enhancing organizational adaptability and resilience in today's dynamic business landscape. AI -
powered systems are becoming more prevalent in t he business world to streamline supply chain
management, improve marketing personalization, automate HR tasks, and facilitate better forecasting. The
widespread adoption of AI in business operations signals a shift in how enterprises are managed from the
typical intuition -based management to a data -driven and algorithm -driven decision -making framework
(Davenport & Ronanki, 2018).
In the last few years, the field of AI in Business Management has seen a significant increase in
research, particularly in relation to its growing importance. But despite this growth of publications, the
discipline is very fractured. Research is spread out  in various disciplines such as information systems,
management science, operations research, marketing, organizational studies, etc. This disciplinary
disintegration has posed difficulties to establishing a common framework of intellectual architecture an d
thematic development of AI in business contexts (Russell, 2010).
In addition, many current research articles have a limited scope of application, such as marketing
and customer analytics, HR and recruitment automation, or predictive maintenance and operation and
supply chain systems. Although these studies provide valua ble insights into the field of domain -specific
research, they do not reflect more comprehensive interdisciplinary integration of AI in business
management as a subject of research as a whole (Wamba et al., 2015 ). Consequently, there is no parallel
synthesis of the intellectual structures, signposting research groups, and growing themes that span the
field. Another important gap in the current body of work is the lack of research on the temporal
development of the theme of AI research. Over time, the field of AI and business has experienced dynamic
changes in its research focus due to the evolving nature of AI technologies and their applications. While
the initial studies were mostly on rule-based expert systems and decision support systems, current research
is starting to reflect an interest in machine learning algorithms, explainable AI, ethical AI governance and
human-AI collaboration frameworks (Dwivedi et al., 2023). However, there is limited literature attuned to
the systematic mapping of these thematic transitions.
Moreover, the ethical implications, algorithmic bias, transparency, and accountability of AI systems
have added new aspects to the field of business management research. AI systems are not just expected
to improve efficiency for organizations; they must also be fair, explainable, and compliant with regulations.
This has led to a growing emphasis within AI research beyond mere technical applications to socio -
technical governance frameworks (Shrestha et al., 2019).
To overcome such drawbacks, bibliometric analysis has been developed as a useful methodological
tool to analyse large amounts of scientific publications in a systematic way. Bibliometric approaches allow
numerical analysis of publication trends, citation n etworks and knowledge structure in a research area
compared with traditional narrative review approaches. Co -citation analysis, co -authorship analysis and
keyword co -occurrence mapping are all techniques that provide valuable insights into the intellectual
architecture and evolution of scientific domains (Aria & Cuccurullo, 2017 ). In addition to bibliometric
methods, thematic analysis can be used to highlight and categorize the most prevalent and new ideas in a
research area and to interpret them. A way to combine bibliometric mapping with thematic structuring is
to develop a full picture of the structural and conceptual development of academic literature. This all -in-

literature in one place. Second, it pinpoints broad themati c groups and their interconnections, and thus
deepens the comprehension of the conceptual structure of the field. Third, it identifies some new research
directions like explainable AI, ethical governance, human-AI teaming and AI for sustainability, which are
useful for future academic research and management practices.

1.1. Research Questions
First, what are the major applications and organizational roles of artificial intelligence identified in
the business -management literature? . Second, w hat organizational, technological, managerial, and
governance factors shape the implementation and use of artificial intelligence in business management? .
Third, what major research gaps and future research directions emerge from the existing literature on
artificial intelligence in business management?

2. METHODOLOGY

A Systematic Thematic Literature Review (STLR) was used in this study to find, evaluate, and
integrate current AI research in business management. Major AI applications, implications on the
organization, emerging themes and gaps in research were reviewed. The review process was based on the
main steps of the PRISMA framework, which included identification, screening, eligibility assessment and
final inclusion. The research design did not include the bibliometric analysis; the research was about
systematic selection of literatures for the themes.
The literature for the topic was retrieved from both databases, namely, Scopus and Web of Science
Core Collection, which are widely used in the field of business, management, information systems, and
other related areas given that they are large databases that cover peer-reviewed research in the field. The
search included journal articles and review articles (English) from 2015 to 2026. The search strategy was
based on the concepts of artificial intelligence, business management, and organizational applicat ions, as
well as managerial outcomes. Artificial Intelligence, Generative Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning,
Deep Learning, Business Management, Organizational Adoption, Organizational Implementation,
Organizational Transformation, Decision Making,  Performance, Innovation, Strategy, Governance were
some key terms. The search strings and filters used in the databases were documented for transparency
and reproducibility.
Studies were selected based on the criteria for the publication period, language, database,
document-type, and business-management relevance. Only research that investigated the technical aspects
without any real business context has been excluded. Prior t o screening, duplicate records were deleted.
The other studies were screened for title and abstract and then full text assessed. The reasons for full text
exclusion were documented based on the pre-defined eligibility criteria and a PRISMA flow diagram was

3. THEMATIC FINDINGS AND DISCUSSION

3.1. Evolution of AI in Business Management
AI has moved from being a computational tool to an integral part of modern business operations.
Initial theorizations of AI in management mostly concerned with rule -based systems and managerial
decision-support tools to enhance operational efficiency and minimize managerial workload. But the field
of AI has undergone a significant transformation from a supportive technology to an organizational
capability thanks to the swift development of Machine Learning (ML), Deep Learning (DL), and high -
level data analytics.
Recent literature has highlighted the potential of AI not just for automating routine tasks but also
as a key driver of value creation, strategic foresight, and the formation of competitive advantage
(Brynjolfsson & Mcafee, 2017 ). Companies are putting more and more AI to use in critical business
operations like marketing intelligence, financial forecasting, human resources analytics, or supply chain
optimization. This shift is an example of a paradigm change from traditional mana gement systems to
intelligent, adaptive and data-driven organizational ecosystems (Benbya et al., 2020). There are three stages
of AI in business management. The initial phase was rule -based expert systems that emulate human
decision making. The second phase was the applications of predictive analytics and machine learning for
forecasting and optimization. The current phase is defined by autonomous and generative AI systems that
are able to learn, adapt and augment decisions in real-time. The sequence illustrates the growing complexity
and sophistication of the integration of AI in business settings (Shrestha et al., 2019).

3.3. AI in Marketing and Consumer Analytics
Marketing is one of the most advanced and extensively researched areas where AI can be applied
to business management. This allows businesses to leverage AI technologies to gain insights into consumer
behaviour from extensive data, and then create highly p ersonalized marketing strategies. Customer
Segmentation, Predictive Targeting and Recommendation Systems are the use cases in which machine
learning algorithms are widely used (Dwivedi et al., 2021 ). According to the research, AI can greatly
improve customer engagement by providing hyper-personalized experiences based on real-time customer
actions. Digital platforms leverage AI-powered recommendation systems that analyse user preferences and
provide personalized content, leading to higher conversion rates and customer retention. Moreover,
Natural Language Processing (NLP) capabilities enable companies to leverage social media engagement,
customer reviews, and feedback data to gauge consumer sentiments and perceptions about the brand (Vial,
2021). In spite of these benefits, there are emerging issues related to consumer privacy, data protection,
and ethical marketing among the literature. This raises concerns about transparency and consent as
consumers become more and more exposed to consumer data  being used for AI -targeting. The lack of
adequate mechanisms can lead to consumer mistrust and ethical questions regarding data usage in AI -
powered marketing systems (Ivanov & Dolgui, 2021).
3.4. AI in Human Resource Management (HRM)
Artificial Intelligence has changed HRM as it has automated many processes within the HR
function including recruitment, employee evaluation, workforce planning, and employee development.
HR systems that utilize AI are becoming more common in order to sift through resumes, forecast
employee performance, and discover skill gaps in offices.
Research shows that AI can streamline recruitment by cutting down on hiring time and boosting
the accuracy of the match between candidates and job openings (Floridi et al., 2018 ). Predictive HR
analytics can also help businesses to predict staff attrition and implement proactive retention plans. This
has resulted in better employee retention and lower recruitment expenses (Haenlein & Kaplan, 2019).
But there is the potential for algorithmic bias in automated HR systems that still needs to be
addressed. Bias in the training data can lead to AI systems perpetuating and potentially exacerbating
discriminatory trends in hiring and promotion outcomes. Thi s creates an ethical question of fairness,
equality and transparency in HRM practices (Huang & Rust, 2021 ). As a result, the importance of
explainable and accountable AI systems has been emphasized to ensure ethical compliance and
organizational accountability.

planning, and predictive maintenance are some of the major use cases for AI systems. The effectiveness
of AI in boosting the efficiency of supply chain operation s is also supported by research, which shows
that AI can increase the accuracy of supply chain forecasts and minimize disruptions in operations (Syam

3.6. AI in Financial Management and Risk Analytics
In the field of finance, AI is playing a key role in various applications, including risk assessment,
credit scoring, algorithmic trading, and fraud detection. Machine learning algorithms are becoming more
vital to financial institutions for the analysis of vast amounts of transactional information and identifying
anomalies. AI is proven to largely improve financial decision-making, as it boosts the accuracy of financial
risk prediction and reduces fraudulent activities (Wamba et al., 2017). Fraud detection solutions powered
by AI are used to detect fraud in real time, allowing suspicious activity to be identified quickly. Also,
algorithmic trading systems leverage AI to make quick trades on the basis of market chang  (Dakalbab et
al., 2024). While this progress has been made, there are still issues of transparency and interpretability of
AI models. Financial regulators and stakeholders have a hard time understanding how complex machine
learning models make decisions, as the models are often referred to as black boxes. This poses a question
of holding them accountable in high-risk financial settings (Mittelstadt et al., 2016).

3.7. Ethical AI, Governance, and Responsible Management
Widely used in business applications, the use of AI has come into focus in the academic world as
a subject of ethical discussion. Issues under focus are algorithmic bias, transparency, accountability,
fairness and data privacy. As AI systems become more ad vanced and powerful, the need for responsible
AI governance is growing, as highlighted by research. Research underscores a growing pressure on
organizations to implement ethical AI governance frameworks to ensure responsible deployment of AI
systems. Transparency is essential when it comes to algorithmic decision making - if it is not present, then
people do not trust and the legitimacy of the organization is compromised (Ribeiro et al., 2016) . In
response to these challenges, there is a new solution known as Ex explainable AI (XAI) which helps people
understand how AI systems produce output. By leveraging XAI, trust, accountability, and regulatory
compliance are improved in decision-making systems that involve AI (Wilson & Daugherty, 2018).
3.8. Human-AI Collaboration and Organizational Transformation
Use of AI to enhance human creativity and decision -making in the creation of new business
models and processes. There is a lot of literature on the Human Augmented Artificial Intelligence from
the past years. There is a lot of literature on the Human Augme nted Artificial Intelligence over last few
years. Increasingly human -AI collaboration is regarded as a strategic way to improve organizations
productivity, creativity and innovation. Research suggests that an organization using hybrid intelligence
systems performs better compared to those that use only human or machine intelligence systems (Wilson
& Daugherty, 2018). AI systems are capable of handling vast amounts of data and performing tasks quickly
and efficiently, whereas humans are able to offer context and ethical considerations. But adapting the
workforce is still a significant challenge as employees are wary o f the impact of AI and are concerned
about job loss and skill redundancy. Reskilling and upskilling actions have to be taken by organizations to
ensure a seamless transition for AI integration.
3.9. Strategic Transformation and Competitive Advantage
AI is a key determinant in the organizational strategy and competitive edge. Companies that
effectively leverage AI in their core operations reaps numerous efficiencies, innovations, and market
responsiveness benefits. It is suggested that AI capability is  now emerging as a critical strategic resource
which improves the performance of the organization and its sustainability  (Mikalef & Gupta, 2021 ).

Thematic analysis of text sources of the literature AI usage in business management shows a very

4. INTEGRATIVE CONCEPTUAL FRAMEWORK
The literature reviewed suggests that AI is not simply a body of technologies, but is also a
technological capability that can impact on the way business functions, organisational capabilities,
governance mechanisms and strategic outcomes are achieved. This study builds an integrative conceptual
framework based on the thematic synthesis, which allows the different dimensions to be synthesized
within one analysis. The framework describes how AI technological capabilities are manifested through
business applications and how these applications in turn, lead to organizational capabilities and strategic
results, while governance supports and informs responsible and effective application of AI, and human
factors influence the successful adoption of AI.
The first level of AI technological capability is the technological underpinning of AI -supported
business management. Examples of these capabilities are machine learning, natural language processing,
generative AI, predictive analytics, automation, data -processing capabilities, and intelligent decision -
support systems. The presence of such capabilities allows organizations to make use of AI in various
business processes, and lays the groundwork for additional organizational adjustments in the future.
The second level, Business -Function Applications, includes business -function applications. AI
applications have been described in the literature in the fields of decision-making, marketing and customer
analytics, HR management, financial management and ris k assessment, supply chain and operations
management. AI can be used to predict and inform, provide analytical insights, enhance marketing by
segmenting and personalizing, aid HRM by analyzing people and supporting recruitment, and optimize
financial management based on risk assessment and fraud detection, and optimize supply chain operations
through forecasting, optimization and intelligent monitoring. These applications are the most common
ways AI technological capabilities are integrated into the activities of organizations.
The third level is about the skills of the organization, which are acquired through the successful
use of AI in business processes. The synthesis highlights that the organizational mechanisms that are
important include analytical, organizational learning, innovation, dynamic, and digital transformation
capabilities. AI-generated data can enhance analytical skills and ongoing communication with AI systems
can support organizational learning and knowledge building. Likewise, AI implementation can aid in
innovation and organizational adjustment by allowing organizations to adapt and adjust quicker to
evolving markets, customer needs, and operational requirements.

Governance and human factors are introduced on the fourth level. The use of AI doesn't always
lead to positive organizational outcomes because the impact of using AI systems is dependent on the way
they are governed and how AI is used alongside human decis ion making. Therefore, responsible use of
AI, explainability, accountability, data governance and human -AI collaboration are significant factors in
the context of the use of AI. Governance mechanisms can be used to tackle issues of transparency,
fairness, privacy, accountability and responsible use, and human -AI collaboration enable the use of
technological capabilities alongside human judgment, contextual knowledge, creativity and managerial
experiences.
In the last level, strategic and organizational outcomes are presented. AI capabilities integrated into
business functions, backed by organizational capabilities and managed through the appropriate
mechanisms can help to achieve operational performance, in novation, organizational transformation,
resilience and sustainable value creation. These results are not definitive and are not to be assumed to be
directly a result of the use of AI. Instead, the framework suggests that organizational value is created as  a
result of the interaction of technological capabilities, functional applications, organizational capabilities,
governance mechanisms and human involvement.
Therefore, the framework suggests a "chain of interdependency" as follows: AI technological
capabilities support business function applications; business function applications support the
development of organizational capabilities; governance mechanisms an d human factors impact on the
responsible and effective use of the capabilities; and the overall process influences strategic and
organizational outcomes. This framework builds on the existing literature, combining technological,
functional, organizational, governance, and strategic aspects of AI into a unified conceptual framework

Observing the state of the art in artificial intelligence applications to business management seen in
Figure 2, there are some key research gaps. First, there is a lack of a cohesive, integrated understanding
and evaluation of theoretical perspectives, as evidence tends to focus on the capabilities of AI technologies
alone, organizational capabilities alone, gover nance alone, human –AI collaboration alone, and strategic
outcomes alone. There is limited research that helps to explain the dimensions together in a unified way
that explains the value creation of AI in the organization. Secondly, contextual evidence is not consistent.
The existing literature is focused in technologically advanced economies and larger organizations and the
developing economies, SMEs  (Small and Medium Sized Enterprises ), diverse industries, and different
institutional environments are relatively under-researched. Thirdly, methodological limitations are present
as most of the literature is based on cross -sectional surveys, conceptual studies, and individual case
analyses. Longitudinal, comparative and mixed -method evidence is still relatively scarce, limiting
understanding of change across the continuum of time in relation to organizational outcomes and the
capabilities of AI. Fourth, the quick advancement of technology ha s opened up new gaps regarding
generative AI, explainable AI, autonomous AI agents, and combining multiple AI functions into
organizational processes. Last but not least, the dimensions of organizations are not sufficiently addressed
in AI research. There is a need for more focus on leadership, employee adaptation, organizational learning,
cross-functional integration and governance to learn how organizations can be successful in integrating
AI into business processes. The gaps point to the need for additio nal research in the field of AI -driven
business transformation that is more context -sensitive, longitudinal, integrated, and organizationally
relevant.

management. While a range of existing research has greatly contributed to the understanding of AI's
applications in specific organizational functions, there are some new fields that need further research. The
next possible research directions are based on the thematic results and the identified research gaps.

5.1. Creating Business Strategy With Generative AI
Organizational practices have recently undergone a paradigm shift with the introduction of
Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI), which enables organizations to generate content, manage
knowledge, plan strategically and make decisions. Research lookin g at the long-term effects of its use on
business strategy is, however, limited. Further research is needed to explore the impact of GenAI on
innovation, enhancement of strategic capabilities, organizational learning, and competitive advantage.
Through longitudinal studies, researchers can gain insights into the ways in which organizations adopt
generative AI in their strategic management processes. Longitudinal studies can yield valuable insight into
how organizations are integrating generative AI into strategic management processes.

6. PRACTICAL IMPLICATIONS

The results of this systematic thematic review are highly informative for managers, organizations,
policy makers and researchers aiming to use AI to drive sustainable business growth and digital
transformation. This review process will bring together existing knowledge and provide an evidence-based
perspective to help inform better adoption of AI and strategy making. The findings imply that for
managers, the use of AI should not be considered only as an automation tool, but as an organizational
capability that enhances their management. To enhance efficiency, innovation, and competitiveness,
managers need to ensure that AI projects are aligned with thei r organizational objectives, incorporate AI
into their daily operations, and foster a culture of data-driven decision-making. Furthermore, investing in
employee education and promoting a learning environment will help them stay on the cutting edge of AI-
powered innovations.
To successfully implement AI, organizations must have a digital transformation strategy.
Companies need to evaluate the extent to which they are ready for digitalization, build strong data
management systems and create a clear roadmap for AI implementation that is in line with the long-term
business goals. Integrating AI requires collaboration and cross -functional working within the business
units, IT professionals and senior management team. Finally, governance structures that foster
transparency, accountability, and ethical AI implementation will help to build trust and ensure sustainable
innovation among stakeholders.
The review underscores the importance for policymakers to strike a balance between fostering
innovation and ensuring ethical use, data privacy and security, and public trust in AI. Governments should
ensure future development of digital infrastructure, AI training and reskilling for the demand of AI
capabilities. Moreover, partnerships among public institutions, industry and academia can support the
creation of responsible AI policies to support sustainable economic growth.
The review offers a number of opportunities for future research. Emerging topics like Generative
AI, Explainable AI, AI governance and AI enabled sustainability are approached with interdisciplinary and
longitudinal research by scholars. Research in develo ping economies and in small and medium sized
enterprises (SMEs) will help improve existing research generalizability and give a fuller picture of the
adoption of AI in different organization contexts.
In summary, the potential of AI for the future of education highlights the need for strategic

successful integration into the educational landscape. By  combining technology, people and policy,
organizations can maximize the benefits of AI, while ensuring that their businesses transform responsibly
and sustainably.

6.1. Theoretical Contributions
It is a systematic thematic review that brings some theoretical contributions to the literature on AI
in business management. The vast amount of AI-related research and its application have fragmented the
knowledge, as pieces of individual business functions or specific technological applications are studied.
The current review process aims at a more holistic perspective on the growing and complex application
of AI in current business management by systemat ically synthesizing this wide body of literature. One
contribution that this review makes is to bring together the disorganized stream of literature to provide a
conceptual perspective. The applications of AI in different domains, like marketing, finance, human
resource management, and supply chain management, have been studied in isolation so far. In this review,
these research streams are connected and the application of AI is discussed as a strategic organizational
capability that contributes to decision -making, innovation, operational excellence and long -term
competitiveness from various business functions.
Second, it pinpoints and clusters the main research themes of the present AI-business management
literature. Through its synthesis of recurring concepts and emerging topics, it gives a structured thematic
framework of the material, which helps to develop t heoretical knowledge and serves as a valuable basis
for further academic research.
Thirdly, this study suggests a broad-based research agenda for future research by identifying gaps
in the current literature. The suggested directions, such as Generative AI, Explainable AI, AI governance,
sustainability, and human –AI collaboration, build on existing theoretical work and promote
interdisciplinary studies in management, information systems, organizational behavior, and ethics.
Last, this review contributes to the theory of business management by bringing together various
aspects of business together in one concept. The review emphasizes that AI is not just a technology for
specific organizational roles, but also a technology tha t enables digital transformation and adds value
strategically to the organization. In sum, these contributions deal with a more solid theoretical grounding
for further study, and they serve to continue evolving the field of business management scholarship
through AI.

7. CONCLUSION

In today's digital era, AI has emerged as a revolutionary tool that is reshaping the ways businesses
make decisions, run operations, deliver customer experiences, and gain competitive edge. With the
growing evolution of AI technologies, research into the use of AI in various business functions has grown
significantly. At the same time, with the rising number of research works, there is also a lack of coherence
in knowledge, which makes it hard to build a global picture of the strategic role of AI in business
management. This systematic thematic review aimed to tackle this challenge, by summarizing the available
literature, pinpointing key research areas, mapping out the most important research gaps and suggesting
research directions.
The review also shows that the use of AI is not only about enhancing operational efficiency but
also a crucial organizational capability that contributes to digital transformation, innovation, and overall
business performance. Thematic analysis focused on areas of AI -driven decision making, marketing,
human resource management, finance, supply chain management, ethical use of AI, governance, and
collaboration between humans and AI. These themes collectively show that AI research is multidisciplinary
and also that AI is becoming increasingly important in various aspects of business management.
This review fills the gap by consolidating existing and disconnected research into one overarching
theme, giving a thorough overview of the field of AI for business management. Beyond the synthesis of
existing knowledge, the review also pinpoints several u nder-researched and under-explored themes such
as Generative AI, Explainable AI, responsible AI governance, sustainability, adoption of AI in developing
economies, and cross -functional organizational integration. The insights gained will support further
theoretical construction and stimulate research to use interdisciplinary and context -oriented
methodologies in studying the transformation of businesses through AI.
