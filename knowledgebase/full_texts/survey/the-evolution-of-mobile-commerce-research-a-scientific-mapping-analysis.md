ABSTRACT
This paper presents a bibliometric analysis in the Mobile Commerce (MC) field to assess publication trends and
thematic developments from 2000 to 2023. Using data from the Web of Science Core Collection and the SciMAT
tool, the study identifies five stages in the evolution of MC, segmented according to technological advancements,
and proposes a technology-driven periodization. Strategic diagrams and evolution maps are used to classify
themes into motor, basic or transversal, specialised and peripheral, and emerging or declining categories.
The results show a growing body of literature, reaching a peak in 2012 and maintaining sustained interest
until 2022. Significant shifts in research themes are observed, moving from early technological aspects to user
acceptance, satisfaction, consumer behaviour, trust, mobile marketing, and more recently, artificial intelligence
and sustainability.
The findings highlight that, despite ongoing challenges related to security and infrastructure, MC has a pos-
itive impact on economic growth and the continuous evolution of business practices. The study concludes by
identifying future research directions, including customer journey analysis, value co-creation, and the integration
of emerging technologies such as blockchain and augmented reality.
1. Introduction
It was in 2000 when 3 G technology improved connectivity and
mobile devices’ functionality (Chakraborty, 2013). Since then, research
and practitioners have paid attention to the topic of MC. The journey
from the first to the current mobile smartphones-with nearly limitless
capabilities- shows the exponential development of technology in a
short time. The evolution of the research has been proportional to that of
the technology (Siau et al., 2003; Hew, 2017; Chhonker et al., 2018;
Luceriet al., 2022).
MC was defined by Tarasewich as “all activities related to a (po-
tential) commercial transaction conducted through communications
networks that interface with wireless (or mobile) devices” (2002, p. 42).
According to GSMA Intelligence (2024), by mid-2024, over 6 billion
people globally subscribed to a mobile service. Consequently, the mobile
industry’s contribution to the global GDP was estimated at 5.8 trillion
dollars in 2024, largely fueled by the ongoing rollout of 5 G networks
(GSMA, 2024). Therefore, it is not only a matter of technology devel-
opment. Organisations and individuals have been able to adopt such
technologies for >20 years, and, nowadays, mobile devices have become
an integral part of everyone’s life, making concepts like co-creation and
the customer journey increasingly relevant, even if they have not yet
been fully explored within MC research.
In this context, mobile technology represents both a technological
and social opportunity that enables new forms of value creation and
interaction within mobile commerce ecosystems (Yenching Liu & Lin,
2025).
MC has experienced rapid growth driven by widespread smartphone
adoption, improved connectivity, and shifting consumer behaviors. It
now accounts for a significant share of global e-commerce and is
continually shaped by technological innovations and evolving business
models. However, despite its rapid advancement and promising outlook,
there is an ongoing debate about which trends will prove truly disrup-
tive and sustainable in the long term. From the integration of artificial
intelligence and immersive technologies like augmented reality to the
rising importance of social commerce and sustainability, various market
players hold differing views on the real impact and viability of these
innovations, highlighting the complexity and dynamism of the MC
ecosystem.

2. Theoretical framework
2.1. Introduction to MC
MC is a commercial modality that has experienced strong growth in
recent years to the point that it can be considered as widely consolidated
in developed countries and in strong expansion in global commerce
(GSMA, 2023). On the other hand, in the academic field it is an emerging
discipline in the process of consolidation and maturation (Hew, 2017;
Thangavel & Chandra, 2023).
As a commercial MC activity it involves the use of mobile technol-
ogies (Faqih & Jaradat, 2015) that reach high levels of popularity
(Nilashi et al., 2015) and can be applied in many different ways (Ha
et al., 2012). Among the reasons for the extensive growth of this com-
mercial modality are the possibility of presenting personalised and
convenient offers to the user, as well as the extensive speed experienced
in purchasing activities (Gupta et al., 2018; Hew et al., 2015) and
multiple aspects discussed extensively in the literature (Wu & Wang,
2005; Wang & Haigh, 2024).
Although MC originated as a subset of E-commerce, its rapid tech-
nological evolution and the unique characteristics of mobile platforms
have driven a distinct research trajectory. This evolution warrants a
focused comparison to highlight the differentiators between MC and
traditional E-commerce research.
Despite their overlap, MC and E-commerce (EC) differ in key
research trends that reflect the unique technological and behavioral
characteristics of mobile platforms. The following table (Table 1) shows
a brief Comparison between MC and EC trends.
Among the various proposals for defining the term MC, we can
highlight the one made by Tarasewich et al. (2002), who defines the
discipline as the set of “activities related to a (potential) commercial
transaction conducted through communications networks that interface
with wireless (or mobile) devices” (p. 42). On the other hand, Hew et al.
(2016) and Kim et al. (2009) point out that MC is any business trans-
action conducted through the network and mobile internet. For their
part, Wu and Wang (2005) consider it to be exchanges (direct or not)
with monetary value carried out through the use of wireless communi-
cation networks.
Following Mahatanankoon et al. (2005) and Hew (2017) it is
possible to identify two modalities of MC: delivery of content, on the one
hand, and conducting transactions. In the first case, it is about making
available to users, through the use of the mobile internet, messages of
very diverse content (financial news, sports, etc.). In the second case, the
aim is to carry out real commercial exchanges of products and/or
services.

research, is intrinsically linked to the evolution of the development of

tative leap took place with the appearance of third-generation technol-
ogy (3 G) through the Universal Mobile Telecommunications System
(UMTS), which provides access to broadband transmissions of packet
text, voice, video, etc. This new technology allows mobile telephony to
be used as “a phone, a computer, a television, a paper, a video

Although cell phones have been present in the world since the 1970s
(Saibaba, 2021), they were first commercialised in the 1980s. However,
the introduction of the mobile network in the commercial world began
in the period from 2000 to 2006. This period is characterised by the
development and deployment of third-generation technology (3 G),
since, compared to the previous technology, 2 G, the possibilities grew
exponentially with Internet access via mobile phones, video calls and
multimedia services. The 2 G technology only allowed voice service, text
messaging and the use of basic data.
From 2007 onwards, a new relevant and profound change was
observed, caused by the broad development of broadband in conjunc-
tion with the launch of the iPhone (2007) and the Android system
(2008), as well as the arrival of application distribution platforms for
cell phones (2008). All of this means the existence of a new scenario for
consumers, users, companies and even institutions in general.
In December 2009, fourth-generation technology (4 G) arrived.
Therefore, the third period of evolution began in 2010 and lasted until
2014. With the arrival of 4 G, there is a substantial improvement in the
conditions of the industry: dynamic access to information, high-speed
mobile Internet without noticeable interruptions. This makes it
possible to access live high-definition videos, download files and docu-
ments very quickly, as well as improved designs and resolutions on
smartphones. Not forgetting, of course, the development of mobile
payment systems (Google Wallet in 2011 and Google Pay in 2014).
From 2015 until 2019, new developments in mobile technology took
place along with relevant updates: camera performance, extended bat-
tery life, night modes, etc. All this led to a strong increase in mobile
broadband subscriptions (from 45 % to 74 %) relative to fixed broad-
band. Another highlight is the sharp increase in mobile penetration
worldwide to the point of showing dominance among cell phone users.
The last stage or turning point has occurred from 2020 onwards and
up to the present day. 5 G technology bursts in and a highly significant
improvement in connection speed on the mobile internet takes place. In
the business world, new opportunities and challenges open up, both for
users and companies, thanks to advances in artificial intelligence, ma-
chine learning, virtual or augmented reality technologies, etc.
2.3. Academic interest and bibliometric studies on mobile commerce
This development of MC and the strong increase in business activities
through the use of mobile devices has originated a great interest from
academics as shown by the various research works on the discipline from
a bibliometric perspective (Chhonker et al., 2018; Du & Li, 2019; Gupta
et al., 2018; Hew, 2017; Saibaba, 2021; Thangavel & Chandra, 2023;
Purnomo et al., 2021; Zerbini et al., 2022). We briefly outline the main
features of each of them.
The work of Hew (2017) focuses on a count or performance analysis
of scholarly publications on “MC and its applications” among papers
published in WoS in the period from 2000 to 2015. Moreover, it is a
global evaluation of the entire time horizon studied without presenting
partial results for different time periods. The search topics of the
research are “mobile commerce” and “m-commerce” joined with the
search operator OR, then there is a generic analysis of the MC topic. The
initial search identifies 1108 documents that are refined to leave only
journal articles in English. The author analyses 875 documents in terms
of impact factor, h-index, and citation counts.
Chhonker et al. (2018) provide the first relational or scientific
mapping study for the years 2008 to 2017. The study analyses 184
documents obtained from the Scopus database focusing the analysis on
MC from the perspective of technological adoption (Chhonker et al.,
2018, p. 211), without restriction to any type of scientific discipline. The
paper only takes into consideration articles published in English with a
clear empirical approach. It exclusively reports the results achieved for
the time period considered in terms of: year; journal; mobile applica-
tions; dominant theories; most published authors; co-authorship
network; construct relationship. The application used in the relational
analysis is Gephi.
Gupta et al. (2018), whose theme or topic is MC research, analyse the
results for 4803 documents obtained from the Scopus database for the
period between 2007 and 2016. The search was formulated to identify
MC-related articles, restricting the search to the period 2007–16 (Gupta
et al., 2018, p. 180). The analysis is performed both for the full-time
horizon and for two periods of identical length: 2007–11 and
2012–16. The authors present a performance or count analysis, in this
case of the following aspects: Most Productive Countries in Publications;
International Collaboration; Subject-Wise Distribution of Research;
Most Productive Organisations; Most Productive Authors; Medium of
Research Communication; significant Keywords; highly Cited Papers.
The study by Du and Li (2019) conducted among the publications
included in WoS between 2007 and 2018 related to the MC theme,
applying as search terms “Mobile commerce” or “M-commerce”, ana-
lyses 1130 documents from a scientific or intellectual mapping
perspective and a global perspective for the entire time horizon. The
authors use the CiteSpace program to analyse the following relational
aspects: most influential authors, institutions, countries, journals and
keywords. Based on the analysis carried out, the authors propose an
integrated scheme of MC studies based on I-Model. I-Model takes into
account 4 dimensions: technology, people, organisation/society, and
information.
With the MC theme, Purnomo et al. (2021) present the results ob-
tained with 3770 documents from the Scopus database published be-
tween 1996 and 2020. To collect the data, the authors used the following
search terms: “(TITLE-ABS-KEY (MC OR mobile-commerce OR mcom-
merce). Their relational analysis carried out with the VOSViewer
application is offered exclusively for the overall period studied, without
identifying additional time divisions. The results offered cover the
following contents: Most Productive Organisational Affiliations: Most
Individual Researcher; Country Based Documents; Subject Area; Docu-
ment Type; Year; Annual Publications; Research Theme Map; Document
Cited; Authorship Network.
Saibaba (2021) also focuses on the documents included in Scopus on
a topic already more oriented to retailing from the consumer's point of
view, mobile shopping behaviour, for the time period 2012–2018
without making divisions of it and presenting both relational and simple
counting aspects. The search keywords used by the author are
<<“m-shopping”, “mobile shopping”, “smartphone shopping”, “mobile
shopping website”, “m-commerce”, and “mobile shopping
application”>> (Saibaba, 2021, p. 11). For this purpose, the author uses
the following software applications: Bibexcel, VOSviewer and Scien-
topy. The aspects analysed in the 158 documents considered include
most productive journals, countries, institutions, papers, and authors.
Zerbini et al. (2022) focuses on the documents included in Web of

stages: 2000–2015 and 2016–2020. The research presents both perfor-
mance analysis (descriptive/simple counting) and scientific mapping

3. Materials and methods
In order to explore the state of the discipline of MC over time
-including the main previous topics covered and the current themes- we
carried out a bibliometric analysis using SciMAT science mapping tool
(Cobo et al., 2011).
There are various science mapping software tools available,
including Bibliometrix, VOSviewer, and SciMAT (Cobo et al., 2012;
Moral-Mu˜noz et al., 2020). According to prior studies, Bibliometrix of-
fers users an extensive suite of techniques through Biblioshiny, while
VOSviewer stands out for its compatibility with multiple data sources
and its advanced visualization capabilities. By contrast, SciMAT distin-
guishes itself with robust pre-processing and export features, making it
exceptionally well-suited for the analytic needs of this study (Cobo et al.,
2012; Moral-Mu˜noz et al., 2020). In particular, SciMAT is unique among
science mapping tools in enabling (a) the analysis of thematic evolution
across scientific fields, (b) an advanced keyword normalization process
which is essential for achieving reliable results, and (c) the integration of
thematic detection with key bibliometric indicators such as citation
counts and h-index values (Cobo et al., 2012). For these reasons, SciMAT
was selected as the primary software for this research, which aims to
provide a thematic overview and identify future research directions
using data sourced exclusively from the Web of Science.
These types of studies explore the impact of a topic, a group of re-
searchers, or a particular piece of work (Cobo et al., 2011). SCIMAT
includes the following stages: (1) data search; (2) data refinement; (3)
development of the network and normalisation; (4) map creation; (5)
analysis and visualisation; and (6) performance analysis (B¨orner et al.,
2005;Cobo et al., 2012). In the first step (i.e., data search), we searched
in the Web of Science (WoS) database (accessed in November 2023) for
relevant articles (see Table 2 for the used search query). The Web of
Science Core Collection was utilised due to its proficiency in identifying
the most relevant results from the journals with the highest standards
(Garrigos et al., 2018). Furthermore, this database ensures compre-
hensive coverage of the social sciences literature (Norris & Oppenheiem,
2007) and provides the supplementary feature of Keywords Plus TM, a
mechanism that selects frequently occurring words in the titles of cited
documents (Garfield & Sher, 1993).
Regarding the search criteria, we identified articles from the Web of
Science Core Collection using keywords related to “Mobile commerce”
such as “m-commerce” “Mobile business” “m-business” and “Mobile
shopp*,” among others, as shown in Table 2. The search was specifically
limited to the Web of Science categories of Management and Business,
including only articles and reviews. We selected “All languages,”
although the vast majority of the texts were in English.
Concerning keyword grouping, we initially obtained 3395 keywords.
Manual grouping was conducted in two phases: the first one with the
participation of an MC expert, and the second performed by an expert in
marketing. We applied a rigorous process and only merged terms rep-
resenting exactly the same concept—for example, mcommerce and m-
commerce, behaviour and behavior, emerging economies and emerging
markets. Similarly, abbreviations and plural and singular were grouped
with their full forms, such as Ad and advertisement, DSS and decision
support system, IT and Information Technology, among others. After
this process, a total of 3245 keywords remained, which were used for the
analysis conducted with SciMAT. As a result, 909 articles and reviews
were identified containing.

4. Results
The distribution of the 909 articles published from 2000 to 2023 is
shown in Fig. 2. The starting point is marked by the third Generation (3
G) with broad bandwidth CDMA, and IP technology. From then, the
academic interest in MC research has shown an increasing trend since
2001. In 2012 there was a peak, with the number of published articles
(60) almost three times the previous year amount, although the highest
number of articles published occurs in 2022 (89).
Although it is common to use periods with the same number of years,
this analysis considers five stages consistent with relevant technological
advances and peak periods in global mobile technology evolution
(2000–2006; 2007–2009; 2010–2014; 2015–2019; 2020–2023) (Fig. 3).
The first one (2000–2006) begins with deploying the third generation (3
G). Where 2 G only offered voice capacity, text messaging, and basic
data, mobile broadband technology brought together mobile internet
access, videocalls, and multimedia services. The second period started in
2007, a turning point in the mobile technology industry. Together with
broadband development, the iPhone launching in 2007, the birth of
Android in 2008, and the introduction of application distribution plat-
forms in 2008 predicted a new scenario for consumers and firms. The
arrival of 4 G networks in December 2009 signifies the beginning of the
third period (2010–2014). It meant a major improvement in the con-
ditions of the mobile industry, such as dynamic information access and
high-speed mobile internet without interruption. It made it possible to
stream high-definition videos, download files quickly, and access HD
video streaming but also improved resolution and design in smart-
phones. Additionally, mobile payment systems were developed (Google
Wallet in 2011 and Google Pay 2014). These systems enable new con-
ditions for MC success. During 2015–2019, developments in mobile
technology (such as camera performance, larger batteries, or night
modes) and significant updates were the basis of adoption in individuals
and firms. According to ITU (2022) the mobile broadband subscriptions
grew from 45 % to 74 % while fixed-broadband subscriptions finished
the year at 15 %. Furthermore, mobile telephone penetration reached a
106 % rate in 2019. This shows an important trend in global inhabitants
who definitively went online in favour of mobile phones. In the period
2020–2023 mobile internet connection speeds were improved signifi-
cantly thanks to the global deployment and expansion of 5 G. Ad-
vancements in artificial intelligence, machine learning, bibliometric
authentication, and the development of augmented or virtual reality
technologies, among others, open new opportunities for businesses and
consumers.
In this subsection we begin identifying the subtopics of MC covered
for each period of time using strategic diagrams, then we examine the
progress of shared keywords between periods. Finally, we conclude with
the evolution of themes through the evolutionary map of the terms used
in this field.
4.1. Thematic evolution of the research field (2000–2023)
The evolutionary map (Fig. 4) represents a picture of the lexicog-
raphy of the field of study. It enables interpreting how different research
themes have developed. Specifically, this longitudinal or evolutionary
map describes the growth of the MC discipline throughout the following
subperiods: 2000–2006, 2007–2009, 2010–2014, 2015–2019,
2020–2023.
The evolution map reveals very clearly how MC research is largely
aligned with technological development. The beginnings of this research
topic (2000–2006) are marked by the importance of the antecedents of
mobile technology use. A research theme, such as E-commerce, was also
mentioned in the first subperiod, but this theme quickly evolved to MC,
and technology in the subperiod 2007–2009, illustrating the huge
irruption that the appearance of the smartphone entailed for the in-
dustry. Although this term had appeared earlier (Pedersen, 2001), in this
period MC started being considered by the research community as a
channel with differentiated characteristics from e-commerce. However,
MC applications failed to meet expectations (Liang & Wei, 2004). As a
consequence, “it is (was) important to shed light on the technological
characteristics affecting the adoption of mobile commerce” (Khalifa & Ning,
2008; Jim´enez-San-Martín, 2017) and during the second and third
subperiods interest in technology and technology adoption remained,
driving to adoption (2010–2014). One period after (2015–2019), adop-
tion evolved to user-acceptance.
User-acceptance takes place when the use of technology becomes part
of the user’s everyday life, providing additional information about the
experience of buying with a mobile device. Still, variables derived from
technology, such as information, perceived usefulness and perceived risk
seem to be relevant. This persistence of utilitarian constructs confirms
the explanatory strength of dominant technology adoption frameworks
such as the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) and the Unified The-
ory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT), which have struc-
tured MC research around performance-based and efficiency-oriented
determinants. However, based on the evolution chart, Mobile Marketing
transitions to M-Shopping and social media, showing a new reality for
apps and augmented reality in the following period. This denoted the
great creativity that companies deployed to reach consumers. In this
sense, research during this period combined new approaches with
traditional themes covered by the research field. As an example, Qin
et al. (2021) proposed a conceptual framework that combined Stimulus-
Organism- Response with other theories like TAM (Davis, 1989) to
demonstrate how real time mobile augmented-reality apps shape con-
sumer behavior.

During this period, Balasubramanian (2002) presented a relevant
conceptualisation of MC, including opportunities and a taxonomy of
applications. MC was not still practical in terms of connection or de-
vices’ capabilities. The academia faced questions about perceptions and
factors related to mobile technology acceptance in the context of busi-

e-commerce” reflecting the perspective of this period. Antecedents, as a

mobile technology acceptance, Bruner and Kumar (2005) analysed the
antecedents of using mobile devices, testing the Technology Acceptance
Model (TAM) in a consumer context and discovered the effect that the
hedonic component had on attitude toward this technology. Soon, mo-
bile users were seen as potential consumers and researchers started
asking consumer-oriented and marketer-oriented questions, such as the
effect of mobile advertising on consumers. One of the most cited studies
in this period, that of Tsang et al. 2004, discussed consumer attitudes
toward receiving SMS-based mobile advertising, recommending
permission-based marketing as a key approach in the mobile marketing
context. From the consumer’s perspective, Wu and Wang (2005)
wondered what drove MC in the Business to Consumer (B2C) MC
environment. At the end of the period, Ling and Wang (2006) address
relational variables such as loyalty, satisfaction, and trust in this new
channel.
During this period, e-commerce was a transversal theme (it presents
important ties with the rest of the themes). Its centrality is based on the
fact that e-commerce is seen as the MC predecessor, and, consequently,
its theories and findings are used to understand the new kind of business.

5. Conclusions
This study robustly illustrates the significant growth trajectory and
evolving landscape of MC research from 2000 to 2023. MC has transi-
tioned from an emergent technology to a mainstream commercial force,
with advancements such as the rise of smartphones and 5 G networks
catalysing its rapid proliferation. The bibliometric analysis has mapped
out the thematic developments in MC, revealing the field's responsive-
ness to technological advancements and shifting consumer behaviours.
This evolution also reflects broader theories of technology adoption,
such as the Technology Acceptance Model (Davis, 1989) and the Unified
Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (Venkatesh et al., 2003),
which help explain how perceptions of usefulness and trust have influ-
enced MC uptake. This approach also enables a more contextualized
understanding of how theoretical and technological shifts have inter-
acted, including periods of exogenous shocks such as the covid-19
pandemic, which accelerated digital transactions worldwide (Vinerean
et al., 2022).
Five aspects of our research differ from previous bibliometric ana-
lyses on MC. First, in our analysis, we employed WoS instead of Scopus
(Chhonker et al., 2018; Gupta et al., 2018; Purnomo et al., 2021; Sai-
baba, 2021). Second, certain studies apply a purely bibliometric per-
formance or count type of analysis versus our relational (scientific or
intellectual mapping) approach, such as the work of Hew (2017) and
Gupta et al. (2018). Third, studies that apply the scientific mapping
methodology employ software for bibliometric analysis that is different
from ours: specifically, Gephi (Chhonker et al., 2018), VOSViewer
(Purnomo et al., 2021; Saibaba, 2021), CiteSpace (Du & Li, 2019), R
Biblioshiny (Thangavel & Chandra, 2023). SciMAT presents a potent
pre-processing and export functionality that fits the analysis’ re-
quirements of this research. SciMAT software enables (1) analysing the
thematic evolution of a discipline, (2) developing the keyword nor-
malisation process and (3) enriching the detected academic themes with
bibliometric indicators (citations, h-index, etc.) (Moral et al., 2020).
Fourth, this study covers a broader temporal scope: the articles indicated
do not go beyond the year 2022 and none of them fully coincides with
our time horizon: 2000–2023. Fifthly, the division of the time horizon
into different periods of analysis; in this sense, our study is the only one

Establishing a parallel between the development of this topic and the
life cycle of a product, it is possible to recognise the phases of intro-
duction, expansion and maturity in which the topic currently finds itself.
The introduction period corresponds to the time frame from 2000 to
2005 and is characterized by a limited number of publications. During
these years, the potential of the technology was beginning to be
visualised by scholars. They were mainly concerned with the back-
ground of using mobile technology, as users showed reluctance in terms
of security and lack of familiarity with mobile technology. This period
focuses on the intention or willingness to use a technology or to consider
MC as a feasible option to buy. This aligns with the early stages of
innovation diffusion theory (Rogers, 2003), where perceived risk and
lack of knowledge slow down initial adoption.
A period of expansion began in 2006 and lasted until 2016. During
this time, a significant increase in publications was observed, reaching a
peak of 60 in 2012. The studies mainly combined themes of intention
and adoption with aspects that were relevant during the introductory
phase of the research line (such as risk or trust) and the contents grad-
ually diversify in relation to consumer behaviour. The adoption of m-
marketing among companies acted as a facilitator of empirical research
and marked the transition to the maturity period. Between 2006 and
2016, debate around MC and mobile marketing focused on overcoming
technical barriers, evolving from technological to consumer-centered
perspectives (Shankar et al., 2010). It was also debated how de-
mographic differences influenced risk perception and willingness to use
mobile services, and the need for data protection mechanisms to foster
trust and adoption was emphasized (Zhang et al., 2015). This period
marked the consolidation of theoretical and managerial discussions on
how mobile technologies reshape marketing strategies and user expe-
rience, and the value of mobile marketing for consumers and retailers
(Str¨om et al., 2014).
In 2017, the MC line shows signs of entering the maturity stage. The
number of publications stabilised following a flat annual trend around
60 articles until 2019, representing sustained interest over time. This
takes place once a positive perception of the use of mobile devices for
shopping is generated in consumers. The research primarily focuses on
buyer acceptance and their shopping experience. Recent debates in the
field have shifted from adoption to continued use and user experience,
questioning the ongoing relevance of the Technology Acceptance Model
and favoring broader frameworks like the Unified Theory of Acceptance
and Use of Technology, highlighting the user’s active role and value co-
creation (Shankar et al., 2010). A second sub-period could be distin-
guished from a peak of publications in 2021 that is maintained today,
which suggests that the maturity period is still in force.
With an academic interest peaking in 2012 and publications hitting
their highest in 2022, the findings depict a vibrant and expanding
research domain. The evolution of key themes, from early technological
concerns to recent focus areas like artificial intelligence and sustain-
ability, underscores the field's dynamic nature. New technologies such
as AI and blockchain are raising theoretical questions about digital trust,
privacy, and consumer empowerment. The literature highlights ongoing
debates about regulation, data security, and the need for robust frame-
works to support blockchain-enabled MC (Rattanawiboonosom and Kan,
2024). While challenges such as security, infrastructure, and user-
friendliness somewhat persist, the study highlights that these are sur-
mountable barriers in the path of MC's advancement.
It is striking that the analysis does not highlight aspects such as the
customer journey or the importance of value co-creation. Aspects that
have proliferated in the design of the online shopping experience, but
seem to have received less attention in MC. Future studies may be
enriched by the development of these concepts and their relationship
with MC.
Overall, the study posits MC research as a multi-faceted domain with
significant contributions to economic growth, job creation, and business
praxis, underpinning its pivotal role in shaping the future of global
commerce. As we look ahead, MC is poised to usher in more innovative
changes, fostering a more integrated, efficient, and consumer-centric
commercial environment. In the future, it is important for research to
closely follow the innovations that are developed in the technological
and business fields, which will be key to avoid the decline of this line of
research when the number of users is increasing. On the other hand, it
will be important to closely follow the advances in e-commerce in order
to achieve synergies between both disciplines. One of the relevant as-
pects to analyse is the future channels for improving the user experience
(Ramos et al., 2019), integration with other technologies (such as
augmented reality or artificial intelligence) and blockchain (to improve
the security of transactions) (Sadiq et al., 2025).

evolving landscape of MC research from 2000 to 2023. MC has evolved
from an emerging technology to a mainstream commercial force, driven
by market demand, technological advancements such as smartphones
and 5 G networks, and shifting consumer behaviors. The bibliometric
analysis maps out the thematic developments in MC, highlighting the

Our research differs from previous bibliometric analyses on MC in
five key ways. First, we use a broader and more comprehensive database
(WoS vs. Scopus). Second, our approach emphasizes relational mapping
(scientific and intellectual connections) over simple bibliometric per-

normalization, and bibliometric enrichment, setting this study apart
from those using alternatives like VOSViewer or CiteSpace. Fourth, the
study encompasses a wider temporal scope, from 2000 to 2023,

segmentation of the timeline is guided by the evolution of mobile
technology, providing a market-driven lens to understand academic
trends.
The study identifies three distinct phases in MC research: intro-
duction (2000–2005), expansion (2006–2016), and maturity
(2017–present). During the introduction phase, research primarily
focused on overcoming user reluctance and addressing security concerns
as scholars explored the potential of the technology. The expansion
phase saw rapid growth in publications, driven by the adoption of mo-
bile marketing and diversification of research themes related to con-
sumer behavior. In the maturity phase, research has stabilized, focusing
on shopper acceptance, enhanced user experiences, and new techno-
logical integrations like AI and sustainability.
Market and technological trends have consistently influenced the
direction of MC research. The emergence of AI, augmented reality, and
blockchain demonstrates the ongoing convergence of technological
innovation and consumer expectations. For instance, AI is reshaping
personalization in MC, while blockchain offers enhanced transaction
security. These external developments have opened new avenues for
research, aligning with market needs and driving the field forward.

customer journey and value co‑creation—aspects increasingly critical in
digital commerce—but in MC they have received little focus, as research
has emphasized adoption and technological usability over interaction
processes and customer experience. One possible explanation for this
gap lies in the methodological and technological challenges associated
with capturing mobile interactions in real time. The fragmented and

6. Future research directions
Future research should address these gaps by analyzing mobile
processes in the context of co-creation and the customer journey, while
remaining attentive to technological and business model innovations to
keep pace with a rapidly expanding user base. Collaboration with e-
commerce research could further foster synergies, particularly in
exploring new channels, enhancing the user experience, and integrating
advanced technologies. In this regard, several research questions
emerge: How do mobile touchpoints uniquely influence the customer
journey compared to traditional e-commerce environments? What
mechanisms drive value co-creation in mobile contexts, and how do
these vary across demographic or geographic segments? How does the
integration of real-time data (e.g., geolocation or behavioral analytics)
reshape the sequencing and perception of mobile customer journeys? To
what extent do push notifications and algorithm-driven personalization
alter decision-making processes across different journey stages? How
can firms measure and model co-creation behaviors within app-based
ecosystems while ensuring compliance with evolving privacy
regulations?
Advancing this agenda will likely require methodological diversifi-
cation. Longitudinal studies could help capture how trust, engagement,
and co-creation practices develop as users become more accustomed to
mobile ecosystems. Experimental research may clarify the specific ef-
fects of interface features—such as personalization cues, visual immer-
sion, or privacy assurances—on user responses and decision-making.
Complementing these approaches with qualitative techniques, including
in-depth interviews, diary-based studies, or detailed journey mapping,
would allow scholars to better understand how individuals navigate
mobile touchpoints in everyday contexts. Combining quantitative and
qualitative evidence may therefore provide a more complete picture of
mobile interaction processes.
Theoretically, future work could benefit from drawing more explic-
itly on established perspectives in marketing and psychology. The
Stimulus–Organism–Response (S–O–R) framework offers a useful lens to
examine how mobile stimuli translate into cognitive and affective re-
actions and, ultimately, behavioral outcomes. Customer Journey Theory
can help structure analyses of how mobile devices reshape the sequence
and intensity of pre-purchase, purchase, and post-purchase experiences.
From psychology, Self-Determination Theory may clarify how mobile
platforms satisfy needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness,
thereby supporting sustained engagement. Flow Theory is also particu-
larly relevant for understanding immersive mobile environments,
especially in contexts involving augmented reality or gamified appli-
cations. Insights from Behavioral Economics—such as framing effects,
heuristics, or default settings—can further illuminate how mobile design
subtly influences consumer choices.
A more explicit cross-disciplinary dialogue would also strengthen the
field. Contributions from consumer psychology can deepen under-
standing of habit formation, emotional attachment, and perceived
control in mobile contexts, while sociological perspectives may shed
light on peer influence, digital communities, and social norms that shape
mobile commerce behaviors. Integrating these strands of research with
marketing scholarship would support more comprehensive explanations
of mobile commerce dynamics that extend beyond technology adoption
and incorporate experiential, cognitive, and social dimensions.
In this context, the emergence of super apps as a novel mobile
commerce paradigm offers a promising avenue for future research,
particularly in understanding how evolving platform architectures and
AI-driven service integration may reshape customer journey design and
value co-creation processes (Hasselwander & Weiss, 2025).
Overall, this study positions MC research as a dynamic and multi-
faceted domain, making significant contributions to global commerce,
economic growth, and job creation. As MC continues to influence market
practices, research must keep pace with external changes, ensuring that
it remains relevant and impactful. Looking ahead, MC is set to drive
further innovation, shaping a more integrated, efficient, and consumer-
centric commercial environment.
From a practical standpoint, the findings of this study offer valuable
insights for marketers, app developers, and retailers seeking to
strengthen their mobile strategies. Marketers can leverage the identified
trends to design more context-aware and personalized campaigns
aligned with users’ mobile behavior patterns. AppG developers can use
the research themes to prioritize usability, trust, and seamless integra-
tion of emerging technologies such as AI and AR, improving engagement
and conversion, as theory indicates. Retailers, meanwhile, can translate
academic insights on consumer acceptance and co-creation into strate-
gies that enhance loyalty, foster customer participation, and optimize
omnichannel experiences.
In addition, firms can use the technological trajectories identified in
this study to proactively align their business models with emerging in-
novations. Rather than adopting AI, augmented reality, or blockchain as
isolated tools, organizations should embed these technologies into their
core value propositions, for example, by developing data-driven
personalization systems, immersive mobile interfaces, or blockchain-
based mechanisms that enhance transaction transparency and trust.
Such alignment allows companies to move beyond incremental digital
adaptation toward structurally integrating innovation into their
competitive strategies.
The sustained presence of themes related to perceived risk, trust, and
data protection also indicates that privacy and security must be treated
as strategic priorities rather than mere regulatory obligations. Practi-
tioners can translate these insights into concrete actions by investing in
secure payment infrastructures, transparent data governance practices,
and user-centGered privacy controls that empower customers and
reinforce long-term engagement. By operationalizing these research
trends into forward-looking strategic decisions, firms can better syn-
chronize technological innovation, customer experience design, and risk
management, thereby narrowing the gap between academic knowledge
and industry practice in MC ecosystems.
