Research on MSME competitiveness has generally explored internal capabilities,
relational networks, and innovation as distinct factors contributing to competitive
advantage. Nevertheless, empirical evidence remains limited regarding the extent
to which mar keting innovation serves as a mediating pathway that connects
proactive behavior and relational capital with competitive advantage, especially
within locally rooted handicraft MSMEs. Accordingly, this study investigates the
influence of proactive behavior and relational capital on competitive advantage,
while also examining the mediating role of marketing innovation among
handicraft MSMEs in East Lombok Regency. The proposed conceptual framework
is grounded in the Resource-Based View (RBV) and Social Capital Theory, both of
which highlight the importance of distinctive organizational resources and
network-based relationships in fostering innovation and sustaining competitive
advantage. Using a quantitative explanatory approach, data were collected
through a survey involving 141 handicraft MSMEs and analyzed using Partial
Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM). The results reveal that
proactive behavior and relational capital positively affect marketing innovation
and competitive advantage. Howev er, marketing innovation does not exert a
significant positive influence on competitive advantage. In addition, marketing
innovation is not supported as a mediating variable in the relationship between
proactive behavior and competitive advantage or between relational capital and
competitive advantage. These findings suggest that proactive behavior and
relational capital function more strongly as direct strategic resources for
improving competitive advantage, whereas marketing innovation, in this
particular context, does not act as an effective mechanism for translating these
resources into competitive outcomes. From a theoretical perspective, this study
extends the application of RBV and Social Capital Theory by demonstrating that
innovation-oriented activi ties do not always convert internal capabilities and
relational assets into competitive advantage. From a practical standpoint,
handicraft MSMEs are encouraged to enhance proactive market-oriented actions
and strengthen relational networks, while also care fully assessing the relevance,
quality, and market alignment of their marketing innovation initiatives.

1. INTRODUCTION
Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) in the handicraft sector play an important role in
Indonesia’s regional economy because they generate employment, support household -based
production, and preserve local cultural values. In the creative economy context, handicraft MSMEs are
not merely economic units, but also carriers of local identity, tra ditional knowledge, and community -
based entrepreneurship. However, this sector increasingly faces competitive pressure from product
similarity, price competition, changing consumer preferences, digital marketing disruption, and the need
for stronger market -oriented differentiation (Kraus, Durst, et al., 2022;  Trianto et al., 2025) . These
conditions make competitive advantage a critical issue for MSME business sustainability, particularly for
business actors whose tangible resources, managerial capacity, and market access remain limited
(Kuncoro & Suriani, 2018; Pratono et al., 2019; Quaye & Mensah, 2019).
The urgency of this issue can be seen in East Lombok Regency, West Nusa Tenggara Province, where
handicraft MSMEs are closely connected to local wisdom and tourism-based economic activities. Several
craft clusters illustrate this potential, including tradi tional weaving in Pringgasela, bamboo weaving in
Loyok, and pottery in Penakak/Masbagik. Pringgasela is widely known for traditional woven textiles,
including Squishy weaving, which reflects Sasak cultural identity and inherited craft knowledge (Haq et
al., 2024; Juniati, 2020). Loyok is promoted as a bamboo craft tourism village and is recognized as a
major bamboo handicraft center in West Nusa Tenggara (Kementerian Pariwisata Republik Indonesia,
2025). Meanwhile, Penakak/Masbagik has a long-standing reputation as a pottery production area, with
craft products rooted in local artistic and cultural practices (Putri et al., 2023) . Nevertheless, these
MSMEs still face structural marketing challenges, including limited modern promotion capability,
dependence on traditional sales channels, weak product positioning, limited collaboration, and uneven
adoption of digital marketing (Haq et al., 2024;  Kraus, Durst, et al., 2022) . Therefore, the competitive
problem of handicraft MSMEs in East Lombok is not merely a production issue, but also a behavioral,
relational, and marketing innovation issue.
From a theoretical perspective, this study is grounded in two complementary approaches: the
Resource-Based View and Social Capital Theory. The Resource -Based View explains how internal
resources and capabilities can become sources of sustained competitive advantage when they are
valuable, rare, difficult to imitate, and difficult to substitute (Barney, 1991; Hidayat, N., & Rahayu, 2024).
In this study, proactive behavior represents an internal behavioral resource because it reflects the ability
of MSME owners or managers to anticipate market changes, identify opportunities, initiate strategic
actions, and respond faster than competitors (Bateman & Crant, 1993; Hassan & Abdelkader, 2024). In
contrast, Social Capital Theory explains how external relationships create value through trust,
information exchange, cooperation, and access to resources beyond the firm’s internal boundaries
(Nahapiet & Ghoshal, 1998). Relational capital is therefore positioned as a network-based resource that
enables MSMEs to obtain market information, strengthen customer and supplier relationships, and
develop collaborative marketing (Juo & Wang, 2022; Nahapiet & Ghoshal, 1998). These two theories are
complementary because competitive advantage in handicraft MSMEs is formed not only through internal
entrepreneurial behavior , but also through the ability to mobilize external relationships into market-
relevant capabilities.
In a dynamic and increasingly digitalized market, proactive behavior and relational capital may not
automatically generate competitive advantage unless they are translated into concrete innovation
practices. Proactive MSME actors are more likely to search for new market opportunities, experiment
with promotional approaches, adapt to consumer preferences, and introduce more responsive
marketing strategies (Bateman & Crant, 1993;  Hassan & Abdelkader, 2024) . Meanwhile, strong
relational capital with customers, suppliers, communities, tourism actors, government institutions, and
business partners can improve access to market information, reduce uncertainty, and facilitate learning
and collaboration (Juo & Wang, 2022; Nahapiet & Ghoshal, 1998). These resources become strategically
meaningful when they encourage marketing innovation, such as the renewal of promotional channels,

Recent MSME studies emphasize that competitive advantage is largely determined by the ability to
convert available resources into capabilities that are relevant to market change (Hidayat, N., & Rahayu,
2024; Trianto et al., 2025) . Marketing innovation is particularly important for micro and small
enterprises because it allows firms with limited tangible resources to compete through creativity,
customer closeness, adaptive promotion, and differentiated market positioning (Quaye & Mensah, 2019;
Trianto et al., 2025) . However, much of the MSME literature still tends to treat innovation as a direct
outcome rather than as a mediating mechanism that explains how behavioral and relational resources
are transformed into competitive advantage (Ferreira et al., 2021;  Hwang et al., 2020) . In addition,
previous studies more frequently examine product innovation, technological innovation, or digital
transformation, while the mediating role of marketing innovation remains relatively underexplored,
especially in traditional handicraft -based MSMEs in developing regional contexts (Kraus, Breier, et al.,
2022; Quaye & Mensah, 2019).
Drawing on the preceding arguments, this study identifies three key gaps in the existing literature.
The first concerns the mechanism gap, as marketing innovation has not been adequately examined as an
intermediary pathway through which proactive behavior and relational capital contribute to competitive
advantage. The second relates to the integration gap, since proactive behavior , which reflects actions at
the owner or managerial level, and relational capital, which represents network -based resources, are
commonly investigated as separate constructs rather than as interconnected determinants. The third is
a contextual gap, as empirical studies focusing on handicraft MSMEs in East Lombok remain scarce, even
though the region is characterized by unique craft clusters, strong connections with tourism activities,
traditional modes of production, and increasing challenges in digital marketing adaptation. In response
to these gaps, this study seeks to examine the effects of proactive behavior and relational capital on the
competitive advantage of handicraft MSMEs in East Lombok Regency, while positioning marketing
innovation as a mediating variable.

2. LITERATURE REVIEW
2.1 Competitive Advantage
The conceptual model in this study is built on the basis of Resource-Based View (RBV) Theory which
explains that competitive advantage is born from the use of unique resources and internal capabilities
that are valuable, rare, and difficult to replicate (Barney, 1991; Penrose, 1959) . This framework is
enriched by the Social Capital Theory/Network perspective which emphasizes the role of relationships,
trust, and networks in facilitating the flow of information and collaboration that is relevant to marketing
innovation (Muna et al., 2024) and Social Capital Theory which emphasizes the role of unique resources
and relationships/networks in driving innovation and competitive advantage (Hidayat, N., & Rahayu,
2024).
Competitive advantage is the relative position of MSMEs that are superior to competitors through
differentiation, reputation, responsiveness, and the ability to retain customers and values that are
difficult to replicate (Hidayat, N., & Rahayu, 2024). Competitive advantage can be created by converting
internal resources and external networks, thus being able to create value that is difficult to replicate
(Hidayat, N., & Rahayu, 2024).
2.2 Proactive Behavior
Proactive Behavior is defined as the behavior of an individual or organization that actively initiates
change, anticipates opportunities, and acts before external pressures arise (Battistelli et al., 2021) .
(Hassan & Abdelkader, 2024)  also defines Proactive behavior as an individual's tendency to take
initiative, anticipate change, and create improvements that are relevant to market opportunities.
Proactive behavior encourages marketing innovation because proactive business actors tend  to be
quicker to try new channels, craft creative promotions, and respond to changing market preferences.

Proactive behavior can also have a direct impact on competitive advantage as it encourages the speed of
strategic decision-making and the ability to take advantage of opportunities before competitors (Hassan
& Abdelkader, 2024) . Risk-taking is implied by the courage to take risky actions related to business
management and the use of business resources for business opportunities where there is an element of
uncertainty (Lumpkin and Dess, 1996).
In the context of MSMEs, proactive behavior encourages business actors to try new markets, carry
out marketing innovations and increase business competitiveness (Kraus, Breier, et al., 2022) . The
proactive behavior of MSME actors is an intangible resource that is able to create a competitive
advantage (Barney, 1991). Empirical studies show that proactive behavior contributes positively to
innovation and the competitive performance of MSMEs (Kraus, Breier, et al., 2022). Research (Keh et al.,
2007) shows that proactive behavior helps MSMEs recognize market opportunities and improve
business performance. The proactive dimension in entrepreneurial orientation also has a positive
relationship with business performance and competitive advantage (Rauch et al., 2009). The empirical
findings in this study indicate the positive influence  of proactive behavior on the competitive advantage
of MSMEs, which further becomes the basis for the formulation of the following hypothesis.
H1:  Proactive behavior has a positive effect on competitive advantage.
H3:  Proactive behavior has a positive effect on marketing innovation.
2.3 Relational Capital
Relational capital is the knowledge contained in the network between a company and external
stakeholders such as customers, trade associations, suppliers and the government that can affect
business performance (Bontis, 1998) . The evaluation of relationships within the organization and
among individuals is referred to as relational capital. According to (Lavie, 2006) , in a networked
environment, relationships are more important to a business than resources. Relational capital makes it
easier to create and acquire knowledge using internal and external resources. The value created from
relationships with customers is able to provide better performance for the company.
Relational capital is an intangible asset that is reflected in the quality of MSMEs' relationships with
customers, suppliers, and business partners (Asiaei et al., 2020) . Strong relationships allow for
information exchange and collaboration that drive innovation and competitive advantage (Xu & Wang,
2022). Relational capital refers to the quality of relationships with customers, suppliers, communities,
and partners that are characterized by trust, interaction intensity, and access to information that is
valuable for business development (Arif, 2025) . Relational capital has been proven to strengthen
marketing innovation through access to market information, partner support, and relationship
credibility that makes it easier for MSMEs to experiment with marketing strategies (Arif, 2025).
Recent research has found that relational capital plays a significant role in increasing innovation
and competitive advantage, especially in community -based small businesses (Xu & Wang, 2022 ).
Relational capital has a direct effect on competitive advantage through reputation, customer loyalty, and
network support that strengthens the market position of MSMEs (Sutrisno et al., 2024). The empirical
findings in this study indicate the positive influence of relational capital on  the competitive advantage
of  MSMEs, which subsequently becomes the basis for the formulation of the following hypothesis.
H2: Relational capital has a positive effect on competitive advantage.
H4:  Relational capital has a positive effect on marketing innovation.
2.4 Marketing Innovation
Marketing innovation includes updates in marketing methods, product design, promotion, and
distribution channels. Marketing innovation allows handicraft MSMEs to create differentiation and
added value based on local culture. Marketing innovation is an upda te to the way MSMEs design,
communicate, distribute, and place offerings across channels, promotions, communications, and market
approaches, thereby increasing value for customers (Trianto et al., 2025) . Marketing innovation
increases competitive advantage because it is able to create differentiation and added value that is more
relevant to customers and expand market reach (Trianto et al., 2025) . Studies show that marketing

3. METHOD
This study adopted a quantitative explanatory survey approach to investigate how proactive
behavior and relational capital contribute to competitive advantage, while also assessing the mediating
role of marketing innovation. This methodological approach wa s considered suitable because the
research was designed to empirically test the proposed relationships among latent variables using
survey-based data (Creswell, 2012). The study population comprised 219 handicraft MSMEs located in
East Lombok Regency, West Nusa Tenggara, Indonesia, as recorded by the NTB Industry Service in 2023.
The data collection process was carried out from March to May 2025.
Respondents were determined through purposive sampling, as the study required participants with
sufficient knowledge of their firms’ marketing practices and operational conditions (Etikan et al., 2016).
The selected respondents were owners or managers of MSMEs who fulfilled four inclusion criteria: the
enterprise had been operating for a minimum of two years, the respondent was actively involved in
marketing-related activities, the business remained act ive at the time of data collection, and the
enterprise belonged to the creative economy -based handicraft MSME category listed by the NTB
Industry Service. The required minimum sample size was estimated using the Raosoft sample size
calculator with a 5% mar gin of error . Based on the population of 219 MSMEs, the minimum sample
requirement was 140 units. This study involved 141 respondents, which was deemed sufficient for PLS-
SEM analysis because the research model consisted of reflective latent constructs and  emphasized
prediction-oriented analysis (Hair & Alamer , 2022; Hair Jr et al., 2021).
The data were gathered using a structured questionnaire measured on a five -point Likert scale,
ranging from 1, indicating strongly disagree, to 5, indicating strongly agree (Likert, 1932). Since the
measurement indicators were derived from prior studies and adapted to the context of Indonesian
MSMEs, the questionnaire underwent a review process to ensure linguistic clarity and contextual
appropriateness. Prior to the main survey, a pre -test was administered to 15 handicraft MSME actors
who shared similar characteristics with the target respondents but were excluded from the final sample.
Based on the pre -test results, several minor revisions were made to improve the wording of the
instrument, in line with established procedures for questionnaire adaptation and pre-testing (Beaton et
al., 2000; Brislin, 1970).
Proactive Behavior was measured using five indicators reflecting the ability of MSME actors to
develop new marketing ideas, encourage continuous improvement, anticipate changes in customer
demand, act proactively in facing future market changes, and take i nitiative in exploiting market
opportunities. The indicators were adapted from the literature on proactive behavior and
entrepreneurial orientation (Bateman & Crant, 1993) . Relational Capital was initially measured using
five indicators: customer trust, relationships with suppliers or business partners, access to market
information and distribution channels, collaboration with banks, and collaboration with government
institutions. These indicators were adapted from the social capital and intellectual capital literature,
which views external relationships as strategic resources that support information access, trust, and
collaboration (Bontis, 1998; Nahapiet & Ghoshal, 1998).
The data analysis was performed using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -
SEM) with the assistance of SmartPLS 3.2.9. This analytical approach was considered appropriate
because PLS-SEM is well suited for research involving relatively l imited sample sizes, reflective latent
variables, and models that emphasize prediction (Hair & Alamer, 2022;  Hair Jr et al., 2021) . The
analytical procedure was carried out in two main phases. First, the measurement model was assessed
by examining outer loadings, Cronbach’s alpha, composite reliability, Average Variance Extracted, the
Fornell-Larcker criterion, and the Heterotrait -Monotrait ratio. Second, the structural model was
evaluated through collinearity diagnostics, path coefficients, the coefficient of determination, effect size,
predictive relevance, and mediation analysis. Finally, the proposed hypotheses were tested using a
bootstrapping procedure with 5,000 resamples.

During measurement model evaluation, two relational capital indicators, namely collaboration with
banks and collaboration with government institutions, were removed because their outer loadings did
not meet the recommended threshold. This removal was stati stically justified and substantively
meaningful. Therefore, the final relational capital construct should be interpreted as market -based
relational capital consisting of customer trust, supplier or business partner relationships, and access to
market information or distribution channels. The discussion of relational capital in this study is limited
to this final measurement scope.
Given that the data were obtained from a single group of respondents using a self -administered
questionnaire, the study evaluated the potential presence of common method bias through both
procedural and statistical approaches. At the procedural level, seve ral preventive measures were
applied, including ensuring respondent anonymity, formulating questionnaire items in a clear and
understandable manner, and arranging predictor and criterion variables separately within the
instrument. For the statistical assessment, Harman’s single-factor test and the full collinearity variance
inflation factor (VIF) were employed. The result of Harman’s single-factor test indicated that the largest
single factor accounted for 37% of the total variance, which remains below the commonly accepted
threshold of 50%. Moreover, all full collinearity VIF values were lower than 3.3. These findings suggest
that common method bias did not represent a substantial concern for the validity of the proposed model
(Kock, 2015; Podsakoff et al., 2003).

This finding becomes even more relevant when it is associated with the characteristics of the

characteristics show that MSME actors rely more on interpersonal relationships and social networks in
running their businesses. Women business actors generally have good interpersonal communication

assets that can improve organizational performance and create sustainable competitiveness.

that resources that are unique, difficult to replicate, and of strategic value can be a source of competitive
advantage. In the context of this study, the network of relationships, customer trust, and the quality of
business relationships ar e intangible resources that are difficult for competitors to replicate because
they are formed through the process of social interaction and business experience in the long term. In
terms of previous research, the results of this study support the research  (Bontis, 1998) which found
that Relational Capital has a positive influence on organizational performance and the competitive

Proactive Behavior, Relational Capital and Marketing Innovation
The findings show that proactive behavior and relational capital in MSMEs make a positive
contribution to increasing marketing innovation. This means that the more appropriate MSMEs are in
utilizing the proactive behavior and relational capital they have, the more they will encourage MSMEs to
proactively carry out Marketing Innovation. The resu lts show that proactive behavior and relational
capital significantly encourage marketing innovation. Proactive MSME actors are more likely to try new
promotional approaches, adjust brand communication, and use alternative distribution channels. This
supports the argument that proactive behavior does not only influence market outcomes directly, but
also stimulates experimentation in marketing activities.
Relational capital also supports marketing innovation because market-based relationships provide
feedback, information, and customer understanding. Trust -based relationships with customers and
business partners can help MSMEs identify which marketing messa ges, product presentations, and
selling channels are more acceptable to the market. Nevertheless, the strength of this effect is smaller
than proactive behavior , suggesting that relational resources require active managerial initiative before
they can be transformed into marketing innovation. Judging from previous research, the results of this
study support the findings (Lumpkin & Dess, 1996) which states that proactivity has a positive influence
on business performance and competitive advantage, especially in organizations operating in a dynamic
business environment. In addition, this study is also consistent with the findings (Kraus, Breier, et al.,
2022) which explain that proactive behavior contributes positively to innovation and competitive
performance of MSMEs, proactive behavior encourages business actors to try new markets, carry out
marketing innovations and increase business competitiveness. Hypot hesis 4, namely that relational
capital has a positive and significant influence on marketing innovation is also supported. These findings
indicate that the stronger the relationship that business actors have with customers, suppliers, the
business community, and other external partners, the higher the ability of MSMEs to create marketing
innovations. In the context of MSMEs, marketing innovation is not only related to changes in promotional
strategies, but also includes updating how to communicate with con sumers, utilizing digital media,
developing distribution channels, and creating a more attractive customer experience that is in
accordance with market needs.
This research supports research that explains that Strong relationships allow for information
exchange and collaboration that drive innovation and competitive advantage (Xu & Wang, 2022). Recent
research has found that relational  capital plays a significant role in increasing innovation and
competitive advantage, especially in community -based small businesses (Khan et al., 2018) , strongly
related to relational capital and marketing innovation (Sutrisno et al., 2024) relational capital has been
proven to strengthen marketing innovation through access to market information, partner support, and
relationship credibility that makes it easier for MSMEs to experiment with marketing strategies (Arif,
2025). However, these findings do not support the research (Khan et al., 2018), that relational capital is
positive and significant to process innovation; (Beltramino et al., 2022)  also provides evidence that
human capital, structural capital and relational capital produce positive and significant effects on
innovation in processes and products.

Marketing Innovation and Competitive Advantage
Marketing innovation does not significantly affect competitive advantage in this study. This finding
suggests that the existence of new marketing methods, promotional techniques, or brand
communication does not automatically create a superior competitive p osition. For micro and small
handicraft businesses, marketing innovation may remain partial, irregular , or limited to surface-level
changes. It may improve visibility or communication but may not yet be strong enough to create
differentiation, customer loyalty, or market response that is difficult to imitate. There are two possible
explanations for this result. First, many handicraft MSMEs still face limited digital capability, managerial
capacity, and financial resources. These limitations can weaken the quality and consistency of marketing
innovation implementation. Second, the measurement of marketing innovation in this study used only
three indicators. Although these indicators were aligned with the Oslo Manual 2018, they did not fully
capture the broader domain of marketing innovation, such as pricing innovation, packaging, customer
experience, market segmentation, digital channel management, and customer relationship methods.
This narrower operationalization may partly explain why marketing innovation d id not significantly
affect competitive advantage.
The results of this study support the research (Kraus, Breier, et al., 2022) which states that most of
the research still focuses on product or technology innovation, while the role of marketing innovation as
a mediating variable has relatively not been studied much because many MSMEs do not focus too much
on marketing innovation. However, it does not support the research Khan (2018) which emphasizes the
importance of intervening variables in the form of innovation. This research focuses on MSMEs in the
handicraft industry, so that the sensitivity of innovation is higher. Therefore, in decision-making, MSME
managers must still pay mor e attention to the importance of product, process, and organizational
innovation, which will enable MSMEs to achieve a better competitive advantage. (Chahal and Bakshi,
2015) explains that the concept of innovation concentrates on creating new outcomes to help businesses
become competitive.
Thus, this study emphasizes that the success of Marketing Innovation in increasing competitive
advantage is not only determined by the existence of the innovation itself, but also by the readiness of
human resources, digital capabilities, organizational ca pacity, and the quality of the implementation of
marketing strategies in MSMEs. In the context of micro and small businesses, marketing innovations that
have not been optimally managed tend to not be able to have a real impact on increasing Competitive
Advantage.
Marketing Innovation Mediates Proactive Behavior and Relational Capital to Competitive
Advantage
The findings show that the marketing innovation variable is not able to mediate the relationship
between proactive behavior and relational capital to competitive advantage. Proactive behavior and
relational capital directly affect competitive advantage, bu t if mediated by marketing innovation or
indirect relationship with marketing innovation mediation, it does not affect competitive advantage.
These findings indicate that the proactive behavior and strength of relationships owned by business
actors have no t been fully translated into effective marketing innovations to create competitive
advantages. In other words, even though MSMEs have the ability to build good social relationships and
active behavior in looking for business opportunities, these abilities have not been followed by the
implementation of innovative marketing strategies that are able to provide real competitive added value.
This condition is very likely influenced by the characteristics of the research respondents, the
majority of whom are micro and small businesses with limited resources, digital capabilities, and
managerial capacity. In small -scale MSMEs, proactive behavior  is often more focused on the
sustainability of business operations than the development of long -term marketing innovations.
Similarly, Relational Capital owned by business actors is more used to maintain customer relationships
and maintain sales, not to encourage the transformation of innovative marketing strategies. As a result,

5. CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS
This study demonstrates that the competitive advantage of handicraft MSMEs in East Lombok is
largely determined by their capacity to mobilize behavioral and relational resources. The empirical
results confirm that proactive behavior and relational capital exert direct positive effects on competitive
advantage. Proactive behavior enhances competitive positioning by encouraging MSME actors to
generate new marketing ideas, pursue continuous improvement, respond in advance to shifts in
customer needs, develop f uture-oriented strategic advantages, and actively seize emerging market
opportunities. In a similar manner, relational capital plays an important role by strengthening customer
trust, expanding networks with suppliers and business partners, improving acces s to market
information, and facilitating distribution channel relationships. These results suggest that handicraft
MSMEs are more likely to achieve stronger competitive positions when entrepreneurial behavior within
the firm is reinforced by external relationships embedded in the market.
The findings further reveal that both proactive behavior and relational capital significantly promote
marketing innovation. Nevertheless, marketing innovation does not have a significant effect on
competitive advantage and does not mediate the relationship  between proactive behavior , relational
capital, and competitive advantage. Accordingly, four hypotheses are supported, namely the direct
effects of proactive behavior and relational capital on competitive advantage and marketing innovation.
In contrast, three hypotheses are not supported, consisting of the direct effect of marketing innovation
on competitive advantage and the two proposed mediation effects. From a theoretical perspective, these
findings contribute to refining the Resource-Based View in the context of MSMEs operating in developing
regions. The absence of significant mediation implies that the resource –capability–advantage
mechanism does not necessarily function in a linear or automatic manner. In this context, proactive
behavior and relational capital appear to operate more as direct strategic resources than as antecedents
that must first be transformed into marketing innovation to generate competitive advantage. Marketing
innovation may become a meaningful organizational capability only when it is accompanied by sufficient
digital competence, managerial preparedness, financial resources, market intelligence, and consistent
execution.

5.1 Theoretical Implications
This study contributes to the Resource-Based View (RBV) by showing that proactive behavior and
relational capital are strategic resources that directly enhance the competitive advantage of handicraft
MSMEs in East Lombok. The findings indicate that competitiveness is shaped not only by tangible assets,
but also by behavioral capabilities and relational resources, such as market responsiveness, customer
trust, business networks, and access to market information. The non -significant effect of marketing
innovation suggests that innovation does not automatically translate into competitive advantage unless
supported by digital readiness, managerial capability, financial capacity, and consistent implementation.
Thus, this study extends the MSME literature by emphasizing business sustainability or sustai nable
competitive advantage rather than broad ESG-based corporate sustainability.
5.2 Practical Implications
Practically, handicraft MSME owners and managers should strengthen proactive behavior by
identifying market opportunities, understanding customer needs, and continuously improving their
products and marketing strategies. They also need to develop relationa l capital through strong
relationships with customers, suppliers, distributors, business partners, and supporting institutions to
improve trust, market access, and business resilience. Since marketing innovation alone does not
significantly improve competitive advantage, local governments, universities, cooperatives, and MSME
agencies should provide integrated support through digital marketing training,  branding assistance,
market linkage programs, and access to financing.
