# A Bibliometric and Content Analysis of Applied Machine Learning Research in Social Media Marketing

2026, 15 (2), 1423-1445 | Research Article
A Bibliometric and Content Analysis of Applied Machine Learning Research
in Social Media Marketing
Hakan Çanlı 1 Özlem Atalık 2
Abstract
This study analyses 632 articles from Web of Science and SCOPUS databases at the intersection of
machine learning, social media and marketing disciplines. Firstly, quantitative content analysis was
performed based on four coding schemas: types of machine learning methodologies, social media
platforms, contributions to marketing and research outputs. Secondly, bibliometric analysis
identified scientific production trends leveraging these classifications. Finally, network mapping
techniques were used to visualize collaboration in the field. Production trends reveals that the field
is vibrant and growing exponentially with a doubling time of 2.2 years. Content analysis exposes the
gaps and critical imbalances in the literature. W hile use of traditional machine learning and deep
learning methods dominate cumulative ly, transformer  models have overtaken traditional deep
learning in annual usage. Thus, the research field is currently undergoing a methodological shift to
transformer and large -scale foundation models . From marketing point of view the field  mainly
focuses on producing customer insight and marketing research (46%) with limited contribution to
other marketing tasks. Microblogging platforms (43%) are positioned as t he dominant data source
in the field and functions as a laboratory for academic research. The ratio between research focusing
on methodological development (49%) and marketing insight (51%) suggests the field is still building
its tools. The network analysis reveals centralized country-level but decentralized institutional and
author-level collaboration, highlighting that the domain is fragmented. To address these gaps and
imbalances in the domain, it is recommended that future research apply advanced models for
relatively undersupported marketing functions. This study utilizes a tailored , scalable, prompt
engineering supported, and replicable methodology  which can be utilized for future bibliometric
analysis.

Introduction
Social media platforms  have provided new ways of engagement as well as generating
massive amount of data as users integrate their daily activities with these systems.
Artificial Intelligence (AI) together with  social media , offers advanced solutions to
marketers supporting variety of marketing tasks. Applications combining AI and big data
from social media platforms has revolutionized marketing analytics, facilitated a deeper
understanding of consumer behavior and enabled brands to refine their strategies based
on feedback and interactions from consumers (Abdulwahid & Şaylan, 2025, p. 59; Wilson
et al., 2024, p. 1) . Additionally, brand loyalty could be enhanced through more relevant
advertising campaigns  (Gülbaşı, 2025, p. 789) , deeper emotional connections could be
built through personalized marketing messages (Gürkan, 2025, p. 24) , and tailored
experiences could increase overall marketing performance (Şaylan et al., 2025, p. 350;
Abdulwahid & Şaylan, 2025, p. 71; D’Arco et al., 2019, p. 103).
The rapid evolution of AI methodologies has shifted marketing research from hypothesis-
driven and deductive analysis, toward data-driven and inductive approaches (Mete, 2023,
p. 109; Bouyousfi & Ouedraogo, 2025, p. 316) . This bibliometric and content analysis is
done during this transition, mapping 632 scholarly articles of the empirical studies
published between 2013 and 2025. So, its core importance is in documenting the ongoing
paradigm shift in the domain regarding marketing, machine learning (ML)  and social
media.
Methodologically, this study differentiates itself from conventional bibliometrics with
quantitative content analysis to capture underlying thematic insights . The difficulty of
scaling is resolved by introducing an AI-powered workflow: leveraging large language
model (LLM) prompt engineering to generate recommendations that streamline manual
filtering and multi -dimensional coding . Human oversight is maintained by  validating
intercoder reliability through Cohen’s Kappa. Use of AI in this manner creates a scalable
methodology that distinguishes it from most content analysis (Dereli & Dereli, 2025, p.
1664). Regarding the coding schemas for the content analysis, we discuss the types of ML
methodologies and social media platforms based on contemporary typologies , broad
marketing functions aligning with marketing value processes , and research output ,
classifying each study based on whether it contributes to methodology development or
provides practical marketing insights. Thus, the analytical process implemented in this
study combines and tailors the conventional methodological steps of bibliometric and
content analysis.
Beyond its methodological integration, with the insight provided by the content analysis,
this research exposes imbalances within the existing literature in terms of marketing
functions supported by, as well as types of social media platforms  used in the analyzed
research. Identification of  these gaps and imbalances helps determine areas for
development in the focused research domain. Besides author, institution and country
collaboration network analysis help s determine existing and potential future
collaboration opportunities.
Finally, this paper underlines the importance of addressing the structural and ethical
issues inherent to the modern AI and social media ecosystems. Thus, uncritical and
purely functional application of ML to social data risks presenting an overly optimistic
view masking societal harm.

Theoretical Framework
ML Methodologies
AI basically is the domain of computer science or computational methods focusing on
developing systems that perform tasks seeking to simulate human cognitive processes
through various methods and technologies. ML methodologies, on the other hand, is a
critical subset of AI, referring to the models that enable learning from and making
predictions based on data  without any explicit programming  (Alpaydin, 2020, p. 32;
Shaveta, 2023, p. 281) . Deep Learning  (DL), a specialized area within ML , employs
multiple layers of simple, adjustable computing elements (Russell & Norvig, 2021, p. 44)
to analyze and interpret complex patterns in large datasets. As early work was based on
modeling neurons connected as a network in the brain, these models are often called
neural networks or deep neural networks (Akın & Şahin, 2024, p. 28; Qamar & Zardari,
2023, p. 130) , even though the analogy is shallow (Russell & Norvig, 2021, p. 801) .  The
number of layers in DL determines its complexity  as multiple layers are capable of
learning increasingly abstract representations (Mienye & Swart, 2024, p. 1).
Transformer Models  (TM) are type of deep learning model s that utilize self -attention
mechanisms which replaces the sequential processing of recurrent neural networks with
parallel processing (Vaswani et al., 2017, p. 1). This approach enabled modeling of long-
range dependencies efficiently facilitating scaling to massive datasets, making it suitable
for large-scale applications such as language models where words in a sequence could be
processed simultaneously (Ercan & Tanyıldızı, 2026, p. 86; Mangkunegara et al., 2025, p.
232). Transformers have also shown notable performance for image and video tasks (Le
et al., 2024, p. 53; Engin Seven & Eylem Yücel, 2025, p. 331) . As one more step forward,
foundation models represent a recent evolution in transformer structures making use of
large data sets. Foundation models are considered an emerging paradigm for training AI
models on broad data at scale which then can be adapted further to a wide range of
downstream tasks via transfer learning. Foundation models attract tremendous economic
incentives due to their capabilities for real-world applications (Bommasani et al., 2022, p.
12).

Analytical Integration
Well established about for a century, content analysis focus on gathering and analyzing
large volumes of content quantitatively or qualitatively (Neuman, 2014, p. 371). Content
analysis derives meaningful insights from content through categorization of data based
on defined coding scheme, a set of instructions or rules describing how to observe and
record content (White & Marsh, 2006, p. 31; Alanka, 2024, p. 71; Korkmaz, 2016, p. 2591).
Basic steps of content analysis may include, establishing hypothesis/research question,
identifying data sources, determining sampling method and analysis unit, establishing
coding scheme in line with hypothesis or research question, coding data, checkin g
reliability, analyzing and reporting results (Alanka, 2024, p. 74; Neuman, 2014, p. 377;
White & Marsh, 2006, p. 30).
Bibliometric research has emerged as a key quantitative tool to analyze the impact and
patterns of publication in a research field to evaluate the field's intellectual, social, and
conceptual structure, and its evolution over time (Öztürk et al., 2024, p. 3335) . The
methodology has gained popularity with the increased awareness (Dilek, 2020, p. 2533)
as well as with the creation and spread of easily accessible online citation databases
(Aydınoğlu et al., 2023, p. 237)  and analyses software (Zupic & Čater, 2015, p. 430)
allowing quantitative analysis in scale. The typical steps involved in bibliometric analysis
include defining the aim of the research, collecting data on the relevant literature (with
sub-steps i.e. selecting database, identifying search terms, filtering, downloading
dataset), analysis and visualization, interpreting the results (Öztürk et al., 2024, p. 3338).
There are many studies combining bibliographic and content analysis techniques as the
later adds new dimensions to the study potentially increasing the depth of the research
(Costa et al., 2023, p. 1)  such as communications (Cesur et al., 2024, p. 23) , internet of
things (Leong et al., 2021, p. 1148) , critical thinking in primary education (Aktoprak &
Hursen, 2022, p. 1) and  higher education (Maral, 2025, p. 1).  Leveraging AI may alleviate
challenges of content analysis by automating the classification enabling researchers to
analyze larger datasets (Dereli & Dereli, 2025, p. 1664) as well as for bibliometric analysis
by automation of data collection, analysis of citations, disambiguation of authors, analysis

Methodology
Objective, Scope and Research Questions
The objective of this study is to map and evaluate from a broader perspective the
published research in the intersection of ML , social media and marketing. The focus is
further constrained with empirical studies that apply ML models to textual data extracted
from social media platforms. This scope ensures emphasis on marketing point of view
with practical results keeping purely commentary papers ou t and capturing the core
communicative layer of social media marketing by focusing on text.
This study fills a gap in the literature by providing, to the best of the author’s knowledge,
the first analysis focused on the defined scope. Additionally, by classifying the research
on four dimensions, the study provides additional depth. In line with this scope, the
research pursued the following questions:
RQ1: How has research evolved and dominated over time by ML methodologies, social
media platforms, contributions to marketing, and research outputs?
RQ2: Which articles, authors, institutions, and countries have contributed most to ML -
based social media marketing research?
RQ3: What is the (a) intellectual, (b) conceptual and (c) social structure of ML-based social
media marketing research?

These steps are applied by the researchers with LLM prompt engineering assisting for

manual filtering and classification processes from title, abstract and keywords of th e
research articles. Interaction with the LLM is implemented via Python application

Data Collection. The selection of data sources for this bibliometric analysis is based on
coverage, quality of bibliometric information, and alignment with the scope. Thus, high-
quality indexing with high -impact journals covered by Web of Science (W OS) is
complemented with SCOPUS, the largest curated abstract and citation database
(Clarivate, 2025; Elsevier, 2025) . By using both, we create a robust and representative
coverage of the core AI/ML and marketing literature.
Implementing a search strategy on citation databases for stated scope presented several
challenges. First challenge concerned the heterogeneous terminology used for textual
data sources. The second challenge lay in selecting research using ML methodologies  as
terminology is complex. Third challenge was identification of empirical studies. These
challenges eventually increased the complexity of the article selection.
To address these challenges, the search process employed a complex query including
both author-defined and platform -generated terms (W OS Keywords Plus and SCOPUS
Indexed Terms). To reduce the risk of missing relevant studies, we implemented a manual
screening step for additional filtering. Despite these efforts, the possibility remains that
some relevant studies with unconventional terminology may not have been captured. The
final search strategy consists of four groups of search keywords representing AI and ML,
social media, textual analysis, and marketing. The search string is developed by refining
keywords iteratively.
Implemented on 28 July 2025, the query retrieved 1525 and 1066 articles respectively from
WOS and SCOPUS databases.  Organization of the data involved matching data fields
from the two databases, combining data into a single set, and elimination of duplications.
A simple heuristic algorithm applied through a datasheet is used to detect duplications
by matching article (DOI number, title), author (names) and journal related fields (journal
name, year and volume number). Eliminating 747 duplications the data set is reduced to
1844 articles.

contribution to marketing  and research type. For each study and each area of interest,

confidence level . LLM produced these outputs inferring from the title, abstract and
keywords of the article. It is important to underline at this point that the information
extracted is semi-structured i.e. not based on predefined codes as in a typical classification

Discussion and Conclusion
Integrating descriptive bibliometrics and content analysis, this study has provided a
comprehensive overview of evolution and key gaps of the research on marketing which
utilizes ML techniques over textual data from social media platforms. Besides, it
demonstrates the benefits of leveraging LLMs to enhance and apply content analysis at
scale.
Before discussing the implications of these findings, several limitations of this study must
be acknowledged. First and most critically, we consider it a scientific and moral
responsibility to state that this analysis does not cover the ethical dimensions as well as
the asymmetric structure present in the social media and the AI ecosystem. Thus, AI -
powered social media research based on mar keting concepts risks presenting an
optimistic view and masking societal harms  (Saura et al., 2024, p. 12). Consequently, we
suggest researchers to complement such analyses with ethical and structural assessments.
Second, despite implementing a comprehensive search strategy and manual screening,
the possibility remains that some relevant studies employing unconventional
terminology were not captured. Third, the coding schema for types of contributions is
inherently overlapping. Consequently, intercoder reliability for the customer experience
& relationship management was fair . This category is reported descriptively and
interpreted broadly.
With regard to the publication output (RQ1) and the intellectual structure of the domain
(RQ3a), the findings indicate that the field is expanding, in a transitional and tool building
phase. A methodological shift is observed from traditional machine learning towards
transformer-based architectures. This pattern aligns with the typical diffusion of
innovation, where researchers normally prefer well established methods at early stages
(MacVaugh & Schiavone, 2010, p. 199) . Less advanced techniques may in time saturate,
leaving leadership to advanced ML approaches . The following years may see decline in
traditional ML /DL methodologies which might have peaked around  2022-2025 and
continued rise in large-scale foundation models setting another evolutionary milestone.
Recent discussions have identified the impact of AI over marketing as a shift from
traditional hypothesis -driven deductive analysis toward data -driven inductive
approaches (Mete, 2023, p. 109; Bouyousfi & Ouedraogo, 2025, p. 316) . This

İtobiad- Research Article • 1439
İnsan ve Toplum Bilimleri Araştırmaları Dergisi | ISSN: 2147-1185 | www.itobiad.com

epistemological finding is evidenced by the massive surge in utilizing high volume data
from social media with 46% of all analyzed research skewed heavily toward customer
insights and market research. Furthermore, cautioning for the correlation fallacy,
researchers underline that transformer models and the consecutive large -scale
foundation models serve as powerful tools that could support causal inference methods
(Mete, 2023, p. 109). The identified paradigm shift starting in 2019 and 2022 with the rise
of context-aware transformer structures signals the opening of this avenue for marketing
research. Considering AI tasks such as summarization and information extractio n, this
shift might be indicating a move from data processing to knowledge generation (Dursun,
2026, p. 251).
Regarding to the conceptual structure of research domain (RQ3b), the statistics indicate
that AI is utilized mostly for producing customer insights and market research. might
The causes for underutilization of other marketing tasks may stem from several
challenges: the incompatibility of AI models with these tasks, the inadequacy of data, and
the complexity of conceptualizing available data. These findings underline the need for
future research to explore advanced ML techniques for diverse marketing functions
beyond customer insights.  Current domination of microblogging as a main data source
indicates that data availability might be dictating the research questions. Additionally,
the advantages of sales/review platforms for supervised learning and network structures
of social networking platforms are noteworthy for new research avenues.

Taking social structure (RQ3c) of the contribut ors (RQ2) into account, the impactful
research is driven by close partnerships among a small group of impactful scholars that
act as knowledge bridges across institutions and continents. Collaboration networks
further illustrate centralized country level collaboration, and decentralized institutional
collaboration. This presents a clear opportunity for scholars and institutions to build
stronger partnerships to consolidate this fragmented domain.
While advanced models present significant potential for development, the practical
barriers for their use are also worth mentioning. These models are typically governed by
restrictive licensing agreements, opaque terms of use, and proprietary access conditions.
Additionally, the financial costs of building and using foundation models introduces a
structural dependency on commercial providers. This social structure further exacerbates
existing inequalities, concentrate power, and limit equitable access to the  benefits of AI
technology (Bommasani et al., 2022, p. 130; Aşkun, 2024, p. 139; Bozdağ Tulum, 2025, p.
320). Similar structure is valid for access to big data in the social media. The external
access to these datasets remains constrained by regulatory and ethical considerations as
well as the commercial strategies treating user data as a core proprietary asset (Kaya,
2025, p. 12) . This gated ecosystem present inequalities and institutional dependencies
biased toward well-resourced institutions.
For future research, we suggest researchers to look into implementing advanced ML
models, supporting more complex tasks of the marketers , aligning data structures with
marketing concepts and making use of the network structures in social media. While the
application of ML to big data from social media data has profoundly advanced marketing
research, the next frontier lies in embracing advanced models to support broader
spectrum of marketing challenges.
