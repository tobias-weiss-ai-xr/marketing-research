Abstract
This book review examines Ekaterina Glebova’s Services in Sport Management, a timely and peda‐
gogically oriented volume that consolidates service management concepts for the sport industry.
The book’s 11 chapters progress from foundational service theories isuch as Service‐Dominant
Logic and the Service‐Profit Chain ito applied topics including the extended marketing mix (7Ps),
customer relationship management, service quality, segmentation, and value co‐creation between
employees and fans. Particular strengths lie in the clear conceptual synthesis, the didactic struc‐
ture, and the systematic use of exercises, self‐assessment questions, and case studies drawn from
major sport properties such as the FIFA World Cup, the NBA, and esports. At the same time, the
models and frameworks remain only lightly supported by empirical evidence, and the case material
is predominantly Western, leaving limited space for emerging sport markets and diverse institu‐
tional contexts. The review argues that future research could build on this conceptual architecture
through practice‐based and comparative studies that examine how service logics are enacted in
different sport ecosystems. Overall, Services in Sport Management is recommended as a core text‐
book for undergraduate and postgraduate programmes in sport management and sport tourism, as
well as a valuable conceptual toolkit for practitioners seeking to design and deliver sport services
in an increasingly experience‐driven and data‐intensive environment.

1 STRUCTURE AND CONTENT
The book’s pedagogical architecture is one of its principal strengths. Its
11 chapters follow a logical progression from conceptual underpinnings
to strategic implementation. The initial chapter adeptly frames the evo‐
lution and economic significance of services, setting a solid foundation.
Subsequent chapters (2‐4) provide a comprehensive analysis of the mar‐
keting environment and the augmented service marketing mix (7Ps),
alongside a welcome discussion on research methodologies pertinent
to the field. The core of the book (Chapters 5‐8) delves into the critical
operational and relational domains of Customer Relationship Manage‐
ment (CRM), service quality metrics, customer segmentation, and the
intricate dynamics of value co‐creation between employees and fans.
The later chapters (9‐11) effectively transition to strategic considera‐
tions, encompassing service distribution, promotion, and the integration

2 CRITICAL EVALUATION
Glebova’s work attains its primary objective: to render complex ser‐
vice theories accessible and relevant to sport management students
and practitioners. The translation of abstract concepts from scholars
like Grönroos et al. (2000) into the sport context is executed with no‐
table clarity. The timely inclusion of digital transformation (Glebova and
Madsen 2024, Desmarchelier 2024), customer experience (CX), and sus‐
tainability discourses ( Glebova 2022) aligns the book with the most
pressing challenges and opportunities in the contemporary sport land‐
scape (Hautbois and Desbordes 2023). However, the book’s conceptual
strength, at times, highlights a relative weakness in empirical validation.
While the proposed models are logically sound and intuitively appealing,
their application would be significantly bolstered by supporting quanti‐
tative data or in‐depth longitudinal case studies. For example, the oper‐
ationalization of the Service‐Profit Chain in a sport context (Chapter 5)
remains largely theoretical. Hence, empirical evidence demonstrating its
impact on profitability would greatly enhance its persuasive power for
critical practitioners and researchers. For instance, future studies may
use a practice‐based approach (Bahadori and Ramjawan 2025, Lissillour
and Silva 2024) to better understand the diverging interest that stake‐
holders will have as efforts are made to implement Service‐Profit Chain
in a sport context. Furthermore, while the case studies from established
Western leagues are illustrative, the book’s scope presents a missed
opportunity for a truly global perspective. The growing economic and
cultural significance of sport in emerging economies in the Middle East,
Africa, and South America, or in fragile states contexts ( Ghrayche et al.
2025), merits more than a passing mention. The book does not include
a critical management research agenda ( Lissillour 2024). Moreover, in
line with indigenous scholarship ( Rodríguez‐Escobar 2024), a compara‐
tive analysis of service delivery models across these diverse ecosystems
would have substantially widened the book’s appeal and applicabil‐
ity. Despite these limitations, its structured pedagogy and theoretical
synthesis make it an exemplary core textbook for undergraduate and
postgraduate courses.
3 CONTRIBUTION AND RECOMMEN‐
DATION
In conclusion, Services in Sport Management makes a valuable and dis‐
tinct contribution to the field. It successfully consolidates a fragmented
body of knowledge into a single, coherent volume, effectively bridg‐
ing the gap between traditional service marketing literature and the
evolving, experience‐driven sport industry. This book is unequivocally
recommended as a foundational text for academic courses in sport and
sport tourism ( Glebova 2022) centric approach to service design and
delivery. For the practitioner, it offers a valuable conceptual toolkit; for
the student, it provides a clear and structured guide to a critical aspect
of the modern sport business.
