ABSTRACT
The increase of digital technologies has brought about a shift in the way EdTech companies in India are
perceived by their marketers. It is evident from the findings of previous studies that phygital marketing has
become an integral part of the decision-making process of the enrollment in an educational institution. This study
focuses on understanding the effect of phygital marketing on the decision-making process regarding student
enrollment in EdTech companies with reference to India.
A questionnaire-based survey was conducted using a structured questionnaire on 300 students, parents, and
working professionals residing in Pune, Mumbai, and Nashik districts of Maharashtra. Descriptive statistics,
factor analysis, and Structural Equation Modelling (SEM) were used to analyze the collected data. Five important
dimensions of phygital marketing include the consumption of digital content, physical touchpoints, omnichannel
experience, personalized communication, and building trust.
Results show that the highest positive influence on student enrollment decision is due to the omnichannel
experience and personalized communication, whereas physical touchpoints play an important role in the case of
first-generation learners.
Keywords: Phygital Marketing, EdTech, Student Enrollment, Consumer Behaviour, Omnichannel, Digital
Marketing, India
1. INTRODUCTION-
With a tremendous growth rate, the Indian EdTech
market is one of the most promising ones in the
world, currently estimated to be worth
approximately USD 7.5 billion in 2023 and expected
to grow to reach USD
30 billion by 2031 (IBEF, 2024). Such companies
as BYJU'S, Unacademy, Vedantu, upGrad, and
Simplilearn revolutionized the traditional process
of studying through digital technologies.
Yet, even as the EdTech industry becomes
increasingly digital, consumers' behavior turns out
to be far more complicated than it might seem.
Neither students nor parents rely on online-only
experiences when enrolling in new programs, still
valuing personal experience, including demo
classes, face-to-face counselling sessions, or
recommendations. This combination of the physical
and the digital constitutes the idea of phygital
marketing, which was invented by merging
'physical' and 'digital' (Batat, 2022). According to
Mele and Russo-Spena (2022), phygital marketing
is a concept that implies providing consumers with
fully immersive experiences that integrate digital
elements into physical spaces. For an EdTech
company, it can mean blending various tools used
in its online presence, such as AI-powered course
recommendations, webinars, social media
marketing, and mobile apps with physical
experiences, including visits to counseling centers
and schools/colleges/universities.
Although this topic holds immense importance for
both practitioners and scholars alike, the literature
review reveals a lack of empirical evidence
regarding the impact of phygital marketing in the
EdTech industry in India. The existing body of
literature mainly concentrates on phygital
marketing in the realms of retail, luxury, and fast-
moving consumer goods. This research aims to
bridge this important research gap.
LITERATURE REVIEW
1.1 Introduction and Development of Phygital
Marketing
The concept of "phygital" was coined to refer to
consumer experiences that combine both physical
and digital attributes (Batat, 2022). According to
Mele and Russo-Spena (2022), phygital is an
innovative consumption space in which digital
technologies enhance the physical aspects of
interaction, thus creating an integrated and
immersive experience. The phygital shift has been
recognized as one of the strategic responses to
changing consumer requirements, as they
increasingly seek both digital simplicity and
physical authenticity (Mele et al., 2023). In 2024,
Batat introduced the Phygital Customer Experience
(PH-CX) model, which conceptualizes phygital
experiences based on such characteristics as
immersion, interactivity, personalization, and

1.2 Consumers’ Behavior and Digital Marketing

Digital marketing has revolutionised the consumer
decision process in the education sector. According
to Kumawat and Bhamboo (2023), digital
marketing tools such as social media ads, SEO, and
content marketing play a pivotal role in shaping
consumer awareness and intention towards EdTech

and product quality moderate the effect of digital
marketing on enrolment choices.
Chen et al. (2023) analyse the purchase intention
behind buying online courses and conclude that
perceived quality, credible instructors, and system
usability act as major factors influencing
consumers’ enrolment choice. Zhang et al. (2022)
expand this discussion by focusing on consumer
satisfaction from online education and intention to
buy further.
1.3 Omnichannel and Phygital Integration in
Education
The integration of physical and digital touchpoints,
which is known as omnichannel marketing, has
been cited as one of the critical drivers of consumer
engagement in the services industry (Banik & Gao,
2023). In the case of EdTech firms, omnichannel
approaches refer to the integration of touchpoints
such as websites, mobile apps, social media
platforms, counselling centers, and educational
events.
According to Santoki et al. (2024), who assess
marketing management innovation practices in the
EdTech industry, omnichannel integration and
personalized communication are the two most
effective marketing strategies for attracting
students. Based on their investigation of Indian
EdTech firms, the researchers find that students
who receive phygital marketing messages through
multiple channels are 2.4 times more likely to enroll
than those who receive messages from only one
channel.
1.4 Role of Trust, Personalization, and Social
Proof
Trust is one of the key antecedents to online
purchase intention (Chandra et al., 2022). In relation
to EdTech, building trust through measures such as
transparency in prices, learner testimonials, free
trial periods, and guarantee of results plays a major
role in affecting enrolment decisions (Kumar &
Masvood, 2025). Personalized marketing
communications through use of artificial
intelligence algorithms and data analysis play an
important role in increasing engagement with
consumers by providing them with suitable content
at the right time. Student success stories, peer
reviews, ratings, and endorsements by influencers
such as educators have also been identified as key
elements of social proof that affect the enrolment
behavior of Generation Z and millennials (Guerra-
Tamez et al., 2024). This is similar to the concept of
eWOM as discussed in the wider literature (Emon et
al., 2024).
1.5 Research Gap
Although previous studies have extensively
explored digital marketing practices in EdTech and
phygital marketing practices in retail sectors, there
is a lack of empirical evidence that explores the
relationship between phygital marketing practices
and enrollment decisions specifically in India’s
EdTech sector. Most existing studies have
considered the digital marketing and physical
marketing separately, without considering their
combined phygital impact.
2. STATEMENT OF THE PROBLEM, OBJECTIVES
& HYPOTHESES
2.1 Statement of the Problem
Indian EdTech businesses have spent heavily on not
only digital marketing strategies (social media, SEO,
application marketing), but also on physical
marketing channels such as counselling centres,
visits to schools and colleges, and career fairs. Still,
no information is available on the impact that such
phygital marketing initiatives exert together in
student enrollment decisions. There is no
empirically tested theoretical model that would
help marketers allocate their investment in phygital
marketing for maximum enrollment success. Thus,
the problem that this study aims to solve is the
following question: How does phygital marketing
impact EdTech student enrollment decisions?
2.2 Objectives of the Study
1. To explore the awareness and perceptions of
phygital marketing strategies among potential
Enrollers in the context of EdTech enrollment.
2. To establish the main dimensions of phygital
marketing that affect EdTech enrollment decision
making.
3. To compare the relative effects of digital
touchpoints and physical touchpoints on EdTech
enrollment decisions.
4. To examine the moderating effect of
demographics (age, gender, location, and income)
on the phygital marketing–Enrollment relationship.
5. To develop and test the structural model of the
effects of phygital marketing on EdTech student
enrollment decisions.

3. RESEARCH DESIGN
3.1 Research Approach
The research design is quantitative in nature and
utilizes a cross-sectional survey design. The primary
data collection tool involved a structured
questionnaire, which was supplemented with
secondary data from relevant published literature,
industry reports, and EdTech firm publications.
3.2 Population and Sampling
The study's population consists of students between
the ages of 15 and 35, parents of school/college-
going children, and professionals who have
registered themselves in, or plan to register for,
EdTech courses in Maharashtra, India. A
convenience sampling technique with elements of
purposive sampling was used to ensure
representation among different age groups, genders,
education levels, and geographic locations (urban,
semi-urban). The sample size aimed at reaching
300 respondents, based on the rule of thumb for
SEM analysis (minimum of 5-10 respondents for
each observed variable) and past literature
(Kumawat & Bhamboo, 2023; Zhang et al., 2022).
After filtering out incomplete and inconsistent
responses, 287 valid responses were analyzed.
3.3 Data Collection Instrument
The survey instrument consisted of five parts: (a)
demographic details, (b) EdTech use and enrolment
behavior, (c) phygital marketing experience (five
dimensions: digital content, physical touchpoints,
omnichannel, personalization, trust-building), (d)
enrolment factors, and (e) open-ended comments.
All construct measures were measured using a 5-
point Likert-type scale ranging from (1 = strongly
disagree to 5 = strongly agree). This research used
scales adopted from Batat (2024), Santoki et al.
(2024), and Chen et al. (2023).
3.4 Validity and Reliability
The face validity was determined via expert
judgment by three marketing scholars and two
industry experts working in the EdTech space. A
pilot study of 30 people was undertaken to improve
clarity in the measurement instruments. The
Cronbach's Alpha coefficient values exceeded 0.80
for all the constructs, which indicated high internal
consistency. The CR values for all constructs were
within the range of 0.82 to 0.91, and AVE scores
were more than 0.50.
3.5 Data Analysis Methods
• Descriptive Statistics: Frequency distribution,
mean, standard deviation
• Factor Analysis: Exploratory factor analysis to
determine latent constructs of phygital marketing
experience
• Reliability Analysis: Cronbach's Alpha,
Composite reliability (CR), AVE
• Correlation Analysis: Pearson correlation
• Structural Equation Modeling (SEM): Using
AMOS 24.0

6. FINDINGS
6.1 Key Finding
1. Phygital Marketing has a major impact on
students' decision to enroll in EdTech
organizations. All the five constructs – Digital
Content Engagement, Physical Touch Points,
Omnichannel Integration, Personalized
Communication, and Trust-Building have a
positive and significant effect on enrollment
behavior.
2. Omnichannel Integration is the most
significant dimension of phygital marketing (β
= 0.418, p < 0.001). Students who receive the
same message consistently on multiple
platforms – digital (apps, social media, emails)
as well as physical (counselling, physical
interactions at events) are more likely to
enroll.
3. Personalized Communication is the second
most significant dimension (β = 0.384, p <
0.001). Personalized recommendations by
artificial intelligence, tailored email campaigns,
and personalized courses are considered
relevant and trustworthy.
4. Third in line of significance is Trust Building
via Phygital Marketing (β = 0.341, p < 0.001).

7. MANAGERIAL IMPLICATIONS
The research results provide a number of important
implications for EdTech marketers, platform
developers, and policy-makers:
7.1 Build Omnichannel Experiences
The need for seamless omnichannel experiences
should be high on the priority list for EdTech
platforms. Inconsistencies in marketing messages,
course content, prices, and customer service will
not do well for conversions. It is important to use a
CRM system that will allow tracking student
interactions with EdTech products and services
throughout the entire customer journey.
7.2 Use AI for Personalization
Taking into account the high effectiveness of
personalized communication, EdTech marketers
should embrace AI solutions and create
personalized course suggestions and
communication messages depending on which
stage of the purchasing process students are
currently experiencing – awareness, consideration,
intention, or final decision.
7.3 Build Multiple Trust Signals
Marketing campaigns for EdTech products and
services can incorporate various trust signals by
featuring successful outcomes that have been
certified and alumni success stories on digital
media; providing free trial classes physically and
virtually; providing detailed fee structure and
refund policies; and resolving queries using
chatbots. Personalized counseling sessions in a
physical environment form very powerful trust
signals for hesitant students.
7.4 Continue Physical Touchpoints in Semi-Urban
Markets
Even after adopting a digital-first strategy, there will
be the need for physical touchpoints, especially in
semi-urban and rural markets. Therefore, when
developing marketing plans, EdTech companies
must not limit their reach only to online marketing
but continue to expand in terms of building physical
learning centers and other facilities.
7.5 Segmentation-Based Phygital Marketing Plans
Since age and location play moderating roles, the
EdTech marketers need to develop customized
marketing campaigns based on the type of segment.
The younger generation and urban students will
react favorably towards social media marketing and
influencer marketing while physical counseling
plays a more important part among older students.
7.6 Policy Recommendations for Policymakers
The government and educational authorities will
encourage EdTech players to establish physical
support infrastructure while simultaneously
focusing on digital delivery modes.
8. FUTURE RESEARCH OPPORTUNITIES
Although this study has contributed significantly to
the literature surrounding phygital marketing and
the EdTech industry, there are multiple
opportunities for future research including:
LONGITUDINAL STUDY DESIGNS: The cross-
sectional nature of this study restricts making any
conclusions regarding causality. Conducting
longitudinal studies following students from first
marketing contact to course completion will yield
valuable insights concerning phygital marketing.
SECTORAL DIFFERENCES: Research may consider
the effectiveness of phygital marketing in various
sub-sectors within the EdTech industry including K-
12 learning, higher education, skills acquisition, and
vocational education.

EDTECH MARKETING: With the growth in
metaverse learning environments, research
