ABSTRACT
Rural women’s entrepreneurial activities play a critical role in economic development and in preserving traditional crafts.
The women weavers of Pochampally, Telangana—renowned for their Ikat sarees—are skilled artisan-entrepreneurs, yet
many struggle to achieve economic independence and sustainable growth because of substantial marketing barriers. Key
challenges include limited digital literacy, weak branding, restricted market access, and stiff competition from mass -
produced and mechanized imitations. Additional constraints are slow technology adoption and sparse use of e-commerce
platforms. To capture the lived experiences of these women, this study uses a mixed -methods approach—combining
surveys with focus groups and in-depth interviews—and relies on qualitative case analysis. Findings indicate that despite
exceptional craftsmanship and a rich cultural heritage, Pochampally’s women weavers require targeted interventions:
training in digital and marketing skills, support for brand development, improved access to online and offline markets, and
government and NGO initiatives to protect intellectual property and counter mechanized knockoffs. The study concludes
that coordinated action by stakeholders is essential to sustain Pochampally Ikat in competitive global markets and to
empower rural women entrepreneurs through socially innovative solutions.

INTRODUCTION
Social transformation of Women Weaver
Entrepreneurs: The social transformation of women
weaver entrepreneurs in India, particularly in regions like
Pochampally, Telangana, reflects a significant shift in
gender roles, economic empowerment, and community
dynamics. This transformation is driven by various factors
including collective entrepreneurship, capacity building,
and the integration of traditional crafts into modern
markets.
Organizations like Chitrika Foundation play a crucial role
in facilitating collective entrepreneurship among women
weavers. By mobilizing women into producer companies,
Chitrika helps them gain access to resources and training
that enhance their entrepreneurial capabilities:
Training and Capacity Building: Chitrika implements
innovative training programs that equip women with the
skills needed to understand market mechanisms and run
sustainable enterprises This training is vital for helping
women navigate the complexities of modern markets.
Community Engagement: Producer companies foster a
sense of community among women weavers, allowing them
to share experiences and support each other. This collective
identity helps break down traditional barriers related to
caste and gender.
Economic Viability: By working together, women can
negotiate better prices for their products and reduce reliance
on exploitative middlemen. This collective approach
increases their income stability and decision-making power
within their households.
Innovations in technology and design are also
transforming the landscape for women weavers in
Telangana:
Assistive Technology: Modha Sivakumar's pedal-operated
machine for handlooms alleviates the physical strain on
weavers, particularly benefiting older artisans or those with
disabilities.  This innovation not only enhances productivity
but also ensures sustained income for weavers.
