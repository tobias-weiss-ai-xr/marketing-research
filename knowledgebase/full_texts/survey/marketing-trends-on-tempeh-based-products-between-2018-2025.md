Abstract
Tempeh sales in Indonesia experienced significant fluctuations and decline from 2018 to 2024 driven
by rising soybean prices and shifting youth preferences toward foreign cuisine. This trend negatively
impacts MSME profitability and contradicts national fo od sovereignty missions known as Asta Cita.
Consequently, this study aims to analyze market trends and the research landscape regarding tempeh
derivatives and their marketing aspects. The study employed bibliometric analysis following PRISMA
2020 guidelines. Data were retrieved from Web of Science and Google Scholar databases resulting in a
final dataset of 2,095 articles analyzed using VOSviewer software. The findings indicate that Indonesia
is the leading contributor to scholarly output with IPB University as the dominant institution. Research
themes are anchored on raw tempeh followed by tempeh chips and tempeh flour. SWOT Analysis is the
most utilized marketing method with Price and Product Quality as the primary variables. The dominant
market segments are plant-based meat followed by plant-based protein and healthy food. Geographically
Malang alongside Semarang and Surabaya are identified as the primary productive regions. These
insights provide an empirical foundation for MSMEs to develop product innovations targeting the plant-
based meat and healthy food segments thereby supporting the green economy and national food
sovereignty goals.
Keywords: Tempeh-based Products; Tempeh Marketing Trends; Bibliometric Analysis;
PRISMA 2020.

1. Introduction
Indonesian Central Bureau of Statistics, (2025) reported fluctuating tempeh sales in Indonesia
during the 2018–2024 period, with notable year -on-year declines: a 2.82% reduction between
2020 and 2021, followed by a more pronounced 4.09% decrease from 2023 to 2024. When
evaluated against alternative protein sources during the same period, tempeh sales in West Java
Province demonstrated competitive disadvantages: 2.3 -fold lower than commercial chicken
eggs, 3% of broiler chicken meat sales volume, and consistently trailing tofu sales within the
legume product category by an average margin of 2% (Central Bureau of Statistics, 2024). Hadi
et al. (2024)  employed multiple forecasting methodologies (Naïve, Moving Average, Single
Exponential Smoothing, and ARIMA) to analyze tempeh sales trends. Their results indicate:

concerning declining consumer preference for tempeh and its derivatives remain an active
research focus among Indonesian scholars  (Caesaria & Kasih, 2022;  Maryam, 2022; Pratama
& Floresti, 2023; The Business Research Company, 2025).
The decline in tempeh consumption can be partially attributed to Indonesian youth's growing
preference for foreign cuisine over traditional foods (Khoiri, 2022). From a market perspective,
tempeh-based products demonstrate lower commercial viability compared to rice -based
alternatives, such as rice packaging products, which benefit from established market segments
due to rice's status as a daily staple food in In donesia  (Firmansyah et al., 2021). This finding
contrasts with (Febryan & Ali, 2024) A study demonstrating the significant influence of green
marketing on consumer purchase decisions  suggests that marketing tempeh-based products as
sustainable alternatives should represent a commercially viable strategy. This trend raises
significant cultural concerns, as tempeh represents an important part of Indonesian culinary
heritage and should ideally serve  as a national symbol of cultural pride. Declining sales and
consumer demand have significantly reduced profit margins for Micro, Small, and Medium
Enterprises (MSMEs) in the food sector, particularly for tempeh producers. This trend
contradicts two of the eight key missions (Asta Cita) of President Prabowo Subianto's
administration, which explicitly prioritize MSME advancement and foster national self-reliance
in food sovereignty through green economy principles as mandated in Presidential Regulation
of the Republic of Indonesia Number 139 of 2024 Concerning the Structuring of Duties and
Functions of the State Ministries of the Red and White Cabinet for the 2024- 2029 Period
(Korniawan, 2024; Suryaningprang, 2024; Prasetiyo et al., 2025).
Given the aforementioned challenges, a comprehensive market remapping study of tempeh -
based processed products since 2018 is imperative to establish an empirical foundation for
developing targeted marketing strategies for MSME stakeholders. This research specifically
aims to analyze market trends of tempeh derivatives over the 2018- 2025 period. The practical
implementation of research outcomes will support the development of evidence -based
marketing frameworks to propose market segmentation for tempeh-based products in the future,
and, further, for MSMEs in the tempeh processing sector, and, further, to foster a  green
economy, aligning with the Asta Cita initiative of the Indonesian President.

2.1 Tempeh
Tempeh is characterized as a widely consumed Indonesian fermented -cultured food product,

comprising tender-cooked soybeans or alternative substrates such as legumes, seeds, cereal
grains, or coconut (Shurtleff & Aoyagi, 2001; Romulo & Surya, 2021) . These ingredients are
cohesively bound by a dense, cotton-like mycelial network of Rhizopus mold, forming a solid,
compact structure. Tempeh has been characterized by its distinctive flavor and texture, often
likened to "Southern fried chicken," "savory veal cutlets," or "seafood fillets." Its appealing,
mildly sweet aroma evokes descriptors such as "nutty," "cheese- like," "reminiscent of
mushrooms," or "yeasty, akin to freshly baked bread." Tempeh was first documented in the
Javanese manuscript Serat Centhini (1815) as "kadhele tempe" (soybean tempeh), recognized
as a traditional Javanese staple (Shurtleff & Aoyagi, 2020, 2022). The earliest microbiological
study on tempeh was conducted by Prinsen Geerligs in 1895, whose work -  alongside
complementary historical sources - suggests that tempeh originated from soybeans introduced
through Chinese trade networks, combined with pre-existing Javanese tape (fermented cassava)
yeast fermentation techniques (Kubota et al., 1996; Pitt & Hocking, 2022). During the colonial
era, tempeh was introduced to Europe, and following Indonesian independence, the initiation
of systematic tempeh research by prominent institutions including LIPI (Indonesian Institute of
Sciences), IPB University (Bogor Agricultural University), and Cornell University, and further,
this scholarly attention culminated in tempeh's 2021 nomination for intangible cultural heritage
status at the Indonesian Intangible Cultural Heritage Determination Session, though ultimately
only tempe mendoan (one of tempeh-based product) received official recognition (Kusumah et
al., 2008; Sudaryantiningsih & Pambudi, 2017; Andayani & Hambali, 2018; Mutiah, 2021).
Farid et al. (2025)  describe the tempe production process from soybeans as beginning with
manual sorting to remove damaged, moldy, or contaminated beans, along with foreign materials
such as pebbles and small twigs. The selected beans are then soaked in clean water for 8 –16
hours. Subsequently, the beans are steamed (or boiled) until they reach optimal tenderness, with
temperature carefully controlled to prevent fungal growth during fermentation. The cooked and
cooled beans are uniformly mixed with tempe starter culture containing Rhizopus mold spores,
then wrapped in banana leaves, perforated plastic, or specialized paper, and allowed to ferment
for 24–48 hours at ambient temperature (approximately 25 –30°C). A comparative dry-weight
analysis indicates that tempeh exhibits a m arkedly enhanced micronutrient and fiber profile
compared to soybeans, highlighted by substantial increases per 100 grams in dietary fiber (3.7
g to 7.2 g), calcium (254 mg to 347 mg), and vitamin B12 (0.2 mcg to 3.9 mcg). Despite these
functional shifts, core macronutrient concentrations remain highly conserved, with protein
(46.2 g versus 46.5 g) and fat (19.1 g versus 19.7 g) demonstrating negligible variance between
the two commodities (Astuti et al., 2000; Aryanta, 2023).
Empirical research by  Pinasti et al. (2020)  revealed tempeh's substantial nutritional
composition, particularly its rich iron content, folate levels, and cobalamin (vitamin B12)
concentration, which collectively contribute to its anti -anemic properties. Recent research by
(Teoh et al., 2024) has empirically validated tempeh's multifunctional health benefits,
including: (1) anti-diabetic properties through glycemic regulation, (2) neuroprotective effects
enhancing cognitive performance, (3) hypocholesterolemic action promoting cardiovascular
health, (4) oncostatic activity with antitumor potential, (5) cellular senescence retardation
exhibiting anti-aging effects, (6) blood pressure modulation via antihypertensive mechanisms,
and (7) dual probiotic and psychobiotic actions improving gut microb iota and mental health.
Despite its documented health benefits, tempeh has been scrutinized for  its high isoflavone
content. These phytoestrogenic compounds have been controversially associated with potential
testosterone reduction in male consumers  (M. Astuti et al., 2000; Dillingham et al., 2005) .

While tempeh itself represents a processed soybean product, it serves as a base material for
numerous secondary derivatives within Indonesian cuisine. Notable processed tempeh products
include tempe mendoan (battered fried tempeh), cireng tempe (tempeh fritters), tempeh-based
vegetable dishes, peyek tempe (tempeh crackers), keripik tempe (tempeh chips), tempe bacem

(tempeh patties) - with continuous innovation driven by culinary creativity (Lestari et al., 2024;
Perdana et al., 2022) . For daily consumption, Indonesians predominantly prepare tempeh
through shallow frying, making tempe goreng (fried tempeh) the most ubiquitous preparation
method in household cooking (Kristian, 2007).

Marketing constitutes a dynamic socio -managerial process involving value creation through
product development, distribution, promotion, and pricing strategies, facilitating mutually

beneficial exchange relationships with customers and stakeholders in evolving market
environments (Kotler & Keller, 2016 ; Tjiptono & Diana, 2020) . Contemporary marketing
theory rests on three foundational pillars: (1) consumer motivation factors (needs, wants, and
demands), (2) strategic market approach (segmentation, targeting, and positioning), and (3)
competitive dynamics. Market segmentation em ploys four key dimensions : geographic,
demographic, psychographic, and behavioral characteristics, while effective targeting requires
segments to meet five criteria: measurable size, substantial profitability, accessibility,
differentiation potential, and actionable strategy. Positioning strategies may utilize attribute -
based, usage- application, user -category, product -class, competitive, price -quality, or value -
based approaches. Porter (1980)  five forces analysis further evaluates market attractiveness
through industry competition, new entrant threats, substitute availability, buyer power, and
supplier influence.
In marketing theory, there is marketing mix concept that originated with Borden (1964)
foundational work presented to the American Marketing Society, later crystallized into the
seminal 4P framework (Product, Price, Place, Promotion) in 1960. Subsequent theoretical
expansions include service marketing 7P model (adding People, Process, Physi cal Evidence),
customer-centric 4C paradigm (Customer, Cost, Convenience, Communication), and digital -
era adaptations like 4V model (Valued Customer, Value to Customer, Value to the Marketer,
Value to Society) and 4E framework (Experience, Everywhere, Exch ange, Evangelism) with
later models recognized as complementary rather than substitutive to core 4P theory (Booms &
Bitner, 1981; Lauterborn, 1990; D. Perreaulty Jr. & McCarthy, 2002; Londhe, 2014; Epuran et
al., 2015) . From a media typology perspective, marketing strategies can be fundamentally
categorized into two distinct paradigms: traditional/conventional marketing and digital
marketing. Modern marketing research has progressively bifurcated into behavioral science
paradigms (examining marketing mix, service quality, consumer decision pathways, etc.) and
brand-centric inquiries (brand image, brand personality, brand loyalty, etc.) (Prasetiyo & Akbar,
2025; Prasetiyo & Ali, 2025).
 3. Research Method
This study employs bibliometric analysis supplemented by in- depth examination to establish
appropriate marketing frameworks for tempeh -based products.  Bibliometric analysis
constitutes a quantitative methodology that examines bibliographic data to delineate emerging
patterns, trends, and conceptual relationships within scientific literature  (Irawan, Elvina, et al.,
2025; Irawan, Nuradina, et al., 2025). Although still within the literature review category, this
method differs from Systematic Literature Review (SLR), which represents a methodologically
stringent approach encompassing the systematic identification, critical appraisal, and synthesis
of extant scholarly works (Alamsyah et al., 2024; Prasetiyo & Rahayu, 2025).
Following PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items  for Systematic Reviews and Meta Analysis)
2020 guidelines, the review process utilizes Web of Science and Google Scholar databases to
examine publications from January 2018 to June 2025 (a 7+ year timeframe), employing some
keyword combinations in English and Indonesian such as “tempeh marketing,” “tempeh-based
products,” “pemasaran tempe,” “produk olahan tempe,” “tempe marketing,” and “tempe-based
products.” The January 2018 starting point was selected to align with the base year of
Indonesian Central Bureau of Statistics (BPS) tempeh sales records (2018 -2024), enabling
asynchronous comparative analysis between this study's findings and official statistical data.
June 2025 was designated as the cutoff date because the research was conducted during this

4.10 Comprehensive discussion on tempeh marketing trends between 2018-2025.
A marketing trend represents a strategic shift or development that changes the market
environment, making its analysis a crucial process for understanding consumer behavior
patterns and market dynamics that affect an industry (Zhang & Chang, 2021;  Siswanto et al.,
2025). To capture these dynamics within the tempeh sector, it is necessary to examine how
business strategies have actively adapted over time. Historically, tempeh has been a staple of
the Indonesian diet. As detailed by Astuti et al. (2000), the origins of these fermented soybean
derivatives were not exclusively indigenous to Indonesia; rather, they were dispersed
throughout the 'natto triangle,' a region spanning Nepal, Japan, and Java. The primary
differentiator for the Javanese variant was the deliberate application of Rhizopus sp. for
fermentation, in stark contrast to the Bacillus sp. typically utilized in the other regions.
Following its emergence in the 16th century, raw tempeh was continually produced and
commercialized through establ ished traditional systems before gradually evolving into a
broader category of derivative goods.
Extracted bibliometric data indicate that by 2018, the commercial focus had already expanded
to include numerous product innovations, ranging from tempeh chips to tempeh- based nata de
soya, tempeh inoculum, tempeh flour, tempeh nuggets, etc. During this baseline year, analytical
frameworks such as SWOT, value chain, and marketing channel analyses were the predominant
methodological strategies, though they were still overwhelmingly applied to the
commercialization of raw tempeh. Additionally, the targeted consumer base remained narrowly
defined around vegan, vegetarian, and healthy-eating cohorts, with market distribution heavily
centralized in Central Java (specifically Semarang and Grobogan) and East Java (notably
Malang). The classification  of tempeh as 'plant -based meat' initially surfaced in the 2019
literature, specifically within a United Kingdom- based study exploring vegetarian  meat
alternatives (Rödl, 2019 ). From that point forward, this particular segmentation , targeting
consumers transitioning away from animal protein, proliferated rapidly across subsequent
publications and eventually secured the highest frequency among all identified market
segments. In contrast, the 'plant -based protein' framework was not documented until 2021
(Aaslyng & Hojer, 2021) , though it later experienced a substantial surge throughout 2024 in
research dedicated to raw tempeh (Degieter, 2024; Puguh et al., 2021; Samad et al., 2024; Teoh
et al., 2024; Werdiningsih et al., 2024). From this period onward, the market segmentation of
tempeh firmly established it as a direct animal protein substitute, strategically capturing vegan,
vegetarian, and flexitarian demographics across prominent Western markets, including Italy,
Sweden, the United States, and Denmark.
A distinct pivot toward highly diversified market targeting became evident post-2022. Notably,
the 'stunting-prevention snack' category gained massive traction during the 2024–2025 period.
Within this timeframe, scholarly attention shifted toward formulate d innovations such as

inherently static.
5. Conclusion
This bibliometric study of 2,095 articles from 2018 to 2025 demonstrates that although tempeh
has successfully expanded its market positioning globally as a premium plant-based protein and
domestically as a functional health food, the strategic commercialization frameworks employed
by MSMEs remain stagnated in basic pricing strategies and conventional SWOT analyses. To
reverse declining domestic sales and align with Indonesia's green economy and food
sovereignty initiatives, industry stakeholders must transition toward advanced digital marketing
and value-driven segmentation. However, the primary limitation of this research is its reliance
on secondary bibliometric data, which captures academic discourse and established market
trends but may not fully reflect the real- time, undocumented commercial realities currently
