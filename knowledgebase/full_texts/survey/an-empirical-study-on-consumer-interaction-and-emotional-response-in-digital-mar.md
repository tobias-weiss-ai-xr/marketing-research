Digital marketing environments increasingly rely on interactive,
personalized, and content -rich interfaces that shape consumers’ emotional
experiences and subsequent behavioural responses. Although prior studies
highlight the importance of consumer engagement in online contexts, limited
empirical work integrates interaction attributes, emotional processing, and
behavioural intention within a unified structural framework. This study
investigates how three core digital interaction features —interactivity,
personalization, and content quality -shape emotional responses and
influence purchase intention. Using survey data from 412 active digital media
users, we employed covariance -based structural equation modelling (AMOS
24) and variance-based modelling (SmartPLS-4) to test the robustness of the
proposed model. Results demonstrate that interactivity, personalization, and
content quality significantly enhance positive emotional responses, which in
turn exert a strong influence on purchase intention. Mediation analys es
confirm that emotional response partially mediates the relationships between
interaction attributes and behavioural intention. Additional multi -group and
interaction analyses reveal moderation effects of gender and age, indicating
demographic heterogene ity in emotional and behavioural pathways. The
findings advance understanding of emotion-driven digital behaviour and offer
data-driven insights for designing emotionally resonant digital marketing
strategies.

1. Introduction
The rapid evolution of digital technologies has fundamentally transformed how consumers interact
with brands, information, and commercial environments. Unlike traditional marketing channels
characterized by passive information exposure, digital platforms e nable dynamic, real -time, and
personalized engagement experiences. Through features such as algorithm -driven
recommendations, interactive interfaces, multimedia storytelling, and user -generated content
mechanisms, contemporary digital ecosystems create imm ersive environments that actively shape
consumers’ cognitive and emotional states. As firms increasingly compete in attention-scarce digital

markets, understanding the psychological mechanisms that translate digital interaction features into
behavioral outcomes has become both theoretically critical and managerially urgent. A growing body
of literature acknowledges that consumer behavior in digital contexts is not driven solely by rational
evaluation but is deeply rooted in affective processes. Emotional responses —such as pleasure,
excitement, trust, and satisfaction—serve as immediate psychological reactions to digital stimuli and
function as proximal predictors of behavioral intentions. Theoretical perspectives including the
Stimulus–Organism–Response (SOR) framework (Mehrabian & Russell, 1974), affective decision -
making theory, and contemporary engagement models emphasize that environmental cues (stimuli)
influence internal emotional states (organism), which subsequently shape behavioral outcomes
(response). In digital environments, interaction attributes such as interactivi ty, personalization, and
content quality may function as critical stimuli that activate emotional processing pathways.
Despite extensive conceptual acknowledgment of emotional engagement in digital marketing,
empirical integration of key digital interaction attributes within a unified structural framework
remains limited. Prior research has often examined interactivity, pe rsonalization, or content quality
in isolation, thereby overlooking their simultaneous and comparative influence on emotional
responses. Moreover, many studies treat emotional engagement as an outcome rather than as a
mediating psychological mechanism link ing digital experiences to behavioral intentions. This
fragmented approach constrains theoretical advancement by failing to clarify the relative strength of
interaction features and the centrality of affective pathways in digital consumption processes.
Additionally, methodological limitations persist within the literature. A substantial portion of prior
research relies on single analytical approaches, limiting robustness and cross-method validation. Few
studies systematically compare covariance -based str uctural equation modeling (CB -SEM) and
variance-based partial least squares modeling (PLS -SEM) to assess the stability of emotional
pathways across estimation techniques. Given increasing calls for methodological rigor and
triangulation in digital behavior  research, dual validation offers stronger empirical grounding and
enhances confidence in structural relationships. Furthermore, the role of demographic heterogeneity
in digital emotional processing remains insufficiently explored. Digital consumers are not
homogeneous in how they interpret, internalize, and respond to interactive cues. Gender and age
differences may mo derate affective responses and behavioral translation processes, yet these
moderating effects are rarely examined within comprehensive emotional frameworks. Ignoring such
heterogeneity risks oversimplifying digital engagement dynamics and limits the precis ion of
managerial recommendations. To address these theoretical and empirical gaps, the present study
develops and validates a comprehensive structural model linking three core digital interaction
attributes—interactivity, personalization, and content quality —to emotional response and
subsequent purchase intention. Grounded in the Stimulus–Organism–Response paradigm, the model
conceptualizes digital interaction features as environmental stimuli, emotional response as the
internal affective organism, and purchase intention as the behavioral response. By empirically testing
emotional response as a central mediating mechanism, the study advances understanding of how
digital experiences are translated into behavioral outcomes.

2.1 Theoretical Foundation
Stimulus–Organism–Response (SOR) Framework in Digital Contexts
Understanding consumer behavior in digital environments requires a theoretical framework that
captures the psychological processes through which external technological stimuli influence internal
states and subsequent behavioral responses. The Stimulus –Organism–Response (SOR) model,
originally proposed by Mehrabian and Russell (1974), provides a robust foundation for explaining
such processes. According to the SOR paradigm, environmental stimuli influence an individual’s
internal psychological state (organism), which subsequently drives behavioral outcomes (response).
This framework has been widely applied in consumer behavior, digital marketing, and human –
computer interaction research to explain how technological environments shape affective and
behavioral r esponses. In digital marketing contexts, interaction attributes such as interactivity,
personalization, and content quality function as environmental stimuli that shape consumers’
emotional and cognitive experiences. These digital stimuli influence the org anism component,
represented by emotional responses such as pleasure, satisfaction, trust, and excitement. Emotional
response, in turn, influences behavioral outcomes, including purchase intention, engagement, and
loyalty. Unlike purely cognitive models such as the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), which focus
primarily on perceived usefulness and ease of use, the SOR framework emphasizes affective
processing as a central mechanism linking technological environments to behavioral responses.  The
relevance of the SOR framework is particularly pronounced in contemporary digital ecosystems
characterized by immersive interfaces, real -time feedback mechanisms, and personalized content
delivery. These features create dynamic environments that active ly stimulate users’ emotional
systems. Emotional responses serve as immediate evaluative reactions that guide decision -making,
often operating more rapidly and intuitively than cognitive deliberation. Thus, emotional response
represents a critical mediatin g mechanism through which digital interaction features influence
behavioral intention. Building on the SOR framework, this study conceptualizes interactivity,

variable, and purchase intention as the response variable. This theoretical structure provides a

2.2 Digital Interactivity as an Environmental Stimulus
Interactivity refers to the degree to which users can actively participate in modifying the form and
content of a mediated environment in real time. In digital marketing contexts, interactivity is
manifested through features such as clickable elements, interactive visualizations, comment systems,
chatbots, gamification mechanisms, and adaptive interfaces. These interactive affordances enable
consumers to engage in bidirectional communication with digital platforms, enhancing perceived
control and involvemen t. From an SOR perspective, interactivity functions as a powerful
environmental stimulus that enhances sensory engagement and psychological immersion. Interactive
environments increase perceived responsiveness and agency, which can stimulate emotional
activation. When consumers perceive that digital systems respond dynamically to their actions, they
experience heightened engagement, enjoyment, and satisfaction. This enhanced engagement
strengthens emotional attachment and positive affective responses. Prior  empirical studies
consistently demonstrate that interactivity enhances emotional engagement and user experience.
Interactive features create experiential richness, enabling consumers to actively explore digital
environments rather than passively receiving  information. This participatory experience increases
emotional stimulation by activating intrinsic motivational systems associated with curiosity,
exploration, and enjoyment. Moreover, interactivity enhances perceived presence, defined as the
subjective e xperience of being psychologically immersed in a mediated environment. Greater
presence intensifies emotional reactions by increasing perceptual realism and psychological
involvement. As digital technologies increasingly incorporate immersive features such as augmented
reality, interactive storytelling, and gamified interfaces, the emotional impact of interactivity
becomes even more pronounced. Therefore, consistent with the SOR framework and emotional
response theory, interactivity is expected to positively influence emotional response.
H1: Interactivity positively influences emotional response.
2.3 Personalization as a Self-Relevant Emotional Stimulus
Personalization refers to the adaptation of digital content, recommendations, and communication to
individual user preferences, behaviors, and characteristics. Advances in data analytics, artificial
intelligence, and machine learning have enabled firms to deliver highly tailored digital experiences.
Personalized recommendations, customized advertisements, and adaptive interfaces increase
perceived relevance and user -centricity. Within the SOR framework, personalization functions as a
highly self -relevant environmental stimulus. Self -relevance is a critical determinant of emotional
activation, as stimuli perceived as personally meaningful evoke stronger emotional responses than
generic stimuli. When digital platforms deliver personalized content, consumers perceive greater
alignment between their preferences and the presented information. This alignment enhances
perceived recognition, validation, and value, triggering positive emot ional responses.  Emotional
response theory suggests that stimuli congruent with individual preferences activate affective

experiences reduce cognitive effort by presenting relevant information, increasing processing fluency

enhances perceived relationship quality between consumers and digital platforms. When consumers
perceive that platforms understand their preferences and needs, they develop stronger emotional

2.4 Content Quality as a Cognitive and Affective Stimulus
Content quality represents the overall excellence of digital content in terms of informativeness,
clarity, accuracy, visual appeal, credibility, and entertainment value. High -quality digital content
enhances both cognitive processing and emotional engageme nt by facilitating understanding and
stimulating affective responses.  Within the SOR framework, content quality functions as a
fundamental environmental stimulus that influences both cognitive evaluation and emotional
reaction. Informative and well -structured content enhances perceived usefulness and
comprehension, reducing cognitive uncertainty and increasing confidence. This cognitive clarity
promotes positive emotional responses such as satisfaction and trust.  Visual aesthetics also play a
critical role in emotional processing. Visually appealing content activates sensory and affective
systems, enhancing emotional stimulation. Multimedia elements such as videos, animations, and
interactive graphics increase sens ory richness, amplifying emotional engagement.  Content quality
also enhances perceived credibility, which is closely linked to emotional trust. Trust represents an
emotional state that reduces perceived risk and enhances psychological comfort. High -quality
content signals reliability and professionalis m, strengthening positive emotional reactions.
Additionally, narrative coherence and storytelling elements enhance emotional immersion by
activating emotional empathy and identification processes. Emotional storytelling enables
consumers to form deeper psychological connections with digital content, st rengthening affective
engagement. Thus, content quality serves as both a cognitive and affective stimulus influencing
emotional response.
H3: Content quality positively influences emotional response.
2.5 Emotional Response as the Organism Mechanism Driving Behavioral Intention
Emotional response represents the internal affective state triggered by environmental stimuli. Within
the SOR framework, emotional response constitutes the organism component that mediates the
relationship between external stimuli and behavioral outcomes. Emotional responses include
affective states such as pleasure, excitement, trust, satisfaction, and emotional attachment.

demonstrates that emotional processing precedes and guides cognitive evaluation in many decision

2.6 Emotional Response as a Mediating Mechanism
The SOR framework emphasizes that environmental stimuli influence behavior indirectly through
internal psychological processes. Emotional response serves as a key mediating mechanism that
translates digital interaction stimuli into behavioral outcomes.  While interactivity, personalization,
and content quality may directly influence behavioral intention, their primary impact occurs through
emotional activation. Emotional response represents the psychological mechanism through which
consumers interpret and  internalize digital stimuli. Positive emotional responses enhance
motivational readiness and behavioral intention.  Mediation occurs because environmental stimuli
first influence emotional states, which subsequently guide behavioral decisions. Emotional response
functions as a psychological bridge connecting digital experiences to behavioral outcomes. Empirical
evidence supports the mediating role of emotional response in digital consumer behavior. Emotional
engagement has been shown to mediate relationships between digital interaction features and
behavioral intention across various digital contexts.  Therefore, emotional response is expected to
mediate the relationship between digital interaction attributes and purchase intention.
H5: Emotional response mediates the relationship between interactivity, personalization, content
quality, and purchase intention.

3.1 Research Design and Analytical Approach
This study employed a quantitative, cross -sectional research design to examine the structural
relationships among digital interaction attributes, emotional response, and purchase intention. A
survey-based approach was adopted to capture consumers’ perceptions and emotional experiences
with digital marketing environments. Quantitative methodology is appropriate for testing

validation through structural equation modeling (SEM). The proposed conceptual model was
examined using a dual-SEM analytical strategy integrating both covariance-based SEM (CB-SEM) and
variance-based SEM (PLS-SEM). CB-SEM, implemented using AMOS 24, was employed to assess the
measurement model, evaluate model fit, and test structural relationships using maximum likelihood
estimation. CB-SEM is particularly su itable for theory confirmation and model validation due to its
ability to assess overall model fit and measurement validity. PLS -SEM, implemented using SmartPLS

effect sizes through bootstrapping procedures. PLS -SEM is particularly effective for prediction -
oriented analysis and does not impose strict distributional assumptions. The combined use of CB -
SEM and PLS -SEM enhances methodological rigor, improves resul t robustness, and provides cross -
method validation of structural relationships, as recommended in contemporary SEM research. This
dual-method approach strengthens the reliability, validity, and generalizability of findings.
3.2 Sampling Procedure and Data Collection
Data were collected using a structured online questionnaire administered to active digital media
users. The target population consisted of individuals who had interacted with digital marketing
content, such as social media advertisements, personalized prod uct recommendations, or branded
digital content, within the previous three months. This inclusion criterion ensured that respondents
had recent and relevant digital interaction experiences. A non -probability convenience sampling
technique was employed due to its suitability for accessing digitally active consumers and its
widespread use in digital consumer behavior research. While probability sampling offers stronger
generalizability, convenience sampling is appropriate for theory testing and structural rel ationship
analysis in behavioral research contexts. A total of 437 responses were initially collected. After data
screening for completeness, response consistency, and outlier detection, 412 valid responses were
retained for analysis, yielding a usable response rate of 94.3%. This sample size exceeds the minimum
requirements for SEM analysis. According to Hair et al. (2022), a minimum sample size of 200 is
sufficient for SEM, while larger samples improve estimation accuracy and model stability.
The demographic characteristics of the sample were as follows:
• Gender: 218 male (52.9%), 194 female (47.1%)
• Age distribution:
o 18–30 years: 256 respondents (62.1%)
o 31–45 years: 156 respondents (37.9%)
This demographic distribution reflects active digital consumers, making the sample appropriate for
examining digital interaction behavior.

5. Conclusion and Discussion
The purpose of this study was to examine how core digital interaction attributes —interactivity,
personalization, and content quality —shape emotional responses and subsequently influence
purchase intention within contemporary digital marketing environments. Grounded in the Stimulus–
Organism–Response (SOR) framework, this research conceptualized digital interaction features as
environmental stimuli, emotional response as the internal organism mechanism, and purchase
intention as the behavioral response. The f indings provide strong empirical support for this
theoretical structure and offer several important insights into emotion -driven digital consumer
behavior. Overall, this study demonstrates that emotional response constitutes the primary

psychological mechanism through which digital interaction attributes influence purchase intention.
In contemporary digital environments, affective engagement operates as the central conduit linking
technological stimuli to consumer behavior. By integrating  theoretical rigor, methodological
robustness, and practical insight, the findings contribute meaningfully to the evolving discourse on
emotion-centered digital marketing.
5.1 Emotional Response as the Central Mechanism in Digital Consumer Behavior
The most prominent finding of this study is the strong effect of emotional response on purchase
intention (β = 0.69). This substantial coefficient confirms that emotional engagement is not merely
an auxiliary factor but a central mechanism driving behavioral intention in digital environments. This
result aligns with affective decision -making theory, which posits that emotional responses precede
and shape cognitive evaluations. In digital contexts characterized by information overload and rapid
decision-making, consumers rely heavily on affective cues to simplify choices. Emotional states such
as pleasure, trust, and excitement function as heuristic signals indicating desirability and value. Thus,
purchase intention emerges not only from rational evaluation but from affective appraisal. The strong
explanatory power of emotional response (R² = 0.52 for purchase intention) reinforces the argument
that affective processes are primary drivers of digital consumer behavior. This finding extends prior
research that has acknowledged emotional engagement but often treated it as a secondary outcome
rather than as a central mediating mechanism. By empirically demonstrating the magnitude of this
relationship, the present study advances the understanding of emotion as a dom inant behavioral
determinant in digital marketing contexts.
5.2 Personalization as the Most Powerful Emotional Stimulus
Among the three digital interaction attributes, personalization exhibited the strongest effect on
emotional response (β = 0.39). This finding underscores the psychological significance of self -
relevance in digital environments. From an SOR perspective, per sonalization represents a highly
individualized stimulus that enhances perceived relevance and congruence with personal
preferences. Emotional response theory suggests that stimuli aligned with personal identity and
needs evoke stronger affective reactions than generic stimuli. Personalized content reduces cognitive
effort, increases processing fluency, and enhances feelings of recognition and validation. These
mechanisms intensify positive emotional states.  This finding strengthens existing literature on
algorithm-driven personalization by highlighting its affective rather than merely functional impact.
While prior studies often emphasize personalization's role in improving usability or efficiency, the
present findings demonstrate its emotional potency. Personalization does not simply provide
convenience; it creates psychological resonance. The dominance of personalization suggests that in
highly competitive digital markets, emotional differentiation increasin gly depends on the ability to
tailor experiences to individual users. Algorithmic systems therefore function not only as
recommendation engines but as emotional architecture tools.
5.3 Interactivity as an Experiential Emotional Driver
Interactivity significantly influences emotional response (β = 0.34), confirming that participatory
digital experiences stimulate affective engagement. Interactive features enhance perceived control,

aligns with engagement theory, which argues that active participation strengthens psychological
involvement. Interactive environments transform users from passiv e information receivers into
active co -creators of experience. Such co -creation enhances emotional intensity by increasing
cognitive absorption and experiential richness. Notably, the effect of interactivity is slightly lower
than personalization, suggesting that while experiential immersion is important, perceived personal
relevance exerts stronger emotional impact. This nuance contributes theoretically by distinguishing
between experiential stimulation and self-relevant stimulation as distinct affective drivers.
5.4 Content Quality as a Foundational Emotional Enabler
Content quality also exerts a significant influence on emotional response (β = 0.27). Although smaller
than the effects of personalization and interactivity, its role remains essential. High -quality content
enhances cognitive clarity, perceived credibility , and aesthetic appeal, which collectively foster
emotional trust and satisfaction. Content quality reduces ambiguity and uncertainty, enabling
consumers to process information with confidence. Emotional comfort often emerges from cognitive
ease and informational reliability. The relatively smaller coefficient does not diminish its importance;
rather, it suggests that content quality functions as a foundational prerequisite for emotional
engagement. Without credible and well -structured content, even personalized and interactive
features may fail to generate sustained positive emotional responses. This layered interpretation
contributes theoretically by positioning content quality as the baseline emotional enabler upon which
personalization and interactivity build stronger affective intensity.
5.5 Mediation Mechanism: Validating the SOR Framework
The mediation analysis confirms that emotional response significantly mediates the relationship
between digital interaction attributes and purchase intention. This finding provides empirical
validation of the SOR framework within digital marketing contexts . Environmental stimuli
(interactivity, personalization, content quality) influence behavioral outcomes indirectly through
internal emotional processing. While direct experiential effects may exist, the primary mechanism
operates through affective activation. Emotional response acts as a psychological bridge translating
digital experience into behavioral motivation. This mediating structure advances prior research that
has often examined digital interaction features and purchase intention without explicitly modeling
the internal emotional mechanism. By empirically confirming this mediation, the study clarifies the
psychological pathway underlying digital engagement.
5.6 Demographic Moderation and Emotional Heterogeneity
The moderation analysis reveals that emotional pathways vary across demographic groups.
Personalization has a stronger effect on emotional response among female consumers, and
emotional response more strongly predicts purchase intention for females. This f inding aligns with
research suggesting that women exhibit greater sensitivity to relational and affective cues in
consumption contexts. Age differences further reveal that younger consumers respond more
strongly to interactivity, whereas older consumers de monstrate a stronger translation of emotion
into purchase intention. Younger users may prioritize stimulation and immersive experiences, while

5.7 Methodological Contribution: Cross-Method Validation
A key methodological contribution of this study is the dual application of CB -SEM and PLS-SEM. The
consistency of structural relationships across both analytical approaches strengthens confidence in
model robustness and stability. The CB-SEM confirms overall model fit and theoretical validity, while
PLS-SEM reinforces predictive accuracy and effect size estimation. Cross-method validation enhances
empirical credibility and addresses methodological critiques often raised in digital consumer behavior
research. The alignment of path coefficients across methods indicates that emotional pathways are
not artifacts of a specific estimation technique but reflect stable structural relationships.
5.8 Theoretical Contributions
This study advances digital consumer behavior literature in several ways:
1. It establishes emotional response as the central mechanism translating digital interaction
features into behavioral intention.
2. It integrates interactivity, personalization, and content quality within a unified structural
framework, enabling comparative evaluation of their emotional impact.
3. It demonstrates that personalization is the most powerful emotional stimulus in algorithm -
driven digital environments.
4. It validates the SOR framework as a robust theoretical model for explaining digital affect -
driven behavior.
5. It reveals demographic heterogeneity in emotional processing, extending theoretical
understanding of segmented digital engagement.
Collectively, these contributions deepen theoretical insight into the psychological mechanisms
underlying contemporary digital consumption.
5.9 Practical Implications
The findings provide clear managerial implications:
• Prioritize personalization systems to maximize emotional resonance.
• Embed interactive features to stimulate immersive engagement.
• Maintain high content quality as a foundational emotional enabler.
• Segment campaigns by demographic emotional sensitivity.
• Design digital experiences around emotional journey mapping rather than purely functional
