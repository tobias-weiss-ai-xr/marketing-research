# A systematic literature review on the fusion of face recognition and neuro-marketing

REVIEW
SN Business & Economics (2026) 6:129
https://doi.org/10.1007/s43546-026-01131-7
Abstract
This study conducts a systematic literature review to examine the intersection of fa -
cial recognition technologies and neuromarketing approaches in marketing research
and practice. Although both domains have received growing attention, prior studies
have largely investigated them in isolation, leaving a gap in our understanding of
their combined potential. Following the PRISMA framework, relevant studies were
identified through the Scopus and Web of Science databases, screened against the
inclusion criteria, and synthesized using thematic analysis. The findings reveal that
while neuromarketing research has traditionally focused on brain-based measures
of consumer responses (EEG, fMRI), and facial recognition studies have empha -
sized biometric profiling and personalization, integration between the two remains
limited. Five key themes emerged from the review: AI-driven profiling, consumer
behavior analysis, cognitive insights, ethical considerations, and marketing applica -
tions. By introducing a conceptual framework, this study posits that facial recogni -
tion and neuromarketing are complementary inputs that converge through cognitive
and emotional processing to influence consumer decision making and marketing
effectiveness, moderated by ethical and regulatory concerns. This review advances
theoretical integration by linking these technologies to established behavioral mod -
els, such as the theory of planned behavior and the technology acceptance model.
This study contributes to marketing scholarship by addressing a clear research gap
and offering a structured roadmap for future empirical research, including predictive
modelling and cross-cultural studies of consumer trust. Practitioners highlight the
opportunities and constraints of deploying these tools to design campaigns that are
both technologically advanced and ethically responsible.
Keywords Neuromarketing · Facial recognition · Consumer behaviour · Cognitive
processing · Ethical marketing.

Introduction
Businesses in this highly competitive marketplace constantly seek innovative and
creative ways to engage with customers. According to research conducted by Hu
et al. ( 2015), there is a growing emphasis on adopting automated marketing strat -
egies to stimulate market demand. In recent years, two emerging disciplines–face
recognition and neuromarketing–have garnered considerable attention. The integra -
tion of artificial intelligence-powered face recognition technology has revolutionized
how businesses gather and analyze consumer data (Spivak et al. 2019). Buckley and
Hunter (2011) highlight that facial recognition can differentiate between individual
customers. By analyzing facial expressions, age, gender, and emotions, companies
can gain valuable insights into customer preferences and behaviors. Marketers can
create detailed profiles of their clients by analyzing data collected from sensors, inter-
net services, and various devices. These insights can be utilized to tailor marketing
campaigns, develop products, and enhance the consumer experience. These insights
allow the customization of products and services that target specific consumer groups
possible (Wu et al. 2015).
Artificial intelligence-powered facial recognition technology in marketing is
increasingly used to segment consumers according to their characteristics and per -
spectives (Nancarrow et al. 1996). Facial recognition is gaining popularity as a mar-
keting tool (Micu et al. 2022). Marketers rely heavily on recommendation system
tools, which use team filtering to target potential consumers. According to Jarek and
Mazurek (2019), customers are segmented using personalized data. Machine learn -
ing models based on artificial intelligence can provide valuable insights by accu -
rately measuring clients’ reactions to facial cues such as eye activities, blinks, pupil
dilations, and head actions. Businesses can use this understanding to develop effec -
tive marketing approaches (Wedel and Pieters 2014). Consumers tend to remember
images of products that evoke strong emotions (Riaz et al. 2018). The relevance of
these images is particularly emphasized in the context of facial recognition data.
Emotional responses play a significant role in influencing emotions and in affecting
the amount of information retained (Riaz et al. 2018). Addressing secrecy concerns is
a critical aspect of the facial recognition technology. Protecting personal information
is key to ensuring user satisfaction (Zhou 2020). Face-recognition marketing, which
focuses on personalized marketing, has yielded higher returns than bulk marketing
strategies (Zhou 2020). This method allows businesses to target prospective custom-
ers based on their emotional reactions to their products or services. Additionally,
face recognition techniques are cost-effective and time-efficient as they can quickly
attract a large audience quickly (Zhou et al. 2020). The results of facial recognition
marketing are measurable and marketers can track consumer responses, thus ensur -
ing a higher level of precision and accuracy (Xu et al. 2021). By combining facial
and emotion recognition, facial recognition marketing provides valuable information
regarding consumers’ reactions to external stimuli (Xu et al. 2021).
Based on customer photos, the feature extraction approach generates a unique
model. A deeper learning strategy has been used to develop more accurate facial rec-
ognition models (Lixandroiu et al. 2020). Face recognition is used to target potential
clients on numerous social media sites. Every day, over 350 million photographs

Although previous studies have independently explored neuromarketing and
facial recognition, research integrating these two domains remains highly frag -
mented. Neuromarketing studies predominantly rely on brain-based measures (EEG
and fMRI) to capture subconscious consumer responses, whereas facial recognition
research has mainly focused on biometric profiling and personalization in retail and
digital contexts. However, little is known about how these technologies can be com-
bined to provide a holistic understanding of consumer behavior, decision-making,
and marketing effectiveness. To address this gap, this study systematically reviews
and synthesizes existing work to propose an integrative conceptual framework that
bridges neuromarketing and facial recognition, thereby extending both theoretical
and practical insights into marketing research. However, a comprehensive exami -
nation of the existing literature on this topic is required. Therefore, to address this
knowledge gap, the following research question is proposed.

neuromarketing contribute to enhancing contemporary marketing strategies?

Recent advances in facial recognition and neuromarketing (2020–2024)
The last three years have marked a significant shift in how research on facial recog -
nition and neuromarketing is being conducted due to developments made to artifi -
cial intelligence, deep learning (DL), and ethical governance frameworks. AI-driven
facial analytics have evolved from a simple demographic-based type of identification
through the use of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and transformer models,
to a far more sophisticated technology that provides marketers with the ability to
interpret various attributes of still facial images including microexpressions, gaze
direction, and emotional state at real-time. This allows marketers to make more accu-
rate predictions of consumers’ responses during their interactions with a brand. At the
same time, there have been significant advances in emotion recognition through DL
techniques that have improved the ability to generalise to diverse cultural, environ -
mental, and situational contexts but alleviating concerns about bias, noise and con -
textual sensitivity (Bellamkonda and Gopalan 2020; Li et al. 2021). Neuromarketing
has evolved beyond exploratory neuroscience approaches and has shifted to a more
integrative model of combining different forms of Neuro-AI methods including EEG,
eye tracking, and physiological measures, with DL algorithms to predict consumers’
preferences, level of involvement and decision-making outcomes at greater levels of
accuracy (Hakim et al. 2021; Aldayel et al. 2020).

A major focus of post-2020 has been on the ethical use of artificial intelligence
(AI) and regulatory compliance as significant global issues arising from the imple -
mentation of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and other similar data
protection laws in countries throughout the world. Current research has begun to
expose the importance of transparency, informed consent, algorithmic accountabil -
ity, and consumer trust as key factors affecting how technology is accepted in bio -
metrics and neuromarketing. These policies reflect a change in how neuromarketing
and facial recognition can be classified, as they are no longer just viewed as new
technological approaches, but also as tools that exist within a socio-technical frame-
work of ethical meaning, and should be used as part of the overall decision-making
process regarding how to use them to their fullest effect. The convergence of neuro -
science and AI is now one of the major areas of research; called Neuro-AI. It allows
researchers to gather multiple types of data from different sources (neural, facial, and
behavioral) into a single model of consumer cognition and emotional reaction. While
the Neuro-AI paradigm allows for the emergence of new ways of understanding and
modelling consumers; it also provides a pathway for researchers to develop theoreti-
cally based, predictive, and ethically grounded marketing intelligence systems. All of
this new research emerging post-2020 situates facial recognition and neuromarketing
at the centre of providing the next generation of data-based marketing strategies, and
reinforces the need for companies to maintain the highest levels of ethical standards
in order to sustain the trust of their consumers and society in general.
Methodology
This study adhered to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and
Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) framework to ensure methodological transparency, repro-
ducibility, and rigor. The literature search was conducted using two leading academic
databases, Scopus and Web of Science (WOS), which provide extensive coverage of
peer-reviewed research across disciplines. Search Strings: The Boolean search terms
used were: ("neuromarketing" OR "neuro-marketing") AND ("face recognition" OR
"facial recognition") AND ("marketing").
PRISMA guidelines were used to screen and determine which of the articles
retrieved were worthy of inclusion according to the research objectives. Each article
was independently reviewed at both the title and abstract stages by two of the authors
to ensure relevance to the research. The articles were reviewed with respect to spe -
cific predetermined inclusion criteria: (i) An explicit focus on neuromarketing and/or
facial recognition in the context of marketing and/or consumer behaviour; (ii) Journal
articles or conference papers that were reviewed by a peer; (iii) Articles published
in English between 2000 and 2023. Those articles that did not have any market -
ing relevance, were published in non-reviewed journals, or contained only technical
information were excluded at this stage. Any disagreements that arose between the
reviewers when reviewing the articles were resolved through discussion and mutual
agreement of both reviewers, and where appropriate, the third reviewer was con -
sulted to eliminate any bias. The same criteria that were used during the first phase
were used to screen full text to establish whether or not the articles met the criteria for

Screening procedure and study selection
Research article selection was processed through the PRISMA framework for sys -
tematic reviews and meta-analyses to promote transparency and reproducibility. The
first database search resulted in 592 records: Scopus (n = 52) and Web of Science
(n = 540). According to the basic relevance criterion and incomplete records were
removed and resulted in 39 Scopus records, leading to 193 total titles/abstracts being
reviewed from Web of Science.
During title and abstract review and screening, any studies that did not meet the
English-language requirement, as well as book chapters, editorial articles, and simi -
larly non-relevant studies regarding Facial Recognition or Neuromarketing within
their marketing/context domains, were eliminated.
As a result, there were 30 full text articles that were retrieved from the search and
included in the eligibility assessment process based on pre-determined inclusion cri-
teria including, but not limited to, application of neuromarketing and/or facial recog-
nition to marketing, consumer behaviour and/or other related businesses. In addition,
a separate examination of a larger pool (160) of articles was conducted to establish
thematic saturation and to ensure comprehensive coverage of the area of research.
Of the 30 full text articles evaluated, 20 of the empirical and review studies spe -
cifically addressed using facial recognition combined with neuromarketing, and
were therefore included in the final qualitative synthesis. Additionally, a total of 106
review articles were also reviewed that provided a variety of theoretical, conceptual,
methodological, and ethical contributions to the evidence base, but which were not
all included within the final thematic synthesis. The multi-phase screening and eligi-
bility assessment of articles ensured that only articles that were high quality, relevant,
and methodologically sound informed the final analysis, thus enhancing the scientific
quality and reliability of the review.
Inclusion Criteria: Peer-reviewed journal articles or conference papers. Written in
English. Explicitly addressed neuromarketing and/or facial recognition in the context
of marketing applications.
The exclusion criterion was non-English publications. Book chapters, editorials,
commentaries, and non-peer-reviewed materials. Studies have focused exclusively
on the technical aspects of facial recognition without marketing relevance. Articles
unavailable in full-text format.
Data Extraction and Analysis: Each eligible article was systematically reviewed
to extract key details, including author(s), year, objectives, methodology, context,
and major findings. The selected studies were then synthesized through thematic
analysis and grouped into recurring categories, including AI-driven profiling, con -
sumer behavior analysis, ethical and privacy concerns, and marketing applications.

Face recognition technology
Artificial intelligence-driven facial recognition technology can be used to observe
eyeball movement in response to external stimuli. Within the marketing domain,
organizations employ neuromarketing technology to ascertain which items capture
the attention of their clientele. This information can be used to deliver specialized
goods and services to consumers. Consequently, facial expressions serve as a conduit
for conveying the sentiments and dispositions of the observer, enabling marketers
to refine their strategies and effectively engage with the target audience (Hu et al.
2015). The ability to identify faces from photographs is advantageous for creating
promotional material. The face recognition task is intricate and requires proficiency
in storing both known and unknown faces. Facial data processing occurs in two dis -
tinct phases (Moriuchi 2021). The initial stage involves categorizing basic details,
whereas the second phase involves collecting facial data to gain further insights into
consumers. Through these two phases, marketers harness the expression of their
clients to tailor their messaging and cultivate a base of loyal consumers (Moriu -
chi 2021). An individual’s neuroinformation influences their facial expressions. In
survey-based research, capturing the emotional aspects of consumer judgment and
choice is challenging. The integration of neuroscience instruments helps to bridge
the gaps inherent in survey-based research. Neuromarketing technologies rely on
clinical brain data and cerebral cues to understand consumer behavior. Among the
various methods employed to generate neurological images, magnetoencephalogra -
phy (MEG) is the most widely used (Skriabin et al., 2021). Other methods, such
as positron emission tomography, electroencephalography, and functional magnetic
resonance imaging (fMRI), have also been employed to create neurological images.
These methods provide compelling evidence that facilitates an understanding of how
brain processes convey information and knowledge through advertisements, thereby
affecting purchasing decisions.
According to Fugate ( 2007), neuromarketing employs stimuli derived from the
mind to construct a shopper behavioral model by connecting internal neural activity
with external consumer actions. The use of facial recognition and neural information
systems aids in the prediction of shopper behavior. Zhou et al. ( 2020) introduced
facial recognition as an innovative marketing technology that records and analyzes
customer response. High-definition cameras are used in facial recognition software
to identify facial expressions and provide personalized recommendations. An exem-
plary instance is found in music recommendation applications that utilize users’ song
preferences to deduce their emotions and suggest other tracks. According to Xu et
al. (2020), social media platforms employ facial expression analysis to comprehend
the details associated with users’ language or comments, and propose advertisements
that align with specific emotions. Facebook, the parent company of Instagram and
WhatsApp (Moriuchi 2021), employs facial recognition technology widely. AI-

Neuromarketing focuses on comprehending consumers’ behavioural patterns and
decision-making processes by investigating neurological responses to marketing
stimuli. By employing tools such as fMRI, EEG, and eye tracking, neuromarketing
researchers can quantitatively assess consumers’ subconscious reactions to differ -

EEG
An electroencephalogram (EEG) is a scientific instrument that gauges brain activ -

resents a frequently employed methodology in neuromarketing, enabling marketers
to better understand consumer behavior by examining neuroscientific data (Zamani
and Naieni 2020). EEG methodologies aid marketers in predicting sales, pinpointing
unspoken factors that influence consumer behavior, assessing willingness to spend,
and better comprehending preferences (Aldayel et al. 2020). Given its superior tem-
poral resolution compared to other cerebral imaging techniques, EEG frequently
resolves marketing-related predicaments. Neuroscientists have used EEG data to
construct classification models and extract significant consumer insights (Hsu and
Chen 2020). EEG is an economical and nonintrusive electrophysiological approach
that employs electrodes to monitor cerebral electrical activity. Research conducted
by Aldayel et al. ( 2020) substantiate the utility of EEG-based neuromarketing tests
in evaluating how films influence individuals’ reactions to visual representations of
destinations. These findings validate the effectiveness of EEG and neuromarketing
for assessing consumer responses (Bastiaansen et al. 2018). Contemporary neuroim-
aging techniques such as electroencephalography have been used to investigate cere-
bral functionality (Alsharif et al. 2021). fMRI, fNIRS, and EEG are methodologies
used to quantitatively measure neural responses to marketing stimuli, including prod-
ucts, branding, advertising, and pricing. These techniques ascertain and evaluate the
efficacy of all marketing stimuli in enhancing their effectiveness, emotional appeal,
memorability, influence on sales, and impact on purchasing decisions (Vecchiato and
Babiloni 2011).

Discussion
Theme 1: AI-Driven Profiling and Personalisation

The convergence of neuromarketing tools and facial recognition technologies has
enabled unprecedented levels of consumer profiling and personalization. Studies
such as Micu et al. ( 2022) illustrate how deep learning algorithms applied to facial
expressions can generate real-time insights into consumer reactions, whereas neu -
romarketing techniques, such as EEG and fMRI, add an additional layer of subcon -
scious cognitive and emotional data. Together, these methods advance the potential
of hyperpersonalized marketing. However, the current literature reveals a meth -
odological gap; most studies employ either neuromarketing or facial recognition
independently, leaving limited empirical evidence of their integrated effectiveness.
Theoretically, this aligns with Technology Adoption Models (TAM/UTAUT), where
trust, usability, and perceived value shape consumer acceptance of AI-driven market-
ing systems.

Theme 2: Consumer Behaviour and Cognitive Insights

A significant strand of neuromarketing research focuses on uncovering the cogni -
tive and emotional drivers of consumer decision making (Stasi et al. 2018; Andrew
et al. 2019). Facial recognition technologies can extend these insights by decoding
micro-expressions and non-verbal cues in real time, thereby offering a more complete
picture of consumer behavior. Despite this promise, integration of facial recognition

into consumer psychological frameworks remains underdeveloped. Current studies
often stop at descriptive correlations without modelling how facial and neurological
data interact. Linking this research to established consumer behavior theories, such
as the theory of planned behavior (TPB) or behavioral reasoning theory (BRT), could
strengthen the explanatory power by situating biometric responses within motiva -
tional and attitudinal contexts.

Theme 3: Ethical and Privacy Concerns

Ethical issues constitute a critical fault in the integration of facial recognition and
neuromarketing. While many studies acknowledge privacy risks, few provide empiri-
cal analyses of consumers’ perceptions or safeguards. Regulatory frameworks such
as the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and the California Consumer
Privacy Act (CCPA) establish boundaries for biometric and neurological data use,
yet academic literature lags in exploring their implications for marketing. Beyond
legal compliance, ethical decision-making frameworks that emphasize transparency,
informed consent, and consumer autonomy should be adopted. Failure to address
these concerns risks eroding consumer trust and undermining the legitimacy of neu -
romarketing applications.

Theme 4: Applications in Marketing Practice

Practical applications of neuromarketing and facial recognition span advertis -
ing, retail, product design, and digital engagement. For instance, EEG-based neu -
romarketing has been used to evaluate advertising effectiveness (Daugherty et al.
2016), whereas facial recognition has facilitated adaptive digital signage in retail
environments (Loh and Shaharuddin 2019). However, the literature indicates that
applications remain fragmented and experimental with little integration into holistic
marketing strategies. Bridging these domains could enable marketers to design con -
sumer experiences that are context aware, emotionally resonant, and dynamically
adaptive. However, widespread implementation will require not only technological
integration, but also alignment with consumer trust and regulatory frameworks.

Theme 5: Future Integration and research directions

Despite growing interest in neuromarketing and facial recognition, theoretical inte -
gration remains limited. Current research rarely combines biometric and neurological
data within established behavioral frameworks such as the theory of planned behavior
or behavioral reasoning theory. Moreover, predictive consumer models fail to incor-
porate biometric cues and cross-cultural perspectives on consumer trust, privacy, and
acceptance are largely absent. Future research should adopt multimodal approaches
that capture neurological signals (EEG and fMRI) along with facial data, explore
predictive modelling for segmentation and personalized advertising, and examine the
cultural and ethical dimensions of technology adoption. Such work could extend both
consumer behavior theory and technology adoption models by embedding biometric
and subconscious processes into their constructs.

A number of recent studies conducted since 2020 demonstrate that the direction

Conceptual framework
The proposed framework highlights how face recognition and neuromarketing oper-
ate as complementary inputs in marketing research and practice. Facial recognition
technologies capture observable biometric and demographic cues, including micro-
expressions, emotional states, and physical traits, whereas neuromarketing tools
(EEG, fMRI, and eye-tracking) provide deeper insights into subconscious and cogni-
tive responses. These data streams converge through cognitive and emotional pro -
cessing, which mediate how consumers interpret stimuli, engage with brands, and
make purchase decisions. Ethical and regulatory factors such as privacy, consent,
and trust function as critical moderators that influence the extent to which these tech-
nologies can be effectively implemented. The outcomes of this integration are two -
fold: an enriched understanding of consumer behavior (decision making, loyalty, and
attitudes) and enhanced marketing effectiveness (personalized campaigns, consumer
engagement, and improved conversion rates). By situating this model within estab -
lished theories such as the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) and the theory of
planned behavior (TPB), the framework advances a conceptual pathway for future
empirical research that connects biometric signals, subconscious processes, and con-
