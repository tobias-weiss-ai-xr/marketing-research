Abstract
:
This
paper

examines the socioeconomic development of
potter and washerman communities
under Mission Kakatiya, using
field
-
survey data from

120 potter respondents and 120 washerman respondents from Kamalapur, Kesamudram, Manchiryal,

and
Khammam.

The findings indicate that Mission Kakatiya had a particularly visible effect on pottery production through improved
perceptions of clay quality, increased water availability and increased production. Among potters, 89.17% reported
increased pottery
production, and 65% reported improved

monthly income after Mission Kakatiya. Among washermen, 60.83% reported that
tank
restoration

helped their occupational activities, while 63.33% perceived additional employment opportunities. However, the results also
reveal persistent constraints, including inadequate modern equipment, irregular income, marketing difficulties, competition f
rom
me
chanised services and limited government support.
The findings from statistical analysis (Chi
-
square results show significant
associations between study location and increased pottery production (χ² = 16.132, p = 0.001), improvement in monthly income
(χ² =
39.414, p < 0.001), and direct occupational suppo
rt (χ² = 57.109, p < 0.001).
The study therefore argues that tank restoration can generate
multidimensional livelihood effects, but these effects
strengthen when physical water infrastructure is accompanied by occupational
technology, credit, marketing, skill development,

and institutional support.

1.

Introduction

Water resources have historically shaped
rural Telangana's
economic and social organisation
. Tanks and ponds have not
merely served as irrigation structures; they have supported a
broader rural production system involving agriculture,
livestock, fisheries and several traditional occupations. The
Government of Telangana describes minor irrigation

tanks as
an important part of the state's rural economy and identifies
tank restoration as a
way to strengthen

water security and
livelihood opportunities. Mission Kakatiya was designed to
restore minor irrigation sources through activities including
desiltation, repair of feeder channels, strengthening of bunds,
repair of sluices and restoration of water
-
storage capacity.
(
Telangana
)
. Water is a foundation of life and livelihoods and
is key to sustainable development. Successful water
management will help achieve many of the 17 Sustainable
Development Goals (SDGs), including SDG6, which aims to
ensure the availability and sustainable
use of water and
sanitation for all (Lal, 2019).

Mission Kakatiya was launched in March 2015 with the
broader objective of restoring Telangana's tank system and
improving water availability. The programme's official
objectives include strengthening minor irrigation
infrastructure, improving water availab
ility and promoting
community
-
based management. (
Yadadri Bhuvanagiri
District
)
. Mission Kakatiya has developed as a revolutionary
program in Telangana, restoring traditional tank systems and
developing different ecosystems that support rural livelihoods
(Lal, 2026).

The economic significance of tank restoration extends beyond
crop production. Traditional occupational groups often
depend upon local ecological resources. Pottermen require
suitable clay and water for production, while washermen
need
accessible,

adequate water resources and appropriate washing
locations. Consequently, changes in
tank conditions can
affect production costs, working conditions, employment
opportunities,

and household incomes.

Recent empirical research on the Kummari community in
Telangana similarly shows that pottery remains an important
livelihood for many households, while access to clay,
technology, production and marketing remain significant
socioeconomic concerns (Harinath

& Rao, 2023).
(
ResearchGate
)

The present study therefore examines whether the restoration
of tanks under Mission Kakatiya has contributed to the
socioeconomic development of pottermen

and washermen
communities in Telangana. The analysis is based on the field
-
survey tables supplied with this study.

2.

Mission Kakatiya and the Rural Livelihood
System

Mission Kakatiya represents a shift from viewing tanks
exclusively as irrigation infrastructure toward recognising
their wider economic and ecological functions. Government
documentation identifies improved groundwater conditions,
agricultural productivity
, livestock development
, and rural
economic revitalisation as

expected benefits of tank
restoration. (
Telangana
)

The socioeconomic pathway can be represented as:

Tank
restoration → improved water storage → better
availability of water/clay → reduced occupational
constraints → increased production/employment →
improved household income and livelihood security
.

For potters, the relationship is particularly relevant because
tank and pond beds can provide clay used in pottery
production. The attached survey shows that 85.83% of the
potter respondents reported that sufficient clay was available
in village tanks and
ponds before Mission Kakatiya, while
ponds were the major source of clay for 71.67% of
respondents. At the same time, 51.67% travelled more than
seven kilometres to collect clay before Mission Kakatiya.

For washermen, the link is even more direct because their
occupational activities require water and appropriate washing
locations. Thus,
restoring tanks can potentially improve the
physical conditions under which they perform their traditional
occupation
.

3.

Objectives of the Study

The study has the following objectives:

1)

To examine the socioeconomic characteristics of
pottermen and washermen households covered by the
survey.

2)

To assess the occupational conditions of pottermen

and
washermen before Mission Kakatiya.

3)

To examine perceived changes in occupational
conditions after tank restoration.

4)

To analyse changes in production, income, employment
and livelihood opportunities.

5)

To identify the continuing constraints faced by the two
communities.

6)

To suggest policy measures for strengthening the
socioeconomic benefits of Mission Kakatiya for
traditional occupational groups.

3.1. Hypotheses

H₀₁:

There is no significant association between the study
location and the reported increase in pottery production after
Mission Kakatiya.

H₀₂:

There is no significant association between the study
location and the reported improvement in monthly income
from pottery after Mission Kakatiya.

H₀₃:

There is no significant association between the study
location and whether potters reported receiving direct
occupational support from Mission Kakatiya.

4.

Methodology

The analysis is based primarily on the tables provided in the
field
-
survey document. The survey covers 120 potter
respondents and 120 washermen respondents,
distributed
across Kamalapur, Kesamudram, Manchiryal,

and
Khammam, with 30 respondents from each location for each
occupational group.

The pottery tables
include demographic characteristics,
occupational structure, landholding, access to clay, distance
travelled for clay collection, clay quality, pottery production,
monthly income, government assistance, marketing,

and post
-
Mission Kakatiya changes. The washermen tables similarly
cover occupational conditions, water availability,
employment, income, entrepreneurial activities, women's
participation, constraints and perceptions of Mission
Kakatiya.

5.

Results and Discussion

Covering 120 potter respondents and 120 washermen
respondents from Kamalapur, Kesamudram, Manchiryal and
Khammam. The pottery section documents demographic,
occupational, clay access, production, income and post
-
Mission Kakatiya conditions.

5.1 Socioeconomic Profile of Pottermen

The demographic table indicates that the pottery sample
consisted predominantly of men. Of the 120 respondents, 112
(93.33%) were male
,

and eight (6.67%) were female. The age
distribution shows that 41 respondents (34.16%)
were aged
46
–
60 years
, while 36 (30%) were aged 36
–
45 years. Only 19
respondents (15.83%) were below 35 years.

Educational attainment was relatively low. 51 respondents
(42.5%) were illiterate, while 31 (25.83%) had primary
education and 19 (15.83%) had secondary education. Only a
small proportion had higher secondary, graduate or
postgraduate qualifications. All 1
20 respondents belonged to
the BC category, and 89.17% lived in nuclear families.

Occupational dependence on pottery was particularly strong.
All 120 respondents identified pottery making as their
primary occupation and reported that
it was their family's
traditional

occupation.

This finding is important because it shows that pottery is not
simply a supplementary economic activity for these
households; it is closely connected to occupational identity
and intergenerational livelihood practices.

5.2 Pottery Production and Economic Conditions

The survey shows that pottery households combine their
traditional occupation with other livelihood activities.
Agriculture was the secondary occupation of 48 respondents
(40%), wage labour of 51 respondents (42.5%), and small
business of 20 respondents (1
6.67%). This occupational
diversification indicates that pottery income alone may not
provide sufficient livelihood security for all households.

5.3 Mission Kakatiya and Availability of Clay for
Pottermen

One of the most important findings concerns clay.

Before
Mission Kakatiya, 60% of the potter respondents reported
difficulty in collecting clay. The problem was particularly
pronounced in Manchiryal and Khammam. Moreover,
51.67% travelled more than seven kilometres to collect clay,
suggesting substantial
time and transport burdens.

The post
-
Mission Kakatiya findings indicate that 79
respondents (65.83%) reported
adequate clay
in tanks and
ponds after the programme, while 115 respondents (95.83%)
reported
improved clay quality
.

However,
the survey also found that

only 16 respondents
(13.33%) reported that the distance to clay collection had
decreased, while 104 (86.67%) said it had not. Similarly, 117
respondents (97.5%) said that expenditure on clay collection
had not declined.

This indicates that Mission Kakatiya's benefits for potters
were not uniform. Improvements in the physical condition or
quality of clay did not necessarily translate into lower
collection costs.

This issue is also recognised in research on Telangana's
Kummari community. Harinath and Rao (2023) report that
although tank restoration creates important water
-
resource
benefits, potters may
struggle to obtain clay because
desiltation and tank
-
related processes can reduce

access to
usable pottery clay. (
ResearchGate
)

5.4 Increase in Pottery Production

The most prominent occupational finding is
increased

pottery
production.

107 out of 120 respondents (89.17%) reported
that pottery production increased after Mission Kakatiya,
while only 13 (10.83%) reported no increase. The proportion
reporting increased production was 100% in Kesamudram,
93.3% in Kamalapur and Khammam, and 70
% in Manchiryal.

Respondents identified four principal ways through which
Mission Kakatiya helped pottery workers:

Increased clay
availability
–

30%
,
Improved quality of clay
–

15.83%
,
Increased water storage
–

41.67%

and
Reduced cost of clay
–

12.5%

Thus, increased water storage was the most frequently
identified benefit.

This finding illustrates the indirect
livelihood pathway of tank restoration. The programme
is not
a pottery
-
development programme in a narrow sense, yet its
water
-
related interventions can influence a traditional
occupation whose production process depends on

natural
resources.

5.6 Socioeconomic Conditions of Washermen

The washermen community represents another traditional
occupational group whose livelihood is closely connected
with water resources.

Before Mission Kakatiya, respondents reported several
occupational problems.
Respondents identified water
shortage (39.17%), lack of proper washing places (24.17%),
less work or customers (23.33%), and dirty water (11.67%)
.

The broader pre
-
Mission Kakatiya condition table identifies
low income and unemployment as the most significant
problem, reported by 55% of respondents, followed by water
scarcity in tanks and ponds
(24.17%) and irregular washing
work caused by lack of water (17.5%)
.

These findings establish that water scarcity was not simply an
environmental problem; it had direct labour
-
market and
income consequences for washermen.

5.7 Restoration of Tanks and Washermen's Occupational
Activities

The survey shows a generally positive occupational response
to tank restoration.

73 respondents (60.83%) reported that
tank
restoration

helped their occupational activities, while 47
(39.17%) did not report such an effect.

Location
-
wise,
Kamalapur

had the strongest response, with
90% of respondents reporting

occupational benefits. The
corresponding proportions were 60% in Kesamudram, 53.3%
in Khammam and 40% in Manchiryal.

This finding supports the interpretation that restoring tanks
can influence occupations that depend directly on

accessible
water.

The official objectives of Mission Kakatiya emphasise
restoring tank capacity, improving water availability, and
revitalising rural economies
. (
Telangana
) The washermen
survey demonstrates one way in which these broad objectives
can be reflected in a traditional rural occupation.

5.9. Women's Participation

The washermen tables also provide evidence concerning
women's participation.

According to the survey
,
38.33%
reported that women participated in washing work.

20%
reported improved employment opportunities for women.

22.5% reported reduced workload because of improved water
availability.

10.83% reported increased support to family
income
, and
8.33% reported women's involvement in other
income
-

The results suggest that improved water availability may have
household
-

men's occupational
roles
. Reduced physical difficulty in accessing water can
potentially affect women's time allocation and participation in
livelihood activities.

However, the relatively modest percentage reporting new
income
-
generating activities indicates

that improved water
resources alone are insufficient to transform gender roles
within the occupation. Additional measures such as women's
entrepreneurship programmes, credit access, skills training
and collective enterprises would be required.

5.10 Living Standards and Social Status

The survey provides a mixed picture of socioeconomic
development among washermen.

63 respondents (52.5%)
reported that Mission Kakatiya improved the living standards
of washermen families, whereas 57 (47.5%) did not report
such improvement.

The impact on social status was considerably weaker: only 27
respondents (22.5%) reported improvement in social status,
while 93 (77.5%) reported no improvement.

This distinction is important. Economic or occupational
improvement does not automatically result in social
-
status
transformation. Traditional occupational groups may
continue to face social constraints even when their income or
employment conditions impro
ve.

The results therefore suggest that the socioeconomic impact
of Mission Kakatiya is multidimensional but uneven. The
programme appears to have a clearer connection with water,
employment and occupational activity than with broader
social
-
status transformati
on.

6.

Discussion

The evidence supports a broader conception of rural
infrastructure. Mission Kakatiya was conceived principally as
a tank
-
restoration and water
-
security intervention, but its
effects can extend through local livelihood networks. The
official programme docum
entation itself recognises that tanks
have functions extending beyond irrigation, including
groundwater recharge, livestock, fisheries and rural economic
activity. (
Telangana
)

The pottery findings illustrate this mechanism through the
resource relationship between tanks, clay and water.
Increased water storage and perceived improvement in clay
quality were associated with increased pottery production
among a large majority of re
spondents.

The washermen findings demonstrate a different pathway.
For washermen, the critical resource is not primarily clay but
accessible water and suitable washing locations. Tank
restoration can therefore reduce an important occupational
constraint.

Nevertheless, the data also demonstrate the limits of
infrastructure
-
led development. Potters continue to report low
prices, transportation problems and competition from
alternative products. Washermen continue to face shortages
of modern machinery, irregular income and competition from
mechanised laundries.

This
aligns with the broader literature on traditional
occupations in Telangana, which emphasises the importance
of technology, market access, institutional support,

and
collective organisation alongside
access to natural resources
(Harinath & Rao, 2023). (
ResearchGate
)

The findings are also compatible with recent empirical work
examining Mission Kakatiya's effects across multiple rural
livelihood groups, which reports improvements in several
occupational outcomes following tank restoration.
(
ResearchGate

8.

Conclusion

Mission Kakatiya demonstrates how investment in water
infrastructure can have socioeconomic implications
extending beyond agriculture. The evidence from the 120
potter and 120 washermen respondents shows that tank
restoration has been associated with impro
vements in several
livelihood dimensions, although the magnitude and nature of
these changes vary across locations and occupational groups.

For pottermen, the most prominent findings are

Suggestions

The evidence therefore suggests that Mission Kakatiya has

skills and social inclusion.
