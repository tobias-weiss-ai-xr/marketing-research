Abstract
Digital transformation is profoundly reshaping rural socio-economic landscapes and livelihood
trajectories worldwide. However, the spatially differentiated mechanisms through which specific
mobile social platforms reconfigure farmers' social-spatial networks and the consequent
heterogeneous livelihood outcomes remain insufficiently theorized and empirically underexplored
within an integrated analytical framework. This study examines that WeChat restructures social and
spatial networks among apple farmers and its differentiated impact on household income. Drawing on
primary survey data from apple farmers in Li County, a major apple-producing region in northwestern
China. we develop and operationalize a " WeChat mediation–network reconfiguration–livelihood
outcome" framework through a mixed-methods approach that integrates spatial network analysis,
social network analysis, and two-stage least squares. The findings reveal that WeChat-mediated sales
networks exhibit fundamentally distinct spatial topological structures and spatial morphologies
compared with conventional marketing channels. Apple farmers' commercial linkages span vast
geographical distances, predominantly interfacing with major consumer markets in eastern China,
thereby bypassing the hierarchical constraints imposed by conventional wholesale intermediaries. The
structural characteristics of social networks and their geographical configurations vary systematically
across farmers' livelihood strategies and geographical locations. Two-stage least squares estimation
confirms that WeChat-based sales exhibit a statistically significant and economically substantive
positive association with apple farmers' income. This effect exhibits pronounced heterogeneity that
the magnitude and underlying mechanisms of income gains differ across livelihood types and
geographical contexts, and are mediated and moderated by the composition and spatial reach of
farmers' social-spatial networks, as well as by the socioeconomic attributes of network contacts,
particularly their educational attainment and occupational status. By integrating social network and
spatial network perspectives, this study advances the theoretical understanding of digital rural
development and provides targeted empirical evidence to inform digital rural revitalization and
agricultural income enhancement in China and beyond.

Introduction
Digital technologies are profoundly reshaping the economic-geographic landscape and social-spatial structures of
rural areas against the backdrop of accelerating global digital transformation1-5. The rapid penetration of mobile
internet and digital platforms has afforded rural communities unprecedented opportunities for market access and
information acquisition, yet simultaneously engendered significant spatial differentiation and social stratification

effects6. China's rural digitalization process is particularly noteworthy. Driven by national strategies such as the
“Guidelines for Digital Village Development 2.0” and the “Rural Revitalization Plan (2024-2027)”, China has
constructed the world's largest rural digital infrastructure network, with steadily rising rural internet penetration
and mobile payment coverage. According to the 2024 Statistical Report on China's Internet Development, rural
internet penetration has reached 63.8%, 5G network coverage extends to over 90% of administrative villages, and
a cumulative total of 337,800 village-level comprehensive delivery and logistics service stations have been
established. This technological transformation is fundamentally altering the organizational logic of rural
socioeconomic space. In particular, the deterritorialization of information flows has accelerated the transformation
of traditional place-based social networks, giving rise to hybrid spatial forms that fuse virtual and physical
dimensions7, 8. In this process, mobile social platforms, as critical technological intermediaries, have become core
vehicles shaping the everyday economic activities and livelihood strategies of rural residents. However, how
exactly these platforms differentially affect livelihood outcomes by reconfiguring farmers' social-spatial networks
remains inadequately theorized and empirically tested in the existing literature.
WeChat, as the most widely used mobile social platform in China, has transcended its original function as a mere
communication tool and evolved into a "Digital Hub" connecting government, market, and farming households.
Since its launch in 2011, WeChat has developed into a comprehensive platform integrating instant messaging,
social networking, mobile payment, information sharing, and government services, achieving deep penetration
among approximately 300 million rural users9–11. From a Sociotechnical System perspective, WeChat is not merely
a technological artifact but rather an Institutional Intermediary embedded within social relations. Its platform
architecture including WeChat groups, Moments, and Mini Programs incorporates specific social logics and
information distribution mechanisms that profoundly shape users' interaction patterns and economic behaviors12, 13.
In the rural context, WeChat is embedded in farmers' daily lives through functionalities such as text, voice, and
video communication, as well as photo and location data sharing, facilitating information acquisition, social
interaction, and agricultural product transactions while substantially reducing the costs of cross-regional
communication and exchange9–11, 13. With the continuous improvement of village-level logistics service networks
and rural e-commerce systems, WeChat has become the primary digital tool through which farmers access
information, maintain social relationships, and even conduct direct sales of agricultural products.
The reconstructive effect of WeChat on farmers' social-spatial networks is manifested in a fundamental shift from
a Village-Locked pattern to cross-regional connectivity. Traditional agricultural production and marketing models
have long been constrained by geographic space and strong-tie social networks rooted in acquaintance-based
relationships, rendering farmers' livelihood activities characterized by pronounced spatial dependence14. However,
with increased rural resident mobility and increasingly frequent urban–rural social interactions14–16, farmers' social
networks have extended far beyond traditional village boundaries. Through its Private-Domain Traffic mechanism,
the WeChat platform enables farmers to establish direct communication channels with customers within their
continuously expanding social networks13. These weak-tie connections, which traverse geographic boundaries and
social strata, often provide non-redundant market information and sales opportunities more effectively than closed
strong-tie networks. Specifically, through WeChat groups and Moments, farmers transcend the confines of
traditional kinship and locality-based networks, extending the scope of their social interactions from close relatives
to distant kin, friends, and even strangers13, 17. Agricultural product sales increasingly rely on these weak ties,
facilitating a direct Producer–Consumer connection model that bypasses the hierarchical constraints of traditional
wholesale channels, effectively achieving a "Production–Consumption Geographic Compression." This transaction
model, grounded in Relational Embeddedness, differs fundamentally from purely marketized e-commerce platform
transactions; its trust foundation is built upon social network relations, thereby possessing a distinctive operational
logic and differentiation effects18.
Surrounding the nexus of digital platforms and rural development, the "Digital Turn" in human geography has
catalyzed multidisciplinary scholarly attention14. Existing research has examined the impacts of digital platforms
on rural economies and societies from multiple dimensions, including everyday interactions between rural
practices and digital platforms14, the contributions of digital platforms to rural knowledge economy spaces and
resilience19, and how WeChat transforms villagers' economic roles, connectivity patterns, and social networks20.
Regarding the relationship between WeChat and farmer income, prior studies have investigated the effects of
mobile social platforms on farmer income21–24. Nevertheless, the extant literature exhibits three critical gaps. First,
in the Network Mechanism dimension, the spatial diffusion mechanisms of social network effects induced by

WeChat among farmers remain unclear, and the heterogeneity in individual farmers' cross-regional network
construction has been overlooked. How farmers of different livelihood types differentially leverage WeChat to
construct sales networks, and what systematic differences characterize their network structures (e.g., density,
centrality, spatial extent), lack empirical delineation. Second, in the Spatial Analysis dimension, the linkage
between the topological structure of social networks and their geographic spatial distribution has yet to be
integrated. Most studies focus either on social network structure or on spatial distance, but few combine social
network analysis with spatial network analysis to reveal the coupling mechanisms of digitally mediated social-
spatial networks. Third, in the livelihood outcome dimension, existing studies predominantly verify statistical
correlations between technology adoption and income (e.g., how livestreaming increases income25) but lack causal
analysis of how social-spatial network reconfiguration differentially affects the incomes of farmers with distinct
livelihood types. In particular, whether mobile social platform use universally increases farmer income or, owing
to the uneven distribution of network capital, exacerbates income polarization across different groups remains a
key question on which no consensus has been reached.
To address the aforementioned research gaps, this study aims to construct an integrative analytical framework of
"WeChat mediation–network reconfiguration–livelihood outcomes," systematically examining how the WeChat
platform differentially affects incomes by reconfiguring the social-spatial networks of apple growers. Taking Li
County in Gansu Province, China—a major apple-producing region in northwestern China—as the case study site,
this research employs an integrated methodological approach encompassing spatial network analysis, social
network analysis, and Two-Stage Least Squares (2SLS) estimation to sequentially address the following research
questions: (1) What structural differences exist between WeChat-based apple sales and traditional sales models?
(2) What heterogeneous characteristics do the WeChat-based social-spatial networks of apple growers exhibit
across different livelihood types and geographic locations? (3) What is the association of WeChat-based sales on
farmer income, and does this effect vary significantly according to livelihood type and network characteristics?
The theoretical contribution of this study extends beyond an empirical account of mobile social platform adoption
in three interrelated ways. First, whereas prior research has generally treated rural network structure as a relatively
stable, geographically bounded property of village society, this study theorizes and empirically demonstrates that
mobile social platforms operate as an active reconfiguring mechanism, superimposing an extended layer of weak
ties onto a pre-existing core of geographically bounded strong ties. We conceptualize this reconfiguration along
three dimensions: tie composition, spatial extension, and structural density and centrality. Second, this study
clarifies, and empirically tests rather than assumes, the pathway through which such reconfiguration matters for
livelihoods. By loosening the spatial and relational constraints that traditionally bounded agricultural product
circulation, network reconfiguration reduces information asymmetry and transaction costs in matching production
with consumption, thereby expanding farmers' effective market access and strengthening their bargaining position
in sales negotiations. Third, the study extends the Sustainable Livelihoods Framework by introducing digital social
capital as a distinct capital form mobilized through mobile social platform-mediated ties, one that interacts with,
rather than substitutes for, farmers' existing endowments of human, financial, and physical capital, thereby helping
to explain the heterogeneous livelihood outcomes observed across farming households with differing initial
network endowments.
In relation to the existing literature, this study is positioned at the intersection of four research traditions. Relative
to the rural social network literature, which has generally emphasized the stability and closure of kinship-based
and locality-based ties, this study foregrounds the plasticity of such ties under digital mediation. Relative to the
digital platform literature, which has predominantly examined adoption, access, and average income effects, this
study redirects analytical attention from whether farmers use mobile social platforms to how mobile social
platform use reorganizes the underlying network structures through which economic and informational resources
flow. Relative to the literature on spatial restructuring in economic geography, including work on the space of
flows, this study operationalizes deterritorialization at the micro-scale of individual farmers' networks through an
integrated approach combining spatial analysis and network analysis. Relative to the sustainable livelihoods
literature, this study treats network topology as an explicit mediating mechanism linking capital endowments to
livelihood outcomes, responding to render more theoretically explicit the pathway connecting asset ownership to
livelihood strategy. Beyond these theoretical contributions, the study provides first-hand evidence from a
northwestern agricultural region for understanding the differentiated impacts of digital platforms on rural
livelihoods in China, and offers an empirical basis for the precision targeting of digital rural revitalization policies.

Conceptual analysis framework
Rural territorial systems are undergoing profound transformations across both social and economic dimensions in
the context of progressive rural digitalization26, 27. Digital connectivity has reconfigured social relations, giving rise
to novel forms of rural social-spatial organization28. The integration of digital technologies into everyday life has
fundamentally altered livelihood patterns, as exemplified by the proliferation of online social networks and e-
commerce activities29. Concurrently, rural economies are experiencing systemic digital transformation through
enhanced network connectivity and information exchange mechanisms that facilitate prosumer convergence,
whereby the emergence of e-commerce platforms, logistics systems, and digital marketplaces has driven the digital
upgrading of agricultural production and distribution30, 31.
Building on this backdrop, this study defines the four core concepts used throughout the analysis as follows. Rural
social-spatial network refers to the totality of social ties maintained by rural households including kinship,
friendship, and market-based relations together with their geographical distribution, spatial reach, and territorial
embeddedness. It is 'social' in its relational content and 'spatial' in its geographical configuration. Mobile social
platform denotes a smartphone-based digital infrastructure (exemplified by WeChat) that integrates instant
messaging, social networking, mobile payment, and information-sharing functions, thereby enabling users to
initiate, maintain, and commercialize social relations irrespective of physical distance. Network reconfiguration is
defined in this study as a multidimensional transformation encompassing three analytically distinct but empirically
interwoven dimensions: (1) tie composition, namely the relative share of strong ties (kin, close friends, fellow
villagers) and weak ties (distant relatives, former migrant-work acquaintances, mobile social platform-connected
buyers and sellers) within a farmer's ego-network; (2) spatial extension, namely the geographic range (intra-village,
township, county, or cross-provincial) across which a farmer's active ties are distributed; and (3) structural density
and centrality, namely the connectivity and brokerage position of a farmer within the broader sales-related network.
Farmer livelihoods, following the Sustainable Livelihoods Framework, refers to the capabilities, assets, and
activities through which farmers make a living. In this study, livelihood outcomes are operationalized primarily
through apple sales income, while livelihood heterogeneity is captured.
The conceptual framework of "WeChat mediation–network reconfiguration–livelihood outcomes" proposed in this
study (Figure 1) integrates the aforementioned transformative dimensions and centers on three core mechanistic
linkages. First, the mobile social platform mediation linkage: WeChat, functioning as a sociotechnical system,
provides farmers with infrastructure for social interaction and economic transactions that transcend geographical
constraints through its platform architecture, encompassing functional modules such as WeChat Groups, Moments,
Instant Messaging, and Mobile Payment. Second, the social-spatial network reconfiguration linkage: apple growers,
particularly core farmers who serve as central actors within social networks, leverage WeChat to connect strong
ties (Family Members) and peripheral actors (Relatives and Friends), and further extend to weak-tie connections
(Distant Friends and Strangers), thereby establishing virtual sales networks that span consumers across diverse
provinces and municipalities. This process can be theorized as the digital accumulation and spatial expansion of
farmers' "Network Capital". Farmers engaged in different livelihood types exhibit marked heterogeneity in their
network construction capacities owing to variations in initial social capital endowments, digital literacy, and
mobility experiences, and such heterogeneity constitutes the core differentiation in their "Digital Livelihood
Capital". Third, the livelihood outcome differentiation linkage: the formation of these networks enables direct
producer-to-consumer sales, thereby bypassing traditional intermediaries. On the one hand, farmers secure higher
unit profit margins and broader market coverage; on the other hand, consumers obtain fresher products at lower
costs, engendering a virtuous cycle within the digital ecosystem. Nevertheless, the efficacy of this emergent sales
model varies according to the scale, structure, and spatial reach of farmers' social networks—farmers possessing
more extensive and diversified digital social networks tend to achieve more pronounced income gains, whereas
those with limited network resources may face the risk of marginalization.

Materials and methods
Study area
Li County (33°35′−34°31′N, 104°37′−105°36′E) is situated in the northern Longnan mountainous region,
representing a critical transition zone among the Qinba Mountains, Loess Plateau, and Qinghai-Tibet Plateau. This
unique geographic location results in a complex topography characterized by high mountains, deep valleys, and
steep slopes, with the terrain sloping from northwest to southeast (Figure 2). This fragile ecological environment
renders the region highly susceptible to natural hazards, including mountain torrents, landslides, and debris flows,
leading to the designation of over 60% of the land area as national-level prohibited or restricted development
zones32. Despite these challenges, Li County has made significant strides in poverty alleviation. Once classified as
a national-level poverty-stricken county, Li County successfully lifted over 1.3 million residents out of poverty by
the end of 2020 through a multi-pronged approach. This approach included targeted poverty alleviation measures
such as industrial development, e-commerce expansion, financial support programs, tourism promotion, and social
assistance strategies. These efforts resulted in the removal of the poverty designation from all nine poverty-
stricken counties (districts) within Li County.

Data sources
This study utilized a structured questionnaire survey to collect primary data. A multi-stage random sampling method
was employed for data acquisition. The first phase involved a pilot survey conducted in April 2023 across key apple-
growing townships in Li County. During this stage, apple farmers were randomly selected to participate in the
questionnaire survey. The pilot served to identify and refine potential ambiguities or issues within the questionnaire
design and administration process.
The subsequent formal survey phase was conducted in 14 administrative villages within 7 major apple-producing
townships in Li County, namely, Yanguan, Kuanchuan, Yongping, Qishan, Shiqiao, Yongxing, and Mahe. These
sites were selected through a three-stage procedure: townships were first selected based on apple-planting
concentration, villages were then randomly drawn within the selected townships, and apple farmers were finally
randomly selected within each village. The target sample size (𝑛) for the formal survey was determined using
Yamane's formula (Equation 1) with a 6% margin of error (e)33,34. The population size (N) for this calculation was
32,078 apple farmers within the selected townships and villages, representing the sampling frame. Based on this
calculation, the target sample size was approximately 275 apple farmers. To ensure sufficient responses and mitigate
the impact of incomplete questionnaires, a total of 300 apple farmers were surveyed through face-to-face interviews.
After excluding questionnaires with missing or inconsistent information, 286 valid questionnaires were obtained,
yielding an effective response rate of 95.33%. Each face-to-face interview lasted approximately 20-30 minutes and
was conducted with the apple farmers head or the family member principally responsible for apple sales, ensuring
that respondents possessed direct knowledge of the apple farmer's marketing practices.

employed by Ye35, households were categorized into four distinct livelihood types based on the proportion of

Comparative analysis
An independent samples t-test was employed to compare key socio-economic, production, and income variables
between two groups of apple farmers: those who adopted WeChat as a marketing platform and those who did not36.
This analysis assessed significant differences in variables such as family size, apple cultivation area, total household
income, apple yield, and apple-specific income between the two groups.
Social-spatial network analysis
This study innovatively combines social and spatial network analysis to investigate the spatial distribution and
intensity of social connections among apple farmers' friends and relatives and their impact on apple sales35. This
integrated approach addresses the limitations of traditional analyses, which social network analysis often overlooks
geographical factors, while spatial network analysis pays insufficient attention to the intensity of social relationships.
By explicitly incorporating the spatial dimension of social interactions, we can more comprehensively understand
how geographical distance, accessibility, and spatial clustering influence the flow of information, resources, and
support, thereby affecting agricultural product sales. This methodology aligns with recent calls for integrating spatial

(1) Social network analysis
Social Network Analysis (SNA) serves as a quantitative analytical tool for revealing interpersonal connection
patterns and their spillover effects. As an interdisciplinary approach integrating graph theory and algebraic models,
SNA enables systematic quantification of linkage paths between nodes and identification of key actors within
networks39. This study employs SNA methodology to investigate the structural characteristics and tie strength of
apple farmers' social networks with their relatives and friends, with particular emphasis on examining how these
social relationship networks influence apple marketing behaviors.
Specifically, this research adopts three critical metrics to characterize farmers' social network features: Network
Hierarchy (H), Degree Centrality (DC), and Closeness Centrality (CC) 40. It should be emphasized that the network
analyzed here is a spatially embedded two-mode (farmer–city) network rather than a conventional one-mode
interpersonal network: one node set comprises apple farmers, and the other comprises the cities in which farmers'
assisting relatives and friends reside. Following the spatial network literature that integrates social network analysis
with geographical space41,42, actor-level centrality measures are reinterpreted at the place level when ties are
geographically anchored. Network Hierarchy reflects the accessibility level of nodes, closely associated with the
administrative ranks of cities where farmers' relatives and friends reside. Degree Centrality measures the number of
direct connections between farmers and their social ties, while Closeness Centrality evaluates the total path distances
within farmers' social networks. This operationalization measures the geographical intensity and breadth of farmers'
social relations, reflecting the extent to which WeChat-mediated social ties span across different urban destinations.
This is precisely the core construct required to examine how mobile social platforms reconfigure farmers' socio-
spatial networks. Therefore, the administrative level of cities, the number of cities, and the distance between apple
farmers and the cities serve as location-level proxy variables for apple farmers' social network. These metrics are

(2) Spatial network analysis
Spatial network analysis has emerged as a powerful analytical framework for understanding the geographical
distribution and characteristics of social ties, particularly in the context of farmer kinship networks43,44. This study
employs geospatial techniques to examine the spatial configuration and topology of these networks. Through
systematic geocoding of apple farmers' locations and their social connections, we construct a comprehensive spatial
network representation that captures the geographic embeddedness of these relationships.
We implement ArcGIS-based network analysis methods to investigate the spatial connectivity patterns and
relationship intensities between apple farmers in Li County and their social contacts who facilitate apple sales
through WeChat platform. The spatial connection trajectories are fundamentally determined by the geographical
proximity and relative positioning between farmers' residential townships and their contacts' urban locations.
Connection strength is quantified based on the frequency of social ties in specific urban centers, visualized through
weighted lines where higher frequencies correspond to greater line weights.
To account for urban hierarchical effects, we adopted China's standardized city classification system, categorizing
urban centers into five distinct tiers based on permanent urban population: small cities (less than 500,000), medium
cities (500,000 to 1 million), large cities (1 million to 5 million), megacities (5 million to 10 million), and super-
cities (over 10 million). These urban hierarchies are represented through graduated symbols, where symbol size

The two-stage least squares analysis
According to existing literature, multiple factors influence farmers' adoption of WeChat-based sales. In addition to
the family head's education level and social networks examined in the baseline regression analysis in this study,
other factors include village internet penetration rate, logistics system, market distance, and rural mobile phone
coverage. Driven by the rural revitalization strategy and the development of digital rural infrastructure, the internet
and logistics infrastructure in the main apple-producing towns of Li County have been progressively improved.
Additionally, the traditional offline sales model primarily relies on collective purchases by wholesalers. Thus, the
aforementioned factors exert a limited impact on the WeChat-based sales in Li County. Therefore, after controlling
for individual characteristics and family attributes, rural mobile phone coverage, as an exogenous variable, has a
significant impact on farmers' WeChat-based sales behavior.
The validity of "village mobile phone usage rate" as an instrument rests on both relevance and the exclusion
restriction. Relevance is straightforward: higher per-capita mobile phone coverage lowers farmers' access costs to
WeChat and is strongly correlated with WeChat-based apple sales (first-stage coefficient significant at the 1% level;
weak-instrument F = 16.38 > 10). The exclusion restriction warrants closer scrutiny, because village-level phone
coverage could in principle proxy for village economic development, infrastructure, or education. Three
considerations mitigate this concern. First, mechanistically, offline apple marketing in Li County is dominated by
wholesalers' collective farm-gate procurement at prices determined in regional wholesale markets; village phone
coverage cannot alter offline prices or quantities, so its income-relevant channel plausibly operates only through
enabling WeChat-based direct sales. Second, our specifications explicitly control for family-head education,
relatives'/friends' education, livelihood type, the township in which the farmer is located, and the cities where
assisting relatives/friends reside, thereby absorbing village- and region-level heterogeneity in development,
infrastructure, and human capital; moreover, the instrument is a demographically normalized penetration rate rather
than a measure of local economic scale. Third, statistically, the identification test (p = 0.21) fails to reject instrument
validity, and a falsification test shows that among apple farmers not selling via WeChat, village phone coverage
exhibits no significant association with household income (p > 0.10), which is inconsistent with a direct, non-
WeChat income channel.

squares (2SLS) method.The empirical analysis results based on the two-stage least squares (2SLS) method (Table 5)

Mechanism analysis
In summary, the network, comparative, and regression evidence presented above point to two specific pathways
through which the network reconfiguration described in the conceptual framework translates into income gains for
apple farmers. Weak tie expansion constitutes one such pathway, corresponding to the spatial extension dimension
of network reconfiguration defined above and operating through the information flow mechanism identified in our
conceptual framework. By extending farmers' informational reach beyond the closed, redundant ties of village based
networks toward non-redundant contacts in distant urban markets, this pathway supplies timely price signals and
demand information that are largely unavailable through local wholesale channels. The spatial network analysis
provides direct evidence for it: surveyed farmers maintain commercial ties across 26 cities, 84.6% of which lie
outside Gansu Province, with particularly dense linkages to first tier metropolises including Beijing, Tianjin,
Shanghai, and Xi'an, among others. The mobilization of trust embedded social capital operates as an additional
channel, corresponding to the tie composition dimension of network reconfiguration and to the trust and resource
mobilization mechanisms identified in our conceptual framework. Transactions conducted through WeChat draw on
existing kinship and friendship ties, in which repeat purchases and onward referrals within an already trusted
network can lower the costs a buyer would otherwise incur in verifying product quality from an unfamiliar seller.
The regression evidence supports this interpretation: both the scale of relatives' and friends' support (coefficient
44.47, p<0.05) and the geographic reach of relatives and friends who assist with WeChat based sales (coefficient
60.18, p<0.05) are significant positive predictors of income, indicating that mobilizable social capital is a material
conduit through which the platform's income effect is realized.
Discussion
This paper discovers that the WeChat platform has transformed the social network landscape of apple farmers'
kinship relationships, reshaping the formation of a novel apple sales model through a dispersed and decentralized
spatial role. Moreover, it has significantly broadened the market sales space and disrupted the traditional closed and
overly dependent sales methods on buyers46,47. As a result, it has effectively boosted the income of apple farmers,
reaffirming the capacity of mobile social platforms to enhance farmers' earnings and facilitate their livelihood
transformation. These findings offer fresh insights for the rejuvenation and advancement of villages situated in
underdeveloped and impoverished areas that possess high-quality agricultural products. Such implications hold
immense practical and strategic value for the establishment of national and regional digital villages, the eradication
of the digital divide in rural communities, the promotion of rural revitalization and high-quality development, and
the facilitation of urban-rural integration.
Impacts on the livelihood
Income growth is the most directly and robustly evidenced dimension, supported by the comparative analysis, the
baseline regression, and the two-stage least squares estimation. Social capital is evidenced both as an outcome and
as a mechanism contributing to income gains, as discussed in the Mechanism analysis above. Farmers' production
decisions are indirectly illuminated by our finding that WeChat-adopting apple farmers achieve higher income from
smaller cultivation areas and lower total output than non-adopters, suggesting a value-intensive rather than a scale-
intensive production strategy, though because we did not directly survey apple farmers' stated production rationale,
we present this as a suggestive rather than confirmed pattern. Digital capability operates in our analysis primarily as
a moderating factor, evidenced by the significant negative association between household-head age and WeChat-
sales income and the significant positive association with education. By contrast, our data do not provide direct
measures of market stability, risk resistance, employment opportunities, or broader household welfare beyond
income, and we accordingly do not claim findings on these dimensions, identifying them instead, together with
panel-data methods capable of addressing them, as directions for future research.
Negative and unequal effects of mobile social platform-mediated livelihood transformation. Beyond the
predominantly positive income effects reported above, our evidence points to three asymmetries and risks. First, the
significant negative association between household-head age and WeChat-sales income (coefficient -88.12, p<0.01),
together with the positive association with education, provides case-based evidence of an age and education-related
digital divide, in which older and less formally educated farmers systematically realize smaller income gains from
mobile social platform-mediated sales than their younger, better-educated counterparts, even where both groups
have adopted the mobile social platform. Second, our social network analysis reveals substantial inter-township
inequality in network centrality, for example Qishan Township exhibiting markedly lower degree centrality and
network hierarchy than Yanguan Township, indicating that farmers in some townships occupy structurally
peripheral positions with fewer and less efficiently arranged ties. Third, our finding that farmers across nearly all

Generalizability beyond Li County
The age-related, network-related, and market-related asymmetries discussed above are documented within a single
case, which invites the question of how far these findings, and the broader argument of this paper, can be expected
to travel beyond Li County. The Li County case indicates that mobile social platform mediation produces a hybrid
network topology, in which transregional weak ties are layered upon, rather than substituted for, locally embedded
strong ties, consistent with the hybrid spatial forms noted at the outset of this paper. The inter-township
heterogeneity in network centrality reinforces this, since digital mediation appears to reconfigure, rather than
dissolve, the influence of geography and prior social endowment, producing what might be described as a national
network geometry rather than a uniform deterritorialization, a pattern plausibly shared by other smallholder regions
in China undergoing comparable digital transitions. Besides, the finding that income gains flow through private-
domain, trust-based channels rather than through centralized marketplace transactions carries a specific implication.
Governance tools designed for centralized e-commerce platforms, such as seller ratings and platform escrow, do not
extend straightforwardly to social commerce of this kind, since transactions here are mediated by pre-existing
relationships rather than by platform-adjudicated reputation systems. Formalizing trust without displacing its
relational foundation instead requires institutional innovations such as geographical indication certification and
standardized direct sales protocols; such county-level branding and standardization measures, this case suggests,
may be more appropriate instruments for social commerce settings than marketplace-style governance transplanted
from centralized platforms.
Limitations
Nevertheless, several limitations were encountered during the research process. First, the study area is characterized
by regional specificity in terms of its geographical environment, economic foundation, and level of digitalization;
therefore, the direct generalization of these findings to other regions or different types of agricultural product
production and sales necessitates further empirical validation, a common challenge in this field48,49. Second, this
research primarily relies on cross-sectional survey data, which constrains its ability to capture the dynamic changes
in farmers' WeChat usage behavior, social network evolution, and livelihood status, thereby limiting the analytical
depth concerning long-term interactive relationships between variables. Third, the depiction of the structural features
and functional mechanisms of farmers' social networks remains relatively coarse; the study has not fully elucidated
how network connection types, tie strength, and interaction content specifically mediate the impact of mobile social
platforms on sales. Furthermore, the research scope was predominantly focused on WeChat, leading to an
insufficient comparative analysis of other digital platforms, such as live-streaming e-commerce and comprehensive
e-commerce platforms, and the synergistic or competitive effects of farmers' multi-channel sales strategies.
Building upon these limitations, future research could be deepened and expanded in several directions. This includes
extending the geographical scope to regions with diverse geographical typologies and economic development levels,
and encompassing a wider array of agricultural products to enhance the external validity of the research conclusions.
Adopting longitudinal tracking surveys or panel data analysis would enable the dynamic monitoring of changes in
farmers' behaviors and livelihoods. Concurrently, investing resources to acquire more fine-grained social network
data and applying sophisticated social network analysis techniques could reveal how network structures and
relational attributes specifically mediate the influence of digital platforms on farmers' market participation and
livelihoods. Additionally, future work should systematically compare the differential roles of various digital
platforms in agricultural product sales and investigate farmers' multi-platform engagement patterns, along with their
synergistic or competitive outcomes. Such endeavors are expected to yield a more comprehensive and in-depth
understanding of the complex interplay between digital technologies, social networks, and rural livelihoods, thereby
providing a more robust scientific foundation for advancing agricultural digital transformation and sustainable rural
development.
Conclusions
This study develops and applies an integrated "WeChat mediation–network reconfiguration–livelihood outcomes"
analytical framework to systematically assess how WeChat-based e-commerce reconfigures the social-spatial
networks of apple farmers in Li County, Gansu Province, China, and how such reconfiguration differentially affects
their income. Employing a mixed-methods approach that combines spatial network analysis, social network analysis,
baseline regression, and two-stage least squares (2SLS) estimation. The findings key conclusions as follows:

The social network analysis reveals significant structural differentiation in farmers' relational networks across both
livelihood types and geographical locations. Mixed-livelihood farmers exhibit the highest degree centrality (0.62)
and network hierarchy (0.40), indicating more centralized and stratified network configurations, whereas agricultural
livelihood farmers demonstrate stronger closeness centrality (0.66), reflecting tighter relational proximity and
potentially more efficient information transmission within their networks. Geographically, townships including
Qishan, Shiqiao, and Kuanchuan display markedly elevated closeness centrality values (0.98, 0.94, and 0.90,
respectively), suggesting denser social ties and stronger intra-network connectivity that confer localized advantages
in collective information sharing and market coordination.
The spatial network analysis reveals substantial urban-rural connectivity mediated by WeChat, with surveyed
farmers maintaining commercial ties across 26 cities, of which 84.6% (22 cities) are located outside Gansu Province,
predominantly in metropolitan areas of eastern China. This finding demonstrates that WeChat effectively enables
farmers to transcend traditional geographical constraints, facilitating a fundamental shift from village-locked
marketing to transregional direct sales. Notably, farmers' livelihood strategies significantly shape the spatial
configuration of their WeChat-based marketing networks: part-time livelihood farmers exhibit the most extensive
spatial reach (16 cities), followed by agricultural livelihood farmers and migrant work livelihood farmers (11 cities
each), while mixed-livelihood farmers maintain the most spatially concentrated networks (6 cities). Nevertheless, all
livelihood types consistently demonstrate the strongest market linkages with Beijing and Longnan, suggesting that
these two cities function as anchoring nodes regardless of individual network heterogeneity.
The empirical analysis demonstrates that WeChat-based apple sales are significantly and positively associated with
farmers' income, with a robust coefficient of 14,888.74 (p < 0.01) in the baseline model; this association remains
significant under 2SLS estimation (coefficient = 0.35, p < 0.01), with diagnostic tests supporting model validity
(weak-IV test = 16.38; identification test p = 0.21).The analysis of apple farmers' perceptions reveals a
predominantly moderate positive impact of WeChat-based apple sales on family income across different townships
and livelihood types. Over 50% of apple farmers in Yanguan and Shiqiao Townships reported a moderate impact,
while a significant proportion in Qishan (31%) and Yongping (22%) Townships experienced a severe impact. 48%
of agricultural livelihood farmers reported a moderate impact, whereas approximately 15% of migrant work and
mixed-livelihood farmers benefiting from broader social networks, reported a more severe influence.
Policy recommendation
Building upon the empirical findings of this study, the following policy recommendations are advanced. First, at the
infrastructural level, given that Li County is situated in the topographically complex Western Qinling mountain
region where digital infrastructure provision and logistics accessibility vary markedly across townships, increased
investment should be directed toward 5G network coverage gap-filling and logistics node extension into remote
mountainous townships, thereby narrowing the digital access inequalities attributable to geographical location.
Second, from the perspective of institutional environment construction, quality traceability and consumer trust
assurance mechanisms for WeChat-based agricultural product transactions should be established, with standardized
requirements for product quality information disclosure in social commerce, thereby enhancing transaction trust
levels within weak-tie networks and promoting the sustainable expansion of farmers' social-spatial networks. Third,
policy efforts should focus on deepening their digital marketing capabilities by establishing a county-level public
branding system, such as the "Li County Apple" geographical indication certification—and standardized protocols
for WeChat-based direct sales, thereby facilitating an upgrade from individualized and informal "Moments-based
retailing" to branded and standardized "private-domain e-commerce." Fourth, "Digital Capacity Empowerment
Initiative" should be implemented through township-level agricultural extension stations and village service centers,
delivering targeted WeChat marketing training that specifically enhances their capacity for spatial network
expansion and weak-tie cultivation. Additionally, cooperative organizations or village collective economic entities
could be mobilized to establish "centralized proxy-selling" models, wherein digitally proficient farmers or returning
rural youth serve as "network hubs" to manage WeChat-based sales on behalf of neighboring farmers, thereby
lowering the threshold for individual farmers' entry into digital markets and preventing the digital divide from
translating into an income divide. Fifth, to mitigate the risk of over-dependence on a narrow set of distant buyers
identified in our spatial network analysis, township agricultural cooperatives should be encouraged to actively
cultivate sales relationships with a more diversified set of destination markets, rather than relying predominantly on
the small number of anchor markets currently dominating farmers' WeChat-based networks. Township-level
extension programs should further prioritize outreach to older and less formally educated farmers identified in this
study as realizing systematically smaller income gains, so that the Digital Capacity Empowerment Initiative
described above functions as a targeted instrument for narrowing the age and education-based digital divide.
Beyond Li County, the two structural risks motivating our recommendations, over-concentration in a narrow set of
distant anchor markets and an age- and education-based digital divide in realized income gains, are unlikely to be
