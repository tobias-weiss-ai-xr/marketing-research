This study aims to examine the relationship between green marketing and
consumer behavior, and to analyze the mediating role of environmental awareness
in the context of North  Sumatra, Indonesia. A quantitative explanatory design was
employed using a cross -sectional survey of 220 consumers with experience
purchasing environmentally friendly products. Data were analyzed using Structural
Equation Modeling–Partial Least Squares (SEM -PLS) with SmartPLS 4.0, applying
bootstrapping with 5,000 resamples. The results indicate that green marketing is
positively associated with consumer behavior (β = 0.312, p < 0.001) and
environmental awareness (β = 0.648, p < 0.001). Environmental awareness is also
significantly associated with consumer behavior (β = 0.421, p < 0.001). Mediation
analysis reveals a significant indirect effect (β = 0.273, p < 0.001), indicating partial
mediation. The model demonstrates moderate explanatory power (R² = 0.420 for
environmental awareness; R² = 0.563 for consumer behavior) and satisfactory
predictive relevance (Q² = 0.301; 0.389). This study is based on a cross -sectional
design, which limits causal inference. Additionally, the use of self-reported data may
introduce common method bias, although collinearity diagnostics indicate
acceptable levels. This study contributes to the literature by providing empirical
evidence of a dual-path mechanism linking green marketing to consumer behavior
through environmental awareness in a regional Indonesian context, which remains
underexplored in prior studies.

1. INTRODUCTION
Environmental degradation, climate change, and unsustainable consumption patterns have become
critical global challenges, encouraging both governments and businesses to adopt sustainability -
oriented strategies. One of the most prominent strategic responses is the implementation of green
marketing, which emphasizes environmentally friendly products, sustainable production processes, and
responsible communication. In contemporary markets, green marketing has evolved beyond a
promotional tool into a strategic mechanism that aligns business practices with environmental
sustainability and consumer expectations.
In emerging economies such as Indonesia, rapid economic growth and increasing consumption
levels intensify environmental pressures. North Sumatra, as one of the key economic regions, has
experienced significant expansion in retail, manufacturing, and MSMEs, which contributes to both
economic development and environmental challenges. Consequently, understanding how green
marketing influences consumer behavior in this regional context becomes increasingly important.
Green consumer behavior is commonly explained through the Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB),
which posits that behavior is influenced by attitudes, subjective norms, and perceived behavioral

Previous studies have consistently reported that green marketing positively influences consumer
behavior by enhancing perceived value, trust, and brand image (Sun et al., 2022; Nguyen et al., 2023). In
addition, environmental awareness has been identified as a key determinant of sustainable
consumption, directly influencing purchasing decisions and behavioral intentions (Testa et al., 2021).
More importantly, recent empirical studies highlight the mediating role of environmental
awareness in the relationship between green marketing and consumer behavior. For example, Bashir et
al. (2022) and Qayyum et al. (2022) found that environmental cognition strengthens the effectiveness
of green marketing strategies by translating marketing stimuli into behavioral outcomes. These findings
suggest that consumer awareness acts as an internal mechanism linking external marketing efforts to
actual behavior.
Despite the growing body of literature, several critical gaps remain.
First, empirical inconsistencies persist regarding the effectiveness of environmental awareness in
driving actual consumer behavior. While some studies confirm its mediating role, others report the
persistence of the attitude –behavior gap, where consumers express environmental concern but fail to
translate it into actual purchasing behavior.
Second, most prior studies are concentrated in developed or highly urbanized regions, particularly
in Java, leaving limited empirical evidence from other Indonesian regions such as North Sumatra. This
raises concerns about the generalizability of findings due to differences in socio-cultural, economic, and
environmental contexts.
Third, although mediation models involving environmental awareness are increasingly common,
many studies focus primarily on direct relationships without explicitly testing dual -path mechanisms
(direct and indirect effects simultaneously). As a result, the underlying process through which green
marketing influences consumer behavior remains insufficiently explained.
Finally, prior studies often introduce additional factors such as digital marketing or social media
without integrating them coherently into the conceptual model. This creates conceptual fragmentation
and reduces theoretical clarity.
This study addresses these gaps by offering several key contributions.
First, it provides empirical evidence from a regional context (North Sumatra), thereby extending
the generalizability of green marketing research beyond dominant urban regions.
Second, this study explicitly examines a dual -path mechanism, where green marketing influences
consumer behavior both directly and indirectly through environmental awareness. This approach
advances prior mediation studies by clarifying the simultaneous operation of behavioral and cognitive
pathways.
Third, the study contributes to resolving the attitude behavior gap debate by empirically
demonstrating how environmental awareness functions as a bridging mechanism between marketing
exposure and actual consumer behavior.
Unlike prior studies that loosely incorporate additional constructs (e.g., digital marketing), this
research maintains conceptual coherence by focusing specifically on green marketing and
environmental awareness as the core explanatory variables within a TPB-based framework.
Based on the theoretical and empirical considerations above, this study aims to:
1. Analyze the relationship between green marketing and consumer behavior
2. Examine the relationship between green marketing and environmental awareness
3. Investigate the relationship between environmental awareness and consumer behavior
4. Test the mediating role of environmental awareness in the relationship between green
marketing and consumer behavior
Accordingly, the following hypotheses are proposed:
1. H1: Green marketing is positively associated with consumer behavior
2. H2: Green marketing is positively associated with environmental awareness

2. METHOD
This study employed a quantitative explanatory design to examine the effect of green marketing on
consumer behavior, with environmental awareness as a mediating variable in North Sumatra, Indonesia.
A cross -sectional survey approach was used, and data were analyzed using Structural Equation
Modeling–Partial Least Squares (SEM-PLS) with SmartPLS 4.0, suitable for testing complex models and
mediation effects.
2.1. Research Design
This study employed a quantitative explanatory design using a cross -sectional survey to examine
the relationships between green marketing, environmental awareness, and consumer behavior. The
analytical approach utilized Structural Equation Modeling –Partial Least Squares (SEM -PLS) with
SmartPLS 4.0, which is appropriate for complex models involving mediation and prediction -oriented
research.
2.2. Sample and Data Collection
The target population consisted of consumers in North Sumatra who have experience purchasing
environmentally friendly products. A purposive sampling technique was applied with the following
criteria:
(1) aged 18 years or older,
(2) prior experience with eco-friendly products, and
(3) residence in North Sumatra.
Data were collected through an online survey (Google Forms) distributed via social media platforms
(e.g., WhatsApp, Instagram) over a period of approximately 6 weeks (January –February 2026). A total
of 245 responses were received, of which 220 valid responses were retained after data screening,
resulting in an effective response rate of approximately 89.8%.
Instead of relying on the “10 -times rule,” this study applies statistical power considerations
following Jacob Cohen. Using an anticipated medium effect size (f² = 0.15), significance level (α = 0.05),
and statistical power (0.80), the minimum required sample size for a model with two predictors is
approximately 107 respondents. Therefore, the final sample size of 220 exceeds the recommended
threshold, ensuring adequate statistical power and robustness of estimates.
2.3 Measurement Instruments
Data were collected using a structured questionnaire adapted from validated scales in recent literature
(2021–2025). All items were measured using a five-point Likert scale (1 = strongly disagree to 5 =
strongly agree).
1. Green Marketing (GM)
Adapted from studies such as Yen -Sheng Chen & Chang (2022) and Qayyum et al. (2022),
covering eco-friendly product attributes, green promotion, pricing, and distribution.
Sample item: “This product is promoted as environmentally friendly.”
2. Environmental Awareness (EA)
Adapted from Testa et al. (2021) and Sun et al. (2022), including environmental knowledge,
concern, and responsibility.
Sample item: “I am aware of the environmental impact of the products I purchase.”
3. Consumer Behavior (CB)

2.4. Data Analysis
Data analysis was conducted using SmartPLS 4.0 in two stages:
1. Measurement Model (Outer Model)
a. Convergent validity: outer loading > 0.70, AVE > 0.50
b. Reliability: Cronbach’s alpha & Composite Reliability > 0.70
c. Discriminant validity: HTMT < 0.90 and Fornell–Larcker criterion
d. Collinearity: VIF < 3.3 (to detect common method bias)
2. Structural Model (Inner Model)
a. Path coefficients (β), t-statistics, and p-values (bootstrapping 5,000 resamples)
b. Coefficient of determination (R²)
c. Effect size (f²)
d. Predictive relevance (Q²)
2.4. Mediation Testing
The mediating role of environmental awareness was assessed using bootstrapping of indirect
effects. Mediation was determined based on the significance of indirect paths (p < 0.05) and the
comparison between direct and indirect effects (partial or full mediation).
To strengthen the robustness of the findings, this study complements the full collinearity
assessment with additional common method bias diagnostics.
First, the full collinearity VIF values for all constructs were below the recommended threshold of
3.3, indicating that multicollinearity and common method bias are unlikely to pose serious concerns.
Second, Harman’s single-factor test was conducted as a supplementary diagnostic. The results show that
the first unrotated factor accounts for less than 50% of the total variance, suggesting that no single factor
dominates the data. This indicates that common method bias is not a substantial issue in this study.
Third, several procedural remedies were implemented during data collection to minimize bias,
including:
1. Ensuring respondent anonymity
2. Using clear and concise measurement items
3. Separating predictor and criterion constructs conceptually
4. Avoiding leading or ambiguous questions
Together, these statistical and procedural approaches provide stronger justification that the results
are not significantly affected by common method bias, thereby enhancing the internal validity of the
study.
3. CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS
Results

This study involved 220 respondents from North Sumatra who have experience purchasing
environmentally friendly products. The majority of respondents were aged between 18 –35 years
(approximately 68%), followed by 36 –50 years (22%) and above 50 years (10%). In terms of gender,
55% were female and 45% were male. Most respondents had a university -level education and were
active users of digital platforms, indicating a relatively high exposure to green marketing information.

4. Summary of Findings
The findings of this study provide strong empirical evidence regarding the role of green marketing
and environmental awareness in shaping consumer behavior in North Sumatra. Overall, the results
indicate that Green Marketing exerts a significant influence on Consumer Behavior, both directly and
indirectly through Environmental Awareness. This suggests that environmentally oriented marketing
strategies are not only capable of encouraging immediate purchasing decisions but also of shaping
consumer behavior through psychological mechanisms.
Furthermore, the results reveal that Green Marketing plays a substantial role in enhancing
Environmental Awareness. This finding highlights that marketing activities extend beyond promotional
functions and act as an important educational tool that increases consumers’ understanding of
environmental issues. As consumers become more exposed to green marketing messages, their
awareness and concern for environmental sustainability tend to increase.
In addition, the study confirms that Environmental Awareness has a significant effect on Consumer
Behavior. Consumers with higher levels of environmental awareness are more likely to engage in
environmentally responsible purchasing behavior, including choosing eco -friendly products and
supporting sustainable brands. This underscores the importance of cognitive and attitudinal factors in
influencing consumption decisions.
Importantly, the analysis also demonstrates that Environmental Awareness partially mediates the
relationship between Green Marketing and Consumer Behavior. This indicates that while green
marketing directly influences consumer behavior, its impact becomes stronger when it successfully
enhances consumers’ environmental awareness. The presence of partial mediation suggests that both
direct and indirect pathways are relevant in explaining consumer behavior.
Taken together, these findings confirm that the integration of environmental values into marketing
strategies is essential for promoting sustainable consumer behavior in North Sumatra. Businesses that
effectively combine green marketing initiatives with efforts to increase environmental awareness are
more likely to influence consumer decisions and contribute to long-term environmental sustainability.
Discussion

as an extension of mediation -based models within the Theory of Planned Behavior framework. While
prior studies primarily conceptualize environmental awareness as a single mediating variable, this
research demonstrates that green marketing operates simultaneously through direct behavioral

mediation but refines existing models by highlighting the coexistence of parallel influence pathways.
From a theoretical standpoint, the relatively strong indirect effect (β = 0.273) compared to the direct
effect (β = 0.312) suggests that green marketing is more effective when it activates internal cognitive
mechanisms rather than relying solely on external persuasion. This finding extends prior mediation
studies by empirically supporting the argument that cognition-driven pathways are dominant in shaping
sustainable consumption, particularly in emerging market contexts.
However, this study also engages critically with contradictory findings in the literature. Several
studies have reported the persistence of the attitude–behavior gap , where high environmental
awareness does not always translate into actual purchasing behavior. The present findings partially
challenge this perspective by showing significant mediation, yet they do not fully eliminate this paradox.
A plausible explanation lies in economic constraints —consumers in North Sumatra may possess
awareness but still face affordability issues when purchasing green products.
In addition, trust -related concerns and greenwashing practices may weaken the translation of
awareness into behavior. When consumers perceive green claims as exaggerated or misleading, the
effectiveness of green marketing may decline despite high awareness levels. This aligns with signaling
theory, suggesting that credibility moderates the effectiveness of environmental communication, an
aspect not explicitly modeled in this study.
Another alternative explanation relates to habitual consumption patterns. Even when awareness is
high, entrenched purchasing habits and limited product availability may reduce behavioral change. This
indicates that awareness alone is necessary but not sufficient, reinforcing the importance of structural
and market-level conditions.
Importantly, the findings must also be interpreted within the context of emerging markets such as
Indonesia. Compared to developed economies, environmental awareness in regions like North Sumatra
is often heterogeneous and unevenly distributed, influenced by education level, digital exposure, and
socio-economic status. Therefore, the strong path from green marketing to environmental awareness (β
= 0.648) suggests that marketing plays a quasi-educational role, which may be more critical in emerging
contexts than in mature markets.
From an analytical perspective, the effect size interpretation (f²) further strengthens the argument.
The large effect of green marketing on environmental awareness (f² ≈ 0.30) indicates that marketing is
a primary driver of cognition, while the moderate effect of awareness on behavior (f² ≈ 0.15 –0.25)
confirms that behavioral change is more complex and influenced by additional factors. Meanwhile, the
smaller direct effect (f² ≈ 0.08–0.15) suggests that purely promotional strategies are insufficient without
cognitive reinforcement.
Conclusion
This study demonstrates that green marketing and environmental awareness are key determinants
of consumer behavior, with environmental awareness acting as a partial mediator that strengthens the
relationship. The findings highlight that sustainable consumer behavior is driven by a dual mechanism,
where external marketing stimuli interact with internal cognitive processes. From a theoretical
perspective, this study advances the literature by clarifying the dual-path influence of green marketing
and providing empirical evidence that contributes to resolving the attitude behavior gap. From a
practical standpoint, the results emphasize that effective green marketing strategies must go beyond
promotion and actively foster environmental awareness. However, the findings should be interpreted in
light of methodological limitations, particularly the cross -sectional design and regional focus. Future
research is encouraged to expand the model by incorporating contextual variables and adopting
longitudinal approaches to strengthen causal inference.
