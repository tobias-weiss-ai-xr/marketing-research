Abstract
The main objective of this study is to analyze the impact of digital marketing skills possessed by
rural farmers and other agro -enterprenuers as it affects the profitability of their agricultural
ventures. Data for the study were collected and evaluated using respondents in selected rural
communities of Niger State, Nigeria.  The study adopts geospatial analysis of mobile network
penetration alongside qualitative impact assessments which was used to evaluate how digital
market platforms can influence the economic viability of agribusinesses in the study area. This
empirical approach incorporates survey-based data collection to capture the nuances of farmer
participation in digital trading environments. The study also utilizes semi -structured interviews
with agricultural extension officers to assess the efficacy of existing marketing outreach programs
and their alignment with the digital adoption patterns observed among rural households. Findings
indicate that farmers who use digital platforms achieve a significant reduction in operational costs
which is primarily driven by inven tory optimization and efficient resource management. The
systematic and deliberate integration of e -commerce channels also facilitates direct access to
broader consumer bases, which enhances overall sales performance and effectively boosts net
household income. Among others, key recommendations of the study are that, because marketing
competencies are fundamental to the economic viability of agribusinesses which serve as a vital
bridge between traditional farming practices and modern, scalable commercial models; these
tools should be entrenched at the grassroots levels by governments, policy makers and other
stakeholders to enshrine knowledge -based execution for profitable agribusiness operations. In
order to sustain this momentum, policymakers must prioritize investments in rural digital
infrastructure and gender -inclusive extension services, which are critical to mitiga ting the
systemic barriers that currently hinder smallholder competitiveness.

Introduction

viability of agricultural enterprises operating within resource -constrained environments
(Inyommom et al., 2024). In Niger State, the application of nuanced marketing techniques—such
as strategic promotion and market research —is essential for mitigating the financial constraints
that frequently impede the scalability of smallholder agribusinesses (Garima et al.,  2023;

effectively reduce the influence of intermediary profiteering while improving their capacity to
negotiate and fulfill demand at optimal price points (Babalulu et al., 2025). Moreover, the adoption
of data-driven decision-making tools and Internet of Things applications can further streamline
supply chain management, allowing for precise tracking of inventory and real-time responsiveness
to fluctuating market demands (Thompson, 2026). This transition toward technological integration
is particularly pertinent in regions where minimal participation in marketing cooperatives currently
hinders the establishment of robust, industrial-scale networks capable of mitigating price volatility
(Kalleya et al., 2023). Consequently, bridging the existing gap in human capital through targeted,
practical training on quality control and market research is essential for enhancing the
competitiveness of these rural enterprises (Raza et al., 2024). Additionally, establishing platforms
that provide real-time updates on market prices and consumer demand can facilitate more informed
decision-making, thereby reducing the reliance on exploitative middlemen (Ali et al., 2024).
Furthermore, the integration of digital tools and mobile -based information exchange platforms
allows agribusinesses to enhance their social network capital, fostering stronger coordination
across the value chain (Baumüller, 2025). This capacity -building pr ocess not only improves
financial recording and operational efficiency but also enables farmers to identify and reduce
systemic losses that occur during distribution (Sengupta et al., 2024). Notably, the adoption of e-
marketing platforms has proven instrumental in enhancing brand visibility and direct customer
engagement, which collectively foster improved market accessibility for rural entrepreneurs
(Tiwari, 2025), (Tiwari, 2025). Nonetheless, the realization of these benefits remains contingent
upon overcoming prevalent barriers such as digital literacy deficits and inadequate infrastructure
(Tiwari, 2025a, 2025b). Addressing these adoption barriers is imperative, as research indicates that
digital transformation and enhanced market linkages are essential for boosting income and
mitigating the persistent challenges associated with limited infrastructure (Ma et al., 2024; Obasi
& Olowu, 2026). Furthermore, fostering public-private partnerships remains a strategic priority to
establish robust cold chain data platforms that integrate temperature control and product
information, thereby minimizing post -harvest losses an d maximizing product value (Jia & Zhu,
2025). Beyond infrastructure, the systematic implementation of cooperative knowledge -sharing
activities is essential to standardize product quality and navigate the inherent volatility of raw
material supply (Balakrishnan et al., 2024).

Literature Review
Existing scholarship emphasizes that digital innovation within agrarian economies requires
significant investment in infrastructure and entrepreneurial education to be effective (Sandhu  et
al., 2024). Specifically, access to information and specialized resources enables rural entrepreneurs
to better navigate fluctuating market conditions and formulate informed business strategies (Nipo
et al., 2024). Moreover, studies have demonstrated that providing smallholder farmers with access
to real-time commodity pricing and digital marketplaces significantly enhances their bargaining
power and reduces transaction costs, ultimately contributing to higher profit margins (Choruma et
al., 2024; Fausiyat, 2025). Furthermore, the adoption of such digital tools facilitates the bypass of
traditional intermediaries, allowing producers to capture a larger share of the value chain by
connecting directly with consumers and larger-scale buyers (Basu et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2025).
However, the effective deployment of these technologies remains hindered by pronounced gaps in
digital literacy and a pervasive lack of critical support infrastructure within rural communities
(Chen & Long, 2024; Samsudin et al., 2024). Consequently, these systemic constraints underscore
the necessity for policy -driven digital literacy initiatives and the development of reliable
connectivity to ensure that smallholder farmers can fully leverage social commerce and mobile -
based market platforms (Li & Zhang, 2024), (Mishra et al., 2025).
Beyond technological adoption, recent findings underscore that institutional framework —
including cooperative models and incentive -based support —are equally vital for aligning
smallholder productivity with broader sustainable development objectives (Utama et al., 2025). In
this context, the development of digitally enabled collaborative hubs offers a pathway to overcome
spatial isolation and resource constraints by consolidating the market power of individual
producers. Furthermore, research suggests that fostering an environment of integrated strategies—
encompassing product diversification and technology adoption —is crucial for transforming
traditional farming into resilient, value -added business models (Popoola, 2026; Suryaningrum &
Lestari, 2025). Such digital t ransformation, when supported by a government -technology-
smallholder framework, promotes systemic scalability and sustainable rural development by
aligning agricultural output with contemporary market demands (Yang & Sun, 2024). Integrating
these disruptive technologies serves to stimulate rural youth entrepreneurship and catalyze the
broader transformation of local food systems (Thaher et al., 2025). Despite these opportunities,
evidence suggests that many smallholder-focused ICT initiatives have struggled to achieve long -
term commercial impact, often serving as limited tools that merely broadcast basic price data rather
than fostering deeper v alue chain integration (Alfonsi et al., 2024). To address this limitation,
scholarly discourse increasingly advocates for the implementation of comprehensive digital
ecosystems that prioritize user -centric design to ensure sustained uptake and practical relevance
for farmers (Gumbi et al., 2023). Moreover, scholars emphasize that successful digital integration
necessitates addressing systemic challenges such as digital illiteracy, language barriers, and a lack
of tailored training programs to ensure technology effectively supports smallhold ers (Mapiye et
al., 2021). Moreover, the development of robust digital ecosystems is contingent upon bridging
the divide between existing technological capabilities and the socio -economic realities of rural
producers (Smidt & Jokonya, 2021).
Additionally, leveraging mobile-based market information can serve as a catalyst for broadening
access to financial services and off-farm employment, thereby diversifying income streams beyond
traditional agricultural output (Khan et al., 2024). However, achieving this integration requires a
shift away from high-tech, resource-intensive systems toward accessible, mobile -based solutions
that align with the current digital competencies of rural farmers (Abdulai et al.,  2023).

Research Methodology
This study employs a mixed -methods research design, integrating geospatial analysis of mobile
network penetration with qualitative impact assessments to evaluate how digital market platforms
influence the economic viability of agribusinesses in Niger State . This empirical approach
incorporates survey-based data collection to capture the nuances of farmer participation in digital
trading environments. The study also utilizes semi -structured interviews with agricultural
extension officers to assess the efficacy of existing marketing outreach programs and their
alignment with the digital adoption patterns observed among rural households. Furthermore, the
research employ s a double -hurdle model to systematically evaluate the drivers of market
participation and the depth of involvement among smallholder producers (Nwangwu et al., 2024).
This analytical framework enables the quantification of how specific marketing competencies and
digital infrastructure availability correlate with variations in profitability across diverse socio -
economic contexts within the region (Fatmawaty & Bur a, 2025; Thompson, 2026). By
synthesizing these empirical insights, the analysis aims to isolate the specific barriers to entry that
currently limit the efficacy of e -marketplaces for the most vulnerable agricultural populations
(Mishra et al., 2025; Rashid & Valilai, 2025). Ultimately, the study seeks to determine whether
enhanced digital literacy and strategic marketing training can serve as primary catalysts for
overcoming the persistent logistics and high transaction costs that currently sti fle the growth of
rural agribusinesses (Fitriyani & Nasir, 2025; Morepje et al., 2024).
Building on this, the study further examines how the strategic utilization of mobile platforms
affects the bargaining power of farmers, who often face significant information asymmetries
compared to larger market intermediaries (Mabuga et al.,  2025). By leveraging data -driven
decision support tools and e-commerce platforms, smallholders can potentially streamline supply
chain complexities and secure more transparent, equitable pricing for their yields (Kumar, 2025;

constrain rural output, enabling small -scale producers to bypass inefficient gatekeepers
(Chaudhary & Suri, 2022; Panggabean et al., 2023). By eliminating these layers of distribution,
farmers gain the agency to negotiate from a position of better market intelligence, securing more
favorable terms and reducing their dependency on speculative price-setting by middlemen (Alam
et al., 2023). This transition toward digitalized value chains also enhances product traceability,
which fosters consumer trust and opens doors to premium market segments previously inaccessible
to rural producers (Ju & Lou, 2024; Nieto, 2023). Furthermore, the a doption of advanced digital
tools enables farmers to achieve superior production quality and efficiency, facilitating the
transition toward modern, sustainable agribusiness practices (Lu et al.,  2025). Moreover, by
reducing information asymmetry and minimizing losses during product circulation, these digital
frameworks significantly improve the overall economic resilience of rural households (Peng et al.,
2025; Zhou et al.,  2024). In addition to these financial gains, the integration of mobile -based
payment systems and digital wallets provides a secure infrastructure for managing cash flow,
thereby fostering long-term financial inclusion and stability for smallholder enterprises (Sawant et
al., 2025). Beyond these transactional benefits, the deployment of such platforms allows farmers
to leverage data -driven insights to align their output with volatile market demand, thereby
optimizing resource allocation and reducing post-harvest wastage (Hosen, 2023).

reduction in operational expenditure, driven primarily by optimized inventory management and
minimized transit-related losses (Rahman et al., 2024). Moreover, this systematic integration of e-
commerce channels facilitates direct access to broader consumer bases, which enhances overall
sales performance and effectively boosts net household income (Guan et al., 2024; Peng et al.,
2025). This trend is further supported by the capacity of e -commerce to mitigate information

Discussion
The findings derived from this study highlight a transformative paradigm where strategic
marketing competencies act as the primary determinant for profitability within rural Niger State
agribusinesses. By leveraging digital capabilities to expand social net works and enhance
bargaining power, these enterprises effectively mitigate the influence of traditional intermediaries,
thereby securing more favorable market access (Zhang et al., 2025). However, the realization of
these benefits remains contingent upon the deliberate acquisition of entrepreneurial skills and
financial management capacities to navigate market volatility (Kahan & Worth, 2026) .
Furthermore, professional networking serves as a critical conduit for resource sharing and
knowledge exchange, which allows agribusiness owners to form strategic alliances and scale their
operations beyond local constraints (Kumar et al.,  2024). Despite this potential, widespread
adoption in rural contexts is currently impeded by infrastructural deficits, including inconsistent
internet connectivity and the limited digital literacy of producers (Sanusi et al.,  2025).
Consequently, bridging these gaps necessitates comprehensive extension services and institutional
support to ensure that farmers can effectively leverage digital tools for sustainable market
engagement (Ahmad et al., 2024; Syahza et al., 2021). Moreover, the development of robust digital
ecosystems can transform farmers’ sales records into verifiable credit scores, effectively

Conclusion and recommendation
This study demonstrates that marketing competencies are fundamental to the economic viability
of agribusinesses in Niger State, serving as a vital bridge between traditional farming practices and
modern, scalable commercial models . To sustain this momentum, policymakers must prioritize
investments in rural digital infrastructure and gender -inclusive extension services, which are
critical to mitigating the systemic barriers that currently hinder smallholder competitiveness
(Essilfie et al., 2025; Geza et al., 2023). Furthermore, fostering robust institutional support and
collaborative group-based approaches will empower farmers to share resources and knowledge,
