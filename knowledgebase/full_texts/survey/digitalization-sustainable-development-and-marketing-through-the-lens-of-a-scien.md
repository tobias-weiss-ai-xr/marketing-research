Abstract
In the context of the accelerated digitalization of economic processes and growing global challenges related to sustainable d evelopment, enterprises’
marketing activities are increasingly shifting towards integrating commercial efficiency with environmental and social respon sibility. In this context,
scientific research at the intersection of marketing, digital transformation, and sustainable development has evolved rapidly  over the past decade.
However, most studies have focused on individual aspects,  including digital marketing tools, green consumer behavior, the implementation of
environmental, social, and governance (ESG) indicators, or technological drivers of digitalization. Despite the growing acade mic interest in these
issues, the implications of these developments for the structure of the scientific literature have not yet been comprehensive ly examined from a
scientometric perspective. This study analyzes publication activity, thematic structure, and scientific collaboration networks at the inte rsection of
marketing, digitalization, and sustainable development over the period 2015 –2026. Based on a unified dataset comprising 3,657 publications, the
study examines chronological trends, international and institutional collaboration, co -authorship, and citation patterns while maintaining a consistent
dataset across all analytical dimensions.  To achieve the research objective, a bibliometric analysis was conducted for the specified period using
Scopus, a widely recognized scientometric database, and VO Sviewer software. The analysis confirms that the vast majority of scientific publications
in this field were published after 2020. Between 2020 and 2025 alone, the annual number of publications increased from 92 to 1,006 documents, with
publications from this period accounting for more than 80% of the entire dataset analyzed. Although no direct temporal correl ation could be
established between the adoption of specific strategic documents on sustainable development and the acceleration of publication activity, the findings
indicate that the period 2022 –2025 was characterized by the highest growth rates in scientific output compared with previous years.  Furthermore,
keyword density visualization revealed several thematic clusters within the research field connecting marketing, digitalizati on, and sustainable
development: digital marketing and commerce; tourism and small and medium -sized enterprise performance; data analytics and machine learning;
consumer behavior and supply chains; and trust, consumer psycholo gy, and ESG -related issues. The analysis of international and institutional
collaboration networks, together with co -authorship patterns, further confirmed the uneven and clustered nature of scientific collaboration, with
research activity concentrated around a relatively limited number of countries, institutions, and authors.  The findings, based on the most recent
available data, provide an objective foundation for understanding the evolution, trends, and interconnections of academic res earch at the intersection
of marketing, digitalization, and sustainable development. This research area is becoming increasingly important in the conte xt of achieving the
global Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) by 2030.

Introduction
The development of digitalization in economic
processes and the intensification of global sustainability
challenges are transforming marketing activity  into an
instrument that combines commercial efficiency with
environmental and social responsibility. Kwilinski
(2023), analyzing 9,527 Scopus publications for 1990 –
2023, confirms the exponential growth of scholarly
interest in the relationship between digital technologies
and sustainable development, noting an annual
publication growth rate of about 36% and identifying five
evolutionary stages of this field. Andriansyah, Anshori
and Andiyani (2023), based on a bibliometric analysis of
218 Scopus publicatio ns for 2019 –2023, similarly show
that research on digital marketing combined with
technological innovation and sustainability peaks in 2023
(79 documents). In parallel, digitalization of marketing is
increasingly viewed as a direct factor of enterprise
sustainability. Diez -Martin, Blanco -Gonzalez and Prado -
Roman (2019), reviewing the 2009 –2018 literature,
outline six key challenges at the intersection of digital
marketing and sustainability: consumer orientation and
value proposition, digital consumer behav ior, digital
"green" marketing, competitive advantage, supply chains,
and organizational capabilities. Kumar, Sharma, Mishra,
Sharma and Bhardwaj (2025), applying LDA topic
modeling to 2,061 Scopus publications (1991 –2025),
show that digital technologies — IoT, blockchain, AI and
digital platforms — catalyze the transformation of
"green" marketing, forming clusters around sustainable
consumption, digital marketing techniques, purchase
intentions and green supply chains. Deng, Lan, Chen,
Zheng and Zhang (202 5), using structural equation
modeling on data from 86 Chinese new -energy
enterprises (2018 –2022), demonstrate that digital
transformation enhances sustainable marketing
performance both directly and through strengthened
market orientation.

Despite this growing body of work, a holistic
conceptual model of the interaction among marketing,
digital transformation and sustainable development
remains underdeveloped. Existing studies mostly address
one of the three areas in isolation — digital marketing, AI
in ma rketing communications, or sustainable

grounded synthesis covering both leading databases

This study applies the bibliometric approach to
analyze publications on the relationships among

development, assessing the evolution of research
priorities in digital marketing, the adoption of AI in

environmental and social responsi bility into business

Literature Review
The contemporary scholarly discourse on the
relationship between marketing, digitalization and
sustainable development is formed at the intersection of
several research directions: sustainable and green
marketing, digital marketing, digital business
transformation, sustainable consumption, and digital
technologies. Analysis of publications in recent years
indicates a gradual shift from the study of individual
marketing tools toward a comprehensive examination  of
digital technologies as a factor transforming the models
of value creation, communication and consumption.
One of the directions that has formed the
methodological foundation of contemporary research is
the rethinking of the concept of sustainable marketing.
Dyck and Manchanda (2021) examine sustainable
marketing through the lens of virtue ethics, emphasizing
the need to shift marketing orientation from short -term
economic gain toward taking into account broader socio -
ecological consequences of economic  activity. Prothero
and McDonagh (2021), in turn, develop the discussion on
the ethical foundations of sustainable marketing and its
compliance with contemporary social and environmental
challenges. This approach broadens the traditional
interpretation of marketing; however, it has a certain
limitation: normative and ethical provisions do not
always translate into measurable indicators of marketing
performance. Thus, early studies of sustainable marketing
largely concentrate on the conceptual substantiation  of its
principles, while the issue of quantitative evaluation of
results remains insufficiently developed.
In parallel, a research line is being formed in which
digitalization is regarded as a factor ensuring
sustainability. George et al. (2021) link digital
sustainability with entrepreneurship and the use of digital
innovations to respond to climate and social challenges.
Knudsen et al. (2021) investigate the impact of
digitalization on the sustainability of competitive
advantage, demonstrating that digit al technologies can
alter the mechanisms of forming and maintaining
enterprises' competitive positions. Gomez -Trujillo and
Gonzalez-Perez (2022) directly examine digital
transformation as a strategy for achieving sustainability.
The distinction of these approaches lies in the fact that
digitalization is no longer confined to the functional
improvement of business processes but is instead
considered in connection with long -term economic,
environmental and social outcomes.
This direction was systematized by G uandalini
(2022), who, on the basis of a systematic literature
review, investigates the relationship between digital
transformation and sustainable development. The author
shows that digitalization can contribute to the
achievement of sustainable development goals by
increasing resource -use efficiency, fostering innovation,
and transforming business models. At the same time,
digital transformation does not have an unambiguously
positive effect: its results depend on the organizational
context, the nature of  the technologies, and enterprises'
ability to integrate digital solutions into an overall
sustainable development strategy. This conclusion is of
fundamental importance for marketing research, since the
use of digital channels per se does not ensure the
sustainability of marketing activity.
A separate body of literature is devoted to green
marketing. Sharma (2021) systematizes research on
consumer behavior and green marketing, demonstrating a
close relationship among environmental attitudes,
marketing stim uli and consumer intentions. Kaur et al.
(2022) deepen this direction through an analysis of the
relationship among green marketing strategies,
environmental attitude and green purchase intention.
Majeed et al. (2022) focus attention on the mediating
effect of green brand image and consumers'
environmental beliefs. García -Salirrosas and Rondon -
Eusebio (2022) likewise link enterprises' marketing
practices to consumers' behavioral characteristics.
Thus, existing studies provide a broad framework for
explaining green purchase intention, but primarily focus
on end-consumer behavior. The role of digital channels in
modifying the influence mechanisms of green marketing
messages remains insufficiently explored. Alkhatib et al.
(2023), through a systematic review of green marketing
in the digital age, demonstrate the gradual convergence of
green and digital marketing during 2012 –2022. Their
findings indicate a shift from communicating
environmental product characteristics toward the use of
digital channels, social me dia and online consumer
interaction, although the review largely reflects an earlier
stage of digitalization. Therefore, further research is
needed to examine contemporary issues such as AI,
algorithmic personalization, influencer marketing, digital
transparency and platform-based business models.
A more systematic view of the evolution of green
marketing was presented by Bhardwaj et al. (2023), who
conducted a bibliometric analysis of research for 2005 –
2022. The advantage of this work is the ability to tra ce
not individual results of empirical studies but the
structure of the scientific field, its thematic dynamics, and
the relationships among research directions. At the same
time, the considerable length of the period studied
reduces the possibility of characterizing the very latest
trends in detail. For an article focused on 2021 –2025, the
results of Bhardwaj et al. should be regarded as a baseline
point of comparison rather than as a sufficient reflection
of the current state of research.

Liu et al. (202 3) similarly examine green marketing
through a CiteSpace -based bibliometric analysis,
identifying approximately 952 Web of Science
publications during 2019 –2023 and highlighting the
structure of relationships among key concepts. However,
the study focuses primarily on green marketing, leaving
the digital dimension less systematically explored. This
limitation creates an opportunity to broaden the
bibliometric perspective by integrating digital marketing,
digital transformation, digital sustainability, ESG a nd
sustainable development. In 2023 –2024, research
increasingly shifts from the digitalization of individual
marketing processes toward its implications for
sustainable business models, innovation and enterprise
performance, as demonstrated by Broccardo et al. (2023),
Lu et al. (2023) and Xu et al. (2023). Consequently,
digitalization is increasingly conceptualized not merely as
a technological characteristic but as a mechanism for
transforming the creation of economic and environmental
value.
Particularly important for contemporary discourse is
the work of Wang and Esperança (2023), which examines
the impact of digital transformation on the market and
ESG performance of small and medium -sized enterprises.
This approach broadens the traditional understanding of
digital marketing, since it links digital changes not only
to communication effectiveness but also to non -financial
outcomes of the enterprise. However, in this case ESG
indicators remain predominantly an outcome
characteristic of activity, while the s pecific contribution
of marketing communications to their formation requires
separate investigation.
In 2024, the direction associated with the convergence
of digitalization and sustainable development intensifies.
Mendez-Picazo et al. (2024) analyze the direct and
indirect effects of digital transformation on sustainable
development in the pre -pandemic and post -pandemic
periods. Valero -Gil et al. (2024) focus attention on the
convergence of digitalization and sustainability, which
indicates the gradual for mation of an integrated research
field. Chen et al. (2024) examine the relationship between
digital transformation and ESG decoupling, while Zhang
and Huang (2024) investigate the role of supply chain
resilience in the relationship between digital
transformation and ESG performance. Taken together,
these works demonstrate the expansion of the research
subject from marketing activity to the entire system of
corporate sustainability.
One of the new directions is the use of artificial
intelligence in digital m arketing. Gündüzyeli (2024)
examines AI in digital marketing within the framework of
sustainable management. At the same time, a
methodological problem arises: digital technologies can
simultaneously increase the effectiveness of marketing
communications and generate new risks associated with
excessive personalization, data privacy, digital inequality
and the ethics of algorithmic decisions. Consequently,
evaluating sustainable digital marketing requires taking
into account not only economic outcomes but al so social
and ethical consequences.
Particular attention should be given to research
published in 2025, which reflects the growing
institutionalization of sustainable digital marketing
(SDM). Rosário and Dias (2025), based on a systematic
review of 84 Scopus publications, identify personalized
messages, social media, influencer collaborations,
environmental brand positioning and digital content as
key mechanisms influencing sustainable consumer
behavior, while also highlighting gaps concerning digital
accessibility and ethical data management. Bashar et al.
(2025) further develop the conceptualization of SDM by
combining systematic review and bibliometric analysis,
linking it to sustainable consumption, eco -friendly
behavior, CSR, social equity, environmental education
and sustainable business models. These studies
demonstrate that sustainable digital marketing extends
beyond the use of digital channels to promote
environmentally friendly products and represents a
broader integration of economic, social and
environmental objectives. At the same time, the broad
temporal coverage of existing reviews limits their ability
to capture the most recent dynamics, highlighting the
need for updated research on the evolution and
integration of digital marketing and sustainable
development.
Another aspect is presented by Ezeh and Dube (2025),
who used VOSviewer for a bibliometric analysis of green
and sustainability marketing. The authors focus attention
on such concepts as sustainability marketing, green
marketing, enviro nmental responsibility, consumer
attitudes, green products, green supply chain
management, CSR and greenwashing. Digitalization does
not act as a central category of this model, which leaves
room for further research into the digital mechanisms of
building trust in sustainability claims.
In parallel, the Digital ESG direction is being formed.
Tan et al. (2025) conducted a bibliometric analysis of 455
documents indexed in Scopus devoted to the relationship
between ESG and digitalization. The authors note the
absence of a unified theoretical framework for Digital
ESG and identify the main thematic directions of the
development of this research field. This is of direct
relevance to marketing research, since digital channels
are increasingly used for ESG communications, the
disclosure of non -financial information, and the
formation of corporate reputation. At the same time,
research on Digital ESG predominantly concentrates on
corporate governance and reporting, while the marketing
dimension of ESG communications  remains less
systematized.
An even broader approach was proposed by Kumar
and Shah (2025), who combined a systematic review with
a bibliometric analysis of 589 publications from Scopus
and Web of Science for 2018 –2025. The study shows a
substantial acceleration of scholarly activity after 2020,
and among the technological drivers it identifies AI,
blockchain and IoT. The thematic clusters encompass
digital governance, ESG transparency and the circular
economy. At the same time, regional disproportions and
an insufficient number of empirical studies in a number
of sectors and countries were identified. The findings
obtained make it possible to interpret Digital ESG as a

direction that extends beyond corporate reporting and is

Thus, the literature analysis makes it possible to
identify three stages in the evolution of the research
discourse. The first is characterized by the predominance
of the concepts of green and sustainable marketing and a
focus on the env ironmental behavior of consumers. The
second is associated with the digitalization of marketing
communications and the integration of digital marketing
with green marketing. The third, most pronounced in
2024–2025, entails the formation of an integrated field at
the intersection of sustainable digital marketing, digital
transformation, ESG and the SDGs.
Consequently, what remains insufficiently studied is
not so much each of the aforementioned directions
individually as their structural interaction within a  single
scientific field. In particular, the relationships among
digital marketing technologies, sustainable consumption,
ESG communications, greenwashing, artificial
intelligence and the achievement of the SDGs require
further analysis. Moreover, most existing bibliometric
studies use different time frames, databases and search
strategies, which complicates a direct comparison of their
results.
It is precisely this circumstance that substantiates the
expediency of conducting the present scientometric
analysis of publications for 2021 –2025 in Scopus using a
unified search based on the keywords "marketing,"
"digitalization" and "sustainable development." Such an
approach makes it possible to determine not only the
dynamics of publication activity but also the structure of
thematic clusters, keyword relationships, and co -
authorship and citation networks. In this context,
bibliometric analysis becomes an instrument for
identifying structural changes in the research field rather
than merely a method of quantitati vely describing
publications.
Research Methods
The empirical basis was formed on the basis of the
international Scopus scientometric database, which is
characterized by a high level of structuring of
bibliographic data, broad disciplinary coverage, and
standardized metadata. The choice of this database is due
to its extensive use in bibliometric studies of related
problem areas and its capacity to enable multi -level
analysis of scholarly publications. Compared with other
sources of scientific information, S copus provides a more
representative reflection of the international scientific
space, which is important for the analysis of
interdisciplinary research in the field of marketing,
digitalization and sustainable development.
The empirical basis of the study was developed
through a systematic search of scientific publications
indexed in the Scopus database. The search strategy was
designed to identify studies addressing the intersection of
marketing, digitalization, and sustainable development.
The search was  conducted across publication titles,
abstracts, and author keywords using the following
combination of terms: (“marketing” OR “digital
marketing” OR “green marketing”) AND
(“digitalization” OR “digital transformation”) AND
(“sustainable development” OR “ESG”).
This search configuration was selected to ensure the
inclusion of publications in which marketing activities,
digital transformation processes, and sustainability -
related issues are examined within a common conceptual
or analytical framework. The se arch was performed in
Scopus on 3 September 2026.
The inclusion criteria covered peer -reviewed journal
articles published in English and available in their final
publication version. Records not corresponding to these
criteria were excluded from the dataset. To improve data
quality and ensure the consistency of the bibliometric
sample, the retrieved records were additionally checked
for duplicate entries. In addition to the automated data -
processing procedures available in Scopus, a manual
verification of b ibliographic metadata was carried out to
identify possible inconsistencies in publication records.
Scopus was selected as the primary data source
because of its broad multidisciplinary coverage,
standardized bibliographic structure, extensive citation
data, and availability of metadata suitable for bibliometric
and scientometric analysis. These characteristics make
the database appropriate for examining interdisciplinary
research trends and collaboration patterns at the
intersection of marketing, digitaliza tion, and sustainable
development.
Although Web of Science is also widely used in
bibliometric research, the present study was deliberately
limited to Scopus in order to maintain methodological
consistency within a single bibliographic environment.
Combining data from Scopus and Web of Science would
require additional procedures for data harmonization,
duplicate removal, author and institutional
disambiguation, and standardization of metadata
structures. Therefore, cross -database comparison and
integration are considered a potential direction for further
research.
The final dataset comprised 3,657 publications. The
dataset represents a static snapshot of Scopus indexing as
of 3 September 2026 and was not updated during the
subsequent stages of analysis. Accordingly, all
bibliometric indicators presented in the study refer to this
fixed dataset.
The analysis covered the period from 2015 to 2026
and included the examination of publication dynamics,
thematic structure, citation patterns, co -authorship,
international scientific collaboration, and institutional
cooperation. A single dataset was retained throughout all
analytical stages in order to ensure comparability of the
results and to avoid changes in the composition of the
sample depending on the specific dimension of
bibliometric analysis.
Particular caution was applied to the interpretation of
data for 2026. As the dataset was retrieved before the end
of the calendar year, the number of publications indexed
for 2026 (794 documents) is preliminary and should not
be interpreted as a complete annual publication output.
The corresponding value reflects only the publications
indexed in Scopus as of the date of data retrieval.

The collected array of publications covers 3,657
sources with a time span corresponding to the period of
formation of the scientific discourse on the problems of

(approximately 2015–2026), which provides the capacity
to analyze long -term trends in the development of

of marketing practices and their alignment with the
principles of sustainable development.
VOSviewer software was used for the visualization
and analysis of the bibliometric data, providing for the
construction of networks of relationships among
keywords, co -authors, cited publications and
bibliographic links (Broccardo, L., Zicari, A., Jabeen, F.,
and Bhatti, Z.A., 2023) . The application of this tool
provides the capacity to identify thematic clusters,
determine the centers o f scientific activity, and map the
structure of research directions at the intersection of
marketing, digitalization and sustainable development.
Bibliometric analysis is a descriptive method of
studying scientific communication, as its primary object
is the body of scientific publications and their structural
characteristics rather than the economic, marketing,
environmental, or social processes discussed in those
studies. Accordingly, the results can be interpreted at two
main levels: a descriptive level , covering publication
volume, temporal dynamics, and subject -area distribution
based on Scopus metadata, and a structural level,
including thematic clusters, co -authorship, institutional
collaboration, and citation networks identified through
VOSviewer. These findings provide an empirical basis
for characterizing the organization and relationships
within the research field, while remaining subject to the
methodological limitations of bibliometric analysis.
At the interpretative level, bibliometric data may
indicate emerging research trends and relationships
between digital transformation, marketing, sustainability,
and related areas; however, they cannot independently
establish causal effects on firms’ economic,
environmental, or social performance. Such findings
should therefore be treated primarily as a basis for
generating research hypotheses, while their empirical
verification requires additional methods, including panel
econometric analysis, structural equation modelling, or
sector-specific case studies. Thus, bibliometric results are
mainly used to describe the dynamics and structure of the
scientific field, whereas conclusions regarding the real -
world effects of digitalization require further empirical
testing.

Research limitations
The interpretation of the obtained results r equires
consideration of methodological limitations related to the
source of bibliographic data, the parameters used to
construct the sample, and the specific characteristics of
the bibliometric indicators applied. Taken together, these
factors may affect the structure of the identified research
field; therefore, the findings should be interpreted within
the boundaries of their representativeness.
The empirical basis of the study was constructed
exclusively using Scopus data. Despite the broad
disciplinary coverage of this database, its indexing policy
may result in the uneven representation of regional and
non-English-language scientific journals. Consequently,
the geographical concentration of publication activity
identified within the analysed dataset may partly reflect
the characteristics of Scopus indexing coverage rather
than solely the actual intensity of research activity in
individual countries. Applying the same search strategy
to Web of Science or Google Scholar data in future
studies would make it possible to assess the robustness of
the identified geographical and thematic patterns.
An additional limitation concerns the language of
publication. The inclusion of English -language
publications only restricts the coverage of research
findings published in national languages and may
reinforce the geographical asymmetry of the resulting
dataset. Under these conditions, the findings primarily
characterize the English -language segment of
international scientific discourse, whereas specific
research traditi ons developed within particular European
and Asian academic communities may be
underrepresented.
The structure of the publication dataset may also be
affected by publication bias. The identified thematic
configuration reflects not only prevailing research
interests but also the conditions under which the
academic publishing system operates, including editorial
priorities and manuscript selection practices. Therefore,
the predominance of publications reporting positive
outcomes of digitalization should not b e interpreted
directly as evidence that this perspective dominates the
entire body of research. Likewise, the frequency with
which a particular term occurs should not be treated as an
independent indicator of its theoretical or practical
significance.
The interpretation of citation relationships should
account for the unequal accumulation of citations over
time. Earlier publications, all else being equal, have had
more time to accumulate citations, which may lead newer
research areas to occupy peripheral po sitions regardless
of their substantive potential. The configuration of
citation networks may also be influenced by self -citation,
reciprocal citation within the same research group, and
disciplinary differences in citation practices. Accordingly,
the position of an author, institution, or thematic cluster
within a citation network should be interpreted with due
consideration of publication chronology and disciplinary
context.
Specific limitations also apply to keyword co -
occurrence analysis. The frequency of terms and the
structure of their relationships reflect the organization of
scientific discourse but do not directly characterize the
actual state of the economic, marketing, social, or
environmental processes under investigation. The results
of such analysis depend on the parameters used to
construct the network, including the minimum term -
frequency threshold, the quality of synonym and spelling -
variant normalization, and the selected clustering
parameters. Changes in these settings may lead to
differences in the number, boundaries, and composition
of thematic clusters. Therefore, the resulting network
configuration should be interpreted as an outcome of
specific methodological procedures rather than as a
definitive and unambiguous representation of the
structure of the research field.
Taken together, these limitations define the
boundaries within which the findings can be generalized.
The analysis makes it possible to characterize trends,
thematic structures, and relationships within the English -
language body of publications indexed in Scopus;
however, the results cannot be directly extrapolated to the
entire global scientific literature or used to establish
causal relationships between digitalization, marketing
activities, and sustainable development outcomes.
Conclusions
The scientometric analysis of the research field at the
intersection of marketing, digitalization and sustainable
development demonstrates that this is a quantitatively
mature but structurally fragmented area of scholarly
inquiry. Publica tion activity increased exponentially,
from 92 documents in 2020 to 1,006 in 2025, indicating a
transition from an emerging interdisciplinary field to an
actively developing research domain. The subject -area
distribution is dominated by Business, Management and
Accounting (1,975), Social Sciences (1,079), and
Economics, Econometrics and Finance (1,042), whereas
Environmental Science (514), Energy (359), and
Agricultural and Biological Sciences (191) remain less
represented. This indicates the predominance o f a
managerial and economic perspective and the insufficient
integration of the natural -science and technical
dimensions of sustainable development.
International collaboration is concentrated around the
United Kingdom, India, Indonesia, Portugal, Spain and
Ukraine, while Latin America, most African countries
and the Middle East remain comparatively
underrepresented. Institutional and co -authorship
networks demonstrate a similar concentration around a
limited number of universities and researchers, leaving
many institutions and authors in peripheral positions.
The citation network has a polycentric but uneven
structure. Its core encompasses strategic management,
digital technologies, marketing and brand management,
tourism, and sustainable development, while several
peripheral clusters remain weakly integrated.
Overall, the findings demonstrate that the field is
quantitatively mature but fragmented in content,

applied clusters in the keyword network ("agriculture,"
"fashion industry," "smart tourism"), is the sectoral
verification of the proposed models in sectors with
heightened environmental sensitivity of production
processes - the agricultural sector, the fashion industry,
and tourism - including with regard to the latest
technological platforms (the metaverse, the platform
economy). This would make it possible to establish the
limits of generalizability of the identified patterns and to
reveal sectoral specificity in the implementation of
sustainable digital marketing.
The implementation of the aforementioned directions
requires a corresponding organizational precondition —
the expansion of interdisciplinary and inter -institutional
research infrastructure, since the identified fragmentation
of the co -authorship network and the concentration of
institutional collaboration around a limited circle of units
constrain the potential for a comprehensive study of the
problem area.
Finally, an independent avenue for further research is
the study of the potential of social marketing as an
instrument for achieving the Sustainable Development
Goals in combination with digital communication
channels - social networks and influencer marketin g -
which logically complements the proposed integrated
model and expands the applied significance of the results
obtained for the formation of public policy and corporate
sustainable-development strategies.

the empirically identified structural disproportions of the
scientific field and are consistent with the logic of
sequential scholarly inquiry - from theoretical
generalization, through the development of measurement
tools, to empirical verification a t the cross -country,
sectoral and institutional levels - which creates a coherent
program of further research at the intersection of
marketing, digitalization and sustainable development.
