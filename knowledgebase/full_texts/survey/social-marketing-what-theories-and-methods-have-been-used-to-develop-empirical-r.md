# Social marketing: what theories and methods have been used to develop empirical research within the field?

REVIEW
International Review on Public and Nonprofit Marketing (2026) 23:481–516
https://doi.org/10.1007/s12208-026-00467-y
Abstract
Social marketing adopts theories and methods used in commercial marketing to
encourage behaviour change within social contexts. A range of theories is available
to help explain behaviours, provided by different fields (such as psychology, sociol -
ogy, communication, and consumer behaviour). However, there is no updated sys -
tematic review that synthesises the theories applied in social marketing alongside
the research methods and thematic domains studied, limiting the field’s ability to
identify recent advances and identify gaps. This study therefore sought to map the
theories, methods and themes used in social marketing research studies by conduct -
ing a systematic literature review following the PRISMA protocol and analysing
findings through an ADO-TCM framework-based approach, offering a structured,
state-of-the-art overview beyond theory-based reviews. The analysis shows that ac -
ademics tend to rely predominantly on psychological theories to develop research in
Social Marketing (e.g., the Theory of Planned Behaviour), alongside a smaller set
of theories from other disciplines. The predominant methods identified are routed as
traditional designs – most commonly surveys (quantitative) and interviews (qualita -
tive), with comparatively limited adoption of more innovative approaches such as
ethnography or consumer neuroscience. The literature also concentrates on promi -
nent themes, including obesity awareness, responsible drinking or anti-drinking,
and pro-environmental behaviour. By systematically integrating theory, method and
theme patterns, this review highlights underexplored theoretical and methodological
opportunities and outlines avenues for future research relevant to both practitioners
and academics.

1 Introduction
Social marketing, formally introduced by Kotler and Zaltman in 1971, adopted the
theories, methods, activities, institutions and processes of commercial marketing
and adapted them to a social context (Andreasen, 2012; Dann, 2010; Kotler, 2022),
seeking to promote personal and societal life quality and avoid harmful behaviours
(Andreasen, 2012). Its activities are usually directly or indirectly (through non-profit
organisations or policymakers) funded by governments that use tax payments (Diet -
rich et al., 2022; Rundle-Thiele et al., 2019).
Throughout fifty years of research within the field, four major eras were identi -
fied: from 1969 to 1989, marketing was used for social good, making use of market-
ing principles to social causes; from 1990 to 1999, voluntary individual behaviour
change was the main goal sought to influence personal behaviours using market -
ing techniques; from 2000 to 2009, research was focused on planification to action
through process orientation, developing systematic planning frameworks and pro -
cesses; from 2010 to 2021, it is called the systems solution era, integrating systems
thinking and upstream approaches (Dietrich et al., 2022).
At its core, social marketing applies communication strategies to drive behaviour
change (Dietrich et al., 2022), a challenge given the need to craft effective messages
(Cole et al., 2016). Another challenge lies in the complexity and difficulty of the
national and global problems that social marketing seeks to solve, such as health
(physical or mental), environmental, racial equity, gender or disability, as strategy
definition requires the consideration of individuals and their behaviour (Akbar et al.,
2022; Rundle-Thiele et al., 2019).
Social marketing has advanced significantly in recent years (Kotler, 2022). As in
other fields, theory serves as a basis for research. Although its use in social market -
ing is considered unusual (Dietrich et al., 2022; Luca & Suggs, 2013; Willmott &
Rundle-Thiele, 2022), it also has potential benefits. As a result, there is an identified
need to support social marketing researchers and practitioners to apply theories and
methods on social marketing research, ensuring the success of social marketing cam-
paigns in driving positive changes in individual behaviour with impact on society
(Dietrich et al., 2022; Willmott & Rundle-Thiele, 2022).
The most recent systematic reviews limited to examining the theories applied in
social marketing research date to 2014 (Brennan et al., 2014; Truong, 2014). More than a
decade later, with an exponential research growth in social marketing starting in 2015, an
updated review is needed to identify advances in theory usage, adding the identification
of the methods and the researched themes, offering an overview of the state-of-the-art
to practitioners and academics and identifying future research avenues/research agenda.
To address this gap, this literature review was conducted to answer the research ques-
tion: What theories and methods have researchers used to develop empirical research
within the field? The following objectives guided it: to identify the theories used in
social marketing (most commonly used theories, their applications, and results); to
identify the methods (most commonly used methods, their application, and results); to
identify underdeveloped themes within the field that require future research.
A research protocol was adapted from Page et al. (2021), and the literature review
analysis and scrutiny respected the ADO-TCM framework (Antecedents, decisions,

2 Method
To structure the literature and address the research question, the authors first identi -
fied relevant literature in the Web of Science (WoS) database and filtered it using
the PRISMA 2020 protocol (Page et al., 2021), consisting of three main stages: (1)
Identification, (2) Screening, and (3) Inclusion, depicted in Fig. 1.
The search for articles was first executed in the Web of Science (WoS) database in
July 2024 (the data was exported on July 12, 2024) and re-executed on February 2026
(February 26, 2026) to capture newly indexed records.
Using the keywords "social marketing" AND "theory" across all fields, the initial
search yielded 661 documents. The results were then restricted to specific document
types, including articles, review articles, early access papers, and subject areas such
as management, business, or communication. The search was also limited to English-
language publications, resulting in 326 articles. To filter results and ensure high-qual-
ity, ranked articles, only those published in journals listed in the AJG-ABS Ranking
List were considered, resulting in a final sample of 271 articles.
The selection of journals from the AJG-ABS Ranking List, which includes the
most important Business journals, was motivated by the need to concentrate on the
most relevant theories and methods to the field. The emphasis on empirical studies
reflects the assumption that rigorous empirical enquiry builds on well-established
theoretical foundations.
During the screening phase, three steps were followed: (1) Exclusion of articles
based on their research approach (bibliometric reviews, historical research, litera -
ture reviews, or theoretical/conceptual papers were excluded) as empirical studies
are required to identify the methods of social marketing research; (2) Exclusion of
articles that could not be retrieved (e.g., due to restricted access); and (3) Exclusion
of articles based on their field. Articles addressing themes such as circular economy
in SMEs, business context marketing strategies, retail pricing, human resources man-
agement, social e-commerce, e-retailing, alcohol branding, fast fashion, and market-
ing were excluded as they did not directly pertain to social marketing. Figure 1 details
the research protocol.
The final sample of 151 articles were summarised using the ADO-TCM frame -
work-based review method developed by Paul et al. ( 2024), deemed as informative,

4 Literature review results and discussion
The literature review results, and discussion are organised as follows: first, a review
of the theories and research contexts within social marketing, followed by an exami-
nation of the methods chose to develop empirical social marketing literature. This
analysis is supported by the TCM component of the ADO–TCM framework, which
allows us to understand how social marketing research is conducted. Finally, we
review antecedents (drivers), decisions (actions), and outcomes (results). The ADO-
TCM framework supporting the findings is available in Appendix A.
4.1 A review of theories and research contexts in social marketing
Theories applied in social marketing have origins in various fields, including sociol-
ogy, psychology, and communication. Among the sample, 65% explicitly utilised
theories applied to social marketing. The remaining studies, while lacking a theoreti-
cal foundation, were retained for their methodological relevance to the field.
Various theories were identified in the literature and listed in Table 4, in descend-
ing chronologically order based on the number of articles. The authors are identified
for each theory, and a brief description of their assumptions is provided, along with
the sample size and related articles. In the table, each theory will be examined and
defined individually, and its use context is discussed.
As shown, the most used theory was the Theory of Planned Behaviour ( n = 40),
followed by the Social Cognitive Theory ( n = 7), the Theory of Reasoned Action
(n = 6) and the Self-determination Theory ( n = 6). The results corroborate Ben Lowe
and Lowe’s (2015) statement that the Theory of Planned Behaviour is a highly cited
and widely used theory for studying behaviour change and is considered the most
influential and impactful for academics (Manstead & Parker, 1995; Richetin et al.,
2011; Schuster, 2015). Also, the Theory of Reasoned Action (Ajzen & Fishbein,
1980) which gave rise to the Theory of Planned Behaviour by adding perceived
behavioural control as a predictor of behaviour, is highlighted in the literature. Then,
Self-Determination Theory is preferred over Cognitive Evaluation Theory as a sub-
theory of the Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan, 2013).

The articles were read and analysed within the sample to analyse the methods used
in social marketing. Consistent with the requirements for scientific publication, the
methods utilised were identified in all articles considered for this review. Table 6

The dominant application areas of research are health (obesity, smoking, drink -
ing) and environment-related (water consumption, pro-environmental, waste), and,
regarding geographic expansion, Australia and the USA are the prominent investors
in research within the field.
As demonstrated, the most used quantitative method was the survey (n = 64); within
the qualitative research approach, the interview ( n = 20), the focus group ( n = 8) and
content analysis ( n = 7). When using a mixed-methods approach, the survey com -
bined with other qualitative methods is the most commonly used method combination
(n = 20). The preference for the survey method explains the dominance of the Theory
of Planned Behaviour in theory usage, as it is easier to apply. Other methods require
investment, especially qualitative ones, to interpret behaviour (David & Rundle-
Thiele, 2018). Also, the preference for quantitative methods between 2008 and 2026
shifted from 1998–2012, when qualitative methods led research (Truong, 2014).

tion (Gordon et al., 2018a, 2018b, 2018c) due to environmental issues and gambling
consumption behavior (Gordon et al., 2023).

Although the various research contexts within the social marketing field share sev-
eral common antecedents (e.g., awareness, subjective norms), decisions (e.g., goal
framing), and outcomes (e.g., behaviour, effectiveness), important contextual speci -
ficities were identified. In particular, pro-environmental consumption and behaviour,
social marketing sponsorship, obesity, and behavioural deterrence exhibit context-
specific antecedents, decisions, and outcomes. In contrast, the breastfeeding context
lacks clearly defined ADO variables.
Furthermore, several variables were found to assume multiple roles across the
ADO dimensions. For instance, donation functions both as an antecedent and an out-
come; efficacy appears as an antecedent, a decision (e.g., in obesity studies), and an
outcome (e.g., in responsible driving campaigns); habit is identified as an antecedent
(e.g., water consumption) and as an outcome (e.g., obesity); and perception operates
as an antecedent across multiple contexts, as an outcome (e.g., HIV prevention and
social marketing community sponsorship), and as a decision (e.g., perceived qual -
ity in behavioural deterrence). Similar patterns were observed for variables such as
physical activity, performance, and empathy.
Given the extensive use of the Theory of Planned Behaviour, it is noteworthy
that key variables such as attitude, intention, and behaviour are widely employed.
Attitude appears across all three ADO dimensions — antecedent, decision, and out -
come— despite Ajzen’s (1991) recommendation that it be primarily used as a predic-
tor. Likewise, intention, which functions as a mediating construct within the theory, is
frequently operationalised as both a decision and an outcome. Additionally, the The-
ory of Consumption Values emerges as particularly relevant, with values consistently
identified as antecedents across multiple contexts and as important emotional drivers.
Overall, the findings indicate that the variables underpinning social marketing
research vary considerably according to context. While some contexts, such as pro-
environmental behaviour and obesity, have identified a rich set of relevant variables,
others, notably breastfeeding, remain underdeveloped in this regard. This gap high -
lights important opportunities for future research.
5 Conclusions
A literature review was conducted to identify the theories and methods researchers use
to develop empirical research in the social marketing field, addressing the research
question and meeting the need for an updated review, as the last review addressing
the same objective (to identify theories) was published in 2014. To strengthen the
contribution of this research article, a methods section was added, offering an over -
view of the theories (with brief explanations of each – Table 4), the methods and their
contexts, and a review of antecedents, decisions, and outcomes (ADO). Deepening,
we identified the most used theories, the combination of theories and methods, the
connection between the body of research and the underpinning theories, and disci -
plinary connections; categorised methodologies; and identified variables.
Starting with the theories, the Theory of Planned Behaviour, the Social Cognitive
Theory, and the Self-Determination Theory were the most frequently used in the
sample. These theories are behavioural theories that explain behaviour in terms of

motivational and cognitive factors. All three theories include variables related to an
individual’s belief in performing a behaviour (e.g., perceived behavioural control,
self-efficacy, and competence) and internal psychological processes that drive action
(e.g., intention, cognitive factors, and autonomy). As social marketing aims to pro -
mote behaviour change for individuals’ and societal good, these theories address it
as an outcome.
The Theory of Planned Behaviour is an easy-to-use theory for explaining behav -
iour; Social Cognitive Theory adds the environmental impact on the outcome and
determinants in social contexts; and Self-Determination adds a relational variable to
explain behaviour. Future research would benefit from the use of mixed theories for
a more integrative interpretation of behaviour within social contexts, as well as from
other methods, such as ethnography, which remain underutilised in social marketing.
The results reinforce the Theory of Planned Behaviour as one of the most efficient
in explaining behaviour, as it is explanatory, offers an understanding of the relation -
ships between variables, and has predictive value, allowing us to predict behaviour
when applying the variables/constructs to reality. Consequently, it is the most used
by academics. In addition, looking at the constructs and variables considered, it was
identified that the Theory of Planned Behaviour from Ajzen ( 1991) was based on
other theories, such as the Self-perception Theory and the Theory of Reasoned Action.
Among behavioural psychology theories, Social Cognitive Theory and Protection-
motivation Theory share the concept of Self-efficacy. While social marketing research
and its core variables are highly context-dependent, certain contexts, such as breast -
feeding, lack clearly defined variables, presenting opportunities for future study.
For future research, it is suggested to explore other theories and other social mar-
keting perspectives, using Social Practice Theory (Bourdieu, 1977) that adds an
intersubjective and relational process view; Exchange Theory (Homans, 1958) that
adds the intangible and tangible benefits as a variable influencing behaviour; and
also more recent models (as Brito and Loureiro ( 2026) used) that may upgrade the
theory, such as the Model of Goal-directed Behaviour and the Negative-state Relief
Model (Batson et al., 1989), that adds emotions, past behaviour and desires to pre -
dicting behaviour (Perugini & Bagozzi, 2001), but that requires empirical application
to strengthen as a theory.
An effort must be made to disrupt the prominent use of the Theory of Planned
Behaviour to explain the individual behaviour within a societal context.
Regarding the methods, the most used were the survey and the interview, with a
range of methods available that can offer a deep understanding of behaviour (such as
consumer neuroscience techniques and ethnography). The methods applied are still
very traditional. Also, considering the time frame and sample characteristics, they are
limited to cross-sectional studies. Social marketing would benefit in the future from
the usage of complex but more complete and deep research making use of consumer
neuroscience techniques (Alsharif et al., 2021) or Grounded Theory, which allows a
deep and extensive comprehension when fields are underdeveloped (Glaser & Strauss,
1967), such as the ones presented then. Also, it would benefit from longitudinal studies.
Subsequently, underdeveloped contexts deserving further attention were iden -
tified within the social marketing field, and future research avenues were opened.

methods and techniques, which remain misrepresented.

marketing field by offering practitioners and academics an overview and summary of
the theories used in social marketing, identifying their authors, and providing brief
explanations. Practitioners and academics can, by reading this research, identify the
theories that best fit and guide their future research.

Furthermore, from a theoretical lens and relying on its implications, the review
indicates that social marketing research remains theoretically concentrated in

and distinguishes mechanisms across levels of analysis. The underrepresentation
