ABSTRACT: Indonesian micro, small, and medium -sized enterprises (MSMEs) are widely recognised as pillars of economic
resilience, employment creation, and community-based entrepreneurship. Yet many MSMEs still struggle to convert digital adoption
into sustained competitiveness. Digital presence can increase visibility, but it does not automatically resolve capability ga ps in
market learning, digital interpretation, innovation routines, and ecosystem access. This study develops a qualitative themati c
synthesis explaining how digital marketing strategy, digital literacy, innovation capability, and ecosystem support collecti vely
revitalise the competitiveness of Indonesian MSMEs in the digital economy. The final synthesis used 48 cited sources, consist ing
of recent peer -reviewed literature, foundational theoretical works, and official Indonesian policy documents. The analysis  was
guided by the resource -based view, dynamic capability theory, and diffusion of innovation theory. Five interrelated themes were
identified: digital marketing as a market -sensing and customer -relationship capability; digital literacy as a managerial fo undation
for meaningful technology use; innovation capability as the mechanism that converts digital interaction into value creation;
ecosystem support as an enabling condition; and strategic integration as the basis of sustainable competitiveness. The findings show
that MSME competitiveness is not strengthened by technology adoption alone. It is revitalised when entrepreneurs are digitall y
literate, marketing is strategically designed, customer information is converted into innovation, and institutional su pport addresses
capability gaps rather than merely encouraging platform onboarding.
KEYWORDS: digital marketing strategy; digital literacy; innovation capability; MSME competitiveness; qualitative thematic
synthesis; Indonesia; digital transformation

1. INTRODUCTION
Micro, small, and medium-sized enterprises (MSMEs) remain a central pillar of Indonesia's economic and social resilience. Official
policy sources consistently describe MSMEs as major contributors to national output and employment. The Ministry of Finance
reports that MSMEs contribute around 61% of national gross domestic product and absorb approximately 97% of the workforce,
while the Coordinating Ministry for Economic Affairs highlights that the export contribution of MSMEs remains much lower t han
their domestic economic role (Coordinating Ministry for Economic Affairs of the Republic of Indonesia, 2025; Ministry of Finance
of the Republic of Indonesia, 2025). This imbalance indicates that strengthening MSME competitiveness cannot be reduced to
increasing the number of enterprises. It requires a deeper capability agenda involving market access, digital competence, pro duct
upgrading, innovation routines, and ecosystem alignment.
The digital economy has changed the competitive logic faced by MSMEs.  In conventional markets, small firms could rely heavily
on location, personal networks, price flexibility, and informal promotion. In platform -based markets, customers compare products
instantly, read reviews, evaluate seller responsiveness, expect conven ient payment, track delivery, and judge after -sales
communication. Digital platforms therefore create opportunity and pressure at the same time. They can expand market reach, bu t
they also expose MSMEs to stronger comparison, faster imitation, algorithmic uncertainty, and more demanding customers.
Recent scholarship confirms that SME digital transformation is a capability -building process rather than a purely technological
project. Studies show that digital readiness, digital literacy, managerial attributes , digital orientation, platform capability, business
model transformation, digital leadership, and ecosystem support shape whether digitalisation improves performance (Anatan & Nur,
2023; Barba-Sanchez et al., 2024; Clemente-Almendros et al., 2024; Escoz Barragan & Becker, 2025; Held et al., 2026; Prakasa &
Jumani, 2024; Sagala & Őri, 2024; Zahoor et al., 2023). This insight is highly relevant for Indonesian MSMEs because owners

The theoretical gap addressed in this article lies in the fragme nted treatment of digital marketing strategy, digital literacy, and

and inconsistent mentoring support.
This article is grounded in three complementary theoretical perspectives. The resource -based view explains why competitive
advantage emerges from valuable, rare, imperfectly imitable, and organisationally embedded resources (Barney, 1991). Dynamic
capability theory explains how firms sense opportunities, seize them, and reconfigure resources under changing market conditions
(Teece, 2007; Teece et al., 1997). Diffusion of innovation theory explains why adoption is more likely when innovation is perceived
as useful, compatible, observable, trialable, and not excessively complex (Rogers, 2003). Together, these perspectives clarify that
MSME competitiveness depends not on digital tools alone, but on the capability to combine digital interaction, managerial
interpretation, innovation routines, and ecosystem support.
Responding to these gaps, the present study develops a qualitative thematic synthesis that explains how digital marketing str ategy,
digital literacy, innovation capability, and ecosystem support operate as an integrated capability system. The study does not test
statistical hypotheses. Instead, it synthesises recent evidence and Indonesian policy context to build a theoretically ground ed and
practically useful explanation of MSME competitiveness in the digital economy.
1.1 Research Objectives
The central research question is: How do digital marketing strategy, digital literacy, innovation capability, and ecosystem s upport
jointly revitalise the competitiveness of Indonesian MSMEs in the digital economy? Speci fically, the study aims to:
a. Synthesise recent peer-reviewed literature and official policy sources on SME/MSME digital transformation, digital marketing,
digital literacy, innovation capability, ecosystem support, and competitiveness;
b. Identify dominant qualitative themes explaining how digital capability is converted into market value and competitive advantage;
c. Develop an integrated interpretive framework for strengthening Indonesian MSME competitiveness; and
d. Formulate practical implications for MSME owners, universities, business mentors, and policy makers.
This study contributes to the literature by developing an integrated capability -based framework of MSME digital competitiveness.
The framework explains that digital marketing strategy, digital literacy, innovation capability, and ecosystem support should not be
viewed as separate managerial instruments, but as mutually reinforcing capabilities. In this framework, digital marketing ena bles
market sensing, digital literacy enables informed decision -making, innovation capability enables value renewal, and ecosystem
support enables capability development.

2. LITERATURE REVIEW AND THEORETICAL FOUNDATION
2.1 Resource-Based View
The resource -based view (RBV) provides a foundational explanation of how organisatio ns achieve and sustain competitive
advantage through the strategic use of internal resources and capabilities. RBV argues that firms are more likely to outperfo rm
competitors when they possess resources that are valuable, rare, imperfectly imitable, and or ganisationally embedded (Barney,
1991). From this perspective, superior performance is not determined only by external industry structure. It also depends on the
ability of a firm to develop, combine, protect, and deploy internal resources that competitors  cannot easily replicate.
In the MSME context, the most strategic digital resources are often intangible. Digital marketing capability, digital platfor m
knowledge, customer data capability, digital literacy, organisational learning, and innovation routines  can strengthen a small firm's
ability to respond to changing customer needs and competitive pressure. Recent studies show that digital capability and digit al
transformation contribute to small -business performance when these capabilities are embedded in m anagerial routines and value -
creation processes (Prakasa & Jumani, 2024; Rosyidiana & Narsa, 2024). Social media accounts, marketplace stores, online payment
systems, and messaging applications are easy for competitors to copy. Their strategic value depend s on the way MSMEs combine
these tools with customer insight, service reliability, data interpretation, and innovation capability.

environments. While RBV emphasises the strategic importance of valuable resources, dynamic capability theory focuses on the

Within this study, digital marketing strategy is interpreted as a sensing capability because it enables MSMEs to observe cust omer

because it converts digital insight into new products, processes, services, and business models. Ecosystem support stre ngthens this

2.3 Diffusion of Innovation Theory
Diffusion of innovation theory explains how new technologies, ideas, and practices are adopted and disseminated across individuals,
organisations, and social systems. Adoption is influenced by perceived relative advantage, compatibility, complexity, trialab ility,
and observability (Rogers, 2003). In the MSME context, digital transformation is not simply a process of acquiring techno logy. It
is a behavioural and organisational process involving learning, experimentation, confidence building, and routinisation.
MSMEs often face adoption barriers such as limited financial resources, uneven digital knowledge, perceived technological
complexity, weak mentoring, and uncertainty about business benefits. Digital literacy reduces these barriers because entrepreneur s
become more capable of evaluating platforms, understanding online customer behaviour, protecting accounts, managing payment
risks, and using digital tools for decision making. Recent Indonesian evidence indicates that government support, technology
acceptance, social -cognitive learning, and digital literacy influence business transformation among micro and small enterprises
(Desmaryani et al., 2024; Raharjo et al., 2024).
2.4 Digital Marketing Strategy as a Market Capability
Digital marketing strategy refers to the systematic use of digital technologies, online platforms, and data -driven communication
channels to create, communicate, deliver, and capture customer value. In the MSME context, digital marketing should not be reduced
to uploading products or posting promotional content. It functions as a market -sensing, customer-relationship, brand-positioning,
and learning capability.
Recent empirical studies show that digital marketing adoption supports SME growth and performance when it is linked to strate gy,
customer engagement, platform learning, and managerial capability. Bruce et al. (2023) found that digital marketing adoption
supports the sustainable growth of SMEs, while Wu et al. (2024) demonstrated that innovativeness, proactiveness, and managerial
capabilities influence digital marketing strategy adoption and organisational performance. Sharabati et al. (2024) similarly show
that online advertising, social media marketing, search engine optimisation, and customer engagement contribute to SME
performance under modern digital transformation conditions.
2.5 Digital Literacy as Foundational Managerial Capability
Digital literacy r efers to the knowledge, competence, and critical judgement required to access, evaluate, create, communicate,
protect, and use digital information effectively. For MSMEs, digital literacy is not limited to operating a smartphone or cre ating
social media co ntent. It includes understanding platform rules, customer data, digital payment risks, privacy, account security,
algorithmic changes, online reputation, and analytics.
Recent evidence positions digital literacy as a key micro -foundation of digital transformation. Zahoor et al. (2023) show that
managers' digital literacy contributes to SME digital transformation through digital technology use and interacts with manage rial
attributes. Indonesian studies also link digital literacy with technology accep tance, business transformation, financial inclusion,
sustainability, and entrepreneurial performance (Al -Shami et al., 2024; Desmaryani et al., 2024; Raharjo et al., 2024; Wardana et
al., 2023). Digital literacy therefore functions as a managerial capabili ty that influences how entrepreneurs interpret opportunities
and risks.
2.6 Innovation Capability and Competitive Renewal
Innovation capability refers to an organisation's capacity to generate, develop, implement, and commercialise new ideas that create
value for customers and improve performance. For MSMEs, innovation does not always mean radical invention. It may involve
better packaging, clearer product bundles, faster service, improved delivery options, more attractive visual identity, safer digital
payment, or more responsive after-sales communication.
Digital adoption becomes competitive only when market information is converted into value creation. Customer reviews can trigger
product redesign, repeated questions can improve product descriptions, aban doned orders can reveal pricing or payment barriers,
and marketplace analytics can guide new variants. Recent studies show that digitalisation contributes to innovation and
competitiveness when firms can transform digital resources into technological innovation, business model renewal, and productivity

2.7 Ecosystem Support
MSME competitiveness is shaped not only by internal capability but also by ecosystem conditions. Infrastructure, logistics,
financing, certification, marketplace governance, digital payment systems, training quality, universities, ment ors, and business
associations influence whether digital transformation becomes a practical opportunity or an additional burden. Smaller firms
frequently lack sufficient internal resources to pursue digital transformation independently; therefore, external  support can reduce
capability gaps.
Recent studies show that government support, technology -organisation-environment configurations, business agility, digital
leadership, and entrepreneurial ecosystems shape SME performance and competitiveness (Chatterjee  et al., 2023; Hidayat -ur-
Rehman & Alsolamy, 2023; N'Dri & Su, 2024; Prasannath et al., 2024; Yusuf et al., 2023). For Indonesia, ecosystem support is
especially important because MSMEs operate under unequal regional and sectoral conditions. Urban firms ma y have stronger
logistics, mentoring access, and digital infrastructure, while rural firms may face weaker connectivity and fewer structured learning
communities.

2.8 Integrated Conceptual Framework and Propositions
Drawing on RBV, dynamic capability theory, and  diffusion of innovation theory, this study proposes an integrated conceptual
framework to explain how digital capabilities collectively contribute to MSME competitiveness. Rather than viewing digital
marketing strategy, digital literacy, innovation capabi lity, and ecosystem support as independent determinants, the framework
conceptualises them as mutually reinforcing capabilities operating within a support ecosystem.
Digital marketing strategy functions as the initial market -facing capability because it en ables MSMEs to reach customers,
communicate value, capture feedback, and observe market behaviour. Digital literacy allows entrepreneurs to evaluate digital
information, manage digital risks, and make better decisions. Innovation capability converts digita l insight into product, process,
service, and business model improvement. Ecosystem support strengthens the whole process by providing infrastructure, policy
support, finance, knowledge networks, certification assistance, and market facilitation.
Because t his article is a qualitative thematic synthesis, the propositions below are interpretive pathways rather than statistical
hypotheses. They are used to organise the conceptual logic of the framework and to guide theoretical interpretation.
Proposition 1 (P1): Digital marketing strategy strengthens digital literacy through market interaction and platform learning.
Proposition 2 (P2): Digital literacy strengthens innovation capability by improving entrepreneurs' ability to interpret and use digital
information.
Proposition 3 (P3): Innovation capability enhances MSME competitiveness through product, process, service, and business -model
improvement.
Proposition 4 (P4): Improved MSME competitiveness creates feedback that reinforces digital marketing strategy, digital literacy,
and innovation capability.

3. MATERIALS AND METHODS
3.1 Research Design
This study used a qualitative documentary research design supported by thematic synthesis. The design is appropriate because the
purpose of the article is not to estimate the statistical magnitude of relationships among constructs, but to develop a theor etically
grounded and practically meaningful explanation of how digital marketing strategy, digital literacy, innovation capability, and
ecosystem support interact in shaping MSME competitiveness.
The analysis combined deductive theoretical sensitivity and inductive pattern recognition. Deductive sensitivity was drawn fr om
RBV, dynamic capability theory, and diffusion of innovation theory (Barney, 1991; Rogers, 2003; Teece, 2007). Inductive attention
was directed to recurring arguments in recent empirical articles, systematic reviews, conceptual studies, and official Indone sian
policy documents.
3.2 Documentary Corpus and Search Strategy
The documentary corpus consisted of peer-reviewed journal articles, foundational theoretical works, and official Indonesian policy
documents. The scholarly corpus prioritised publications from 2023 to 2026 when available, while foundational theoretical sources
were retained because RBV, dynamic capability theory, and diffusion of innovation theory are established bodies of theory tha t
cannot be replaced by recent citations alone.
Searches were conducted using combinations of the following terms: SME digital transformation, MSME digital marketing, digital
marketing capability, digital literacy SMEs, innovation capability SMEs, business agility SMEs, dynamic capability digital
transformation, SME competitiven ess digital economy, sustainable digital transformation SMEs, digital orientation SMEs, SME
digitalisation barriers, SME innovation performance, and Indonesia MSME digitalisation. A representative search string was: (SME
OR MSME) AND (digital transformation OR digital marketing OR digital literacy OR innovation capability) AND (competitiveness
OR performance OR sustainable competitive advantage). Sources were traced through publisher platforms and indexing -oriented
repositories, including ScienceDirect, SpringerLink, Taylor & Francis Online, MDPI, Heliyon, DOAJ, institutional repositories, and
official Indonesian government websites.
The inclusion process was relevance-driven and transparent. Articles were retained when they directly supported one or more focal
constructs: digital marketing strategy, digital literacy, innovation capability, digital transformation, ecosystem support, b usiness
agility, or MSME/SME competitiveness. Official policy documents were used only to contextualise the economic importance  of
Indonesian MSMEs. Non -peer-reviewed opinion pieces, unsupported web summaries, marketing brochures, and sources with
unclear bibliographic status were excluded.

3.4 Literature Synthesis Procedure
The synthesis process followed a structured thematic procedure. First, all selected documents were read to identify their res earch
focus, theoretical orientation, methodological approach, and key findings. During this stage, attention was given not only to reported
results but also to the conceptual assumptions used by each study. Second, relevant statements were extracted and organised
according to the ir relationship with digital marketing strategy, digital literacy, innovation capability, ecosystem support, and
competitiveness.
Third, initial codes were generated. Deductive codes were derived from the theoretical framework, including strategic resourc e,
valuable capability, sensing, seizing, reconfiguration, relative advantage, compatibility, complexity, trialability, observab ility, and
routinisation. Inductive codes emerged from the literature itself, including customer trust, digital confidence, platf orm discipline,
account security, payment safety, marketplace learning, content consistency, analytics use, practical mentoring, and ecosyste m
inequality.
Fourth, related codes were grouped into broader analytical categories. Codes such as customer feedbac k, market responsiveness,
content discipline, customer engagement, and platform analytics were grouped under digital marketing as a market -sensing and
customer-relationship capability. Codes such as account security, payment safety, analytics interpretatio n, and digital confidence
were grouped under digital literacy as a managerial capability. Codes such as product redesign, process improvement, packagin g
renewal, business-model adjustment, and service innovation were grouped under innovation capability as a conversion mechanism.
3.5 Data Analysis Procedure
Thematic analysis followed six stages adapted from established qualitative thematic analysis logic (Braun & Clarke, 2021). Fi rst,
selected documents were read repeatedly to establish familiarity with thei r concepts, findings, and contexts. Second, relevant
statements were coded according to their relationship with digital marketing strategy, digital literacy, innovation capabilit y,
ecosystem support, and competitiveness. Third, similar codes were grouped i nto candidate themes. Fourth, the themes were
reviewed against the full corpus to ensure internal coherence and external distinction. Fifth, each theme was named and defin ed.
Sixth, the themes were interpreted through RBV, dynamic capability theory, and di ffusion of innovation theory.
This procedure strengthened analytical transparency by clarifying how the study moved from selected documents to codes, from
codes to themes, and from themes to theoretical interpretation. The process also reduced the risk of producing a merely narrative
review because each theme was grounded in recurring evidence and linked explicitly to the study's theoretical framework.
3.6 Trustworthiness and Ethical Considerations
Trustworthiness was strengthened through source triangulation, theory triangulation, transparent inclusion criteria, and an audit trail
of literature decisions. Source triangulation compared empirical articles, systematic reviews, conceptual studies, foundational theory
sources, and official policy documents. Theo ry triangulation used RBV, dynamic capability theory, and diffusion of innovation
theory to interpret recurring findings from different perspectives.
Because this study did not collect primary data from human participants, it did not involve personal ident ifiers, interviews, or
respondent anonymisation. Ethical attention was directed to accurate citation, avoidance of fabricated empirical claims, care ful
paraphrasing, and fair representation of source arguments. Claims about journal quartile status should b e rechecked before formal
submission because quartile status may change across Scopus, SJR, and journal ranking databases.

4. RESULTS
The thematic synthesis produced five interrelated themes that explain how Indonesian MSMEs can transform digital adoption into
competitiveness. The themes do not represent independent variables in a statistical model. Rather, they represent interpretiv e
capability domains that operate sequentially and interactively. Digital marketing strategy creates market visibility and cus tomer
interaction. Digital literacy enables entrepreneurs to interpret digital information and manage technological risks. Innovati on
capability converts customer insight and platform learning into improved value creation. Ecosystem support reduces externa l
constraints and strengthens capability development. Strategic integration connects these domains into a coherent competitiven ess
pathway.
The synthesis indicates that MSME competitiveness is not revitalised merely by increasing digital presence. Competit iveness
emerges when digital tools become embedded in disciplined managerial routines, learning practices, customer relationships,
innovation processes, and supportive institutional arrangements. This finding is particularly relevant for Indonesian MSMEs because
many enterprises adopt digital platforms under resource limitations, uneven digital competence, and strong competitive pressu re
from similar sellers.
4.1 Digital Marketing as a Market-Sensing and Customer-Relationship Capability
The first theme show s that digital marketing should not be reduced to online promotion. It strengthens competitiveness when it
functions as market sensing, communication discipline, customer relationship management, and value positioning. Studies on digital
marketing adoption  and SME performance demonstrate that digital tools create business value when entrepreneurs use them to
understand customer needs, communicate consistently, build trust, and evaluate market response (Amin et al., 2025; Bruce et a l.,
2023; Hokmabadi et al., 2024; Noer et al., 2025; Sharabati et al., 2024; Wu et al., 2024).
For Indonesian MSMEs, social media and marketplaces should not be treated merely as display windows. They need to become
learning systems. Customer comments, repeated questions, abandoned  orders, complaints, reviews, and engagement patterns
provide signals that can inform product presentation, packaging, pricing, service response, and communication tone. A digital ly
present MSME becomes competitive only when it uses such signals to improve  decisions.
4.2 Digital Literacy as the Foundation of Managerial Digital Capability
The second theme confirms that digital literacy is a foundational managerial capability. Entrepreneurs with stronger digital literacy
are better able to evaluate platform c redibility, interpret analytics, manage digital payment risks, protect accounts, respond to
algorithmic changes, and compare the effectiveness of digital channels. Recent studies link literacy and digital capability w ith
sustainability, business transformation, financial inclusion, and entrepreneurial performance (Al -Shami et al., 2024; Costa Melo et
al., 2023; Desmaryani et al., 2024; Martínez-Peláez et al., 2024; Mick et al., 2024; Raharjo et al., 2024; Sagala & Őri, 2024; Wardana
et al., 2023; Zahoor et al., 2023).
Digital literacy also mediates the psychological side of transformation. Entrepreneurs with limited digital confidence may av oid
experimentation, outsource critical decisions without understanding them, or abandon platforms when early results a re
disappointing. Capability development should therefore include confidence building, not only technical instruction. A human -
centred approach recognises that learning occurs gradually and must be connected to the actual problems faced by MSME owners.
4.3 Innovation Capability as a Conversion Mechanism
The third theme explains why digital adoption alone is insufficient. Digital platforms increase visibility, but visibility do es not
automatically create durable competitiveness. Innovation capability convert s digital exposure into value creation. Customer
feedback can trigger product redesign, analytics can guide new variants, delivery problems can lead to process improvement, a nd
online competition can stimulate stronger branding and differentiation.
This interpretation is supported by studies showing that digitalisation contributes to innovation and performance when firms can
transform digital resources into new products, improved processes, and business-model renewal (da Silva et al., 2025; Hassan et al.,
2024; Jiao et al., 2025; Larios-Francia & Ferasso, 2023; Radicic & Petkovic, 2023; Romero & Mammadov, 2025; Valdez-Juárez et
al., 2024; Wang et al., 2023). In practical terms, innovation for MSMEs does not always require expensive research and
development. It can start with better packaging, clearer product bundles, more reliable delivery information, digital payment
integration, or more responsive after-sales service.
4.4 Ecosystem Support as an Enabling Condition
The fourth theme highlights that MSME digital transformation is not only an internal business issue. Ecosystem support shapes the
ability of entrepreneurs to use digital tools productively. Infrastructure, logistics, financing, certification, marketplace rules, digital
payment systems, training quality, and mentoring networks influence whether digital transformation becomes a practical opportunity
or an additional burden.

4.5 Strategic Integration as the Basis of Sustainable Competitiveness
The fifth theme is the integrative finding. Digital marketing strategy, digital literacy, innovation capability, and ecosystem su pport

5. DISCUSSION
5.1 Theoretical Contributions
This study offers three theoretical contributions to the literature on MSME competitiveness in the digital economy. First, it advances
an integrated capability-based interpretation of MSME digital competitiveness. Previous studies often examine digital marketing
strategy, digital literacy, and innovation capability as separate predictors of SME or MSME performance. This study argues th at
these constructs should be understood as interdependent capabilities rather than isolated managerial activities. Digital marketing
creates market interaction and customer information, digital literacy enables entrepreneurs to interpret and use digital info rmation
responsibly, and innovation capability converts this information into product, process, service, and business -model improvement.
Second, the study extends the resource -based view by clarifying that digital tools themselves rarely constitute rare or inimitable
resources for MSMEs. Social media accounts, marketplace stores, digital payment systems, and communication applications are
widely accessible and easily imitated. Their strategic value emerges only when they are embedded in disciplined routines, customer
learning, trust-building practices, data interpretation, and continuous innovation. Therefore, the competitive resource is not platform
adoption, but the organisational capability to combine digital tools with market understanding and adaptive value creation.
Third, the study contributes to dynamic capability theory by specifying a sequential yet interactive logic of MSME digital re newal.
Digital marketing strategy functions as a sensing capability, digital literacy functions as a seizing capability, innovatio n capability
functions as a reconfiguring capability, and ecosystem support functions as an enabling condition. This interpretation shows that
MSME competitiveness is revitalised not through technology adoption alone, but through the continuous renewal of capabilities.
5.2 Practical Implications for MSME Owners
For MSME owners, digital competitiveness should be built through simple but consistent routines. First, entrepreneurs should define
target customers and value propositions before selecting platforms.  Second, digital content should be managed as a trust -building

Fourth, basic digital literacy should include security, payment safet y, privacy, and analytics. Fifth, innovation should be treated as

Small improvements can produce meaningful competitiveness. Examples include redesigning product photos, improving packaging,

5.3 Policy and Institutional Implications
For policy makers, MSME digitalisation programmes should shift from quantity -based onboarding to capability -based outcomes.
Rather than measuring only the number of MSMEs registered on platforms, programmes should evaluate whether MSMEs can
maintain content consistency, manage customer response, improve product quality, protect digital accounts, use payment system s
safely, and convert online interaction into sales and innovation.
Universities can play a strategic role through MSME digital clinics, applied student projects, product photography laboratories,
marketplace mentoring, business analytics assistance, cybersecurity awareness, and short practical modules. Such initiatives can
translate academic knowledge into community impact while giving students real entrepreneurial learning experience. Business
associations and local governments can complement this by providing sector-specific mentoring and peer-learning communities.
5.4 Indonesian Contextualisation of MSME Digital Competitiveness
The Indonesian MSME context gives this study distinctive practical and theoretical relevance. MSMEs in Indonesia are not a
homogeneous group. They operate across diverse sectors such as culinary products , batik, fashion, craft, retail, services,
agribusiness, and household -based production. Their levels of digital readiness also differ substantially depending on location,
owner capability, access to infrastructure, logistics, financing, mentoring, and mar ket networks. As a result, digital transformation
cannot be designed as a uniform programme for all MSMEs.
In Indonesia, many MSMEs rely on practical and low -cost digital channels such as WhatsApp Business, Instagram, Facebook,
TikTok, Shopee, Tokopedia, G oogle Business Profile, and digital payment systems such as QRIS. These tools are relatively
accessible, but their effective use requires more than account ownership. MSME owners need the ability to prepare clear produ ct
descriptions, produce credible visu al content, respond quickly to inquiries, manage customer complaints, protect digital accounts,
calculate promotional costs, evaluate platform performance, and maintain service reliability after transactions occur.
Therefore, MSME digital competitiveness i n Indonesia should not be measured only by the number of enterprises connected to
digital platforms. More meaningful indicators include content consistency, customer response quality, transaction safety, rep eat
purchase, customer trust, product improvement , service innovation, profitability, and market expansion. This contextual
interpretation reinforces the central argument of the study: digital transformation becomes competitive only when it is trans lated
into practical, repeated, and capability-enhancing business routines.
5.5 Limitations and Future Research
This study has limitations. First, it is a qualitative documentary study, so it does not measure the statistical strength of relationships
among constructs. Second, although the corpus prioritised rec ent, traceable, and reputable sources, journal quartile status and
indexing status may change across databases and years; therefore, authors should verify quartile status again before formal
submission. Third, the study synthesises literature and policy documents rather than collecting primary interview data from MSME
owners.
Future research can conduct interviews across sectors such as culinary, fashion, batik, craft, services, and manufacturing to examine
how entrepreneurs experience digital transformatio n in daily practice. A sequential mixed -methods design may also be useful.
Qualitative interviews can identify lived experiences and barriers, while survey-based SEM can test the relationships among digital
marketing strategy, digital literacy, innovation capability, ecosystem support, and competitiveness quantitatively. Future studies
may also compare whether the capability system works differently across urban and rural MSMEs, export -oriented and domestic
firms, and product-based and service-based sectors.

6. CONCLUSION
This study concludes that the competitiveness of Indonesian MSMEs in the digital economy is revitalised through an integrated
capability system consisting of digital marketing strategy, digital literacy, innovation capability, and ecosyste m support. Digital
marketing strategy enables MSMEs to sense market opportunities, communicate value, engage customers, and collect feedback.
Digital literacy enables entrepreneurs to interpret digital information, manage platform risks, protect transactio ns, and make more
informed managerial decisions. Innovation capability converts digital interaction into improvements in products, processes, services,
