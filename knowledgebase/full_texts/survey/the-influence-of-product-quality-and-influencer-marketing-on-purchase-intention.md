ABSTRACT
The accelerating digital transformation of the fashion industry has considerably intensified
competitive dynamics among local brands, rendering product quality, influencer marketing, and
consumer engagement increasingly instrumental in shaping purchase int ention. This study
examines the effects of product quality and influencer marketing on consumers’ purchase
intention toward the Karung Jantan brand, while positioning customer engagement as a
moderating construct. A quantitative research approach was adopt ed, involving Generation Z
consumers in Bali selected through purposive sampling. Primary data were elicited through a five-
point Likert -scale questionnaire and subsequently examined using Partial Least Squares
Structural Equation Modelling (PLS -SEM) with SmartPLS 4.1. The analytical framework
incorporated measurement -model evaluation, structural -model assessment, and bootstrapping
procedures for hypothesis verification. The findings indicate that product quality and influencer
marketing both exert positive and statistically significant effects on purchase intention. Customer
engagement further demonstrates a significant moderating role by amplifying the relationship
between product quality and purchase intention, as well as intensifying the influence of influencer
marketing on purchase intention. The model also exhibits substantial explanatory and predictive
capability. These findings accentuate the importance of an integrated marketing paradigm that
synergises superior product quality, strategically calibra ted influencer marketing, and sustained
customer engagement to foster stronger consumer purchase intention and reinforce the
competitive positioning of local fashion brands.
Keywords: Product Quality, Influencer Marketing, Customer Engagement, Purchase Intention

INTRODUCTION
The global fashion industry has undergone substantial expansion, driven by
evolving lifestyles, technological innovation, digitalisation, and trend -oriented
consumption (Nabillah et al., 2023). In In donesia, fashion represents a prominent
creative-economy sector, while digitalisation has intensified market competition and
transformed consumer purchasing behaviour, particularly among Millennials and
Generation Z. Kadafi et al., (2023) contend that the proliferation of local fashion brands
necessitates increasingly adaptive strategies to accommodate heterogeneous consumer
preferences, while Arhofa and Andarini (2023) highlight the transformative role of

al., (2024) demonstrate that influencer reputation can shape followers' pe rceptions and

THEORY BASIS
Ajzen (1991) formulates the Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) as an elaboration
of the Theory of Reasoned Action, asserting that behavioural intention is principally
determined by three interrelated antecedents: attitude toward the behaviour, subjective
norm, and perceived behavioural control. Within the present study, product quality
represents consumers’ evaluative attitudes toward the product, whereas influencer
marketing encapsulates social influence conveyed through recommendations and
persuasive endorsements. Perceived behavioural control refers to consumers’ perceptions
regarding the accessibility and feasibility of acquiring Karung Jantan products. Customer
engagement is incorporated as a moderating construct, reflecting the extent to which
consumers’ active involvement with the brand may amplify the effects of product quality
and influencer marketing on purchase intention.
Kotler and Keller (2012) in Aini and Andjarwati (2020) conceptualise product
quality as a product’s inherent capacity to perform i n accordance with, or exceed,
consumers’ anticipated expectations. Mulia (2020) further delineates product quality as
the degree to which a product is capable of accommodating consumers’ needs,
preferences, and expectations. Accordingly, product quality en compasses the extent to
which a product successfully fulfils both its functional requirements and experiential
expectations. Mullins et al., (2008) in Kumrotin et al., (2021) identify several fundamental
dimensions of product quality, encompassing performa nce, durability, conformance to
specifications, features, reliability, aesthetics, and perceived quality.
Influencer marketing constitutes a contemporary promotional strategy that
leverages individuals possessing substantial social visibility and persuasiv e influence to
endorse brands or products through digital platforms (Agustin and Amron, 2022). Chopra
et al., (2021) in Seruni et al., (2024) describe it as a collaborative marketing practice
between brands and influential individuals whose prominence with in social media or
particular fields enables them to shape audience perceptions and responses. According to
Agustin and Amron (2022) in Ashari et al., (2024), influencer marketing encompasses
visibility, credibility, attractiveness, and power.

Sharma (2022 ) in Zulhaidar (2024) conceptualises customer engagement as an
interactive process encompassing consumers' emotional, cognitive, and physical
involvement with a company through product or service experiences. Bening and
Kurniawati (2019) in Hanifah (2024) similarly characterise it as a psychological state
emerging from intensive consumer –business interactions and experiences. Customer
engagement therefore represents the depth of consumers' relational involvement with a
brand. Razmus (2021) in Lisdayanti and  Nilasari (2025) identifies three dimensions:
cognitive engagement, emotional engagement, and behavioural engagement.
Kotler and Keller (2009) in Caniago and Rustanto (2022) define purchase intention
as a behavioural response reflecting consumers' inclination and willingness to purchase a
particular product. Swastha (2019) in Lestari (2024) further associates purchase intention
with consumers' prospective commitment to undertake a purchase. According to
Ferdinand (2006) in Luthfiyatillah et al., (2020), pur chase intention comprises
transactional intention, referential intention, preferential intention, and exploratory
intention.

METHOD
This research adopts a quantitative methodological framework involving
Generation Z consumers in Bali who have been exposed to or engaged with Karung
Jantan’s social-media content. Respondents were selected through purposive sampling,
while primary data were obtained by administering a structured questionnaire employing
a five-point Likert scale through Google Forms. The empirical data were subsequently
processed using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling (PLS -SEM)
facilitated by SmartPLS 4.0 . The evaluation procedure comprised an assessment of the
measurement model, including convergent validity, discriminant validity, and construct
reliability, followed by an examination of the structural model based on the coefficient of
determination (R²) and predictive relevance (Q²). Hypothesis verification was performed
through the bootstrapping procedure, with inferential decisions determined by T-statistics
and P-values using a 5% significance threshold.

RESULTS
Validity and Reliability Testing
SmartPLS 4.1 results confirm that all indicators achieved outer loadings above 0.70,
substantiating satisfactory convergent validity. HTMT coefficients below 0.90 further
affirm adequate discriminant validity. Reliability indices were exceptionally strong, with
Cronbach’s Alpha ranging from 0.915 –0.957 and Composite Reliability from 0.942 –
0.965. These findings collectively attest to the measurement model’s high psychometric
rigour, internal coherence, and measurement dependability.
Structural Model Evaluation
The structural model produced an R² value of 0.533, indicating that product quality,
influencer marketing, customer engagement, and their moderating effects collectively
explain 53.3% of the variance in purchase intention. The remaining 46.7% is attributable
to other factors beyond the proposed model. Furthermore, the Q² value of 0.400 confirms
satisfactory predictive relevance, demonstrating that the model possesses adequate
explanatory and predictive capacity in interpreting consumers’ purchase intention toward
Karung Jantan products.
Hypothesis Testing
Based on the empirical analysis conducted using SmartPLS 4.1 and the
bootstrapping technique, the hypothesised  relationships among the constructs can be
interpreted as follows:
1. Hypothesis 1 (Product Quality → Purchase Intention)
The structural-model estimation indicates that product quality has a positive and
statistically significant association with purchase intention. The obtained path
coefficient is positive, while the corresponding t -statistic exceeds the critical
threshold and the P-value remains below the 5% significance level. This evidence
confirms that favourable perceptions of product quality are accompanied by
stronger consumer purchase intention toward Karung Jantan products. The
statistical evidence therefore provides  sufficient grounds for accepting the first
hypothesis.
2. Hypothesis 2 (Influencer Marketing → Purchase Intention)
The empirical findings reveal a positive and statistically significant relationship
between influencer marketing and purchase intention. The e stimated path

3. Hypothesis 3 (Customer Engagement × Product Quality → Purchase Intention)
The moderation analysis demonstrates that customer engagement significantly

4. Hypothesis 4 (Customer Engagement × Influencer Marketing → Purchase

The results further establish that customer engagement significa ntly amplifies the

indicates a positive moderating direction, accompanied by a t -statistic exceeding
the critical threshold and a P -value below the accepted significance leve l. This
finding suggests that influencer marketing becomes more influential in generating
purchase intention when consumers maintain stronger and more sustained
engagement with the brand. The fourth hypothesis is therefore empirically
supported, confirming  the reinforcing role of customer engagement within the
proposed relationship.
Discussions
The empirical evidence confirms that product quality positively and significantly
influences purchase intention. Consumers’ favourable perceptions of performance,
durability, specification conformity, and overall quality can strengthen their inclination
to purchase Karung Jantan products. Within the Theory of Planned Behavior, product
quality contributes to the development of positive attitudes toward purchasing behaviour,

thereby reinforcing behavioural intention (Ajzen, 1991). This result is consistent with
findings from Susanto and Realize (2022), Pramesti et al., (2024), Mohd et al., (2013) in
Sundari and Lailla (2025), and Puspitasari (2016) in Riyanto and Satinah (2023).
Influencer marketing also demonstrates a positive and significant effect on purchase
intention. Influencers perceived as credible, appealing, and persuasive can strengthen
consumers’ willingness to consider and purchase Karung Jantan products. Endorsements,
product demonstrations, and authentic consumption narratives can enrich consumers’
informational resources and reduce uncertainty during product evaluation. From the TPB
perspective, this mechanism corresponds primarily with subjective norm, as  influential
social figures can shape consumers’ perceptions of socially supported purchasing
behaviour (Ajzen, 1991). The finding is supported by Al-Mu’ani et al., (2023), George et
al., (2025), Lengkawati and Saputra (2021) in Agustin and Amron (2022), M arsha Putri
(2024), and Agustini et al., (2022).
Customer engagement significantly strengthens the relationship between product
quality and purchase intention. Consumers who actively interact with brand content,
examine product information, communicate fee dback, and participate in brand -related
activities tend to develop more favourable and informed evaluations of product quality.
Such involvement enhances the persuasive relevance of quality perceptions and
subsequently reinforces purchase intention. In TPB , customer engagement can intensify
attitude formation by encouraging deeper cognitive evaluation of product attributes
(Ajzen, 1991). This result is consistent with Nabillah et al., (2023) and Hunowu et al.,
(2023.
Customer engagement likewise strengthens  the relationship between influencer
marketing and purchase intention. Greater consumer involvement increases receptiveness
to influencer-generated information because highly engaged consumers are more inclined
to seek, process, and interact with brand -related communications. This sustained
interaction can heighten message salience and facilitate the internalisation of influencer
recommendations. From the TPB perspective, engagement can reinforce subjective norm
by intensifying consumers’ exposure to social ly mediated information and persuasive
cues (Ajzen, 1991). These findings are aligned with Hidayati et al., (2024), Hariyanti and
Wirapraja (2018) in Seruni et al., (2024), and Israr et al., (2025).

CONCLUSION
The findings of this study demonstrate that purchase intention toward the Karung
Jantan brand is significantly influenced by product quality and influencer marketing.
Higher product quality and more effective influencer marketing can substantially enhance
consumers’ propensity to purchase. Customer engagement further strengthens both
relationships, indicating that active consumer involvement amplifies the effectiveness of
product quality and influencer marketing in stimulating purchase intention. Therefore,
Karung Jantan should pursue an integrated marketing strategy that combines consistent
product improvement, effective influencer utilisation, and sustained customer
engagement to cultivate stronger consumer purchase intention.
