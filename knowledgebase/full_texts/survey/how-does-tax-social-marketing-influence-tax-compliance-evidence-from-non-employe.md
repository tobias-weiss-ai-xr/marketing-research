ABSTRACT: Tax compliance among non -employee individual taxpayers remains a challenge, highlighting the need for more
effective tax social marketing strategies. This study examines the effect of tax social marketing on tax compliance through t he
mediating role of attitude toward tax compliance among non -employee individual taxpayers in Palembang, Indonesia. A
quantitative survey was conducted using purposive sampling with 126 valid responses. Data were analyzed using Partial Least
Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM) with SmartPLS 4. The results indicate that tax social marketing has a significant
positive effect on attitude toward tax compliance. Attitude toward tax compliance, in turn, has a significant positive effect  on tax
compliance. However, tax social marketing has no significant direct effect on tax compliance, indicating that attitude toward  tax
compliance fully mediates the relationship between tax social marketing and tax compliance. Overall, the evidence suggests th at
tax social marketing promotes voluntary tax compliance not through a direct behavioral effect, but by first fostering positiv e
attitudes toward tax compliance, which subsequently encourage voluntary compliance. This underscores that the effectiveness
of tax social marketing lies not merely in disseminating tax -related information, but in shaping taxpayers' positive attitudes
toward tax compliance. Furthermore, this study provides empirical support for the application of Social Marketing Theory and
the Theory of Planned Behavior by demonstrating that attitude toward tax compliance serves as the key psychological
mechanism through which tax social marketing influences voluntary tax compliance among non -employee individual taxpayers.

INTRODUCTION
Tax revenue constitutes one of the primary sources of government income and plays a crucial role in financing public
expenditure and supporting national development. In Indonesia, the self -assessment system places taxpayers at the center of
tax administration by requiring them to calculate, pay, and report their own tax obligations. Under this system, voluntary
compliance becomes essential to ensure the effectiveness of tax collection. However, achieving and maintaining high levels of
tax compliance remains a persistent challenge, particularly among non -employee individual taxpayers who are responsible for
fulfilling their tax obligations independently.
Compared with employee taxpayers, non -employee individual taxpayers generally face more complex tax responsibilities. They
are required to maintain records of their income, calculate tax liabilities, generate billing codes, make tax payments, and s ubmit
annual tax returns without direct withholding mechanisms. Such conditions may increase the likelihood of errors, non -
compliance, or delays in fulfilling tax obligations. Therefore, encouraging tax compliance among this group requires not only
regulatory enforcement but also effective educational and communication strategies capable of fostering positive perceptions
and attitudes toward taxation.
In public sector organizations, social marketing has increasingly been adopted as a strategy to encourage voluntary behaviora l
change through education and persuasive communication rather than coercive enforcement. To address this challenge, the
Directorate General of Taxes (DGT) has implemented various tax education and communication initiatives aimed at improving
taxpayers' awareness and compliance. These initiatives include tax education programs, social media campaigns, webinars, tax
classes, official websites, and other outreach activities. From a social marketing perspective, such initiatives represent ef forts to

extends beyond information dissemination by seeking to shape knowledge, attitudes, and behavioral change through the

Previous research has produced mixed evidence regarding the effectiveness of tax education and communication initiatives in
improving tax compliance. Some studies suggest that educational campaigns encourage taxpayers to comply voluntarily. For
instance, Mascagni et al. (2024) showed that taxpayer education programs improved compliance by reducing compliance
barriers and strengthening taxpayers' understanding of their tax obligations. Likewise, Kurnia and Khomsiyah (2023) reported
that digital services, social media exposure, and tax knowledge were associated with higher compliance among younger
taxpayers.
Nevertheless, other studies indicate that greater exposure to tax information does not always lead directly to compliant
behavior. Although education and communication may increase awareness and understanding, these improvements do not
necessarily translate into immediate behavioral change. This inconsistency suggests that the influence of tax social marketin g
may occur indirectly through taxpayers' internal psychological processes rather than through a direct behavioral response.
The Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) (Ajzen, 1991) offers a theoretical explanation for this process. TPB proposes that behav ior
is shaped by attitude, subjective norms, and perceived behavioral control. Among the three determinants, attitude reflects an
individual's overall evaluation of performing a particular behavior and is expected to influence subsequent behavioral decisi ons.
In the taxation context, taxpayers who perceive tax compliance as beneficial, important, and socially responsible are more li kely
to fulfill their tax obligations. Accordingly, attitude is expected to function as an important mechanism through which tax s ocial
marketing influences tax compliance.
Compared with previous studies that mainly emphasized tax knowledge, tax sanctions, or service quality, the present study
adopts a social marketing perspective. It conceptualizes tax education and communication programs implemented by the
Directorate General of Taxes (DGT) as tax social marketing strategies. Rather than viewing these initiatives simply as channe ls for
disseminating information, they are regarded as strategic interventions designed to influence taxpayers' attitudes and
encourage voluntary tax compliance.
Despite extensive research on tax compliance, relatively little attention has been given to the mediating role of attitude in
explaining how tax social marketing affects compliance behavior, particularly among non -employee individual taxpayers. Existing
studies have largely focused on the direct effects of tax education, communication, or awareness, leaving the underlying
psychological mechanism insufficiently explored. In addition, empirical evidence on non -employee individual taxpayers in
Indonesia remains scarce, even though this group has distinctive characteristics and faces different compliance challenges fr om
employee taxpayers.
To address these gaps, this study investigates the influence of tax social marketing on tax compliance among non -employee
individual taxpayers in Palembang, Indonesia, while examining the mediating role of attitude toward tax compliance. Tax socia l
marketing is operationalized using the Social Marketing Mix, comprising product, price, place, and promotion, to represent ta x
education and communication initiatives implemented by the Directorate General of Taxes.
This study contributes to the literature in three ways. First, it broadens the application of Social Marketing Theory within the
taxation context by examining tax social marketing among non -employee individual taxpayers. Second, it extends the Theory of
Planned Behavior by demonstrating that attitude toward tax compliance explains how tax social marketing translates into
compliance behavior. Finally, the findings offer practical insights for tax authorities in designing communication and educat ional
strategies that not only deliver tax information but also cultivate positive taxpayer attitudes that support sustainable volu ntary
tax compliance.

benefit of individuals and society rather than commercial profit (Kotler & Zaltman, 1971). Unlike commercial marketing, which
focuses on financial outcomes, social marketing aims to promote socially desirable behaviors through communication,

communication initiatives to encourage voluntary tax compliance.
Consistent with Lee and Kotler (2019), tax social marketing is operationalized using the Social Marketing Mix, comprising
product, price, place, and promotion.  Product represents the desired behavioral outcome, namely voluntary tax compliance,
together with the value taxpayers perceive from complying with their tax obligations. Price represents the psychological,
cognitive, and practical barriers perceived by taxpayers when fulfilling their tax obligations. Place refers to the accessibi lity of tax

Theory of Planned Behavior
The Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) proposes that an individual's behavior is primarily driven by behavioral intention, whic h is
shaped by attitude, subjective norms, and perceived behavioral control (Ajzen, 1991). Among these determinants, attitude
represents an individual's overall evaluation of a particular behavior as favorable or unfavorable.  Communication and
educational interventions may shape individuals' behavioral beliefs, which subsequently influence their attitudes toward
performing that behavior. In the taxation context, tax social marketing functions as such an intervention by shaping taxpayer s'
beliefs and evaluations regarding tax compliance. Therefore, attitude is viewed as the key psychological mechanism through
which tax social marketing influences tax compliance. In this study, attitude toward tax compliance is operationalized through
cognitive, affective, and conative dimensions based on the multidimensional attitude framework developed by Rosenberg and
Hovland (1960).
Tax Compliance
Tax compliance refers to taxpayers' willingness to fulfill their tax obligations accurately, completely, and within the requi red time
frame in accordance with applicable tax regulations. Contemporary tax compliance literature emphasizes that voluntary
compliance depends not only on enforcement but also on taxpayers' trust in tax authorities and their perceptions of fairness and
transparency (Kirchler et al., 2008). Under the Slippery Slope Framework, voluntary compliance is strengthened when taxpayers
perceive tax authorities as trustworthy, transparent, and supportive.  These factors highlight that voluntary compliance is
influenced not only by enforcement mechanisms but also by taxpayers' psychological evaluations of the tax system.
Accordingly, communication strategies that foster trust and positive taxpayer attitudes are expected to promote higher levels  of
tax compliance among non-employee individual taxpayers.

METHODOLOGY
This study employed a quantitative survey design to examine the effect of tax social marketing on tax compliance among non -
employee individual taxpayers in Palembang, Indonesia, with attitude toward tax compliance serving as a mediating variable.
The study population comprised 35,182 registered non -employee individual taxpayers (DGT, 2024). Using purposive sampling,
126 valid responses were collected through a structured questionnaire with an eight -point semantic differential scale.
Respondents were required to be registered non -employee individual taxpayers residing or conducting economic activities in
Palembang and to have been exposed to at least one tax social marketing initiative, such as tax education programs or digital tax
communication, within the previous year. For descriptive purposes, respondents were also asked to report the frequency of
their exposure during the previous three months.  The sample size exceeded the minimum requirement for PLS -SEM analysis
based on the recommendation of Hair et al. (2022). Tax social marketing was measured using the Social Marketing Mix
dimensions (product, price, place, and promotion), while attitude toward tax compliance was assessed through cognitive,
affective, and conative dimensions. Tax compliance was measured based on respondents' perceived compliance behavior. Data
were analyzed using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM) with SmartPLS 4. The measurement and
structural models were evaluated, and hypotheses were tested using the bootstrapping procedure.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
A total of 126 valid responses were included in the analysis. The sample was relatively balanced by gender, comprising 66 fem ale
respondents (52%) and 60 male respondents (48%). Respondents aged over 35 years constituted the largest age group (34.9%),
followed by those aged 26 –30 years (30.2%) and 31 –35 years (22.2%), indicating that the respondents were predominantly
within the productive working-age population.
A bachelor's degree was the most common educational qualification  (59%), while entrepreneurs represented the largest
occupational group (32%), followed by professionals (21%), individuals with multiple occupations (16%), freelancers (15%), an d
other occupations (16%). This distribution reflects the diversity of non -employee individual taxpayers in Palembang,
encompassing entrepreneurs, professionals, freelancers, and individuals with multiple sources of income.

DISCUSSION
The empirical findings indicate that tax social marketing plays a significant role in shaping taxpayers' attitudes toward tax
compliance. This result suggests that tax education, digital communication, taxpayer assistance, and other communication
initiatives implemented by the Directorate General of Taxes contribute to more positive taxpayer attitudes toward tax
compliance. Consistent with Social Marketing Theory, these findings support the argument that strategically designed
communication initiatives influence beliefs, perceptions, and attitudes before encouraging voluntary behavioral change (Kotle r
& Zaltman, 1971; Andreasen, 1995) . In this study, the social marketing mix, consisting of product, price, place, and promotion,
collectively contributes to shaping taxpayers' attitudes toward tax compliance . Taxpayers’ attitudes are influenced not only by
the content of tax messages, but also by perceived benefits, psychological barriers, accessibility of tax information, and th e
credibility of communication channels.
These findings are aligned with the Theory of Planned Behavior (Ajzen, 1991), which explains that attitude is formed through
individuals’ beliefs and evaluations of a particular behavior. In the taxation context, clear, relevant, and accessible tax
information may strengthen taxpayers’ beliefs that tax compliance is important, beneficial, and socially responsible. This re sult
supports previous studies by Hanum et al. (2024), Utami et al. (2025), Syahrial and Harefa (2025), Hanif et al. (2025), and
Hidayati and Sanulika (2024), which found that tax education, communication quality, message credibility, and digital tax
campaigns contribute to positive taxpayer attitudes.
The results also confirm that attitude has a significant positive effect on tax compliance. This finding supports the Theory of
Planned Behavior, which suggests that positive attitudes toward a behavior encourage individuals to perform that behavior.
Taxpayers who perceive tax compliance as beneficial, important, and worthwhile are more likely to voluntarily fulfill their tax
obligations. This is particularly relevant for non -employee individual taxpayers, who must calculate, pay, and report their taxes
independently. The result is in line with Hanum et al. (2024), Shaharuddin et al. (2023), Himawan and Widyasari (2021), and
Wicaksono et al. (2024), who found that positive taxpayer attitudes significantly encourage compliance behavior.
However, the direct effect of tax social marketing on tax compliance was not significant. This suggests that exposure to tax
education, campaigns, or digital communication does not automatically lead to compliant behavior. Taxpayers may understand
and respond positively to tax messages, but such exposure may not immediately translate into actual actions such as calculati ng,
paying, and reporting taxes. The findings suggest that although respondents generally perceived tax social marketing positively,
information exposure alone was insufficient to directly influence compliant behavior. Taxpayers may recognize the benefits of
taxation and respond positively to tax campaigns, yet such responses do not automatically translate into actual compliance
unless they are accompanied by positive attitudes toward tax compliance.
The insignificant direct effect supports the view that social marketing works through a gradual behavioral change process. This
finding supports the proposition that behavioral change is preceded by changes in taxpayers' beliefs, evaluations, and attitu des
rather than occurring immediately after exposure to tax -related communication . This finding is consistent with Azizah et al.
(2025), Kurnia and Khomsiyah (2023), and Mascagni et al. (2024), who showed that tax education and digital communication do
not always directly increase compliance, but may work through changes in knowledge, perception, and internal evaluation.
The most important finding of this study is the significant mediating role of attitude. Tax social marketing significantly in fluences
tax compliance through attitude, while its direct effect on compliance is not significant. The results demonstrate that attitude
toward tax compliance functions as the primary psychological mechanism through which tax social marketing influences
voluntary tax compliance. In other words, tax social marketing becomes effective when it successfully shapes taxpayers’ positive
attitudes toward compliance. This finding strengthens the integration of Social Marketing Theory and the Theory of Planned
Behavior by showing that external communication strategies influence compliance behavior through internal psychological
evaluation.
Overall, these findings advance the understanding of tax compliance behavior by demonstrating that tax social marketing does
not directly influence tax compliance but operates through attitude toward tax compliance. The study provides empirical
support for integrating Social Marketing Theory with the Theory of Planned Behavior, showing that attitude toward tax
compliance functions as the key psychological mechanism through which external communication strategies translate into
voluntary tax compliance among non-employee individual taxpayers.
From a practical perspective, these findings suggest that the effectiveness of tax social marketing depends not merely on
increasing the volume or frequency of communication, but on its ability to cultivate positive attitudes toward tax compliance .
Tax authorities should therefore design communication strategies that emphasize the benefits of taxation, reduce perceived
complexity and psychological barriers, strengthen trust in tax administration, and provide accessible and credible tax

CONCLUSIONS
This research investigated the role of tax social marketing in promoting tax compliance among non -employee individual
taxpayers in Palembang, Indonesia, while considering attitude toward tax compliance as a mediating mechanism. The results
indicate that tax social marketing significantly strengthens taxpayers' attitudes, and favorable attitudes subsequently enhan ce
tax compliance. However, no significant direct relationship was found between tax social marketing and tax compliance. Instead,
its effect is transmitted indirectly through attitude toward tax compliance, demonstrating that taxpayers' psychological
evaluations are essential for translating tax social marketing into voluntary tax compliance. Overall, this study demonstrate s that
the effectiveness of tax social marketing depends not on communication alone, but on its ability to cultivate positive attitu des
toward tax compliance. By providing empirical evidence for the integration of Social Marketing Theory and the Theory of
Planned Behavior, this study confirms that attitude toward tax compliance functions as the key psychological mechanism linkin g
tax social marketing to voluntary tax compliance among non -employee individual taxpayers.  From a practical perspective, the
findings suggest that tax authorities should prioritize communication strategies that shape taxpayers' attitudes rather than
merely increasing the dissemination of tax-related information.

LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE RESEARCH
This research has several limitations that should be considered when interpreting the findings. First, the analysis focused
exclusively on non -employee individual taxpayers in Palembang, limiting the extent to which the findings can be generalized to
other regions in Indonesia. Second, this study examined attitude as the primary mediating mechanism, although other
psychological and institutional factors, such as trust in tax authorities, tax morale, and perceived fairness, may also influ ence tax
compliance. Finally, the cross -sectional nature of the research captures taxpayers' perceptions at a single point in time and
therefore cannot explain how attitudes and compliance evolve over time.
Future research could broaden the geographical coverage, include larger and more diverse samples, and incorporate additional
explanatory variables to develop a more comprehensive understanding of tax compliance behavior. Longitudinal designs would
also be valuable for examining how tax social marketing shapes taxpayers' attitudes and compliance over time. In addition,
further research may compare the effectiveness of different tax social marketing channels, particularly digital platforms, in
influencing taxpayers' attitudes and compliance. Given that Instagram was identified as the respondents' primary source of tax -
related information, future studies could compare the effectiveness of Instagram with other digital platforms, such as TikTok ,
YouTube, and the official DGT website, in shaping taxpayers' attitudes and compliance behavior.  Additionally, future studies may
also explore which types of educational content, communication formats, and message characteristics generate the greatest
taxpayer engagement, including views, interactions, and feedback. Such insights could assist the Directorate General of Taxes  in
developing and implementing  more adaptive, engaging, and audience -oriented tax social marketing strategies that strengthen
positive attitudes toward tax compliance and ultimately promote voluntary tax compliance.
