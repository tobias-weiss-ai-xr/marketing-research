Conceptual papers do not materialize spontaneously, rather,
they require careful effort and thoughtful reflection. Writ -
ing such papers necessitates a deep familiarity with a broad
range of literature that provides a fertile ground for ideas
to emerge and develop. During this reflective process, new
ideas may be reformulated, reconnected, and recontextual -
ized from older ones. The process can lead to the revitaliza-
tion or evolution of existing theories, the discovery of novel
conceptual insights, and the development of new theories.
Occasionally, new ideas may “stand in the light by them -
selves, only faintly connected to the old ideas” (Salomone,
1993, p. 74). Regardless of the type of theoretical outcome,
conceptual papers can push marketing researchers to con -
sider novel ways of thinking about the phenomena they
investigate that can propel new understandings.
All scholarly papers should provide evidence to sup -
port their conceptual assertions, though the nature of this
evidence varies (Vargo & Koskela-Houtari, 2020). Hence,
the primary purpose of conceptual papers is to advance
theory development rather than report empirical testing.
Such papers seek to bridge existing theories in novel ways,
link work across disciplines, offer multi-level insights and
broaden the scope of our thinking (Goldberg, 2015, p. 128).

Introduction
Pursuing novel marketing concepts and precisely delin -
eating their conceptual breadth and depth is crucial for
developing new marketing theories. In doing so, market -
ing scholars must consider their relationship to existing
concepts, positioning these new ideas within the context of
established theories and managerial practices. Developing
conceptual papers enhances the marketing field by review -
ing current knowledge, identifying tensions and inconsis -
tencies, highlighting significant gaps, offering new insights,
and proposing future research agendas. This process results
in theoretical contributions that “refine, reconceptualize,
or even replace existing ways of viewing a phenomenon”
(Hulland, 2020, p. 27). Thus, we investigate how authors
can navigate the journey from conceptual idea to conceptual
article.

Abstract
Exploring and articulating novel marketing concepts are essential for developing new marketing theories. Conceptual
papers enhance the marketing field by reviewing current knowledge, identifying inconsistencies, highlighting gaps, offer -
ing insights, and proposing future research agendas. We investigate how scholars can transform conceptual ideas into
robust articles by providing a guide for marketing researchers with five key strategies or beacons for overcoming chal -
lenges in conceptual writing. This year, winners of the 2024 AMS Review–Sheth Foundation Doctoral Competition for
Conceptual Articles (DoCCA) are presented.

The beacons in conceptual writing: A guide for
researchers
Writing conceptual papers is often challenging, and we pro-
pose using a set of five beacons to guide scholars in navi -
gating these difficulties. Beacons serve as reference points,
providing guidance and inspiration for writing theory-based
papers, much like a lighthouse or beacons help a ship navi -
gate safely through stormy weather. We propose a series of
five of these beacons to aid authors seeking to successfully
navigate the review process, which we will detail in the fol-
lowing sections (see Fig. 1). While starting and ending with
Beacons I and V , respectively, Beacons II, III, and IV need
to be worked through iteratively.
Beacon I: Prospecting for topics and ideas
Conceptual research topics can be found in the world
around us, an author’s passions, and academic and
industry literature. Marketing scholars need to be
curious about why responses or behaviors occur in
consumers, or how certain marketing strategies work
better in certain conditions. Moreover, passions are a
great place to start as authors already have a keen sense
of what are the important trends and who is influential
or a contributor, among other key information. Where
this information is not readily available to an author,
they can turn to academic and industry literatures to
prospect for topical information or further inspiration
(e.g., future research avenues). We advise not restrict-
ing your literature searches to solely the academic
marketing, branding, or advertising literature but also
reaching out and discovering what other academic dis-
ciplines or practitioners think are important avenues
for scholarly inquiry.
After finding an interesting research topic, narrowing
down that broader topic into a conceptual idea that
is researchable and important can be challenging for
even experienced authors, however, we suggest a few
things to keep in mind that we borrowed from writing
on theory development (Steward & Zinkhan, 2006;
Sutton & Staw, 1995; Weick, 1995; Kaplan, 1964;
Merton, 1967). First, the conceptual idea could feasi -
bly explain a “why,” that is, rationalize the connection
between phenomena through a logical narrative of
causation. Second, the idea could lead to a theory that

Beacon II: Looking backwards to move forwards
As the metaphor of standing on the shoulders of giants
teaches, scholars need to integrate the logic and insights
that already exist in the academic literature when attempt -
ing to generate a new theory. There are many possible meth-
ods that authors could use to review the literature (Snyder,
2019), including systematic (Hulland & Houston, 2020;
Munn et al., 2018; Greenhalgh et al., 2005; Tranfield et al.,
2003), narrative (Greenhalgh et al., 2005), scoping (Pham et
al., 2014; Munn et al., 2018), bibliometric (Hulland, 2024;
Donthu et al., 2021), or meta-analysis (Hulland & Houston,
2020; Chan & Arvey, 2012) reviews. However, regardless
of which review method is chosen, authors should take care
to also review seminal articles, books, or other contributions
that are well cited in concert with cutting edge recent contri-
butions that have yet to produce a measurable impact (i.e.,
not a lot of citations). This is no small feat for an author
or even a team of authors to review contributions, compile
their logic and insights, and then analyze these findings. We
advise authors to be organized to systematize the literature
review process, and perhaps create literature tables to both
synthesize and integrate the author’s analysis of the relevant
literature.
Beacon III: Systematizing your conceptual thinking
Armed with a deep understanding of the past literature, con-
ceptual scholars need to formalize their logic. While they
are not theory alone (Sutton & Staw, 1995), visual depic -
tions that simplify the logical narrative add much to both the
readability and interpretability of the conceptual argument.
These visual depictions often take the form of simple box-
and-arrow models or figures that illustrate causal relation -
ships between concepts or variables, however, authors may
choose a more elaborate method of visualization as long as
it does not distract from the central logical narrative.
As the aphorism, the perfect is the enemy of the good
cautions that researchers need to carefully balance parsi -
mony against comprehensiveness or complexity when for -
mally conceptualizing (Deshpande, 1983). This is true both
for written logical statements and visual illustrations, as the
central conceptual narrative may get lost in the minds of
readers if there are too many concepts or linkages between
concepts.
Beacon IV: Telling your conceptual story
Engaging with radical, critical, or controversial ideas can
enrich the conceptual paper by challenging established
norms and provoking thought. When incorporating such
elements, it is crucial to ground the arguments in robust the-
oretical frameworks and empirical evidence, ensuring that
the propositions are stimulating and credible. This approach
highlights the innovative nature of the conceptualization. It
situates it within a dynamic scholarly debate, encouraging
readers to reassess their assumptions and perspectives by
addressing contentious issues head-on, a critical discourse,
pushing the boundaries of the field, and fostering an environ-
ment where academic growth and transformative insights
are possible. Acknowledging potential counterarguments
and engaging with contrasting viewpoints can strengthen
the position, demonstrating a comprehensive and balanced
analysis. This methodological rigor and openness to debate
can make the conceptual paper an original contribution to
ongoing scholarly conversations.
To effectively convey the substance of the conceptual
paper, the narrative needs to be carefully crafted, meaning
that the story should be clear, compelling, and engaging to
the reader from the beginning. Start by first situating the
research within the broader context of the existing literature,
highlighting gaps or paradoxes that the paper intends to fill.
This helps to understand the significance and originality
of the work. The theme should guide the structure of the
paper, ensuring that every section contributes to building a
cohesive argument. Avoid unnecessary jargon and strive for
clarity to make complex ideas accessible to the reader. Use
transitional phrases to ensure the logical flow and coherence
and signposts to guide the reader through the argument,
making it easy for them to follow the reasoning. Moreover,
using illustrative examples can enhance the understanding
of the conceptual paper. Finally, reflect on the implications
of the findings, how they contribute to the field, and suggest
avenues for future research. This underscores the relevance
of your study and invites further scholarly inquiry, position-
ing it as a springboard for ongoing academic dialogue.

paper should articulate how it contributes to the specific aca-

Conclusions
From the above discussions of the beacons for conceptual
writing, we now turn to the DoCCA 2024. We had 31 con -
ceptual submissions that were considered for the competition
that endured a rigorous review process. Table 1 recognizes
the six winning submissions of the DoCCA 2024, provides a
one-line summary, and outlines why it is a winner. The win-
ning topics were from diverse areas of marketing thought,
namely, B2B marketing management, inventor-marketing
strategies, marketing leadership, marketing technologies,
digital consumer behavior, and consumer culture. Consider-
ing calls for marketing research for society (e.g., de Ruyter
et al., 2022), winning submissions have the potential to
have significant social impact, that is, ensure well-being
in B2B marketing, nurture innovations to commercial suc -
cess, transform brands for social good through leadership,
humanizing artificial intelligence marketing, unpacking the
mysteries of digital behavior, and understanding the fluid
nature of identities and products. While only six submis -
sions could win, see Table 1 for a profounder description,
we also selected six runners-up listed here by the submitting
doctoral student in alphabetical first name order: Abhishek
Nirjar (Texas Tech University, USA), David Goyeneche
(University of Queensland, Australia), Khushboo Vaishnav
(IFHE University Hyderabad, India), Md Al Amin (Queen
Mary University, United Kingdom), Rindratiana Rahaja -
son (ESCP Business School, France), and Xing “Jerry” Jin
(University of New South Wales, Australia). Overall, we are
impressed with both the diversity and scope of all submis -
sions and wish these aspiring marketing theoreticians well
in their future pursuits!
