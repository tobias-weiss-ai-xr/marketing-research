Introduction: The sports industry, particularly Olympic sports, requires
systematic support programs for the development of marketing activities.
This study aimed to design a policy model for supportive programs of sports
organizations in Iraq regarding marketing activities in Olympic sports, with a
case study of swimming.
Methodology: This study employed a sequential exploratory mixed -method
approach. In the qualitative phase, the systematic grounded theory method
was utilized, and data were collected through semi -structured interviews
with 22 experts. In the quantitative p hase, the extracted model was tested
using a researcher -made questionnaire and structural equation modeling
with Smart-PLS software on a sample of 384 participants.
Findings: The final model indicated that supportive policymaking (the core
phenomenon) comprises two main categories: "supportive programs" and
"marketing activities." This phenomenon is influenced by causal conditions
(managerial, economic, socio-cultural, and educational factors). The extracted
strategies are shaped by contextual and intervening conditions, leading to
outcomes such as swimming development, strengthening international
relations, and socio -economic transformations. The quantitative results also
confirmed the model's fit (GOF=0.433).
Conclusion: The proposed model can serve as a guiding framework for Iraqi
sports policymakers towards systematic development of marketing and
attracting effective support in Olympic sports.

1- Introduction
 The industrialization of sport in the last century has made it one of the largest industries in the world
(Mullin et al., 2007). Today, sports marketing, as a global revenue -generating industry, contributes
to the sustainable development of sports organizations through tools such as ticket sales, television
rights and sponsorship ((Cayolla et al., 2023). However, taking advantage of these opportunities
requires the existence of coherent policies and support programs. (Hums et al., 2023).

Despite its high potential in sports and a young population, Iraq faces several challenges in the
development of Olympic sports such as swimming. Lack of financial resources, poor infrastructure,
and lack of effective support programs are among the obstacl es that prevent the full exploitation of
the marketing potential of these disciplines (Al -Mhanna et al., 2023). Previous research has also
shown that the lack of clear policies and institutional support is one of the most important obstacles
to the growth of championship sports in developing countries (Gammelsæter, 2021).
Swimming, as one of the most popular Olympic sports, has unique characteristics that can achieve
significant growth with appropriate investment in marketing. However, the lack of a local model for
support policy-making has caused marketing activities in this field to be carried out in a scattered and
inefficient manner. Therefore, the present study was conducted with the aim of designing a policy -
making model for the support programs of Iraqi sports organizations for the marketing activities of
swimming.
2. Theoretical foundations and research background
 2-1. Sports marketing and sponsorship
Sports marketing includes all activities designed to satisfy the needs and wants of sports customers
through an exchange process (Moline, 2007). Pitts and Statler (1996) define sports marketing as a
process of planning and implementing activities for the product, pricing, promotion, and distribution
of a sports product.
Sponsorship is defined as the provision of contributions such as money, people, and equipment to an
individual or organization by a commercial organization with the aim of achieving commercial
objectives (Trail, 2020). The IEG Sponsorship Report found that  sport accounted for 69% of all
sponsorship spending (IEG, 2003).
2-2. Research Background
Domestic research has shown that several factors affect sports marketing. Esmailinejad et al. (1403)
identified the components of innovative marketing strategies. Hessami et al. (1403) presented a
model of motivational marketing in sports. Nasrawi et al. (1402) identified the components of sports
marketing capabilities in Iraqi football.
In foreign research, Gupta et al. (2024) showed the impact of sports marketing on increasing brand
awareness. Kapinen et al. (2023) examined the business behaviors of sports entrepreneurs. Lee and
Walsh (2011) presented a combined SWOT and AHP model for sports marketing outsourcing.
A summary of the background shows that no comprehensive research has been conducted so far in
the field of marketing support programs for sports organizations, especially in Iraq and in the field of
swimming.

3-2. Qualitative section
Population and Sample: Participants included 22 experts in the field of sports policy and marketing
in Iraq, who were selected using purposive and snowball sampling methods.
Data collection tool: in-depth and semi-structured interviews.
Data analysis: Open, axial, and selective coding stages were performed. To ensure the reliability of
the findings, criteria of acceptability, transferability, confirmability, and reliability were used (Lincoln
& Guba, 1985). Test-retest reliability was 87.9%.
3-3. Quantitative section
Population and sample : The statistical population included managers, experts, coaches and active
athletes in the field of swimming in Iraq. The sample size was estimated to be 384 people who were
selected by stratified random sampling method.
Data collection tool: A 99-item researcher-made questionnaire with a five-point Likert scale.
Data analysis : Validity and reliability were confirmed using factor loadings, Cronbach's alpha,
composite reliability, and convergent and divergent validity. Smart -PLS 3 software was used to test
the structural model.

5. Discussion and Conclusion
5-1. Discussion
The aim of the present study was to design a policy model for the support programs of sports
organizations in Iraq from the marketing activities of Olympic sports disciplines with a case study of
swimming. This study, using a sequential mixed exploratory a pproach and using the data -driven
theory method in the qualitative part and structural equations in the quantitative part, has identified

and validated the dimensions, components, and causal relationships affecting this area. The research
findings indicate that the policy of support programs, as a multidimensional phenomenon, is
influenced by a set of causal, contextual, and intervening factors, and adopting appropriate strategies
in the governance, financial, cultural, and educational dimensions can lead to desirable outcomes
such as the sustainable development of swimming, the promotion of its international status, and the
prosperity of the sports economy. In the following, each of the research questions is examined and
interpreted separately.
The research findings showed that the policy -making pattern of support programs is influenced by
causal conditions (managerial, economic, socio -cultural, and educational factors). This finding is
consistent with the research of Andronicano (2021), which em phasizes the role of transparency in
good governance, and Gamisater (2021), which points to the effectiveness of management systems.
Management factors, including transparency in decision -making, efficiency of administrative
systems, and the presence of managers with sufficient expertise, play a key role in formulating
supportive policies. The economic situation and financing are also key challenges, which is consistent
with the research of Pianqua (2022) and Gosselin et al. (2020).
Cultural and social conditions, including public attitudes toward sports, play a significant role in
marketing success. This finding is consistent with research by Dilnoza (2023) and Oronbayo (2023).
The educational system is also consistent with research by Liu et al. (2023) and Lucier (2023).
Contextual conditions (organizational structure, laws and regulations, socio -cultural context) and
intervening conditions (leadership, financial, and cultural challenges) influence strategies. This
finding is consistent with research by Hames et al. (2023) and Mir and Garcia (2021).
Extractive strategies include governance -supervision, financial -resource, socio -cultural, and skills
development strategies, which are consistent with the research of Dees et al. (2022) and Howard et
al. (2024).v
The consequences of this model include the growth and development of swimming, strengthening
international relations, social changes, and strengthening the sports economy, which is in line with
the research of Golbova et al. (2024) and Zhou et al. (2023).
This study designed a comprehensive model for policymaking support for swimming in Iraq. Based
on the findings, causal, contextual, and intervening factors influence the strategies and outcomes of
this area. The presented model can be used as a guiding fra mework for Iraqi sports policymakers to
systematically develop marketing and attract effective support for Olympic sports.
Implementing this model requires coordination between sports institutions, the government, the
private sector, and society. Sports organizations must prepare the ground for attracting financial and
human capital by reviewing their management structures and utilizing new marketing methods. The
government also plays a vital role in achieving these goals by enacting supportive laws and providing
the necessary infrastructure.

5.3. Limitations and Suggestions
Limitations: Focus on swimming and the country of Iraq, specific socio -economic conditions at the
time of the research, and lack of relevant research background.
Practical suggestions:
• Holding specialized training courses for sports managers
• Developing transparent support laws to attract investors
• Implementing awareness campaigns to promote the culture of swimming
• Establishing specialized sports marketing training centers
Research suggestions:
• Comparative study of support policies of successful countries
• Examining the role of digital technologies in the marketing of Olympic sports
• Deeper study of socio-cultural barriers
