ABSTRACT

Multi-tier loyalty programs (MTLPs) have become a dominant strategic instrument for
customer retention and value creation, yet the literature remains fragmented with limited
systematic synthesis. This study aims to comprehensively review and map the resear ch
landscape of MTLPs by identifying core dimensions, antecedents, outcomes, moderating
factors, and research gaps. Employing a Systematic Literature Review (SLR) following
PRISMA 2020 protocol integrated with bibliometric analysis using VOSviewer, this
research systematically searched Scopus, Web of Science, and Google Scholar for
publications from 2022 to 2026. From 1,247 initial records, 29 articles met the inclusion
criteria for final synthesis. The analysis reveals eight interrelated dimensions of MTLP s
Tiered Structure, Differential Rewards, Customer Segmentation, Dynamic Engagement,
Perceived Value & Aspirational Goals, Retention Targeting, Spending Pattern Evolution, and
Aspirational Status & Psychological Ownership  which are influenced by five antecedents
(Consumer Behavior, Service Design, Marketing Strategy, Digital Technology, and Brand
Perception) and moderated by three factors (Industry Context, Consumer Demographics,
and Digital Infrastructure). Five primary ou tcomes were identified: Customer Retention,
Customer Lifetime Value, Brand Loyalty, Share of Wallet, and Word-of-Mouth & Advocacy.
Bibliometric mapping uncovers six critical research gaps: limited longitudinal studies,
insufficient psychological mechanism understanding, unexplored AI/ML integration, lack of
evidence-based tier structure optimization, minimal cross -cultural analysis, and under -
researched unintended consequences including loyalty fatigue and privacy concerns. This
study contributes an integrated conceptual framework connecting MTLPs dimensions with
their antecedents, moderators, and outcomes, offering both theoretical enrichment for
academic discourse and evidence -based practical guidelines for program designers across
industries.

Keywords: Multi-tier loyalty programs, tiered loyalty programs, customer retention,
customer lifetime value, systematic literature review, bibliometric analysis

In a contemporary business landscape characterized by intense competition and ever-
increasing customer acquisition costs, loyalty programs have evolved from mere
transactional instruments to strategic pillars in customer relationship management. This
phenomenon is increasingly emerging as it is found that retaining existing customers can be
five times more cost -effective than acquiring new customers (Pereira et al., 2025a)  . In the

MTLPs categorize customers into tiered hierarchies ranging from the basic level

likely to make a repeat purchase when they feel valued (M+C Saatchi Performance, 2026) .
These findings indicate that tiered loyalty programs are not just nice-to-have, but a strategic
imperative for businesses looking to build a sustainable competitive advantage.
Although its popularity has soared with the average American consumer joining 15.5
loyalty programs in 2024, an increase of 10% compared to 2022, the engagement rate
actually decreased by 10% in the same period(M+C Saatchi Performance, 2026). This irony
highlights the critical gap between program design and its actual effectiveness. The
fundamental question that arises is: what factors determine the success of a tiered loyalty
program, and how its operational mechanisms can be optimized to drive long-term customer
retention and increased customer lifetime value (CLV).
Various studies have attempted to answer this question from various perspectives .
(Viswanathan et al., 2022)  examined the effect of status in MTLPs using a quantitative
approach and found that tiers had a significant effect on  customers' share of wallets, but the
impact was moderated by tenure and intensity of past purchases . On the other hand, (Zeng
et al., 2022a)  explores communication strategies in MTLPs and shows that  different
feedback framing on accumulation versus residual progress is effective for different tiers .
Recent study by (Wu et al., 2026)  even revealed that the tier structure with the number of
tiers must be adjusted to the brand position; Luxury brands benefit more from a multi -tier
structure, while non-luxury brands are better suited to a simpler structure.

THEORETICAL REVIEW

Theoretical Foundation of Multi-Level Loyalty Programs
Multi-tier loyalty programs (MTLPs) are rooted in the intersection of three main
theoretical frameworks: hierarchy of needs theory, social exchange theory, and social status
theory. In a hierarchical perspective, MTLPs tap into the human need for self -recognition
and actualization by  creating a tiered structure that allows customers to feel progress
(Maslow, 1943; Alderfer, 1969). Each tier from Bronze to Platinum represents the
achievement of higher status, which in turn triggers an intrinsic motivation to continuously
increase transaction value in order to gain exclusive recognition . Social exchange theory
(Blau, 1964 ) complements this framework by explaining that the customer -brand
relationship is based on a benefit-cost calculus; When customers feel that the rewards they
receive (room upgrades, lounge access, personal concierge) are comparable to or exceed
their investment (annual spending), affective loyalty is naturally formed. Meanwhile, the
theory of social status (Veblen, 1899; Bourdieu, 1984) provides a sociological lens through
which tier membership serves as a communicable status signal to the social environment,
reinforcing  the perceived value of the program beyond its functional benefits.
More specifically, the effectiveness of MTLPs in driving tier migration is explained
by goal-setting theory (goal-setting theory; Locke & Latham, 2002) dan teori dissonansi
kognitif (Festinger, 1957). Tier structures with clear entry criteria ($0 -5,000 for Bronze to
$40,000+ for Platinum) create concrete goals that guide customer buying behavior. When a
customer is on the verge of a tier upgrade for example, it has spent $38,000 of the target
$40,000 goal gradient  effect (Hull, 1932) Accelerating production to achieve higher
status(Kemp & Dłużewska, 2026) . On the other hand, the theory of cognitive dissonance
explains why customers who have reached the high tier are more likely to maintain or even
increase their spending: the inconsistency between the status of the tier that has been
achieved and the declining purchasing behavior creates psychological discomfort that must
be resolved by remaining loyal. This phenomenon is reinforced by the status quo bias
(Samuelson & Zeckhauser, 1988) which makes customers reluctant to lose the privileges
that have been enjoyed, so the churn rate in the premium tier is relatively lower than the
basic tier.
From an operational perspective, MTLPs are constructed through four key
mechanisms: tier progression system, reward accumulation and redemption, personalized
communication, and gamification integration. The tier progression system manages status
tracking, qualification rules, and anniversary management to create urgency and reduce tier
abandonment. Accumulation and redemption rewards are designed to maximize perceived
value through  different earning rates per tier (10-15 points/USD) and increased redemption
values ($0.01 -$0.015/points). Personalized communication ensures that the message
delivered is relevant to the tier of each Bronze customer receiving the boost to achieve
Silver, while Platinum customer s receive exclusive recognition through the relationship
manager. Gamification, with elements of milestones, leaderboards, and reward surprises,
serves as a catalyst to increase engagement frequency  and extend the customer lifecycle in
the program. All of these mechanisms are supported by an ecosystem of partnerships with

RESEARCH METHODS

This study applies  a systematic literature review  (SLR) approach integrated with
bibliometric analysis to comprehensively examine the dimensions, antecedents, and
outcomes of tiered loyalty programs. The research design follows the PRISMA protocol to
ensure transparency and minimization of bias at every stage of the literature review (Belloti
et al., 2019; KUNOW & LANGER, 2021). The selection of the SLR method is based on its
ability to synthesize findings spread into a structured knowledge framework (Nobari et al.,
2021), While bibliometric analysis through co-occurrence mapping , co-authorship, and
citation analysis allows the identification of evolutionary trends as well as research fronts
in the corpus of literature (Belloti et al., 2019) . The combination of these two approaches
allows research to answer not only "what is known" but also "how that knowledge develops
and where it is headed in the future".
Literature searches are carried out systematically through the Scopus database, Web
of Science, and Google Scholar with a publication range of 2022-2026. Search strategies use
a combination of Boolean keywords: ("tiered loyalty program" OR "multi -tier loyalty" OR
"loyalty program tier") AND ("customer retention" OR "lifetime value" OR "brand loyalty")
AND ("antecedent" OR "outcome" OR "moderator"). The selection process is carried out in
four stages according to PRISMA. An initial search yielded 1,247 articles; After the removal
of duplication there were 835 articles that underwent  title and abstract screening, resulting
in 127 potential article s. The full-text assessment phase  excluded 98 articles with details:
42 articles were not tiered loyalty programs, 31 articles did not have a measure of customer
commitment, 15 articles did not have  a social identification component, and 10 articles were
outside the context of consumer behavior. Finally, 29 articles were included in the final
synthesis. This rigorous exclusion process ensures that only studies of substantial relevance
and adequate methodological quality are analyzed, in line with recommendations (Mohit et
al., 2026).
The inclusion criteria include articles that: (1) discuss loyalty programs with a tier
structure of at least two levels; (2) measure outcome dimensions  such as retention, CLV, or
brand loyalty; (3) identify the antecedents of program effectiveness; and (4) published in
indexed journals or reputable proceedings. Exclusion criteria include: (1) studies without
tier structure; (2) employee loyal ty or B2B studies; (3) opinion articles without empirical

DISCUSSION

The results of the thematic analysis of the 29 selected articles identified eight key
dimensions of multi -layered loyalty programs that are interrelated and form an integrated
loyalty ecosystem. These eight dimensions can be grouped into three strategic la yers:
structural foundations, operational mechanisms, and psychological outcomes.

Structural Foundations: Tiered Structure and Customer Segmentation
The Tiered Structure Dimension   is a fundamental architecture that distinguishes
MTLPs from conventional loyalty programs. This multi -level hierarchical architecture
categorizes customers based on transaction value and engagement, creating a clear
progression path from the basic tier to the premium tier. (Viswanathan et al., 2022) confirms
that tiers in MTLPs have a significant causal effect on  customers' share of wallets , with
stronger effects on customers with shorter membership periods. The study quantified  the
intangible effects of status and found that tenure moderated the relationship between tier
and purchasing behavior.
The Customer Segmentation dimension  complements the structural foundation by
partitioning customers based on behavioral, transactional, and psychographic variables for
personalization. The RFM ( Recency, Frequency, Monetary ) approach, behavioral
clustering, and customer lifetime value scoring  allow programs to effectively target
communications per segment (Lo et al., 2017) . This is in line with the findings that the
symbolic benefits of tier memberships such as status recognition and taste have been strong
determinants of program satisfaction and loyalty (Javanian, 2026; M+C Saatchi
Performance, 2026).

Operational Mechanism: Reward, Engagement and Retention
The Differentiated Reward dimension operates through different reward catalogs per
tier, points accumulation mechanisms, and redemption options. The widening benefit gap
between tiers such as exclusive lounge access, suite upgrades, and personal concierge for
premium tiers is designed to create increased perceived value (Javanian, 2026) emphasizes
that the effectiveness of rewards depends on alignment between program design and
customer preferences(Kim & Baker, 2020).The Dynamic Engagement dimension  integrates
gamification, communication, and  experiential touchpoints to create ongoing motivation.
Elements such as milestone tracking , progress visualization, and leaderboards take
advantage  of the goal gradient effect of a psychological phenomenon where customers
increase efforts as they approach the next tier target. (Zeng et al., 2022b)  Shows that
different feedback framing strategies  accumulate versus residual progress are effective for
different tiers. In the context of restaurant mobile apps, gamification enhances customer
experience and foster loyalty through need satisfaction and value co -creation, with a user
engagement mechanism driven by motivational affordances that meet autonomy,
competence, and relatedness needs based on Self -Determination Theory (Sutrisno et al.,
2026b)
The Retention Targeting dimension  implements a tier -based differentiated retention
strategy, using churn prediction models and identification of at -risk customers. Loyalty
programs have been shown to be effective in encouraging short -term behavioral loyal ty,
especially among elite tier members, but their effectiveness in building long-term emotional
loyalty still requires the deepening of affective relationships. In hierarchical loyalty
programs, customers who experience point expiration show higher anger levels than
demotion status, and point extension strategies are more effective than status extension
strategies in reducing perceived unfairness, especially among endowed -status customers
who show higher embarrassment and perceived unfairness(Shin & Casidy, 2021).

Psychological Outcomes: Perceived Value and Aspiration Status
The Perceived Value & Aspirational Goals dimension  focuses on creating a perceived
value gradient that drives tier migration. A strategically designed benefit gap between tiers
creates aspirational goals that motivate increased spending . (Wu et al., 2026)  reveals that
tier structures should be adjusted to the position of luxury brands are more likely to benefit
from multi-tier structures, while non-luxury brands are more suited to simpler structures.
The Aspiration Status & Psychological Ownership dimension  complements the
framework by encouraging emotional loyalty through the fulfillment of the need for
recognition and belongingness. Tier status has behavioral implications that go beyond
purchase transactions, reinforcing  the perceived value of a program beyond its functional
benefits(Hamdani et al., 2023).

CONCLUSION

This study concludes that multi-tier loyalty programs (MTLPs) are built on eight main
interrelated dimensions of Tiered Structure, Differentiated Rewards, Customer
Segmentation, Dynamic Engagement, Perceived Value & Aspirational Goals, Retention
Targeting, Spending Pattern Evolution, and Aspiration Status & Psychological Ownership
which form an integrated loyalty ecosystem. The effectiveness of MTLPs does not only
depend on financial incentives alone, but also on meeting customers' psychological needs
for recognition, belonging, and self-actualization through status signaling mechanisms and
goal gradient effects. The success of the program is measured through five  main outcomes
Customer Retention, Customer Lifetime Value (CLV), Brand Loyalty, Share of Wallet, and
Word-of-Mouth & Advocacy which are influenced by five antecedents (Consumer Behavior,
Service Design, Marketing Strategy, Digital Technology, and Brand Perception) and
moderated by Industry Context, Consumer Demographics, and Technology Infrastructure.
Bibliometric analysis revealed that MTLPs research still shows significant
fragmentation with six  main research gaps: limitations of longitudinal research, inadequate
understanding of psychological mechanisms, limited exploration of AI/ML integration, lack
of optimal tier structure design guidelines, lack of cross -cultural analysis, and lack of
attention to negative con sequences such as loyalty fatigue  and privacy concerns. This
research contributes to the development of loyalty program theory by presenting an
integrated conceptual framework that connects the eight dimensions of MTLPs with their
antecedents, moderation factors, and outcomes, while providing practical guidance for
program designers in various industries.

SUGGESTIONS

For practitioners, four main things are recommended: (1) optimization of tier structure
based on the position of luxury brands is more advantageous to a multi -tier structure (4+
tiers), while non -luxury brands are more suitable for 2 -3 tiers (Wu et al., 20 26); (2)
investment in technology and personalization, including blockchain and AI for real -time
scalability and personalization  (Matysik, 2025); (3)  psychology-based reward design with
symbolic and experiential elements to build psychological ownership and emotional loyalty
(Hamdani et al., 2023); and (4)  mitigation of loyalty fatigue by maintaining the simplicity
and flexibility of the program, considering that the expiration point can trigger anger higher
than the status of demotion  (Shin & Cassidy, 2021). For the next researcher, the
recommended research agenda includes 5 -10 years longitudinal studies, exploration of
psychological mechanisms, AI/ML integration, optimization of tier structure design, cross -
cultural research in non-Western markets, as well as investigation of negative consequences
such as status reinforcing bias and loyalty fatigue to produce ethical and sustainable loyalty
program design guidelines.
