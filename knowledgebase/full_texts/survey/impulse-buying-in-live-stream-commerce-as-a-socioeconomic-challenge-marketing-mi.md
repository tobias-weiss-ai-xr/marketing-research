Abstract: Impulse buying in live -stream commerce has become one of the emerging
socioeconomic challenges of digital markets in developing economies, as real -time
promotional pressure and emotionally charged selling may expand unplanned household
spending and heighten consumer vulnerability. Research on these socioeconomic challenges
has so far concentrated on isolated live -stream cues and single psychological mechanisms
such as trust, flow or involvement, leaving the joint operation of seller -controlled people,
product, price and promotion cues through parallel cognitive and affective channels
unexplained, particularly in Bangladesh. The aim of this study is to determine how four
marketing-mix stimuli shape consumers' cognitive and affective states and, through these
organismic states, impulse buying within the stimulus –organism–response framework. The
empirical basis is an online survey of Bangladeshi live -stream shoppers conducted from
January to March 2026, which yielded 368 valid responses from 404 questionnaires,  with
55.71 percent male respondents, 56.79 percent aged 18 –25, 54.89 percent students and
44.84 percent holding a postgraduate degree. Data were gathered through a self -
administered questionnaire of 22 five -point Likert items under convenience sampling an d
processed with confirmatory factor analysis, covariance -based structural equation
modeling and bias -corrected bootstrapping with 5,000 resamples in Analysis of Moment
Structures 26 and Statistical Package for the Social Sciences 26. The measurement model
showed an acceptable  fit, with a chi -square to degrees of freedom ratio of 2.304, a
comparative fit index of 0.953 and a root mean square error of approximation of 0.060,
while composite reliability ranged from 0.776 to 0.938. Affective state was more strongly
associated with impulse buying than cognitive state, with standardized coefficients of 0.469
and 0.288, whereas price (0.462) and people (0.343) were the strongest predictors of
affective state and promotion (0.331) of cognitive state, the model explaining 50.8, 23.8 and
37.2 percent of the variance in affective state, cognitive state and impulse buying.
Bootstrapped mediation confirmed significant indirect effects of price (0.217) and people
(0.161) through affective state and of promotion (0.095) through cognitive state , whereas
product showed a negative indirect effect on impulse buying through cognitive state of
−0.047, with a 95 percent confidence interval from −0.053 to −0.012. The findings indicate
that price and people showed the strongest positive indirect associations with  impulse
buying through affective state, and they offer live -stream retailers an evidence base for
designing transparent pricing and promotional communication while opening avenues for
future research on socioeconomic challenges using probability samples, longitudinal designs
and behavioral transaction data.

INTRODUCTION

Live-stream commerce has transformed online
shopping from static product search into an
interactive, real -time, and socially embedded retail
experience, with “shoppertainment” in the Asia Pacific
region projected to become a 1 trillion United States
dollar (USD) opportunity ( TikTok, 2022 ). In contrast
to conventional e -commerce, live -stream retailing
allows consumers to watch product demonstrations,
build rapport through communication with streamers,
receive real-time information on the products they are
interested in, and respond to time -sensitive selling
cues throughout their shopping journey ( Lu & Chen,
2021; Wongkitrungrueng & Assarut, 2020 ;
Wongkitrungrueng et al., 2020 ). This interactive
environment heightens social presence, touchpoints,
and emotional involvement in the product category,
which allows consumers to make rapid and impulsive
purchase decisions (Hu & Chaudhry, 2020; Ming et al.,
2021). Alongside its commercial benefits, this shift
raises socioeconomic concerns related to consumer
vulnerability, household spending, and the growing
use of psychologically aggressive marketing
techniques. Live-stream platforms have consequently
attracted research attention as retail environments
that can encourage impulse buying through social
interaction, scarcity information, promotional
intensity, and emotional pressure ( Huo et al., 2023 ;
Lee & Chen, 2021; Lo et al., 2022).
In Bangladesh, social media platforms such as
Facebook have become a widely used channel for
online business and shopping ( Miah et al., 2022), thus
presenting an emerging -market context appropriate
for investigating live -stream impulse buying. Social
commerce has become an important component of the
digital economy in Bangladesh, influencing consumer
purchasing practices and creating new oppor tunities
for online retailers and emerging businesses ( Miah et
al., 2022 ). Live-stream commerce is especially
pertinent to these contexts because it merge s live
product demonstrations, interaction between seller
and consumer, and immediacy in purchasing via e -
commerce platforms and social networking sites like
Facebook Live ( Lee & Chen, 2021 ). Here, consumers
tend to rely on streamers' reputation and product
introduction as well as price and/or value information
from fellow users and promotional cues to alleviate
uncertainty in the purchase decision -making process
(Lu & Chen, 2021 ). Bangladesh therefore offers a
relevant emerging -market setting for examining
consumers’ cognitive and affective responses.
Beyond marketing implications, impulse buying in
live-stream commerce represents an emerging
socioeconomic concern, particularly in developing
economies where digital consumption is rapidly
expanding. The interactive and persuasive nature of
live-stream pl atforms may encourage unplanned
purchasing decisions through social influence, scarcity
cues, and emotional stimulation, potentially affecting
household consumption patterns and financial
decision-making ( Chan et al., 2017 ; Good & Hyman,
2020; Zhang et al., 2024 ). Therefore, examining the
psychological mechanisms underlying impulse buying
is important for understanding responsible digital
consumption in emerging markets.
Recent studies have indicated that live -stream
commerce has fundamentally changed online retail by
integrating real -time interaction, entertainment,
social presence, and immediate purchasing
opportunities, thereby shortening the consumer
decision journey a nd increasing impulsive purchase
tendencies (Luo et al., 2024; Zhang & Zhang, 2024).
Based on the stimulus–organism–response (S-O-R)
framework, this paper delineates live -stream
marketing mix elements as external stimuli,
consumers' cognitive and affective states as internal
organismic responses, and impulse buying as a
behavioral response  (Huo et al., 2023 ; Lee & Chen,
2021; Ming et al., 2021). This framework is well suited
to live -stream shopping. Consumers are exposed to
real-time marketing signals, social presence, product -
related information, sales promotion, and interactive
communication, which may activate both rational
evaluation and em otional arousal and subsequently
encourage impulse purchases ( Gong & Jiang, 2023 ;
Huo et al., 2023; Ming et al., 2021).
This study therefore examines how live -stream
marketing-mix stimuli (people, product, price, and
promotion) relate to impulse buying through
consumers’ cognitive and affective states in the
Bangladesh context. The study seeks to provide insight
into socioe conomic challenges associated with live -

LITERATURE REVIEW
Live-Stream Commerce, Impulse Buying, and the S-
O-R Framework
Live-stream commerce is a two-way form of digital
retail that combines real -time video presentations
with social interaction, product demonstrations, and
instant purchase opportunities. In contrast to
traditional e-commerce, where consumers largely rely
on still images and textual summaries, live -stream
shopping allows consumers to observe products in
use, ask streamers questions in real time, interact with
presenters and respond immediately to promotional
cues. Under the S -O-R framework, Lee and Chen
(2021) examined live -stream commerce directly,
while Lu and Chen (2021)  showed that purchase
decisions may depend on reduced uncertainty in live -
streaming settings. Beyond its business applications,
live-stream commerce has attracted scholarly
attention because of its potential socioeconomic
challenges, including effects on h ousehold
consumption, consumer vulnerability and
psychologically intensive marketing in digital
contexts.
Impulse buying is a central feature of live -stream
commerce, involving rapid, spontaneous and largely
unplanned purchasing decisions. Previous studies
have shown that social presence, streamer presence,
product information, interaction quality, sales
promotion and flow experience are associated with
impulse buying in live -stream environments (Gong &
Jiang, 2023; Huo et al., 2023; Ming et al., 2021).
The S -O-R model is widely used to explain how
external environmental signals influence consumers’
internal psychological states and subsequent
behavioral responses. In live -stream commerce, such
external stimuli may include streamer characteristics,
product presentation, viewer interaction, price offers
and promotional intensity. These stimuli affect
consumers’ internal organismic states ( Jacoby, 2002;
Mehrabian & Russell, 1974 ), which in live -stream
commerce may include cognition, emotion, trust,
perceived value, enjoyment and flow (Huo et al., 2023;
Lee & Chen, 2021; Ming et al., 2021).
S-O-R has been used in studies to explain live -
stream impulse buying by making a connection
between external cues and internal consumer states
with spontaneous purchasing consequences. Recent
live-stream commerce studies have continued to
validate the appl icability of the S -O-R framework by
demonstrating that external live -stream cues
influence internal psychological states ( Chen et al.,
2024; Feng et al., 2024 ; Li et al., 2022 ). These
organismic states may subsequently stimulate
impulse buying behavior in live -stream commerce (Li
et al., 2024 ; Zhang et al., 2024 ). Lee and Chen (2021)
used this S-O-R framework to describe impulse buying
in the live-stream commerce context.
Ming et al. (2021)  indicated that social presence
and telepresence affect trust and flow state, which
then leads to impulse buying. Huo et al. (2023)  also
researched the impact of live -streaming stimuli on
impulse buying based on internal consumer responses
using the S-O-R model.
Grounded in this framework, the current research
conceptualizes people, product, price and promotion
as live -stream marketing stimuli. Consumers’
cognitive and affective states are treated as organismic
responses, while impulse buying is treated as the
behavioral response. This framework is appropriate
because live -stream shopping exposes consumers to
real-time marketing triggers that may activate both
rational evaluation and emotional arousal before
purchase decisions are made. Although the S -O-R
model has been widely used in live-stream-commerce
research, previous studies have mainly examined
selected psychological mechanisms, including trust,
flow, involvement and consumer engagement. The
simultaneous examination of cognitive and affective
states therefore remains relatively under -researched,
especially in emerging-market contexts.
Marketing Mix Stimuli in Live-Stream Retailing
Marketing-mix elements, conventionally defined as
seller-controllable variables such as product, price,
place and promotion ( Kotler & Keller, 2016 ) and
extended in the services marketing mix to include
people ( Ho et al., 2022 ), are relevant in live -stream
retailing because they constitute cues that consumers
experience directly while watching a live shopping
session. Prior research has increasingly acknowledged

People, product, price and promotion were

retailing. The people element refers to streamers or
presenters who communicate with consumers,
demonstrate products and strengthen viewers’
confidence in what they observe.
Product cues include product quality, usefulness,
performance, explanation and demonstration. Price -

money and time -limited pricing. Time -limited
promotions, scarcity persuasion and price perceptions
can intensify consumers’ urgency and stimulate
impulsive purchase decisions in live -stream
commerce (Feng et al., 2024; Qu et al., 2023).
Promotion cues include discounts, giveaways, free
delivery, flash sales and other short -term incentives.
Together, these stimuli form the immediate marketing
environment that consumers assess, respond to
emotionally and act upon in live -stream shopping
(Gong & Jiang, 2023; Ho et al., 2022; Huo et al., 2023).
Empirical evidence on how these four marketing -
mix elements jointly influence consumer behavior in
Bangladesh’s live -stream-commerce context remains
limited.
Marketing Mix Stimuli and Cognitive State
In live -stream shopping, cognitive state refers to
consumers’ evaluation of product information,
perceived usefulness and offer value, drawing
conceptually on the central processing route
described in the elaboration likelihood model (Petty &
Cacioppo, 1986).
Consumers do not merely observe products
passively in live -stream commerce; while watching a
live session, they assess streamer credibility, product
usefulness, product quality, price fairness and
promotional benefits. Streamers contribute
cognitively by p roviding information, demonstrating
products, answering product -related questions and
reducing uncertainty.
Chen and Lin (2018)  examined factors associated
with live-stream usage intentions, while Lu and Chen
(2021) highlighted the importance of uncertainty
reduction in consumers’ purchase intentions within
live-stream commerce.
Product-related cues can also activate cognitive
processing because consumers evaluate quality,
performance, usefulness and fit with their
requirements. Product demonstrations and
explanations help consumers assess whether a
product offers value and meets their needs.
Gong and Jiang (2023)  confirmed that impulse
buying behavior in live -stream commerce is partly
explained by product -related factors. Likewise, price
cues prompt consumers to weigh benefits against
costs, and promotional cues draw attention to savings
potential, scarcity and ti me-limited purchase
opportunities.
Ho et al. (2022)  highlighted the relevance of
marketing-mix elements to live -stream purchase
behavior. Sales promotion has also been identified as
an important stimulus in live -stream impulse buying.
Therefore, consumers exposed to credible streamers,
clear product presen tation, reasonable pricing and
engaging promotional reminders are likely to have
more opportunities for cognitive evaluation in live -
stream shopping. Thus, the following hypothesis is
formulated:

Marketing Mix Stimuli and Affective State
Affective state refers to consumers’ emotional
responses during live -stream shopping, including
excitement, enjoyment, pleasure, emotional arousal
and self -reward, reflecting the “feeling, not thinking”
quality that characterizes impulsive consumption
(Holbrook & Hirschman, 1982 ; Verplanken &
Herabadi, 2001 ). Live -stream commerce is not only
informative but also experiential ( Holbrook &
Hirschman, 1982 ). Streamers may build emotional
connections by being friendly, entertaining,
interactive and confident in real time. Hu and
Chaudhry (2020)  found that relational bonds drive
consumer engagement in e -commerce live streaming,
where social and emotional relationships between
sellers and consumers play an important role. Ming et
al. (2021)  also demonstrated the effect of social
presence and telepresence on internal states such as
trust and flow, which in turn induce impulse buying.
Consumers’ affective state may also be influenced
by product presentation. The persuasive nature of
live-stream commerce may increase consumer
vulnerability by encouraging rapid purchasing
decisions under conditions of social influence,
emotional stimulati on and reduced deliberation
(Zhang et al., 2024 ). Emotional attraction and
excitement may arise through attractive product
demonstrations, novel presentation, visual appeal and
perceived desirability. Because consumers may
perceive offers as limited, price cues and promotional
mechanisms can create urg ency, satisfaction and fear
of missing out. Recent research indicates that
developments in live-stream shopping can strengthen
consumers’ emotional connections and encourage
impulsive purchasing ( Huo et al., 2023 ). Luo et al.
(2024) theorized that customer engagement and deal
proneness can moderate the effects of live streaming
on impulse buying.
People, product, price and promotion are therefore
expected to influence consumers’ affective responses
in live -stream purchasing. Skilled streamers may
generate enjoyment and trust; appealing product
presentation may elicit excitement; fair pricing may
provide satisfaction; and promotional cues may create
urgency and strengthen emotional desire. Hence, the
following hypothesis is proposed:
H2: Marketing mix stimuli —(a) people, (b)
product, (c) price, and (d) promotion —are positively
associated with consumers’ affective state.
Cognitive State, Affective State, and Impulse
Buying
Impulse buying in live -stream commerce can be
driven by both cognitive and affective factors. Rook
(1987) defined impulse buying as a sudden, often
powerful urge to buy. Consistent with this definition,
impulse buying is understood here as an emotional
and spontaneous response that may nevertheless
involve rapid cognitive assessments before an
unplanned purch ase. For example, a viewer may
quickly conclude that a product is useful, inexpensive,
good quality or offered at a reasonable price. Such an
assessment may rationalize an impulse purchase.
Gong and Jiang (2023)  emphasized the positive
relationship between product cognitive involvement
and impulse buying in live-stream commerce.
Impulse buying is also closely linked to affective
states because live-stream shopping largely generates
excitement, pleasure, urgency, enjoyment, and
emotional desire. Real -time interaction, social
presence, streamer enthusiasm, product appeal, and
promotional urgency can intensify these affective
responses and encourage impulse buying. Lee and
Chen (2021)  demonstrated that live -stream stimuli
affect consumers' internal conditions and their
impulse buying.
Similarly, according to Ming et al. (2021) , social
presence and telepresence stimulate trust and flow,
which in turn affect impulse buying. Recent studies
support these results, indicating that impulse
purchases in live -stream commerce are often
explained by a combination of cognitive and affectiv e
organismic mechanisms ( Feng et al., 2024 ; Xia et al.,
2024).
Accordingly, both cognitive and affective states are
hypothesized to influence impulse buying positively in
live-stream shopping. Recent research indicates that
emotional reactions are not the only drivers of impulse
buying in live -stream commerce, as cons umers may
also make rapid cognitive evaluations during impulse

Mediating Role of Cognitive and Affective State
Within the S -O-R framework, external stimuli are
theorized to relate to behavioral responses through
consumers’ internal psychological states. In live -
stream shopping, marketing -mix stimuli are expected
to influence impulse buying behavior through
consumers’ cognitive and affective processing. For
example, a credible streamer may reduce uncertainty
and increase consumer confidence; product -related
information may increase perceived usefulness; price
cues may strengthen perceived value; and promotions
may cr eate urgency. Cognitive and emotional
processes are then expected to be associated with
impulse buying.
This reasoning is consistent with recent studies
that examine mediating mechanisms in live -stream
commerce. Chen et al. (2022) studied the relationship
between live -stream marketing strategy and
impulsive purchasing, with involvement acting as a
mediator. Ming et al. (2021) demonstrated how social
presence influences impulse buying through the
mediation of trust and flow. Huo et al. (2023)
identified flow experience as an organismic
mechanism connecting live -stream stimuli and
impulse buying. Luo et al. (2024)  highlighted
consumer engagement as an intermediary process
linking live -streaming cues with impulse -buying
tendency. These studies provide preliminary evidence
that consumers’ internal states are important
psychological mediators connecting live -stream
triggers and spontaneous purchasing. To date, trust,
flow and involvement have been studied as mediators
in prior live -stream research, but cognitive and
affective states have rarely been examined in parallel
as mediators between marketing -mix stimuli and
impulse buying behavior. This gap is especially
important in developing economies, where live -
stream commerce is increasingly influencing
consumer choices and household spending habits.
Based on this reasoning, cognitive state is expected
to mediate the relationship between marketing -mix
stimuli and impulse buying behavior because
consumers first process relevant information, such as
streamer credibility, product value, price fairness and
promotional benefits, before making an unplanned
purchase. Affective state is also expected to mediate
these relationships because live -stream mar keting
cues can elicit excitement, enjoyment and desirability,
which may encourage impulse buying. Accordingly,
the following hypotheses are tested:
H4: Cognitive state positively mediates the relation
between (a) people, (b) product, (c) price, and (d)
promotion and impulsive buying, such that each
stimulus is expected to have a positive indirect effect
on impulsive buying through cognitive state.
H5: Affective state positively mediates the relation
between (a) people, (b) product, (c) price, and (d)
promotion and impulsive buying, such that each
stimulus is expected to have a positive indirect effect
on impulsive buying through affective state.
Although research on live -stream commerce is
growing, three important gaps remain. First, previous
studies have mainly focused on isolated live -stream
cues such as streamer features (e.g., presence and
interaction quality), product involvement, product
uncertainty, sales promotion and platform affordances
(Chen et al., 2022 ; Gong & Jiang, 2023 ; Ming et al.,
2021). Prior research has adopted either a marketing-
strategy or 7Ps perspective, but it has primarily
examined purchase intention or involvement rather
than people, product, price and promotion as an
integrated stimulus framework for impulse buying
(Chen et al., 2022 ; Ho et al., 2022 ). Second, previous
studies have mainly explored trust, flow, involvement
and consumer engagement as mediators, while the
simultaneous exploration of cognitive and affective
states as complementary psychological processes
between marketing stimuli and impu lse buying has
received little consideration (Chen et al., 2022; Huo et
al., 2023 ; Ming et al., 2021 ), even though recent
evidence suggests that impulse buying is shaped
through multiple cognitive and affective processes
(Feng et al., 2024; Xia et al., 2024).
Third, the socioeconomic challenges of live -stream
commerce, including its effects on household
consumption and consumer decision -making in
emerging markets, have not been well studied, and
evidence from Bangladesh remains limited because

METHODOLOGY
The research adopted a positivist paradigm. Within
this paradigm, the study examines consumer decision-
making in live-stream shopping through a hypothesis-
testing framework based on observed relationships
among the specified constructs. Primary data were
collected using self -administered questionnaires
completed by participants with live -stream shopping
experience. The proposed hypotheses were tested
using structural equation modeling (SEM) to examine
relationships among the study variables.
Research Design and Instrument Development
On the basis of the preceding discussion, this
research proposes that people, product, price and
promotion serve as live -stream marketing stimuli.
These stimuli are associated with consumers’
cognitive and affective states, which, in turn, are
associated with impulse buying behavior. Cognitive
and affective states are therefore examined as
mediating m echanisms between the experienced
marketing-mix stimuli and impulse buying.
This model contributes to live -stream commerce
literature by combining selected marketing mix
elements in the context of the S -O-R framework and
providing an explanation for impulse buying through
a combination of rational and affective information
processing.
The proposed research model extends the existing
literature by considering multiple marketing -mix
stimuli and dual psychological mechanisms
simultaneously within the framework of an emerging
economy, as shown in Figure 1.

The measurement items were adapted from

and consistency of measurement. The wording of

understanding while maintaining consistency with the
original scales (Churchill, 1979).
The questionnaire began with study information
and electronic consent, followed by a screening
question on previous live -stream purchasing, Section

questions, and Section 2 with 22 measurement items.
The measurement items were adapted from previous
literature.
The items for people were adapted from Chen et al.
(2022), while the items for product, price, cognitive
state and impulse buying were adapted from Gong and
Jiang (2023) , with Chen et al. (2022)  contributing

The measurement items for promotion, cognitive

Data Collection
The study’s primary data were collected through
an online survey conducted using Google Forms. The
study focused on Bangladeshi consumers with live -
stream shopping experience. Respondents who had
never engaged in live-stream shopping were excluded
from the analysis.
The questionnaire comprised one screening
question, seven demographic questions and 22 self -
report items. The screening question verified that all
retained respondents had prior live -stream shopping
experience. Seven variables were included in the
questionnaire: marketing-mix stimuli (people, product,
price and promotion), affective state, cognitive state and
impulse buying.  Using convenience sampling,
respondents were recruited through social media
platforms, e -mail, Facebook groups, and online
communities across Bangladesh. Data were gathered
between January and March 2026. A total of 404
responses were obtained. Screening i dentified seven
duplicate responses, and a further 29 responses were
excluded because the respondents had no prior live -
stream-shopping experience. This left 368 valid
responses for the final analysis. The final analytical
sample was determined by excluding the seven duplicate
responses and the 29 responses from participants
without previous live-stream shopping experience.

Data Analysis Procedure and Ethical
Considerations
Analysis of Moment Structures (AMOS) 26 and
Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) 26
were used to examine the collected data. Reliability
and validity were tested using confirmatory factor
analysis (CFA), and the model was verified using SEM
(Byrne, 1998/2013 ). Before covariance -based SEM
was conducted, data screening was performed using
IBM SPSS Statistics and AMOS 26.
The dataset was examined for missing values,
duplicate responses, eligibility, normality, and potential
outliers. As all questionnaire items were set as
mandatory, no incomplete responses were identified.
Seven duplicate responses and 29 respondents
without prior live -stream shopping experience were
removed, resulting in a final sample of 368 valid
responses. Normality and outlier assessments were
conducted using AMOS 26 diagnostics.
Although some deviation from multivariate
normality was observed, maximum likelihood
estimation was retained ( Bentler, 1983 ), supported
by the adequate sample size and bootstrap
procedure. The structural model was estimated using
maximum likelihood estimation with 5,000 bootstrap
iterations to obtain robust standard errors and
significance estimates.
Potential multivariate outliers were assessed
using Mahalanobis  distance. No additional exclusions
resulting from this assessment are reported. The
measurement model was assessed through CFA, and
the structural relationships among the constructs
were examined by SEM. Bootstrapping procedures
were used to test the significance of indirect effects
and the mediating roles of cognitive and affective
states. Reliability was measured using Cronbach’s
alpha and composite reliability, and validity was
measured through converge nt and discriminant
validity. Participation in the survey was voluntary. All
participants were informed about the study aims and
procedures, and confidentiality was protected because
no personally identifying information was collected.

DISCUSSION
Using the S-O-R model, this study investigated the
relationships among marketing -mix stimuli,
consumers’ cognitive and affective states, and impulse
buying behavior. The results indicate strong
relationships between cognitive and affective states
and impul se buying behavior, as well as several
relationships that differ from previous findings in the
live-stream-commerce literature.
The results suggested that cognitive state
(β = 0.288, p < 0.001) and affective state (β = 0.469,
p < 0.001) were positively related to impulse buying
behavior. This is consistent with Lee and Chen (2021),
who showed that live -stream stimuli shape
consumers' internal states and, through them, impulse
buying; with Ming et al. (2021) , who identified trust
and flow as such states; and with Huo et al. (2023) ,
who confirmed flow experience as the organismic link
between live-stream stimuli and impulse buying. The
stronger association of affective state with impulse

emotional responses may play a larger role in impulse

This finding extends previous studies, which have
examined cognitive and affective processes separately

Among the marketing -mix stimuli, promotion was
positively related to cognitive state (β  = 0.331, p <
0.001). This finding agrees with Ho et al. (2022) , who
found that promotion within the 7Ps marketing mix
shapes watching and purchase intentions on live -
streaming platforms, and with Huo et al. (2023) , who
identified promotional stimuli among the drivers of
impulse buying in live -streaming shopping. Price, in
turn, was positively and significantly associated with
affective state (β = 0.462, p < 0.001). This result is
consistent with Lu and Chen (2021), who showed that
information conveyed during live streams reduces
uncertainty and strengthens purchase intention, and
with Wongkitrungrueng and Assarut (2020) , who
found that the utilitarian value of live -stream offers
builds consumer trust and engagement with sellers.
Some results, however, were unexpected. Product
was negatively related to cognitive state (β = −0.163, p
= 0.003), contrary to previous studies suggesting that
product information, demonstrations and product
involvement can support consumer evaluation by
reducing cognitive uncertainty (Gong & Jiang, 2023; Lu
& Chen, 2021 ). This may be because live -streaming
environments can increase cognitive complexity
through extensive product information,
demonstrations or concerns about product
authenticity. This interpretation should, however, be
treated cautiously and requires furth er empirical
investigation.
There was no statistically significant relationship
between price and cognitive state. This result
contradicts earlier research indicating that price
fairness and value perception can play a role in
consumers’ rational evaluation processes ( Chen et al.,
2022; Lu & Chen, 2021). Analytical evaluation may not
be the only way in which price cues are processed, as
consumers may also use price cues as indicators of
perceived attractiveness, emotional value or
situational appeal. Similarly, product was not
significantly associated with affective state, whereas
previous research has shown that the emotional
appeal of product display and performance may be
important ( Gong & Jiang, 2023 ). This indicates that
emotional cues relating to a product do not necessarily
translate into consumers’ emotional responses, and
that interaction with the streamer, entertainment
value or promotions may be more relevant.
The mediation results help to clarify the
psychological processes underlying impulse buying.
Several relationships between marketing -mix stimuli
and impulse buying were mediated by cognitive and
affective states, although only some were supported.
For inst ance, the negative indirect association
between product and impulse buying through
cognitive state suggests that product cues do not
necessarily predict increased impulse buying. This
finding extends existing live -stream-commerce
studies that predominantly  focus on positive
mediating factors such as trust, flow, involvement and
engagement (Huo et al., 2023; Luo et al., 2024; Ming et
al., 2021 ). The results of the present study indicate
that psychological mechanisms in live -stream
commerce may differ depending on the type of
stimulus and consumers’ interpretation of the cues.
This research contributes to the literature on live -
stream commerce by showing that marketing stimuli
do not affect psychological processes uniformly. The
study also integrates cognitive and affective states into
the S -O-R model to examine consumers’ react ions to
live-stream shopping environments in an emerging -
market setting. However, the results should be
interpreted as associations rather than causal effects
because the study used a cross -sectional design with
self-reported survey data. Longitudinal desi gns,
experimental manipulation and behavioral
transaction data could provide stronger evidence
concerning the temporal ordering of live -stream
stimuli, psychological states and impulse buying
behavior.
This study makes three theoretical contributions to
the literature. With the S -O-R model, this research
shows how marketing stimuli are associated with
cognitive and affective states and, in turn, with
customer behavior. Previous research has mostly
examined the technological factors ( Dong & Wang,
2018), specific cues, e-store layouts (Chan et al., 2017),

with behavior such as shopping and social
interactions. Both cognitive and affective states were
positively associated  with impulse buying, while the
mediation analysis identified statistically significant
indirect associations for only some of the proposed
stimulus–response relationships.
The findings provide several practical implications
for live -stream retailers, e -commerce vendors and
digital marketers. E -commerce vendors should
identify, recruit and develop reliable, proficient
streamers with strong live -stream communication
skills ( Chen & Lin, 2018 ). Suppliers should provide
products that perform well and meet consumers’
needs. Merchants should offer transparent pricing that
helps consumers compare value before purchasing,
and live -streaming vendors should present
promotional offers with clear condi tions so that
consumers can evaluate them before purchasing. The
findings also indicate that streamers should provide
comprehensive product information and explain
product use occasions and scenarios to improve
viewers’ understanding and product appraisal.
Product pricing should be communicated clearly to
support viewers’ understanding and purchasing
decisions. E -commerce vendors should also offer
reliable platforms and ensure that viewers have a
clear, real -time view of the product. Promotional
offers, including instant discounts or special offers,
should be explained clearly to support informed
purchase decisions. On the  basis of the results,
marketers may focus on clear and well -explained
promotional offers and transparent, fair pricing
communication, as these factors had the largest
positive path coefficients for cognitive assessment and
affective responses, respectivel y. The study’s results
may help online sellers optimize live -stream
marketing practices while also considering the
socioeconomic challenges associated with
participants’ purchasing behavior.
CONCLUSIONS
The paper explored the relationships among
marketing-mix stimuli, cognitive state, affective state
and customers’ impulse buying behavior in live -
stream commerce in Bangladesh. Based on the S -O-R
framework and data from 368 respondents, the article
found t hat people and promotion were significantly
and positively associated with both cognitive and
affective states; price was significantly and positively
associated with affective state but not with cognitive
state; and product was significantly and negativel y
associated with cognitive state but was not
significantly associated with affective state.
Furthermore, the analysis revealed that customers’
affective state had a larger path coefficient predicting
impulse buying behavior than cognitive state. In
addition, promotion had the largest positive
standardized coefficient predicting cognitive state,
while price had the largest positive standardized
coefficient predicting affective state.
Overall, the research contributes empirical
evidence from Bangladesh, where live-stream impulse
buying remains under-researched and may be shaped
by distinctive consumer behaviors. The paper also
offers a theoretically grounded  interpretation of

This study has several limitations that should be
considered when interpreting the findings. The
research collected primary data exclusively from
respondents in Bangladesh. This limitation does not
necessarily confine the relevance of the findings to
live-commerce platforms in Bangladesh, but the
generalizability of the results should be treated with
caution. Another limitation is the use of convenience
sampling, and future studies should employ more
systematic sampling methods. Despite these
limitations, t he study adds to current research on
impulse buying through live shopping.
Furthermore, the study employed self-reported data,
which may not fully represent real decision -making.
Consumers may also be reluctant to acknowledge
impulsive tendencies. Subsequent research could use
quasi-experimental techniques or data analytics
strategies based on behavioral data obtained from
shopping platforms.
Although this study examined the relevant literature
to identify significant variables, it may have overlooked
additional factors, such as cultural background or
individual characteristics. Further studies could
incorporate additional elements to provide a  more
comprehensive perspective on impulse purchasing
behavior among live-stream consumers.
A further limitation concerns the measurement of the
organismic constructs. The cognitive -state indicators
capture respondents' perception of the necessity, value,
and limited availability of the products recommended by
streamers, and the affective -state i ndicators capture
shopping to improve mood, relieve stress, or provide
self-reward; these items may not fully represent the
transient cognitive and affective states experienced
during live -stream exposure implied by the S -O-R
framework. Two of the promotio n items (being
persuaded to buy by discounts and being convinced to
purchase by free delivery) and one impulse-buying item
(products that are rarely used, which may reflect post-
purchase use rather than impulsiveness) also show some
conceptual overlap with the outcome construct.
These items were retained because they matched the
adapted source scales, but the overlap limits the content
validity of the promotion, cognitive-state, affective-state,
and impulse-buying measures, and the reported path
coefficients should be interpreted as relationships
among these specific indicators rather than as clean tests
of the broader S-O-R constructs. Future research should
develop and validate items that more directly and
exclusively capture transient, in-the-moment cognitive
and affective reactions to live -stream stimuli, separate
from behavioral or post-purchase content.
In addition, because the data are cross -sectional
and self -reported and were collected through
convenience sampling, the associations reported here
should not be interpreted as causal, and the findings
should be generalized cautiously beyond the sampled
population of Bangladeshi live -stream shoppers. The
structural model also did not estimate the direct paths
from the marketing -mix stimuli to impulse buying
behavior, so the analysis can identify statistically
significant indirect effects but cannot distingu ish
partial from full mediation.

Appendix A
Survey Questionnaire: Impulse Buying in Live-Stream Commerce
This questionnaire is designed to collect data for the research titled “Impulse Buying in Live -Stream Commerce as a
Socioeconomic Challenge: Marketing Mix Stimuli and the Roles of Cognitive and Affective States.” The study examines the
relationship between the live stream marketing mix stimuli (people, product, price, and promotion) and the consumers' impulse
buying, with the mediation of consumers' cognitive and affective states in the context of live stream commerce in Bangladesh.
The results of this study will be used to help inform the body of literature on the socioeconomic issues of e -commerce and will
serve to inform e-commerce vendors, live-stream retailers, and digital marketers on the marketing stimuli that have the greatest
impact on consumer decision-making and impulsive buying.
Please read the following statement carefully before proceeding with the survey.
• Participation in this survey is voluntary, and the respondents can withdraw from the survey anytime, before submission.
• Collected data will be kept confidential and used for academic purposes only in an aggregated/statistical form; individual
responses will not be identifiable.
• No personally identifying information (e.g., name, contact details, or address) is collected as part of this survey.
• The survey will take approximately 5–10 minutes to complete.
• There are no known risks associated with participating in this study.
