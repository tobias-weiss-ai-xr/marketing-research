ABSTRACT
Culture-oriented tourism plays a vital role in empowering rural economies and
safeguarding local wisdom. However, marketing practices in this sector
frequently prioritize commercial appeal rather than embodying indigenous
cultural values. This study invest igates the influence of cultural tourism
marketing strategies on rural destination branding, with the Tri Hita Karana
(THK) philosophy serving as a mediating variable. Grounded in value -based
marketing and cultural sustainability perspectives, this research emphasizes the
integration of local wisdom into destination branding as a strategic intangible
asset. Data were collected through a structured Likert -scale survey involving 121
tourism stakeholders and community members in Jatiluwih Tourism Villag e, a
UNESCO World Heritage Site in Bali, Indonesia. The data were analyzed using
Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM). The findings
demonstrate that cultural tourism marketing strategies have a significant positive
effect on destina tion branding. Moreover, Tri Hita Karana values significantly
mediate this relationship, indicating that marketing strategies aligned with
spiritual harmony (Parhyangan), social cohesion (Pawongan), and environmental
balance ( Palemahan) strengthen destinat ion brand authenticity and credibility.
The results contribute theoretically by extending destination marketing and
branding literature through the empirical integration of an indigenous
philosophical framework into a structural model. Practically, the fin dings suggest
that rural tourism managers and policymakers should embed cultural and spiritual
values into marketing communications to enhance sustainable brand identity,
visitor trust, and long -term destination resilience. Overall, this study highlights
that culturally grounded marketing strategies are essential for developing
authentic and sustainable rural tourism destinations in culturally rich regions.

INTRODUCTION
Tourism plays a crucial role in shaping local economies and cultural identity, particularly in developing regions
where it serves as a driver of community empowerment and sustainability. This shift highlights the growing importance
of cultural tourism marketing, which focuses on promoting authentic cultural experiences while preserving the identity
of the host community (Ekka et al., 2023). The Tri Hita Karana (THK) philosophy rooted in Balinese culture provides a
holistic framework for maintaining harmony between humans ( Pawongan), nature ( Palemahan), and  the divine
(Parhyangan) (Sugiartawan et al., 2022) . As a core principle of community life and spiritual balance, THK aligns with
global concepts of sustainable development and responsible tourism management (Astuti et al., 2024).  Integrating THK
into marketing strategies may create a more authentic and value -oriented destination identity, connecting local cultural
wisdom with modern marketing approaches  (Sitiari et al., 2024) . This study aims to analyze the influence of cultural
tourism marketing strategies on rural destination branding, with Tri Hita Karana values serving as a mediating variable.
The choice of Jatiluwih Village a UNESCO designated cultural landscape in Bali is particularly relevant, as it represents
the living practice of THK philosophy through its subak irrigation system and harmonious relationship between humans
and nature (Ardani et al., 2025) . However, current destination branding in Jatiluwih remains focused largely on scenic
attractions rather than deeper spiritual and cultural values, creating a gap in aligning cultural heritage with marketing
strategy.

LITERATURE REVIEW
Evolution Of Cultural Tourism And Its Marketing Implications
Cultural tourism has evolved from a focus on tangible heritage (monuments, artifacts, and traditions) to an
emphasis on experience-based and value driven tourism (Rahman & Baddam, 2021) . In his seminal review, (Richards,
2018) highlighted the transformation of cultural tourism from static consumption to active co -creation, where visitors
seek authenticity and connection rather than mere entertainment. This evolution underscores a paradigm shift in
destination marketing, which must now promote culture not as a product, but as a meaningful experience and narrative.
Marketing cultural tourism thus  requires incorporating storytelling, community participation, and cultural co -creation
into branding strategies (Confetto et al., 2023). Such strategies go beyond traditional advertising to convey the
emotional and spiritual essence of place identity. How ever, as noted by  (Risfandini, 2024)  when culture becomes
commodified, marketing often conf licts with the local community’s values highlighting the need for a framework that
balances economic objectives with cultural integrity.

Marketing Strategy And Destination Branding
Marketing strategy in tourism refers to the systematic process of identify ing market opportunities, designing
value propositions, and positioning destinations to attract and retain visitors. Effective marketing strategies integrate
product differentiation, storytelling, and customer engagement to shape a destination’s image and reputation (Made et
al., 2020; Pramono & Juliana, 2025).
In the context of cultural tourism, marketing strategies should highlight authenticity, local wisdom, and
emotional value to strengthen destination identity (Zhou et al., 2021). Strategic marketing thus plays a vital role in
building destination branding, defined as the symbolic representation of a place’s identity in the minds of visitors
(Escobar-Farfán et al., 2024). Empirical studies confirm that strategic marketing initiatives emphasizing sustainability
and culture positively influence tourists’ perceptions, satisfaction, and loyalty (Confetto et al., 2023; Zhou et al., 2021).
When aligned with community values, these strategies enhance the brand’s credibility and distinctiveness.

The Tri Hita Karana Philosophy as a Cultural Sustainability Framework
Tri Hita Karana (THK) translated as “the three causes of well -being”is a Balinese philosophical system
emphasizing harmony between humans and God (Parhyangan), humans and other humans (Pawongan), and humans
and nature ( Palemahan) (Y ulianthini et al., 2025). Scholars regard THK as an indigenous sustainability model deeply
embedded in Balinese daily life, governance, and environmental management (Salamanca et al., n.d.) Empirical
research has demonstrated that THK -based practices promote environmental balance and social harmony (Luh et al.,
2023).  However, quantitative analyses ex ploring how THK influences marketing -related outcomes  such as brand
image, loyalty, and destination identity  remain scarce.  Sugiartawan et al. (2022)  argue that THK has been invoked
rhetorically in policy and tourism planning, but often lacks systematic integration into measurable marketing
frameworks. This study addresses that limitation by positioning THK not merely as a cultural value but as a mediating
mechanism between marketing strategy a nd destination branding. It hypothesizes that when marketing strategies
embody THK principles, they produce morally resonant and culturally authentic brand identities, strengthening visitors’
emotional attachment and sustainable behavioral intentions.

THK philosophy, meaning “three causes of well -being,” embodies the Balinese concept of harmony between all
living beings and nature (Sitiari et al., 2024) . It serves as a holistic cultural framework guiding social conduct,
environmental management, and spiritual balance. Integrating THK values into tourism marketing introduces ethical
and cultural depth into destination branding. THK -based marketing narratives convey messages of balance, gratitude,
and sustainability values that resonate deeply with both local communities and global tourists Sugiartawan et al. (2022).
When mark eting strategies embody THK principles, they not only promote destinations but also reflect moral and
spiritual harmony, reinforcing authenticity and emotional attachment among visitors (Prihadi et al., 2024) . Therefore,
THK is expected to mediate t he relationship between marketing strategy and destination branding by translating
promotional efforts into culturally meaningful experiences.

Destination Branding in Rural Cultural Contexts
Destination branding refers to the process of developing a plac e’s identity to influence visitors’ perceptions and
loyalty (Garanti et al., 2024). In rural tourism, branding is more than visual representation —it reflects the community’s
cultural coherence and shared meaning (Garanti et al., 2024) . Research on sustainable place branding has shown that
cultural distinctiveness and authenticity drive visitors’ satisfaction and willingness to recommend destinations (Escobar-
Farfán et al., 2024) . For culturally rich rural areas such as Bali’s Jatiluwih Village, branding authenticity depends on
how deeply marketing narratives align with community philosophy. Integrating Tri Hita Karana into branding strategies
provides a unique value proposition that differentiates destinations not only by scenic beauty but by spiritual and ethical
depth. This integration transforms branding from a commercial endeavor into a representation of shared values between
visitors and hosts, ensuring long-term sustainability.

Tri Hita Karana and Destination Branding
The Tri Hita Karana (THK) philosophy representing harmony among humans, nature, and the divine serves as a
profound foundation for sustainable tourism in Bali  (Bagus et al., 2023) . Beyond its spiritual dimension, THK has
evolved into a framework for ethical tourism management, balancing economic advancement with cultural and
environmental responsibility (Ardani et al., 2025) . Recent studies emphasize that the internalization of THK values in
tourism branding contributes to a destination’s perceived authenticity, trust, and emotional resonance (Y ulianthini et al.,
2025). THK -based branding fosters co -creation experiences that link visitors with community values, enabling
destinations to differentiate themselves through cultural meaning rather than mere visual imagery. Y ulianthini et al.
(2025) found that THK-driven co-creation practices significantly enhance tourists’ perceptions of cultural integrity and
satisfaction, positioning the ph ilosophy as a strategic pillar in shaping brand identity. Similarly, (Escobar-Farfán et al.,
2024). Highlight that embedding local cultural values within brand narratives strengthens destination loyalty and
competitive advantage. Therefore, the integration of Tri Hita Karana within destination branding is expected to produce
more sustainable, trustworthy, and emotionally compelling brand identities.

Critical Synthesis and Research Gap
The reviewed literature reveals several key tensions: While cultural authenticity is widely acknowledged as vital,
many marketing practices still lead to cultural commodification. Destination marketing research often focuses on
organizational and promotional factors, neglecting philosophical -cultural dimensions (Confetto et al., 2023) . Studies of
Tri Hita Karana largely remain descriptive and normative, lack ing empirical models that link it with destination
marketing and branding performance. To bridge these gaps, this research develops and empirically tests a conceptual
model that connects Cultural Tourism Marketing → Tri Hita Karana → Destination Branding. It integrates theories of
value-based marketing, cultural sustainability, and indigenous philosophy to demonstrate how local wisdom can be
leveraged as a strategic intangible asset for sustainable rural tourism development and brand competitiveness.

METHOD
This study adopts a quantitative research design employing the Partial Least Squares Structural Equation
Modeling (PLS -SEM) approach to examine the causal relationship between marketing strategy (X), Tri Hita Karana
(Z), and destination branding (Y).  The PLS-SEM technique was chosen due to its suitability for complex models and
moderate sample sizes, as well as its ability to assess latent constructs with reflective indicators (Hair, 2022) . The
research follows an explanatory design, aiming to measure both direct and indirect effects among the variables. The
analysis was conducted using SmartPLS 4.0 software, which allows simultaneous assessment of both measurement
(outer) and structural (inner) models to ensure validity and reliability. The study was conducted in Jatiluwih Village,
Tabanan Regency, Bali, a UNESCO World Heritage Site known for its Subak irrigation system and cultural tourism
practices grounded in the Tri Hita Karana (THK) philosophy.
The population of this study consisted of local stakeholders involved in tourism management including homestay
owners, cultural site managers, local tourism committees, and community leaders. The sampling technique used was
purposive sampling, targeting individuals directly involved in tourism operations and marketing practices. A total of
121 valid responses were obtained, recommended for PLS -SEM (ten times the maximum number of indicators in the

RESULT
The evaluation of the measurement model was conducted to ensure that all co nstructs and indicators used in this
study met the criteria of validity and reliability. The assessment included convergent validity, construct reliability, and
the coefficient of determination (R²). The results indicate that all indicators of marketing st rategy exhibit loading factor
values ranging from 0.795 to 0.878, exceeding the recommended threshold of 0.70. This confirms that each indicator
adequately represents the marketing strategy construct. Furthermore, the composite reliability value of 0.892 a nd
cronbach’s alpha of 0.889 demonstrate a high level of internal consistency. The average variance extracted value of
0.693 also exceeds the minimum criterion of 0.50, indicating satisfactory convergent validity for this construct  (Hair,
2022).
For the Destination Branding (Y) construct, the loading factor values range between 0.857 and 0.912, reflecting
strong indicator contributions. The construct shows excellent reliability, as evidenced by a composite reliability value of
0.942 and a Cronbach’s Alpha of 0.924. Additionally, the A VE value of 0.766 suggests that more than 76% of the
variance in the indicators is explained by the construct. The R ² value of 0.673 indicates that the exogenous variables in
the model explain 67.3% of the variance in destination branding, which can be categorized as substantial (Hair, 2022).

DISCUSSION
This study provide empirical evidence that cultural tourism marketing strategies have a positive and significant
effect on  the branding of rural tourist destinations. These findings are consistent with the literature that confirms that
culture-based marketing strategies can strengthen perceptions of authenticity and increase traveller loyalty (Garanti et
al., 2024; Yanan et al., 2024)
Furthermore, integrating Tri Hita Karana values has proven to be an important role in strengthening the
relationship between marketing and brand ing strategies. This philosophy allows for the creation of a balance between
the spiritual (Parhyangan), social (Pawongan), and ecological (Palemahan) dimensions, so that destinations are not only
perceived as tourist attractions, but also as spaces for me aningful cultural experiences (Ardani et al., 2025; Bagus et al.,
2023; Dewi et al., 2023; Prihadi et al., 2024; Sitiari et al., 2024; Sugiartawan et al., 2022; Udayana & Dwijendra, 2022;
Y ulianthini et al., 2025). A significant mediation role suggests that marketing strat egies executed without considering
local values tend to produce a superficial image. On the other hand, when the value of THK is internalized in the
process of communication, products, and services, the image of the destination becomes stronger, more consi stent, and
trusted by tourists (Escobar-Farfán et al., 2024) . These results expand the theory of destination marketing by showing
that local cultural values are not only an element of identity, b ut also a strategic mechanism in creating a destination's
competitive advantage. Tourism village managers can implement these results through promotional strategies
highlighting spirituality, social harmony, and nature preservation as the core message of destination communication.

CONCLUSION
The findings of this study carry both theoretical and practical implications. Theoretically, this research extends
the literature on culture-based tourism marketing by confirming the mediating role of Tri Hita Karana (THK) values in
strengthening the relationship between marketing strategies and destination branding. This contributes to a deeper
understanding of how indigenous philosophies can serve as a strategic framework for sustainable tourism development
in rural a reas. Practically, the study provides valuable insights for destination managers and policymakers, suggesting
that marketing initiatives should integrate local wisdom, community engagement, and spiritual balance to enhance the
authenticity and resilience of destination brands.
For future research, several directions can be explored. First, longitudinal studies could be conducted to examine
the long-term influence of cultural marketing on brand loyalty and visitor satisfaction. Second, comparative analyses
between rural and urban destinations may reveal contextual differences in applying cultural values. Third, incorporating
qualitative approaches such as interviews or ethnographic studies could provide richer insights into how THK values
are internalised by stakeholders. Finally, integrating digital marketing variables —such as social media engagement and
virtual storytelling—would further expand the practical framework for sustainable cultural tourism marketing.
