Abstract
Tea culture tourism combines the historical aspects of tea culture with the ecological environment and leisure activities. Tr aditional tourism is no
longer sufficient to meet the evolving needs and desires of tourists, whose lifestyles and values have unde rgone significant changes. Consequently,
many tourists have increasingly embraced tea culture tourism. However, there is a significant knowledge gap in understanding the efficacy of tea
culture ecotourism as a marketing strategy. Addressing this gap is crucial for developing targeted strategies that appeal to tourists and for promoting
Sri Lankan tea globally. This study aims to identify the factors affecting clients' perceptions of the effectiveness of tea culture ecotourism, investigate
the relationship b etween tea promotion strategies and client perceptions of the efficacy of these experiences, and develop evidence -based
recommendations for promoting tea culture ecotourism as an effective strategy for tea promotion. The study was conducted at S ky Cafe, lo cated
within the Halpewatte Tea Factory in Hela Halpe, Bandarawela District, Sri Lanka. A total of 130 visitors who experienced tea culture ec otourism at
this location were selected using systematic random sampling and surveyed using pretested questionnaires and interviews to collect data. Descriptive
and statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software. The research findings indicate that clients perceive ecotourism experiences centred on
tea culture as more impactful when such experiences are characterised  by cultural immersion and authenticity, alongside contributions to the local
community's well -being. The study findings reveal a significant positive correlation between educational interpretation and environmental
responsibility, as well as the perceived  effectiveness of these ecotourism experiences. A robust correlation coefficient (R = 0.746) was identified
among the independent variables —namely, cultural immersion, authenticity, local community well -being, educational interpretation, and
environmental responsibility —and the dependent variable, which is clients' perceptions of the effectiveness of tea culture ecotourism. The correlation
accounts for 55.6% of the variance in clients' perceptions, highlighting the substantial influence that these factors exert on their assessment of the
effectiveness of tea culture ecotourism. The study findings prove that tea culture ecotourism can succeed by enhancing educat ional programs,
enforcing environmental sustainability, empowering local communities, creating aut hentic experiences, and implementing continuous evaluation.
These strategies collectively enrich visitor experiences while supporting local economies and preserving the ecosystems that sustain tea cultivation.
By adopting these recommendations, policymaker s can harness the potential of tea culture ecotourism as a sustainable tool for economic
development, cultural preservation, and environmental stewardship.

INTRODUCTION
Tea is cultivated in around 36 countries globally; however, this widespread production can drive
up costs. Thus, implementing non -crop diversification strategies, such as tea tourism, emerges as a
beneficial alternative (Dissanayake, 2017). Developing tea -centered tourism can unlock new niche
markets and strengthen the tea industry through better promotion and authentic cultural experiences.
Niche tourism involves tailoring specific tourism products to meet the preferences of particular traveler
segments (I . Fernando et al., 2017). Tea culture ecotourism, a recognized emerging niche, has the
potential to significantly boost the industry’s contribution to the Gross Domestic Product (GDP) with
appropriate investment and promotion (Jolliffe, 2007). Ultimately, tea tourism offers visitors authentic
experiences and presents a viable path for enhancing profitability and sustainability in Sri Lanka's tea
sector. Tea tourism is a tactical enabler to enhance the brand image of Ceylon tea by delivering
experiential exposure to the entire tea culture of Sri Lanka (Dissanayake, 2017). Tea tourism can be
developed into various niches, including rural tourism, exclusive tourism, community tourism, health
tourism, heritage tourism, and ecotourism (Jolliffe, 2007). Ecotourism  is a new form of tourism that
concerns the conflict between economic development and environmental well -being. The priority is to
minimize the ecological footprint of tourism through ecotourism, while still allowing people to enjoy
nature and improving people’s quality of life (Zhou et al., 2023).
Many tea -producing regions have begun to integrate the ecology of mountain -grown tea,
traditional tea-making techniques, tea folklore, and tea culture to promote in-depth tourism development
(Zhou et al., 2023). According to Zhang (2004), tea culture can b e divided into three dimensions. The
first dimension is the material aspect, which encompasses the production and consumption of tea. The
second dimension is the behavioural  aspect, which includes activities such as visiting tea houses,
attending tea exhibitions, and participating in tea events. The third dimension is the spiritual aspect,
which involves aesthetic, religious, and artistic elements. Culture and tourism share a  mutually
beneficial relationship that enhances the appeal and competitiveness of regions. Cultural tourism
leverages artistic potential as a key attraction, recognizing culture as a significant asset for the tourism
sector (Muhammad and Kuswarini, 2019). Culture is the essence of tourism. Tourism falls into the trap
of superficiality and mediocrity due to the absence of cultural elements in tourism activities. Blending
ecotourism with tea culture not only elevates the ecological value and overall quality of experiences
given by tourism, but also it has been helpful to revitalize and strengthen the local tea industry (Zhou et
al., 2023). In general, tea culture tourism combines the historical tea culture features with the ecological
environment and leisure activities. When people travel more, their lifestyles shift, and gradual changes
in values occur. As a result of that, the traditional type of tourism becomes less appealing as tourists are
willing to have experiences that offer something unique. T his trend has fueled the rise of tea culture
ecotourism, which has high potential to cater to the desire of modern tourists for immersive and
enriching experiences. Ultimately, it will promote tea worldwide.

Tea comprises both intangible and tangible cultural elements. The customs and traditions
associated with tea consumption in various countries, such as daily tea drinking practices in China, the
Japanese tea ceremony known as “Chanoyu”, the European afterno on tea symbolizing wealth and
elegance, herbal tea traditions in Islamic countries, Malaysian milk tea, horse -millk tea in Tibet, apple
tea in Turkey, and the pickled tea culture called “Miang ” among the northern Thai people are some of
the examples for intangible cultural aspects (Khaokhrueamuang and Chueamchaitrakun, 2019.).
Tangible culture encompasses physical objects, such as tea ware, as well as the material culture
symbolized by tea estates, historical buildings, and machinery used in tea processing and trading. Both
intangible and tangible tea cultures are often converted into tourism products to fulfill visitor demand
(Khaokhrueamuang and Chueamchaitrakun, 2019).  While tea tourism has emerged as a popular avenue
for promoting tea in producing regions globally, tea culture ecotourism, a niche segment of tea tourism,
has not been thoroughly explored. The unique blend of Sri Lanka's rich tea culture with the  principles
of ecotourism creates a strong foundation for tea culture ecotourism. However, there is still a lack of
information regarding the factors that influence clientele perceptions of tea culture ecotourism. Limited
research has examined the effectiv eness of marketing approaches oriented towards tea culture
ecotourism for promoting Ceylon tea. Addressing this knowledge gap is crucial for developing targeted
marketing campaigns that can attract suitable audiences and effectively promote Ceylon tea in b oth
domestic and international markets. Ultimately, improved marketing strategies will not only revitalize
Ceylon tea but also enhance the well -being of local communities and support sustainable tourism
practices
Objectives
This study aims to identify the factors affecting clients' perceptions of the effectiveness of tea
culture ecotourism, investigate the relationship between tea promotion strategies and client perceptions
of the efficacy of these experiences, and develop ev idence-based recommendations for promoting tea
culture ecotourism as an effective strategy for tea promotion.

The conceptual framework developed for this study aims to illustrate the relationship between

Travel-related Factors
This study assessed travel-related factors, including previous experiences, travel purpose, travel
style, and tour type, to understand tourists' motivations for engaging with tea culture ecotourism. The
purpose of travel, whether for relaxation, cultural exploration, or adventure, influences tourists' activities
and destinations (Sugathapala  and A.S.H., n.d.). Travel style (solo vs. group) influences interaction
levels, while the type of tour (plantation vs. factory) affects visitor perceptions and satisfaction. Previous
experiences also shape expectations, with seasoned travelers more inclin ed towards novel experiences
(Xinyue Li, 2022; Pinto da Silva et al., 2019).
Educational Interpretation
Learning plays a pivotal role in enhancing the tourist experience, allowing travelers to view their
journeys as educational opportunities (Hewage  et al., 2023). As tourists increasingly seek knowledge,
tourism experiences can facilitate both formal and informal education (Sthapit  et al., 2024). This study
emphasizes educational interpretation as a key factor in visitor engagement through knowledge
acquisition, particularly within the context of tea culture ecotourism. Participants reflected on their
understanding of Sri Lankan tea cultivation history and various stages of tea manufacturing, while also
engaging their senses through the aroma and freshness of tea leaves. This focus highlights how
enhancing visitors' comprehension of tea culture can significantly enrich their overall experience.
Environmental Responsibility
The independent variable of environmental responsibility was assessed through tourists'
perceptions of sustainable practices in tea culture. This included evaluations of eco -friendly methods,
like using bamboo baskets for plucking leaves and the benefits o f shade trees in creating a calm
environment. Participants also reflected on conservation efforts, supporting measures that preserve
nature's aesthetic beauty (Pinto da Silva et al., 2019). Research highlights that tourists value
environmental preservation, with findings indicating significant willingness to pay for conserving tea
resources (Feng et al., 2012).
Well-being of Local Community
The well-being of local communities engaged in tea culture ecotourism is significantly influenced
by various factors, including employment opportunities and economic contributions. Participants
reported satisfaction with job creation resulting from tourist  visits, indicating that these interactions
enhance local livelihoods. Additionally, tourist spending contributes to local wages, fostering economic
benefits that enhance the community's quality of life. Empowered local hosts expressed pride in their
role within the tourism experience, reinforcing positive attitudes toward tourism. This research

Cultural Immersion and Authenticity
The exploration of cultural immersion and authenticity within tea culture yields profound insights
into participant engagement and appreciation. Understanding traditional practices, like the plucking
technique of “one bud and two leaves” and orthodox manuf acturing methods, deepens participants'
connection to tea (Li, 2022). Enhanced tasting experiences were reported as participants engaged with
diverse tea ware and expressed a keen interest in traditional ceremonies, underscoring their cultural
importance (Banerjee & Tyagi, 2024). This potential for annual tea festivals to attract niche tourism is
supported by the allure of such rituals in China and England (Yılmaz & Kumar Dixit, 2023). Moreover,
interactions with tea pluckers and insights into local customs, such as wearing a “bindi,” enriched their
experiences, reinforcing the cultural heritage and significance of scenic landscapes and historical
factories ( Muhammad & Kuswarini, 2019; Khaokhrueamuang & Chueamchaitrakun, 2019).
Understanding these perceptions illuminates how sustainability influences attitudes toward the tea
culture ecotourism.
Clientele Perception of the Effectiveness of Tea Culture Ecotourism
The study assessed clientele perceptions of tea culture ecotourism through various statements
reflecting visitor experiences and satisfaction levels. This included enjoyment, perceived value, and
appreciation of tea culture. Participants rated whether thei r experiences met or surpassed expectations
for an enriching engagement and their overall satisfaction with the offerings. Key factors included
willingness to recommend the program, revisit intentions, and interest in purchasing local tea products.
Research indicates that enhancing customer involvement and co -creation in tourism can significantly
boost satisfaction. Moreover, effective marketing that emphasizes unique offerings in tea tourism
increases brand awareness and customer loyalty (Dissanayake, 2017 ). Hence, a wareness of the brand
was also evaluated.  Understanding clientele perceptions is crucial for developing targeted marketing
strategies that appeal to potential tourists.
These elements emphasize the importance of cultural experiences in shaping perceptions of tea

Sample and Sampling Technique
In this study, the systematic sampling technique, as explained by Thomas (2023), was employed
to gather data from a sample of 130 participants who visited the Sky Cafe at the Uva Halpewatte Tea
Factory between May and June, 2024.   In this study, every third visitor to the café who had prior
experience participating in tea culture ecotourism programs at the research site was systematically
selected for inclusion. This approach ensured that only individuals with relevant experiences  were
included in the study, following the systematic sampling methodology outlined by Thomas (2023). This
method emphasizes the selection of individuals at regular intervals, which is particularly valuable when
an exact population size is unknown due to t he unavailability of a comprehensive visitor list. By
implementing this sampling technique, the study aimed to gather insights from a well -defined segment
of the population, thereby enhancing the reliability and relevance of the findings.
Data on clients' perceptions of tea culture ecotourism were collected through a combination of
self-administered structured questionnaires and in-depth interviews, aligning with the study's objectives.
The questionnaire, developed in Google Forms, was distributed among systematically selected visitors
in a one-on-one, face-to-face manner at the site. Participants were asked to provide their responses on -
site to ensure the reliability of the information. Designed in paper format, the questionnaires utilized a
5-point Likert scale to measure subjective attitudes, with responses categorized as follows: Strongly
Disagree (1), Disagree (2), Neutral (3), Agree (4), and Strongly Agree (5). The structured questionnaires
incorporated a mix of Likert scales, multiple -choice questions, and both closed-ended and open-ended
formats to effectively capture both qualitative and quantitative data. To enhance the questionnaire data,
in-depth interviews were conducted with a subset of participants, providing deeper insights into the
complex relationships between the independent and dependent variables. Field observations also played
a crucial role in enriching the primary data collected during this research.
Qualitative variables measured using the Likert scales were analyzed for index values  in
conjunction with the multiple linear regression model. This approach enabled a more precise and
quantitative assessment of the independent variables and the dependent variable. Index values were
computed to numerically represent the qualitative aspects of these variables, enhancing the accuracy and
interpretability of the multiple linear regression analysis.

Conclusions
The research establishes a significant relationship between educational interpretation and
environmental responsibility within the context of tea culture ecotourism. The strong positive correlation
(R = 0.746) among the identified independent variables —cultural immersion, authenticity, local
community well-being, educational interpretation, and environmental responsibility —illustrates their
collective impact on clients' perceptions of the effectiveness of tea culture ecotourism.
The correlation accounts for 55.6% of the variance in clients' perceptions, highlighting the
substantial influence that these factors exert on their assessment of the effectiveness of tea culture
ecotourism. The study findings prove that tea culture ecotourism can succeed by enhancing educational
programs, enforcing environmental sustainability, empowering local communities, creating authentic
experiences, and implementing continuous evaluation.
In conclusion, this research provides valuable insights into clientele perceptions regarding the
effectiveness of tea culture ecotourism -oriented marketing approaches for tea promotion. By
understanding demographic influences and preferences, stakeholders can develop targeted marketing
strategies that resonate with both local and international audiences.  This holistic approach is essential
for the successful advancement of tea culture ecotourism, ensuring that it meets both ecological and
cultural objectives while enhancing visitor experiences.

enhancement of educational programs within tea culture ecotourism initiatives. This approach not only
