Abstract: This study examines the mediating role of brand image in the relationship between social media marketing
activities, brand authenticity, and purchase intention among Generation Z consumers of TeeFees Clothing Brand in
Manado, North Sulawesi. A quantitative  causal-associative design was deployed using a structured survey
administered to a sample of 200 active social media users who are familiar with the brand. Data analysis was
performed using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM) in SmartPLS 4. The empirical
findings demonstrate that all seven hypotheses are statistically supported. Specifically, both social media marketing
activities and brand authenticity exert direct positive influences on brand image and purchase intention. Furthermore,
the structural model assessment confirms that brand image serves as a significant partial mediator, indicating that
while digital marketing and authenticity directly motivate buying behavior, their commercial impacts are
substantially optimized when they successfully cultivate a strong and unique brand image in consumers' memories.
Practically, the findings suggest that local apparel brands must prioritize interactive video content on platforms like
TikTok and Instagram while continuously in tegrating local cultural pride and identity -driven design concepts into
their branding to sustain competitive advantages.

Keywords: Social Media Marketing, Brand Authenticity, Brand Image, Purchase Intention, Generation Z

INTRODUCTION

Research Background
Over the past two decades, digital technology has fundamentally transformed how commercial enterprises
engage with consumers. Platforms such as Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, and X (formerly Twitter) have evolved
from basic networking channels into comprehensive commercial ecosystems where consumer preferences are
shaped, brand meanings are co-created, and purchasing decisions are actively formulated.
Within this digital sphere, Social Media Marketing (SMM) has emerged as an indispensable discipline for
contemporary brand managers. Unlike conventional mass media advertising, which relies on unidirectional message
dissemination and intermittent promotion al exposures, SMM facilitates dynamic, two -way interpersonal
communication (Kaplan & Haenlein, 2010). Through engaging visual storytelling and electronic word -of-mouth
(eWOM) amplification, social media marketing serves as a powerful exogenous catalyst cap able of shaping both
cognitive evaluations and commercial behavior.
Simultaneously, the digital marketplace has given rise to heightened consumer skepticism toward
manufactured promotional content, propelling brand authenticity into the forefront of marketing scholarship. Brand
authenticity represents the extent to which consumers perceive a brand as genuine, continuous, credible, and value-
aligned (Morhart et al., 2015; Napoli et al., 2014).
In the marketing literature, Brand image conceptualized as the totality of mental associations, beliefs, and
attitudes held by consumers toward a brand (Keller, 1993; Aaker, 1996)  functions as a central pillar of Customer -

Generation Z (born between 1997 and 2012) represents a pivotal consumer demographic driving current

LITERATURE REVIEW

Purchase Intention
Purchase Intention represents a consumer's conscious plan, readiness, and subjective probability of
purchasing a product or service in the future (Spears & Singh, 2004; Ferdinand, 2006). It serves as the single most
dependable proxy and behavioral precurso r for actual purchasing conduct in marketing scholarship. According to
Ferdinand (2006), purchase intention encompasses four core dimensions: (1) transactional intention (the direct
willingness to purchase), (2) referential intention (the willingness to recommend the brand to peers), (3) preferential
intention (exhibiting primary brand preference over competitors), and (4) explorative intention (actively seeking
information and following brand developments).

Social Media Marketing
Social Media Marketing (SMM) is defined as the strategic process of creating, communicating, and
delivering value through collaborative digital platforms to foster customer relationships and build brand equity
(Kaplan & Haenlein, 2010; Yadav & Rahman, 2017 ). Yadav & Rahman (2017) established that effective SMM
consists of five essential operational dimensions: (1) content quality (aesthetic appeal, creativity, and visual
production excellence), (2) informativeness (delivering accurate, timely, and complete product information), (3)
interactivity (facilitating responsive two -way engagement and dialogue), (4) personalization (tailoring content to

Brand Authenticity
Brand Authenticity captures the consumer's subjective perception that a brand is true to itself, honest,
reliable, and deeply committed to its core values (Morhart et al., 2015; Napoli et al., 2014). In competitive lifestyle
markets, authenticity serves as a powerful defense against consumer cynicism. Morhart et al. (2015) operationalize
brand authenticity across four distinct dimensions: (1) continuity (historical consistency and stability in style and
character over time), (2) credibility (transparency, truthfulness, and honesty in product claims), (3) integrity (moral
responsibility and consistency between values and actions), and (4) symbolism (cultural resonance, local pride, and
personal identity alignment).

Brand Image
Brand Image refers to the collective set of perceptual impressions, beliefs, and emotional associations linked
to a brand in consumer memory (Keller, 1993; Kotler & Keller, 2016). Keller (1993) emphasizes that brand image
is structured along three primary dimensions: (1) brand strength (confidence in functional quality, durability, and
reliability), (2) brand favorability (positive affective evaluations and belief that brand attributes satisfy consumer
needs), and (3) brand uniqueness (distinctive competiti ve attributes that clearly differentiate the brand from
alternative choices). In this investigation, brand image is hypothesized to mediate the transmission of marketing
stimuli to transactional outcomes.

Empirical Studies
An & Ngo (2025) examined the influence of Social Media Marketing Activities (SMMAs) on Generation
Z’s purchase intentions and eWOM, with perceived quality and perceived value as mediating factors. Using a
quantitative approach, data were collected from 315 Generation Z participan ts in Vietnam through a structured
questionnaire. The study explore d various dimensions of SMMAs  interaction, customization, trendiness, and
entertainment and their effects on perceived quality, perceived value, eWOM, and purchase intention. Results show
that SMMAs have a stronger impact on perceived quality than on perceived value suggesting that social media
marketing strategies are particularly effective in shaping how Generation Z evaluates product quality. Additionally,
perceived quality significantly influences perceived value, eWOM, and purchase intention. Similarly, perceived
value positively impacts eWOM and purchase intention while eWOM itself plays a direct role in driving purchase
intention. These findings highlight the interconnected role of social media marketing in influencing consumer
behavior through both direct and mediated effects.
Atrisia, Syafawi, & Sari  (2023) set out to look at how social media marketing in the fashion business in
Indonesia, a developing nation, affects consumers' propensity to make a purchase. This study surveyed 118 people
in Indonesia using an online survey instrument. The bootstrappi ng method was used to test the hypotheses and the
mediating roles of brand awareness and customer satisfaction. Despite the direct influence of social media marketing
on purchase intention, brand awareness and customer satisfaction do not mediate the relationship between the two.

RESEARCH METHOD

Research Approach
This study employs a quantitative causal -associative research design. Causal -associative research
investigates cause-and-effect relationships and tests theoretical propositions by analyzing statistical associations and
mediating mechanisms among measured constructs (Creswell & Creswell, 2018).

Population, Sample Size, and Sampling Techniques
The target population comprises Generation Z consumers residing in Manado who are active social media
users and familiar with TeeFees Clothing Brand. Because the exact size of this consumer population cannot be
determined definitively, non -probability purp osive sampling was deployed. Specific purposive inclusion criteria
required respondents to: (1) belong to Generation Z (aged 14 to 29 years old during data collection), (2) reside or
study in Manado, North Sulawesi, (3) actively use social media (Instagram or TikTok), and (4) follow or be actively
familiar with TeeFees Clothing Brand. A sample of 200 qualified respondents was successfully recruited.

Data Type, Source, and Collection Method
Primary data was collected directly through a self -administered structured online questionnaire hosted on
Google Forms and distributed across social media channels (Instagram and TikTok). Secondary data was acquired
from academic journals, textbooks, indus try statistics (DataReportal, BAPAREKRAF), and brand social media
archives. All questionnaire items were measured using a 5-point Likert scale ranging from 1 ('Strongly Disagree') to
5 ('Strongly Agree').

Data Analysis Methods
Partial Least Squares (PLS-SEM)
PLS-SEM is especially suited for complex causal models incorporating mediating constructs,
formative/reflective indicators, and exploratory extensions of theory (Hair et al., 2019). The analytical procedure
involves two systematic stages:
1. Measurement Model (Outer Model) Evaluation: Evaluates construct reliability and validity. Indicator reliability
requires outer factor loadings ≥ 0.708. Internal consistency reliability is confirmed when Cronbach's Alpha and
Composite Reliability (rho_c) exceed 0.70. Convergent validity requires an Average Variance Extracted (AVE)
≥ 0.50. Discriminant validity is appraised via the Fornell -Larcker criterion where the square root of AVE for
each construct must exceed its bivariate correlations with all other constructs and cross-loading inspection
2. Structural Model (Inner Model) Evaluation: Evaluates predictive power and hypothesis significance.
Multicollinearity is assessed via Variance Inflation Factor (VIF < 5.0, ideally < 3.0). Explanatory power is
measured by the Coefficient of Determination (R2 values of 0.75, 0.50, and 0.25 indicate substantial, moderate,
and weak power, respectively). Effect sizes are evaluated using Cohen's f2 (0.02 small, 0.15 medium, 0.35 large).
Out-of-sample predictive relevance is verified using PLSpredict (Q2 predict > 0). Path coefficients (β) and
statistical significance are evaluated through non-parametric bootstrapping with 5,000 resamples at a two-tailed
significance level of α = 0.05 (critical t ≥ 1.96, p < 0.05). Finally, mediation effects are tested by calculating the
Variance Accounted For (VAF = Indirect Effect / Total Effect); VAF > 80% denotes full mediation, 20%–80%
denotes partial mediation, and < 20% indicates no mediation

leverage digital channels and strategic positioning to drive consumer engagement and purchase decisions among
younger cohorts. By integrating social media marketing activities and brand authenticity into a unified framework,
the results shed light on the cognitive and affective pathways that shape consumer behavior in a regional market.
This study validates the strategic importance of building strong brand perceptions rather than relying solely on direct

functions as a signal of value congruence, strengthening the  emotional and identity -based bond between Gen Z
consumers and the brand.
A particularly important finding is that brand image acts as a key psychological mediator that links marketing
inputs to purchase intentions. Within the theoretical framework of the Stimulus -Organism-Response (S -O-R)
paradigm proposed by Mehrabian and Russ ell (1974), social media marketing activities and brand authenticity
signals function as environmental stimuli (Stimulus) that act upon the consumer's internal cognitive and emotional
states. The resulting brand image represents the organismic processing ( Organism) that registers, evaluates, and
consolidates these marketing inputs, which subsequently drives purchase intention as the final behavioral output
(Response).
  Furthermore, the results demonstrate that both social media marketing and brand authenticity exert direct
influences on purchase intentions, highlighting the multiple pathways through which consumer decisions are formed.
Viewed through the lens of Consumer Behavior Theories, purchase intentions are shaped not only by mediated brand
perceptions but also by immediate digital touchpoints and direct evaluations of brand integrity. While a positive
brand image is a critical long -term asset, immediate consumer actions can also be stimulated by direct value
congruence and highly relevant social media content that simplifies the consumer decision-making journey.
These findings are particularly relevant considering the unique psychological and behavioral characteristics
of Generation Z consumers, who operate as digitally native individuals with high media literacy and a strong
preference for authentic brand relationships. Drawing from contemporary Consumer Behavior Theories, this cohort
is characterized by a deep skepticism toward traditional, corporate advertising, relying instead on transparent
communications, peer recommendations, and community-driven consumption. The study shows that to successfully
engage Gen Z, local brands must align their promotional activities with genuine social values and community stories.
Overall, this study highlights that the commercial viability of regional clothing brands in the digital age depends on
a balanced approach that combines digital marketing with authentic brand building. By showing that brand image
mediates the effects of social media marketing and brand authenticity on purchase intentions, this research offers a
valuable framework for local businesses. Grounded in Consumer Behavior Theories and generational characteristics,
the study indicates that fashion brand managers must invest in high-quality digital content and cultivate a transparent,
culturally resonant brand identity to build sustainable competitive advantages.

Conclusion

1. Social Media Marketing exerts a positive and significant direct influence on the Brand Image of TeeFees

communications on TikTok and Instagram represent the primary foundation for establishing strong, favorable,
and unique brand associations in young consumers' memories.
2. Brand Authenticity has a positive and significant direct influence on Brand Image (β = 0.488, t = 10.997, p <
0.001). The consistency, credibility, integrity, and cultural symbolism embedded in TeeFees' brand identity
significantly reinforce consumer trust  and elevate its competitive differentiation against mass -market
alternatives.
