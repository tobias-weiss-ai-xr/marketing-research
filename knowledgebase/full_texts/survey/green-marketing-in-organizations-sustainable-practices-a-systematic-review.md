ABSTRACT

Objective: The objective of this study is to investigate the potential organizational applications of green marketing
that contribute to sustainability.

Theoretical framework: The relationship between marketing and sustainability poses challenges for
organizations, which can support the proposal of green marketing to encourage a shift away from traditional
commercial practices toward a model that places the environment at its core.

Method: The methodology adopted for this research comprises a systematic literature review, based on a search
for articles on the subject in the Scopus database, which served to support the information used for the research
analysis.

Results and Discussion: The results revealed that studies on green marketing are not yet well-established and that
there has been an increase in publications since 2019. Green marketing can contribute to sustainable value creation
within organizational dynamics; it fosters an env ironment of shared responsibility in product purchasing; and it
influences the organization’s perspective on the environment.

Research Implications: The limitations of this article are evident in the scope of the data collected, which, while
providing an overview of the current state of the field, leave open the question of the best approach for future
research.

Originality/Value: The gap addressed by this study lies in the need for future research that explores this topic in
greater depth. By demonstrating a deeper understanding of the current state of the art, the authors pave the way for
the development of future frameworks that can be tested within organizations.

Keywords: Green Marketing, Environmental Marketing, Sustainable Consumption.

1 INTRODUCTION

Market transformations are driven by the demand for sustainable consumption,
reflecting a call for actions that preserve the environment and promote quality of life
(Dangelico; Vocalelli, 2017; Nekmahmud, 2020). Marketing professionals need to value
limited natural resources and adopt sustainable approaches in their proposals, using green
marketing solutions to meet these demands with minimal environmental impact ( Amoako et
al., 2020; Vilkaite-Vaitone et al., 2022).
This study analyzes how knowledge of and the adoption of green marketing strategies
can guide sustainable solutions that align with new consumption patterns (AMOAKO et al.,
2020; Baumann et al ., 2002). Discussions on organizational practices reveal that many
companies are still in the early stages of implementing sustainability and face social and
economic challenges (Chen et al., 2019). This research seeks to answer: Which applications of
green marketing can generate sustainable benefits?
The systematic review in this study followed the PRISMA model (Moher et al., 2015)
and highlights the importance of green marketing in organizational strategies, promoting
sustainable consumption practices (Saleem, 2021). Green marketing activities impact strategic
and operational processes, enabling companies to develop products with a lower environmental
impact and to inform the public about the effects of consumption on society ( Susanty et al.,
2021; Vilkaite-Vaitone et al., 2022).
Furthermore, the study emphasizes the need to organize knowledge regarding the
applications of green marketing . Although theoretical contributions exist, there is a lack of
justification for the challenges faced by organizations in implementing sustainable practices.
The current debate indicates that many companies are still in the early stages of this journey.
To deepen this analysis, publications from the Scopus database spanning the last ten
years (2013–2022) were selected, applying the principles of the PRISMA method. The article
is structured into an introduction, a theoretical framework on green marketing, methods used,
results and discussions, followed by final considerations.

3 METHODOLOGY: SYSTEMATIC REVIEW OF GREEN MARKETING

The study employed a systematic review in accordance with the PRISMA guidelines
(MOHER et al., 2015), which guided the analysis of results and the eligibility of studies. The
systematic survey of relevant publications contributes to updating academic studies and
informing future research.
The stages of the systematic review include:
1. Formulation of the guiding question: Definition of the study’s objective.
2. Identification of articles: Selection of the database, in this case, Scopus.
3. Search criteria : Use of keywords such as “ green marketing ,” “environmental
marketing,” and “eco-marketing.”
4. Data collection: Analysis of the selected publications.
5. Results: Analysis of the findings relevant to the study.
6. Interpretations: Considerations regarding the main information extracted.
The guiding research question was: “What organizational applications of green
marketing can generate sustainable benefits?”, focusing on publications from the last ten years
(2013–2022). Data collection considered criteria such as impact factor and the English
language, resulting in the analysis of 284 articles using relevance filters and int erpretation of
the full-text articles.

After the data were collected (as shown in Figure 01), the data were filtered and the final
criteria for article selection were established, using the following: (i) the Field-Weighted
Citation Impact  (FWCI), a metric used to calculate the ratio of an article’s citations to the
average number of citations received by all similar articles over a 3-year period, revealing how
well-cited the document is compared to others (Halevi; Moed; Bar-Ilan, 2016; Khor; YU, 2016).
An FWCI greater than 1 means that the article is cited more than expected based on the average
(Scopus®, 2022); and (ii) the journals had an h-index greater than or equal to 50.
The h-index is a metric used by the Coordination for the Improvement of Higher
Education Personnel (CAPES) to classify the quality of a journal. A journal with an h-index
greater than or equal to 50 is classified as A1 or A2 in the fields of Public and Business
Administration, Accounting, and Tourism, as shown in Figure 01, which presents the scheme
used for filtering and selecting articles.
Based on this metric, publications from the Scopus® database were considered, as it is
regarded as one of the most reliable and relevant databases worldwide for academic studies
(Grácio; Oliveira, 2012; Guerrero-Bote; Moya-Anegón, 2012). Finally, these 18 articles were
reviewed; they addressed the central question of the study and the impact of the selected
contributions met the criteria of this research note.

4 RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

4.1 RESULTS OF THE SYSTEMATIC LITERATURE REVIEW

The publications analyzed were selected based on pre -established criteria, resulting in
18 relevant articles. These results indicate that topics related to green marketing have gained
prominence over the past ten years, particularly in practices that promo te sustainable products
and alternative consumption solutions. This trend reflects an increase in the impact of green
marketing on organizations, taking into account consumer behavior and the perception of values
associated with sustainable products, as we ll as concerns regarding public health and
environmental protection.
The articles were analyzed using NVivo software, which facilitated the identification of
the most significant terms. Articles No. 13 and 18 were considered particularly relevant due to
their approach and h-index of 200, highlighting their influence. The year of publication (2022)
was also taken into account, as it affects citations and the FWCI factor. Following evaluation,
both articles were recognized for their significant contributions to the subject matter.
The results of the data collection are presented in Table 1, which details the topics
addressed, publication titles, authors, year of publication, journal, number of citations in
Scopus, and the impacts measured by the h-index and FWCI of the selected articles.
Based on these results, it was possible to infer that studies on the green marketing
approach are not yet well -established and have seen a growing number of publications since
2019.

academic studies across diverse organizational contexts. In particular, the selected studies fall

organizations: (a) differentiation and competitiveness strategies, (b) new consumption criteria,
which depend on consumer behavior in purchasing decisions, (c) marketing attributes that drive
sustainable practices, and (d) the perception of value in terms of quality of life.

The analyses show that green marketing has a direct impact on marketing strategies,

6 CONCLUSION

This study analyzed the applications of green marketing in sustainable organizations.
The results showed that market trends increase environmental awareness, influencing
marketing strategies and creating opportunities for changes in companies’ image and offerings.
Green marketing generates positive impacts on the emerging economy, benefiting
consumers, the government, and society by increasing commitment to the environment. It
benefits the industry through innovative proposals and creates a win -win situation, aligni ng
with consumer demands and the UN’s sustainable development goals while promoting
conscious actions regarding scarce natural resources.

6.1 RESEARCH LIMITATIONS

The limitations of this research include the exclusive use of the Scopus platform, which,
although widely respected, may restrict the scope of the study. The research prioritized impact
criteria (h-index and FWC) in the selection of publications.

6.2 CONTRIBUTIONS TO FUTURE STUDIES

Future research that integrates literature reviews and technological solutions for green
marketing, using methodological approaches from the applied social sciences, will contribute
to advancing this field. Furthermore, empirical studies on the challenges of strategic marketing
planning in developing countries and certifications in green marketing practices can enrich the
understanding and maturity of the field.
