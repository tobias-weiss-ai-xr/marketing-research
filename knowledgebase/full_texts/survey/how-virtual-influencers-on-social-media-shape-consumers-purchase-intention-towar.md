Introduction: With the rapid development of social media, virtual influencers
have emerged as an important information source in brand communication and
digital marketing, demonstrating considerable potential in promoting sustainable
consumption, such as organic food. However, empirical research examining how
virtual influencer characteristics influence consumers’ purchase intention toward
organic food and the underlying psychological mechanisms remains limited.
Methods: Grounded in the Stimulus–Organism–Response (SOR) framework,
this study develops a theoretical model linking virtual influencer characteristics,
consumer engagement, and organic food purchase intention, and systematically
investigates the effects of attractiveness, authenticity, and trustworthiness on
consumers’ purchase intention. Using a quantitative research design, data were
collected through an online survey, yielding 447 valid responses.
Results: Structural equation modeling was employed to test the proposed hypoth-
eses, and a bias-corrected bootstrap procedure was applied to examine the mediat-
ing effects. The results indicate that attractiveness, authenticity, and trustworthiness
of virtual influencers all exert significant positive effects on consumer engagement,
with trustworthiness showing the strongest influence. Consumer engagement, in
turn, has a significant positive effect on purchase intention toward organic food.
Further mediation analysis reveals that consumer engagement partially mediates the
relationships between virtual influencer characteristics and organic food purchase
intention.
Discussion: The findings extend existing research on virtual influencers by situ -
ating it within the context of organic food and sustainable consumption, and
deepen the understanding of consumer engagement as a key psychological
mechanism in digital marketing. From a practical perspective, the study provides
implications for organic food companies and social media marketing strategies,
highlighting the importance of enhancing virtual influencers’ trustworthiness,
attractiveness, and authenticity to stimulate consumer engagement and, ulti -
mately, strengthen purchase intention.

1 Introduction
With the rapid development of social media, digital marketing
practices have continuously evolved, and virtual influencers have
emerged as a novel information source facilitating communication
between brands and consumers (Jhawar et al., 2023). Influencer mar-
keting, which involves encouraging social media influencers to pro-
mote sponsored brands, has become a major component of digital
advertising strategies and is projected to generate approximately USD
33 billion in revenue by 2027 (Giuffredi-Kähr et al., 2022). Notably,
the market value of influencer marketing has grown exponentially
over the past decade, increasing from USD 1.7 billion in 2016 to USD
21.1 billion in 2023, reflecting the substantial commercial expansion
of this marketing model (Fu et al., 2026). Within this rapidly growing
ecosystem, virtual influencers represent a technologically advanced
and increasingly strategic extension of influencer marketing.
Recent market evidence further indicates that the adoption of vir-
tual influencers in digital marketing has accelerated significantly
(Laszkiewicz and Kalinska-Kula, 2023). The global virtual influencer
market is expanding at a double-digit annual growth rate, with an
increasing number of brands incorporating computer-generated influ-
encers into their marketing strategies to enhance brand consistency,
maintain message control, and mitigate reputational risks (Kim et al.,
2023; Pujadas-Gómez et al., 2025). Compared with traditional human
influencers, virtual influencers—created through computer-generated
imagery and artificial intelligence technologies—offer advantages such
as high controllability, consistent visual identity, and sustainable oper-
ation (Audrezet et al., 2025). Owing to these characteristics, virtual
influencers have been increasingly adopted in brand communication
and product promotion strategies (Kim and Park, 2023). In recent
years, their application has expanded beyond low-involvement prod-
uct categories such as fashion and cosmetics to more trust-sensitive
and value-oriented domains, including food, health, and sustainable
consumption, thereby attracting growing attention from both scholars
and practitioners (Park et al., 2024).
In the context of food consumption, rising consumer awareness
of health and environmental issues has positioned organic food as an
important component of sustainable consumption (Li and Shan, 2025;
Wojciechowska-Solis and Barska, 2021). However, organic food prod-
ucts are often associated with price premiums and information asym-
metry, which increase consumers’ perceived risk during the purchase
decision-making process (Pandey et al., 2020). Consequently, con-
sumers tend to rely more heavily on external informational cues and
credible information sources when evaluating organic food products
(Stanton and Cook, 2019; Li and Chen, 2025). Against this backdrop,
identifying effective communication strategies that enhance consum-
ers’ purchase intention toward organic food has become a critical issue
in food marketing and sustainable consumption research (Łopacińska,
2025). Whether virtual influencers can function as a new type of infor-
mation source to influence consumers’ organic food purchase inten-
tion, and through what mechanisms such influence may occur,
remains an open question that warrants systematic investigation.
Existing research on virtual influencers has predominantly
focused on their visual appearance, interaction styles, and brand
congruence, with most empirical evidence derived from fashion,
cosmetics, and entertainment-related contexts ( Franke et al., 2023 ;
Haikel-Elsabeh, 2023 ). Although these studies generally report
positive effects of virtual influencer characteristics on consumer
attitudes or behavioral intentions ( Gerlich, 2023 ), their findings
remain largely context-specific and provide limited explanatory
power for product categories characterized by higher trust require -
ments and stronger value orientations, such as organic food.
Moreover, empirical research explicitly adopting a consumer
engagement perspective in virtual influencer contexts remains
scarce. Consumer engagement, conceptualized as consumers’ psy -
chological investment and interactive tendencies during informa -
tion exchange, has been widely recognized as a key mechanism
linking external marketing stimuli to behavioral responses in digital
environments (Hollebeek and Macky, 2019 ; Loureiro et al., 2020 ).
However, how consumer engagement operates as an intervening
psychological mechanism in virtual influencer–driven promotion
of organic food—a context involving heightened concerns about
trust, authenticity, and ethical values—has yet to be systematically
examined.
To address these gaps, this study adopts the Stimulus–Organism–
Response (SOR) framework as an integrative theoretical lens. While
SOR has been widely applied in consumer behavior research (Erensoy
et al., 2024), its application to virtual influencer marketing in trust-
sensitive and value-oriented food contexts remains limited. The
framework is particularly suitable for the present study because it
explicitly captures how external marketing stimuli (virtual influencer
characteristics) are internalized through organismic states (consumer
engagement), which subsequently shape behavioral outcomes (organic
food purchase intention). By applying this framework, the present
study moves beyond descriptive associations and provides a mecha-
nism-based explanation of how and why virtual influencers influence
consumers’ purchase intention in organic food digital marketing.
2 Literature review
2.1 SOR theory
The SOR framework was originally developed in the field of envi-
ronmental psychology to explain how external environmental stimuli
influence individuals’ internal psychological states, which in turn lead
to behavioral responses (Vieira, 2013). According to this framework,
individual behavior is not a direct reaction to external stimuli; rather,
it is mediated by internal cognitive and affective processes occurring
at the “organism” stage. As such, the SOR theory emphasizes the
causal transmission from external stimuli to internal psychological
mechanisms and subsequently to behavioral outcomes, providing a
systematic analytical framework for understanding complex con -
sumer behaviors (Erensoy et al., 2024).
In consumer behavior research, the SOR framework has been
widely applied to examine how marketing stimuli affect consumers’
attitudes and behavioral intentions. Prior studies commonly concep-
tualize product attributes, marketing messages, or media characteris-
tics as stimulus variables ( Huang et al., 2022 ; Wei et al., 2025 );
consumers’ cognitive evaluations, emotional responses, or psychologi-
cal states as organism variables (Li and Chen, 2025); and purchase
intention, usage intention, or actual behavior as response variables
(Zhu et al., 2020 ). Compared with direct-effect models, the SOR
framework allows for a more nuanced understanding of the psycho-
logical mechanisms underlying consumer behavior and has therefore
been extensively adopted in studies of digital marketing, online con-
sumption, and social media contexts (Bui et al., 2025).

Within social media marketing environments, modes of informa-

tute critical external stimuli shaping consumer behavior (Wei et al.,
2025). As a novel type of information source, virtual influencers pres-
ent salient visual attributes, content presentation styles, and interac-
tion designs that are readily perceived by consumers and function as
important situational stimuli (Byun and Ahn, 2023). These stimuli do
not directly translate into purchase behavior; instead, they first influ-
ence consumers’ psychological response states during the interaction
process—such as attention allocation, emotional resonance, and will-
ingness to engage—which subsequently shape behavioral decision-
making (Y ang et al., 2025). Accordingly, the SOR framework provides
a robust theoretical foundation for examining the underlying mecha-
nisms through which virtual influencers affect consumer behavior.
Guided by the SOR framework, this study conceptualizes virtual
influencer attractiveness, authenticity, and trustworthiness as external
stimulus variables; consumer engagement as the internal organismic
response formed in the context of virtual influencer–driven promo-
tion; and organic food purchase intention as the ultimate behavioral
response. By constructing a structured “stimulus–organism–response”
pathway, this study aims to systematically elucidate how virtual influ-
encer characteristics influence consumers’ purchase intention toward
organic food through the activation of consumer engagement.
2.2 Virtual influencers
Virtual influencers refer to digitally created personas generated
through computer-generated imagery, artificial intelligence, and digi-
tal modeling technologies (Mrad et al., 2022). These virtual entities
engage in content creation, social interaction, and brand endorsement
on social media platforms in a human-like manner (Ma and Li, 2024).
Compared with human influencers, virtual influencers offer advan-
tages such as high controllability of appearance, lower operational risk,
and greater consistency in content presentation, which has led to their
increasing adoption in brand communication and digital marketing
practices (Gammarano and Arruda Filho, 2025). With continuous
technological advancements, virtual influencers have become increas-
ingly sophisticated in both visual presentation and interactive capabil-
ity, thereby enhancing their perceived social presence and influence
among consumers.
From a consumer behavior perspective, source characteristics are
widely recognized as critical external stimuli shaping consumers’ psy-
chological responses and behavioral intentions in digital environ -
ments ( Shiau et al., 2022 ). Within the SOR framework, virtual
influencers function as information sources whose perceptible attri-
butes constitute external stimuli that are internalized by consumers
and subsequently translated into behavioral outcomes (Zhou et al.,
2024). Prior research identifies attractiveness, authenticity, and trust-
worthiness as core dimensions of source characteristics influencing
consumer responses, and these dimensions are likewise theoretically
relevant in virtual influencer contexts ( Laszkiewicz and Kalinska-
Kula, 2023; Y oo et al., 2025). However, existing studies have largely
examined these attributes in low-involvement or hedonic product
categories, leaving their applicability to value-oriented and trust-sen-
sitive domains, such as organic food, insufficiently explored.
In organic food consumption, consumers often exhibit heightened
concerns regarding product authenticity, health benefits, and ethical
production practices (Grauel, 2016). Although virtual influencers are
not human agents, prior studies suggest that trust toward digital
information sources can be formed through perceived credibility,
message consistency, and value congruence rather than human
authenticity alone (Hollebeek and Macky, 2019). The stable personas,
coherent narratives, and controlled information disclosure typically
associated with virtual influencers may reduce perceived uncertainty
and enhance information reliability (Lim and Lee, 2023), thereby pro-
viding a theoretically plausible basis for their influence in trust-sensi-
tive consumption contexts.
Attractiveness of virtual influencers primarily manifests in visual
design, esthetic appeal, and the hedonic quality of content presenta-
tion (Kim and Baek, 2024). Synthesizing prior findings, studies indi-
cate that higher perceived attractiveness enhances attention, emotional
resonance, and interaction willingness in social media environments
(Loureiro et al., 2020; Stein et al., 2024). Nevertheless, most evidence
remains confined to entertainment or fashion-related contexts (Zhang
et al., 2025), and whether such effects translate into deeper consumer
engagement in value-oriented domains such as organic food remains
underexplored. Accordingly, this study proposes:
H1a: Virtual influencer attractiveness has a positive effect on
consumer engagement.
Authenticity reflects consumers’ perceptions of persona consis-
tency, credibility of content expression, and congruence between the
virtual influencer and the endorsed brand (Ju et al., 2024). Although
virtual influencers are inherently artificial, prior research suggests that
perceived authenticity can still emerge when communication appears
coherent, interaction styles are natural, and value alignment is salient
(Lou et al., 2023). Empirical studies further indicate that authenticity
enhances psychological involvement and interaction tendencies by
strengthening perceived credibility and reducing skepticism (Kothari
et al., 2025). However, how authenticity-driven engagement operates
in organic food promotion has not been systematically examined.
Therefore, the following hypothesis is proposed:
H1b: Virtual influencer authenticity has a positive effect on con-
sumer engagement.
Trustworthiness refers to consumers’ overall evaluation of the reli-
ability, expertise, and perceived motives of an information source (Yo o
et al., 2025). Synthesized evidence from digital marketing research
suggests that source trustworthiness plays a particularly salient role in
value-oriented consumption contexts, where perceived risk and infor-
mation asymmetry are relatively high (Khwaja et al., 2020; Xu et al.,
2024). When virtual influencers are perceived as trustworthy, consum-
ers are more likely to accept the conveyed information and exhibit
higher levels of psychological investment and interactive engagement
(Kim et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025). Despite its theoretical importance,
empirical research examining trustworthiness-driven engagement in
virtual influencer–based organic food promotion remains limited.
Accordingly, this study proposes:
H1c: Virtual influencer trustworthiness has a positive effect on
consumer engagement.

Consumer engagement is commonly conceptualized as a holistic
state of cognitive, emotional, and behavioral investment that

consumers exhibit during interactions with brands, products, or infor-
mation sources ( Bilro and Loureiro, 2020 ). Synthesizing prior
research, studies generally agree that consumer engagement reflects
the depth and quality of consumers’ interactive relationships in digital
environments, encompassing not only observable behaviors (e.g.,
liking, commenting, and following) but also underlying psychological
involvement (Theodorakopoulos and Theodoropoulou, 2024 ). As
social media platforms increasingly facilitate two-way communication
and value co-creation, consumer engagement has emerged as a central
construct in explaining how marketing stimuli are internalized and
translated into consumer responses (Cheung et al., 2021).
Within the SOR framework, consumer engagement is widely
regarded as a key organismic state that mediates the relationship
between external stimuli and behavioral outcomes (Piligrimienė et al.,
2020). Rather than exerting direct effects on behavior, external mar-
keting stimuli first influence consumers’ psychological involvement,
which subsequently shapes their behavioral intentions (Shiau et al.,
2022). In virtual influencer–driven promotional contexts, perceptible
characteristics of virtual influencers—such as attractiveness, authen-
ticity, and trustworthiness—serve as salient external stimuli, while
consumer engagement captures consumers’ psychological and interac-
tive tendencies formed during repeated exposure to and interaction
with influencer-generated content (Yu et al., 2024). Accordingly, con-
sumer engagement represents a critical psychological mechanism
through which virtual influencer characteristics are expected to influ-
ence consumer behavior.
Prior empirical research reports a positive association between
consumer engagement and purchase intention across various digital
marketing contexts (Bergel et al., 2019 ; Habib et al., 2022). Highly
engaged consumers tend to develop stronger attitudinal alignment,
emotional attachment, and perceived relevance, thereby increasing the
likelihood of favorable purchase decisions. However, existing evidence
is predominantly derived from conventional product categories and
brand communities (Matute et al., 2021; Wong and Chung, 2023),
offering limited insight into value-oriented and trust-sensitive
domains such as organic food consumption. In such contexts, pur-
chase decisions are often shaped by concerns related to health benefits,
environmental responsibility, and product credibility, suggesting that
the engagement–purchase relationship may operate through more
complex psychological processes (Lazaroiu et al., 2019).
By repeatedly interacting with promotional content, consumers
can deepen their understanding, reduce perceived uncertainty, and
strengthen emotional involvement, all of which are particularly salient
in organic food decision-making (Iqbal et al., 2021). Nevertheless,
despite the recognized importance of consumer engagement in digital
marketing (Gu and Duan, 2024), its mediating role in virtual influ-
encer–driven organic food promotion remains underexplored.
Addressing this gap, the present study explicitly examines consumer
engagement as a key mechanism linking virtual influencer character-
istics to organic food purchase intention. Accordingly, the following
hypothesis is proposed:
H2: Consumer engagement has a positive effect on organic food
purchase intention.
Beyond its direct effect, consumer engagement is expected to
mediate the relationships between virtual influencer characteristics
and purchase intention. Synthesizing prior findings, virtual influencer
attractiveness can capture consumers’ attention and interest, thereby
enhancing engagement and facilitating the formation of purchase
intention (AlFarraj et al., 2021; Zhang et al., 2025). Similarly, perceived
authenticity strengthens psychological identification and interaction
willingness, increasing the likelihood that positive purchase evalua-
tions emerge during the engagement process ( Jiang et al., 2025 ).
Moreover, trustworthiness reduces information uncertainty and
enhances perceived credibility, reinforcing consumers’ engagement-
related tendencies and subsequent purchase intention (Chen et al.,
2025). Based on this integrative reasoning, the following hypotheses
are proposed:
H3a: Consumer engagement mediates the relationship between
virtual influencer attractiveness and organic food purchase
intention.
H3b: Consumer engagement mediates the relationship between
virtual influencer authenticity and organic food purchase
intention.
H3c: Consumer engagement mediates the relationship between
virtual influencer trustworthiness and organic food purchase
intention.
2.4 Purchase intention
Purchase intention is widely conceptualized as the subjective
probability that a consumer will engage in a purchasing behavior
toward a specific product or brand in a given future context (Kytö et
al., 2019). Synthesizing prior consumer behavior research, scholars
generally agree that purchase intention serves as a robust proximal
predictor of actual purchasing behavior, reflecting consumers’ evalu-
ative judgments and decision readiness ( Rana and Paul, 2017 ).
Consequently, purchase intention has been extensively employed as a
core outcome variable in studies examining how cognitive evaluations
and psychological processes translate into consumption-related
behaviors.
In the context of organic food consumption, however, the forma-
tion of purchase intention exhibits greater complexity than in conven-
tional food markets. Existing studies indicate that organic food
purchase intention is shaped not only by functional considerations,
such as perceived quality or utility, but also by value-oriented concerns
including health benefits, environmental responsibility, and ethical
considerations (Hemmerling et al., 2015). At the same time, price pre-
miums and information asymmetry surrounding organic food prod-
ucts increase consumers’ perceived risk, making purchase intention
more contingent on external informational cues and source credibility
(Wu et al., 2021). This body of research highlights that organic food
purchase intention is particularly sensitive to how product-related
information is communicated and evaluated.
Recent studies further suggest that social media environments
intensify the situational dependence of purchase intention by enabling
interactive, immersive, and socially embedded information exchange
(Zhang and Zhang, 2023). As a novel type of information source, vir-
tual influencers differ from traditional endorsers in their digitally
constructed personas and controlled content presentation, which may
alter how consumers process and internalize product information
(Tseng and Ou, 2025). While prior research has examined the influ-
ence of influencer marketing on purchase intention ( Li and Peng,
2021), empirical evidence remains fragmented regarding the

2.5 Theoretical model construction
Based on the foregoing review of the SOR framework, virtual
influencer characteristics, consumer engagement, and purchase inten-
tion, this study develops a theoretical model to explain how virtual
influencers promote consumers’ purchase intention toward organic
food in a social media context, as illustrated in Figure 1. The model
aims to elucidate, from a consumer behavior perspective, how virtual
influencers, as emerging information sources, influence consumers’
purchase decision-making through the psychological mechanism of
consumer engagement.
Specifically, virtual influencer attractiveness, authenticity, and
trustworthiness are conceptualized as external stimulus variables.
These characteristics represent salient source attributes that consum-
ers can directly perceive when exposed to virtual influencer content
and constitute important situational stimuli shaping consumers’ psy-
chological responses. Consumer engagement is defined as the organ-
ismic variable, reflecting consumers’ psychological involvement and
interaction tendencies formed in the context of virtual influencer–
driven promotion, and serves as a key mediating mechanism linking
external stimuli to behavioral outcomes. Organic food purchase inten-
tion is treated as the response variable, capturing consumers’ behav-
ioral decision tendencies resulting from the combined influence of
external stimuli and internal psychological reactions.
3 Methodology
3.1 Sample and data collection
This study targeted consumers with prior exposure to virtual influ-
encer content on social media, focusing on the context of virtual influ-
encers promoting organic food products. Given that virtual influencers
remain an emerging phenomenon and that no comprehensive sampling
frame exists for identifying consumers with verified exposure, a non-
probability sampling strategy combining convenience sampling and
snowball sampling was adopted. This approach is commonly used in
digital marketing research when the target population is difficult to enu-
merate and when access to information-rich respondents is required
(Emerson, 2015). Snowball sampling was particularly suitable, as con-
sumers who follow or interact with virtual influencers are often embed-
ded in online social networks with similar media consumption behaviors.
To ensure alignment between the sample and the research objec-
tives, a screening question was placed at the beginning of the ques-
tionnaire to confirm respondents’ prior exposure to virtual
influencer–related content. Only those who met this criterion were
allowed to proceed. Respondents were then presented with a stan-
dardized scenario describing virtual influencers promoting organic
food, including product information, content presentation, and inter-
action formats. This procedure ensured that all participants completed
the questionnaire under a consistent research context, thereby
enhancing response comparability and internal validity. Data were
collected via an online questionnaire, which consisted of demographic
questions and measurement items for virtual influencer characteris-
tics, consumer engagement, and organic food purchase intention.
Prior to the formal survey, a pilot test was conducted to assess item
clarity and contextual appropriateness, and minor wording refine-
ments were made accordingly.
A total of 532 questionnaires were collected through social media
platforms. After excluding incomplete responses and abnormal
response patterns, 447 valid questionnaires were retained for analysis,
yielding an effective response rate of 84.02%. With regard to sample
size adequacy, the final sample exceeds commonly recommended
thresholds for covariance-based structural equation modeling (CB-
SEM), which suggest a minimum of 200 cases or a ratio of at least 10
observations per estimated parameter (Gaskin et al., 2025). Given the
complexity of the proposed model, a sample size of 447 is considered
sufficient to ensure reliable parameter estimation and robust hypoth-
esis testing. The demographic characteristics of the sample are sum-
marized in Table 1, indicating adequate diversity for examining the
proposed model in the context of social media–based organic food
promotion.

4 Results
4.1 Reliability and validity assessment
Prior to testing the structural model, the reliability and validity of
the measurement model were systematically assessed (Cheung et al.,
2024; Fornell and Larcker, 1981). As shown in Table 3, the Cronbach’s
alpha coefficients for all latent constructs ranged from 0.806 to 0.838,
exceeding the recommended threshold of 0.70 and indicating satisfac-
tory internal consistency. In addition, the composite reliability (CR)
values for all constructs were above 0.80, further confirming the reli-
ability of the measurement model.
With respect to convergent validity, all standardized factor load-
ings were greater than 0.70, suggesting that the observed indicators
adequately represent their corresponding latent constructs. Moreover,
the average variance extracted (AVE) values for all constructs exceeded
the recommended cutoff of 0.50, indicating that each construct
explains a substantial proportion of the variance in its measurement
items and demonstrating adequate convergent validity. Discriminant
validity was assessed using the Fornell–Larcker criterion. As presented
in Table 4, the square roots of the AVE values for all constructs were
greater than the inter-construct correlation coefficients, indicating
satisfactory discriminant validity among the latent variables.
4.2 Common method bias
To assess the potential risk of common method bias (CMB), mul-
tiple statistical procedures were employed. First, Harman’s single-factor
test was conducted by entering all measurement items into an unro-
tated exploratory factor analysis (Podsakoff et al., 2003). The results
indicate that the first factor accounted for 42.009% of the total variance,
which is below the commonly accepted threshold of 50%. This suggests
that no single factor dominated the measurement results, and common
method bias is unlikely to pose a serious threat to the study’s findings.
In addition, a confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) was performed
to further examine common method bias by specifying a single-factor
model in which all measurement items were loaded onto one latent
construct (Mishra, 2016). The fit of this single-factor model was then
compared with that of the proposed multi-factor measurement model.
The results show that the single-factor model exhibited poor overall
fit ( χ2/df = 6.958, CFI = 0.777, TLI = 0.747, RMSEA = 0.116,
SRMR = 0.074), which was substantially worse than the fit of the
multi-factor measurement model ( χ2/df = 1.778, CFI = 0.973,
TLI = 0.967, RMSEA = 0.042, SRMR = 0.032). These findings further
indicate that common method bias is unlikely to have a dominant
influence on the measurement structure or the empirical results of
this study.

5 Conclusion
5.1 Main findings
Grounded in the SOR framework, this study examines how virtual
influencers on social media shape consumers’ purchase intention
toward organic food through the mediating role of consumer engage-
ment. By integrating structural equation modeling with bias-corrected
bootstrap mediation analysis, the findings offer several theoretically
meaningful insights into the psychological mechanisms underlying
virtual influencer–driven marketing effects.
First, the results demonstrate that all three dimensions of virtual
influencer characteristics—attractiveness, authenticity, and trustwor-
thiness—exert significant positive effects on consumer engagement.
From the perspective of the SOR framework, these characteristics
function as salient external stimuli that capture consumers’ attention,
enhance perceived relevance, and reduce informational ambiguity in
social media environments. Consistent with prior studies in digital
influencer marketing, attractiveness enhances hedonic appeal and
attentional engagement (Pérez-Vega et al., 2018), while authenticity
fosters psychological identification and perceived sincerity (Fritz et al.,
2017). Notably, trustworthiness exhibits the strongest effect on con-
sumer engagement, underscoring the critical role of source credibility
in value-oriented and trust-sensitive product contexts such as organic
food (Busch et al., 2024). This finding converges with earlier research
emphasizing that consumers rely more heavily on credible informa-
tion sources when evaluating products associated with health, envi-
ronmental responsibility, and ethical attributes (Nagy et al., 2022 ).
Compared with studies focusing on low-involvement products (e.g.,
fashion or cosmetics), the present results extend existing evidence by
demonstrating that trustworthiness becomes particularly salient in
sustainable food consumption contexts.
Second, consumer engagement is found to have a significant posi-
tive impact on organic food purchase intention. This finding supports
the SOR framework’s proposition that organismic responses play a
central role in translating external stimuli into behavioral outcomes.
Rather than responding directly to virtual influencer characteristics,
consumers first develop varying levels of psychological and behavioral
involvement—such as attention, emotional resonance, and interactive
participation—which subsequently shape their purchase intentions.
This result aligns with prior empirical research indicating that engaged
consumers tend to exhibit stronger attitudinal alignment and higher
behavioral commitment in digital marketing environments (Diao et
al., 2025; Kamyabi et al., 2025). In the organic food context, where
purchase decisions are often accompanied by uncertainty and value
trade-offs, engagement appears to serve as a mechanism that deepens
information processing and reduces perceived risk, thereby strength-
ening purchase intention.
Third, the mediation analysis reveals that consumer engage -
ment partially mediates the relationships between virtual influencer
characteristics and organic food purchase intention. This finding
suggests that while engagement constitutes a key organismic mech -
anism within the SOR framework, virtual influencer characteristics
may also influence purchase intention through additional cognitive
or affective pathways not fully captured by engagement alone.
Similar patterns of partial mediation have been reported in prior
studies on influencer marketing and social media persuasion, indi -
cating that influencer attributes can simultaneously activate mul -
tiple psychological processes ( Belanche et al., 2021 ; Filieri et al.,
2023). By empirically validating consumer engagement as a partial
mediator in the context of virtual influencers and organic food, this
study contributes to the literature by clarifying how SOR-based
mechanisms operate in sustainable consumption settings and by
extending existing models beyond purely hedonic or entertain -
ment-driven consumption contexts.

This study offers several contributions to the literature on vir -
tual influencers and consumer behavior. First, by applying the

5.3 Limitations
Despite the theoretical and empirical contributions of this
study, several limitations should be acknowledged and provide
avenues for future research. First, this study relies on cross-sec -
tional survey data to examine the relationships among virtual
influencer characteristics, consumer engagement, and purchase
intention. Although structural equation modeling enables the
analysis of complex relationships, causal inference remains lim -
ited. Future research could adopt longitudinal designs or experi -
mental methods to more thoroughly examine the dynamic effects
of virtual influencer characteristics on consumer engagement and
purchase intention. Second, the sample was collected through an
online survey using non-probability sampling methods. While the
sample demonstrates a certain degree of demographic diversity,
its representativeness is limited. Future studies could conduct
cross-cultural comparisons or employ more rigorous sampling
strategies to assess the robustness and generalizability of the find -
ings across different market contexts. Third, this study focuses on
attractiveness, authenticity, and trustworthiness as key dimen -
sions of virtual influencer characteristics and examines consumer
engagement as the primary mediating variable, while other poten -
tially influential factors—such as technological realism, interac -
tion styles, persona design of virtual influencers, as well as
consumer-level characteristics including values, environmental
awareness, or technology acceptance—were not incorporated.
Future research may extend the current framework by integrating
additional variables to develop a more comprehensive under -
standing of virtual influencer marketing effects.
