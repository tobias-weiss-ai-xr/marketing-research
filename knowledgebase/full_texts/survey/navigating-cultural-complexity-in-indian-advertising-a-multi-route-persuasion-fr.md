Abstract
Advertising in India straddles a unique space between rapid sociocultural upheaval and ingrained
tradition, which has led to a known trend of consumer initiated boycotts and curbing of campaigns.
While persuasion through cultural acquisition and persuasion through traditionalism have been studied,
existing models stop short of explaining a phenomenon wherein two sets of seemingly similar
consumers respond discrepantly to the same culturally targeted advertising. This conceptual article
aims at bridging the said chasm by synthesizing the Elaboration Likelihood Model, Cultural Congruence
Theory and dogmatism theory, postulating dogmatism as the crucial psychographic Moderator
explaining why a culturally similar set of consumers will either be embroiled in elaborating on the ad in
the central-route or be swept away in peripheral-route persuasion, processes inﬂuenced by a set of
powerful emotionally charged cues and low-dogmatic advertisers abetting identity-defensive opposition
if they are fed culturally incongruent advertisements, thereby effecting the attitudes toward the ad and
purchase intention in two very dissimilar ways. Provides a psychographic-based segmentation method
for multi-route campaign strategy that mitigates potential market 'alienation', with implications to social
marketing efforts to improve public health, literacy, and gender equity. Shows how the dogmatism
construct can be formally adopted into the ELM–CCT framework within a non-Western, emerging-market
context, and advances theory and concept measure to enable the study of dogmatism in future empirical
research.
1. Introduction
India exempliﬁes perhaps the most complex and strategic advertising environment in the world. With
population in excess of 1.44 billion (United Nations 2019) and 22 recognized languages (Census of India
2011), successful communication strategies cannot rely on mass generalizations (World Bank 2023).
The highly stratiﬁed stratiﬁed composition of India is well-documented: the Census of India notes the
existence of over 3000 caste groups and sub-castes (Census of India 2011). Such structural
heterogeneity has been hypothesized to form a nexus for identity-oriented consumer vulnerability
(Sharma and Bumb 2020). At the same time, the nation is home to a digitized, often globally oriented,
urban millennial population -over 65% of the population as under 35 (The World Bank 2023)- as well as
culturally conservative cohorts for whom religion, caste, and kinship still govern many socio-behavioral
norms. The resulting contrast is what we dub the Indian advertising paradox: advertising appeals that
work well in culturally progressive segments beget defensive encounters among conservative audiences,
conversely.
The paradox has left scholarship and entered the documented world of commercial practice. In 2020,
Tanishq's Interfaith Baby Shower campaign "Ekatvam"-which used illustrations of an inter-faith mother-
and-baby shower as a metonym for unity-elicited a coordinated effort to threaten physical outlets and
get the campaign pulled within 48hours (Limbu and Mukherjee 2025). In 2021, FabIndia's "Jashn-e-
Riwaaz" campaign, ﬂagged-up for cultural effacement for appropriating an Urdu title for a Diwali
collection, precipitated calls for a boycott.

In 2019, Surf Excel's holy Holi campaign "Ring of Fire" was lampooned for its inter-religious messaging.
But the three are distinct: Tanishq's charges directly referenced powerful religiously centralist beliefs
interfaith marriage, whereas the latter consisted only of linguistic-cultural framing that revealed nothing
about doctrinal content. The test is not whether these various campaigns occasionening them, merely
differ along this dimension; it is instead whether Proposition 2 forecasts stiffer defensive mechanisms
against religiously salient alarm-systems than secular cultural challenges.
Despite the academic and commercial signiﬁcance of these examples, existing persuasion frameworks
presently do not incorporate the psychographic factors driving consumer differences in the reception of
culturally threatening messages. Simply adding a demographic variable (e.g., age or location) does not
account for cognitive variations in receptivity to belief-challenging material (Lohman 2010). Although
MOP theory (the ELM; Petty and Cacioppo 1986) acknowledges that "individual differences" mediate the
route to persuasion it does not specify the variable that modulates the role of cultural threat on individual
cognition in this paradigm.
CCT (de Mooij and Hofstede 2010)recognizes the role that cultural ﬁt plays; however, it does not
elaborate on the causal factors generating divergent contextual reactions within cultures. Dogmatism
(Rokeach 1956; Shearman and Levine 2006) articulates that negatively biased cognition drives aversion
to dissonant cues but has yet to be employed as a process moderator in non-Western advertising.
This study ﬁlls these gaps by developing a model in which dogmatism moderates the utility of targeting
in activating different persuasion routes, which in turn affects subsequent attitudinal effects. As a
prerequisite to developing that model, it is essential to ﬁrst understand what dogmatism is not: while
related conceptually, empirically, dogmatism is clearly distinct from NFC and openness-to-experience as
operationalized by Shearman and Levine's (2006) revised dogmatism scale. We elaborate on the
theoretical importance of these constructs being empirically distinct in Section 2.4 below.
The study is guided by four primary research questions:
RQ1: How do culturally targeted advertising stimuli (traditional vs. modern; emotional vs. rational)
differentially activate central versus peripheral processing routes?
RQ2: In what speciﬁc ways does consumer dogmatism moderate the activation of central versus
peripheral processing routes in response to culturally congruent versus incongruent advertising
messages?
RQ3: How does the processing route activated under varying levels of dogmatism mediate the
relationship between advertising stimuli and persuasion outcomes (attitude toward the advertisement
and purchase intention)?
RQ4: Under what speciﬁc conditions does the differential effectiveness of emotional versus rational
appeals emerge for high- versus low-dogmatic consumers?

This work makes three important theoretical contributions. First, it advances the formal integration of
dogmatism as a process-level moderator within the ELM that has been claimed but never systematically
elaborated (Carpenter 2015; Wagner and Petty 2022). Second, it extends CCT by clarifying the
psychological process through which (mis)aligned motivation moderates differential persuasion
outcomes across psychographic segments. Third, it investigates a non-Western, emerging market
context, addressing critiques of the applicability of traditional persuasion theories developed within
Western individualistic cultures (Shavitt, Lee, and Johnson 2018). For practitioners, the framework
provides the ﬁrst empirically validated psychographic segmentation model for application in complex
markets, with implications for message development and campaign risk management. The paper
follows this outline. Section 2 discusses the theory synthesis of CCT, dogmatism psychology, and ELM.
Section 3 presents the model, identiﬁes the three cases within it, and develops ten stage theories.
Section 4 summarizes the insights for theory. Section 5 discusses business and policy implications.
Section 6 detailed future research directions. Section 7 addresses limitations and boundaries. Section 8
concludes.
2. Theoretical Foundations
2.1 Culture, Advertising, and Cultural Congruence Theory
Given that culture-deﬁned as the shared values, norms, symbols and belief systems by which society
creates shared meaning (Hofstede 2001)-is a key factor affecting the effectiveness of advertising, in
India this culture is characterized by high power distance (77 PDI), moderate individualism (48 IDV), high
long-term orientation (51 LTO)by Hofstede's country level indices (Hofstede Insights 2024) and strong
feelings of uncertainty across religions and caste communities (de Mooij and Hofstede 2010). Cultural
Congruence Theory (CCT) provides the formal model whereby culture inﬂuences marketing
effectiveness. CCT hypothesizes that advertising effects are a positive function of feltcongruence,
deﬁned as the extent of ﬁt between the cultural cues of a message and the consumer's cognitive
schemas (de Mooij and Hofstede 2010; Song et al. 2018). High congruence increases cognitive ﬂuency
which then increases positive affectivity and reduces the distance between the self-concept and the
brand (Torelli et al.2012). CCT hypothesizes that cultural incongruity increases CSGC (schema activation
costs); in other words, schema activation occurs at the expense of schema conﬂict costs, and
elaborative effort increases unless the consumer perceives the schema conﬂict as "typical" (Cui et al.
2012). In a new market context, Grant, Bailey, and Ogbuehi (2017) found signiﬁcantly lower brand
evaluation scores for television ads exhibiting a broader cultural mismatch with the local sample than
the corresponding narrowly deﬁned matched ad conditions where product quality signals were held
constant. CCT does not explain why consumers within the same cultural population are differentially
affected by incongruence. Johnson and Grier (2011) illustrate that, for the individual consumer, a sense
of 'felt targetedness'-the belief that the ad was designed speciﬁcally for her-has this positive impact if
perceived intergroup similarity and negative effects if perceived stereotyping. Uskul, Sherman, and
Fitzgibbon (2009) show that collectivists respond better to health messages that are frameddelivery'said
Page 4/25

to be focused on minimizing developmentsin a prevention-oriented manner (individuals who employ this
strategy prefer to avoid the possibility of a bad outcome). Consistent with the constraints imposed by
high UA in Indian cultures, this approach uses loss framing.3 The incorporated individual difference
moderator of dogmatism (eschewing authority) furthers the need for individual differences moderators
in CCT predictions.
2.2 Dogmatism as a Cognitive-Dispositional Construct
In The Open and Closed Mind, Rokeach (1956) deﬁned dogmatism as the 'structural rigidity of an
individual's cognitive system-.that is, whether one's belief and disbelief systems and the relationships
among them are susceptible or resistant to cognitive challenges'. Rokeach drew a crucial conceptual
distinction between the content of people's beliefs (e.g. Religious or political beliefs) and dogmatism-a
structural property of the mind which may exist independently of any speciﬁc content dimension. This is
a key concept in advertising research which suggest that the level of dogmatism has an effects on
persuasion processing that is independent of the cognitive content of the message-a purchaser's
precursors of content domain message acceptance.
The revised 20-item dogmatism scale (Shearman and Levine 2006) displayed robust internal
consistency (Cronbach’s by = .87) as well as empirical discriminant validity from existing measures of
need for cognition (NFC) and right-wing authoritarianism (RWA). Such discrimination is theorized to
matter: NFC measures the motivation for effortful cognition whereas dogmatism measures the
resistance to effortful cognition that would produce belief-inconsistent inference. These are correlated
but incompete, and Section 2.4 explicitly considers their distinct roles in the model below. Cognitively,
high-dogmatic persons update their probabilistic beliefs more slowly (Zmigrod et al. 2021), have reduced
perspective taking (Shearman and Levine 2006), and rely more heavily on heuristics to evaluate value-
laden information (Crowson 2009). Corresponding neural evidence from Zmigrod et al. (2021) suggests
low perceptual sensitivity at the neural level in range of belief updating, and low ﬂexibility for belief
updating after experiencing disconﬁrming evidence.In consumer behavior, dogmatism functions as a
moderator of brand attitude formation especially when messaging cues induce belief-challenging
change (Lohman 2010). Aji and Dharmmesta (2019) among Indonesian Muslim consumers report that
highly dogmatic consumers exhibit lower favorable attitude towards religiously incongruent
advertisements by depressing perceived message credibility-a pathway consistent with peripheral
rejection rather than central-route refutation. While such a mechanism-level anchor is valuable, it is also
based on a single-religion, single-country consumer sample. The generalization to India's multi-religious,
caste-stratiﬁed consumer population -where threat to identity may also trigger protest through Hindu-
Muslim, interfaith angles; regional language or dialect issues; or caste-community based disequilibrium-
is an assumption we explicitly ﬂag, rather than accept as veriﬁed (see also Section 7 on Limitations).
2.3 The Elaboration Likelihood Model (ELM)
The most widely tested theory describing when persuasive messages form or change attitudes is
theElaboration Likelihood Model(Petty and Cacioppo 1986; Petty, Barden, and Wheeler 2009). This theory
Page 5/25

hypothesizes that whether a message results in stable attitude formation depends on how much an
individual thinks-or elaborates-about message arguments (the central route) as opposed to thinking
relying on affective heuristics (the peripheral route). If elaboration occurs, then the message leads to
central route attitudes; if it doesn't, then the message leads to peripheral route attitudes. Central route
attitudes exhibit more temporal stability, greater resistance to counterpersuasion, and stronger
behavioral prediction than peripheral route attitudes (Wagner and Petty 2022; Akbari 2015). Carpenter
(2015)meta-analysis of 134 studies found argument strength contributes to attitudes under high
elaboration (ELM central route) conditions but not under low EL(A)ML peripheral route) conditions-
arguably the most apparent empirical support for the dual route hypothesis. Shahab, Ghazali, and Mohtar
(2021) found in a broad review of 147 ELMA/ inﬂuencer communication literature that the core ELMR2
logic applies in digital and inﬂuencer marketing scenarios when individual-difference moderators are
controlled for- precisely the rationale for adding dogmatism as a moderator to this framework.
2.4 Theoretical Integration: Dogmatism as an ELM
Moderator, and its Distinction from Related Constructs
The logical pathway connecting CCT, dogmatism and the ELM is a linear one. The stimuli in question-the
culturally embedded advertisements (whether defender of the traditional Indian order (values and
practices associated with religion, family, kin, and community) or advocate of the emergent order (values
such as autonomy of the individual, inter religious mate choice, decreasing salience of kin and
community))-elicit schema-based evaluative responses in consumers. CCT argues that the direction of
the consumers' evaluative responses is determined by the match between the advertisement's cues and
the consumer's pre-existing schema.
The ELM proposes that the consumers' evaluative responses determines the extent of motivation to
elaborate, and thus the processing pathway. Dogmatism introduces the crucial link that indicates which
consumers will feel their schema accounts as an identity threat that ultimately overwhelm central-route
processing. But this integration must be precise about what the added value of dogmatism over NFC and
openness-to-experience. NFC indeed measures a motivation to effortful thinking (full-stop) that leads
high-NFC individuals to elaborate more across many types of content. Dogmatism measures a content-
conditional attenuation ofelaboration: high-dogmatic individuals do elaborate more on non-threat
content, and are speciﬁcally motivated to withhold elaboration when it would be strategically effective to
do so. Openness-to-experience captures a broader, more dimensionally represented tendency to
attraction to novelty, to which low dogmatism is highly related. In our proposed model,dogmatism's
unique theoretical contribution to understanding route moderation is the identity-threat hypothesis: the
claim that culturally incongruent content activates deep-seated threat and defensiveness, rather than
exploration, a distinction that NFC and personality openness-to-experience do not themselves predict
directly. Future research validating the current model (see Section 6.1) should also include all 3 variables
as simultaneous predictors. For a high-dogmatic consumer, encountering a signal that is culturally
incongruent engenders identity threat-psychological threat originating from a perception that one's basic
tenets are under assault (Rokeach 1956; Crowson 2009)-which triggers motivated cognition by tolling
Page 6/25

the bell on elaborative engagement (Kunda 1990). In contrast to low-dogmatic consumers who are
motivated to elaborate no matter the circumstance, high-dogmatic consumers will rely on peripheral
cues-emotionally powerful cultural symbols (or emotional dissonance if incongruent) to produce snap,
defense-motivation-induced attitudes-rather than on centrally processed arguments. Because high-
dogmatic consumers' motivation to elaborate-regardless of cultural viability-is unaffected by
identiﬁcation with the message, they can systematically scrutinize the quality of centrally provided
arguments, whereas low-dogmatic consumers cannot. These two factors conspire to deliver the model's
central prediction: a strong interaction emerges between type of appeal and dogmatism level (developed
in the following formal section).

3.2 Development of Propositions
3.2.1 Advertising Stimuli and Processing Route Activation
(P1)
The core assumption of the ELM is that features of the message modify the likelihood that elaboration
will occur thus activating the route (Petty, Barden, and Wheeler 2009). Culturally targeted advertising
stimuli include two independent features pertinent to activating elaboration:1. Cultural frame-whether
message content aﬃrms traditional Indian views (intergenerational family bonds, religious practice,
social cohesion) or describes more progressive experiences(gender role choice, urban cosmopolitanism,
individualistic aspiration) and 2. Type of appeal-whether the persuasive strategy emphasizes a rational
argument (product features, evidence, reasoning) or an emotional response (nostalgia, ﬁliality, fear,
pride). Rational communications provide factual cases for argument (can be elaborated through the
central route if motivation/ability is suﬃcient). Emotional appeals serve as peripheral cues (Petty and
Briñol 2015). Kitchen et al. (2014) ﬁnd thatmessage structure(argument (E.g., product features, evidence,
reasoning) versus heuristic (E.g., height, skin fairness)elaboration of arguments differs as a function of
message structure independently of involvement.
Page 8/25

Proposition 1
(P1): Culturally relevant advertising stimuli differentially elicit processing routes such that (a) of the
messages with rational appeals, those embedded in either cultural frame selectively elicit increased
central elaboration relative to the rational appeals with an emotional framing, and (b) of the messages
with an emotional appeal, those with either cultural frame selectively elicit increased peripheral
processing relative to the emotional appeals with a rational framing (as a sense-making heuristic). A null
test of the proposition would be a lack of an effect of argument density on thought-listing scores
(latencies) relative to emotionally-framed arguments, a result which would fail to support the central
tenets of the ELM Prediction-structure account.
3.2.2 Dogmatism and Peripheral Route Preference under
Cultural Disruption (P2 & P3)
The identity-threat moderation of route activation rests on identity-threat motivation. Both Rokeach
(1956)'s theory and Zmigrod et al. (2021)'s empirical studies suggest that high-dogmatic primaries
interpret culturally-incongruent messages as threats to their identity-constituting beliefs. This identity
threat induces a cognitively closed, low-elaboration processing mode: high-dogmatic consumers do not
elaborate high-elaboration issues and argument sources but instead selectively attend to low-elaboration
peripheral sources to form heuristically driven attitude judgments for defense-motivation. Aji and
Dharmmesta (2019)'s evidence - high-dogmatic primary voters given religiously-incongruent ads produce
less counterargument-generation and more source credibility reliance -presented as a plausible
mechanism-style explanation of how, in the context of 21st-century Indonesia Muslim consumers, the
identity-threat mechanism may operate in response to the temporal-separated images of Indo-Pak
civilization and complex cross-caste interaction. This high-dogmatic mechanism explanation with
Indonesia Muslim consumers as an admittedly unrepresentative sample of India's complex religious and
caste pluralism constitutes only a presumed, not demonstrated, account for all multidimensional forms
of cultural incongruences in India, and cross-cultural validation always remains a matter of future
research (Section 6.5). The Indian context exacerbates the identity-threat described here, given the
simultaneous salience of caste, religious, and regional identities (Sharma & Bumb 2020). Of course, P2
below crucially distinguishes between the intensity of identity threat-cues, depending on what is
underneath the challenge: the Tanishq controversy-directly framing interfaith marriage as a Hindu-
identity threat-with the religiously salient absent of doctrine is a higher-threat event than the FabIndia
controversy, utilizing the innocuous doctrine-free linguistic-cultural framing. It predicts, accordingly, a
higher peripheral preference and more resistant negative attitudes in the Tanishq case.
Proposition 2
(P2): Under expectations of cultural incongruence to the ﬁlmic message frame (i.e., progressive/modern
advertising frame), high-dogmatic consumers are decidedly more likely to process peripheral-route
instead of central-route information because identity-threat related motivation diminishes elaborative
Page 9/25

engagement with argument information. Evidence for the moderating effect of advertising frame to the
inﬂuence of dogmatism that is consistent with the identity-threat model would include an interaction
whereby a given advertisement would elicit lower argument elaboration by high-dogmatic audiences in
the presence of culturally incongruent than instead of congruent advertisements. If instead, high-
dogmatic consumers sent down the same depth of argument elaboration instructions in the presence of
culturally congruent and incongruent advertisements, then evidence for the absence of a moderation
effect of advertising frame, consistent with the identity threat model, would have been provided.
Proposition
(P3): When dogmatism is low, consumers will be more likely to adopt the central route than when
dogmatism is high, since such consumers exhibit an openness disposition to maintain the elaborative
motivation available when cultural content is novel. This effect will be strengthened by the availability of
high-quality rational arguments and weakened when appeals are purely emotional. Null result will be
found if the depth of elaboration of low-dogmatic consumers can not surpass that of high-dogmatic
members when the message is dissonant with culture.
3.2.3 Differential Appeal Effectiveness by Dogmatism
Level (P4 & P5)
The differential route preferences shown in P2 and P3 make predictions that can be directly tested with
respect to appeal effectiveness-when high-dogmat consumers are exposed to culturally consonant
messages, emotional appeals serve as a identity-check, granting consumers the beneﬁt of culture on an
emotional level while thereby reinforcing leanings toward the positive via the peripheral route without
engaging the central (elaborative) route (Kujur and Singh 2018). This is in line with Petty and Briñol's
(2015) emotion-as-information model: A positive affect arises from a congruent emotional appeal that
conﬁrms the consumer's existing cultural schema, resulting in positive attitude toward the ad through an
iden 744-tity matching heuristic. Yet for high-dogmatic consumers, cultural incongruence prevents the
same emotional appeals from offering this beneﬁt, and instead may bolster defensive rejection by
beckoning more intense focus on identity-phenomena.
Proposition 4
(P4): On the whole, positive attitude toward the ad will be signiﬁcantly greater with high-dogmatic
consumers versus low-dogmatic consumers under emotional appeals than under rational appeals, but
only when the advertising frame is culturally congruent with dogmatism. This positive attitude difference
will be mediated by identity-validation emotion. Under culturally incongruent condition, the positive
attitude difference will be no longer signiﬁcant, and emotion may even lead to more defensive rejection
for high-dogmatic consumer in the case of emotional appeals. A null hypothesis will be concluded if the
three-way Audience Dogmatism × Appeal Type × Cultural Congruence interaction effect of P4 on Aad is
statistically non-signiﬁcant in the experimental testing.

For low-dogmatic consumers, then, effect advantage ﬂips back to rational appeals in culturally
progressive situations. Because low-dogmatic consumers adopt a reasoned reasoning style, they
evaluate arguments based on their strength rather than to maintain identity. Meta-analytic evidence from
Carpenter (2015) substantiates that argument quality effects are strongest with high-ELM, exactly the
typifying factor for low-dogmatic consumers in this model. In culturally progressive settings, rational
appeals serve as argumentative support structures that enable low-dogmatic consumers to
systematically process novel information for long-lasting attitude change (Minton 2020).
Proposition 5
(P5): When the frame of ad is culturally progressive or culture disruptive, low-dogmatic consumers will
show signiﬁcantly more positive net attitude toward ad (Aad) in response to rational appeals than high-
dogmatic consumers by the mediated moderation effect of argument elaboration quality via the central
route. A null result will be observed if the argument quality will have similar impact on Aad for high and

3.2.4 Cultural Alignment as Identity Aﬃrmation for High-
Dogmatic Consumers (P6)
For high- dogmatic consumers, however, cultural consistency was not just a schema-matching facilitator
(as CCT predicts for all consumers); it was a psychologically unique identity-aﬃrmation device. When ad
content conﬁrmed the cultural paradigm within which the consumer had been raised-by aﬃrming the
consumer's use ofreligious iconry,eddyingthe traditional family unit,encierlingthe community's will-it
provided what Kunda (1990) calledmotivational conﬁrmation, evidence that the consumer's strongly-held
views had empirical social-psychological backing. The identity-aﬃrmation aspect of cultural congruence
clearly tamped down the psychological drive for defensive processing, allowing peripheral route
acceptance of the ad's persuasive goals.
Limbu and Mukherjee (2025) ﬁnd in the Indian ad market that cultural value congruence profoundly
predictsAaDetalone, with this effect ampliﬁed by consumer GC, a construct closely aligned with high
dogmatism. Song et al. (2018) replicate this pattern outside of South Asia.
Proposition 6
(P6): Cultural congruency will have a strong positive inﬂuence on Aad, PI, and AUD for high-dogmatic
consumers, but not low-dogmatic consumers, because cultural congruency will serve as an identity-
aﬃrmation mechanism for high-dogmatic consumers and thus lowering defensive processing
motivation so they yield to peripheral route processing and persuasion. For low-dogmatic consumers,
cultural congruency will not serve as identity-aﬃrmation, only giving some cognitive ﬂuency; they thus
will not trigger the high-dogmatic response. If there is no signiﬁcant Cultural Alignment×Dogmatism on
Aad, then the hypothesis will not be supported.

3.2.5 Cultural Disruption and Central Route Persuasion for
Low-Dogmatic Consumers (P7)
Cultural disruption creates an initial incongruity response in all consumers. For high-dogmatic
consumers, this generates an ideational processing style and peripheral rejection (P2). For low-dogmatic
consumers, however, incongruity can be processed ideationally-Berlyne (1960) proposed an incongruity-
resolution theory which has been applied by Meyers-Levy & Tybout (1989) to advertising-articulate it can
produce either favorable, interesting imagery-based evaluations or negative, peripheral imagery based
evaluations when the incongruity can be mentally 'resolved'. In other words, artful incongruity which is
neither too dull nor too extreme arouses the interest of low-dogmatic viewers and causes escalation of
processing when a convenient argument ﬁtting the content of the message is arrived upon.
Proposition 7
(P7): Cultural disruption enhances persuasion outcomes among low-dogmatic consumers for
highquality message arguments, because the incongruity triggers further elaborative processing
resulting in durable argument-based attitude formation via the central route. The enhacement of
persuasion outcomes by cultural disruption presumes a minimum level of argument strength: culturally
disruptive message with weak arguments induces negative attitudes even among low-dogmatic
consumers, as a result of argument-scrutiny backlash effect. A null result would present that argument
quality exerts no different effects on low-dogmatic versus high-dogmatic consumers' Aad under a
culturally disruptive treatment.
3.2.6 Processing Route and Attitude Durability (P8)
A central premise of the ELM-is that central route attitudes are both more enduring over time and more
behaviorally predictive than peripheral attitude counterparts-proffers an explicit postulation about the
downstream ramiﬁcations of dogmatism-moderated route choice: Wagner and Petty (2022) summarize

3.2.7 Sequential Attitude Effects on Purchase Intention
(P9)
The route-dependent Aad → Brand Attitude (Ab) → PI pathway represents one of the most robust ﬁndings
in advertising (MacKenzie, Lutz, and Belch 1986). Rather than a new theoretical insight, P9 is the model's
architecture assumptions of this route modulated Aad to behavior translation. Ariﬃn, Ismail, and Shah
(2016) report standardized path coeﬃcients of.52(.Aad → Ab) and.63(.Ab → PI) for this pathway in an
Islamic advertising context. The present model adds to this well-established chain a formal condition for
route type: whenever centrally-formed Aad achieves more powerful downstream behavior prediction,
argument-based attitude representations provide more accessible and more stable evaluative
associations at the time of purchase decision.
Proposition 9
(P9): Aad and attitude toward the advertisement combine to inﬂuence brand attitude (Ab) positively,
which then predicts purchase intention (PI) positively in a serial mediated pathway. The Aad to Ab to PI
chain is moderated by the type of processing route: the centrally-processed Aad yields a signiﬁcantly
greater Ab to PI link than the peripherally-processed Aad because argument-based attitudes create more
available evaluative structures at the purchase decision.
3.2.8 Direct Effect of Dogmatism on Ad Attitude under
Cultural Disruption (P10)
In addition to the moderated-mediation pathway already speciﬁed (P2 and P4), the model posits that
culturally disruptive advertising inﬂuences Aad directly for high-dogmatic consumers when a culture is
disrupted. This must be distinguished conceptually from the route-mediated effect; the former must be
conceptually different from the mediated route used in P2 and P4. The peripheral route (P2) explains
attitude development as a low-elaboration, heuristic-dependent process. Although it is faster than routes
requiring high elaboration, it is still a form of route-based attitude formation. P10 speciﬁes a qualitatively
different process: pre-elaborative and automatic processing that precedes the more effort-intensive
selection of a route. Fazio (1990) provides an argument for this type of process with the chronically
accessible negative attitude toward the attitude object, which can precede elaboration, noting that: an
attitude already installed can be used to make evaluations without further construing, consequently the
evaluative response begins with the attitude before a construal has occurred. For highdogmatic
consumers a culture-disrupting advertising message could: activate chronically accessible negative
affective responses to belief-violating content-a direct attitude rejection that precedes the peripheral
route. The discriminant empirical marker between P10 and P2/P4 is quantiﬁable: place the onset times
of latency response makers along the processing locus dimension to inform the researcher about
Page 13/25

whether negative Aad occurs prior to argument processing markers (consistent with a pre-elaborative
P10 mechanism) or subsequent to heuristic cue activation (peripherally-route P2/P4 mechanism). An
automatic attitude activation measure via IAT toward the culturally disruptive stimulus may serve as
another means of providing discriminant evidence of the P10 pathway independent of route-mediated
measures.
Proposition 10
(P10): For high-dogmatic consumers, consumer dogmatism has a direct, signiﬁcant negative impact on
Aad toward culturally disruptive advertisements through a reﬂexive evaluative rejection process
activated by the pre-elaborative identity threat route that precedes route-based elaborative processing
(i.e., the direct route impact). The direct impact enhances the indirect impact of dogmatism on Aad
through the moderation of processing routes impact (P8/P9), is stronger when advertising stimuli
directly activates culturally salient beliefs of religion, and can be empirically distinguished from the via-
route process (P2/P4) effect using measures of response latency onset and implicit evaluative attitudes.
This null hypothesis would be that the direct path from Dogmatism to Aad in a culturally disruptive
context is not signiﬁcant controlling for the via route path.
3.3 Illustrative Application: Mapping the Three Motivating
Cases onto the Framework
The three advertising controversies cited in the Introduction are not merely rhetorical scene-setting; they
provide an informal test of the model's discriminant and convergent predictions.
The Tanishq "Ekatvam" controversy (2020) represents the model's highest-threat scenario. The
advertisement depicted an interfaith baby shower-directly implicating the religiously salient Hindu
identity of the most vocal objectors. P2 predicts that religiously salient threats produce the strongest
peripheral processing preference in high-dogmatic consumers. P10 predicts a rapid, pre-elaborative
direct rejection activating before any argument is processed. The withdrawal speed (48 hours) and the
nature of the backlash (coordinated, visceral, store-level threats) are consistent with both predictions:
this was not a deliberative counterargument response but an automatic identity-defensive activation.
The FabIndia "Jashn-e-Riwaaz" controversy (2021) involved linguistic-cultural framing-an Urdu name for
a Diwali collection-without direct doctrinal content. P2 speciﬁcally predicts that secular cultural
challenges produce weaker peripheral preference than religiously salient ones. Consistent with this, the
FabIndia backlash, while commercially signiﬁcant, did not reach the same intensity as Tanishq's (no
store-level threats; controversy was resolved without withdrawal in some markets). This gradient
between the two cases is a direct, observable implication of P2's moderation sub-clause, providing
informal discriminant support.
The Surf Excel "Ring of Fire" controversy (2019) involved interreligious cooperation framed within a Holi
narrative. The campaign also implicated religious identity, predicting a P2/P10 response, though the Holi
Page 14/25

framing provided a partially congruent cultural anchor (a Hindu festival). The mixed response-signiﬁcant
negative publicity but not store-level threats-suggests the model's predictions are sensitive to the degree
of identity implication. Advertisements that disrupt religious identity within a predominantly secular
festivity context produce intermediate effects, consistent with P2's reference to the "strength of cultural
threat" as a moderator of peripheral preference strength.
4. Theoretical Contributions
This study proposes three distinct theoretical advancements to the ﬁelds of consumer behavior and
persuasion research.
The primary contribution more speciﬁcally formalizes dogmatism as a process-level moderator in the
ELM. Whereas the ELM ﬁnds elaboration likelihood is a function of motivation and ability (Petty and
Cacioppo 1986; Wagner and Petty 2022), it does not articulate the personality-based factors that enable
motivation in the culturally-sensitive advertising domain. Carpenter (2015) identiﬁed this as an important
theoretical lacuna that the present framework seeks to bridge by explicating the identity-threat process
(different from NFC and openness-to-experience) through which dogmatism determines whether central
or peripheral processing ensues when consumers encounter culturally-congruent versus incongruent
messages.
The second contribution is the expansion of the Cultural Congruence Theory. Existing CCT explains why
cultural congruence leads to better advertising outcomes (de Mooij and Hofstede 2010; Song et al.
2018) though it does not draw distinctions between consumers on different levels of heterogeneity
among consumers in terms of responses to cultural incongruence. This model makes two expansions to
CCT: ﬁrst, breaking apart the congruence-effectiveness link into two psychographically conditioned
pathways: identity-aﬃrmation in high-dogmatic consumers and cognitive ﬂuency in low-dogmatic
consumers, and second, while improving CCT by identifying conditions under which cultural disruption
may aid persuasion (low-dogmatic consumers considering strong rational messages), to reconcile this
and make it a psychographically differentiated application of incongruity-resolution theory (Meyers-Levy
and Tybout 1989).
The third contribution is also adjunct: the framework suggests that India is a theoretically justiﬁed
validation setting for predictions derived from the ELM and CCT under the cross-cultural conditions
hitherto neglected by Western Paradigm dominant research. India's structural collectivism, religious
diversity, caste social structure, and modernizing consumer culture is arguably an inordinately rigorous
test of the cross cultural applicability of persuasion theories(Shavitt, Lee, and Johnson 2018).
Importantly, the framework enables empirically testable predictions between dogmatism conditions
within India as a setting, not merely making the case for "more cross-cultural research."

5.1 Strategic Segmentation and Campaign Architecture
The most straightforward managerial conclusion of this model is to advocate abandoning demographic
segmentation as the exclusive input for campaign design in multicultural advertising contexts in favor of
psychographic segmentation. Demographics as proxies of cultural conservatism-age, region, or
aﬄuence-are ineﬃcient predictors of dogmatism and, subsequently, of Optimal message strategy, the
most reasoned route of the dual process. In India, brands should consider including validated, short-
dogmatism measures (e.g., a 10-item short-form of the Shearman and Levine [2006] scale) in consumer
insights protocols, and using the obtained psychological proﬁles as- inputs for dual-route campaign
design.
For high-dogmatic consumers, the model speciﬁes an identity reaﬃrming, peripheral-route
communications. Ads should focus on culturally congruentfamiliar positive cultural artifacts for that
segment identity (visuals of regional festivals, cultural community narratives, reinforcement of traditional
gender roles), appeals should induce affect that is culturally positive and precisely not novel or
aspirationalnorward-looking. The examples imply that the decision to withhold culturally progressive
content from a high-dogmatic segment (which appear to be a controlling, anxiety-reductionstrategy) is
not merely a creative choice but an insurance against identity-threat responses that jeopardize brand
perceptions by eliciting tribal defensiveness. P2 and P10 jointly predict that culturally alienating content
for high-dogmatic segments will be not only ineffective but upsetting their cultural core will generate
identity-threat reactions that harm brand perceptions, as the incidents at FabIndia and Tanishq have
demonstrated.
For a low-dogmatic consumer, the model suggests a central route argument quality approach in
communication. Communications aimed at this segment need to contain substantial, evidence-based
communication content-product attributes, beneﬁts, functional demonstrations, health/environmental
data,exhibited with suﬃcient magnitude to promote elaborative processing. Progressive messages can
work advantageously for this segment: coupled with good argument quality, it creates the attention
attracting incongruity that P7 anticipates to enhance elaboration and produce stable positive attitude.
Progressive positioning strategies should channel culturally incongruent creattive messaging toward
low-dogmatic consumers, while channeling culturally unincongruent, emotionally resonant messaging
toward high-dogmatic segments through different media.
5.2 Media Channel Implications
Approach to media placement should follow the same psychographic approach. For the high-dogmatic,
reliance on central processing, the best media choice would be the environment that cannibes inﬂuence
their tendencies to process information via central route. For high-dogmatic, these may include
environments where the medium has culturally high legitimacy (e.g., regional language TV, community-
oriented digital communities), or the environment related to culturally legitimate institutions. For the low-
dogmatic segment, environments that enable argument elaboration may have matching beneﬁt,
supporting the elaboration needed for central processing (e.g., longer, rational-focused product reviews
Page 16/25

on digital or online environments that promote discussion and debate, engaged comment threads.).
Longer length video places, pre-roll ad spots or connected TV ad placements with longer exposure
length (> 15 sec.) may deliver more effective rational messaging than shorter spots that limit argument
development.

The model provides a differentiatedPersuasionframework that not only meets the standard behavior
change communication toolkit norms but also caters to the changing dynamics of persuasion needed
for gov/post/health communication agencies and social marketers trying to nudge or induce desired
behaviors among Indian populations-IMNCT and GV-between other issues such as vaccination. Tobacco
cessation. Women's maternal nutrition, girls schooling, and prevention of gender-based violence.In highly

approach will undoubtedly backﬁre and miss the mark entirely with each segment: high-dogmatists will
always be exposed to culturally appropriate messaging that simply will not be deep enough for low-
dogmatists, and vice versa. The best policy will be to align culturally sensitive creative messages across
the psychographically supported media channels at play for each segment.
6. Future Research Directions
6.1 Empirical Validation of the Moderated-Mediation
Architecture
The main empirical goal should be direct experiment to test the moderated-mediation model. The
recommended method is a 2 (cultural frame: traditional versus contemporary) by 2 (appeal: emotional
versus rational) by 2 (dogmatism: high versus low) between-subjects factorial experiment with Aad and
PCI as primary DV and processing route as the mediator. Processing route should be measured along
with thought-listing protocol and response latency measures; the NFC scale can be used as an additional
convergent validity to check and as a secondary means for assessing the behavior route of processing,
but should not replace the behavioral measures of processing route given the of discriminant validity
problems outlined in Section 2.4. Stimuli material should be geared toward a purchase product category
Page 17/25

which holds personal or social meaning for the target culture, but not too central to one's identity (e.g.,
food, personal care) so that cultural frame manipulation is not confounded with status toward this
purchase. Checks should include Cui et al.'s (2012) cultural congruence scale and Petty and Cacioppo's
(1986) elaboration scale items.Most critically, the validation should feature dogmatism, NFC, and
openness-to-experience as simultaneous moderators to exclude the possibility that the unique
contribution of dogmatism to route selection is statistically artifactual. To be suﬃciently powered for a
three-way factorial design at an alpha of .05 and 1 − beta of .80 with a conservative f=.15 (small effect),
a minimum of N = 320 should be recruited from Indian consumers in at least two geographic regions of
the country.
6.2 Measurement of Processing Route via Neurological
and Behavioral Indicators
Administering thought-listing protocols depends on introspection, can be confounded by social
desirability effects, and isespecially challenging when consumers are high dogmatists and may not
report identity-defensivethinking. In future work, researchers should triangulate these ﬁndings with
behavioral and neurological measures. Eye-tracking can provide an operational deﬁnition of elaborative
engagement through ﬁxations and return ﬁxations toward argument-dense versus visual-affective
sections of advertisements.
Event-related potential (ERP) investigation of P300 amplitude (ahypothetically has been identiﬁed as a
neural correlate of the depth of cognitive elaboration; Yurcer et al., 2014; Nikopour et al., 2014) can be
utilized to explore the direct, unmediated neural evidence of the dogmatismroute of activation
hypothesis predicted by P10, in support of Zmigrod et al.’s (2021) neurological dogmatism research.
Implicit association tests (IAT) can provide an additional measure of automatic attitude activation as a
proxy for the preelaborative direct rejection route described in P10.
6.3 Longitudinal Assessment of Attitude Stability
Differences
Proposition 8
predicts central route attitudes are more temporally stable and behaviorally predictive than peripheral
route attitudes. Tests must measure the decay or stability trajectories of attitude strength for both routes
among experimentally exposed consumers over time. Ideally, a three-wave panel (immediate post-
exposure, two-week-, and six-week-follow-up) study with intervening competitive advertising
(i.e.,counterpersuasion) would empirically test this hypothesis-structural equation modeling with latent
growth curve analysis could then model attitude decay trajectories as a function of processing route and
dogmatism.

6.4 Extension to Digital and Social Media Advertising
Environments
The model was developed on a broadcast and print advertising context, but India's novel digital
advertising ecosystem – dominated by 700 + million internet users and short-form video consumption –
differs markedly in its processing context. Future work should test whether the dogmatism × route ×
appeal interaction holds in digital environments, where the peripheral cues (engagement
metrics/inﬂuencer trust cues) are more immediately available, and argument elaboration is inhibited by
the format. Critical questions include:
1. (a) Whether inﬂuencer source heuristics serve as peripheral shortcut cues, bolstering low-dogmatic
consumers' message acceptance when the source is itself culturally preferential?
2. (b) Whether signals of social proof (comment sentiment/share volume) moderate dogmatism's
effect on route selection?
3. (c) Whether the short-form video format inhibits low-dogmatic consumers' use of rational appeal by
limiting argument development?
6.5 Cross-Cultural Replication and Boundary Condition
Testing
Although theoretically applicable in India, the model needs further testing in other multi-cultural markets
that have both advertising triggered backlash and large variation in dogmatism as the deﬁnition of a
viable replication. This can be found in Indonesia (largest Muslim countrodemocracy, accusations of
interfaith advertising), Brazil (large cultural diversity, accusation of race and gender based advertising)
and United States (continuing culture-warring todate that affects brand communication). Cross national
replications would allow for systematic testing of whether the identity-threat mechanism (discussed
before) would work in the same manner if the 'threatening' content differentiates from the Hindu-Muslim
interfaith content in India to 'race' in US or 'secular vs religious' in the Indonesian or Brazilian
marketplace.
6.6 Mediating Role of Moral Emotions
The mediating process underlies the model’s speciﬁcation of dogmatism as the predictor of peripheral
route preference, but does not detail what emotions in particular evoke this threat response. Future
research should explore if the various moral emotions disgust (related to reactions of norm-breaking
behavior, as in the Tanishq and FabIndia cases), contempt (related to the threat of one's in-group status),
and moral outrage (related to notional willful threats to cultural system integrity)-serve as mediators of
the dogmatism–route effect. Rozin, Haidt, and McCauley (2000) report that disgust elicits more
automatic and durable attitude formation than either contempt or anger (as in moral outrage).
Advertising that stimulates consumer disgust while high-dogmatic may create more deeply-ingrained
negative Aad that proves less susceptible to corrective messages later.

7. Limitations and Scope Conditions
As a conceptual paper, this study's limitations are inherent to the theoretical-synthesis approach rather
than to empirical methodology. The following scope conditions deﬁne where the model's predictions are
likely to hold and where they require qualiﬁcation.
First, the model treats dogmatism as a binary (high vs. low) moderator for propositional clarity, but the
underlying construct is continuous. Empirical testing should employ latent proﬁle analysis or continuous
moderation rather than median-split approaches, as the latter artiﬁcially inﬂates Type I error rates and
discards meaningful variance in the middle of the distribution. The propositions as stated describe
extreme proﬁles; effects at intermediate dogmatism levels remain theoretically unspeciﬁed.
Second, the identity-threat mechanism's primary empirical anchor-Aji and Dharmmesta (2019)-derives
from Indonesian Muslim consumers responding to religiously incongruent advertising. India's religious
and caste plurality means that the mechanism may operate through different speciﬁc identity threats
(e.g., caste community disruption, regional linguistic identity, or Dalit identity challenges) that are not
captured by a single-religion study. The present model assumes the identity-threat mechanism
generalizes across these triggers, but empirical cross-context veriﬁcation is required before this can be
treated as demonstrated rather than proposed.
Third, the model does not yet account for rival moderators of advertising processing route, including
religiosity, right-wing authoritarianism (RWA), and need for closure (NFC). Each of these constructs
shares theoretical adjacency with dogmatism and has established advertising-response literatures.
Future empirical work should enter these as covariates alongside dogmatism to establish the latter's
unique predictive contribution, which is theoretically justiﬁed by the identity-threat mechanism but not
yet empirically isolated.
Fourth, the model was developed with traditional broadcast and print advertising in mind. Its application
to digital advertising environments-where peripheral cues (likes, shares, algorithmic ampliﬁcation) may
operate through mechanisms not captured by the original ELM-requires the extensions described in
Section 6.4 rather than direct application of the present propositions.
Fifth, all ten propositions remain empirically untested. The moderated-mediation architecture is
theoretically grounded and supported by adjacent empirical literatures, but the speciﬁc interaction
predictions (particularly P4's three-way interaction and P10's discriminant empirical signature) may not
survive experimental testing. Authors of future empirical tests are encouraged to specify in advance
(pre-registration) what null or reversed patterns would mean for the model's theoretical framework rather
than treating non-signiﬁcant interactions as null results to be explained away.
Sixth, the proposed sample size (N = 320) is adequate for detecting medium-sized three-way interactions
at conventional power levels, but smaller effects-which are plausible given the complexity of the model-
Page 20/25

would require substantially larger samples. Any empirical replication should conduct a priori power
analysis based on the speciﬁc effect size expected rather than treating N = 320 as a universal threshold.
8. Conclusion
This study proposes an integrated conceptual model to address a recognized theoretical and practical
gap in persuasion science: the absence of a formally speciﬁed psychographic framework for explaining
heterogeneous consumer responses to culturally complex advertising in non-Western, multicultural
environments. By formally integrating dogmatism as a process-level moderator within the ELM and
linking this dual-route mechanism with CCT's schema-activation dynamics, the model speciﬁes how
identical advertisements-with equivalent product and execution quality-can simultaneously foster
positive attitudes in one consumer segment while triggering identity-defensive rejection in another.
The ten propositions developed in Section 3, grounded in existing empirical literatures and organized
through a systematic integration of dogmatism theory, ELM, and CCT, provide a falsiﬁable and
experimentally testable structure for future research. The model's core prediction-that high-dogmatic
consumers rely on peripheral cues to form attitudes that are positive when cultural congruence is
present but brittle in the face of competitive counter-messaging, while low-dogmatic consumers employ
central route processing yielding more stable argument-based attitudes-generates a speciﬁc and
distinctive empirical signature that differentiates this framework from adjacent accounts based on NFC
or openness-to-experience alone.
The informal illustrative analysis in Section 3.3 demonstrates that the model's gradient predictions-
stronger defensive responses for religiously salient threats (Tanishq) than for secular-cultural challenges
(FabIndia) than for ambiguous festivity-context disruptions (Surf Excel)-are consistent with the
documented intensity differential across the three motivating cases. This convergent informal evidence,
while not a substitute for experimental testing, provides preliminary external validity support for the
model's discriminant predictions.
For practitioners, the framework offers a psychographic segmentation model that moves beyond
demographic targeting toward the identiﬁcation of consumers' dispositional tolerance for belief-
discrepant information. For public health authorities and social marketers, the model supplies a multi-
route communication strategy allowing for differentiated message design, appeal tailoring, and
messenger selection across psychographic segments, with the dual goals of immediate acceptance and
long-term attitude stability.
The limitations documented in Section 7 deﬁne the model's current scope: it is a conceptual framework
whose propositions are directional, theoretically grounded, and ready for empirical testing-not a
conﬁrmed theory of Indian advertising response. The expensive pattern of advertising backlash in Indian
markets is, on this framework's account, a predictable consequence of single-route, culturally
generalized communication strategies applied to a psychographically diverse consumer base. The
Page 21/25

appropriate response is not cultural restraint but psychographically informed, multi-route campaign
architecture-a response for which this conceptual model provides the theoretical scaffolding.
