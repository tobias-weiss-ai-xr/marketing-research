1. INTRODUCTION
Potatoes are a major commercial vegetable crop in Indonesia and constitute a
vital segment of the national agricultural market . According to the Central Statistics
Agency, potato production in Indonesia reached 1.42 million tons in 2022, which was
4.21% higher than in the previous year (1.36 million tons) . Overall, potato production
in the country demonstrates a positive trend despite fluctuations in annual output
volumes, while 2022 became the most productive year over the last decade. This
production is distributed across 21 provinces, among which East J ava holds a leading
position, accounting for 381,090  tons of output and making a substantial contribution
to national vegetable production [1 ; 2].
Analysis of potato production dynamics in East Java during 2017 –2022 based
on official statistical publicatio ns and annual regional reports [2; 3] also confirms a
steady upward trend. According to BPS East Java, 2022 was characteri sed by the
highest production level during the analy sed period. The main potato production
centres in the province include Pasuruan, P robolinggo, and the Malang agglomeration
(Malang Regency, Malang City, and Batu City) [4]. Among these territories, Malang
represents a particularly relevant case for studying the functioning of the potato value

chain, since the region combines substantial  production volumes, a developed
intermediary network, and a high sensitivity to price and information imbalances.
Previous studies based on the Structure–Conduct–Performance (SCP)
framework indicate that the potato market in Malang demonstrates features o f a
competitive environment with elements of monopolistic competition, whereas the
distribution of income among marketing institutions remains relatively balanced or
moderately uneven [5]. The functioning of the potato sector significantly affects
regional socio-economic development through farmer incomes, employment
generation, retail trade support, and infrastructure development. Nevertheless, the
realisation of this potential is constrained by a number of persistent structural and
institutional problems that reduce the efficiency and resilience of the supply chain.
One of the key challenges is that high market demand for potatoes does not
automatically strengthen the bargaining position of farmers, who largely remain price
takers with limited influence on marketing conditions [6; 7]. This problem is
aggravated by res tricted access to market information and pronounced information
asymmetry regarding prices, as confirmed by studies analy sing the price transmission
problems in regional markets [8; 9], as well as research devoted to the role of
intermediaries and marketin g margins under asymmetric information conditions [10].
Additional constraints arise from the uneven level of producers’ knowledge
regarding product quality parameters and market requirements, which complicates the
formation of fair prices and weakens oppo rtunities for product differentiation  [11].
Therefore, market entry and exit are associated with organi sational and institutional
barriers, while production performance largely depends on the behaviour of buyers,
competitors, and prevailing trading practices [12; 13]. Further risks are associated with
uncertainty of demand and trust, particularly within the segments of seed potatoes and
innovative food products [14; 15], as well as with post -harvest handling and food
safety issues within the marketing chain [16].
Furthermore, a significant challenge stems from  the limited ability of producers
to generate higher value added through processing activities and to the insufficient
coordination among supply chain participants, including farmers, local traders,
wholesalers, and retail networks [17; 18]. Empirical evidence from different regions
of Indonesia confirms that potato supply chain management often remains
fragmented, thereby complicating  the coordination of product, information, and
financial flows [19 ; 20]. Consequently , transaction costs increase, efficiency losses
emerge, and the adaptability of the supply chain to market changes decreases.
Within contemporary academic discourse, the su stainable development of agri -
food systems has been formed predominantly  within two interconnected but
relatively autonomous research streams. The first stream focuses on supply chain
management, operational efficiency, environmental standards, and the red uction of
negative environmental impacts within agricultural production and logistics systems
[21; 2 2]. In this context, particular attention is devoted to Green Supply Chain
Management ( GSCM), including coordination mechanisms, environmental
performance indicators, and the ecological moderni sation of value chains.

The second research stream concentrates on the market and behavioural
dimensions of sustainability, particularly green consumer behaviour [2 3], sustainable
food consumption, willingness to pay fo r environmentally oriented products [24], and
marketing instruments aimed at promoting “green” products. However, despite the
common orientation of both streams toward s sustainable development goals, the
interaction between operational sustainability  transformation and market -oriented
management remains insufficiently investigated.
Operational environmental initiatives within supply chains are frequently
analysed separat ely from marketing strategies; conversely , studies devoted to green
consumers often overlook the real institutional, technological, and logistical
constraints of agri -food production systems. Such fragmentation creates a substantial
research gap, since the  environmental transformation of supply chains does not
automatically ensure market success without integrati ng segmentation, targeting, and
positioning (STP)  strategies and accounting for  the behavioural characteristics of
potential green consumers within specific territorial and sectoral contexts.
Under these conditions, there is a growing need for  research integrating the
institutional and market logic of the SCP framework with environm ental and
operational instruments of supply chain management. In its contemporary
interpretation, GSCM focuses on the environmental orientation of products and
processes, including eco -design, reduction of environmental impacts, and
enhancement of resource  efficiency [25]. At the same time, environmentally oriented
products are frequently associated with higher production and distribution costs,
which increase prices and potentially constrain market demand [ 26; 27]. Therefore,
managerial decisions should combine environmental sustainability with economic
feasibility and the creation of consumer value [ 28].
In this context, the integration of the operational “green” logic of supply chain
management with market instruments, particularly STP strategies oriented towards
environmentally conscious consumers , is of paramount importance . Such integration
not only facilitates the improvement of environmental performance, but also strengthens
the long-term competitiveness and market sustainability of agri-food systems.
Accordingly, the purpose of this study is to substantiate an integrated approach
to combining GSCM and STP -marketing strategies within the development of a
sustainable potato market using the potato sector of Malang as an empirical case . To
achieve this purpose, the following research objectives were formulated: (1)  to
identify relevant technologies and green supply chain strategies applicable to the
potato sector in Malang; (2)  to evaluate critical aspects of potato supply chain
functioning that can be optimi sed from an environmental perspective; (3)  to identify
potential segments of env ironmentally conscious potato consumers; and (4)  to
analyse the applicability of STP strategies within the potato market in Malang.

2.2. GSCM in agri -food systems: practices, drivers, and links with
performance
The GSCM literature has evolved from describing isolated environmental
initiatives to analy sing complex configurations of practices encompassing sourcing,
production, logistics, and reverse flows. Syntheses of empirical evidence across
sectors indicate that  GSCM implementation is generally as sociated with
improvements in environmental performance and, under certain organi sational
conditions, with enhanced economic and social performance of firms [ 30; 31].
However, the strength of these relationships depends substantially on managerial
maturity, the level of cooperation with suppliers, and the availability of measurement
systems that align “green” objectives with operational performance indicators.
In the agri -food context, researchers emphasi se the critical role of reducing
losses and waste, im proving energy efficiency, transitioning to sustainable sourcing,
and applying environmental criteria in supplier selection, as agricultural supply
chains are structurally vulnerable to post -harvest losses, quality degradation, and
information asymmetries [32]. Empirical evidence suggests that GSCM tools
significantly strengthen environmental outcomes when they are integrated into
supplier interactions and standardi sed within operational procedures [ 33]. At the
same time, implementation barriers – such as l imited capabilities, cost pressures,
uneven supplier readiness, and data shortages – often hinder the transition from
isolated practices to systemic supply chain improvement [ 34].
Special emphasis is placed on the need to jointly analy se environmental and

2.3. Potato value chains: market structure , information asymmetries, and
coordination constraints
Potato markets constitute a revealing empirical setting, as they combine high
basic demand, significant quality heterogeneity, and strong dependence on logistics
and post -harvest handling. Studies acr oss countries confirm that farmers often
possess limited bargaining  power and remain “price takers ”, while intermediaries
benefit from information asymmetries within the chain [ 6]. Experimental assessments
of potato farmers demonstrate that limited market transparency can increase
intermediary margins and reduce producer incomes, highlighting the need for
institutional rules and coordination mechanisms [10 ].
Price transmission studies show that spatial and institutional frictions weaken
the transmission of price signals along the chain, generating inefficiencies and
increasing inc ome uncertainty for producers [8 ]. Structural analyses of determinants
of potato producer prices confirm the high applied value of this approach for
explaining the f unctioning of potato markets, particularly under conditions dominated
by smallholders [9 ]. An additional challenge relates to post-harvest handling and food
safety, as weak quality control and safety risks undermine consumer trust and
constrain opportuniti es for product differentiation, including differentiation based on
“green” attributes [1 6].
Empirical studies from Indonesia describe potato chain management as
fragmented, with insufficient coordination of material, information, and financial
flows among farmers, traders, wholesalers, and retail networks [ 36]. The
consequences include higher transaction costs, efficiency losses, and a reduced
capacity of the chain to adapt to changes in the market environment, especially when
sustainability requires verifi cation through traceability, environmental standards, and
measurable impact indicators.
2.4. Green consumer behaviour and sustainable food consumption models:
demand drivers for sustainably produced potatoes
Research on consumer behaviour indicates that pro-environmental purchasing is
shaped by a combination of values, knowledge, trust in information signals,
perceived effectiveness of individual choices, and situational constraints, particularly
price and availability. At the same time, nu merous empirical studies document an
attitude – behaviour gap, especially when “green” choices require additional costs or
effort [ 37]. Literature reviews emphasi se that perceptions of environmental attributes
largely depend on communication quality, trans parency, and consumers’ ability to
interpret sustainability signals [ 38].
Green STP is predicated on the recognition that “green” consumers do not

lifestyles and sustainability perce ptions enable the identification of segments with

organic food consumption can be predicted using structured behavioural models;
however, the strength of individual determin ants varies significantly across territorial
and cultural contexts, thereby justifying the need for locali sed consumer

significant only when such signals are understood and trusted, reinforcing the

practical basis for targeting [ 42]. At the same time,  value-based approaches highlight
the role of biospheric values and environmental concern, the effects of which are
moderated by household financial capacity and product availability [ 43].
Thus, demand for environmentally friendly potato products  is not an  automatic
outcome of production greening, but rather requires a segment -based approach, trust
in sustainability attributes, and alignment of product and logistics solutions with the
buyer’s value proposition.
2.5. Integration of GSCM and green STP: substa ntiating an integrative
research framework
Despite their shared orientation towards sustainable development goals, the two
research streams remain weakly integrated. Operational  GSCM initiatives are often
analysed without direct links to segmentation and positioning, while studies of green
consumer behaviour frequently abstract from production and logistics constraints.
The results of the bibliometric screening are consistent with this interpretation,
demonstrating clearly delineated thema tic “cores” within each stream and relatively
weak connections between the operational GSCM logic and the market -oriented STP
logic.
For potato value chains, this gap is particularly significant. Chain greening –
such as reducing pesticide loads, improving  packaging, developing reverse logistics,
and implementing traceability systems – entails additional costs and organi sational
changes that will not be economically sustainable unless these efforts are transformed
into clear consumer value and competitive m arket positioning. This necessitates an
integrated research framework within which: (i) GSCM practices and bottlenecks
along the chain are identified; (ii) potential green consumers are empirically
segmented and profiled; and (iii) STP solutions are develo ped that align the chain’s
operational capabilities with consumer expectations and willingness to pay.
Accordingly, the literature review and bibliometric screening results confirm
that GSCM approaches demonstrate proven environmental effectiveness, yet th eir
economic viability in agri -food chains largely depends on market conditions and
consumer behaviour; potato chains are characteri sed by coordination constraints,
information asymmetries, and quality risks that complicate environmental
differentiation; and green consumers are heterogeneous, necessitating the application
of STP logic. This substantiates the need for a methodology that simultaneously

3. METHODOLOGY
3.1. Research design
The study was conducted within an applied research design aimed at integrating
an operational and logistical assessment of the sustainable potato value chain with the
marketing logic of segmentation–targeting–positioning and empirical profiling of
potential “green” consumers. The overall research logic envisages the sequential
implementation of three interrelated stages:  (i) description and diagnosis of the
supply chain and GSCM practices at the leve l of producers and trading
intermediaries; (ii)  assessment of market structure and interactions among chain
participants using the SCP framework; (iii)  identification of consumer segments,
selection of target groups, and development of the positioning of environmentally
friendly potato products  based on STP logic and analysis of potential demand.
Methodologically, the study relies on a com bination of descriptive quantitative
procedures, comparative analysis across territories, and analytical interpretation of
the linkages between the “ greening” of the operational segment of the chain and the
possibility of converting these efforts into market outcomes through a segment -based
approach and positioning.
3.2. Study area and justification for locat ion selection
The study area was purposively selected based on the speciali sation of the
Malang region in potato production and the presence of different organi sational and
market models within the potato supply chain. The Malang agglomeration is one of
the important potato -producing areas in  East Java and represents a representative
empirical setting for analy sing the interaction between agricultural producers,
intermediaries, distributors, and consumers within the agri -food value chain.
To ensure territ orial comparability and variability of production conditions,
three locations with different levels of potato production concentration were selected:
(1) Bumiaji Subdistrict, (2)  Poncokusumo Subdistrict, and (3)  Batu City/Regency.
According to regional sta tistical data for 2024, these territories differ significantly in
production volumes, organi sational characteristics of potato farming, logistics
conditions, and the structure of marketing channels. Such differentiation makes it

possible to compare supply chain practices and identify differences in the
implementation of GSCM elements under various production and market conditions.
The selection of these territories was not intended to proportionally represent the
total population of potato producers in the region. Instead, the research design
followed a comparative analytical logic aimed at identifying differences in supply
chain functioning and environmental practices across territories characteri sed by
different levels of production concentration and marke t organisation. This approach is
methodologically justified for applied agri -food supply chain studies focused on
identifying operational bottlenecks, coordination mechanisms, and opportunities for
green transformation.
In addition, the selected locations differ in terms of accessibility to distribution
channels, the intensity of interactions between farmers and intermediaries, and the
level of integration into regional food markets. This allows for a more comprehensive
assessment of the operational, enviro nmental, and marketing dimensions of the potato
supply chain within the framework of GSCM and SCP approach.

5. DISCUSSION
5.1. Interpretation of the results and theoretical contribution of the study
The obtained empirical results indicate that the transformation of the potato
value chain in Malang toward s sustainable development  remains fragmented and
uneven across different stages of supply chain functioning. Despite the
implementation of certain environmentally oriented production and logistics
practices, the overall level of integration between operational sustainability
transformation and market mechanisms remains insufficient. This confirms that the
implementation of isolated environmental initiatives alone does not ensure the
formation of an integrated sustainable agri -food supply chain capable of generating
stable demand and long-term competitive advantages.
The results of the study demonstrate that the highest performance indicators are
associated with production technologies, storage systems, and selected elements of
distribution, whereas transportation decarboni sation, re newable energy use, reverse
logistics, and waste management systems remain insufficiently developed. In this
context, the findings support the conclusions of international studies indicating that
the sustainable transformation of agri -food supply chains is  often characteri sed by
structural asymmetry between environmentally oriented production processes and
less developed downstream logistics and circular economy mechanisms [46; 47].
At the same time, unlike many previous studies focused predominantly on
technological or operational aspects of GSCM, the obtained results demonstrate that
the effectiveness of sustainability transformation cannot be explained solely through
technological modernisation. Empirical evidence indicates that environmental
improvements in production and logistics systems have limited market effects when
they are not accompanied by effective marketing mechanisms, consumer -oriented
communication, and trust-based sustainability positioning strategies.
An important theoretical contribution of the study lies in identifying a
substantial gap between the operational “greening” of the potato value chain and
consumers’ perception of sustainability -related value. The findin gs demonstrate that
even under conditions of partial implementation of environmentally oriented
practices within the supply chain, consumers do not always perceive these practices

as a significant criterion influencing purchasing decisions. Consequently,
environmental transformation at the operational level is insufficiently converted into
market differentiation and stable consumer demand.
In this context, the results significantly contribute to the scientific discussion
regarding the attitude –behaviour gap  phenomenon. International studies frequently
emphasise that environmentally conscious consumers demonstrate a willingness to
support sustainable food products and pay a price premium for environmentally
verified goods [ 48–50]. However, the results obtained in Malang indicate that
positive attitudes toward s environmental sustainability do not automatically translate
into actual purchasing behaviour due to high price sensitivity, limited accessibility of
environmentally differentiated  products, low tr ust in sustainability -related
information, and weak visuali sation of environmental attributes within the retail
environment.
Therefore, the study demonstrates that the attitude –behaviour gap in the market
for sustainable food products is formed not only by  individual consumer preferences,
but also by structural market constraints and insufficient integration between
sustainability practices and consumer communication mechanisms. This extends the
findings of previous studies by emphasi sing the necessity of s imultaneously
overcoming operational, informational, and market barriers within sustainable agri -
food supply chains.
Another important scientific contribution of the study is the integration of
GSCM and the STP -marketing approach within a unified analytical framework.
Whereas in most previous studies sustainable supply chains and consumer behaviour
have been examined as relatively autonomous research fields, the obtained results
confirm the necessity  of their integrated consideration as interconnected components
of sustainable market development. In particular, the findings demonstrate that
environmentally oriented supply chain practices remain economically vulnerable in
the absence of effective marke t segmentation, clear targeting strategies, and credible
sustainability positioning capable of transforming environmental attributes into
consumer value.
The results also indicate that the sustainability transformation of the agri -food
sector should be con sidered not merely as a technological process, but as a
multidimensional socio -economic transformation simultaneously encompassing
operational moderni sation, institutional adaptation, behavioural change, and market
restructuring. In this respect, the study  supports the contemporary international
scientific discourse emphasi sing the importance of integrated approaches combining
sustainability management, consumer behaviour analysis, and strategic marketing
instruments [51].

The obtained results indicate that traditional binary approaches dividing
consumers into environmentally conscious and non -conscious categories are
insufficient for sustainable market development within the agri -food sector. Instead,
multidimensional segmentation approaches are required, taking into account
behavioural characteristics, psychographic profiles, trust in environmental
information, purchasing constraints, and contextual market conditions.  Particular
importance should be attached to consumer segmentation according to sensitivity to
sustainability-related information, environmental values, and willingness to support
environmentally oriented consumption practices. Empirical findings demonstrate that
younger and more educated consumers exhibit relatively higher sustainability
orientation and constitute  the most promising target groups for the development of
environmentally friendly potato products.
At the same time, the low targeting indicator demonstrates that even when
potentially promising consumer segments exist, they are not transformed into clearl y
defined target groups supported by appropriate communication channels,
differentiated value propositions, and demand -stimulation instruments. The findings
confirm that mass targeting strategies for sustainable products remain ineffective
under conditions  of high price sensitivity and insufficiently developed environmental
social norms.
The study also demonstrates that consumers’ willingness to support
environmentally friendly products largely depends on trust in sustainability claims,
product accessibilit y, and the transparency of environmental information. This is
consistent with previous studies confirming that consumers are significantly more
likely to support sustainable products under conditions of credible certification
systems, traceability mechanisms, and transparent communication practices [ 48; 52].
Although the positioning indicator is somewhat higher compared with
segmentation and targeting indicators, it remains insufficient for the formation of
stable sustainability -oriented market positioning.  The obtained results indicate that
environmentally friendly potato products have not yet formed a stable association
with sustainability in consumers’ perceptions.  This is particularly important given
that international studies demonstrate that effective green positioning requires not
only the presence of environmental product attributes, but also their clear articulation,
transparency, credibility, and practical relevance for consumers [ 48; 51]. In the case
of Malang, sustainability -oriented positioning i s constrained by underdeveloped

practical terms, this implies the necessity of implementing certification systems,
traceability instruments, environmentally oriented branding strategies, and
differentiated comm unication approaches adapted to the characteristics of specific
consumer segments.
5.3. Managerial and policy implications
The results of the study indicate  that strengthening the sustainability of the
potato value chain in Malang requires simultaneous transformation at operational,
managerial, institutional, and market levels. In this regard, the obtained findings
generate a number of important managerial an d policy implications for the key
stakeholders of the agri -food sector.
For agricultural producers and cooperatives, the priority direction involves
moving from fragmented implementation of individual environmental practices
towards their systematic integr ation across all stages of the production and logistics
cycle. In particular, special attention should be devoted to the standardi sation of
environmentally oriented agricultural practices, reduction of pesticide intensity,
efficient use of water r esources, and implementation of basic traceability mechanisms
capable of increasing consumer trust in environmental product attributes.
For distributors and logistics operators, the results emphasi se the importance of
transportation moderni sation, route optimisation, and the development of reverse
logistics systems. Since the Deliver and Return components demonstrated the lowest
indicators within the Green SCOR  framework, logistics sustainability moderni sation
should become one of the main priorities of s ustainable supply chain transformation.
The gradual implementation of environmentally sustainable transportation solutions
and integrated waste management systems will contribute not only to environmental
improvements, but also to enhanced operational effi ciency and reduced product
losses.
Processing enterprises and retail actors should focus on strengthening quality
control systems, harmoni sation of product standards, and improvement of
communication regarding sustainability -related product characteristics . The findings
demonstrate that environmentally friendly product attributes remain insufficiently
visible and weakly differentiated within the retail environment. Consequently,
investments in sustainability communication, informational support, digital
communication channels, and trustworthy labelling systems may substantially
strengthen consumer perception of environmentally differentiated products.
From the perspective of strategic marketing management, the results confirm the
necessity of applying multid imensional STP -oriented approaches based on
behavioural, psychographic, demographic, and contextual segmentation criteria.

quality, food safety, supply reliability, and transparency of production processes.
At the policy level, the findings indicate that public authorities and institutions
should play a more active role in supporting sustainable agri -food supply chain
transformation. Priority policy directions include investments in sustainable log istics
infrastructure, support for renewable energy integration, training programs for
farmers and small enterprises, dissemination of environmental standards, and the

The study also confirms the importance of strengthening institutional

6. CONCLUSIONS
The results of the study demonstrate that the implementation of GSCM
principles within the potato sector of Malang remains at an early-to-intermediate
stage of development and is characteri sed by substantial asymmetry between the level
of operational sustainability transformation and market outcomes. The empirical
evidence indicates that certain elements of the sustainable supply chain already
function relatively effectively, particularly within production processes, storage
systems, and selected logistics operations. At the same time, transportation
decarbonisation, renewab le energy integration, reverse logistics,  and waste
management systems remain insufficiently developed, which significantly constrains
the environmental integrity of the supply chain and limits the formation of additional
market value.
The results of the Green SCOR assessment confirmed the uneven maturity of
key supply chain processes. The Source component recorded the highest score (3.64),
reflecting relatively stable access to local raw materials and established relationships
between producers and supplie rs. In contrast, the lowest values were exhibited by the
Return (1.64) and Deliver (2.13) components, confirming the insufficient
development of reverse logistics, circular economy  practices, and environmentally
sustainable transportation systems.
The results of the consumer survey also demonstrated the existence of latent but
fragmented market potential for environmentally friendly potato products. Although
the level of consumers’ environmental awareness is relatively high (3.51),
sustainability-related attitudes do not automatically transform into actual purchasing
behaviour due to high price sensitivity, limited accessibility of environmentally
differentiated products, and insuffi cient trust in sustainability -related information.

Particularly important is the finding that environmentally conscious consumers in
Malang do not constitute a homogeneous market segment, but instead are characterised
by substantial behavioural, psychographic, and demographic differences.
The STP analysis confirmed the weak level of integration between sustainability
practices within the supply chain and market -oriented management mechanisms. Low
indicators for Segmentation (1.30), Targeting (1.36), and Gen eral STP Assessment
(1.37) demonstrated the absence of a systematic approach to the formation of
environmentally differentiated market strategies. Furthermore, the Positioning
indicator (2.23) demonstrated that environmentally friendly potato products have not
yet formed a stable sustainability association in consumers’ perceptions.
These findings indicate that  the key problem of sustainable potato market
development in Malang lies not only in the insufficient technological moderni sation
of the  supply chain, but also in the weak integration between operational
sustainability practices, consumer communication mechanisms, and strategic
marketing instruments. The study confirmed that the environmental transformation of
agri-food supply chains does not automatically generate market demand in the
absence of effective STP -oriented strategies, credible sustainability positioning, and
trust-based communication systems.
The scientific contribution of the study lies in the development of an integrated
approach to analy sing the interrelationships between GSCM, consumer behaviour,
and the STP -marketing approach within the framework of sustainable agri -food
market development. Unlike most previous studies, in which operational
sustainability and consumer -oriented marketing analysis were considered
predominantly as autonomous directions of research, the present study demonstrates
the necessity of their comprehensive integration in order to ensure the long -term
competitiveness of sustainable food systems.
The practical significance of the findings lies in their application to  the
formation of sustainable supply chain strategies within the agri -food sector,
improvement of environmentally oriented marketing policies, development of
traceability systems, certifica tion mechanisms, and sustainability communication
instruments. The findings may be used by producers, distributors, retailers, local
authorities, and development institutions to enhance sustainability integration in
supply chain management and to develop e nvironmentally differentiated market
strategies.
The findings confirm that the effective development of the sustainable potato
market requires the simultaneous transformation of both supply -side and demand-side
mechanisms. Only the comprehensive integratio n of GSCM, strategic marketing
instruments, environmentally oriented communication, and consumer trust
mechanisms creates the preconditions for the long -term sustainable competitiveness
of the agri-food sector amid increasing global sustainability requirem ents.

7. LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE RESEARCH
Despite the important empirical results regarding the integration of GSCM and

STP-oriented sustainable market development within  the potato sector of Malang, the
study has several limitations that should be considered when interpreting the findings.
The empirical analysis was geographically limited to selected districts of Malang.
Although the studied region represents one of the major potato production centres in
Indonesia, the results cannot be directly gen eralised to other regional or national agri -
food systems operating under different institutional, socio -economic, and
infrastructural conditions.  The research relied on non -probability sampling
approaches, including purposive, snowball, and convenience sam pling methods,
which may limit the statistical generali sability of the findings. However, such
approaches are methodologically appropriate for applied agri -food supply chain
research involving partially informal market structures and exploratory consumer
profiling within a specific regional context.
The study was based predominantly on a descriptive quantitative approach ,
which enabled the identification of current sustainability practices and behavioural
characteristics of consumers, but limited the scope for advanced causal modelling of
interrelationships between supply chain actors and consumers. Therefore, a promising
direction for future studies involves the application of mixed -methods approaches
that combine quantitative modelling, qualitative interviews, and case -study analysis.
The research relied primarily on cross -sectional survey data reflecting
respondents’ perceptions at a specific point in time. Consequently, the study cannot
trace the long -term evolution of sustainability -oriented consumer behaviour and
sustainable supply chain transformation processes . Future longitudinal studies could
provide a deeper understanding of the dynamics of sustainability -related attitudes,
market structures, and operational practices.
Another limitation  is the relatively restricted analysis of the technological and
financial feasibility of implementing specific sustainability -oriented solutions,
especially for small and medium -sized agricultural enterprises. In this regard,
promising directions for futur e research include comparative economic and
environmental assessments of specific sustainable technologies, renewable energy
systems, traceability mechanisms, and logistics moderni sation tools.
Furthermore, although the study confirmed the importance of institutional
support and inter -organisational coordination, the effectiveness of existing public
policies, cooperative mechanisms, and public –private partnership models was not
empirically evaluated. This creates promising opportunities for future studies related
to the assessment of institutional mechanisms supporting sustainable agri -food supply
chain transformation and environmentally oriented market development.
