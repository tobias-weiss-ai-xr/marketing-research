Abstract
Small and Medium Entreprises (SMEs) play a vital role in economic growth in Sri
Lanka. Marketing is one of the leading challenges encountered by SMEs and marketing
skill deprivation leads to entrepreneurial failures. Entrepreneurial Marketing (EM) has
emerged as a strategic approach that enables SMEs to overcome these constraints and
enhance their marketing performance. Despite its growing importance, empirical
evidence on the influence of EM on SMEs’ marketing performance in Sri Lanka remains
limited. Therefore, this study examines the role of EM on the marketing performance of
SMEs in Sri Lanka. A quantitative research design was adopted using a cross-sectional
survey design. Primary data were collected from 200 samples across the five districts of the
Northern Province, Sri Lanka. The data were analyzed using reliability, Pearson
correlation, and multiple regression analyses. The findings revealed that all three
dimensions of entrepreneurial marketing have significant influence on SMEs’ marketing
performance. Among these dimensions, innovation orientation was identified as the most
influential predictor of marketing performance. This study adds valuable insights into the
Entrepreneurial Marketing literature by providing empirical evidence from an emerging
economy and offers practical implications for SME owners, practitioners, and
policymakers. The findings suggest that strengthening innovation orientation, value
creation, and opportunity focus practices can enhance the marketing performance of
SMEs, particularly in developing nations like Sri Lanka.

Keywords: Entrepreneurial Marketing, Innovation Orientation, Marketing
Performance, Opportunity Focus, Value Creation.

Introduction
EM has emerged as an alternative
marketing strategy for SMEs in facing
the challenges successfully and
handling the limited resources (Sadiku-
Dushi et al., 2019). EM plays a vital role
in ensuring sustainable yielding for
SMEs during frequent market
dynamics. Sri Lanka's SMEs are defined
as having 1-299 employees (Micro 1-9,
Small 10-49, Medium 50 -299)
(International Labour Organization
(ILO) Report, 2023). If
entrepreneurship is considered the soul
of a business, marketing becomes the
flesh (Lam & Harker, 2015).
The National Policy Framework for
Small Medium Enterprise (SME)
Development, a government policy
document for SME sector in Sri Lanka,
outlines that Sri Lankan SME sector
constitutes over 75% of the total
enterprises, provides 45% of
employment, and contributes 52% to the
Gross Domestic Production (GDP).
EM is defined as a focused marketing
process in identifying opportunities and
creating customer value through
innovativeness, creativity, networking,
and dimensions of EM (Hill et al.,
2010). EM is centered on formulating
marketing techniques for business
operations to attain and maintain a
competitive edge. EM supports an
enterprise during its turbulent market
conditions and its effectiveness relies on
available varieties of operational
resources and techniques (Asad et al.
2023). SMEs need EM practices to
become innovative and competitive and
to handle the entrepreneurial operations
to satisfy dynamic market requirements
better than competitors (Amadasun &
Mutezo, 2025).
Marketing performance is the outcome
or achievement of marketing objectives
through an organization’s marketing-
related strategies and activities.
Marketing performance directly
supports a firm’s competitiveness and
sustainability in the market place. This
study intends to identify the real impact
of EM on marketing performance of
SMEs in Sri Lankan context.  Zahara et
al., (2023) found that entrepreneurial
marketing has positive and significant
impact on digital marketing capabilities
and on marketing performance. The
entrepreneurial marketing dimensions
such as pro-activeness, opportunity-
focused, innovation-oriented, customer
intensity, resource leveraging and value
creation have significant positive
relationship with market performance
(Panama & Ayodele, 2020).
Poor literacy, lack of staying power, and
domination of competitors restrict
SMEs’ capacity to elevate their business
performance and sustainability. Current
literature largely focuses on the influence
of EM on financial performance or
organizational performance in general. In
light of this, limited studies have
specifically examined the influence of EM
on SME’s marketing performance in
developing economies. Also, the
available literature dealt with limited
geographical regions or industry
sectors, limiting the generalizability of
their findings to the Sri Lankan SME
sector. This study complements the
existing knowledge gaps and tries to
establish the influence of EM on the
marketing performance of SMEs in Sri
Lanka. By analyzing the role of EM and
its influence on marketing performance,
this study aims to insert valuable
marketing insights into SMEs in Sri
Lanka.
The primary research question of this
study is to examine the influence of
entrepreneurial marketing on
marketing performance of SMEs in
Sri Lanka. Specifically, this study

Literature Review
Theoretical Review
This study embraces Sarasvathy’s
Effectuation Theory (2001) to explore
how EM strategies undertaken by
SMEs in Sri Lanka affect their
marketing performance. According to
this theory, entrepreneurs’ function
under uncertain conditions and
capitalize their opportunities by
leveraging avail able sources.
Entrepreneurs using effectual reason
actively shape market dynamics
through adaptive strategies without
waiting for market equilibrium. This
theory emphasizes that how
entrepreneurs remain flexible towards
shifting customer needs and
unpredictable market conditions.
EM strategies are used within the
market dynamics, allowing
entrepreneurs to experience new value
propositions and different customer
responses, leading to adopt their
offerings. This aligns with Collinson
and Shaw’s EM theory (2001), which
underscores the need for adaptive
approaches in dynamic environments.
Hence, this study posits that
entrepreneurs navigate uncertainty,
build market presence, and enhance
marketing outcomes.
Empirical Review
EM is a collection of innovative,
proactive and risk-taking activities that
create, communicate and deliver value
to the target market (Whalen et al.,
2015). EM leads to achieve competitive
advantage (Hacioglu et al., 2012; Kraus
et al., 2010; Morrish & Morrish, 2011).
There is a s ignificant positive
relationship between marketing skills
and sales growth (Ayiku & Grant,
2021). Therefore, EM elements have a
positive link with SMEs’ marketing
performance (Chen, 2019). Previous
researchers strongly recommended
opportunity focus, innovation
orientation, and value creation are the
key dimensions of entrepreneurial
marketing (Sadiku-Dushi et al., 2019;
Eggers et al., 2020)
Marketing Performance
Marketing performance deals with
customer acquisition, market
penetration, brand awareness, and sales
conversion (Suci et al. 2022; Raza et al.
2023). It is reflected in the enterprise’s
ability to build customer loyalty, and
respond customer demands. Agyapong
et al., (2021), defines marketing
performance as an indicator of how well
SMEs set themselves in competitive
markets by using digital platforms, and
unique promotional strategies.
Marketing performance is achieved as
the result of customer engagement,
networking, and customer satisfaction,
which enable firms to attract new
market segments, and improve their
brand identity. SMEs integrate
relationship marketing, strategic
alliances, and excellent service delivery

Entrepreneurial Marketing and
Marketing Performance
Zaid (2022) and Liliyan (2020) found
that the entrepreneurial marketing
significantly and positively influences
marketing performance. Previous
studies also concluded that the
entrepreneurial marketing positively
influences marketing performance and
digital marketing capabilities Zahara et
al., 2023; Harini et al., 2025). Henshaw
and Ingo (2024) stated that
entrepreneurial marketing strategies
have significant and positive
relationships with customer acquisition
and customer retention, leading to
higher marketing performance. These
evidences strongly justify that there is
an influence of entrepreneurial
marketing on marketing performance.
Innovation Orientation
In the literature of EM, innovation
orientation is defined as the firm’s
capability to consistently develop and
implement new ideas and practices to
improve its offerings (Taneja &
Toombs 2014). Innovation orientation
aids businesses to maintain its
relevance in competitive markets by
adopting to consumer and market
changes (Kraus, Harms & Fink 2010).
It leads to launch novel products and
services to better engage the target
market (Boso, Story and Cadogan,
2013), and to respond to customer
demands with unique solutions, which
lead to higher satisfaction and loyalty
(Yoon and Solomon, 2017).
Innovation in EM is not only limited
to production, but also focuses
marketing and customer interaction
(Mort, Weerawardena & Liesch
2012).
Nguyen et al., (2022) found that the
innovation-oriented solutions will
improve SMEs customer
responsiveness and overall marketing
performance. Inadequate innovation-
oriented approaches may hinder
marketing performance of SMEs.
Therefore, the following hypothesis is
proposed:
H1: Innovation orientation
significantly influences SMEs’
marketing performance
Value Creation
Value creation specifies the process to
create and deliver products with
competitive benefits to customers to
foster long-term engagement (Morris,
Schindehutte & LaForge 2002). It
focuses on creating mutual value by
deeply understanding customer needs,
and personalizing experiences. Read
et al. (2009) explained value creation
as a strategic EM approach to offer
delightful experiences, and
convenience to customers. Businesses
generate value by creating higher
utility solutions to consumers and
adapting towards their demands
(Zahra, Neubaum & Huse 2000).
In the business context, value creation
is a vital concept as it enables firms to
utilize their limited resources
efficiently by considering the most
wanted benefits to customers. It also
leads differentiation, develops
customer engagement, and sustains
long-term growth (Kraus et al. 2012).
Empirical evidence shows that value
creation strategies support business to
achieve customer retention, customer
loyalty and customer advocacy
(Gilmore, Carson & Grant 2001),
which lead to marketing
performances. Agyapong et al., (2021)
revealed that delivering superior
customer value ensures higher
customer acquisition, loyalty, and
market penetration, leading to higher

Opportunity Focus
Opportunity focus is the
identification, evaluation, and
exploitation of new businesses under
uncertain market conditions (Morris et
al. 2002). It is a readiness of the
business to act on emerging market
requirements, or market
inefficiencies. Lumpkin and Dess
(1996) consider opportunity focus as a
central idea of entrepreneurial
orientation to attract market niches
and gain benefits of blue ocean
strategy. Opportunity-focused firms
become more successful in
understanding market requirements
and enhancing marketing performance
(Kraus et al. 2010).
Whalen et al., (2015) found that prior
identification of opportunities
enhances customer value offering and
marketing effectiveness. Opportunity
focus allows businesses to have power
in competitive markets. The ability to
act on contemporary needs enhances
market reach, and long-term results.
Therefore, the following hypothesis is
proposed:
H3: Opportunity focus significantly
influences SMEs’ marketing
performance.

Findings and Discussion
This study focused on investigating the
influence of EM on marketing
performance of SMEs in Sri Lanka,
targeting the Northern Province. The
findings of this study fit the evidence
of previous studies, highlighting the
influence of EM and marketing
performance (Ayiku & Grant, 2021;
Nguyen et al., 2022; Boso et al., 2013).
The results reveals that SMEs need to
give more concern on innovative
initiatives and leveraging opportunities
during the implementation of EM
practices to achieve more desirable
marketing performance.
All the predictors of EM, innovation
orientation, value creation, and
opportunity focus, have significant
positive relationship with marketing
performance, especially a strong
relationship from innovation
orientation.  The regression analysis
shows a significant influence of all the
predictors on dependent variable with
the overall variation of 52.3%, and the
rest could be explained by other
factors which are not included in this
study. Innovation orientation is
identified as the most dominating
factor among all three predictors in
influencing the marketing
performance of SMEs.
Conclusion and
Recommendations
The results of this study significantly
contribute to the literature of
entrepreneurial marketing, as it was
rarely discussed with marketing
performance in the context of
developing economies. This study
concludes that innovative
entrepreneurial marketing initiatives
bring more desirable marketing
outcomes to the business, thereby a
substantial amount of time to be spent
on designing effective entrepreneurial
marketing practices. Further, it is also
recommended that the SME owners or
managers should focus on capitalizing
available opportunities to attain their
marketing goals. Searching
opportunities may enable SMEs to
move with the pace of technology and
dynamic market conditions.
Even though, creating value is the
prime obligation of any marketer, this
study reveals that other variables,
innovation orientation and
opportunity focus, have higher
influence on marketing performance
than value creation. It clearly shows
that going with the market trend is
always preferrable to cater more
customers.
