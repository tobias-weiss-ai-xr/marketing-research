Abstract
This study investigates the relationship between Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) performance and firms’
financial health within the context of sustainability and green signalling. Drawing on signalling theory and the resource-
based view, the research examines whether ESG performance serves as a credible indicator of financial strength and
stability. Using a secondary dataset comprising 985 firm-level observations, the study employs quantitative analysis,
including descriptive statistics, correlation, and regression techniques. The findings reveal a positive and statistically
significant relationship between ESG performance and financial health indicators. Specifically, ESG shows a stronger
association with the Piotroski score, indicating its influence on operational efficiency and financial strength, while its
relationship with the Altman Z-scoreis comparatively weaker,suggestinga more limited impact on overall financial stability.
These results highlight that sustainability practices contributemore directlyto internal performance rather than immediate
solvency outcomes. The study also emphasizes the importance of aligning green marketing signals with actual ESG
performance to enhance credibility and avoid greenwashing risks. Despite limitations related to secondary data and proxy-
based measurement, the research provides valuable insights into the role of ESG as a strategic tool for improving financial
outcomes. The findings contribute tothe growing literature onsustainability and corporate performance.
Keywords:ESG performance, financial health, greenmarketing signals, sustainability, signalling theory
1. Introduction
Corporate sustainability no longer remains a peripheral
issue; it is now a strategic priority due to the growing
environmental challenges and regulatory pressures, as
well as increased expectations on the part of
stakeholders. Not only to ensure compliance,
organizations are now being increasingly required to
integrate sustainability into their core business strategies
(Fatima et al., 2023; Masocha and Fatoki, 2018). In this
context, companies are actively using green marketing
cues to signify their environmental commitment and
improve corporate image and affect stakeholder
perceptions. These signs, which are enshrined in
promotional strategies and company disclosures, are
critical towards creating trust and reputation that are
essential in maintaining competitive advantage (Alam
and Islam, 2021; Lunde, 2018). Meanwhile, as an
instrument of assessing corporate sustainability
performance, Environmental, Social, and Governance
(ESG) metrics have become a standardized tool,
allowing firms to quantify both environmental and
social impacts as well as enhancing transparency and
accountability(Eccles et al., 2014;Grougiouet al., 2024).
Although there is a general increase in the focus on
sustainability practices, it is yet to be determined to
what extent sustainability initiatives can lead to tangible
financial results. The previous studies indicate that a set
of strategies aimed at sustainability can result in
improved financial performance due to the ability to
strengthen the corporate reputation, build trust in
stakeholders, and achieve the efficacy of the operations
(Salam and Jahed, 2023; Yan et al., 2022). But empirical
results have been shown to be inconsistent, in part
because of the differences in measurement methods,
and the lack of integrated systems that establish links
between environmental activities and financial
outcomes. Specifically, the idea of environmental cost
accounting, which is designed to capture and allocate
environmental expenditures, is underutilized or
reported poorly in most companies, thus limiting the
capacity to assess its direct impact on financial
performance (Al-Mawali, 2021; Mondal et al., 2024). As
a result, big data ESG datasets have become more
prominent as alternative sources of information, but
this is mostly a reflection of outcomes, rather than
underlying managerial processes.

The fact that secondary ESG data is relied upon
introduces a critical research gap in that the secondary
ESG data is dependent on the use of proxy variables to
represent complex constructs like green marketing and
environmental accounting. Although the ESG
indicators help to gain a good idea of the sustainability
performance of the firms, they do not directly reflect
the strategic and accounting processes that enable the
firms to achieve the above outcomes. This restriction
highlights the necessity to have empirical frameworks
that combine green marketing signals, ESG
performance, and financial health metrics to give a
more comprehensive picture of the sustainability-
performance relationship (Ahmed et al., 2024; Al-
Tamimi, 2022). It is especially crucial to address this gap
in the context of global corporate data where
heterogeneity among industries and regions necessitates
scalable and robust methods of analysis.
The paper utilizes the concepts of the sustainability
practices and financial performance to clarify the
relationship between sustainability practices and
financial performance. According to the stakeholder
theory, firms undertakesustainability initiatives tofulfill
the expectations of different stakeholders, and thus,
increase the level of legitimacy and survival in the long
term (Freeman, 1984). According to the resource-based
view, sustainability capabilities, such as ESG
management, have the potential to be resources used as
strategic resources to generate competitive advantage
(Barney, 1991). In the meantime, signaling theory
focuses on how sustainability disclosures can help to
reduce information asymmetry and to establish
credibility with the market (Spence, 1973). By
combining these two views, the study will explore how
the signals of the sustainability-performance nexus
impact ESG performance and how ESG performance,
in turn, contribute to financial health, thus, providing a
more subtle insight into the sustainability-performance
nexus.
Research Objectives
 To examine the impact of green marketing
signals on corporate ESG performance using
proxy indicators derived from sustainability
and marketing-related variables
 To analyze the relationship between ESG
performance and firms’ financial health,
measured through financial stability indicators
suchas Altman Z-scoreand Piotroski score
 To investigate the mediating role of ESG
performance in the relationship between green
marketing signals and financial health
outcomes in a global corporate context
2. Methodology
2.1 Research Design
The research design in this study is quantitative and
explanatory research design because it aims to explore
the relationship between the green marketing signals
and the ESG performance and financial health. The
cross-sectional approach is used because the analysis will
be performed using the firm-level observations of a
secondary data. The research takes a deductive approach
to research, the hypotheses are based on the already
known theories like the stakeholder theory, the
resource-based view, and the signaling theory. The
design is suitable to test causal relations on a large scale
usinglarge scale data.
2.2 Data Source and Sample
The data needed in this study are takenout of a publicly
accessible Kaggle dataset that consists of ESG and
financial data of approximately 7,600 companies and
more than 50,000 observations. The dataset consists of
companies of various industries and geographical
regions, which increases external validity. It offers the
standardized measures including ESG scores,
sustainability indicators, and financial health measures.
Companies that lack or are missing key variables are not
included as this will guarantee reliability of the data.
The last sample is a diverse and strong sample that can
beused to carry out a large-scale empirical study.
2.3 Variable Measurement
The study defines constructs with proxy variables based
on the data. The indicators of green marketing signals
are measured using marketing and advertising
controversy indicator (reverse coded) and SDG related
variable such as climate action and responsible
consumption. The overall ESG score is used to measure
ESG performance and it captures the environmental,
social and governance aspects. The financial health is
determined by the Altman Z-score and Piotroski F-score
which are popular measures of firm robustness. Control
variables such as industry type and environmental
scandals are included to explain the heterogeneity in
firms.
2.4 Data Analysis Techniques
Data preprocessing which involves data processing
techniques such as missing values, normalization, and
treating outliers are employed to analyze the data to
ensure robustness. Descriptive statistics are calculated to
summarize the data and have an idea of how it is
distributed. Correlation analysis is done to evaluate the
relationships and identify multicollinearity between
variables. Then multiple regression analysis is used to
test the direct relationships between variables. The
mediation analysis with the bootstrapping methods is
used to test the indirect effect of ESG performance
between the signals of green marketing and financial
health.
2.5 Reliability and Validity
The research follows the set of reliability and validity
standards to achieve the methodological rigor. The
construct validity is facilitated by using widely accepted
ESG and financial indicators of the dataset.
Multicollinearity is evaluated based on the variance
inflation factors (VIF), and it should not exceed
acceptable levels. Checks on robustness are carried out
by testing alternative specifications of the model. As

4. Discussion
The results suggest that the financial health of the firms
has a positive relationship with the performance of ESG,
but the relationship varies among financial indicators.
According to the correlation results, the Piotroski score is
more strongly correlated with the ESG performance than
the winsorized Altman Z-score, implying that the
operational financial strength is more closely associated
with the ESG performance than is the broader
bankruptcy-risk stability. This helps prove the argument
that the impact of sustainability performance on long-
term financial stability might be more gradual and
indirect.
These results are in line with signaling theory that
proposes that firms employ ESG disclosures and
sustainability practices to minimize information
asymmetry and communicate credibility to external
stakeholders. ESG performance also serves as a strategic
indicator that can lead to increased investor confidence,
brand value, and reputational legitimacy (Lee et al., 2022;
Yudha et al., 2024). In this respect, more transparent,
governed and resilient, firms with more robust ESG
profiles can be seen as more transparent, better governed,
and more resilient, thus enhancing the market and
stakeholder assessment. This definition is also in line with
the evidence that voluntary sustainability reporting is able
to increase the value of firms by indicating organizational
quality and commitment to the long-term (Friske et al.,
2023).
The fact that ESG is more strongly related with Piotroski
score further indicates that sustainability performance
might be linked with the fundamental aspects of the
operation of the firms. The positive ESGPiotroski
correlation suggests that the companies with higher
sustainability performance can also exhibit the higher
level of internal financial discipline. This result aligns
with the previous literature that showed that ESG
practices and non-financial reporting can contribute to
financial performance by enhancing resource distribution,
accountability, and trustworthiness among stakeholders
(Crous et al., 2022; Whelan et al., 2021). It is also helpful
to agree with the notion that sustainable finance and
ESG-driven strategies can help in corporate reputation
and organizational resilience (Arhinful etal., 2025).
Nonetheless, the relatively low correlation between ESG
and the Altman Z-score indicates that the ESG
performance is not necessarily enough to strongly explain
the reduction of bankruptcy-risks or overall financial
stability. Various structural factors influence financial
stability and these factors are capital structure, liquidity,
industry risk, and macroeconomic factors. Thus, it could
actually be that ESG is functioning as an element of
financial resilience, as opposed to being a direct
determinant of solvency. The current interpretation is
aligned with the recent findings that the relationship
between ESG and performance is dependent on the
contextual and institutional conditions, especially in
industries where a long payback period is required when
sustainability investments aremade (Mirza et al., 2025).
The implications of the results to green marketing and
sustainability signaling are also discussed. The ESG
indicators can help increase the validity of the claims of
sustainability in green marketing due to the fact that they
can offer measurable evidence of sustainability
performance. Nonetheless, companies that articulate
environmental responsibility, but perform poorly in terms
of ESR, will be seen as perpetrating greenwashing. These
perceptions have the potential to undermine consumer
confidence and harm corporate legitimacy (Szabo and
Webster, 2021). Hence, transparent ESG reporting and
verifiable sustainability results should be in support of
green marketing and not a symbolic communication
alone.
Regarding the reporting aspect, the results support the
significance of designed sustainability disclosure models.
Because ESG metrics are signals to the stakeholders, the
quality, consistency and comparability of sustainability
reporting is necessary to transform sustainability activities
into financial and reputational value. This goes in line
with the argument of Abeysekera (2022) that
sustainability reporting should offer decision-useful
information that relates environmental and social
performance with organizational accountability. The
application of ESG data as a proxy of sustainability
performance in this study shows the value and the
limitation of secondary ESG data: it helps to conduct
empirical tests on a large scale but does not necessarily
capture the mechanisms of environmental accounting of
firms.
The discussion affirms that ESG performance is positively
related to financial health, especially operational financial
prowess, whereas its impact on financial health seems less
pronounced. The paper builds upon the signaling theory
by demonstrating that ESG performance is a plausible
sustainability signal in corporate financial analysis. It also
adds to the green marketing literature by showing that
sustainability communication needs to be supported by
quantifiable ESG results in order to produce both
financial and reputational benefits.
Conclusion
This paper has studied the connection between
sustainability performance and financial health of firms
using a large-scale secondary dataset, which gives empirical
evidence on the sustainability-performance nexus. The
results show that the ESG performance is positively
related to financial performance, with a stronger positive
relationship observed on the operation financial strength
reflected in the Piotroski score rather than on the overall
financial stability, as measured by the Altman Z-score.
This indicates that the role of sustainability practices in
enhancing internal efficiency, quality of governance, and
resource management, may be more immediate, whereas
their effects on long-term financial stability may be more
long-term. The findings confirm the theoretical premises
