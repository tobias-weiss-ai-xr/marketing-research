Abstract
This article focuses on reviewing technology trends related to the creative and cultural
industries (CCIs) and their impact on the development of cultural goods and services.
Through a systematic literature review and a technology oversight process, the most
influential global technology trends for CCIs are identified, with a specific focus
on digital content. The results reveal that these technology trends are driving changes
in the creation, the production, the distribution, and the consumption of digital
content. On the demand side, there has been a significant increase in the consump-
tion of digital content, fueled by improved access and technological efficiency. The
pandemic has further accelerated this shift. On the supply side, CCIs have lever-
aged internet access and mobile devices to promote brands, deploy digital market-
ing strategies, and expand e-commerce. The study identifies key subsectors of CCIs
in the digital content field, including audiovisual, design, editorial and publishing,
music and entertainment, digital advertising, information technology, software, com-
puter services, and video games. Finally, it highlights the key role of CCIs in the transi-
tion toward a more sustainable and socially responsible economy. This study provides
valuable insights into the dynamics of CCIs and the technological trends reshaping this
sector, fostering innovation and professionalization in the creative economy.

Introduction
As the global economy continues to evolve, the concept of knowledge economy is emerg-
ing, surpassing the scope of the creative economy. While creative economy focuses on
using technology to produce goods and services that generate economic benefits, knowl-
edge economy is broader in scope. It is built on essential pillars, such as the production,
transfer, and exchange of knowledge, collective learning systems, creative activity, inno -
vation, and the integration of information and communication technologies (ICTs) into
production processes (Newbigin, 2010).

development of territories, transforming them into intelligent spaces that contribute to
improving life quality.

tries and have promoted cultural diversity. These industries are emerging as key driv -

Reference framework
Contextual framework
In recent decades, the traditional extractivist economic model has undergone signif -
icant changes, paving the way for a transition toward knowledge and creative econo -
mies. Despite differing in concept, these models share the commonality of generating
goods and services based on ST&I, protected by intellectual property rights, thereby
promoting economic and social growth. In Colombia, creative economy, known as the
orange economy since the enactment of Law 1834 of 2017, seeks to formalize employ -
ment in the cultural sector and strengthen ancestral knowledge and cultural heritage
(Calle, 2020). CCIs aim to promote, distribute, and generate products and services that
protect culture and facilitate social inclusion and cultural diversity. Based on the New -
bigin Guide classification (2010) and the Ministry of Culture regional mapping (2019),
CCIs are divided into eight sectors and subsectors, covering a diverse and rich market of
cultural activities contributing to employment and economic growth. These categories
share the characteristic of creating spaces for social integration and promoting entre -
preneurship, in addition to representing intangible values inherent to the identity of the
community (Fig. 1).
Beyond their cultural and social contributions, CCIs cut across several economic
activities, providing value and innovation through the application of technology, crea -
tivity, and research. This activity growth is driving the need for public policy to support
CCI development and growth and for employment in the cultural sector to be formal -
ized (Galloway & Dunlop, 2007). Culture plays a key role in CCIs as a pillar of collec -
tive and individual development. It is understood as a construct combining different
activities around learning from the past and working with ideas and symbols (Echever -
ría, 2019). Creativity, emphasized as a key tool in Latin America and the Caribbean, is
considered an integral part of economic transformation. Intellectual property is a key

component in shaping creative and knowledge economies, enabling the monetization of
products with cultural and economic values.
The development of research and creative process (RCP) capabilities is described as a
bridge linking scientific knowledge with artistic creativity, contributing to the rational
understanding of the social and cultural world. Examples from countries, such as South
Korea, India, Japan, and the United States, illustrate how the promotion of RCP contrib -
utes to the growth of industries, such as the audiovisual, video games, design, culture
and software industries, thanks to policies that foster creativity, strategic alliances, and
efforts strengthening human capital (La República, 2019).
In the specific case of South Korea, the “Korean Wave” has boosted the video game
industry, generating significant employment and exports. In India, CCIs significantly
contribute to the economy, creating jobs, boosting tourism, and generating national
income. Japan, a world reference in the video game industry, has achieved outstanding
figures in exports and GDP contribution. The United States, with a robust creative econ-
omy, has a trade surplus and a significant percentage of employment in the CCI sector
(La República, 2021).
With the boom of digital content in CCIs, it is evident that their efficient produc -
tion and distribution, interactivity, capacity for updating, and easy maintenance make
them popular and accessible products. This phenomenon points to the need to formu -
late public policies that promote the development of CCIs, strengthen the institutional
framework in the cultural sector, and encourage the formalization of employment. To
summarize, this study highlights the relevance of CCIs as key drivers of economic and
social development and their substantial contribution to the global landscape.

Theoretical framework
Today’s society is in the midst of a major transformation, driven by the generation of
knowledge, creativity, and innovation, and marks a transition from the extractivist eco -
nomic paradigm. A key factor in the transformation to a knowledge-based society is
to value these skills as resources for the development of intangible goods and services.
Thorsby (2008) and Smith (1988) initially pointed to CCI industries as unprofitable
activities. This perspective has evolved with technological progress, thereby transform -
ing the economic viability of these activities.
The evolution from the term “culture industry” coined by German critics in 1947 to
CCIs has marked a significant shift. Horkheimer et al. (2002) conceptualization of the
“culture industry” as an economic activity integrating cultural goods for economic gain
has given way to a broader understanding of CCIs. Calling for cultural transforma -
tion beyond mere commercialization, Szpilbarg and Saferstein (2014) pointed to a shift
toward approaches emphasizing the social and symbolic value of culture.
García (2002) contributed to the definition of CCIs, mentioning that they are a set of
activities that produce profitable and scalable cultural goods and services. Despite initial
skepticism, the concept of cultural industry has evolved into cultural and creative indus -
tries, emphasizing the interconnection between activities and goods through production
chains. The classification of CCIs according to United Nations Conference on Trade and
Development (UNCTAD) and The United Nations Development Programme (UNDP)
(2010) focuses on the structural characteristics of goods and services, recognizing the
importance of cultural expression and intangibles in these activities. The economy of
cultural goods and services is viewed from the perspective of creativity, emphasizing
the added value of artisanal, innovative, and artistic products with a technological base.
CCIs address current issues and are fundamental to national identity and economic
transformation.
The transformation of CCIs in recent decades has been influenced by new technolo -
gies that have provided products with immersive, customized, and flexible experiences
(Rodríguez, 2021). Regarding the future of CCIs, technology is emerging as a key driv -
ing force, integrating into the digital revolution and transforming business models. The
Inter-American Development Bank (IDB) report identified five drivers of change: narra -
tive in cultural events, geographic and climatic events, technology, intellectual property,
and employment (Borrero, 2023). In this context, technology emerges as the determin -
ing factor for the future of CCIs, driving the creative revolution in fashion, music, video
games, film, and other sectors. Intellectual property plays a key role in the distribution
and protection of cultural products, with a focus on the use of technologies such as
blockchain. Employment, especially through online platforms offering job opportunities,
is an influential component in future of CCIs. The digital, creative, and knowledge-based
economy has been transformed by these platforms and has contributed to the closing of
the inequality gap and social equality.
In addition to these technological advancements, macrotrends are also shaping the
landscape of CCIs. Macrotrends refer to pervasive, long-term shifts that significantly
influence the development of entire industries, economies, and societies. These trends
are often driven by factors, such as technological innovations, demographic changes, and
environmental imperatives, and they typically span multiple sectors and geographical

Page 6 of 29Ortiz‑Ospino et al. Journal of Innovation and Entrepreneurship           (2025) 14:39
regions (Howkins, 2002; Valdivieso, 2022). Macrotrends can lead to the redefinition of
industries by fostering new business models, regulatory frameworks, and cultural shifts.
On the other hand, microtrends are smaller, more niche changes that emerge within
specific sectors or consumer groups. While they may not have the broad impact of mac -
rotrends, microtrends can be highly influential in shaping the behaviors, preferences,
and innovations within a particular industry or community (Rodríguez, 2021). Over
time, microtrends may evolve into macrotrends if their influence spreads or if they align
with larger economic or societal shifts (UNCTAD & UNDP , 2010).
Research methods and coding
This study adopted a qualitative research approach, utilizing a documentary method
to address the research problem related to identifying technological trends in the CCI
sector. This approach aimed to collect, analyze, and assess primary and secondary doc -
uments and references related to the topic to obtain a solid understanding of the techno-
logical trends in the sector and express a specific viewpoint on its evolution (Hernández
et al., 2014).
Method
The research document was developed in four stages to achieve the established objec -
tives. These stages include identifying information needs, data collection, data analysis
and validation of information in the field of CCIs, and presenting results. Together, these
stages form what is known as technology oversight. The effectiveness of a technology
oversight process largely depends on the application of a structured and appropriate
method (San Juan & Rodríguez, 2016). The approach used in this research is depicted
in the following flow chart. This approach was carefully designed to ensure the effective
monitoring of technological trends in the CCIs (Fig. 2).
Figure 3 illustrates the analytical system used in this systematic literature review. Vari-
ous information sources, including scientific databases, patents, and text mining, were
employed. Through a categorization and co-occurrence analysis of terms, 10 macro -
trends and 30 microtrends were identified as key drivers of technological evolution in
the creative and cultural industries. This analysis was validated through triangulation
with industry reports and expert input, ensuring the reliability of the findings.
The following section provides a detailed description of each methodological phase
that constitutes the Technology Foresight process developed in this study. This process is
closely linked to the Systematic Analysis Framework applied for the identification, cate -
gorization, and validation of technological trends in the CCIs. This methodology ensures
a structured and reproducible approach, enabling the identification of key terms, the
analysis of their co-occurrence, and the validation of findings through triangulation with
specialized sources.

Obtaining information
The methodology employed in this research involved an exhaustive search and review of
literature from secondary sources to monitor trends within the field of study. A diverse
array of information sources was analyzed, ensuring a comprehensive and structured data
collection process. However, patent records were excluded due to their limited relevance to
this study. This decision was based on the fact that patents primarily capture trends within
sectors characterized by distinct production, distribution, and consumption dynamics,
which differ from the focus of this research. To ensure the rigor and reliability of the data-
set, a meticulous data curation process was conducted using recognized databases, includ-
ing Scopus, Web of Science, IEEE, and Google Patents (John et al., 2025; Kalia et al., 2022).
For data analysis, a co-occurrence analysis of terms approach was employed, enabling the
identification of thematic trends through keyword analysis. This process highlighted recur-
rent terms, such as Smart Cities, Blockchain Disruption and NFT, Gig Economy, Immer-
sive Technologies, Creation of New Products and Content to Improve the User Experience,
Regulatory and Normative Frameworks, Business Model Opportunities, Changes in Con-
sumer Behavior, and Market Performance (González-Sarmiento et al., 2021; Zupic & Čater,
2014). These findings provide valuable insights into emerging research priorities and the
evolving landscape of technological trends.
A total of 70 academic articles were reviewed and analyzed, along with 24 specialized
websites and blogs. The literature review integrated both general information sources and
specialized publications focused on the creative industries sector. Additionally, industry-
specific reports from specialized sources were identified to provide deeper insights into
market dynamics and technological advancements.
To ensure a comprehensive and diverse dataset, secondary sources encompassed market
studies, reports from consulting firms and think tanks, and press articles, offering a broad
perspective on industry trends and innovation. Moreover, open-access and subscription-
based databases, such as UNCTAD, DANE, Euromonitor, CB Insights, and Statista were
systematically analyzed, enabling the identification of quantitative and qualitative trends
relevant to the study. This approach ensured a robust methodological foundation, integrat-
ing both academic and industry-driven perspectives to enhance the validity and applicabil-
ity of the findings.

Analyzing information
Information about trends in the creative industries was scattered and varied. To
address this challenge, it was decided to structure and organize all the information
into a trends matrix. In this matrix, the most relevant sources of information were
linked to each of the findings, and detailed descriptions of the trends based on these
sources were provided. In total, 30 microtrends were identified and consolidated,
which were then grouped into 10 macrotrends. These macrotrends were organized
according to their impact on demand, supply, and the ecosystem.
Given that the data encompassed both qualitative and quantitative aspects, the
data processing and cleaning process was essential to standardize and homogenize
the information. The results were mainly presented in a qualitative format to facili -
tate their reading and interpretation. In analyzing the information collected, a matrix
was used to assess the impact of the trends, allowing each trend to be assigned to
the appropriate category. This provided a deeper understanding of the scope of each
trend and their impact on the ecosystem as a whole. Finally, all the information was
consolidated into a format clearly indicating the impact of each trend on supply,
demand, and the ecosystem. This process provided a structured and comprehensive
view of trends in the CCIs.
Validation of information
To ensure the validity and reliability of the findings, a validation process was con -
ducted through triangulation, combining the analysis of reports with expert consulta -
tion. This approach allowed for cross-referencing and enriching the information from
multiple perspectives, ensuring greater accuracy in data interpretation.
Expert validation was a fundamental component of the methodological process.
Professionals with extensive experience in the field were selected based on their aca -
demic, research, and/or practical expertise in the study area. Their role was to assess
the relevance, clarity, and applicability of the descriptors used in the trend matrix, as
well as the appropriateness of the terminology used to define the identified trends.
The validation process involved review sessions and critical analysis, during which
experts provided qualitative feedback on the conceptual coherence and practical use -
fulness of the information presented. Based on their observations, adjustments were
made to refine the terminology and ensure that the descriptors accurately reflected
the studied reality. This approach not only enhanced the structure and content of the
trend matrix but also strengthened its applicability in academic and professional con -
texts, ensuring that the resulting document was both comprehensible and relevant to
the target audience.

and collaborative networks in building smart cities. These changes are aimed at meeting
the emission reduction and energy efficiency targets set by the European Commission.

Blockchain disruption and NFT
Blockchain technology, a distributed data recording system, is growing steadily. It is
projected that at least 10% of global GDP will be stored on blockchain platforms by the
end of 2025 (Deloitte, 2018). Blockchain’s versatility renders it applicable across various
domains, including ensuring the security of financial transactions, safeguarding medi -
cal records, facilitating smart contracts, enabling asset tracking, protecting intellectual
property, establishing distributed databases, and more. Its benefits include safe pay -
ments, cryptoasset exchange, non-fungible tokens (NFTs), intellectual property protec -
tion, mediation between the real and digital worlds, interoperability with AI, metaverse
creation, smart contracts, peer-to-peer (P2P) transfers, and identity verification. The
microtrends associated with the blockchain macrotrend are presented below.
1. Transaction and validation possibilities
Blockchain technology is revolutionizing transactions and information verification,
ranging from direct payments without intermediaries to the implementation of smart
contracts and the creation of NFTs for digital art. It is also improving identity verifica -
tion and creating new digital spaces such as cryptovoxels. This trend is solving everyday
challenges and is in high demand, especially in the creative and entertainment industries
for solving historic problems, such as ticketing and rights management.
2. NFTs as certificates of value
NFTs are cryptographic codes that represent unique digital assets, creating scarcity
and exclusivity in the digital world. These certificates of value have revolutionized art,
video games, and revenue sharing in the value chain (Deloitte, 2018). The NFT market
has grown exponentially, with works selling in the million dollars price. Their trading
volume has reached impressive figures, showing their ability to democratize intellectual
property and profits in the creative industries. NFTs not only protect the intellectual
property of creators but also provide opportunities for innovation and transformation in
the creative economy.

Gig economy
Collaborative economy, also known as “gig economy, ” has emerged as an alternative to
traditional work that offers workers flexibility and autonomy. Evolved partly out of the
2008 labor crisis, this form of casual employment allows people to choose when and
where to work according to their needs, often through online platforms. However, it
presents challenges, such as job instability and potential loneliness, for workers (BBVA,
2022).
Despite the challenges, collaborative economy is gaining ground, with platforms,
such as TaskRabbit, Uber, and Airbnb, spreading worldwide. This form of economy
encourages collaboration to improve products and services, rather than simply sharing
resources. These new forms of collaboration are giving rise to innovative communities
and meeting places, which could be the driving force behind the creation of innovation
districts in relation to CCIs, especially in the digital content sector (BBVA, 2022). The
microtrend associated with the macrotrend of the gig economy is presented below.
1. Emergence of innovation hubs to gather creative people to create digital content col -
laboratively or with alternative work arrangements
Innovation hubs are emerging as spaces where creators come together to collaborate
and produce digital content together or through alternative work arrangements. These
forms of community work foster the creation of larger and more robust collaborative
ecosystems, thereby transforming the production and consumption of digital content.
Innovation partnerships are changing the way people come together to develop innova -
tive new ideas and products. They are spreading around the world and fostering collabo-

and the flexibility it offers consumers, which reflects a growing trend (Garrido & Padron,
2021).
Discussion
In the 1990s, the concepts of creative industries and cultural economy were introduced
in Europe, with the United Kingdom leading the way in this economic strategy, which
focused on creative sectors, such as music, performing arts, design, and film, among
others. This approach sought an alternative to traditional capitalism and highlighted
the importance of creativity, cultural heritage, and the arts in the midst of the current
economic transition. CCIs gained relevance by challenging the historical stigmas that
have considered cultural and artistic activities to be unproductive. In a decade that seeks

Page 26 of 29Ortiz‑Ospino et al. Journal of Innovation and Entrepreneurship           (2025) 14:39
to distance itself from extractivist economy and explore new ways of generating wealth,
they have become an attractive proposal.
Intellectual property is depicted as a crucial axis in CCIs as it is directly linked to the
creation, production, and dissemination of copyrighted material. Authors such as Bor -
rero (2023) suggested that creative talent can be an engine for sustainable global devel -
opment in an era of disruptive technological change, emphasizing the importance of
participation in knowledge economy. Globally, countries, such as South Korea, India,
Japan, and the United States, have boosted creative industries through policies promot -
ing creativity and talent by establishing strategic alliances and using investment funds
and tax incentives. The segmentation structure of creative economy focuses on creative
functional creations, especially in the creation, production, dissemination, and distribu -
tion of digital content.
The results of this study reveal a clear convergence between technology and creativ -
ity in reshaping the CCIs. The identification of 10 macrotrends and their corresponding
30 microtrends offers a structured roadmap for understanding how digital transforma -
tions are driving innovation within these industries. For example, the rise of immersive
technologies, such as virtual and augmented reality, alongside advancements in artificial
intelligence, is fostering new forms of content creation and consumption (Kriukova &
Ameridze, 2021). These technologies are not only changing the creative process but also
the ways in which audiences engage with cultural products.
Furthermore, the growing prominence of blockchain and non-fungible tokens (NFTs)
signals a shift toward decentralized ownership models, where creators can maintain
greater control over their intellectual property and revenue streams (Hoque & Littmann,
2020). This microtrend, part of a broader macrotrend of digital disruption, could rede -
fine the economic models underpinning the CCIs, creating opportunities for more equi -
table value distribution across the creative value chain (Fritz et al., 2020).
Future research should explore how these trends interact over time, particularly
in the context of emerging markets and developing economies where the adoption of
such technologies may follow different trajectories. By understanding the intersections
between macrotrends like the gig economy and smart cities, and microtrends, such as
digital communities and innovation hubs, scholars and practitioners alike can better
anticipate shifts in consumer demand and the types of creative content that will be most
valued in future (UNESCO, 2021).
These insights are not only valuable for academic research but also for policy-makers
and industry professionals looking to harness the potential of these trends to drive eco -
nomic growth, cultural preservation, and innovation within the creative economy.
To effectively leverage these insights, it is crucial to understand how they are grouped
according to their impact on the ecosystem, demand, and supply axes. The creativity of
those involved in the CCIs is essential to adjust to the changing demands of the con -
text and generate new forms of trade in creative goods and services. On the demand
side, there is an increase in the consumption of digital content with the growth of tech -
nological developments, as the digital content market is increasingly relevant. The
pandemic has accelerated the adoption and development of this content, providing
efficient and agile forms of communication, commerce, production, and consumption.
On the supply side, technological developments in digital environments have enabled

Page 27 of 29
Ortiz‑Ospino et al. Journal of Innovation and Entrepreneurship           (2025) 14:39

new entertainment services, activities, and businesses to emerge. Companies are taking
advantage of social networks and e-commerce, adjusting to new digital media to pro -
mote brands and facilitate the purchase and sale of products.
In summary, technological development significantly influences the production, dis -
tribution, and consumption of digital content, thereby creating opportunities for com -
panies to communicate and reach new markets. CCIs are positioned as key elements in
this transformation of business systems, with creativity and technology being key drivers
for the future of these industries.
Conclusion
This research focused on an analysis of technological trends affecting CCI in the field of
digital content around the world. A qualitative analysis and a systematic literature review
were conducted with a focus on technology monitoring. Accordingly, 10 macro-tech -
nology trends were identified. These trends were considered to impact demand, supply,
and the overall CCI ecosystem. These findings provide a deeper understanding of the
dynamics of CCIs over time and the technology trends around them. The focus is on the
key role of innovation in the competitiveness and economic development of territories.
Creative economy, which combines commercial and cultural value, has become a key
focus for governments and businesses worldwide. Its contribution to economic growth,
innovation, and prosperity is acknowledged as is its importance for economic diversifi -
cation and inclusive development. In this context, there is an emphasis on the need for
the effective integration of CCIs into government policy agendas and business strategies.
This goes beyond wealth creation, as CCIs play a key role in building social structures,
strengthening territorial identities, and contributing to the economy as a whole.
In conclusion, this study highlights the importance of understanding and embracing
technological trends in the field of CCIs and how they can influence the transformation
of business systems. The subsectors identified, such as the audiovisual industry, design,
music, digital advertising, information technology, software, and video games, have a
key role to play in this evolving context.
Limitations
This study presents several limitations that should be considered. First, the research
relies on secondary sources, including scientific databases, industry reports, and text
mining, which may introduce biases due to the availability and quality of the analyzed
information. Additionally, although a technology foresight approach and a systematic lit-
erature review were employed to identify macro and microtrends, the qualitative nature
of the study limits the possibility of quantitative validation or statistical measurement of
the impact of these trends. Moreover, the rapid technological evolution within the CCIs
implies that some findings may become outdated over time, highlighting the need for
continuous updates. Furthermore, the categorization of emerging trends through the -
matic analysis involves a degree of subjective interpretation, despite the use of triangula-
tion techniques with experts to minimize this bias. Finally, some identified trends may
be more applicable to specific geographical contexts or industry segments, suggesting

the need for further studies with segmented approaches and complementary method -
ologies to achieve greater precision in assessing their impact.
