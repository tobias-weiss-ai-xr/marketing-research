Introduction: Artiﬁcial Intelligence (AI) is reshaping the global business
landscape by transforming how organisations approach marketing. Despite its
growing adoption, research on AI in marketing remains limited in emerging
economies like South Africa, where contextual challenges and opportunities
are underexplored. This study explores the adoption and application of AI
within a structured marketing process framework (Situational Analysis,
Objectives, Strategy, Tactics, Action, and Control) in South Africa’s e-
commerce sector. It examines how digital marketing professionals integrate AI
tools to enhance marketing effectiveness and organisational performance.
Methods: A quantitative research approach was employed, utilising data from
415 digital marketing professionals.
Results: The results indicate widespread AI adoption in the tactical and action-
oriented stages of the marketing framework, particularly in content
personalisation, digital advertising, and customer support. However, strategic
and control-oriented applications like predictive analytics and voice search
exhibit lower adoption rates due to technical constraints.
Discussion: The study outlines a theoretical contribution to marketing
communication theory by demonstrating how AI enables precise sender-
receiver matching and dynamic feedback loops. It concludes with implications
for South African practitioners and policymakers, emphasising the need for
robust data governance and infrastructure development.

1 Introduction
Artiﬁcial intelligence (AI), particularly machine learning (ML), is driving an inevitable
digital transformation across industries by enhancing efﬁciency, personalisation, and
customer engagement ( Bakonyi, 2024; Volkmar et al. , 2022). While AI is increasingly
powering customer-brand interactions in global retail settings ( Campbell et al. , 2020),
empirical research on its practical application remains at an early stage in emerging
economies like South Africa. To address this gap, this study examines AI adoption
through the lens of a structured marketing process framework. By utilising the
SOSTAC model comprising Situational Analysis, Objectives, Strategy, Tactics, Action,
and Control, this research provides a systematic perspective on how AI tools are
integrated into organisational work/f_lows ( Smith, 2018). Establishing these distinct

In South Africa’s rapidly growing e-commerce sector, AI

2.1 The marketing process framework and
AI integration
To systematically analyse AI’s role, this study uti lises the
SOSTAC marketing process framework, which consists of six
integrated stages: Situational Analysis, Objectives, Strategy, Tactics,
Action, and Control ( Smith, 2018). Establishing these phases is
critical because it allows researchers to identify exactly where AI-
driven automation replaces or enhances human decisi on-making.
Situational Analysis involves a comprehensive audit  of internal
capabilities and external market constraints, while Strategy focuses
on Segmentation, Targeting, and Positioning (STP). The latter
stages, Tactics, Action, and Control, deal with the  operational
implementation and real-time monitoring of marketin g activities

2.2 Theoretical contribution to marketing
communication theory
AI integration extends traditional marketing communication
theory by shifting models from one-to-many toward iterative,
data-rich dialogues ( Sharma et al. , 2023; Smith, 2018).
Speciﬁcally, AI enables more precise sender-receiver matching
and dynamic two-way interactions, which reshape message
relevance and timing ( Smith, 2018; Volkmar et al. , 2022). These
enhancements align with core constructs of source credibility
and message tailoring, while introducing new theoretical
tensions regarding trust and transparency in automated
persuasion ( Malapane and Ndlovu , 2022). By embedding AI
into the control stage, ﬁrms create adaptive messaging loops that
represent a mechanism-level evolution of communication theory
(Sharma et al. , 2023).

2.3.2 AI in content personalisation (targeted
product recommendation)
Closely related to AI in content marketing for targeted
audiences, AI-driven personalisation has become a cornerstone
of modern marketing strategies, particularly within the context
of product recommendations. By analysing consumer data, such
as browsing history and purchase behaviour, AI systems can
deliver personalised product recommendations that are tailored
to individual requirements and preferences ( Gao et al. , 2023;
Pearson, 2019). This approach has been shown to signiﬁcantly
enhance customer satisfaction and loyalty, as consumers receive
more relevant and timely recommendations that meet their
needs ( AIContentfy, 2023; Gkikas and Theodoridis , 2022). AI’s
ability to learn and adapt to changing consumer behaviour and
preferences ensures that product recommendations remain
relevant at any given time.
2.3.3 AI in digital advertising (targeted advertising)
Targeted advertising is an effective marketing communication
tool ( Farahat and Bailey , 2012). AI is currently playing a crucial
role in optimising digital advertising efforts by enabling targeted
advertising. Through the analysis of consumer data, AI
algorithms can identify the most relevant audiences for speciﬁc
ads, thereby increasing the efﬁciency and effectiveness of
advertising campaigns ( Sharma, 2023; Siegel, 2023). Advertising,
powered by AI, allows marketers to automatically purchase ad
space and deliver personalised ads in real-time, maximising
return on investment (ROI). Additionally, AI-driven analytics
provide insights into ad performance, enabling continuous
optimisation (Lee and Cho , 2020).
2.3.4 AI in customer support, such as chatbots
Customer support is crucial in maintaining excellen t business-
customer relationships (Sheth et al., 2020). The integration of AI in
customer support, particularly through chatbots, ha s transformed
how businesses interact with their customers ( AIContentfy, 2023).
AI-powered chatbots are capable of handling a wide range of
customer inquiries, providing instant and accurate responses
(Gkikas and Theodoridis , 2022). These chatbots utilise natural
language processing (NLP) to understand and respond  to
consumer queries, improving customer satisfaction b y reducing
response times and providing 24/7 support ( Olujimi and Ade-
Ibijola, 2023). Moreover, AI chatbots can learn from past
interactions, enhancing their ability to handle com plex queries
over time, thereby ensuring satisﬁed customers.
2.3.5 AI in voice search through natural language
processing (NLP)
Voice search is another area where AI excels, signiﬁcantly
enhancing the customer shopping experience by providing more
intuitive and hands-free interactions to customers. AI’s
application in voice search is largely driven by advancements in
NLP, which enable machines to understand and process human
language ( Kepuska and Bohouta , 2018). This technology has
become increasingly important as more consumers rely on voice
search for information retrieval and to assist them in their
online shopping. Through voice-activated assistants such as Siri,
Alexa, and Google Assistant, customers can effortlessly search
for products, receive personalised recommendations, and even
complete purchases without manually navigating through
websites or apps ( Logicai, 2020). This streamlines the shopping
process and creates a more engaging and accessible customer
experience, catering to consumers who prioritise convenience
and multitasking. Furthermore, AI-driven voice search is also
used to gather and analyse customer data to reﬁne search
algorithms, anticipate user needs, and deliver highly tailored
recommendations (Klaus and Zaichkowsky , 2020).
2.3.6 AI in virtual search through image detection
software
Image recognition technology, powered by AI, has
signiﬁcantly expanded the possibilities for virtual search,
transforming the way in which consumers discover and engage
with their online shopping. By leveraging sophisticated
algorithms and deep learning models, AI can analyse and
interpret visual data, enabling users to search for items by
simply uploading a photo or scanning an image ( Gkikas and
Theodoridis, 2022). This technology, employed by platforms
such as Google Lens, allows consumers to take a photo of an
item and ﬁnd similar products online ( Shiau et al. , 2020). The
ability of AI to analyse visual content and match it with relevant
products enhances the shopping experience and opens up new
avenues for e-commerce. This functionality eliminates the need
for text-based queries, making the search process more intuitive
and accessible, especially when users are unsure of the
speciﬁcations of the product. For example, a consumer can take
a picture of a desired outﬁt, piece of furniture, or accessory, and
the AI system will instantly identify and suggest matching or
complementary products available for purchase ( Deldjoo et al. ,
2023). This not only streamlines the shopping journey, but also
drives engagement by catering to visual discovery, which is
particularly appealing in the case of fashion and home décor.
2.3.7 AI in targeted and personalised emails
Email marketing has been in existence for quite some time.
However, AI’s role in email marketing extends beyond simple
automation to delivering highly targeted and personalised
emails. By analysing consumer behaviour and preferences, AI
systems can craft personalised email content that resonates with
each recipient ( Potdar, 2024). This includes tailoring subject
lines, content, and product recommendations within the email,
which increases open rates, click-through rates, and overall
engagement ( Sedani et al. , 2019). AI can also determine the
optimal time to send emails, ensuring that messages reach
recipients when they are most likely to engage ( Sharma, 2023).

2.3.9 AI in website testing and social media
business page optimisation (search engine
optimisation)
AI can play a signiﬁcant role in enhancing website
performance and social media business page optimisation
through automated testing and SEO practices ( Sharma, 2023).
AI tools can conduct A/B testing on various web page elements,
such as headlines, images, and calls to action, to determine
which versions perform best. Additionally, AI-powered SEO
tools analyse search engine algorithms and consumer behaviour
to optimise content for better visibility and ranking on search
engines ( Quin et al. , 2024). This continuous optimisation helps
businesses attract more organic trafﬁc and improve their online
presence in the long run.
2.3.10 AI in the collection and veriﬁcation of
consumer reviews
Consumer reviews are a critical aspect of online reputation
management, and AI plays a signiﬁcant role in collecting and
verifying these reviews. AI systems can automatically gather
reviews from multiple platforms and analyse them for
authenticity, ﬁltering out fake or biased reviews. This not only
ensures the integrity of the review system but also helps
businesses maintain trust with their customers. AI’s ability to
analyse review sentiment and identify recurring themes also
provides valuable feedback for improving products and services.
2.3.11 AI in robotics for speed, accuracy, and
better customer experience
Efﬁciency is empowered through AI ( AIContentfy, 2023) AI-
powered robotics is increasingly used in various industries to
enhance speed, accuracy, and customer experience. In retail and
warehousing, robots equipped with AI are used for inventory
management, order fulﬁlment, and delivery. These robots can
operate with high precision, reducing errors and improving
efﬁciency. In customer-facing roles, such as hospitality, AI-
driven robots can provide personalised services, such as check-
in assistance or room service delivery, enhancing the overall
customer experience.
2.3.12 AI in analytics (predictive and sentiment
analysis)
AI-driven analytics, including predictive and sentiment
analysis, are essential tools for understanding consumer
behaviour and predicting future trends ( AIContentfy, 2023;
Sharma, 2023). Predictive analytics uses AI algorithms to analyse
historical data and forecast future outcomes, enabling businesses
to anticipate customer needs and make data-driven decisions
(Adesina et al. , 2024). Sentiment analysis, on the other hand,
evaluates consumer emotions expressed in text, such as reviews
or social media posts, providing insights into customer
satisfaction and brand perception ( Mehraliyev et al. , 2022).
These AI-driven insights help businesses tailor their strategies to
meet consumer expectations ( Usman et al. , 2024).
2.3.13 AI in measuring user experience
Measuring customer experience is vital for any organisation
that seeks to improve its bottom line, and AI-aided measuring
experience has been proven to be an effective strategy. Siegel
(2023) is of the view that measuring user experience (UX) is
crucial for optimising digital platforms, and AI provides
sophisticated tools for this purpose. AI can track and analyse
user interactions on websites and apps, identifying pain points
and areas for improvement. Machine learning algorithms can
predict consumer behaviour, allowing businesses to make
proactive changes that enhance UX ( Rane, 2023). Furthermore,
AI-driven UX analytics can segment consumers based on their
behaviour, enabling personalised experiences that cater to
different consumer groups.
2.3.14 AI in understanding consumer behaviour
Understanding consumer behaviour is at the core of effective
marketing, and AI offers powerful tools for this purpose ( Siegel,
2023). AI can analyse vast datasets, including purchase history,
browsing patterns, and social media activity, to uncover insights
into consumer preferences and motivations ( Ribeiro et al. ,
2024). This deep understanding allows businesses to predict
consumer behaviour and tailor their marketing efforts

3 Method
The study falls within a positivist research paradigm and
applies a quantitative research method. The target population
includes professionals engaged in digital marketing or
professionals responsible for the integration of AI in the
marketing activities of businesses in the South African retail
market. An international research company collected the data
from research participants (sample size: N = 415) (professionals
responsible for digital marketing) in the South African retail
market through self-administered questionnaires via online
means. Through this method, the theoretical foundation of the
study’s constructs, deﬁnitions and conceptualisation of
constructs was utilised to guide and generate a pool of items or
statements for the research instrument ( Morgado et al. , 2017).
A four-point Likert scale was used to avoid social desirability
bias or respondent indecisiveness. The research proposal and
questionnaire were submitted to an appropriate research ethics
committee of the university and received approval (NWU-
00620-24-A4). The study adheres to the principles of integrity,
transparency, and accountability in its conduct, reporting, and
dissemination of research.
Data were collected through a structured online sur vey
administered by an international research company w ith expertise
in digital market research. The company recruited r espondents
from its established online consumer and profession al panels,
which served as the sampling frame. A non-probabili ty purposive
sampling approach was employed to ensure that only respondents
with relevant professional experience in digital ma rketing and e-
commerce were included in the study. Eligibility cr iteria required
respondents to be currently employed in marketing-r elated roles
and to have direct involvement in digital marketing  activities.
Invitations to participate were distributed electro nically, and
responses were collected anonymously.
The sample of the study consisted of 415 respondents, of
whom 38.8% are male, 60.7% are female, and 0.5% are non-
binary. The scale development process followed in this study is
the deductive method, where an understanding of the
phenomenon is attained through a review of extant literature
(Hinkin, 1995). The participants of the study reported that
14.5% (60) of their organisations employ fewer than 10
employees (micro enterprises), 22.7% (94) employ between 10
and 49 employees (small enterprises), 34.0% (141) employ
between 50 and 249 employees (medium enterprises), and 28.9%
(120) employ more than 250 employees (large enterprises). This
distribution highlights that the majority of organisations
represented in the sample fall within the SMME sector,
particularly medium-sized enterprises, which constituted the
largest group. Given the recognised role of SMMEs in fostering
economic growth, job creation, innovation, and inclusivity, the
predominance of micro, small, and medium enterprises in the
sample underscores their signiﬁcance as critical drivers of
economic development, while the presence of large
organisations re/f_lects the broader business landscape within
which SMMEs operate.
4 Results and discussion
4.1 Participants
The study participants held a diverse range of roles and
responsibilities within the digital marketing domain. Digital
marketing managers and directors constituted the largest group,
representing 31.3% of respondents. This was followed by equal
representation (19.3% each) of IT professionals involved in
digital marketing and professionals responsible for online or e-
commerce functions. Digital marketing specialists accounted for
13.5% of participants, while the remaining 16.6% occupied other
positions within the organisations. This distribution re/f_lects a
comprehensive cross-section of expertise relevant to the
application and management of digital marketing practices.
Regarding the organisational context of the participants, the
majority of respondents (79.8%) were afﬁliated with
organisations that are entirely South African-owned, while
smaller proportions reported belonging to subsidiaries of
international organisations (12.5%), franchises (6%), or other
forms of ownership (1.7%). In terms of organisational
competence in applying and leveraging AI tools, 46% of
participants indicated that their organisation operates at level 1
(highly competent), 41.4% at level 2 (competent), 11.3% at level
3 (moderately competent), and only 1.2% at level 4
(incompetent). Most organisations (54.9%) offer both physical
goods and services, followed by those providing services only
(30.1%) and physical goods only (14.9%). The business models
with respect to online presence were varied, with the largest
segment (32.8%) belonging to organisations operating primarily
as physical stores, followed by brick-and-click retailers (26.5%)
and omnichannel retailers (21%), while the remaining
participants were afﬁliated with pure-play online retailers or
direct-to-consumer (DTC) brands operating exclusively online.
This distribution highlights the diversity of organisational
contexts represented in the study and provides an important
backdrop for understanding participants’ perspectives on AI
adoption in marketing.

personalisation, and digital advertising, and weake r integration

consumer behaviour analysis and voice-based interac tion. This
pattern contributes to theory by suggesting that AI  integration
in marketing follows an incremental, capability-dri ven logic,
where organisations initially deploy AI in operatio nally
tangible and performance-visible activities before extending it
to more complex, cognition-intensive domains. In do ing so,
the study enriches emerging theoretical debates on AI-enabled
