ABSTRACT: India ’s fast-moving consumer goods (FMCG) industry is undergoing sig-
nificant transformation, primarily fueled by digitalization. This shift is supported by growing
internet access, the widespread adoption of affordable mobile technologies, and evolving con-
sumer expectations. With over 800 million internet users, digital platforms have become central
to brand–consumer interactions, yet consumer behaviour in FMCGs remains complex, char-
acterised by frequent, low-involvement purchases that are highly sensitive to promotions, peer
influence, and convenience. While previous studies have examined digital adoption broadly,
limited research has systematically assessed how specific digital marketing drivers—social media
influencers, convenience, and price offers —shape consumer buying behaviour in the FMCG
context. The study employed a quantitative research design and collected data from 203 parti-
cipants through a structured questionnaire. All constructs were assessed using a Likert scale, and
the proposed framework was tested using Partial Least Squares–Structural Equation Modelling
(PLS-SEM). Reliability analysis confirmed high internal consistency Cronbach ’s a = 0.973,
while confirmatory factor analysis validated the measurement model. The results revealed that
price offers exert the strongest infl uence on consumer buying behaviour b =0 . 5 4 ,p < 0.05,
followed by convenience b =0 . 2 0 ,p< 0.05, whereas social media influencers showed a com-
paratively weaker effect b =0 . 1 9 ,p < 0.05. The study offers practical insights for FMCG
marketers to design targeted digital strategies that balance affordability with convenience and
trust. Policy recommendations include strength ening digital literacy, promoting responsible
influencer advertising, and expanding digital infrastructure to support inclusive consumer
engagement. The findings contribute to both managerial practice and academic understanding
of digital consumer behaviour in emerging economies.

choices and behaviors. This multidimensional perspective remains highly relevant, continuing to
inform modern marketing research across both t raditional and digital marketplaces [3] high-

3 PROBLEM STATEMENT
The swift rise of digital marketing in India has reshaped the way consumers interact with
brands, with the fast-moving consumer goods (FMCG) sector —one of the country ’s largest
and most competitive industries —being at the forefront of this transformation. With more
than 800 million internet users and rising adoption of e-commerce, FMCG companies are
compelled to integrate digital tools such as influencer marketing, price-based promotions,
and convenience-driven platforms to attract and retain consumers 1,2. However, consumer
buying behaviour in this sector remains complex, shaped by frequent, low-involvement
purchases where decisions are influenced by peer reviews, promotions, and ease of access
rather than long-term loyalty 3,4. Although prior studies highlight the role of digital chan-
nels in shaping consumer perceptions 5,6, limited research systematically examines how
social media influencers, convenience, and price offers jointly impact consumer decision-
making in FMCGs. This gap underscores the need to analyse these determinants in an
Indian context, where shifting digital habits, diverse income groups, and competitive pres-
sures demand a deeper understanding of the factors driving the consumer behaviour.

4 METHODOLOGY
The present study employs a structured methodology to assess the impact of digital marketing on
consumer purchase behavior within the FMCG sector. The research design, sampling frame-
work, data collection methods, and analytical procedures are articulated to establish methodo-
logical rigor and reliability. A quantitative r esearch design was adopted, emphasizing the
collection of primary data through a structured questionnaire survey. A non-probability con-
venience sampling technique was utilized, whe reby respondents were se lected based on their
accessibility and readiness to provide responses—an approach frequently applied in exploratory
and behavioral research [20,21] This approach was considered feasible and efficient for reaching
digitally active consumers within a limited timeframe. The final sample consisted of 203 respon-
dents, deemed sufficient for exploratory analysis using structural equation modelling SEM. The
questionnaire gathered demographic details along with constructs related to influencer marketing,
convenience, price offers, and consumer buying behaviour in digital FMCG contexts.
For data analysis, a combination of statistical tools was applied to enhance robustness
and validity:
l Microsoft Excel for data cleaning and descriptive statistics.
l Jamovi software for reliability testing and inferential analysis.
l The study employed Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling (PLS-SEM)
for data analysis, utilizing the SeminR package in R. This approach was selected due to
its suitability for predictive modeling, handling complex relationships among latent
constructs, and its robustness with relatively small to medium sample sizes [22].
This multi-tool approach ensured both analytical rigour and flexibility, enabling accurate
inferences about the relationships between digital marketing variables and consumer pur-
chasing behaviour.

5 DATA ANALYSIS
The descriptive analysis was conducted across different income groups using variables such as
IN1 and IN2 influencers, CV1 and CV2 convenience, and PO1 price offer. Each income
category had a distinct number of participants, ranging from 14 to 78, with the largest repre-
sentation from the “less than ₹2.5 lakh per annum” group. This ensured adequate coverage of
lower-income respondents. Notably, there were no missing responses across groups, indicating
high data completeness and reliability. The mean values for most variables ranged between 2.0
and 3.0, suggesting that participants generally expressed neutral or moderately favourable
opinions. Higher-income groups displayed slightly higher mean scores compared to lower-
income groups, reflecting more positive perceptions. The median values closely aligned with
the means, confirming that responses were clustered around the centre.
The standard deviation values, ranging between 1.1 and 1.5, highlighted moderate
variability, indicating some differences in perceptions within income groups. Minimum and
maximum scores across all variables fell within the 1 –5 range, consistent with the Likert
scale used for data collection. To test distributional properties, the Shapiro –Wilk test was
applied. Since most p-values were below 0.05, the data significantly deviated from normality,
suggesting that non-parametric statistical tests would be more appropriate for further ana-
lysis. The reliability analysis Table 1 demonstrated strong internal consistency, with
Cronbach’s a and McDonald ’s w both reported at 0.973. These values confirm that the
measurement scale was highly reliable and that the items captured a cohesive construct. The
average standard deviation of 1.16 indicated a moderate level of agreement, with some
variability among responses. Such strong reliability scores imply that the scale can be con-
fidently applied for advanced statistical analysis.
The correlation heatmap Figure 2 depicted inherent interrelationships among the vari-
ables, confirming high correlations and suggesting potential overlap or multicollinearity
among certain items.The results of the confirmatory factor analysis CFA are presented in
Table 2. The factor loadings illustrate the extent to which indicators contribute to their
respective constructs. Among influencer indicators, IN3 exhibited the highest standardised

6 CONCLUSION
The findings of this study reaffirm the critical role of fast-moving consumer goods FMCGs in
daily life, as they provide essential, low-cost, high-volume products such as food, beverages,
toiletries, and household cleaning items. Success in this sector depends on the ability of com-
panies to balance efficiency, branding, innovation, and supply chain management while
aligning with sustainability and ethical practices. The analysis highlights that consumer beha-
viour in the FMCG sector is largely shaped by quick and frequent purchasing decisions, often

essential to sustaining competitiveness in a rapidly evolving digital marketplace.
7 LIMITATIONS OF THE STUDY
Although the present study contributes significantly to understanding the role of digital mar-
keting in shaping consumer buying behavior within the FMCG sector, certain limitations
should be acknowledged. The foremost concern lies in the reliance on a non-probability con-
venience sampling approach, which, while practical for exploratory research, may constrain
the representativeness of the sample and limit the generalizability of the findings to the wider
consumer population [20,21]. Second, the sample size of 203 respondents, while sufficient for
exploratory SEM analysis, may not fully capture the diversity of consumer behaviour across
India’s vast socio-economic and regional segments 2. Third, the study primarily focused on
three determinants social media influencers, convenience, and price offers while other poten-
tially significant factors such as brand trust, cultural influences, and consumer attitudes
towards sustainability were not included, leaving scope for future research 3.
8 PRACTICAL IMPLICATIONS & FUTURE POLICY RECOMMENDATIONS
8.1 Practical Implications
The findings of this study hold significant implications for FMCG companies and marketers.
First, since price offers emerged as the strongest determinant of consumer buying behaviour,
firms should design competitive and personalized discount strategies to retain cost-sensitive
consumers. Second, convenience was shown to play a moderate role, highlighting the impor-
tance of investing in user-friendly e-commerce platforms, mobile applications, and last-mile
delivery systems that enhance accessibility. Third, while influencers had a weaker effect com-
pared to price and convenience, their role in brand-building and awareness cannot be ignored;
therefore, FMCG firms should strategically collaborate with micro- and niche-influencers to
target specific consumer groups rather than relying solely on celebrity endorsements.
8.2 Future policy recommendations
From a policy perspective, regulators and industry bodies should encourage responsible digital
marketing practices by establishing clear guidelines for influencer advertising to ensure
authenticity and transparency in brand endorsements. Policymakers may also promote digital
literacy initiatives that empower consumers, particularly in rural and semi-urban areas, to
make informed online purchasing decisions. Furthermore, government support for digital
infrastructure development, such as wider internet penetration and secure digital payment
systems, will strengthen consumer confidence and expand access to FMCG products. Finally,
as sustainability becomes a critical concern, policies incentivizing eco-friendly packaging and
ethical marketing could align industry growth with long-term consumer welfare.
