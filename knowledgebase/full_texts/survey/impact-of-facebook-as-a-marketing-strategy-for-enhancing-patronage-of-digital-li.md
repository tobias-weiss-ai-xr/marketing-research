Abstract
This study investigated the impact of Facebook as a marketing strategy for enhancing patronage
of digital libraries in tertiary institutions in Imo State, Nigeria. Th e study was guided by five
research questions focusing on the extent to which tertiary institution libraries use Facebook for
marketing digital library resources and services, the influence of Facebook information
dissemination on users' awareness, the inf luence of promotional content and interactive
communication on patronage, and the influence of multimedia content on digital library patronage.
A descriptive survey research design was adopted. The population of the study comprised 9,201
final-year student s in eight tertiary institutions in Imo State, while a sample of 950 final -year
students was drawn from five selected universities using purposive sampling. Data were collected
using a researcher -developed instrument titled Facebook Marketing Strategy on D igital Library
Patronage Questionnaire (FMS -DLPQ). The instrument was validated by experts in Library and
Information Science, Educational Measurement and Evaluation, and Educational
Technology/ICT, while a Cronbach's alpha reliability coefficient of 0.70 was established. Mean
and standard deviation were used to answer the research questions. The findings revealed that
tertiary institution libraries used Facebook for marketing digital library resources and services to
a high extent, with a grand mean of 3.04. Facebook information dissemination was found to have
a high influence on users' awareness of digital library resources (grand mean = 3.14). Facebook
promotional content also had a high perceived influence on digital library patronage (grand mean
= 3.01). Interactive communication through Facebook had a high perceived influence on patronage

Introduction
The contemporary educational environment is
increasingly influenced by rapid developments in
information and communication technology
(ICT), the Internet and digital information
systems. These developments have transformed
the way information is created, organized,
accessed, disseminated and utilized. Tertiary
institutions are particularly affected by these
changes because teaching, learning and research
depend heavily on timely access to releva nt and
reliable information. Consequently, academic
libraries have moved beyond their traditional
roles of collecting, organizing and preserving
print materials to providing electronic and digital
information resources and services.
The emergence of digita l libraries represents one
of the major transformations in academic
librarianship. A digital library provides users with
organized access to information resources in
electronic formats through computer networks
and Internet-enabled technologies. Digital library
collections may include electronic books,
electronic journals, online databases, institutional
repositories, electronic theses and dissertations,
conference proceedings, digitized documents and
other scholarly materials. The availability of these
resources enables students, lecturers and
researchers to obtain relevant information without
necessarily visiting the physical library. Digital
libraries therefore provide opportunities for
convenient, flexible and remote access to
scholarly information. Digital library resources do
not automatically translate into effective
utilization. A digital library may possess
extensive electronic resources and sophisticated
technological infrastructure, yet experience low
patronage when users are unaware of the
resources, do not know how to access them or do
not perceive them as relevant to their academic
needs. This makes awareness creation,
communication, promotion and marketing
important components of contemporary library
services.
Marketing in the library context refers to a
planned process through which library resources
and services are communicated to existing and
potential users in order to create awareness,
demonstrate value and encourage utilization.
Unlike commercial marketin g, the primary
objective of library marketing is not profit
maximization but improved awareness,
accessibility, user satisfaction and utilization of
information resources and services. Effective
marketing enables libraries to communicate the
relevance of t heir resources to users and to
maintain continuous relationships with their
academic communities.  Traditionally, academic
libraries have employed methods such as library

orientation programmes, posters, flyers, notice boards, newsletters, brochures, exhib itions, seminars
and face-to-face communication to promote their services. Although these methods remain useful, the
changing communication habits of students and academic staff have created the need for libraries to
adopt more contemporary and interactive  marketing approaches. Social media platforms have
consequently become increasingly relevant to library marketing.
Social media are Internet -based platforms that enable individuals and organizations to create, share,
communicate and interact with information within online communities. Social media have transformed
organizational communication from a largely one-way process into a more interactive and participatory
process. Libraries can use social media to announce new resources, promote services, provide research
assistance, answer questions, share educational materials, provide links to electronic resources and
obtain feedback from users. Among the social -media platforms that have been used for organizational
communication and marketing is Facebook.
Facebook provides organizations with opportunities to establish pages and communities, publish text
and multimedia content, share links, communicate directly with users and monitor engagement. For
academic libraries, these functions create opportunities to promote digital library resources and services
to students, lecturers and researchers. Facebook can serve as a marketing channel through which
libraries disseminate information about electronic books, electronic journals, databases, inst itutional
repositories, research -support services and other digital resources. Librarians can also use Facebook
posts to provide tutorials on accessing databases, demonstrate search strategies, announce new
electronic resources, advertise library programmes and respond to users' questions. Such activities may
increase users' awareness and potentially encourage them to access and utilize digital library resources.
The relevance of social media to academic libraries has been established in several studies. Uc he and
Udo-Anyanwu (2018), for example, reported that librarians in tertiary institutions in Abia and Imo
States were aware of and utilized social media for library-related activities. Their findings demonstrate
that social media had become relevant to lib rary service delivery in the study area. Nevertheless,
challenges such as inadequate Internet connectivity and unreliable electricity supply were identified as
factors affecting effective social -media utilization. Similarly, Odocha et al. (2024) examined s ocial
networking as a marketing strategy for effective service delivery in academic libraries in Imo State.
Their work demonstrates that social networking has potential as an avenue for marketing library
services. However, the existence of social-networking activities does not necessarily establish that they
result in increased patronage of specific digital library resources. This distinction is important because
social-media presence, user engagement and actual utilization of digital library resources are different
dimensions of library performance.
Facebook marketing can potentially influence digital library patronage through several mechanisms.
First, it can increase awareness by informing users about available electronic resources. Second, it can
facilitate access by providing direct links to databases, repositories and electronic collections. Third, it
can promote engagement through comments, reactions, messages and discussions. Fourth, multimedia
content such as videos and demonstrations can improve use rs' understanding of how to use digital
library services. Fifth, timely responses from librarians can reduce difficulties encountered by users
when attempting to access electronic resources. The effectiveness of Facebook marketing, however,
may depend on t he quality and frequency of information disseminated. A Facebook page that merely
posts occasional administrative announcements may have limited influence on digital library patronage.
Conversely, a library that consistently provides relevant, attractive a nd user -oriented content may
generate greater engagement. The frequency of posts, quality of promotional messages, relevance of

Fong et al. (2019) investigated the use of Facebook among seven university libraries in Hong Kong.
The researchers adopted a mixed -method approach involving online observation and web -content
analysis of the libraries' Fa cebook pages. The study found that most of the university libraries had
adopted Facebook as a marketing and communication tool. However, the level of user engagement was
generally low. Communication -oriented posts and video posts attracted greater attentio n from library
patrons than other forms of content. The study demonstrates that merely creating a Facebook page may
not automatically increase library patronage; rather, the quality, relevance and presentation of Facebook
content are important in attractin g and retaining users.   Fong et al. (2020) conducted a comparative
study examining social media as a marketing platform in academic libraries. The study focused on the
Facebook page of the University of Hong Kong Libraries and compared students' and faculty members'
perceptions and engagement. The findings showed that students were more engaged with the library's
Facebook marketing activities than faculty members. The researchers also found that users' information
needs, the nature of social -media content and the level of interaction influenced users' acceptance and
participation. However, some Facebook marketing activities received inadequate attention and reactions
from users. The findings suggest that Facebook can support library marketing and patronage when
content is aligned with users' needs and when libraries encourage interaction with their target audience.
Fasae (2021) examined strategies for using social media in academic library services in Ghana. The
study adopted a quantitative survey design and  collected data from 110 library staff working in public
and private universities. The findings revealed that academic libraries needed well -formulated social-
media strategies to identify appropriate platforms, reach targeted audiences and increase user
engagement. However, the study also found that academic libraries often neglected systematic social -
media planning, user engagement strategies and regular content updates. The study concluded that
libraries require appropriate social -media policies, dedicate d personnel and deliberate engagement
strategies to maximize the benefits of social media in library -service delivery.  Mensah and Onyancha
(2022) investigated patrons' awareness of and engagement with social media in academic libraries in
Ghana. The resea rchers used a cross -sectional survey involving 417 students and library staff from
public, private and technical universities. The findings showed that academic libraries had adopted
several social-media applications, with Facebook and instant messaging em erging as the most popular
and frequently used platforms for accessing library services and resources and for communicating with
libraries. The study provides strong empirical support for the argument that Facebook can facilitate
awareness, communication a nd access to library resources among tertiary -institution users. Emiri and
Lulu-Pokubo (2024) investigated the application of social media in marketing library products and
services in university libraries in Delta and Edo States, Nigeria. The study adopted a survey design and
used a census sample of 77 librarians from eight university libraries. Findings revealed that Facebook,
WhatsApp and other social -media platforms were available for marketing library services, although
their actual utilization for marketing was generally inefficient. Facebook was among the platforms used
by the libraries, and social media was found to facilitate interaction, information sharing and users'
involvement in library decision-making processes.
The Technology Acceptance Model developed by Davis (1989) provides an important theoretical
explanation for this relationship. The model proposes that perceived usefulness and perceived ease of
use influence individuals' acceptance and use of technology. Applied to digital libraries, users are more
likely to utilize resources promoted through Facebook when they perceive the resources as useful and

easy to access. Thus, Facebook marketing may increase patronage when it communicates the usefulness
of digital resources and makes access easier for users.The Uses and Gratifications perspective also
provides an explanation for the use of Facebook by library patrons. The theory views media users as
active participants who select media and content based on their information and communication needs.
Students and researchers may therefore interact with a library's Facebook page because they need

The Nigerian tertiary education environment makes the investigation of Facebook marketing
particularly relevant. Nigeri an universities, polytechnics and colleges of education have increasingly
adopted electronic information resources to support teaching, learning and research. However,
challenges relating to awareness, digital literacy, Internet access, electricity supply,  ICT competence
and inadequate promotion may limit effective utilization of these resources. Consequently, libraries
need marketing approaches capable of reaching users within the digital environments in which they
increasingly communicate and seek information. As digital information resources become increasingly
important, there is a need for libraries to employ effective methods of promoting these resources.
Facebook may provide a relatively accessible platform through which academic libraries can reach a
large number of users, communicate continuously with them and encourage the utilization of digital
library resources.
Despite the growing use of social media in academic libraries, there is still a need to establish whether
Facebook marketing actually tra nslates into increased digital library patronage. A library may have a
Facebook page with many followers, likes or comments without necessarily experiencing
corresponding increases in database usage, e-book access, electronic-journal utilization or institutional
repository visits. Therefore, measuring actual patronage rather than simply measuring social -media
presence is important.
Furthermore, existing studies in Nigeria have generally examined social media awareness, adoption,
utilization or marketing of library services. Fewer studies have specifically investigated the impact of
Facebook as a marketing strategy on patronage of digital libraries, particularly among users of tertiary
institutions in Imo State. This creates an important empirical gap that the present study seeks to address.
The study therefore focuses on the impact of Facebook as a marketing strategy for enhancing patronage
of digital libraries in tertiary institutions in Imo State. Specifically, it seeks to examine whether
Facebook informati on dissemination, promotional content, interactive communication, multimedia
content, links to digital resources, posting frequency and librarians' responsiveness influence users'
awareness, access and utilization of digital library resources.

Statement of the Problem
Digital libraries have become important components of tertiary education because they provide
students, lecturers and researchers with access to electronic information resources required for teaching,
learning and research. Tertiary institutions in Imo State have made investments in electronic resources
and digital library services. Ideally, these resources should be widely known, easily accessible and
frequently utilized by members of the academic community. However, the availability of digital library
resources does not necessarily guarantee their patronage. Some students and academic staff may be
unaware of the electronic resources provided by their institutions. Others may know that such resources
exist but may not know how to access or eff ectively use them. Consequently, valuable electronic

resources may remain underutilized despite the financial and technological investments made by tertiary
institutions.
The situation raises concerns about how academic libraries can effectively communicate the availability
and usefulness of digital resources to their users. Traditional methods of library promotion may not be
sufficient to reach users who increasingly obtain information and communicate through digital
platforms. Social media, particularly F acebook, provides an opportunity for libraries to communicate
directly with users and promote their services in an interactive environment.
Although academic libraries have increasingly adopted social media, the actual effectiveness of
Facebook as a market ing strategy remains unclear. The presence of a library Facebook page does not
necessarily indicate that users are accessing digital library resources because of Facebook promotion.
Similarly, the number of followers, reactions, comments and shares may not necessarily represent actual
patronage.
There is therefore a problem of insufficient empirical evidence concerning the extent to which Facebook
marketing activities influence actual patronage of digital libraries in tertiary institutions in Imo State. It
is not sufficiently established whether information dissemination, promotional content, interactive
communication, multimedia presentations, direct resource links and librarians' responsiveness through
Facebook lead to increased awareness, access and utili zation of digital library resources.The problem
is further complicated by factors such as poor Internet connectivity, inadequate electricity supply, digital
literacy challenges, users' attitudes, inadequate ICT skills and possible institutional limitations . These
factors may reduce the effectiveness of Facebook as a marketing strategy even where librarians are
actively using the platform.
If this situation continues, tertiary institutions may continue to invest considerable resources in digital
library coll ections without achieving corresponding levels of utilization. Underutilization of digital
resources may reduce the contribution of academic libraries to teaching, learning and research. The
problem of this study, therefore, is the uncertain extent to which Facebook, when deliberately employed
as a marketing strategy, influences patronage of digital library resources and services in tertiary
institutions in Imo State. The study seeks to provide empirical evidence on this relationship and
determine which dim ensions of Facebook marketing are most effective in enhancing digital library
patronage.

Methodology
The study adopted a descriptive survey research design . The Facebook marketing strategy were
measured in terms of information dissemination, promotional content, interactive communication,
multimedia content, Digital library patronage was measured in terms of users' awareness, access,
frequency of use and uti lization of electronic books, electronic journals, electronic databases,
institutional repositories and other digital library services. The study was carried out in Imo State,
Nigeria. Imo State is one of the states in the South -East geopolitical zone of N igeria. The state has a
strong concentration of tertiary institutions, including universities, polytechnics and colleges of
education. The state is suitable for the study because tertiary institutions in the area provide academic
library services and increasingly make use of electronic information resources. These resources include
electronic books, electronic journals, online databases, institutional repositories, electronic theses and
dissertations and other Internet -based scholarly materials. The study f ocused on tertiary institutions in
Imo State that have functional academic libraries, digital library facilities and identifiable social-media
platforms, particularly Facebook. The geographical location is also appropriate because the increasing
adoption of ICT and social-media platforms among students, academic staff and librarians provides an
appropriate environment for investigating Facebook marketing and digital library patronage. The
population of the study comprised all the nine thousand two hundred and one (9,201) final year students
in the eight (8) tertiary institutions in Imo State. Out of these tertiary institutions, two (2) are public
universities, three (3) are state universities, two (2) polytechnic and one (1) college of Education A
sample of 950 final year students drawn from five (5) universities in the study area. This is made up of
Federal University of Technology (FUTO = 350), Alvan Ikoku Federal University of Education
(AIFUE=280), Imo State University (IMSU= 350), Kingsely Ozumbambadiwe University
(KOMU=47) and University of Agriculture and Environmental sciences (UAES=38). Purposive
sampling technique was used to select all the five (5) universities in Imo State because they have the
same capacity funding from tetfund and enough ICT faci lities that can accommodate the students. The
instrument for data collection was a researchers developed questionnaire titled: “Facebook Marketing
Strategy on Digital Library Patronage Questionnaire (FMS-DLPQ).” The questionnaire was developed
from the obj ectives of the study. The questionnaire comprise two sections designed to collect
information about respondents' demographic characteristics on Facebook marketing practices on digital
library patronage.  This Section A contains information such as: institu tion; category of institution;
respondent status; age range; frequency of Internet use; and experience with digital library services.

The finding indicates that Facebook has become an important marketing channel through which
academic libraries communicate their resources and services to users. This finding agrees with Fong et
al. (2019), who found that university libraries adopted Facebook as a marketing and communication
tool, although the level of engagement depended on the nature and quality of the content. It also agrees

with Fasae (2021), who emphasized that academic libraries require deliberate social-media strategies to
reach target audiences and improve engagement. Similarly, Emiri and Lulu -Pokubo (2024) found that
Facebook and other social-media platforms were available for marketing library products and services
in Nigerian university libraries. The relatively lower score for librarians' responsiveness suggests that
the libraries are stronger in broadcasting information than in two -way communication. This is an
important area for improvement because Facebook provides opportunities for users to ask questions,
obtain assistance and interact directly with librarians.
This finding suggested that Facebook serves as an effective information-dissemination channel through
which students become aware of e-books, electronic journals, databases and other electronic resources.
It supports the finding of Mensah and Onyancha (2022) that Facebook was among the most popular
social-media platforms used for accessing library services and communicating with academic libraries.
The finding is also consistent with Fong et al. (2020), who found that the nature of social-media content
and its alignment with users' information needs influenced users' participation in academic library
social-media activities. The finding is important because awareness is an essential first step toward
digital-library patronage. Students cannot utilize electronic resources that they do not know exist.
Facebook therefore provides a means through which libr aries can continuously remind users about
available resources and services.
The finding indicated that carefully designed promotional messages can stimulate students' interest in
digital-library resources and encourage them to explore and use such resource s. This agrees with Fong
et al. (2019), who found that the type and presentation of Facebook content influenced users' attention
and engagement. It also supports Fong et al. (2020), whose findings showed that social -media content
aligned with users' information needs could encourage participation. The finding further supports Fasae
(2021), who argued that academic libraries need systematic social -media strategies, regular content
updates and deliberate user-engagement approaches to maximize the benefits of social media.
The finding suggests that interactive communication can assist users in understanding how to access
and utilize digital library resources. However, the relatively lower mean for asking librarians questions
and receiving responses indicates th at the interactive component of Facebook marketing could be
strengthened. The finding is consistent with Mensah and Onyancha (2022), who reported that social
media supported communication and engagement between academic libraries and their patrons. It also
agrees with Fong et al. (2020), who found that interaction was one of the factors influencing users'
acceptance and participation in academic-library social-media activities.
The finding suggests that visual and multimedia materials can make library marke ting messages more
attractive, understandable and engaging. Videos can demonstrate how to search databases, access e -
books and use electronic journals, while images can attract attention to new resources and library
services. This finding strongly supports Fong et al. (2019), who found that video posts attracted greater
attention from library users. It also supports the broader conclusion of Fong et al. (2020) that the nature
of social -media content influences user engagement. The fact that multimedia conte nt recorded the
highest grand mean suggests that tertiary institution libraries may obtain greater engagement when
Facebook marketing goes beyond plain text and incorporates images, instructional videos,
demonstrations and other visual materials.

Recommendations
Based on the findings and conclusion of the study, the following recommendations are made:
1. Tertiary institution libraries should develop formal Facebook marketing strategies. Libraries
should have clearly defined objectives, target audiences, content plans and schedules for
promoting digital library resources through Facebook.
2. Libraries should regularly disseminate information about digital  resources. Information
concerning new e -books, electronic journals, databases, institutional repositories and other
electronic resources should be posted regularly to increase users' awareness.
3. Promotional content should be user-oriented. Librarians should design promotional messages
around students' teaching, learning and research needs rather than merely announcing the
existence of library resources.
4. Libraries should strengthen interactive communication. Librarians should respond promptly to
questions, comments and messages from students through Facebook. A designated librarian or
