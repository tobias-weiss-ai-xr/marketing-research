The fruit-based beverage (FBB) segment is a fast -growing sector of the non -
alcoholic beverage market, as driven by the soaring consumer demand in the
market. Online promotional cues are very important in assessing the
authenticity, nutritional value and brand credibility of the product in digitally
mediated retail settings, which makes digital marketing a particularly
effective influence in this category. Nevertheless, the current literature on
digital promotional practices is not unified, but rather fragme nted and often
generalized across the food and FMCG industries, without a focused and
theory-integrated understanding of consumer buying behaviour in the
specific context of fruit -based beverages. To fill this gap, the current study is
a systematic literat ure review, with evidence synthesized based on the
PRISMA 2020 guidelines, including 47 peer -reviewed studies. The results
indicate that the digital promotional practices, such as social media
marketing, influencer marketing, content marketing, and electro nic word-of-
mouth, are interactive stimuli of retail service, which influence consumer
decision making through thinking and feeling processes. Attitudes, brand
trust, perceived usefulness, and emotional engagement are the key mediating
constructs, with brand trust appearing to be especially crucial in the context
of health-sensitive beverages. Based on these insights, the study constructs a
sector-specific conceptual framework built around the Stimulus -Organism-
Response (SOR) model, which is supported by th e Theory of Planned
Behaviour (TPB), Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) and Hierarchy of
Effects Model (HEM). The research will make its contribution to the multi -
theoretical analysis of digital consumer behaviour, which is context -specific,
and present practical implications to marketers and policymakers.

1. Introduction
Fruit-based beverages (FBBs) are one of the non -alcoholic drink industry's fastest -growing areas in
the global market due to customers' growing demands for functional, convenient, and healthful
beverages. Consumer purchasing behaviour in premium juice categories is increasingly influenced by
convenience, health orientation, and percei ved product quality attributes [23]. The market for fruit
juices in the world is estimated to be USD 89.56 billion in year 2025, and is expected to reach USD
141.94 billion in 2032, as the market has been experiencing a gradual growth with the shift of
consumer preference to the nutritional ly enriched and natural fruit juice [48]. This change in soft

Digital retail ecosystems have inverted the consumer behavior and thinking of buying fruit-based
drinks in this evolving market environment. The digital transformation of the marketing mix has
significantly altered how food and beverage brands communicate value and interact with consumers
in retail environments [42].  Some of the critical interaction points available for consumers to acquire
information about a product, communicate with brands and make a purchasing decision are the
digital platforms, which include e -commerce environments, mobile applications, and soc ial media

exposure to digital promotional stimuli in food and beverage retailing contexts [40]. According to
recent assessment data, social media platforms have emerged as the primary avenues for food and
beverage advertising exposure among consumer groups with digital connect ions. [29].  The factors
having the strongest impact on consumer perception, trust and behavior are the online promotion
strategies including influencer marketing, branded contents and electronic word-of-mouth  [18]. The

that consumers make. The effectiveness of online marketing can be significant in the fruit based

more reliant on external informational cues and credibility signals to purchase something, as they are
often uncertain about the amount of sugar in it, processing methods, and health -related claims ([6 ,

Numerous studies have examined consumer behaviour in relation to food and beverages and digital
marketing; however, the literature remains disjointed, and no universal and industry -specific
synthesis exists. The amount of studies that take into account the  application of separate

Additionally, the vast majority of systematic reviews performed so far are rather general in their

based beverage retailing, where the role of health perceptions and credibility issues play the central
role [36]. In overcoming those limitations, the present paper is designed to include those empirical
researches that have been conducted on the effects of digital promotion activities and consumers'
decisions on the retailing of fruit based beverages by performing a systematic literature review. It
uses a PRISMA 2020-based methodology [32] and integrates information from the Theory of Planned

2. Theoretical Background
To provide insight into the influence that digital promotional practice has on consumer buying
behaviour, a robust theoretical basis needs to be established on the basis of how consumers react to
marketing stimuli in digitally mediated retail settings. Pro motional content in online and Omni
channel settings serves as a service experience of the brand, forming brand credibility, perceived risk,
and purchase intention [25]. Such an attitude is especially significant for health related categories like
drinks, as people's purchase intention towards health related products is also driven by trust,
authenticity and social proof [28]. Responses from consumers is a process that is cognitive, affective,
social and technological, however most previous studies have drawn on only one theoretical
perspective and consequently not captured a holistic understanding of the same. To address this
research gap, this paper aims to present a research framework that integrates various theories such
as the S-O-R, Planned Behavior theory, Technology Acceptance Model and Hierarchy of Effects model.

The Stimulus -Organism-Response (SOR) model is considered the fundamental theory used in this
study, and digital promotional actions are taken to be the stimuli that impact the inner psychological
process and behavior of the organism [30, 45]. Digital promotional campaigns such as social media
ads, influencers and digital platform communication have an impact on cognitive evalu ations and
emotional responses [16].  The stimuli affect trust, perceived value, and emotional involvement
which play vital roles in decreasing uncertainty in the category of health -oriented products [25].
These processes also influence emotional and appraisal reac tions to online advertisements [45].
These intentional behaviours are transformed into attitudes, subjective norms, and perceived
behavioural control via the Theory of Planned Behaviour [1]. Online marketing content has the power
to alter perceptions of both product quality and brand trustworthiness. [46]. Social media facilitates
the social norm, peer pressure and recommendations from social media influencers [26]. All these
factors influence intention of buying for the health goods segment highly [2] Consumers' reaction is
reinforced by the availability of trust on cyberspace.
The Technology Acceptance Model emphasises the role of platform -related features in shaping

growing relevance of online brand community engagement in shaping cognitive, emotional, and
behavioural consumer responses in digital retail settings [15]. An interactive media environment, on

3. Conceptual Framework of Digital Promotional as a Retail Consumer Experience
To understand how digital promotional practices influence consumer buying decisions in fruit-based
beverage retailing, this study develops a sector -specific conceptual framework that conceptualizes
digital promotions as digitally mediated retail service interactions, where customer views,
engagement, and decision -making processes are influenced by online touchpoints [25,5].The
framework is based on the Stimulus -Organism-Response (SOR) paradigm, which describes how
internal psychological states are triggered by external stimuli and then lead to behavioural reactions.
[45, 30] In this case, the organism represents the consumer's cognitive and affective processes, the
stimuli represent digital promotional methods, and the response represents the consumer's
purchase decisions. The Theory of Planned Behaviour, Technology Acceptance Model, and Hierarchy
of Effects Model—which explain how attitudes, subjective norms, perceived behavioral control, and
technological perception can affect consumer response in the digital marketing environment —are
then added t o the framework to enhance it. [1 , 13, 27]. Moreover, recently it has also confirmed
that these models are useful in order to explain consumer response with mediated digital
environments and the phased response process. [3, 10]. Unlike the previous studies which are built
on a general FMCG context, the framework developed here is focused on fruit based beverages,
where perceived health-related and trustworthiness, product authenticity and information validity is
critically important for consumer response [6 , 21] By adding features of the fruit based beverages
sector, such as high emphasis on health information, the need for trust -based communication, and
the importance of social validation on reducing the risk and increasing the purchase intention [26].

In this context, digital promotional actions served as external stimuli through which consumers
encountered fruit based beverages brands in digital retail environment. Social media marketing
allows the continuity of interaction through its interactivity [5]. These digitally enabled promotional
interactions reflect the broader transformation of retail marketing systems toward technology-driven
consumer engagement models [42].  Brand generated contents and interactive advertising forms
enable immersion in the retailing environment [43]. Influencer marketing can affect brand
assessment through its credibility and authenticity [14] and perceived quality and healthy benefits of
product through expertness and social identification [26]. Reach, credibility, and interaction drive the
effect of influencer marketing [7]. Electronic word of mouth lessens information symmetry and
increases trust through social verification  [18]. Content strategies like storytelling increase product
value and are important in health related categories. Mobile and platform based advertisements can
further influence the effect by increasing product access and coverage. Therefore, Digital
promotional actions function as interactive retail stimuli that deliver not only product information,
but also product value and consumption experience.
Such external stimuli affect internal cognitive and affective processes which mediate consumer
responses. The perceived relevancy and credibility of promotional messages influence consumer
attitudes [46]. The brand trust appears as a key mediating variable that lowers perceived risk in the
health-related category of products [28]. Evaluative consistency is also reinforced by mechanisms of

4. Methodology
The current paper falls under the systematic literature review (SLR) methodology to synthesize
existing knowledge about digital marketing strategies and consumer buying behaviour in the fruit
beverage segment. The SLR approach makes sure to have transparency, reproducibility, and
methodological rigour in the identification, evaluation, and integrat ion of previous research [32]. It
is also most useful in summarizing disjointed streams of research and identifying theoretical patterns
and research gaps in the field of retailing and consumer behaviour [35]. It is conducted according to
the PRISMA 2020 guidelines as they provide a structured way to identify and screen studies and
conduct an eligibility and inclusion process. A structured search strategy and selection process will
enhance the credibility and replicability of the review. Furthermore, a pre-determined coding scheme
and thematic synthesis was conducted in order to extract and analyze data, and to guarantee the
reliability of inter-study comparisons.
A literature search was conducted for all relevant peer -reviewed papers using major electronic
literature databases such as Scopus, Web of Science, ScienceDirect, Emerald Insight, SpringerLink and
Google Scholar. A keyword search was employed combining fac tors of digital marketing behavior,
outcomes of consumer behavior, and product category of beverage and combined together using
Boolean operators so that to capture as many relevancy items as possible. Only studies written in
English that have been publish ed between 2015 and 2025 were considered to ensure consistency
and to reflect modern trends in online marketing, especially the explosive growth of online platforms,
such as food delivery applications, that further increase the consumer exposure to online

and forward citation tracking that identified another 15 relevant studies to give a final sample of 47
peer-reviewed articles included in the review. Additional theoretical and contextual references were
incorporated to support framework development and conceptual interpretation beyond the final
PRISMA sample. The synthesis was aimed at studying convergence, divergence, and integration
patterns across studies. Since the review was pe rformed based solely on published sources, the
review met the recognized criteria of academic integrity and research ethics.

5. Result and Synthesis
After the screening based on PRISMA 2020, 47 peer -reviewed studies are involved in the final
synthesis [32]. The trends of publishing indicate that there is a significant increase in scholarly interest
since 2018 and is connected to the rapid rise of the scholarly interest in influencer marketing, social
media platforms, and data-driven digital promotion in the  retail setting. Over 2/3 of the research is
published since 2019, which indicates the topicality of digital mediated promotions in consumer
services. Quantitative designs, specifically cross-sectional surveys and structural equation modelling,
are dominant in the literature, with a relative lack of experimental and mixed-method designs. There
is a relative lack of qualitative research, which indicates insufficient contextual and experiential
information. Most of the research is based in Europe, North Ameri ca, and East Asia, with little
representation of emerging markets, which implies that existing results are context -dependent and
may not comprehensively represent the varied retail settings. The use of multi -theoretical
approaches to comprehend consumer be haviour in digital mediated retail environments is justified
by the fact that the Stimulus -Organism-Response model, the Theory of Planned Behaviour, and the
Technology Acceptance Model are frequently and infrequently combined.
Thematic synthesis shows that digital promotional practices are the key retail stimuli that influence
consumer-brand relationships of fruit -based beverage brands in digitally mediated space. The
marketing approach that gains the most popularity is social m edia marketing, where the usage of
Instagram and YouTube and other social sites is the most powerful driver of involvement by visual
rich, short, and interactive content [47]. Paid advertising, branded content, and interactive posts are
always effective to boost brand awareness, consumer engagement, and positive attitudinal reactions
[5]. Influencer marketing is a form of persuasion, and perceived authenticity, credibility, and
alignment with brand values are the main factors that influence its effectiveness. [14].Experimental
evidence further suggests that influencer -endorsed food promotions can significantly shape
consumer food preferences and consumption behaviour through p arasocial influence mechanisms
[12].  Relational intermediaries also play the role of influencers, as perceived risk is reduced and trust
is enhanced with the help of social validation and p arasocial interaction [26]. Promotional
effectiveness is also enhanced by electronic word -of-mouth as it minimizes information asymmetry
and increases normative influence  [18]. The perceived value and credibility can be improved with
the help of the content strategy, including storytelling and informational messaging, especially in the
context of health -related drinks where consumers are more inclined to analyze the nutritional
statements [6]. Mobile and platform -based advertisement also augments the frequency and
accessibility of exposure further propagating the overall effect of digital promotion stimulus [43]. The
expansion of food delivery applications has further increased consumer exposure to digitally
mediated promotional content within food and beverage retail environments [40].  In addition, digital
marketing strategies enable the value co -creation of retail service environments, where interactive
components such as likes, comments, shares, and user -generated content allow customers to
participate in the development of brand na rratives. Through interactive social media experiences,
consumer participation in online brand communities also improves cognitive, emotional, and
behavioural engagement with brands [15]. According to service -dominant logic, this participative

Digital promotional stimuli have a mediating effect on consumer responses via a cognitive and
affective response. The relevance, credibility, and informativeness of digital content determine
consumer attitudes, especially when it comes to beverages with health orientation, where consumers
actively evaluate product claims [46]. Social media advertising also influences cognitive evaluations
and attitudinal responses by increasing consumers’ attention, message processing, and engagement
with promotional content [16].  The need to decrease the perceived risk in digitally mediated settings
leads to the development of brand trust as one of the prima ry mediating constructs [28]. Recent
consumer psychology research also highlights that nutritional awareness and habitual consumption
patterns significantly shape functional beverage purchasing behaviour in digital consumption
environments [3]. Credibility of influencers, peer validation, and socially endorsed cues of
consumption further enhance trust [26]. Perceived usefulness drives consumer engagement with
digital platforms [13], with recent studies confirming its role in enhancing interaction w ith online
promotional content [22 , 37].  The effectiveness of the promotional stimuli is further enhanced by
affective processes like emotional involvement and consumer -brand attachment, especially by
interactive content and community-based experiences [24]. All the psychological processes establish
how the electronic promotional signals are internalised and converted into significant as sessments
in shopping settings.
Such mental and emotional processes are then converted into behavioural results which are evident
in the digital retail environment. According to the Hierarchy of Effects Model, consumer responses
progress through cognitive, affective, a nd conative stages [27, 10].  On a cognitive level, brand
awareness and product knowledge are boosted due to repeated exposure to digital promotions
through higher information availability and salience [10]. Credible and interesting promotional
content helps to build positive brand attitudes, trust, and emotional attachment at the affective level
[25]. The results of these responses contribute to conative outcomes, such as a purchase intention
and actual buying behaviour, which is influenced by both attitudinal judgments and social norms in
digital settings [3]. Digital engagement also leads to long -term behavioural effects like customer
loyalty, brand advocacy, and repeat purchase behaviour, particularly in brand communities on social
media [36]. Broader promotional ecosystems, including sponsorship-based marketing activities, also
contribute to sustained brand exposure and long -term consumer enga gement among younger
audiences [4]. TABLE 1 summarizes the synthesized results with respect to theoretical foundations,

predominance of cross -sectional research designs limits causal interpretation within the existing
literature [41]. Emerging technologies such as AI -driven personalization, immersive virtual
environments, and advanced digital engagement systems represent promising directions for future
research in digitally mediated consumer behaviour [11 , 44].
7. Conclusions
The study is proposed to be a systematic literature review that combines the empirical evidence of
the consumer purchasing behavior and digital promotional practices in the fruit -based beverage
(FBB) retail sector following the PRISMA 2020 guidelines. 47 p eer-reviewed articles provide a
synthesis of the current literature that is both coherent and sector specific, addressing the disjointed
nature of the current literature. The findings reveal that digital promotion techniques, such as social
media marketing , influencer marketing, content marketing and emotional engagement, have a
significant influence on consumers' behaviour for fruit drinks. In this category, where the consumer
is highly sensitive to nutrition claims, authenticity and health concerns are al so important factors
influencing purchase intention and buying behaviour in addition to brand trust and informational
credibility. The study is considered to contribute to the literature, as it builds a multi -theoretical
conceptual model that combines two of the most widely used models in consumer studies: The
Stimulus-Organism-Response model and the Theory of Planned Behaviour; and in technology studies:
The Technology Acceptance Model and the Hierarchy of Effects Model, to explain consumer
responses in digital mediated fruit -based beverage retailing environments. The findings underscore
the need for transparent and credible digital promotional practices and approaches that are
