Introduction: This study examines the psychological foundations of virtual
influencer research within the broader context of artificial intelligence (AI) and data-
driven personalization, advertising, marketing communication, and human–AI
interaction. As AI-generated social actors become increasingly embedded in
digitally mediated environments, research on virtual influencers has expanded
rapidly across psychology, communication, marketing, and media studies. However,
the field remains conceptually fragmented, limiting understanding of its dominant
psychological themes, intellectual structure, and future research directions.
Methods: This study employed the SPAR-4-SLR protocol to conduct a
systematic literature review of virtual influencer research. Articles were
retrieved from four major databases and screened using predefined inclusion
and exclusion criteria, resulting in a final sample of 116 peer-reviewed studies
published between 2015 and 2025. Bibliometric techniques, including
performance analysis and science mapping using VOSviewer, were applied to
examine publication trends, collaboration patterns, influential contributors,
and thematic structures within the field.
Results: The findings indicate that virtual influencer research has expanded
rapidly since 2021 and has evolved into an increasingly interdisciplinary field.
China emerged as the leading contributor in terms of publication output, while
analyses of publications, citations, and co-authorships revealed a fragmented
intellectual structure, with influence distributed across multiple research teams.
Keyword co-occurrence analysis identified seven thematic clusters, highlighting
authenticity, trust, anthropomorphism, consumer engagement, artificial
intelligence, and digital marketing as the dominant research themes.

1 Introduction
The rapid advancement of artificial intelligence (AI), including
generative AI technologies, computer-generated imagery (CGI),
and data-driven personalization, has transformed the ways
individuals interact with digital environments and socially
mediated content. Within this evolving landscape, virtual
influencers (VIs), digitally created personas with curated
identities and social media presences, have emerged as
increasingly visible actors in online communication ecosystems
(Barari et al., 2025). Unlike traditional human influencers,
virtual influencers are algorithmically designed, technologically
mediated entities that can simulate social interaction, emotional
expression, and interpersonal engagement across digital
platforms. Their growing popularity raises important
psychological questions concerning how individuals perceive,
emotionally process, and socially respond to non-human yet
human-like agents in mediated environments (Ju et al., 2024;
Khalfallah and Keller, 2026).
Recent advances in computer-generated imagery (CGI),
generative AI, and machine learning have accelerated the
development of increasingly realistic virtual influencers capable
of sustained interaction with online audiences. These
technologies challenge traditional assumptions that social
influence and relational engagement require biological human
presence, as consumers increasingly form connections with
algorithmically generated identities rather than physically
existing individuals (Gambetti and Kozinets, 2024). Research
grounded in human–computer interaction and media
psychology suggests that individuals frequently apply social rules
and expectations to artificial agents when sufficient social cues
are present, particularly in contexts involving
anthropomorphism, emotional expressiveness, and perceived
social presence (Chatzigeorgiou et al., 2025; Momeni, 2025).
As virtual influencers become more sophisticated and socially
embedded, scholars across psychology, communication, and
digital media studies have begun examining the cognitive and
emotional mechanisms underlying AI-mediated influence.
Existing research demonstrates that virtual influencers can shape
consumer attitudes, trust formation, emotional attachment, and
behavioral intentions through carefully designed aesthetic,
narrative, and interactive features (Dong, 2025). In particular,
constructs such as parasocial interaction, anthropomorphism,
perceived authenticity, and social presence have emerged as
central explanatory mechanisms in the literature. Studies
increasingly investigate how individuals negotiate the tension
between the artificiality of virtual influencers and their capacity to
evoke perceived intimacy, credibility, and emotional connection
(Ju et al., 2024; Khalfallah and Keller, 2025).
The growing prominence of virtual influencers also reflects
broader shifts in digitally mediated social interaction. Through
advances in generative AI and adaptive content systems, virtual
influencers are capable of personalized communication,
continuous interaction, and identity consistency across multiple
online environments, including social media platforms and
immersive virtual spaces such as the metaverse (Christou et al.,
2025; Pilelienė and Bogoyavlenska, 2025). These developments
position virtual influencers not merely as communication tools
but as psychologically meaningful social actors capable of
influencing perception, engagement, and relational experiences
in digital environments.
Despite the rapid expansion of this field, the academic
literature on virtual influencers remains fragmented across
disciplines and theoretical perspectives. Existing studies
frequently examine isolated constructs such as
anthropomorphism, attractiveness, disclosure effects, or trust
without situating these findings within a broader intellectual
structure or cumulative research trajectory (Vila-López et al.,
2025). Moreover, because virtual influencer research intersects
psychology, communication studies, computer science,
marketing, and media studies, knowledge production remains
dispersed across journals, methodologies, and geographic
contexts (Bansal et al., 2024). As a result, there is currently a
limited understanding of how the field has evolved conceptually,
where thematic consolidation has occurred, and which
important research gaps remain insufficiently addressed.
Addressing this fragmentation requires a systematic,
quantitative synthesis that examines both the structural
evolution and the conceptual development of the literature.
Bibliometric analysis, when integrated with systematic literature

The primary objective of this study is to provide a

interaction. Specifically, this study aims to:
(1) examine the evolution of virtual influencer research

(2) identify the most influential countries, journals, authors,
and publications contributing to the field;
(3) map the dominant collaboration patterns, thematic

(4) identify emerging research gaps and future directions

research questions:
RQ1: How has virtual influencer research evolved between
2015 and 2025?
RQ2: Which countries, journals, authors, and publications
have contributed most prominently to the development of the
field?
RQ3: What collaboration patterns, thematic structures, and
keyword networks characterize contemporary virtual
influencer research?
RQ4: What research gaps and future directions emerge from
the existing literature?
Building upon previous systematic literature reviews, bibliometric
analyses, and meta-analyses, the present study integrates
systematic literature review procedures with multiple
bibliometric techniques to provide a more comprehensive
assessment of virtual influencer research. Using a final dataset of
116 peer-reviewed journal articles published between 2015 and
2025, the study combines performance analysis and science
mapping to examine publication trends, geographical
distribution, source productivity, methodological characteristics,
influential authors and publications, citation impact, co-
authorship networks, and keyword co-occurrence patterns. This
integrated approach provides a comprehensive understanding of
the intellectual development, collaboration structure, and
thematic evolution of virtual influencer research while
identifying emerging opportunities for future investigation.
The study makes three principal contributions. First, it
integrates systematic literature review procedures with
bibliometric analysis to provide a comprehensive synthesis of the
intellectual development of virtual influencer research. Second, it
identifies the principal publication trends, influential contributors,
collaboration patterns, citation impact, and thematic research
clusters that characterize the field. Third, it highlights important
research gaps, including the limited availability of longitudinal
research, cross-cultural evidence, and interdisciplinary
collaboration, thereby providing a structured agenda for future
research in advertising, marketing communication, consumer
psychology, and human–AI interaction.
2 Literature review of main review
studies
Recent review studies have played a central role in
consolidating the rapidly expanding literature on virtual
influencers by synthesizing existing evidence, identifying
research trends, and highlighting emerging directions for future
investigation. These studies have progressively advanced the
understanding of virtual influencers from conceptual discussions
toward more comprehensive evaluations of consumer behavior,
marketing communication, artificial intelligence, and human–
AI interaction.
Among the most comprehensive contributions, Barari et al.
(2025) conducted a meta-analysis of 71 studies comprising 135
experimental investigations to compare the effectiveness of
virtual and human influencers. Their findings indicate that
although virtual influencers have become increasingly
prominent in digital marketing, human influencers generally
remain more effective in generating consumer engagement and
purchase intentions. The study further demonstrates that
perceived credibility and attractiveness are the principal
mechanisms underlying influencer effectiveness, suggesting that
virtual influencers continue to face significant challenges in
establishing persuasive power comparable to that of their
human counterparts.
The systematic literature review conducted by Khalfallah and
Keller (2025) further expands this perspective by examining
authenticity, ethics, and transparency in virtual influencer
marketing. Based on the analysis of 51 journal articles, the
authors identify consumer trust as the central mechanism
linking disclosure transparency and consumer engagement,
leading to the development of the Virtual Influencer Trust and
Engagement Model (VITEM). Their findings further suggest
that cultural and regulatory contexts significantly influence
consumer responses to virtual influencers, emphasizing that
effective virtual influencer strategies should be adapted to
different market environments rather than adopting a
universal approach.
Recent bibliometric reviews have complemented these
systematic syntheses by examining the intellectual structure
and evolution of the field. Vila-López et al. (2025) mapped
the knowledge structur e of virtual influencer research and
highlighted its interdisciplinary nature across marketing,
communication, and computer science. Similarly , Kusumawati
et al. (2024) employed bibliometric techniques using
VOSviewer to identify emerging research themes,
collabora tion patterns, and the developme nt of avatar
marketing research. Collect ively, these studies indicate that
virtual influencer research has evolved into an increasingly

3 Methodology
This study adopts a bibliometric research design to
systematically examine the scholarly literature on virtual
influencers and synthetic media within the marketing and
communication domains. Bibliometric analysis enables the
quantitative mapping of scientific knowledge, allowing
researchers to identify publication trends, thematic structures,
and intellectual linkages within a research field (Donthu et al.,
2021). This approach is particularly suitable for emerging and
rapidly evolving domains, such as virtual influencer research,
where knowledge is dispersed across diverse disciplines,
including psychology, retail management, and computer science.
3.1 Inclusion and exclusion criteria
To ensure that the studies selected for this bibliometric review
were aligned with the research objectives, explicit inclusion and
exclusion criteria were established. This review focuses on
virtual influencers, AI-powered avatars, and synthetic media
within the context of branding, consumer behavior, and
marketing communication. Particular attention was given to
studies examining the effectiveness of digital personas in
influencing consumer engagement, trust, authenticity, brand
outcomes, and purchase intentions. Studies addressing the
ethical implications, transparency, and governance of AI-
generated influencers and synthetic endorsements were also
included, as these issues represent important emerging research
themes within the literature (Khalfallah and Keller, 2025;
Momeni, 2025).
The selection of articles was constrained to peer-reviewed,
open-access journal articles published in English between 2015
and 2025. This timeframe was chosen to capture the field’s
transition from early CGI experiments to the current era of
generative AI. Restricting the review to English-language journal
articles ensured methodological consistency and high-quality

3.2 Data sources
To explore the current state of research, a multi-source
literature search was conducted. Bibliometric techniques provide
an objective means of mapping scientific knowledge and enable
researchers to examine publication patterns, intellectual
structures, and thematic developments within a research field
(Koseoglu, 2016). To ensure comprehensive coverage of the
interdisciplinary nature of virtual influencer research, four major
bibliographic sources were selected: Scopus, Web of Science
(WoS), IEEE Xplore, and Taylor & Francis Online.
These sources were selected because they collectively provide
broad coverage of business, marketing, communication,
information systems, and computer science while offering high-
quality bibliographic metadata suitable for bibliometric analysis.
Scopus and Web of Science provide the interdisciplinary
foundation necessary for mapping scientific knowledge, IEEE
Xplore captures research on technological developments related
to artificial intelligence and virtual influencers, and Taylor &
Francis Online offers extensive coverage of marketing,
communication, and media studies (Donthu et al., 2021).
Other sources, including ScienceDirect, SpringerLink,
Emerald, ProQuest, EBSCO, and Google Scholar, were not
included because they either substantially overlap with the
indexing coverage of Scopus and Web of Science, provide
publisher-specific rather than multidisciplinary coverage, or
present limitations for bibliometric analysis due to inconsistent
metadata quality and export functionality. Although the
exclusion of these sources may have resulted in the omission of
a small number of relevant publications, the combined use of
Scopus, Web of Science, IEEE Xplore, and Taylor & Francis
Online provides broad and reliable coverage of the virtual
influencer literature while ensuring methodological consistency
and data quality for bibliometric analysis.
3.3 Search strategy
This bibliometric review employed a systematic and replicable
search strategy in accordance with the SPAR-4-SLR guidelines to
ensure methodological rigor and comprehensive coverage of the
literature. The overall article identification, screening, and
selection process is summarized in Figure 1. Bibliometric
techniques enable objective and reproducible analysis of research
fields, supporting quantitative analyses such as keyword co-
occurrence, bibliographic coupling, and citation mapping (Marzi
et al., 2025). Following the framework established by Kitchenham
and Charters (2007), the review process began with a structured
search across four major bibliographic sources: Scopus, Web of
Science, IEEE Xplore, and Taylor & Francis Online, using
predefined search strings aligned with the research objectives.
The search strategy was designed to capture the diverse
terminology used to describe virtual influencers and AI-driven
digital personas within marketing and communication research.
In Scopus, the following query was applied to the TITLE-ABS-
KEY fields:
TITLE-ABS-KEY ((“virtual influencer*” OR “CGI influencer*”
OR “AI influencer*” OR “digital persona*” OR “virtual
avatar*”) AND (“marketing” OR “branding” OR
“communication” OR “consumer behavior”))
A comparable query was applied in Web of Science using the
Topic (TS) field:
TS = ((“virtual influencer*” OR “CGI influencer*” OR “AI
influencer*” OR “digital persona*” OR “virtual avatar*”)
AND (“marketing” OR “branding” OR “communication”
OR “consumer behavior”))
Equivalent Boolean logic was adapted for IEEE Xplore and
Taylor & Francis to maintain conceptual consistency across
platforms, while accounting for database-specific constraints on
search syntax. This approach ensured broad coverage of research
on virtual influencers and AI-driven digital personas while
maintaining conceptual consistency across the selected
bibliographic sources. The search was restricted to peer-reviewed

4 Results
4.1 Annual distribution of publications
The systematic search and data extraction were conducted and
completed in December 2025. The final dataset comprised 116
peer-reviewed journal articles, and Figure 2 illustrates the annual
distribution of publications included in the final sample.
The first article in the dataset addressing virtual influencer-
related concepts appeared in 2017 (Deng et al., 2017). While the
broader concepts of virtual avatars, synthetic characters, and
digital humans have roots in earlier studies on computer
animation and virtual environments, research explicitly
examining virtual influencers within marketing communication
is relatively recent. Similar to the evolution of other AI-enabled
digital technologies, several years elapsed before virtual
influencers emerged as a distinct area of scholarly inquiry
within marketing and consumer behavior research (Kumar
et al., 2024).
As shown in Figure 2, research on virtual influencers
began to gain academic attention in 2021, with three
publications, including studies examining compara tive social
media strategies (Arsenyan and Mirowska, 2021), and the
commercia l use of hyper-realis tic virtual influencers such as
Lil Miquela (Rodrigo-Martín et al., 2021). From 2022
onward, publication output increased substantially , rising
from 6 publications in 2022 to 17 publicat ions in 2023,
before reaching 40 publications in 2024.
The upward trend continued in 2025, with 48 publications
identified in the final dataset. These include early-access and
forthcoming articles available at the time the systematic search
was completed in December 2025 (Barari et al., 2025; Dong, 2025).
The sharp increase in publication output between 2023 and
2024 suggests a period of rapid expansion in virtual influencer
research. This trend appears to coincide with major advances in
generative artificial intelligence and computer-generated imagery
(CGI), together with increasing commercial adoption of virtual
influencers within digital marketing and social commerce
(Pilelienė and Bogoyavlenska, 2025). The continued growth
observed in 2025 further indicates that virtual influencer
research is evolving into an increasingly established area of
inquiry, as reflected by the growing number of empirical
investigations, systematic literature reviews, and theory-oriented
studies (Khalfallah and Keller, 2025).

4.2 Country-wise distribution of
publications
The analysis of geographic distribution reveals a diverse global
interest in virtual influencer research, with publications
originating from more than 40 countries. This broad geographic
representation demonstrates the growing international interest in
virtual influencers across marketing, communication, artificial
intelligence, and consumer behavior research. The distribution
also highlights the 10 most productive countries contributing to
the literature, indicating that research activity is concentrated in
regions with advanced digital infrastructure, strong artificial
intelligence capabilities, and highly developed social
media ecosystems.
As illustrated in Figure 3, China is the leading contributor
with 16 publications, considera bly exceeding all other
countries in the dataset. The United Kingdom, the United
States, and Germany each contributed seven publications,
representing the principal Western research hubs within the
virtual influencr literature. Research originating from these
countries frequently examines consumer psychology , trust,
authenticity, anthropom orphism, and the ethical implicatio ns
of AI-generated communication agents (Khalfallah and Keller,
2025; Momeni, 2025).
The observed geographic distribution also reflects the real-
world development and commercial adoption of virtual
influencer ecosystems. Countries such as China, the United
States, Japan, and South Korea have been among the earliest
adopters of AI-generated virtual influencers through well-known
digital personas, virtual idols, and advanced content creation
industries. Consequently, the concentration of scholarly
publications in these technologically advanced regions appears
to mirror the rapid expansion of virtual-influencer applications
across marketing, entertainment, and social commerce.
The Asia-Pacific region is further represented by Australia,
South Korea, Malaysia, Indonesia, and India, illustrating the
increasing geographical diversification of virtual influencer
research. Studies from these countries frequently explore cultural
dimensions of digital identity, consumer engagement, and
technology adoption within different social and marketing
contexts (Miyake, 2023). Australia also contributes through
influential review-based research, including meta-analytic studies
comparing human and virtual influencer effectiveness (Barari
et al., 2025), while Spain has contributed important bibliometric
and systematic review research synthesizing the rapidly
expanding literature and identifying future research directions
(Vila-López et al., 2025).
4.3 Source distribution of publications
The 116 articles in the final dataset were published across a
diverse range of academic journals, reflecting the
interdisciplinary nature of virtual influencer research. The
distribution is dominated by high-impact journals in marketing,
business, electronic commerce, and technology, suggesting that
virtual influencer research has become established across
multiple academic disciplines. The Journal of Business Research

5.2 Which countries, journals, authors, and
publications have contributed most
prominently to the development of the
field? (RQ2)
The findings indicate that research on virtual influencers
exhibits a clear geographic concentration, with China emerging
as the most productive country, followed by the United
Kingdom, the United States, and Germany. As demonstrated in
the country-wise analysis (Section 4.2), this distribution reflects
not only differences in research capacity but also the practical
development of virtual influencer ecosystems across these
regions. China’s leading position is consistent with the rapid
expansion of virtual idols, AI-generated streamers, and
platform-based social commerce, while countries such as the
United States, Japan, and South Korea have established
commercially successful virtual influencers that have stimulated
growing academic interest in consumer behavior, digital
marketing, and human–AI interaction (Luo and Kim, 2024; Yu
et al., 2024). These findings suggest that countries with more
developed virtual influencer ecosystems also tend to generate
greater scholarly output, indicating a close relationship between
commercial adoption and academic research activity.
Regional differences are also evident in research orientation.
East Asian studies frequently examine virtual influencers within
platform-based commerce environments, emphasizing
livestreaming, digital commerce, and consumer conversion,
whereas European research places greater emphasis on
psychological mechanisms such as trust, authenticity, credibility,
and parasocial interaction. Studies from the United Kingdom,
Germany, and Spain increasingly investigate how perceived
authenticity, transparency, expertise, and psychological distance
shape consumer attitudes and behavioral responses toward
virtual influencers (Ameen et al., 2024). This geographical
diversity demonstrates that virtual influencer research has
developed through complementary regional perspectives that
collectively enrich the field’s interdisciplinary nature.
The analysis of author productivity and collaboration further
illustrates the distributed nature of knowledge production.

influence consumer responses.

effectiveness of virtual influencers through measurable
marketing outcomes. This observation is consistent with the
methodological analysis presented in Section 4.4, which showed

5.4 What research gaps and future
directions emerge from the existing
literature? (RQ4)
Despite the rapid expansion of virtual influencer research, this
review identifies several important conceptual, methodological,
and empirical gaps that present opportunities for future
investigation. These gaps closely align with the thematic clusters
identified through the keyword co-occurrence analysis and
reflect the field’s evolving interdisciplinary nature.
One important research gap concerns the relationship between
authenticity, transparency, and consumer trust. The keyword
analysis demonstrates that authenticity, anthropomorphism,
parasocial interaction, and human-likeness represent central
themes within the literature. However, existing studies continue
to debate how authenticity should be conceptualized in AI-
mediated communication. While Kim and Wang (2024) suggest
that insufficient disclosure may reduce consumer trust, Audrezet
et al. (2025) argue that authenticity can also emerge through
narrative consistency and symbolic coherence. These findings
suggest that future research should move beyond a binary view
of disclosure and examine broader issues, including perceived
intentionality, algorithmic transparency, ethical governance, and
consumer perceptions of AI-generated communicators.
A second research gap relates to the predominance of
short-term behavioral outcomes. As demonstrated in the
methodological analysis, experimental and quantitative survey
designs account for the majority of the empirical literature,
whereas longitudinal investigations remain limited. Consequently,

literature published in leading journals across marketing,
communication, consumer behavior, and digital media. At
the same time, the co-authorship analysis indicat es that

digital marketing. Collectively, these findings suggest that the
field has progressively shifted from emphasizing technological
development to understanding the psychological and marketing
mechanisms by which virtual influencers shape consumer
attitudes and behavioral outcomes.

study extends previous review research by integrating
publication performance, citation analysis, collaboration
networks, and thematic mapping within a single comprehensive
framework. This integrated perspective provides a more holistic
understanding of how virtual influencer research has evolved,
identifies areas of thematic convergence and fragmentation, and
highlights opportunities for greater interdisciplinary
collaboration and theoretical integration.
The study also identifies several priorities for future research.
Despite the rapid expansion of the literature, important gaps
remain regarding longitudinal evidence, cross-cultural
comparisons, methodological diversity, ethical governance,
transparency, and the implications of increasingly sophisticated
generative AI technologies. Addressing these issues may
contribute to a more comprehensive theoretical understanding

Overall, this study provides a comprehensive synthesis of the
