1. Introduction

The explosive growth of live -streaming e-commerce has generated substantial gross merchandise value, with tourism
emerging as a particularly dynamic sector (Li et al., 2018; Qiu et al., 2021; Song et al., 2021). Especially after the epidemic, the
combination of live -streaming and tourism has brought new opportunities to the industry, making it an indispensable
marketing channel (Jackson et al., 2020; Xie et al., 2022). Abidin et al. (2025) further noted that current trends include mobility,
cultural and heritage tourism, digital landscape transformation, and the growing use of virtual reality.
Unlike conventional e -commerce, tourism live -streaming involves a critical post -purchase stage: offline voucher
verification. Wang and Guo (2023)  noted that verification vouchers carry fixed redemption windows, after which automatic
refunds are triggered; consumers may also cancel at any time. At this stage, consumers have committed financially yet retain
full discretion over whether to complete co nsumption. A decision context that traditional consumer behavior theories, which
treat purchase completion as the endpoint, have not adequately addressed. A high verification rate directly drives tourist
arrivals and local employment (e.g., tour guides, ho tel and catering staff), making verification behavior a pressing concern for
merchants and destinations alike.
A key but overlooked driver of this post -purchase decision is online reviews. Consumers regularly consult reviews for
hotels and travel products  (El-Said, 2020; Kim and Kim, 2020; Wen et al., 2021), and from a stimulus-organism-response (SOR)
perspective, reviews function as external informational stimuli that shape cognitive and affective evaluations before behavioral
Abstract Tourism live -streaming e -commerce features a structurally unique post -purchase stage in which financially
committed consumers retain full discretion to withdraw before offline voucher verification, a decision window that existing
consumer behavior theories have not adequately addressed. This conceptual paper investigates how fake positive reviews,
as post-purchase informational stimuli, reshape consumer psychology and influence offline verification intentions. Drawing
on an integrative literature review of Scopus and Google Scholar, this study develops a theoretical model integrating the
SOR framework and the TPB. The three TPB antecedents reconceptualized here as dynamic mediators rather than static
pre-purchase predictors, which in turn drive offline verif ication intention (R). Six conceptual propositions are developed,
proposing that fake positive reviews positively influence all three mediators and that each mediator positively relates to
verification intention. These are theoretically derived propositions awaiting empirical validation, not empirical findings. The
positive-direction pathways are bounded by four conditions: sufficiently high perceived review credibility, moderate-to-low
consumer skepticism, hedonic rather than utilitarian product type, and limited prior travel experience. While fake positive
reviews may temporarily increase verification willingness, over -reliance risks eroding long -term trust and platform
credibility. Three theoretical contributions are made: offline verification is reframed  as an independent post -purchase
behavioral decision; the three TPB constructs are repositioned as dynamic mediators susceptible to post -purchase
informational disruption, introducing the concept of "intention destabilization"; and fake reviews are identif ied as
theoretically legitimate post-purchase stimuli within SOR, extending its explanatory scope beyond the point of purchase in
experiential consumption contexts with delayed and reversible consumption structures.

2.Literature Review

2.1. Literature Review Methodology

To ensure transparency and replicability, this section briefly describes the integrative literature review protocol that
underpins the theoretical development. The review was conducted by systematically searching two major academic databases:
Scopus and Google Scholar.
Inclusion criteria were: (1) empirical or conceptual studies addressing at least one thematic cluster; (2) publication in
peer-reviewed academic journals or edited scholarly volumes; (3) publication year between 2008 and 2025. Studies were
excluded if they  focused exclusively on technical detection algorithms without addressing consumer psychology, or if their
findings were not translatable to the tourism or live -streaming context. Theoretical selection followed an explicit
complementarity logic: SOR was ch osen because it provides a causal architecture for modeling how environmental stimuli
trigger internal psychological states and observable responses; TPB was selected because it operationalizes those internal
states, attitude, subjective norm, perceived be havioral control, as measurable constructs with established predictive validity
for planned behavior. Neither framework alone captures both the stimulus -driven disruption caused by fake reviews and the
deliberative, reversible nature of verification decisi ons; their integration is therefore theoretically necessary rather than
combinatorially arbitrary.

2.2. Fake Reviews

Fake reviews are deliberately written by customers or merchants to exaggerate product advantages or damage
competitor reputations (Jindal and Liu, 2008) . Most current studies focus on analyzing the intrinsic motivations behind the
posting of fake reviews (Gössling et al., 2018; Hlee et al., 2021; Luca and Zervas, 2016) , the characteristics of fake reviews
(Banerjee, 2022; Petrescu et al., 2023), or identification detection (Wang et al., 2021), etc. Critically, however, these streams
of research share a common limitation: they overwhelmingly adopt a pre -purchase or platform -governance perspective,
examining how fake reviews are produced or detected rather than how they reshape consumer psychology afte r purchase.
This orientation leaves the post-purchase decision stage analytically absent from the fake review literature.
From the perspective of consumer influence, fake reviews may mislead consumers into forming fake beliefs and
attitudes in the early stages of purchasing new products (Shih et al., 2022). This misleading behavior can weaken the credibility,
usefulness, and value of online reviews (Luca and Zervas, 2016; Munzel, 2016), causing consumers to feel deceived (Riquelme
et al., 2016) and distrustful, ultimately affecting their negative online reputation and repurchase intention (Ahmad and Sun,
2018). However, for experiential products (Zhang, et al., 2023), when consumers perceive that online reviews may be fake,
their experiential tendencies will be enhanced.  To this end, this paper focuses on the impact of fake positive reviews
(manipulated by merchants) on hedonic products (tourism products) in the live-streaming e-commerce scenario on consumers.
This focus is theoretically motivated: fake positive reviews represent the most commercially prevalent form of review
manipulation in tourism live-streaming (Ansari et al., 2021; Wu et al., 2020) , and hedonic products are particularly susceptible
to affective amplification through such reviews (Zhang, et al., 2023). Therefore, the literature on fake reviews establishes that
deceptive review content distorts consumer cognition and affect. But it stops short of theorizing how this distortion operate s
after purchase, within the specific reversible-consumption structure of tourism live-streaming.

2.3. Tourism Live-streaming E-commerce (TLsEc) and Consumer Behavior

Live-streaming commerce has rapidly evolved into a central form of social commerce by integrating real -time
interaction, multimedia presentations, and instant purchasing. Recent reviews demonstrate that research in this domain has
expanded significantly, w ith dominant themes centering on impulse buying, trust formation, social interaction, and the
extensive application of the stimulus -organism-response (SOR) framework (Herzallah et al. , 2025). These themes are not
coincidental: the real-time, emotionally immersive nature of live-streaming creates precisely the conditions, high arousal, social
presence, time pressure, under which SOR's stimulus -driven logic applies most directly. However, this same literature reveals
a structural blindspot. It almost exclusively models the purchase decision as the endpoint, leaving what happens between
purchase and consumption untheorized. These studies indicate a clear shift from technology adoption perspective s toward
psychological and behavioral explanations of consumer responses in live -streaming environments. Within this broader
landscape, tourism live -streaming e -commerce represents a distinct and theoretically challenging context. Unlike physical
goods, payment does not signify the completion of consumption; instead, consumers retain discretion over whether to verify
electronic vouchers at the destination (Wang and Guo, 2023). This post-purchase, pre-consumption stage introduces decision

This reversibility is not a peripheral operational detail but a theoretically significant feature that distinguishes tourism
live-streaming from all other e-commerce contexts studied under the SOR framework. It creates an extended decision window
in which post-purchase informational stimuli, most notably, fake reviews that consumers encounter after purchase but before
verification, can meaningfully alter behavioral intentions. This structural feature to the fake review literature reviewed, t he
present paper argues that fake reviews function as post -purchase stimuli that activate SOR processes within this verification
window, reshaping the psychological states that TPB identifies as proximal determinants of behavior.
Implicit in the existing research on verification behavior is the assumption that consumers’ intentions remain stable
once a purchase has been completed. Consequently, verification is treated as an operational compliance issue rather than as
an ongoing decision that may be reshaped by new post -purchase information. This assumption limits existing models’ ability
to account for situations in which consumers actively reconsider their decisions at the post -purchase stage.

2.4. SOR Theory Model

SOR theory can determine how environmental stimuli affect the formation of individual internal evaluations
(consumers) and ultimately lead to behaviour (responses) (Mehrabian and Russell, 1974). Liang et al. (2024) used SOR theory
to verify the driving factors of consumers' impulsive travel intentions in tourism live-streaming, which confirmed the practicality
of this theory in the field of tourism live -streaming. Kumar et al. (2024) showed that fake reviews can serve as an external
stimulus in the SOR framework, confirming that deceptive content qualifies as an environmental cue capable of triggering
internal cognitive and affective responses. This finding is pivotal for the present paper: if fake reviews constitute legitimate
SOR stimuli, then their influence need not terminate at the purchase moment but may extend into any subsequent decision
window in which consumers remain evaluatively active. Recent research further emphasizes th e emotional and evaluative
disruptions caused by deceptive content. For example, Dong et al. (2025) demonstrated that visual review formatting affects
trust heuristics, illustrating how even format -level cues, not only valence, alter organismic processing. While Li et al. (2024)
revealed that fake reviews elicit anxiety and reduce anticipated satisfaction, directly demonstrating that deceptive stimuli
reshape affective states that persist beyond the purchase moment and into post-purchase behavioral contexts. These findings
collectively suggest that SOR is capable of modeling post-purchase informational disruption, yet existing applications have not
extended the framework to the verification stage of tourism live -streaming. To address this gap, this study extends the SOR
framework by conceptualizing post -purchase psychological states as dynamic evaluative processes shaped by subsequent
informational stimuli.

2.5. TPB Theory

The Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) has been extensively validated for predicting deliberate and planned behaviors,
particularly in tourism contexts that involve time investment, resource planning, and social considerations (Ajzen, 2020;
Rozenkowska, 2023). Its core constructs, attitude, subjective norm, and perceived behavioral control, are especially suited to
explaining decisions that require reflective evaluation rather than impulsive reactions.
Despite its strength in predicting planned behaviors, TPB alone does not explicitly account for how external
informational stimuli, such as fake reviews, dynamically reshape attitudes, norms, and perceptions of control after pur chase.
This limitation is structurally embedded in TPB's design. In the tourism live -streaming context, however, consumers have
already formed an intention (to verify) at the point of purchase. What the post -purchase window introduces is the possibility
of intention revision under new informational stimuli. TPB, as traditionally applied, offers no mechanism for this revision
process. This is precisely where SOR's stimulus -organism-response logic becomes necessary: by reconceptualizing TPB's three
constructs as organismic mediators (O) that are themselves shaped by environmental stimuli (S) the integrated SOR -TPB
framework acquires the temporal flexibility that neither theory possesses alone. As Tam et al.  (2024) argued, TPB
underperforms when applied to spontaneous or emotionally charged post-decision behaviours. The interaction of information
overload and delayed gratification, typical in tourism live-streaming, requires frameworks that allow for cognitive reevaluation
beyond initial intention.

2.6. Integrative Insight and Research Gap

The theoretical gap this paper addresses is narrow and specific: it is not about fake reviews in general, nor about live -
streaming commerce in general, but about the absence of any theoretical account of how post-purchase informational stimuli
reshape beha vioral intention within a reversible, delayed -consumption structure. Existing studies are limited in three key
respects. First, they predominantly examine verification behavior through managerial interventions (e.g., reminders),
overlooking informational s timuli that operate independently of firm control. Second, prior research rarely theorizes
verification as a post -purchase decision, implicitly assuming that consumer intentions remain stable once payment is
completed. Third, the role of deceptive informat ion, such as fake reviews, has been largely ignored in this context, despite its

3. Propositions Development

Rather than triggering immediate behavioral responses, fake reviews influence offline verification intention by
reshaping consumers’ post -purchase psychological states, thereby necessitating a mediating structure. Based on the
combination of SOR theory (Mehrabian and Russell, 1974)  and TPB theory, this research proposes that fake reviews affect
consumers' offline verification intentions through three mediating variables: attitudes, subjective norms, and perceived
behavioral control. Across all three pathways, fake reviews are tempor ally positioned after purchase but before verification,
preceding the formation of verification -related psychological states. The proposed relationships are visually summarized in

3.1. Attitude towards Verification

Unlike pre-purchase evaluations, the post -purchase but pre -consumption stage represents a psychologically unstable
decision window in tourism live-streaming e-commerce, where consumers may actively reconsider whether completing offline
verification remains worthwhile, despite having already paid.
Fake positive reviews often construct an idealized product image by exaggerating advantages and downplaying
shortcomings (Sahut et al., 2024). Even when consumers are aware of potential manipulation, such reviews may still shape
favorable evaluations in hedonic contexts such as tourism (Zhang, et al., 2023). This positive attitudinal effect, however, is not
unconditional. It is most likely to emerge when consumers have low skepticism toward the review source, when the product is
affectively rich rather than utilitarian, and when the consumer has limited prior experience that would allow direct comparison
against a known baseline. In contrast, consumers with high prior travel experience or high review skepticism may process the
same fake positive reviews analytically, reducing or reversing the attitudinal effect. Accordingly, consumer attitude serves as a
mediating variable linking fake positive reviews to consumers' intention to engage in offline verification behaviour.
In a post -purchase context, evaluative attitudes toward verification are not fixed at the point of payment but remain
susceptible to subsequent informational cues. Accordingly, fake reviews influence offline verification intention through post -
purchase psychological reevaluation, rather than exerting a direct behavioral effect. Specifically, because fake positive reviews
exaggerate product advantages in an affectively loaded context where consumers have already committed financially, they are
more likely to  reinforce than undermine evaluative approval of the verification decision, producing a net positive effect on
attitude. Therefore, this paper proposes the following propositions:

3.2. Perceived Behavioral Control

Perceived behavioral control reflects consumers’ perceived ability and resource availability to complete offline
verification (Ajzen, 1991; Bandura, 2001; Reza Jalilvand and Samiei, 2012; Trafimow et al., 2002). In the context of fake reviews,
a large number of positive reviews can enhance consumers' trust in products (Zhang et al., 2023). When consumers believe
that products and services are reliable, they perceive greater control over their purchase decisions, thereby enhancing
perceived behavioural control. This mechanism operates most clearly when review credibility is perceived as suf ficiently high
and when consumers' general skepticism toward online reviews is moderate or low. When skepticism is elevated, the same
volume of positive reviews may instead amplify perceived uncertainty, because the consumer recognizes potential
manipulation but cannot reliably distinguish authentic from deceptive content, thereby reducing rather than enhancing
perceived behavioral control. This skepticism-moderated reversal constitutes one of the boundary conditions addressed. Here,
confidence does not reflect affective or evaluative approval of the purchase but rather consumers’ perceived ability to manage
uncertainty and execute verification -related actions. Especially in the online shopping model of tourism live -streaming e -
commerce, consumers cannot exp erience the product in person and are more dependent on online reviews (Yadav et al.,
2024).
Perceived behavioral control reflects consumers' perception of the capabilities, resources, and opportunities required
in the verification process (Trafimow et al., 2002). Fake reviews do not directly compel verification behavior; instead, they alter
consumers’ perceived feasibility of completing verification, which subsequently shapes intention. Specifically, because a hig h
volume of positive reviews reduces perceived product uncertainty and signals that others have successfully completed similar
experiences, consumers infer that verification is feasible and within their control, thereby elevating perceived behavioral
control. Therefore, this paper proposes the following propositions:
Proposition 3 (P3): Fake reviews positively influence consumers’ perceived behavioral control toward offline verification
in tourism live-streaming e-commerce.
Proposition 4 (P4)： Perceived behavioral control positively mediates the relationship between fake reviews and offline
verification intention.

3.3. Subjective Norm

At the post -purchase stage, consumers’ verification decisions are further shaped by perceived social expectations
regarding whether verification is appropriate or socially endorsed. Subjective norm is a key concept in the theory of planned
behaviour, refer ring to the perceived expectations of important others, such as family members and friends, regarding an
individual's performance of a specific behaviour (Ajzen, 1991) . Within the post -purchase context, subjective norms reflect
perceived social expectations regarding whether verification is socially endorsed.  In addition, online word of mouth
significantly influences consumers' subjective norms (Halim et al., 2022). A large number of continuous positive reviews can
lead consumers to believe that these reviews reflect the true opinions of the majority, thereby affecting their subjective norms.
Fake positive reviews can still effectively increase consumers' purchase intention when targeting hedonic products (such
as tourism products) (Zhang, et al., 2023). In particular, in the field of tourism, a large number of positive reviews can influence
subjective norms, ultimately increasing consumers' willingness to travel (Iriobe and Abiola-Oke, 2019). The social norm-shaping
effect of fake reviews, however, depends critically on the consumer's perception of review authenticity. When consumers
perceive that the consensus expressed in reviews is genuine, they are more likely to internalize it as a social norm. Conversely,
when review credibility is perceived as low, the normative force of positive reviews is attenuated or nullified (Cheung and
Thadani, 2012; Salminen et al., 2022). The influence of fake reviews on subjective norms depends on the individual’s perception
of important others’ expectations. In this context, reviews from other consumers act as a proxy for such social expectations
(Cheung and Thadani, 2012). In the context of tourism live-streaming e-commerce, consumers face offline verification decisions
involving uncertain future experiences and are more dependent on others' opinions. Fake positive reviews will exaggerate the
product's advantages, leading consumers to believe that using the product can enhance their social image, thereby increasing
their intention to verify offline. Furthermore, (Joo et al., 2020) found that subjective norms significantly impact consumers'
willingness to travel to rural tourism destinations. Moreover, Subjective norms also play a significant positive role in tour ists'
intention to visit green hotels (Fauzi et al., 2024). Specifically, because fake positive reviews simulate broad social consensus
around product quality and experiential value, consumers infer that verification is the socially expected and approved course
of action, thereby strengthening subjective norms in  favor of completing offline verification. Therefore, this paper proposes
the following propositions:

3.4. Conceptual Framework and Model Justification

In the SOR framework, fake reviews serve as external stimuli (S), attitudes, subjective norms, and perceived behavioral
control as organismic mediators (O), and verification intention as the response (R). Unlike prior SOR -TPB applications, which
primarily model pre-purchase intentions, the present framework reconceptualizes verification as a post-purchase reevaluation
process, highlighting how fake reviews reshape attitudes, norms, and control perceptions before consumption.  The proposed
main-effect pathways operate under a set of implicit boundary conditions. Specifically, the positive direction of fake reviews'
influence is most applicable when perceived review credibility is sufficiently high, consumer skepticism is moderate or low, the
product is hedonic rather than utilitarian, and the consumer has limited prior experience with comparable tourism products.
When these conditions are not met, particularly when skepticism is high or credibility is perceived as low, fake reviews may
instead erode trust and suppress verification intention. Future empirical work should explicitly test these boundary conditions
as moderators of the proposed pathways.

4. Conclusions

This paper has developed a conceptual model explaining how fake positive reviews reshape consumers' post -purchase
psychological states, attitude, perceived behavioral control, and subjective norm, and thereby influence offline verification
intention in tourism live-streaming e-commerce.

4.1. Theoretical Significance

This paper makes three distinct theoretical contributions. First, it redefines offline verification as an independent post -
purchase behavioral decision rather than a procedural continuation of purchase, thereby extending the temporal scope of
consumer beha vior theory into a neglected decision stage. Second, it repositions the three TPB constructs as dynamic
mediators responsive to post -purchase informational stimuli, rather than static pre -purchase predictors, introducing the
concept of "intention destabilization" under informational threat. Third, it identifies fake reviews as a theoretically legitimate
post-purchase stimulus within the SOR framework, demonstrating that SOR's explanatory power extends beyond the point of
purchase when applied to experiential consumption contexts with delayed and reversible consumption structures.

4.2. Practical Significance

Relaxing the proposition of post -purchase intention stability reframes verification as a cognitive and informational
decision rather than a purely operational issue. Traditional managerial logic treats verification failures as execution probl ems,
investing heavily in reminders and deadline nudges. This framework suggests that unmanaged fake reviews may temporarily
boost verification rates but undermine long -term trust and destination credibility. Managerial focus should therefore shift
toward governing the post-purchase information environment: platforms should adopt authenticity-centric review design and
credibility-weighted ranking, while merchants should replace inflated positivity with expectation -calibrating content such as
realistic itineraries and transparent constraints. These recommendations are directional guidance pending empirical validation.

4.3. Proposed Empirical Research Design

The present paper is conceptual in nature and does not itself conduct empirical testing. To enhance the testability of
the proposed model and provide a clear agenda for future validation, this section outlines a research design that subsequent
studies may adopt. The design addresses the operationalization of key constructs, appropriate sampling, and analytical
strategies, and should be read as a research agenda. First, the empirical context should focus on tourism live -streaming e-
commerce platforms where consumers purchase experiential products through electronic vouchers with delayed consumption.
A suitable sample would consist of consumers who have completed a live -streaming purchase but have not yet verified their
vouchers, as this group directly reflects the post-purchase but pre-consumption decision stage theorized in this study. Second,
key constructs can be operationalized using established measurement scales. Fake reviews may be manipulated or measured
through perceived review authenticity, exaggerat ion, or inconsistency. Attitudes toward verification, subjective norms, and
perceived behavioral control can be adapted from validated TPB scales. In contrast, offline verification intention can be
captured as consumers’ stated likelihood of completing vou cher verification within the valid period. Third, survey -based
structural equation modeling could test the mediating roles of attitudes, subjective norms, and perceived behavioral control.
Experimental designs may manipulate exposure to fake versus authent ic reviews to establish causal effects on post -purchase
intention revision. Longitudinal or behavioral tracking data could further examine how verification intentions evolve under
changing informational conditions.

4.4. Limitations and Future Research

Despite its theoretical contributions, this study has several limitations that also suggest directions for future research.
First, this framework examines a simplified review environment by focusing on fake positive reviews as a post -purchase
informational stimulus, and the proposed positive-direction pathways are bounded by conditions that the model does not yet
formally test. Most notably, review credibility, consumer skepticism, product type (hedonic vs. utilitarian), and prior trave l
experience. Future studies should incorporate these as explicit moderators to identify the conditions under which fake reviews
amplify versus suppress verification intention. In real -world settings, consumers are often exposed to a combination of fake
positive, fake negative , and authentic reviews. Future research could extend the proposed framework by examining how
conflicting review valence shapes post -purchase cognitive dissonance, trust calibration, and verification intentions. Such
extensions would allow scholars to mode l post -purchase decision -making in more complex, ecologically valid information
environments. Second, although this study conceptualizes attitudes, subjective norms, and perceived behavioral control as
dynamic mediators, it does not model their temporal ev olution. Longitudinal or process -oriented designs could clarify when
and how post-purchase information most strongly reshapes intentions. Third, the model is developed within a Chinese tourism
live-streaming context, which features platform structures, con sumer behaviors, and review ecosystems that may differ
substantially from other markets. The generalizability of the proposed pathways to other cultural or institutional contexts
remains to be established, and cross-cultural comparative studies are encouraged.
