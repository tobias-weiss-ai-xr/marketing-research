Abstract
Against the background of digital economy -driven cultural tourism transformation, artificial
intelligence has become an important force for reorganizing tourism resources, generating consumption
scenarios, and improving consumption conversion. Focusing on Qingyuan’s all -for-one tourism, this
study examines how AI can transform dispersed tourism resources into continuous, participatory, and
consumable scenarios, and how such transformation can enhance the conversion from tourist arrival to
value creation. A qualitative case -based analysis was conducted by integrating the theoretical
perspectives of digital tourism, scenario production, consumer behavior conversion, and human –AI
collaboration. The analysis examined Qingyuan’s resource structure, consumption scenario
characteristics, practical constraints, AI -enabled mechanisms, and conversion pathways. The findings
show that Qingyuan has a composite resource base formed by ecological landscapes, hot -spring
wellness, rafting sports, rural leisure, ethnic culture, and characteristic agricultural products. However,
its tourism consumption still faces fragmented scenarios, insufficient cultural expression, weak digital
service capacity, limited tourist data utilization, and relatively short consumption chains. AI can
address these problems through six mechanisms: intelligent perception, intelligent recommendation,
intelligent interaction, intelligent generation, immersive experience, and data feedback. These
mechanisms enable tourist demand identification, product matching, service optimization, content co -
creation, experience upgrading, and dynamic supply adjustment. The study further indicates that
effective AI empowerment does not depend on the simple introduction of intelligent devices or digital
platforms, but on embedding AI into the whole process of tourist consumption behavior. This study
proposes a scenario-oriented conversion framework for Qingyuan’s all-for-one tourism, suggesting that
data-driven demand recognition, experience -based scenario design, and feedback -based supply
optimization are key to transforming resource advantages into consumption value.

Keywords: Artificial intelligence; cultural tourism; consumption scenario; consumption conversion;
all-for-one tourism; Qingyuan

1. Introduction
1.1 Research Background
The digital economy is reshaping the production, communication, experience, and
consumption of cultural tourism. New -generation digital technologies, including big data,
artificial intelligence (AI), cloud computing, virtual reality, augmented reality, and
generative AI, are no longer merely auxiliary tools for tourism management. Rather, they are
increasingly involved in reorganizing tourism resources, generating consumption scenarios,
enhancing tourist experience, and improving consumption conversion. Recent studies on
digital tourism and smart tourism have shifted from information services and online
platforms to broader concerns with AI, immersive technologies, blockchain, mobile
applications, and data -driven tourism governance. Wu et al. argue that digital technologies
are transforming the organizational forms and development models of tourism while creating
new tourism scenarios, business formats, and operational models [1]. Thus, AI -enabled
cultural tourism consumption should not be understood simply as a matter of service
efficiency, but as a process through which destination resources are transformed into
experiential and consumable scenarios.

The rise of generative AI further strengthens this
transformation. Compared with conventional digital
technologies, generative AI can not only process data but
also produce texts, images, videos, audio content, and
interactive narratives. Li et al . note that although the
application of generative AI in tourism remains at an early
stage, its potential in content generation, tourist interaction,
personalized services, and managerial decision -making is
becoming increasingly evident [2]. This suggests that tourism
destinations are moving from the “display of resources”
toward the generation of situated experiences and tourist co -
creation.
On the demand side, cultural tourism consumption is
shifting from sightseeing -oriented consumption to scenario -
based, experiential, and affective consumption. Tourists
increasingly value how they experience a destination,
whether the experience is shareable, and whether it provides
emotional value. AI can be embedded in the whole tourist
consumption process through tourist profiling, intelligent
recommendation, smart customer service, digital guiding,
intelligent payment, and evaluation analysis. Siddik et al .
show that AI applications can enhance tourism efficiency
and smart tourism capability and contribute to tourism
growth and sustainable development [3]. However, AI -
enabled innovation also has limitations. Zhang et al . find
that although AI -generated content has advantages in
efficiency, it cannot fully replace professionally generated
interpretation in terms of informativeness, emotional appeal,
and empathy [4]. Therefore, AI -enabled scenario innovation
in cultural tourism should be understood as human –AI
collaboration rather than technological substitution.
From the perspective of cultural heritage and local cultural
communication, AI and immersive technologies are also
expanding the boundaries of tourism experience.
Sangamuang et al. show that generative AI and metaverse -
based applications can support real -time guidance,
personalized learning, and the dissemination of cultural
heritage [5]. Research on AIGC in rural cultural tourism
similarly suggests that AI -generated content can improve
rural tourism experience, enhance operational efficiency,
preserve cultural heritage, and stimulate local economic
growth [6]. These studies indicate that AI is applicable not
only to large-scale urban tourism platforms, but also to rural
tourism, local culture, intangible cultural heritage, and
distinctive local products.
Qingyuan, located in northern Guangdong, has multiple
tourism resources, including ecological landscapes, hot -
spring wellness, rafting sports, rural leisure, ethnic culture,
characteristic agricultural products, and proximity to the
Guangdong–Hong Kong –Macao Greater Bay Area tourist
market. With the development of all -for-one tourism,
Qingyuan’s tourism competition is no longer limited to
individual scenic spots. Instead, it increasingly depends on
the systemic coordination of scenic areas, rural
communities, accommodation, catering, transportation,
cultural and creative products, agricultural products, night -
time consumption, and digital services. How AI can
transform Qingyuan’s dispersed tourism resources into
continuous consumption scenarios and improve the
conversion from tourist arrival to consumption, from
experience to sharing, and from one -time consumption to
compound consumption constitutes the starting point of this
study.

1.2 Problem Awareness
Although AI is widely regarded as a key driver of cultural
tourism transformation, technological intervention does not
automatically generate consumption growth. In many
practices of cultural tourism digitalization, AI is still mainly
used for platform construction, online promotion, smart
guiding, electronic ticketing, or data visualization. Its deeper
role in reconstructing consumption scenarios and shaping
consumption conversion mechanisms remains insufficiently
examined. Therefore, the central issue of this study is not
whether Qingyuan needs AI, but how AI can be
meaningfully embedded in the consumption process of
Qingyuan’s all -for-one tourism and transform its resource
advantages into scenario and consumption advantages.
This issue is particularly relevant to Qingyuan. Although
Qingyuan has abundant tourism resources, resource
abundance does not necessarily lead to effective
consumption conversion. In practice, tourism consumption
in Qingyuan remains concentrated in relatively mature
products such as rafting, hot springs, scenic sightseeing, and
rural leisure, while tourist expenditure is still largely
associated with tickets, catering, and accommodation. By
contrast, high value -added consumption, including cultural
experience, night -time tourism, cultural and creative
shopping, study tours, wellness services, digital experiences,
and post -trip repurchase, remains underdeveloped.
Qingyuan already has strong arrival attractiveness, but how
to enhance stay attractiveness, consumption attractiveness,
and revisit attractiveness remains a key issue for the high -
quality development of all-for-one tourism.
From the perspective of scenario structure, Qingyuan’s
ecological, rural, ethnic cultural, wellness, outdoor sports,
and agricultural resources remain relatively dispersed. The
connections among scenic areas, villages, homestays,
catering businesses, transportation services, cultural and
creative products, and agricultural specialties are not yet
sufficiently integrated. As a result, a stable consumption
chain and scenario -based closed loop have not been fully
formed. Existing studies emphasize that the value of digital
technologies lies not only in online services but also in their
ability to generate new tourism scenarios and promote the
integration of the digital economy and the real tourism
economy [1]. For Qingyuan, therefore, the key to AI
empowerment is not merely the construction of smart
platforms, but the reorganization of all -for-one tourism
resources into continuous scenarios that are perceptible,
participatory, consumable, and shareable.
Accordingly, this study asks: in the context where all -for-
one tourism is shifting from resource development to
scenario operation and from traffic attraction to value
conversion, how can AI reconstruct Qingyuan’s tourism
consumption scenarios through demand identification,
content generation, intelligent interaction, immersive
experience, and data feedback, thereby enhancing the
conversion from tourist arrival and experience to compound
consumption, social sharing, and repeated consumption?
To answer this question, this study takes Qingyuan’s all-for-
one tourism as a case and examines the theoretical logic,
practical problems, operating mechanisms, and conversion
pathways of AI -enabled cultural tourism consumption
scenario innovation. The study argues that AI empowerment
in Qingyuan does not lie in the simple introduction of
intelligent devices or digital platforms. Rather, it lies in
embedding AI into the whole process of tourist consumption

2. Theoretical Logic of AI -Enabled Innovation in
Cultural Tourism Consumption Scenarios
2.1 From Resource Supply to Scenario Production
Traditional tourism consumption has long been organized
around a resource -supply logic. Natural landscapes,
historical sites, folk cultures, and leisure facilities constitute
the primary basis through which destinations attract tourists,
while tourist expenditure is mainly concentrated on entrance
tickets, transportation, accommodation, and catering. Under
this model, tourism resources themselves are regarded as the
core source of destination competitiveness, and
consumption conversion depends largely on resource
visibility, destination reputation, and the reception capacity
of scenic areas.
However, in the context of the digital economy and the
experience economy, the core of tourism consumption has
shifted from whether a destination possesses abundant
resources to how such resources can be organized into
scenarios that are experiential, interactive, communicable,
and consumable. A consumption scenario is not merely a
display space for tourism resources. Rather, it is an
experience system jointly constituted by spatial
environment, cultural narrative, technological mediation,
service process, and tourist emotion. The digital economy
facilitates the coordination of tourism elements such as
food, accommodation, transportation, sightseeing, shopping,
and entertainment within cultural tourism consumption
scenarios. It also promotes the deep integration of digital
technologies and cultural industries, thereby enhancing the
emotional experience and value -added potential of cultural
tourism.
Therefore, the first layer of the logic through which AI
enables innovation in cultural tourism consumption
scenarios lies in the shift from resource supply to scenario
production. If an ecological scenic area is presented only as
an object of sightseeing, its consumption conversion may
remain limited to ticket revenue. However, if the same
resource is reorganized through AI guiding, immersive
imagery, parent–child study tours, wellness courses, night -
time activities, and cultural and creative products, it may
generate a much longer and more diversified consumption
chain.

2.2 From Tourist Arrival to Behavioral Conversion
Cultural tourism consumption does not occur automatically
once tourists arrive at a destination. Rather, it is a
continuous behavioral process consisting of cognition,
interest, interaction, decision-making, purchase, and sharing.
The value of AI lies in its capacity to intervene at multiple
points in this process, reduce tourists’ decision -making
costs, improve the fit between tourist demand and
destination experience, and increase the likelihood of
consumption.
In the pre -trip stage, AI can identify tourist preferences
through search data, social media data, and platform
behavioral data, thereby enabling precise recommendation
and content -based attraction. During the trip, intelligent
guiding, smart customer service, smart navigation, and real -
time consumption recommendation can enhance tourist
experience and guide tourists toward catering,
accommodation, cultural and creative shopping, night -time
activities, and other consumption nodes. In the post -trip
stage, natural language processing, image recognition, and
evaluation analysis can identify tourist feedback and
promote repurchase and communication through
personalized recommendation, membership operation, and
content co-creation.
Existing studies based on the SICAS consumer behavior
model suggest that AI can identify consumption demand
through tourist profiling and market trend monitoring,
stimulate tourist interest through personalized
recommendation and AIGC content, improve interactive
experience through smart customer service and intelligent
tour guiding, facilitate purchase decisions through
intelligent payment and immersive scenarios, and promote
evaluation sharing through image processing and natural
language recognition.

2.3 From Technological Embedding to Field
Reconstruction
AI is not merely a single technological tool embedded in the
cultural tourism industry. It is also a mediating force that
may reshape the relationships among tourists, space, culture,
and consumption. Digital intelligence technologies can
transcend the limitations of physical space and promote the
transformation of cultural tourism spaces from closed
physical fields to hybrid fields characterized by the
coexistence of virtual and physical dimensions. This
transformation is achieved through virtual spatial extension,
relational reorganization, and the reproduction of meaning.
Relevant studies on the activation of red cultural heritage
have argued that digital intelligence technologies, as a form
of “meta-medium,” can drive the transformation of cultural
fields through the virtual extension of physical space, the
embodied reorganization of social relations, and the
semantic reconstruction of historical meaning.
For Qingyuan’s all-for-one tourism, this means that the role
of AI should not be understood merely as efficiency
enhancement. Rather, it should be understood as field
reconstruction. AI can reconnect dispersed ecological
resources, rural spaces, ethnic cultures, agricultural
products, accommodation facilities, and transportation
nodes, enabling tourists to form continuous experiences and
continuous consumption across the all -for-one tourism
space. Accordingly, the competitive logic of Qingyuan’s all-
for-one tourism shifts from competition among individual
scenic spots to competition among scenario networks.

3. Developmental Foundations and Practical Problems of
Qingyuan’s All -for-One Tourism Consumption
Scenarios
3.1 Developmental Foundations of Qingyuan’s All -for-
One Tourism Consumption Scenarios
Qingyuan’s all -for-one tourism is characterized by a
relatively high degree of resource complexity. First,
Qingyuan possesses ecological resources such as mountains,
canyons, rivers, forests, and lakes, which provide a basis for
ecological sightseeing, forest wellness, outdoor sports, and
leisure vacations. Second, tourism products such as rafting,
hot springs, camping, and homestays have already
developed a certain level of market recognition, enabling
Qingyuan to serve weekend tourism, parent –child tourism,

self-driving tourism, and short -distance vacation demand
from the Guangdong–Hong Kong–Macao Greater Bay Area.
Third, areas such as Liannan and Lianshan possess ethnic
cultural resources associated with Yao and Zhuang
communities, which can support ethnic festivals, intangible
cultural heritage experiences, village tourism, and cultural
study tours. Fourth, local agricultural products such as
Qingyuan chicken, Yingde black tea, and Lianzhou
flowering cabbage have a strong basis for consumption
conversion and can be integrated with tourism shopping,
culinary experiences, and cultural and creative product
development. Fifth, Qingyuan is adjacent to major Pearl
River Delta cities such as Guangzhou, Foshan, Shenzhen,
and Dongguan, giving it a favorable location for developing
regional leisure consumption.
These resources provide the foundation for innovation in
Qingyuan’s all -for-one tourism consumption scenarios.
Unlike single-attraction destinations, Qingyuan’s advantage
lies in the combinatory potential among ecology, rural
space, culture, wellness, sports, and agricultural products.
The intervention of AI can further improve the connection
efficiency among these resources, transforming dispersed
tourism sites into continuous consumption scenarios.

3.2 Practical Problems in Qingyuan’s All -for-One
Tourism Consumption Scenarios
First, consumption scenarios remain dispersed, and scenario
linkage is insufficient. Qingyuan has a wide range of
tourism resources distributed across different areas, yet
scenic spots, villages, homestays, catering businesses,
cultural and creative products, agricultural products, and
transportation services have not yet formed sufficient
linkages. Tourists often complete short -duration
consumption around a single tourism product, while cross -
regional and cross -sector consumption loops remain
underdeveloped.
Second, cultural expression in tourism products remains
insufficient, and local distinctiveness is not yet strong
enough. Some tourism products still remain at the level of
natural sightseeing, photo -taking, and basic leisure. The
transformation of Qingyuan’s local culture, ethnic culture,
ecological culture, agricultural culture, and culinary culture
into distinctive tourism experiences remains inadequate. As
a result, many consumption scenarios lack memorable
symbols and communicative appeal. Studies on the digital
economy have similarly pointed out that cultural tourism
integration often faces problems such as insufficient
exploration of regional culture, lack of product uniqueness,
and weak experiential and interactive qualities.
Third, digital service capacity remains limited. Some
tourism scenarios in Qingyuan still have room for
improvement in smart guiding, intelligent recommendation,
smart customer service, visitor flow prediction, smart
parking, and data analysis. Tourists may still face problems
such as insufficient information, high decision -making
costs, and delayed service responses in pre-trip planning, in-
trip navigation, catering and accommodation selection, and
post-trip feedback.
Fourth, high value -added consumption scenarios are
insufficiently developed. Tourist expenditure remains
concentrated on basic consumption items such as entrance
tickets, transportation, catering, and accommodation.
Consumption potential in night -time tourism, cultural and
creative shopping, wellness courses, study -tour products,
digital experiences, and post -trip repurchase has not yet
been fully released.
Fifth, tourist data utilization remains inadequate. Tourism
data are dispersed across scenic areas, platforms, hotels,
homestays, catering businesses, transportation services, and
social media platforms. A unified system for data analysis
and tourist profiling has not yet been fully established,
which constrains precise marketing, product matching, and
consumption conversion.

4. Mechanisms of AI -Enabled Innovation in Qingyuan’s
All-for-One Tourism Consumption Scenarios
4.1 Intelligent Perception: From Traffic Statistics to
Demand Insight
AI first enables insight into tourist demand through data
collection and intelligent analysis. Qingyuan can use search
data, booking data, consumption data, location data,
evaluation data, and social media content to identify the
travel motivations, consumption capacity, preference
structures, and experiential needs of different tourist groups.
For example, young tourists from the Greater Bay Area may
be more interested in rafting, camping, night -time social
activities, and short -video check -ins. Parent –child families
may pay more attention to study tours, safety, convenient
accommodation, and nature education. Older tourists may
be more concerned with hot -spring wellness, slow travel
routes, and age -friendly services. Culturally motivated
tourists may be interested in Yao culture, intangible cultural
heritage experiences, and local cuisine. Through intelligent
perception, Qingyuan’s all -for-one tourism can move from
experience-based operation toward data-driven operation.

4.2 Intelligent Recommendation: From Single -Point
Push to All-for-One Matching
AI can automatically match Qingyuan’s tourism products
and route combinations according to tourist profiles, travel
time, budget level, companion type, and interest
preferences. Research on digital cultural tourism suggests
that big data technologies can collect tourists’ consumption
preferences, behavioral data, and social media interactions,
and use such information to adjust marketing strategies and
improve marketing conversion rates.
In the case of Qingyuan, intelligent recommendation can
combine rafting, camping, and night -time barbecue into
youth-oriented outdoor routes; hot springs, forest slow
travel, and healthy cuisine into wellness routes; ethnic
villages, intangible cultural heritage experiences, distinctive
catering, and cultural and creative shopping into cultural
experience routes; and agricultural experiences, nature
education, and rural homestays into parent –child study-tour
routes. In this way, tourists are no longer simply directed to
a single scenic spot; instead, they are guided into a
continuous consumption network.

4.3 Intelligent Interaction: From Passive Service to Real-
Time Companionship
AI can enhance tourist interaction through smart customer
service, AI tour guides, voice interpretation, digital humans,
intelligent question answering, and smart navigation. Smart
customer service can respond to tourist inquiries around the
clock, while intelligent guiding can provide route guidance,
scenic interpretation, queue reminders, parking guidance,
and safety warnings. Previous studies have noted that the
application of AI in cultural tourism is mainly reflected in

For Qingyuan’s all -for-one tourism, the value of intelligent

appropriate moments. For instance, after tourists complete a
rafting experience, the system may recommend nearby

4.4 Intelligent Generation: From Destination
Communication to Content Co-Creation
AIGC provides a new mechanism for cultural tourism
content production in Qingyuan. By generating short -video
scripts, travel guides, route posters, digital interpretation
texts, virtual travel notes, and personalized commemorative
content, Qingyuan can improve the efficiency of cultural
tourism communication and incorporate tourists into the
process of content co-creation.
Centered on key local symbols such as Qingyuan chicken,
Yingde black tea, rafting, hot springs, Yao culture, and rural
homestays, AIGC can generate serial and thematic
communication content. This can shift Qingyuan’s tourism
image from single -resource promotion toward narrative -
based, personalized, and socialized expression. Tourists can
also use AI tools to generate personal travel notes, travel
posters, and short -video scripts, thereby forming a content
conversion chain of “experience –expression–sharing–re-
dissemination.”

4.5 Immersive Experience: From Viewing Landscapes to
Entering Scenarios
The combination of AI with VR/AR, digital twins,
holographic projection, and interactive imaging can
transform Qingyuan’s tourism experience from static
sightseeing to immersive participation. Relevant research
suggests that VR, AR, and MR technologies can expand the
space for experiential and immersive cultural tourism,
generate new forms of consumption, and transform tourists
from simple passers-by into participants in tourism stories.
Qingyuan can develop immersive digital performances
around Yao culture, digital image spaces around the
Beijiang ecological landscape, digital restoration displays
around ancient villages, safety simulation experiences
around rafting, and light -and-shadow performances and
interactive check -in spaces around night -time tourism.
Immersive experiences can increase tourist stay time,
participation depth, and consumption value added.

4.6 Data Feedback: From Experience -Based
Management to Dynamic Optimization
AI can analyze tourist comments, complaints, social media
posts, platform ratings, and consumption behavior to
identify tourist satisfaction points and pain points. Through
natural language processing and sentiment analysis,
problems such as parking difficulties, long queues, poor
service, high prices, and weak experiences can be identified
in a timely manner and fed back to managers and operators.
As a result, Qingyuan’s all -for-one tourism can form a
closed-loop mechanism of “data analysis –problem
identification–product adjustment –service optimization –
consumption enhancement.” Consumption scenarios are no
longer the result of one -off design; rather, they become
dynamic systems that are continuously iterated through
tourist feedback and data analysis.

5. Practical Pathways for Enhancing Consumption
Conversion in Qingyuan’s All -for-One Tourism through
AI
5.1 Enhancing Precision Marketing Conversion through
Tourist Profiling
Qingyuan can form segmented tourist profiles based on
tourist data, distinguishing among Greater Bay Area
weekend tourists, parent –child study -tour tourists, young
outdoor tourists, senior wellness tourists, ethnic culture
experience tourists, self -driving camping tourists, and
culinary shopping tourists. Different groups correspond to
different consumption motivations and conversion
pathways.
For young tourists, rafting, camping, night -time
consumption, and social media check -ins can be
emphasized. For parent –child tourists, nature education,
rural study tours, and safety services can be highlighted. For
senior tourists, hot -spring wellness, slow travel routes, and
age-friendly experiences can be prioritized. For culturally
motivated tourists, Yao culture, intangible cultural heritage
experiences, and local cuisine can be foregrounded. The key
to precision marketing is not to expand promotional
coverage indiscriminately, but to improve the matching
efficiency between products and demand.

5.2 Extending the Consumption Chain through All -for-
One Route Design
Qingyuan can transform dispersed resources into composite
route products and guide tourists from single -point
sightseeing to multi -point consumption. Potential routes
include ecological wellness routes, outdoor sports routes,
ethnic culture routes, rural study -tour routes, night -time
consumption routes, and culinary-cultural creative routes.
Ecological wellness routes can integrate forests, hot springs,
homestays, and healthy cuisine. Outdoor sports routes can
integrate rafting, hiking, camping, and night -time barbecue.
Ethnic culture routes can integrate Yao villages, intangible
cultural heritage experiences, ethnic festivals, and cultural
and creative shopping. Rural study -tour routes can integrate
agricultural experiences, nature education, parent –child
courses, and rural homestays. Night -time consumption
routes can integrate Beijiang  night tours, night markets,
light-and-shadow performances, and culinary consumption.
Culinary-cultural creative routes can integrate Qingyuan
chicken, Yingde black tea, local agricultural products, and
souvenirs.

5.3 Enhancing In -Trip Consumption Conversion
through Smart Services
Qingyuan can treat smart services as key nodes of
consumption conversion rather than merely as information -
query interfaces. A smart service system can cover
intelligent question answering, route planning, ticket
reservation, accommodation recommendation, catering
recommendation, parking navigation, visitor flow warnings,
and complaint feedback.
During the tourist journey, the system can provide scenario -
based recommendations according to real-time location, stay

5.4 Improving Communication Conversion through
AIGC Content Marketing
Qingyuan can use AIGC to construct a mechanism for
continuous content production, generating communication

Yingde black tea, rafting, hot springs, Yao culture, and rural

Specifically, AIGC can generate short-video scripts, graphic

5.5 Increasing Value -Added Consumption through
Immersive Experiences
Qingyuan can transform ethnic culture, ecological culture,
rural culture, and night -time consumption into participatory
immersive experience products. Examples include Yao
culture immersive experience halls, intangible cultural
heritage interactive workshops, Beijiang ecological digital
exhibitions, ancient village digital restoration spaces, night -
time light -and-shadow performances, and AI -based nature
education courses.
The purpose of immersive experience is not simply to create
visual spectacles. Rather, it is to enhance tourist stay time
and consumption willingness through narrative, interaction,
and emotional value. Only when technology is deeply
integrated with local culture can immersive experience
avoid becoming hollow or homogeneous.

5.6 Expanding Post-Trip Consumption through Cultural
and Creative Products and Agricultural Product
Transformation
Qingyuan can extend tourism consumption from on -site
experience to post-trip purchase. Based on elements such as
Qingyuan chicken, Yingde black tea, Lianzhou flowering
cabbage, Yao patterns, rafting culture, and hot -spring
wellness, cultural and creative products, agricultural product
gift boxes, digital souvenirs, and personalized customized
goods can be developed.
AI can be used for cultural and creative pattern generation,
packaging design, IP image design, and personalized
product recommendation. In this way, tourists can transform
the “Qingyuan experience” into consumable, shareable, and
repurchasable products. Cultural and creative products and
agricultural products are no longer merely tourism
accessories; they become important extensions of
Qingyuan’s tourism consumption chain.

6. Supporting Conditions and Optimization Suggestions
6.1 Improving Digital Infrastructure
AI-enabled all -for-one tourism requires stable digital
infrastructure. Qingyuan can strengthen network coverage,
smart parking, electronic ticketing, intelligent guiding,
visitor flow sensing, and safety monitoring in key scenic
areas, rural tourism sites, homestay clusters, transportation
nodes, and night -time consumption spaces. Insufficient
digital infrastructure may constrain the deep application of
digital technologies in cultural tourism consumption
scenarios, resulting in unsmooth service experiences and
limited release of technological value.

6.2 Strengthening Cultural Tourism Data Governance
Qingyuan’s all -for-one tourism needs to break down data
barriers among governments, scenic areas, platforms, hotels,
homestays, catering businesses, transportation services, and
social media. Unified data standards and sharing
mechanisms should be established. At the same time, tourist
privacy protection, algorithmic transparency, and data
security governance need to be strengthened to avoid
excessive data collection, algorithmic manipulation,
discriminatory pricing, and false recommendation.

6.3 Enhancing the Digital Capacity of Cultural Tourism
Operators
Among Qingyuan’s cultural tourism operators, small and
medium-sized scenic areas, homestays, restaurants,
agricultural product enterprises, and rural tourism
businesses account for a relatively high proportion. Their
digital capacity directly affects the effectiveness of AI
applications. Therefore, it is necessary to enhance operators’
capabilities in AI tool use, digital marketing, content
production, platform operation, and tourist evaluation
analysis, so that digital capacity can extend from a limited
number of platforms to a broader range of cultural tourism
actors.

6.4 Strengthening the Transformation of Local Cultural
Content
AI cannot replace local culture itself. Qingyuan needs to
further explore Yao culture, tea culture, culinary culture,
ecological culture, rural culture, and red culture, and
transform them into content that can be experienced,
consumed, and communicated. Only when local culture
remains at the center can AI avoid becoming a superficial
technological ornament and genuinely serve Qingyuan’s
differentiated tourism expression.

6.5 Improving Consumer Rights Protection
With the increase in intelligent recommendation, online
booking, and digital consumption, consumer rights
protection has become an important condition for the
development of Qingyuan’s digital cultural tourism. Digital
cultural tourism research suggests that consumers’ rights to
information, fair transaction, and choice should be clearly
defined; diversified complaint channels should be
developed; a credit system for cultural tourism enterprises
should be established; and social media platforms should be
used to enhance consumers’ risk awareness and rights -
protection capacity. In promoting AI -based cultural tourism
applications, Qingyuan should also improve mechanisms
related to price transparency, complaint handling, review
supervision, data protection, and risk notification.

6.6 Addressing the Adaptive Needs of Different Tourist
Groups
AI-enabled all -for-one tourism in Qingyuan should not
serve only young tourists; it should also consider the needs
of parent –child families, middle -aged tourists, and older
tourists. Young tourists value social communication and
emotional experience; parent–child tourists value safety and
educational functions; middle -aged tourists value quality
and cost performance; and older tourists value convenience
and age-friendly services. These differences determine that
AI services cannot adopt a single model, but should instead
provide stratified and adaptive supply.

7. Conclusion
Taking Qingyuan’s all -for-one tourism as a case, this study
analyzes the theoretical logic, practical problems, operating
mechanisms, and practical pathways of AI -enabled
consumption scenario innovation and conversion
enhancement. The study argues that the significance of AI
for Qingyuan’s all -for-one tourism lies not only in
improving marketing efficiency and service convenience,
but more importantly in promoting the transition of
Qingyuan tourism from resource -driven development to
scenario-driven development and from traffic attraction to
value conversion through tourist profiling, intelligent
recommendation, smart services, AIGC content generation,
immersive experience, and data feedback.
Qingyuan possesses diverse resources, including ecological
landscapes, hot-spring wellness, rafting sports, rural leisure,
ethnic culture, and characteristic agricultural products.
However, it also faces problems such as dispersed
consumption scenarios, insufficient cultural expression,
limited digital service capacity, inadequate tourist data
utilization, and relatively short consumption chains. AI can
reconstruct Qingyuan’s all -for-one tourism consumption
scenarios through demand identification, product matching,
service optimization, content co -creation, experience
upgrading, and feedback analysis, thereby increasing tourist
stay time, comprehensive consumption capacity, and
willingness to revisit and share.
Therefore, the future development of Qingyuan’s all-for-one
tourism does not depend simply on the introduction of
intelligent devices or digital platforms. Rather, it depends on
embedding AI into the entire process of tourist consumption
behavior: identifying tourists through data, responding to
demand through scenarios, promoting consumption through
experience, optimizing supply through feedback, and
ultimately realizing the transformation from tourist arrival to
value conversion.
