Abstract: Against the backdrop of global carbon neutrality, China dominates the global new energy
vehicle (NEV) market, yet existing service quality research often applies the classic SERVQUAL scale
without adapting it to NEV industrial scenarios, leaving a gap in i ntegrated frameworks that explain
consumer behavioral intentions in NEV service settings. This study addresses this gap by constructing
a localized, five -dimensional SERVQUAL service quality model for China's new energy vehicle
aftersales and charging service scenarios and examining the parallel mediating roles of perceived value
and customer satisfaction in the relationship between service quality and behavioural intention.
Convenience online sampling combined with city -tier stratified distribution was use d, and 302 valid
questionnaires from Chinese NEV users aged 18 and older with actual vehicle usage experience were
collected; SPSS 26.0 and AMOS 24.0 were used for exploratory factor analysis, confirmatory factor
analysis, structural equation modeling (SEM ) and mediation analysis. Results show that reliability,
responsiveness, assurance, tangibility, and empathy are positively associated with perceived value
(β=.140~.284, p<.01) and satisfaction (β=.112~.361, p<.05), with tangibility and empathy showing the
largest effects on satisfaction. Perceived value (β=.467, p<.001) and satisfaction (β =.574, p<.001) play
complementary mediating roles in the relationship between service quality and behavioral intention.
This study theoretically expands SERVQUAL's application boundary by revising measurement items to
match full-lifecycle NEV consumption scenarios. It empirically offers targeted service optimization
strategies for NEV enterprises and policy suggestions for regulators on carbon reduction. Limitations
include convenience sampling, which limits sample representativeness, and the exclusion of industry -
specific moderating variables such as regional charging infrastructure coverage; future research can add
industry contextual factors as moderators and adopt stra tified random sampling for broader
generalization.

1. Introduction
Amid global climate change and the ongoing transition to sustainable development, reducing carbon
emissions in the transportation sector has become a core priority for countries worldwide. The rapid
expansion of new energy vehicles (NEVs) offers a reliable path for the automotive industry to  realize
low-carbon transformation and sustainable operation[1]. As the world’s largest NEV consumption and
manufacturing market, China has kept its leading position in annual output and sales volume. Data
released by the China Association of Automobile Ma nufacturers (2025) shows that China’s NEV
production and sales volume both surpassed 12.8 million units in 2024, topping global rankings for ten
straight years[2]. At the same time, the domestic market penetration rate of new energy vehicles reached
40.9%, reflecting continued expansion in the low-carbon vehicle market[3].
As the NEV market shifts from a policy-driven to a market-driven stage, consumer demand is moving
from a focus on product functionality to a more comprehensive evaluation of service experiences across
the entire consumption lifecycle[4]. In this evolving context, service quality is positively associated with
consumer satisfaction and behavioral intention, particularly in promoting sustainable consumption
behaviors. Existing au tomotive-industry research confirms that after -sales service, charging support
facilities, and personalized service experiences relate to users ’ follow-up consumption decisions and
willingness to recommend the brand.
Despite abundant research on NEV consum er decision-making, three clear research gaps remain in
the current literature. First, most existing papers researching NEV service quality directly copy the
original standard SERVQUAL scale without targeted revision for charging stations, battery maintenance
and full -cycle aftersales scenarios; scholars seldom build service evaluation systems matching the
unique industrial characteristics of new energy vehicles, and previous studies separate aftersales service
assessment and low-carbon vehicle market development into two unrelated research directions.
Second, existing empirical research mostly focuses on bivariate relationships between a single service
dimension and consumer outcomes, failing to simultaneously compare the relative impact of all five
SERVQUAL dimensions or quantitatively decompose the sequential mediation paths.
Third, domestic NEV-related research mostly focuses on vehicle hardware performance, purchase
subsidy policies, and charging infrastructure construction; few papers systematically di scuss full -
lifecycle after-sales service systems and the internal transmission logic that affects users' long -term
repurchase and brand recommendation behaviors. Based on the above research deficiencies, this study
revises the five -dimension SERVQUAL scale  for China's NEV market to build an integrated
multidimensional mediation model, quantifies the relative influence degree of each service dimension,
and explains how complete service experience predicts stable sustainable consumption intentions, filling
the theoretical and empirical gap in existing low-carbon vehicle service research.
Although existing studies on NEVs have largely focused on technological innovation, policy
incentives, and market expansion, they have paid relatively little attention to the continuous influence
of service quality on users' consumption decisions. In particular, scholars rarely combine classic service
quality evaluation tools with low -carbon automobile market characteristics to carry out systematic
empirical testing.
Specifically, this study pursues the following three objectives to fill the above research gaps: (1) to
examine the relative importance of different service quality dimensions as perceived by NEV consumers
and compare their differentiated effects on cognitive percei ved value and affective satisfaction; (2) to
analyze the chained mediation pathways through which multidimensional service quality predicts pro-
environmental behavioral intention via perceived value and satisfaction from the industrial service
operation perspective; (3) to extend the application of SERVQUAL theory by  revising scale items to
adapt to NEV full-cycle service scenarios, thereby enriching the theoretical understanding of consumer
behavioral intention in the NEV sector and providing measurable em pirical evidence for subsequent

Compared with existing literature that replicates the SERVQUAL framework in traditional fuel

2. Theoretical Background
2.1 Service Quality Theory
The SERVQUAL model, originally proposed by Parasuraman et al(1988), conceptualizes service
quality as a multidimensional construct comprising tangibility, reliability, responsiveness, assu rance,
and empathy. This framework has been widely applied across various service industries to evaluate the
gap between customer expectations and perceived performance[5].
In the context of new energy vehicles (NEVs), applying the SERVQUAL model to after-sales services
and charging environments provides a structured approach to measuring service quality in emerging
green mobility systems. As the NEV industry rapidly develops, consumers' expectations have diversified
and now extend beyond product performance to the service experience.
This study adapts the SERVQUAL dimensions to the NEV context. Specifically, tangibility refers to
the physical condition of charging infrastructure and service facilities; reliability denotes the ability of
service providers to deliver consistent and dependable services; responsiveness reflects the promptness
and efficiency of service responses; assurance relates to the competence and credibility conveyed by
service personnel; and empathy captures the extent to which service pro viders understand and address
individual customer needs[6][7].
By contextualizing the SERVQUAL model within the NEV sector, this study not only evaluates
service quality but also establishes a clear analytical framework for exploring how service resources
relate to users' follow-up consumption choices in the low-carbon vehicle market.

2.2 Characteristics of Service Quality
Service quality is commonly understood as the discrepancy between customer expectations and actual
service performance. Previous resear ch defined service quality as the outcome of this comparison,
emphasizing the importance of reducing expectation-performance gaps[7]. In highly competitive service
environments, consumers play a central role in evaluating service quality, making their perc eptions
critical to firm success[8].
The SERVQUAL model further operationalizes service quality into five measurable dimensions,
widely validated across industries such as healthcare, banking, tourism, and online services[9][10].
However, most existing aut omobile industry research uses the original dimension definitions without
targeted adjustments for new energy vehicle charging and maintenance links, which reduces the

accuracy of evaluation results. In the NEV context, these dimensions can be re -interpreted to fit
industrial features as follows:
Reliability: The ability to deliver promised services accurately and consistently, such as ensuring
maintenance quality and minimizing service errors[6][11].
Responsiveness: The willingness and capability to provide timely and effective service support, which
is positively associated with customer evaluations and satisfaction[12]. More complete resource
provisioning by firms correlates with higher final service quality[7].
Assurance: A core element of service quality that is related to customer satisfaction[13]. Professional
knowledge, courteous behavior, and credibility displayed by staff foster customer trust and strengthen
confidence in services[14][15].
Tangibility: Physical facilities, modernization of equipment, and staff appearance. Good tangibility
provides a comfortable service environment and positively influences employees’ Responsiveness

Empathy: The degree to which service providers recognize individualized customer needs and
respond emotionally. High -quality personalized service (e.g., voluntary apologies, individualized
attention) is associated with increased satisfaction; lack of empathy may co rrelate with
dissatisfaction[6][13].
The five dimensions of service quality jointly determine NEV users' overall service experience, and
favourable service conditions relate to users' continued consumption and brand recommendation
behaviors within the low-carbon vehicle market.

2.3 Perceived Value
Perceived value refers to a consumer's overall assessment of a product or service's utility based on
perceptions of what is received and what is given[18]. It is the benefit that a customer perceives relative
to the total costs incurred to obtain the service[19]. Consumers evaluate value by comparing satisfaction
gained against costs paid, a concept particularly pertinent when assessing complex products such as
NEVs.
Xu Huimin (2024) decomposed perceived value for NEVs into four dimensions —functional value,
experience value, economic value, and social value —corresponding to evaluations of vehicle
practicality, affective experience, cost -effectiveness, and social recognition. For example, functional
value concerns vehicle performance and practicality; experience value reflects comprehensive feelings
formed during long -term vehicle usage; economic value relates to cost efficiency; and social value
reflects social status or recognition derived from vehicle ownership[20].
Kotler & Keller (2016) argued that perceived value includes not only subjective perceptions but also
actual economic value, which plays an important role when consumers weigh monetary expenditure
against the product’s utility[20]. In the NEV context, consumers’ assessments of price-performance and
economic efficiency are associated with purchase decisions. Thus, this study analyzes how perceived
value predicts NEV consumers’ purchasing decisions and derives insights to inform NEV marketing and
service strategies[21].

2.4 Customer Satisfaction
Customer satisfaction is defined as a consumer’s affective response resulting from the comparison
between perceived performance and prior expectations[19]. It represents a cumulative evaluation of the
consumption experience and plays a central role in shaping future behavioral tendencies[22][23].
Extensive research has demonstrated that service quality is positively associated with customer
satisfaction[7]. When service performance meets or exceeds expectations, consumers are more likely to

In the context of NEVs, satisfaction is influenced not only by vehicle hardware performance but also

2.5 Behavioral Intention
Behavioral intentions represent a consumer’s propensity to perform a future behavior and are a
primary predictor of actual behavior in service management research[26]. Behavioral intention is the
subjective manifestation of attitudes and beliefs as be havioral likelihood following satisfaction[27].
Behavioral intentions can be classified as favorable or unfavorable; favorable behaviors include positive
word-of-mouth, repurchase intention, loyalty, and long -term relationship maintenance[28]. Word -of-
mouth involves communicating positive or negative information among two or more individuals[29].
Positive word-of-mouth is an important driver of corporate promotion, whereas negative word-of-mouth
can generate distrust toward a product or brand[30]. Thus, con sumers’ behavioral intentions predict
whether they will repurchase or recommend products and serve as critical indicators for firm
strategy[31]. In the NEV market, repurchase intention and word-of-mouth recommendations are crucial
for market-share expansion: higher satisfaction is positively associated with repurchase intentions and
the likelihood of recommending to others, thereby correlating with corporate-growth prospects.
Empirical research by Xu Huimin (2024) indicates that satisfaction can predict pos itive behavioral
tendencies. This study therefore analyzes the effect of satisfaction on NEV consumers' behavioral
intentions and derives strategies to raise customer satisfaction and promote repurchase and word -of-
mouth recommendations[11].
Stable behavioral intention is essential for the sustainable growth of the NEV market, and clarifying
the driving factors of such intentions can help enterprises and regulators jointly expand sustainable
vehicle consumption.

2.6 Contrastive Analysis of Existing Research Paradigms for NEV Service Quality Evaluation
Two distinct research paradigms exist in current low -carbon vehicle consumption research: the
SERVQUAL service quality evaluation framework and research focusing on low -carbon market
development characteristics, and most existing academic papers adopt only one paradigm for empirical
testing, resulting in inconsistent research conclusions and a lack of cross-system comparative analysis.
First, classic SERVQUAL gap theory centres on comparing users' servic e expectations with actual
service experience to explain the logic of brand satisfaction formation. However, most early applications
of this model do not account for the unique supporting service attributes of new energy vehicles, such
as charging piles an d battery maintenance, so the evaluation system cannot reflect the actual service
operation status of NEV brands. In contrast, existing research on low -carbon vehicle market
development mostly discusses macro policy and industrial infrastructure factors. I t rarely incorporates
full-cycle after-sales service experience into the research framework, which reduces its explanatory
power for individual user consumption decisions.
Second, existing empirical literature presents inconsistent conclusions about the relative influence of
each service dimension. Some traditional fuel-vehicle service studies find responsiveness the strongest
correlate of satisfaction. At the same time, research on low -carbon consumer goods focuses more on
physical-supporting facilities and personalized-customized-service links. These divergent results arise
because scholars use separate research frameworks without building a unified measurement model that

reliability, responsiveness, assurance, tangibility, and empathy—and their effects on perceived value and

with the unique service links of the new energy vehicle industry such as charging station operation and
battery maintenance, this paper co nstructs the research model displayed in [Fig. 1]. By analyzing the
path relationships in the model, this research offers theoretical supplements and practical service

3.2 Measurement of Variables and Analytical Methods
3.2.1 Operational Definitions and Measurement
This study examines four core constructs: service quality, perceived value, customer satisfaction, and
behavioral intention. The questionnaire-scale development workflow: (1) Extract mature measurement
items from classic published English-language literature. (2) Complete forward-translation to Chinese
and back-translation to English to check semantic consistency. (3) Conduct a pilot test with 42 real NEV-
user respondents. Items with factor lo adings below  0.6 were eliminated. (4) Invite two automotive -
industry marketing practitioners and one management-major professor to review and revise ambiguous
items, delete content that involves subjective green psychological feelings, and finally form the formal
questionnaire to ensure content reliability and validity.  Service quality is defined as users’ overall
evaluation of brand service level and is measured using five dimensions: reliability, responsiveness,
assurance, tangibility, and empathy[29].
Perceived value refers to users’ comprehensive trade-off judgment between service gains and all input
costs[8]. Customer satisfaction refers to users' objective evaluation of the gap between pre-consumption
expectations and actual service performance[19] . Behavioral intention represents the likelihood that
users will engage in future consumption behaviours such as repurchase and brand recommendation[26].

5. Conclusion
This study examined the impact of service quality on consumers' perceived value, customer
satisfaction, and behavioral intention in the context of new energy vehicles (NEVs), based on the five
dimensions of the SERVQUAL model. Using structural equation modelling, this study provides
empirical evidence of how service quality influences consumer decision-making.
The results indicate that all five dimensions of service quality—reliability, responsiveness, assurance,
tangibility, and empathy —significantly and positively affect both perceived value and customer
satisfaction. Among these, reliability and empathy exert relatively stronger effects on perceived value,
whereas tangibility and empathy show g reater influence on customer satisfaction. These findings
highlight the importance of both functional performance and emotional interaction in shaping
consumers' service evaluations.
Furthermore, both perceived value and customer satisfaction significantly  enhance behavioral
intention, with customer satisfaction demonstrating a stronger effect. This suggests that consumers’
behavioral responses are influenced not only by cognitive evaluations of value but also by affective
experiences derived from service interactions.
The mediation analysis confirms that perceived value and customer satisfaction play significant,
complementary mediating roles in the relationship between service quality and behavioral intention.
This finding supports the proposed mechanism in which service quality is transformed into behavioral
intention through consumer evaluation processes.
This study contributes to the literature in three main aspects.
First, it extends the application of the SERVQUAL model to the NEV context by incorporating both
after-sales service and charging infrastructure, thereby broadening the scope of service quality research

in emerging green industries. Second, the study integrates service quality, perceived value, customer
satisfaction, and behavioral  intention into a unified structural model, showing that consumers'
evaluations—such as perceived value and satisfaction —serve as key mechanisms linking service
experience to behavioral intention.
Third, the study enriches the theoretical understanding of  consumer behavioral intention by
establishing a structured pathway from service quality to behavioral intention through mediating
mechanisms. This integrated framework helps bridge the gap between traditional service quality
research and sustainability-oriented consumer behaviour studies.
The findings provide several practical implications for both firms and policymakers.
First, NEV enterprises should prioritize improving service quality across multiple dimensions,
particularly reliability and empathy. Improving after-sales service systems and providing personalized
customer experiences can significantly increase perceived value and satisfaction.
Second, firms should strengthen trust -building mechanisms by improving transparency, service
consistency, and communication efficiency. Clear service standards and long-term service commitments
can turn positive perceptions into behavioral outcomes.
Third, governments and industry regulators should promote standardized green service systems,
including charging infrastructure, battery recycling, and environmental regulations. Such institutional
support can reinforce consumer confidence and facilitate the diffusion of sustainable mobility. Despite
its contributions, this study has several limitations. Convenience sampling may limit the generalizability
of the findings, and future studies should use more representative sampling methods.
In addition, this study focuses on the traditional SERVQUAL dimensions; however, with the rapid
development of NEV technologies, new service attributes such as digital services, intelligent platforms,
and data-driven service systems are becoming increasingly important. Future research could incorporate
these emerging factors to strengthen the model's explanatory power further.
Although procedural remedies were implemented to mitigate common method bias (CMB), Harman's
single-factor test revealed that the first factor accounted for more than 50% of the total variance.
Therefore, researchers cannot entirely rule out common method variance, and future studies should use
multiple data sources, temporal separation, or additional statistical techniques to address it.
Finally, future studies may deepen the integration of consumer trust, technology acceptance, and
service innovation by examining fac tors such as environmental awareness, social norms, and policy
perception, thereby providing a more comprehensive understanding of sustainable consumer behaviour.
In conclusion, this study provides both theoretical and practical insights into how service q uality
influences consumer behavior in the NEV context and offers meaningful implications for promoting
sustainable consumption and green mobility development.
