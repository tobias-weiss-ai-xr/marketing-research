ABSTRACT
Introduction
Indonesia’s subsidized three -kilogram liquefied petroleum gas
program is intended to protect household and microenterprise
access to affordable energy, yet distribution irregularities, localized
scarcity, price deviations, and uneven implementation of digi tal
subsidy governance continue to challenge its effectiveness. These
problems raise broader questions about how physical distribution
and provider -operated digital systems create customer value and
shape purchasing behavior in a regulated essential-energy market.
Objectives
This study examines the relationships of distribution, scarcity, and
consumer-perceived implementation of Merchant App MyPertamina
with three -kilogram liquefied petroleum gas purchase decisions. It
further investigates customer perceived value as an interv ening
mechanism linking these distributional and digital -governance
conditions with purchasing behavior.
Method
A quantitative explanatory cross -sectional design was employed
using data from 205 three -kilogram liquefied petroleum gas users
across all 23 districts of Langkat Regency, North Sumatra, Indonesia.
Respondents were selected through purposive sampling, and data
were collected from March to May 2026 using a five-point Likert-scale
questionnaire. The measurement and structural models were
evaluated using partial least squares structural equation modeling
with SmartPLS 4.0.

Results
Distribution was positively associated with both customer perceived
value and purchase decisions, while customer perceived value was
positively associated with purchase decisions. Consumer -perceived
implementation of Merchant App MyPertamina significantly improved
customer perceived value but showed no significant direct
relationship with purchase decisions. Scarcity did not significantly
influence customer perceived value, and its indirect relationship with
purchase decisions through customer perceived val ue was
unsupported. Customer perceived value partially mediated the
relationship between distribution and purchase decisions and fully
mediated the relationship between Merchant App MyPertamina
implementation and purchase decisions. The model explained 59. 4%
of the variance in purchase decisions and 51.5% of the variance in
customer perceived value.
Implications
The findings indicate that reliable physical distribution and consumer-
visible value creation are more important than digitalization alone in
shaping subsidized energy purchasing behavior. Digital subsidy
governance should therefore emphasize transparency,  fairness,
service clarity, and accountable targeting alongside reliable last-mile
supply.
Originality/Novelty
This study integrates physical distribution, scarcity, provider-operated
digital subsidy governance, customer perceived value, and purchase
behavior within one model and identifies customer perceived value as
the mechanism connecting distributional and dig ital-governance
conditions with consumer behavior in a subsidized essential -energy
market.

INTRODUCTION
Subsidized household energy is not merely a pricing intervention; it is a governance
instrument that determines whether vulnerable households can obtain essential
energy affordably, reliably, and on equitable terms. Across developing countries, the
welfare effects of energy subsidies depend less on expenditure alone than on targeting
quality, institutional effectiveness, transparency, and spatially sensitive delivery (Gobel
et al., 2024; Shittu et al., 2024). Indonesia’s 3-kg liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) program
illustrates this challenge. The government regulates its provision, distribution, and
pricing through Presidential Regulation No. 104 of 2007, as amended by Presidential
Regulation No. 70 of 2021, positioning LPG 3 kg as a subsidized commodity intended for

eligible households and microenterprises. Consequently, distribution performance has
direct implications for basic -needs fulfillment , microenterprise continuity, energy
access, and the credibility of social protection.
The scale of the program intensifies these governance pressures. National LPG 3 kg
distribution increased from 6.53 million metric tons in 2018 to 7.80 million in 2022, 8.04
million in 2023, and 8.23 million in 2024. International evidence similarly shows that
subsidized cooking-fuel programs remain vulnerable to unreliable supply, geographic
inequality, price noncompliance, leakage, and exclusion or inclusion errors (Gould et al.,
2024; Khanwilkar et al., 2021; Shupler et al., 2021; Wahyuni et al., 2026). From an Islamic
economics perspective, these are not only efficiency failures. Publicly subsidized
resources constitute an amanah that should be allocated according to ‘adl and
maslahah, while diversion, discriminatory access, and hoarding undermine distributive
justice and public welfare (Daipon et al., 2025; Handika et al., 2025; Parwati et al., 2025).
The central problem arises when adequate aggregate supply does not translate
into reliable last -mile access. Local scarcity can coexist with national availability
because of fragmented distribution, delayed deliveries, uneven outlet coverage, weak
supervision, strategic withholding, demand uncertainty, and opportunistic intermediary
behavior (Cohen et al., 2016 ; Ihle et al., 2020 ; Levi et al., 2022 ). Indonesia exhibits the
same paradox. An Ombudsman investigation found that 36.62% of LPG bases and
36.67% of retailers had experienced scarcity, while 50.85% of consumers reported
difficulty obtaining LPG 3 kg. Retail prices ranged from IDR 30,000 to IDR  70,000 per
cylinder, and 11.27% of bases sold above the official ceiling price. Such conditions
increase search costs, waiting time, price uncertainty, and perceived unfairness,
potentially weakening trust in a subsidy intended to protect vulnerable consumers.
Addressing this problem requires more than increasing aggregate supply. A
general solution must combine reliable physical distribution, accurate beneficiary
targeting, enforceable price compliance, transparent transactions, and data -based
supervision. Supply-chain governance emphasizes availability, timeliness, accessibility,
and coordination, whereas evidence on subsidized cooking fuels shows that reliable
delivery infrastructure and nearby distributors are critical for sustained use (Puzzolo et
al., 2019 ; Rao et al., 2020 ; Williams et al., 2025 ). Consumer responses also require
attention because scarcity does not operate uniformly across product categories.
Scarcity cues often increase purchase urgency, but for essential or utilitarian goods
they are more closely associated with anxiety, stockpil ing, and immediate need
fulfillment than with exclusivity -based desire (Barton et al., 2022 ; Cheng et al., 2023 ;
Omar et al., 2021 ). In Islamic economics, preventing artificial scarcity and protecting
access are consistent with the prohibition of ihtikar and the objective of preserving
public welfare (Handika et al., 2025).
Indonesia has increasingly pursued a more specific solution through transaction
digitalization using Merchant App MyPertamina, operationalized at LPG bases through
the Merchant Apps Pangkalan system. The system links LPG 3 kg transactions to citizens’
national identification numbers, records purchases, and supports beneficiary
verification. By March 11, 2024, the database contained 189.2 million identification

reporting, and monitoring, although exclusion, workarounds, privacy risks, and digital
divides can limit their effectiveness (Bird & Hanedar, 2023; Ganesh et al., 2024; Masiero

The effectiveness of digitalization therefore depends on whether technological and
service improvements generate value that citizens can perceive. The Technology
Acceptance Model emphasizes perceived usefulness and ease of use (Davis, 1989 ),
while the Information Systems Success Model highlights system, information, and
service quality as foundations of implementation (DeLone & McLean, 2003 ). Recent
evidence further indicates that digital technology may affect consumer behavior
indirectly because users evaluate the benefits, convenience, trust, and service
outcomes enabled by technology rather than the technological infrastructure itself
(Heller et al., 2021 ; Joo & Hwang, 2025 ; Sitangganga et al., 2025 ). Customer perceived
value provides an appropriate mechanism for this translation because it integrates
benefits and sacrifices and can include functional, economic, convenience, trust,
fairness, and overall-value judgments (Blut et al., 2024). In subsidized services, fairness
and trust also resonate with ‘adl and amanah without treating them as separately
measured constructs.
Previous LPG research has examined distribution, price, scarcity, service quality,
subsidy targeting, and application implementation, but these streams remain largely
fragmented. Kurniawan (2023) linked distribution, price, and scarcity to LPG purchasing
behavior, while Lestari et al. (2025) emphasized price, need, and scarcity. Akdyaputra &
Sudarman (2025) showed that Merchant Apps MyPertamina can improve
transparency, transaction recording, stock monitoring, and consumer verification, yet
implementation constraints remain. Broader structural models also tend to connect
only subsets of the relevant mechanis ms: policy and perceived value in new -energy
vehicles (Chen et al., 2019), digital public services and energy security at the macro level
(Ha, 2022 ), or e -service quality, perceived value, and purchase intention in digital
markets (Meiquan, 2025). Accordingly, the unresolved issue is how physical distribution,
perceived scarcity, provider -operated digital subsidy governance, and customer
perceived value jointly relate to consumer behavior in a regulated essential -energy
market.
Against this background, this study examines the relationships of distribution,
scarcity, and consumer-perceived implementation of Merchant App MyPertamina with
LPG 3 kg purchase decisions, both directly and through customer perceived value. The
proposed framework treats perceived value as the mechanism through which physical
access, scarcity experiences, and digital -governance conditions are translated into
economic, functional, convenience, trust, fairness, and overall -benefit evaluations
before behavioral responses emerge. Its contribution therefore lies not simply in
combining variables, but in testing whether customer perceived value connects

evaluating these relationships.
LITERATURE REVIEW
Distribution and Customer Perceived Value
Distribution creates customer value when it converts physical supply into reliable
access. In logistics and channel theory, availability, accessibility, delivery consistency,
and price conformity reduce the monetary and non -monetary sacrifices consumers
incur to obtain a product (Guliyev et al., 2024; Kazançoğlu et al., 2024; Wolny et al., 2026).
Recent evidence similarly shows that information gaps about availability increase
search costs, while irregular supply forces beneficiaries to seek products outside formal
channels, thereby weakening effective value (Atayev, 2022 ; Pragya, 2025 ). Reliable
distribution can also strengthen affordability and supply assurance through
coordinated procurement and inventory management (Yenet et al., 2023 ; Zhu et al.,
2023). In the LPG 3 kg context, smooth, geographically accessible, and rule -compliant
distribution should therefore increase perceived functional and economic benefits
(Andadari et al., 2020; Suliyono & Syafiuddin, 2025).
The value implications of distribution are especially important in subsidized
markets because consumers evaluate not only convenience but also fairness and
entitlement. Transparent and traceable distribution systems can strengthen perceived
fairness by red ucing information asymmetry and intermediary opportunism (Bala &
Kaur, 2022; Park & Li, 2021). Perceived price fairness, in turn, supports favorable purchase
evaluations, while procedural and distributive fairness can strengthen stakeholder trust
and embeddedness (Xu et al., 2024 ; Zhou et al., 2020 ). From an Islamic economics
perspective, reliable access and rule -compliant allocation are consistent with ‘adl,
amanah, and maslahah because public subsidies should reach eligible beneficiaries
without unjust exclusion or diversion. Accordingly, this study proposes that better LPG 3
kg distribution increases customer perceived value (H1).
Scarcity and Customer Perceived Value
Scarcity can increase perceived value because reduced availability makes a product
appear more important, desirable, or difficult to replace. Commodity theory and
scarcity research show that limited availability can heighten subjective valuation and
willingness to pay, particularly when scarcity signals strong demand or social
importance (Barton et al., 2022 ; Roux et al., 2023 ; Wrabel et al., 2022 ). For utilitarian
products, demand -based scarcity tends to be more influential than supply -based
scarcity because high demand signals widespread need rather than exclusivity (Das et
al., 2018; Hamilton & Hosany, 2023). In the LPG 3 kg market, limited stocks may therefore
increase the perceived importance of the product for household cooking and

microenterprise continuity, supporting the proposed positive relationship (Kusuma &
Dunan, 2026; Sanjaya et al., 2025; Yusriani & Patiro, 2025).
Nevertheless, the value effect of scarcity is not uniformly positive. Scarcity can
increase travel time, search effort, price uncertainty, frustration, and dependence on
informal sellers, all of which raise perceived sacrifice and may reduce overall value
(Herbon & Kogan, 2022 ; Tuncer et al., 2024 ). When scarcity appears strategically
created, consumers may infer manipulation and respond with reactance rather than
enhanced valuation (Gupta & Gentry, 2019 ). These concerns are especially salient for
subsidized necessities, where limited substitutes constrain consumer choice. In Islamic
economics, artificial scarcity caused by withholding or hoarding is inconsistent with the
prohibition of ihtikar because it harms welfare and distorts fair access (Karmaen, 2026;
Zelvian et al., 2025 ). Despite these countervailing costs, the scarcity -signaling
mechanism provides the basis for testing H2: scarcity positively influences customer
perceived value.
Merchant App MyPertamina Implementation and Customer Perceived Value
Consumer-perceived implementation of Merchant App MyPertamina can create value
when digital governance improves the transaction environment rather than merely
adding technology. Public -service research shows that system quality, information
quality, transparency, and perceived usefulness enhance citizens’ evaluations of digital
services by simplifying procedures and improving access to reliable information
(Chohan & Hu, 2020 ; Luna et al., 2024 ). Data accuracy and completeness also
strengthen trust in digital government, while digital transaction records can support
transparency and reduce opportunities for manipulation (Almuqrin et al., 2022; Sahur &
Amiruddin, 2023 ). Applied to LPG 3 kg, effective MAP implementation can make
transactions appear more orderly, accurate, traceable, and useful, thereby increasing
perceived benefits relative to the effort involved (Ilmi et al., 2026; J. M. Putra & Menorizah,
2025).
The value created by digital subsidy systems also depends on whether
beneficiaries perceive the process as fair and trustworthy. Digital verification and
electronic point-of-sale systems can reduce leakage and improve accountability, but
their benefits wea ken when systems are inaccessible, inaccurate, or difficult to use
(Nyaki et al., 2026 ; Ray & Chaudhary, 2026 ). Trust often mediates the conversion of
system and information quality into public value, suggesting that technological
performance alone is insufficient (Abdulkareem & Mohd Ramli, 2022). In Islamic terms,
accurate recording and transparent beneficiary verification can be interpreted as
institutional mechanisms supporting amanah and procedural ‘adl, although these
principles are not modeled as separate latent variables here (Abdurrahman, 2025; Fadil
et al., 2026; Nouruzzaman et al., 2026). Therefore, H3 proposes that stronger consumer-
perceived MAP implementation positively affects customer perceived value.

Distribution and LPG 3 Kg Purchase Decisions
Distribution performance can influence purchase decisions because consumers
cannot buy a product that is unavailable, inaccessible, or delivered too late. Last -mile
distribution studies show that product availability and delivery reliability are
foundational conditions for market access, particularly in essential -goods settings
(Adusei et al., 2025 ; Odo et al., 2024 ). Poor transport infrastructure, inconsistent
schedules, and information asymmetry can raise retail prices and interrupt timely
access, thereby constraining consumers’ ability to complete intended purchases
(Mulatu et al., 2026 ; Oluleye, 2025 ). Conversely, efficient logistics and reliable
warehousing maintain product presence at the point of need and support purchase
continuity (Pratiwi et al., 2025 ). For LPG 3 kg, accessible and dependable distribution
should facilitate purchasing when household or business demand arises (Maidiana &
Hidayat, 2021; Octaviani et al., 2023).
This relationship is particularly consequential in a regulated subsidy system
because distribution quality also determines whether eligible consumers can purchase
through official channels at regulated prices. Reliable supply reduces the need to
search informal outlets, limits uncertainty about stock, and lowers the risk of paying
above the official price. Timeliness and consistency can therefore convert policy
entitlement into practical purchasing opportunity. From a welfare perspective, such
access reflects more than logistical efficiency: it concerns the equitable realization of a
public subsidy. Islamic distributive justice similarly requires that essential subsidized
goods remain accessible to rightful beneficiaries rather than being diverted toward
higher-paying or better-connected users (Muhit et al., 2023 ; Mulyany & Furqani, 2019 ;
Rezeki et al., 2023). Based on the accessibility mechanism and prior empirical evidence,
H4 proposes that distribution positively influences LPG 3 kg purchase decisions.
Scarcity and LPG 3 Kg Purchase Decisions
Scarcity is expected to influence purchase decisions because limited availability can
create urgency and fear of future unavailability. Meta-analytic evidence indicates that
scarcity cues generally increase purchase intentions, although effects vary by product
type and scarcity source (Barton et al., 2022 ; Ladeira et al., 2023 ). For essential and
utilitarian goods, demand-based scarcity is particularly relevant because consumers
interpret heavy demand as evidence of widespread need (Shi et al., 2020 ). Crisis
research also shows that perceived scarcity can generate anxiety, fear of missing out,
and panic buying, encouraging consumers to purchase earlier or in larger quantities
when products become available (Ahmad et al., 2025 ; Omar et al., 2021 ). These
mechanisms support the positive scarcity –purchase relationship proposed for LPG 3
kg.
However, necessity-driven consumption can also weaken the conventional scarcity
effect. Financially constrained consumers may be unable to increase purchases even
when scarcity is salient, while regulated prices and limited substitutes can make
purchasing largely need-driven rather than desirability-driven (De Bruijn & Antonides,
2022; Herbon & Kogan, 2022). Scarcity may also induce anger, switching, or withdrawal

subsidized LPG, these boundary conditions are theoretically important because

consumption. Islamic economics further treats artificial scarcity and hoarding as
welfare-reducing distortions. Nevertheless, because urgency remains the dominant
directional mechanism in prior scarcity research, H5 tests whether scarcity positively
influences LPG 3 kg purchase decisions.
Merchant App MyPertamina Implementation and LPG 3 Kg Purchase Decisions
Digital subsidy systems can affect purchase decisions when they improve the reliability
and legitimacy of official -channel transactions. Research on digital public services
indicates that transparency, information quality, and trust shape citizens’ willingness to
use formal digital channels (Morić et al., 2024 ; Venkatesh et al., 2016 ). Identity
verification can improve service inclusion, while transaction recording provides an
auditable trail that may strengthen confidence in service delivery (Addo & Senyo, 2021;
Khorana et al., 2024). In the LPG 3 kg context, consumer-perceived MAP implementation
may therefore support purchasing through official bases by making beneficiary
verification, transaction procedures, and subsidy rules clearer and more predictable.
This reasoning is consisten t with prior work linking digital service effectiveness to
purchasing behavior (Abdillahi, 2026; Adi & Supriyanto, 2025).
The expected direct effect should nevertheless be interpreted cautiously because
MAP is operated primarily by LPG bases rather than by consumers themselves. Provider-
operated technology often shapes behavior through the service outcomes it enables,
not through direct consumer interaction with the underlying system. Transparency may
build trust, but privacy concerns, false verification, or cumbersome procedures can
offset these benefits (Karwatzki et al., 2017 ; Theodorakopoulos et al., 2026 ). Moreover,
digital subsidy design can influence behavior through price and access mechanisms
rather than through technology exposure alone (Liu et al., 2025 ). From an Islamic
governance perspective, digital records can support amanah and market supervision
when they improve accountability. Accordingly, H6 tests whether stronger consumer -
perceived MAP implementation positively influences LPG 3 kg purchase decisions.
Customer Perceived Value and LPG 3 Kg Purchase Decisions
Customer perceived value explains purchase decisions by integrating what consumers
receive with what they sacrifice. Zeithaml (1988) conceptualizes value as an overall
utility judgment based on benefits and costs, while later multidimensional approaches
distinguish functional, economic, emotional, social, and contextual forms of value
(Sweeney & Soutar, 2001 ). A meta -analysis of 687 studies confirms that models
incorporating benefits, sacrifices, and overall value are especially effective in predicting
satisfaction and repurchase outcomes (Blut et al., 2024 ). In essential-goods contexts,
functional utility and economic value are particularly salient because consumers
prioritize reliability, affordability, and fit with everyday needs (Luo et al., 2022 ). Thus,

consumers should be more willing to purchase LPG 3 kg when its perceived benefits
outweigh monetary and non-monetary sacrifices.
For subsidized public services, perceived value also includes convenience, trust,
fairness, and legitimacy. Time and effort can weigh heavily in value assessments,
sometimes more than price itself, while confidence benefits and institutional legitimacy
support favorable behavioral intentions (Blanco-González et al., 2025 ; Del Castillo
Puente & Dopico, 2026 ; Gremler et al., 2020 ). Fairness is particularly relevant when
access depends on eligibility rules, regulated prices, and official distribution channels.
In Islamic economics, a transaction perceived as accessible, fair, trustworthy, and
beneficial is consistent with ‘adl, amanah, and maslahah, although the empirical
construct remains customer perceived value. Therefore, H7 proposes that higher
customer perceived value positively influences LPG 3 kg purchase decisions.
The Mediating Role of Customer Perceived Value in the Distribution –Purchase
Decision Relationship
Distribution may influence purchase behavior indirectly because consumers first
evaluate what reliable access means for the value of the transaction. Prior research
supports a quality–value–behavior chain in which logistics quality improves perceived
value, which then increases purchase or repurchase intentions (Nguyen, 2019; J.-F. Wu
& Chang, 2016 ). Availability, timeliness, delivery condition, and channel consistency
reduce uncertainty and non-monetary costs, thereby improving the consumer’s overall
evaluation before behavior occurs ( Murfield et al., 2017 ; Yang et al., 2024 ). Reliable
logistics can also protect consumers from failed purchases and costly alternative
searches. In the LPG 3 kg context, easier access, consistent availability, and regulated
pricing should therefore raise perceived functional, economic, and convenience value
before strengthening purchase decisions (Octaviani et al., 2023).
This mediating mechanism is particularly relevant for subsidized goods because
distribution quality also communicates whether the subsidy system works fairly in
practice. When eligible consumers can obtain LPG from nearby official bases at
predictable pric es, distribution creates both instrumental value and procedural
reassurance. Conversely, irregular supply or forced reliance on informal sellers can
erode value even when aggregate supply is adequate. Empirical evidence from
multichannel and e-commerce settings indicates that perceived value can carry the
effect of distribution quality into later behavioral outcomes (Correa et al., 2021; Saniyyah
& Kustiawan, 2025 ). Within an Islamic distributive framework, reliable access can be
interpreted as supporting equitable benefit realization. Accordingly, H8 proposes that
customer perceived value mediates the positive effect of distribution on LPG 3 kg
purchase decisions.
The Mediating Role of Customer Perceived Value in the Scarcity –Purchase
Decision Relationship
Scarcity can influence purchase behavior  through perceived value when limited
availability increases the subjective importance or desirability of a product.

Experimental and retail studies show that scarcity can raise utilitarian and hedonic
value, with perceived value subsequently increasing product acceptance or purchase
intention (Jackson & Xu, 2022; Wu et al., 2012). This mechanism is plausible for LPG 3 kg
because intermittent availability can make consumers recognize its importance for
cooking and microenterprise operations more sharply. Under this logic, scarcity first
elevates the perceived benefit of securing the product and then encourages purchase
when supply reappears. The proposed mediation is therefore consistent with the
argument that scarce LPG becomes more salient and valuable in daily life before
shaping purchasing behavior (Abi & Siregar, 2026).
Yet the mediating pathway is theoretically contested in essential and subsidized
markets. Scarcity can simultaneously increase search costs, travel expenses, price
uncertainty, anger, and perceived unfairness, reducing the net value of the transaction
(Herbon & Kogan, 2022 ; Meuthia et al., 2023 ). Extreme scarcity may even produce an
inverted relationship in which anxiety and frustration overwhelm any value -
enhancement effect (Putriyandari et al., 2025 ). These boundary conditions are
important because subsidized LPG is a necessity rather than an exclusivity -seeking
purchase. Artificial scarcity would also conflict with Islamic prohibitions on ihtikar and
with consumer -welfare protection. Nevertheless, the conventional scarcity –value
mechanism remains empirically testable; therefore, H9 proposes that customer
perceived value mediates the effect of scarcity on LPG 3 kg purchase decisions.
The Mediating Role of Customer Perceived Value in the MAP –Purchase Decision
Relationship
The effect of digital subsidy governance on purchasing behavior may operate primarily
through customer perceived value. Digital-service research shows that system quality,
information quality, transparency, and convenience improve value perceptions, which
then shape trust, satisfaction, and behavioral intentions (Chohan & Hu, 2020; Luna et al.,
2024; Supraptiningsih, 2025 ). A broad meta -analytic literature similarly identifies
perceived value as a central mechanism translating service inputs into repurchase and
continued-use outcomes (Blut et al., 2024). Evidence from MyPertamina also indicates
that perceived usefulness supports behavioral intention, although intention does not
always translate directly into actual use, suggesting an intervening evaluative process
(Jatnika et al., 2023 ). This makes customer perceived value theoretically suitable for
explaining how MAP-related service improvements influence LPG purchasing behavior.
For provider -operated systems such as MAP, mediation is especially plausible
because consumers experience the consequences of technology rather than operating
its full infrastructure. Accurate verification, transparent transaction records, shorter
procedures, and clearer eligibility rules can increase convenience, trust, procedural
fairness, and perceived usefulness ( Riofita, 2025; Chaniago et al., 2025 ). When these
benefits outweigh privacy concerns, waiting time, or verification burdens, consumers
are more likely to value purchasing through official LPG bases. This mechanism is
compatible with Islamic notions of amanah and ‘adl because digital governance can
support accountable and equitable subsidy delivery without treating those principles

Research Gap and the Significance of the Study
Existing research remains fragmented across logistics, scarcity, digital governance,
customer value, and purchasing behavior . Distribution studies emphasize availability,
last-mile access, and service reliability; scarcity research focuses on urgency, anxiety,
and perceived desirability; and digital -government research examines transparency,
trust, system quality, and adoption. Meanwhile, customer perceived value is commonly
tested in commercial service settings rather than in regulated subsidy systems. Prior
LPG studies also tend to examine distribution, price, scarcity, service quality, or
application implementation separately. Consequently, limited evidence explains how
physical distribution, perceived scarcity, and provider -operated digital subsidy
governance jointly shape consumer behavior through a common value -evaluation
mechanism in subsidized essential-energy markets.
This gap is theoretically significant because subsidized LPG differs from ordinary
commercial products: demand is necessity -driven, prices are regulated, eligibility is
policy-defined, and distribution carries explicit welfare and justice implications.
Integrating customer perceived value allows the model to connect operational
conditions with consumer evaluations of economic benefit, functionality, convenience,
trust, fairness, and overall utility. It also permits a more disciplined incorporation of
Islamic economics by interpreting equitable access, accountability, welfare protection,
and anti-hoarding principles as normative lenses for subsidy governance rather than
as unmeasured latent variables. Accordingly, the study contributes by testing ten direct
and mediated relationships within one structural framework and by examining whether
conventional scarcity and technology -adoption expectations hold in a regulated,
subsidized LPG 3 kg market in Langkat Regency.
METHOD
Research Design and Analytical Framework
This study employed a quantitative explanatory, cross -sectional design to examine
structural associations among physical distribution, perceived scarcity, consumer -
perceived implementation of Merchant App MyPertamina  (MAP), customer perceived
value, and LPG 3 kg purchase decisions. Distribution, scarcity, and MAP implementation
were specified as exogenous constructs, customer perceived value as an intervening
construct, and purchase decision as the endogenous construc t. The design was
intended to test multiple direct and indirect relationships simultaneously rather than to
establish temporal causality. Partial least squares structural equation modeling  (PLS-
SEM) was selected because the model contains several latent variables and mediation
paths that require simultaneous assessment of measurement quality and structural
relationships. The analytical procedure therefore combined evaluation of the
measurement model with estimation of path coefficients, explained variance,

predictive relevance, and indirect effects, consistent with the PLS -SEM framework
described by Hair et al. (2021).
Research Setting, Population, and Sampling
The study was conducted in Langkat Regency, North Sumatra Province, Indonesia, from
March to May 2026. Langkat contains 23 districts and encompasses urban, rural,
coastal, plantation, and hilly areas, creating substantial variation in distance to LPG
bases and in the accessibility of subsidized LPG 3 kg. The target population comprised
LPG 3 kg users residing in Langkat  Regency. Purposive sampling was used because
respondents needed direct experience with the product and its distribution process.
Eligible participants were residents of Langkat  who used LPG 3 kg, had purchased it
during the previous six months, and had experience with transactions through agents,
LPG bases, retailers, or transaction-recording procedures associated with MAP. A total
of 205 complete questionnaires were retained fo r analysis. Coverage across all 23
districts increased geographical heterogeneity, but it was not treated as evidence of
probability-based representativeness.
Construct Operationalization and Measurement
Data were collected using a structured questionnaire with five -point Likert response
options ranging from strongly disagree to strongly agree. Distribution was measured
through LPG availability, continuity of supply, location accessibility, targeting accuracy,
price conformity, and distribution equity ( Maidiana & Hidayat, 2021 ; Octaviani et al.,
2023). Scarcity captured difficulty obtaining LPG, stockouts, search time, queues,
purchase restrictions, and price consequences (Abi & Siregar, 2026 ; Qu & Liu, 2022 ).
Consumer-perceived MAP implementation covered transaction ease, service speed,
beneficiary-data accuracy, recording transparency, targeting accuracy, data security,
and consistency of application use (Octaviani et al., 2023). Customer perceived value
and purchase decision were assessed through perceived benefits, cost and time
efficiency, transaction experience, purchase frequency, quota compliance, channel
choice, and repurchase tendency (Pham et al., 2018 ; Utami et al., 2025 ; L.-Y. Wu et al.,
2014).
Data Collection and Sources
Primary data were obtained through both offline and online questionnaire
administration. Offline distribution was conducted at agents, LPG bases, and other LPG
3 kg sales locations to reach consumers with direct experience of stock availability,
prices, queues, and purchasing procedures. Online distribution used digital forms and
community communication channels to broaden participation among consumers
familiar with technology-mediated services. Combining the two modes was intended
to avoid restricting the sample to respondents who were highly digitally active.
Secondary information was drawn from local-government documents, distribution and
quota records, information from agents or LPG bases, and operational materials
concerning Merchant App MyPertamina. These materials were used to contextualize,
rather than replace, respondents’ self -reported perceptions. Because the latent

constructs were measured from consumer responses collected within a single survey
period, the empirical findings were interpreted as cross -sectional structural
associations rather than definitive causal effects.
Measurement Model Assessment
The data were analyzed with SmartPLS 4.0 (Ringle et al., 2024) in two sequential stages.
The first stage evaluated the reflective measurement model through indicator
reliability, convergent validity, discriminant validity, and internal consistency.
Convergent validity was assessed using outer loadings and average var iance
extracted, with outer loadings of approximately 0.70 or higher and average variance
extracted above 0.50 used as the principal criteria. Internal consistency was assessed
using Cronbach’s alpha and composite reliability, with values of at least 0.70 treated as
acceptable. Discriminant validity in the original analytical procedure was examined
using cross-loadings and the Fornell–Larcker criterion by comparing the square root of
average variance extracted with interconstruct correlations.
Structural Model and Hypothesis Testing
The second stage evaluated the structural model and tested the proposed direct and
mediated relationships. Model assessment included coefficients of determination for
the endogenous constructs, predictive relevance, path coefficients, effect sizes, and
indirect effects. Hypotheses were evaluated using the SmartPLS  bootstrapping
procedure at a 5% significance level; relationships were treated as statistically
significant when the t -statistic exceeded 1.96 and the p -value was below 0.05.
Mediation was assessed by examining the significance and direction of the indire ct
paths from distribution, scarcity, and consumer -perceived MAP implementation to
purchase decision through customer perceived value, together with the corresponding
direct paths. This approach allowed partial and full mediation patterns to be
distinguished within the specified model. Given the purposive sampling strategy, cross-
sectional design, and reliance on consumer-reported measures, statistical significance
was interpreted within the study sample and not as proof of population -wide or
longitudinal causal effects.

Distribution and Customer Perceived Value
The findings support H1 by showing that distribution had a positive and statistically
significant relationship with customer perceived value ( β = 0.354, t = 5.166, p < 0.001).
Consumers who perceived LPG 3 kg as more available, accessible, evenly distributed,
and obtainable through appropriate channels also evaluated the product and service
process more favorably. This pattern is consistent with the argument that distribution
quality reduces search time, uncertainty, and non-monetary effort while increasing the
practical benefits associated with consumption. In a subsidized essential -goods
market, distribution therefore contributes not only to physical availability but also to the
perceived usefulness, convenience, and fairness of obtaining the product.
This result is consistent with studies showing that product availability and supply
regularity shape customer evaluations by reducing search costs and preventing
beneficiaries from being pushed toward alternative markets (Atayev, 2022 ; Pragya,
2025). Evidence from subsidized and essential -goods settings also indicates that
transparency and traceability can strengthen fairness perceptions and improve value
evaluations (Bala & Kaur, 2022 ; Park & Li, 2021 ). At the same time, Rashid & Rasheed
(2024) found that individual logistics dimensions may not independently predict
satisfaction, suggesting that distribution should be interpreted as a broader service
configuration rather than as a single operational attribute. The present findings
therefore supp ort a multidimensional interpretation of distribution -related value in
which accessibility, reliability, and procedural conditions operate jointly.
Theoretically, the finding positions distribution as an antecedent of customer
perceived value rather than merely a logistical background condition. It reinforces the
proposition that consumers evaluate subsidized LPG through both benefits received
and sacrifices avoided, consistent with Zeithaml’s (1988) value framework. Practically,
Pertamina, agents, and LPG bases should prioritize stock continuity, geographic
accessibility, transparent allocation, and compliance with regulated prices because
these conditions affect how consumers evaluate the subsidy it self. From an Islamic
economics perspective, reliable and equitable distribution is consistent with ‘adl,
amanah, and maslahah because publicly supported resources should reach eligible
consumers without avoidable exclusion or diversion. Distribution quality consequently
has behavioral, distributive, and broader welfare significance.

H2 was not supported. Scarcity showed a negative but statistically nonsignificant
relationship with customer perceived value ( β = −0.080, t = 1.433, p = 0.152). This result
indicates that greater difficulty in obtaining LPG 3 kg did not systematically make
consumers perceive the product as more valuable, despite the conventional
expectation that limited availability heightens subjective importance. Scar city may
simultaneously increase consumers’ awareness of LPG as an essential product while
also increasing the sacrifices required to obtain it, including search time, queues,

uncertainty, and potential price deviations. These competing mechanisms may offset
each other, generating no significant net change in perceived value among households
and microenterprises that already regard LPG 3 kg as an important everyday necessity.
The finding contrasts with commodity and scarcity research demonstrating that
reduced availability can increase desirability, perceived value, and willingness to pay
(Roux et al., 2023; Wrabel et al., 2022). For utilitarian products, demand-based scarcity
may particularly increase perceived importance because high demand signals
widespread need (Barton et al., 2022 ; Hamilton & Hosany, 2023 ). However, other

it creates access difficulties, search costs, price uncertainty, frustration, or suspicions of
manipulation (Gupta & Gentry, 2019; Tuncer et al., 2024). Herbon & Kogan (2022) further
demonstrate that scarcity in subsidized essential -goods markets can increase travel
and price burdens despite the continued presence of subsidy mechanisms.
Theoretically, this result identifies a boundary condition for the conventional
scarcity–value relationship. In regulated necessity markets, scarcity may fail to operate
as a favorable  value signal because baseline utility is already high and consumers
cannot readily convert limited supply into exclusivity -based desirability. Practically,
nonsignificance should not be interpreted as evidence that scarcity is harmless.
Shortages may still increase waiting time, informal-market dependence, search costs,
and perceived procedural unfairness. From an Islamic economics perspective,
artificially induced scarcity or withholding that raises consumer burdens conflicts with
the prohibition of ihtikar and with welfare protection. Policy should therefore continue
emphasizing supply continuity, price compliance, and prevention of distribution
practices that impose disproportionate costs on eligible beneficiaries.

H5 was reported as unsupported, indicating that scarcity did not significantly explain

searching for an d purchasing the product whether scarcity is relatively high or low,

anxiety, fear, and social influence (Ahmad et al., 2025; Omar et al., 2021). However, other
evidence provides a plausible explanation for the nonsignificant result. Financially
constrained consumers may lack the capacity to increase purchases despite
perceived scarcity (De Bruijn & Antonides, 2022 ), while purchases of subsidized
necessities can remain need -driven even when scarcity increases search costs and
prices (Herbon & Kogan, 2022). Failed acquisition can also produce anger rather than
stronger purchase intention (Biraglia et al., 2021).
Theoretically, the finding suggests that scarcity theory requires qualification when
applied to regulated essential commodities. When baseline need is already high and
substitutes are limited, scarcity may affect acquisition costs and inconvenience rather
than the underlying decision to purchase. Practically, authorities should therefore

et al., 2022; Pollard et al., 2018).
Amanah, Transparency, Accountability, and Hisbah in Digital Subsidy
Governance
The significant relationship between MAP implementation and customer perceived
value can be interpreted through amanah, transparency, accountability, and hisbah.
Digital subsidy systems can operationalize amanah by recording transactions, verifying
eligible users, and creating auditable information that reduces opportunities for misuse.
Islamic scholarship treats public subsidies as a state trust that must be managed
responsibly and distributed to entitled benefi ciaries (Daipon et al., 2025 ; Mohamed et
al., 2025). Digital records are also consistent with transparency in muamalah because
they improve documentation and traceability (Najib et al., 2025). From this perspective,
MAP contributes to value when consumers perceive that digital governance makes
distribution more orderly, transparent, and credible, rather than when the technology
merely exists as an administrative requirement.
However, digitalization does not automatically guarantee Islamic accountability.
Evidence shows that identity fraud, barcode misuse, weak data integration, unstable
connectivity, and low digital literacy can continue to undermine subsidy targeting
despite digital verification (Ilmi et al., 2026 ; J. M. Putra & Menorizah, 2025 ; Wibowo &
Abdullah, 2024). The principle of hisbah therefore remains relevant because market
supervision requires continuous monitoring, institutional coordination, and corrective
action rather than reliance on technology alone (Riani & Oktalina, 2025; Saoki & Mahir,
2025). The policy implication is that MAP should be integrated with welfare databases,
layered verification, and accessible service procedures. Such arrangements would
strengthen amanah and procedural justice by ensuring that digital efficiency does not
create new barriers for eligible beneficiaries or weaken human oversight.
Customer Perceived Value, Maslahah, and Maqasid al-Shariah
The strong role of customer perceived value in LPG 3 kg purchasing behavior  can be
interpreted through maslahah and maqasid al -shariah. Islamic economics does not
reduce value to subjective satisfaction or market price; value is also assessed by
whether consumption protects welfare, wealth, and legitimate human needs (Ananda
et al., 2025; Fachruddin & Pratama, 2024). Subsidized LPG can be viewed as supporting
hifz al-mal by lowering household energy expenditure and hifz al -nafs by facilitating
access to a basic cooking fuel (Klongrua et al., 2025; Syahriani et al., 2024). Accordingly,
consumers’ positive value assessments may reflect not only economic savings but also

the broader benefit of reliable access to an essential commodity that supports
everyday household and productive activities.
This interpretation also explains why customer perceived value functions as a
bridge between distribution, digital governance, and purchase decisions. When
consumers perceive LPG access as affordable, convenient, fair, and trustworthy, the
subsidy produces  a form of maslahah that is both individual and social. Islamic
economics places such welfare effects within a hierarchy of needs, where necessities
receive priority over discretionary consumption (A. Arifin, 2024; Azizah et al., 2025; Habibi
et al., 2024; Sari et al., 2025; Yusrizal et al., 2025). The policy implication is that improving
perceived value should not be pursued through promotional strategies, but through fair
prices, reliable availability, transparent procedures, and protection from unnecessary
costs. In this context, perceived value becomes an empirical expression of whether the
subsidy system actually delivers welfare consistent with maqasid -oriented public
policy.
Scarcity, Ihtikar, Price Exploitation, and Consumer Protection
The nonsignificant effect of scarcity on customer perceived value and purchase
decisions should be interpreted cautiously from an Islamic perspective. Statistical
nonsignificance does not mean that scarcity is ethically irrelevant or that shortages
result from hoarding. The Consensus evidence emphasizes that when shortages are
artificially created through ihtikar, supply manipulation, or speculative withholding, they
violate Islamic market ethics because they distort access and impose unjust costs on
consumers (Fadilah et al., 2025 ; Faisal et al., 2025 ). In the present context, consumers
may continue purchasing LPG because it is a basic necessity, so scarcity may increase
inconvenience and financial sacrifice without meaningfully changing the underlying
need to buy. This helps explain why scarcity can remain socially harmful even when its
estimated behavioral effect is weak.
Islamic consumer protection therefore requires policy attention to the
consequences of scarcity rather than only its statistical relationship with purchasing
behavior. Price exploitation, sales above regulated ceilings, artificial withholding, and
weak supervision can undermine ‘adl and hifz al-mal by transferring additional burdens
to low-income households (Z. Arifin, 2021; J. P. Putra & Zumaroh, 2025). Islamic business
ethics also requires firms to avoid exploiting necessity-driven demand and to preserve
fairness in transactions (Ilham S. et al., 2024; Khairunnisa et al., 2025; U-Mar et al., 2024).
Regulators should strengthen stock monitoring, price transparency, complaint
mechanisms, and enforcement against hoarding or manipulation. Such measures are
consistent with the prohibition of ihtikar and with maqasid -oriented consumer
protection because they seek to preserve access, reduce uncertainty, and prevent
vulnerable consumers from bearing disproportionate costs during supply disruptions.
Islamic Policy Implications for Subsidized Energy Governance
The overall findings imply that Islamic subsidy governance should prioritize reliable
physical distribution and welfare creation rather than treating digital monitoring as an
end in itself. Islamic economics assigns the state a responsibility to safeguard basic

needs and ensure that essential resources are distributed justly, particularly when
market mechanisms do not protect vulnerable groups (Abdul Rahim, 2015; Rahim, 2013).
Because distribution and customer perceived value were more important than digital
technology alone, policy should first secure physical availability, affordability, and fair
access. Digital systems should then support these objectives through better tar geting
and accountability. This ordering is consistent with maslahah because governance
quality should be judged by the welfare it produces rather than by administrative or
technological sophistication alone (Mulyany & Furqani, 2019; Siddiqi, 2014).
A maqasid -oriented policy framework should therefore combine supply -chain
reliability, targeted subsidies, transparent digital records, consumer protection, and
long-term empowerment. Islamic distribution seeks not only to relieve immediate
hardship but al so to prevent persistent dependency and strengthen the economic
position of vulnerable groups (Arum et al., 2026 ; Dzulkepli & Barom, 2021 ; Isnan et al.,
2026; Jannah & Alifa, 2026 ). For LPG 3 kg, this implies maintaining reliable access for
eligible households while continuously improving targeting, supervision, and policy
efficiency. Digital tools such as MAP should serve amanah and hisbah by supporting
verifiable allocation rather  than replacing physical distribution or human oversight.
Ultimately, the legitimacy of subsidized energy governance rests on whether it
advances ‘adl , protects wealth, prevents exploitation, and produces shared welfare
rather than merely improving transaction administration (Asmawati, 2021 ; Syibly &
Purwanto, 2021).
CONCLUSION
This study shows that the behavior of LPG 3 kg consumers in Langkat Regency is shaped
more strongly by distribution quality and customer perceived value than by scarcity or
digitalization alone. Distribution was positively associated with both customer
perceived value and purchase decisions, while customer perceived value itself
emerged as a major predictor of purchasing behavior. Consumer -perceived
implementation of Merchant App MyPertamina  significantly improved customer
perceived value but did not directly influence purchase decisions, indicating that digital
governance becomes behaviorally relevant when consumers experience tangible
benefits from it. Scarcity, by contrast, did not signifi cantly explain customer perceived
value or purchase decisions, suggesting that the essential and necessity -driven
character of subsidized LPG weakens the conventional scarcity effect observed in
discretionary markets.
The mediation results clarify the mechanisms underlying these relationships.
Customer perceived value partially mediated the relationship between distribution and
purchase decisions, indicating that reliable physical access influences behavior both
directly and through improved evaluations of convenience, fairness, usefulness, and
overall benefit. In contrast, customer perceived value fully mediated the relationship
between Merchant App MyPertamina  implementation and purchase decisions,
showing that provider -operated digital technology affects consumers primarily

through the value they derive from better transparency, service orderliness, verification,
and procedural clarity. From an Islamic economics perspective, these findings are
consistent with the principles of ‘adl, amanah, maslahah, and consumer protection
because effective subsidy governance must translate public resources into equitable,
accountable, and welfare -enhancing access rather than technological compliance
alone.
The study contributes to existing knowledge by integrating physical distribution,
perceived scarcity, provider-operated digital subsidy governance, customer perceived
value, and purchase behavior  within a single structural framework for a regulated
essential-energy market. Its contribution lies not merely in combining variables, but in
identifying customer perceived value as the mechanism that connects operational and
digital governance conditions  with consumer behavior  while revealing a boundary
condition for scarcity theory in necessity-based consumption. These findings imply that
policy should prioritize reliable last -mile distribution, fair targeting, transparent
procedures, and consumer-visible service benefits. Further research should test these
relationships across regions, user groups, and time periods and incorporate objective
transaction, stock, price, and system -performance data to assess whether the
observed mechanisms remain stable beyond cross-sectional perceptions.
Limitation of the Study
This study has several limitations that should guide interpretation of its findings. First,
the data were collected from 205 LPG 3 kg users in Langkat  Regency using purposive
sampling. Although respondents were drawn from all 23 districts, this geographical
coverage does not provide probability -based representativeness, and the results
should not be generalized automatically to other regions or to the entire population of
subsidized LPG users. Second, the cross -sectional design captures perceptions and
purchasing behavior within a single observation period and therefore cannot establish
temporal causality. The estimated structural relationships should be understood as
associations consistent with the proposed theoretical model rather than definitive
evidence that changes i n distribution, scarcity, or digital implementation cause
subsequent changes in purchasing behavior.
A further limitation concerns measurement and data sources. All principal
constructs were assessed through self -reported questionnaires, creating potential
common-source and social -desirability bias. Consumer -perceived Merchant App
MyPertamina implementation was evaluated from the perspective of LPG buyers even
though the application is primarily operated by LPG bases; consequently, the construct
reflects experienced implementation rather than objective system performance. The
removal of the MAP6 data-security indicator also narrowed the final construct’s content
domain. Moreover, the analysis did not integrate transaction logs, base -level stock
records, actual purchase prices, delivery schedules, network disruptions, or audit data.
Important contextual variables such as household income, digital literacy, distance to
official outlets, trust in government, and subsidy eligibility were also outside the tested
model and may explain additional variation.

Recommendations for Future Research
Future research should strengthen external validity by examining subsidized LPG users
across multiple provinces and contrasting urban, rural, coastal, plantation, and remote
settings. Probability-based sampling and larger samples would permit more defensible
population inference, while longitudinal designs could determine whether changes in
distribution reliability, scarcity conditions, or digital governance precede changes in
perceived value and purchasing behavior. Comparative analyses between households
and micro, small, and medium enterprises would also clarify whether necessity,
business dependence, and purchasing frequency alter the structural relationships.
Researchers should further investigate potential mode rators such as income, subsidy
eligibility, digital literacy, trust in public institutions, geographic accessibility, and
previous experience with digital transactions to identify for whom and under what
conditions the observed relationships are strongest.
Future studies should also combine perceptual surveys with objective operational
evidence. Merchant App MyPertamina  transaction records, LPG base inventories,
delivery schedules, actual retail prices, geographic distance measures, and system -
outage data could be integrated to evaluate whether consumer perceptions
correspond with observable distribution and digital -performance conditions.
Qualitative interviews with consumers, base operators, agents, and regulators would
help explain why scarcity does not necessarily translate into stronger value perceptions
or purchase decisions and how digital systems create or fail to create consumer-visible
benefits. Additional work could explicitly operationalize procedural fairness, distributive
justice, amanah, trust, and maslahah-related welfare outcomes rather than using
Islamic economics only as an interpretive lens. Such extensions would deepen
understanding of ethical and effective subsidy governance in regulated essential -
energy markets.
