ABSTRACT: The rapid integration of artificial intelligence (AI) has transformed digital marketing by enabling brands to cr eate
personalized, automated, and data -driven promotional content. This study examines the impact of AI -generated marketing
content on consumer behavior, with a specific focus on purchase intention. The study investigates how consumer perception,
perceived credibility, perceived authenticity, and consumer trust influence consumers' willingness to purchase products promoted
through AI-generated marketing content. A quantitative research design was adopted, and primary data were collected through
a structured online questionnaire using a five -point Likert scale. Out of 184 initial responses, 154 valid responses were retained
after data screening. Data were analyzed using descriptive statistics, Cronbach's alpha reliability testing, Pearson correlat ion, and
multiple regression analysis, supplemented by Variance Inflation Factor (VIF) diagnostics to assess multicollinearity and Harman's
single-factor test to evaluate common method bias. The reliability results showed excellent internal consistency across all
constructs (Cronbach's alpha > 0.95). Although these uniformly high alpha values may reflect construct overlap rather than
independent measurement, multicollinearity diagnostics confirmed that VIF values remained within acceptable thresholds (< 5.0).
Harman's single-factor test indicated that no single factor accounted for a majority of the total variance, providing partial
assurance against common method bias. Correlation analysis revealed strong positive relationships between purchase intention
and all four p redictors. The regression model explained 81.9% of the variance in purchase intention. Perceived authenticity
emerged as the strongest predictor (β = 0.388), followed by consumer trust (β = 0.265) and consumer perception (β = 0.228).
Although perceived credibility demonstrated a strong bivariate correlation with purchase intention (r = 0.817, p < 0.001), its direct
effect became non-significant in the multivariate model (β = 0.077, p = 0.317), consistent with full mediation by consumer trust.
These findings suggest that AI-generated marketing content can significantly influence consumer purchase intention—particularly
among young, digitally active consumers —when it is perceived as authentic, trustworthy, and aligned with consumer
expectations. The study cont ributes to AI marketing literature by highlighting the mediating role of trust in the credibility –
purchase intention pathway and underscores the centrality of authenticity in consumer acceptance of AI -driven marketing
communication.

1. INTRODUCTION
The rise of artificial intelligence (AI) has transformed marketing by enabling organizations to automate content creation,
personalize customer interactions, and enhance decision -making. Advances in generative AI, especially conversational systems

like ChatGPT, have empowered businesses to generate marketing content more efficiently and at scale. AI tools are now widely
used in advertising, customer engagement, product recommendations, and content marketing, reshaping how firms connect with
consumers (Davenport et al., 2020). AI's integration into marketing forms a crucial part of broader digital transformation efforts,
which aim to improve processes, customer experiences, and business models through advanced technology (Verhoef et al., 2021).
As AI -generated content becomes more prevalent, consumers increasingly encounter machinecreated information, raising
concerns about authenticity, credibility, transparency, and trustworthiness (Dwivedi et al., 2023). Trust is essential for su ccessful
organization–consumer relationships. The Commitment -Trust Theory highlights trust as key to long -term customer loyalty and
cooperation (Morgan & Hunt, 1994). In digital settings, where information quality can be uncertain, trust is even more vital.
Consumers may hesitate to  trust AI-generated marketing if they perceive it as biased, inaccurate, or lacking human judgment
(Dwivedi et al., 2023).
Signaling Theory offers a framework to understand consumer evaluation of AI-generated content. It posits that when information
asymmetry exists, signals such as transparency about AI use, perceived credibility, and authenticity help reduce uncertainty and
build trust. Authenticity, in particular, strongly influences consumer perceptions and brand evaluations, as consumers favor
communications that feel genuine and trustworthy (Napoli et al., 2014). Consumer behavior theories such as the Theory of Planned
Behavior (TPB) explain that beliefs and perceptions shape attitudes, which in turn shape behavioral intentions (Ajzen, 1991).  In
the c ontext of AI -generated content, consumers' assessments of trustworthiness and authenticity can positively affect their
willingness to engage with or purchase products promoted through AI -driven messages.
Despite AI's growing role in marketing, research on  how AI-generated content affects consumer trust and purchase intentions
remains limited. Most studies focus on AI's technological or organizational dimensions rather than on consumer perceptions
(Davenport et al., 2020; Dwivedi et al., 2023). This study a ddresses that gap by examining how consumer perception, perceived
credibility, perceived authenticity, and consumer trust jointly influence purchase intention toward AI -generated marketing
content. Importantly, the study explores trust as a potential media tor linking credibility perceptions to purchase behavior —a
theoretical pathway suggested by prior literature but rarely tested directly in the context of AI marketing.

2. LITERATURE REVIEW
2.1. Artificial Intelligence and Digital Marketing
The rapid evolution  of artificial intelligence (AI) has fundamentally restructured modern marketing strategies, acting as a core
driver of broader digital transformation. Verhoef et al. (2021) define digital transformation as a multidisciplinary agenda t hat
reshapes organizational processes and business models. Within this paradigm, AI serves as the technological engine that allows
organizations to adapt dynamically. Davenport et al. (2020) emphasize that AI's potential to revolutionize marketing lies in its
unparalleled ability to enhance customer insights, optimize pricing, and automate customer service, thereby drastically reducing
operational costs while boosting effectiveness. Grewal et al. (2020) position technology as the defining force of future mark eting
landscapes, and Chesbrough (2020) highlights how open innovation and digital agility are essential for rapid managerial recovery
in post -disruption environments. Huang and Rust (2021) offer a strategic framework for AI in marketing, categorizing its
intelligence into mechanical, thinking, and feeling capacities, which collectively enable highly personalized customer interactions.
The ongoing relevance of this transformation is echoed by Mariani et al.
(2022) and De Cremer (2024).
2.2. Consumer Perception of AI-Generated Marketing Content
While early iterations of AI focused on data analytics and voice assistants (Kaplan & Haenlein, 2019), the introduction of generative
AI systems—most notably ChatGPT—has accelerated the production of sophisticated, human-like text, audio, and visual content.
Dwivedi et al. (2023) describe conversational AI as a transformative force capable of scaling productivity and personalizing
communication across various business contexts. Huang and Rust (2024) and Kietzmann et al. (2025) argue that generative AI will
dictate the future operational baseline of digital marketing. However, widespread deployment of AI content alters the traditi onal
customer experience (Ameen et al., 2021). Delbaere et al. (2021) highlight that while personalization is valued, machine-generated
messaging introduces distinct challenges regarding ethics, privacy, and systemic bias —such as the algorithmic biases in digital
advertising documented by Lambrecht and Tucker (2019).
2.3. Perceived Credibility
In digital environments, co nsumers face significant information asymmetry, meaning they cannot directly verify the underlying
quality or origin of digital content. Signaling Theory posits that organizations must use observable signals to communicate
unobservable qualities such as reliability and competence. Erdem and Swait (2004) establish that brand credibility acts as a primary
signal that reduces perceived risk and drives consumer choice. Metzger and Flanagin (2013) emphasize that establishing credibility

is the foundational chall enge in online information environments. When applied to modern digital ecosystems, signals such as
message value and source credibility directly influence consumer trust (Lou & Yuan, 2019). Shin (2021) demonstrates that the
explainability and interpretability of AI systems act as crucial signals that allow consumers to trust automated outputs.
Critically, prior literature suggests that credibility does not always exert a direct effect on behavioral outcomes. Rather, its
influence is frequently mediated th rough trust —credibility builds confidence in the information source, and that confidence
subsequently drives behavioral intention (Kim et al., 2008; Gefen, 2000). This theoretical pathway motivates the present stud y's
positioning of consumer trust as a mediator in the credibility–purchase intention relationship.
2.4. Perceived Authenticity
Authenticity is a powerful psychological driver wherein consumers perceive a brand or message as genuine, sincere, and aligne d
with its core values. Napoli et al. (2014) operationalized the measurement of consumer-based brand authenticity, demonstrating
that authentic brands foster deep emotional bonds and loyalty, and confirming the direct, positive impact of authenticity on
consumer responses. For AI -generated marketing, maintaining authenticity is paramount yet challenging, as machine -generated
text can be perceived as artificia l or lacking human experience. When AI communication maintains brand consistency and
perceived transparency, it sustains brand equity and marketing effectiveness (Yoo et al., 2000; Xu et al., 2014).
2.5. Consumer Trust and Purchase Intention
Consumer decision-making and purchase intentions are deeply rooted in behavioral frameworks. Ajzen's (1991) Theory of Planned
Behavior (TPB) argues that intentions are shaped by an individual's attitudes, subjective norms, and perceived behavioral control.
The broader technological acceptance literature (Venkatesh et al., 2003; Rogers, 2003) explains how populations adopt and
interact with new digital systems. Petty and Cacioppo's (1986) Elaboration Likelihood Model distinguishes between central and
peripheral routes of persuasion that guide attitude formation and change. Recently, Gursoy et al. (2024) applied these frameworks
to evaluate consumer acceptance of AI-generated content in marketing communications, confirming the critical role of trust as a
behavioral antecedent.
The present study draws on this body of work to propose a model in which consumer perception, perceived credibility, and
perceived authenticity serve as antecedents to consumer trust, which in turn drives purchase intention. This structu re resolves
the theoretical inconsistency that would arise from treating credibility and trust as independent parallel predictors —since
credibility, by definitional overlap, operates through trust rather than alongside it.

The conceptual framework of this study proposes that consumer perception, perceived credibility, and perceived authenticity
influence purchase intention both directly and indirectly through consumer trust. This structure is consistent with the view  that
consumers rely on psychological evaluations —filtered through trust —when deciding whether to respond positively to AI -
generated marketing content.

4. RESEARCH METHODOLOGY
4.1. Research Design and Approach
This study adopted a quantitative, explanatory research design. The quantitative approach was suitable because the study
measured consumer attitudes and behavioral intentions using structured
Likert-scale survey items. The study was explanatory because it examined the effect of multiple independent variables on purchase
intention. A deductive approach was followed, as the study tested hypotheses derived from prior concepts in AI marketing, tru st,
credibility, authenticity, and consumer behavior.
4.2. Population and Sampling
The target population consisted of consumers exposed to digital marketing content, including advertisements, social media posts,
emails, product recommendations, and other promo tional messages that may be created or personalized using AI. A non -
probability convenience sampling technique was used because respondents were selected based on accessibility, willingness to
participate, and exposure to digital platforms. It is acknowled ged that this approach limits the representativeness of the sample
to wider consumer populations, particularly older and non -student demographics.
4.3. Sample Size and Respondent Profile
A total of 184 survey responses were initially collected. After removing  non-consenting and incomplete responses, 154 valid
responses were retained for final analysis. The sample was dominated by young digital consumers, with 90.9% of respondents
aged 18–24 years and 89.0% identifying as students. While this demographic profil e reflects a highly tech-literate, digitally active
cohort with frequent exposure to AI-generated content, it also constitutes a limitation discussed further in Section 10.
4.4. Data Collection Instrument
Primary data were collected through a structured onlin e questionnaire. The questionnaire contained three sections: consent and
study introduction, demographic information, and Likertscale items measuring the main constructs. The Likert scale ranged fro m
1 = Strongly Disagree to 5 = Strongly Agree. The five co nstructs were Consumer Perception, Perceived Credibility, Perceived
Authenticity, Consumer Trust, and Purchase Intention. Each construct contained six items, producing a total of 30 measurement
items.

6. DISCUSSION
The results show that AI -generated marketing content can significantly influence consumer purchase intention when consumers
perceive the content positively, consider it authentic, and trust the brand's use of AI. The strong model fit (R² = 0.819) in dicates
that psychological evaluations of AI-generated content are highly explanatory of consumer behavioral intention within the study's
sample context.
Perceived authenticity was the most influential predictor of purchase intention (β = 0.388). This finding su ggests that consumers
are more likely to respond positively to AI -generated marketing content when it feels genuine, emotionally relatable, and
consistent with the brand's identity — even when they are aware that the content is machine -generated. This resul t aligns with
Napoli et al. (2014), who demonstrate that authenticity perception generates emotional bonds that translate into behavioral
responses. For AI specifically, this implies that functional effectiveness (accuracy, speed, personalization) is insuf ficient if the
content fails to resonate at an emotional and relational level.
Consumer trust was the second strongest predictor (β = 0.265), confirming that trust remains central in digital and AI -mediated
marketing environments. This finding is consistent with the Commitment-Trust Theory (Morgan & Hunt, 1994) and with empirical
research linking AI explainability to trust formation (Shin, 2021). Consumers are more likely to consider purchasing from bra nds
that use AI responsibly, ethically, and transparently.
Consumer perception was a significant but comparatively weaker predictor (β = 0.228), indicating that the functional utility and
relevance of AI-generated content positively affect purchase intention, but that this effect is subordinate to authenticity and trust.
The non -significant direct effect of perceived credibility (β = 0.077, p = 0.317) warrants careful theoretical interpretation. To
formally evaluate the full mediation claim advanced in H2, the Baron and Kenny (1986) four -step procedure was a pplied. Step 1
confirmed that perceived credibility significantly predicted purchase intention in a simple regression (β = 0.817, p < 0.001). Step 2
confirmed that perceived credibility significantly predicted consumer trust (r = 0.872, p < 0.001). Step 3 confirmed that consumer
trust significantly predicted purchase intention when entered alongside perceived credibility (β = 0.265, p < 0.001). Step 4
confirmed that the direct effect of perceived credibility became non -significant after controlling for cons umer trust (β = 0.077, p
= 0.317), consistent with full mediation. Given its strong bivariate correlation (r = 0.817) and its borderline VIF, the pattern is best
understood as evidence of full mediation by consumer trust. In AI -driven communication environ ments, structural credibility —
the belief that content is accurate and reliable —appears to function as a prerequisite or baseline condition rather than a direct
behavioral driver. Once perceived credibility has been established, its effect on purchase intention is transmitted entirely through
consumer trust. This interpretation is consistent with Kim et al. (2008) and Gefen (2000), who demonstrate that in digital
commerce, credibility shapes trust, which in turn drives behavioral intent. Marketers should the refore recognize that credibility
cannot be dismissed as irrelevant; rather, it is a necessary antecedent to the trust that ultimately determines purchase beha vior.

7. THEORETICAL AND MANAGERIAL IMPLICATIONS
7.1. Theoretical Implications
This study makes several contributions to the literature on AI-generated marketing content. First, it empirically demonstrates that
authenticity and trust are stronger direct predictors of purchase intention than perceived credibility when examined
simultaneously, extending prior findings on authenticity (Napoli et al., 2014) and trust (Morgan & Hunt, 1994) to the emerging
context of AIgenerated content. Second, the study advances the theoretical argument that consumer trust fully mediates the
credibility–purchase intention pathway in AI marketing contexts —a conceptual clarification that resolves the apparent
contradiction between credibility's strong bivariate correlation and its non -significant regression coefficient. Third, the study
illustrates that highly overlapping constructs (credibility and trust) should be examined using mediation frameworks rather than
treated as independent parallel predictors in regression models; future research should adopt PLS -SEM or covariance-based SEM
to test these pathways explicitly. Fourth, the results reinforce the continued relevance of Signaling Theory (Erdem & Swait, 2004)
and the Commitment-Trust Theory (Morgan & Hunt, 1994) as complementary frameworks for explaining consumer responses to
AI-generated communication.
7.2. Managerial Implications
For marketers targeting young, digitally active consumers —particularly Gen Z students who are frequent users of digital
platforms—the findings offer the following practical guidance. First, AIgenerated content should not be design ed solely for
operational efficiency, speed, or personalization scale. Brands must ensure that AI -generated messages remain emotionally
relatable, tonally authentic, and brand -consistent. Second, since consumer trust mediates the credibility –purchase inten tion
relationship, perceived credibility must be established before trust can develop; brands should invest in transparency signal s—
such as explicit AI disclosure, accuracy commitments, and responsible use policies—that build foundational credibility. Third, once

8. CONCLUSION
This study examined the impact of AI -generated marketing content on consumer behavior, focusing on purchase intention. The
results demonstrate that consumer perception, perceived authenticity, and  consumer trust significantly influence purchase
intention, while perceived credibility does not exert a statistically significant direct effect in the multivariate model. Th e findings
are most parsimoniously interpreted as indicating that perceived credib ility operates through consumer trust rather than
alongside it—a full mediation pattern consistent with established digital trust literature. The regression model explained 81.9% of
the variance in purchase intention within the study sample, indicating str ong explanatory power under the specific demographic
conditions of the study.
The study concludes that AI -generated marketing content is most effective when it is perceived as authentic, trustworthy, and
aligned with consumer expectations. Authenticity wa s the strongest direct determinant of purchase intention, underscoring the
need for brands to maintain sincerity, emotional relevance, and brand consistency in AI -driven communication. Credibility, while
not a direct driver of purchase intention, remains a  necessary precondition for trust formation and should not be overlooked. As
AI continues to reshape digital marketing, brands must balance technological efficiency with human -centered values —
transparency, trust, and authentic engagement—to achieve sustainable behavioral outcomes.

9. LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE RESEARCH
This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, a non-probability convenience sampling technique was used,
which limits the statistical generalizability of findings. Secon d, the sample was heavily dominated by students aged 18 –24 years
(90.9%), representing a highly tech -literate, low -income cohort whose trust barriers regarding AI -generated content may be
substantially lower than those of older, higher -income, or non -student consumer populations. The findings should therefore be
interpreted as reflecting the perceptions and behavioral tendencies of young digital consumers rather than the broader consumer
market. Third, Cronbach's alpha values above 0.95 across all construct s raise concerns about item redundancy; the constructs —
particularly perceived credibility and consumer trust —share significant definitional overlap, and future studies should assess
discriminant validity through Average Variance Extracted (AVE) and confirm atory factor analysis. Fourth, the study relied on self -
reported Likertscale data, which may be subject to social desirability bias and acquiescence effects. Fifth, the cross-sectional design
precludes causal inference.
Future research should address thes e limitations in several ways. Larger and more demographically diverse samples —spanning
age groups, income levels, and cultural contexts —would enhance generalizability. The application of Partial Least Squares
Structural Equation Modeling (PLSSEM) would en able explicit testing of the trust mediation pathway suggested by this study, as
well as the examination of potential moderation effects (e.g., generational differences, prior AI exposure). Additional const ructs
such as perceived transparency, privacy conc ern, personalization, AI disclosure, and algorithmic fairness merit investigation.
Longitudinal designs would allow researchers to track how consumer trust and purchase intentions toward AI -generated content
evolve as exposure to AI marketing increases.
