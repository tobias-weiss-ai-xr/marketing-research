Abstract: Eye makeup includes everything from eyeshadow to eyeliner, mascara, brow tools, and even eyelash
enhancements, and has been in use since Egypt and Mesopotamia, some 4,000 years ago. Although there is a lot we know
about eye makeup, as it is ubiquitous across various cultures and has a huge economic impact on the global beauty industry
(estimated of over 500 billion USD), eye makeup is an under-developed and undocumented field of academic research. This
paper provides a thorough scholarly review of the literature on eye cosmetics across seven thematic areas: (1) History of eye
cosmetics from religious ritual to modern aesthetic practice; (2) theoretical frameworks such as feminist studies, material
culture, and semiotics, socio -technical studies, visual studi es and consumer culture; (3) consumer behavior and market
dynamics; (4) health, safety, and cosmetic chemistry; (5) cultural and aesthetic differences between Western and non -
Western traditions; (6) media representation and social perception; and (7) methodological approaches used in the study of
eye cosmetics. Although eye makeup is seen as a means for self-expression, identity construction, gender performance, and
social signaling, the literature is fraught with lacunae: underexamined populations and intersectional analysis, definitional
and ontological ambiguity over product categories, less application of rigorous theory, less longitudinal safety studies for
emerging cosmetic technologies, and a predominant focus on Western consumer markets. A lexicon ba sed on an ontology
for eye-makeup studies is proposed, along with the need to apply thoroughly feminist, practice and postcolonial theories,
and to broaden ethnographic and cross-cultural studies to further the field.

I. INTRODUCTION

Eye Makeup is the use of different products and
technique applied to the eye area to enhance or change the
look. It comprises preparations (such as skin care products),
eye contouring procedures (such as tints for the eyes,
eyeshadow, eye liner), eyelash enhancement (such as eyelash
extensions, eyelash mascara), and eyelid decoration (such as
eye stickers, eyelash liner). Common tools include fingers,
sponges and brushes. Definitions and usages vary between
different cultures. For example, Chinese eye make -up gives
ideal proportions while the Japanese makeup styles embrace
irregularities. Eye makeup is an important practice that has a
variety of social significance (Biesiadecka et al., 2023).
Consumer behavior, market dynamics, health issues, cultural
variation, media representations and social perceptions are
explored, and many studies utilize feminist theory, visual
rhetoric, and other sociocultural approaches. However, there
are still significant areas where there is still more to learn:
Underexamined popu lations; Unexamined sun and skin
differences globally; Long -term effects of new cosmetic
technologies; Intersectional analysis. Product, style and
technology changes are indicative of society. Evidence of use
of eye make -up since antiquity for practical an d, later,
cosmetic purposes, with many different uses. Historically,
eye cosmetics originated in religious ritual, and have adopted
various purposes in terms of aesthetics and identity in
different cultures.

 Theoretical Frameworks in Makeup Studies
Makeup and cosmetics analysis have become more
popular in the past 10 years, and the study of makeup and
cosmetics has become a field of study known as cosmetics
studies and makeup studies. A vast number of publications
exists in the field of cosmetics, but only a little has been found
with a specific focus on cosmetics. While makeup studies are
a field that is still emerging, it has seen a rapid increase.
Though many scholars and practitioners study the makeup,
there is a lack of research on it. The analysis of eye make-up
and of the broader domain of make-up studies may yield new
knowledge about social practices, technologies, and cultural
meanings. Eye makeup may be simply a subtrade of makeup
studies that specialize in the application of beautifying
products to the eyes, lashes, and brows. The study focuses on
the ways in which young women apply eye make -up to
influence society's perceptions of their physical appearance,
identity and norms of makeup application. There are types of
eye makeup based on the intended area of the face and types
of products such as eye shadow (eyelids), mascara
(eyelashes), brow tools (skin around the brow) and eyeliner
(eyelids contours). The eye makeup analysis extends
geographically, culturally and historically to gain a deeper
understanding of cultural diffe rences in eye make -up
practices. Eye makeup and cosmetics as a subject area can be
approached from several different theoretical lenses such as:
feminist studies; material culture studies; semiotics/media
culture; socio-technical studies; visual studies; and consumer
culture. These all-shed light on different aspects of the
practice of eye make -up and make for different kinds of
insight into the contemporary practice of eye make -up. The
socio-cultural functions of beauty can be studied as a part of
feminist studies, the role of technology and technology's
impact on values and attitudes in material culture, the supply
and demand analysis of consumer culture, and finally, the
eye-construction of society and the impact of visual
technology on consumption cultures through studies of visual
and media cultures. (Mingzhe Li et al., 2022).

 Consumer Behavior and Market Dynamics
Eye make -up such as eyeliner, mascara, eyeshadow,
eyebrow products and false eyelashes impact women and
men across the globe. The colour cosmetics sector shows an
ongoing strong growth – it is “a valuable segment of the
beauty and personal care industry” and “an important growth
opportunity for many large and small cosmetic manufactures”
(Montgomery & Ulrich, 2015). Total beauty industry revenue
“is estimated to be over 500 billion USD” (Biesiadecka et al.,
2023). The use of eye make -up continues to hold a place of
importance in the cultural and aesthetic aspects: “In the
cultural and aesthetic context, eye make -up is “a means of
self-expression,” and it is also the canvas for art creation”
(Hamelin et al., 2018). The study of consumer behaviour and
market dynamics associated with the buying of eye makeup
products has been conducted on three fronts: (1) market
segmentation, (2) branding and (3) consumer behaviour.
Research has been strong and strategic, especially about
positioning of global and regional cosm etic brands with
Moroccan Muslim women, since the women want to preserve
their traditional Islamic values but still follow the global
aesthetic and consumer trends. The perceived importance and
the economic downturn are factors that influence buying
behavior and lead to a decrease or increase in acceptance of
culturally congruent cosmetic types and brand and/or retailer
switching.

III. HEALTH, SAFETY, AND COSMETIC
CHEMISTRY

Eye Makeup products are applied around the eyes and
removed for the day as soon as it's over, and eye shadows are
made in powder, cream and pencil. Some of these products
have been recorded as being used since Antiquity and others
seem more recent. The common ingredients in eye shadows
may include dyes, pigments, and oils while the preservatives,
perfumes, and thickeners are typically not a part of a
hypoallergenic eye shadow. Due to the fact that mascara is
frequently applied in the eye, there is also a great interest in
the possible toxicological effects (Hervouchet, 1965;
Kotsoulas, 1968; Jones & Postma, 1980) and the side-effects
of the application of mascara to the eye (Hervouchet, 1965;
Kotsoulas, 1968; Jones & Postma, 1980). Eye make -up
components are usually preserved with bactericides due to the
introduction of cosmetics into the eye vicinity. For those who
wear contact lenses, lenses or corneal shields can catch
mascara particles in contact lens. One of the reported risks of
prolonged lens use includes cosmetics, primarily mascara.
Formulation of mascara is done so that a cosmesis pro duct
can be easily used. The toxicity of the ingredients in mascara
has been reviewed and a list of dangerous ingredients has
been made (Panico et al., 2019). Numerous formulations of
the different kinds of pigments, waxes and oils have been
used are documented as early as the 1920s and mascara is still
one of the most popular cosmetic products available in most
countries today. Because of the relevant number of ocular
interactions that occur with the use of mascara, it was found
relevant to study the composition used for the purpose from a
safety point of view. Between the end of the 1960s and the
start of the 1970s, particular make up constituent lists have
been passed between the pigments, waxes and synthetic oils.
Although a high proportion of ocular con tacts is generated,
mascara formulation seems to be the least characterized in the
"mascara composition" presented, which may be a reflection
of both safety and trade secret issues. Active contact lens
wearers' use of eye shadows, eyeliners and mascara has been
investigated, in the context of the questions raised on the
cosmetic acceptability of these products. The safety of
treatment of currently available mascara products in south
eastern Italy has been studied. Based on the eye shadow
ingredients listed, the formulation of mascara in the range
surveyed offers a lower hazard in terms of potential eye
effects caused by the frequent use of the lens (lens use). Solid
mascara compositions are now readily accessible on the
market. However, the individuals contacted only form a small
part of the population, and the effects of individual
pigmentation are still very small (Couteau et al., 2022). The
use of mascara has been recorded from a broad cross-section
of the population among both lens wearers and non -wearers
in a number of other countries. Information on cosmetics
extensively used on the eyes has also been gathered by
clientele. In general, a limited number of over -the-counter
preparations of mascara commercially available in south -
eastern Italy have been analyzed. The amount of eye shadow,
eyeliner and mascara sold in 1979 has been communicated.

 Cultural and Aesthetic Variations in Eye Cosmetic
Practices
In many cultures, eye cosmetics are utilized, such as eye
lash enhancement, eyelid coloring and tinting for the
eyebrows. There is little understanding of cultural and
aesthetic variation except for some ethnographic/historical
work by Salis (1952), Evans (1972) and Kahn (1983). As
opposed to the Western traditions a nd western Middle
Eastern tradition, less attention is paid to Asian styles.
Practices and materials are related to racial and ethnic school,
region, and community (Chaudhry, 1983; Rosenberg, 2000).
Portraiture shows a variety of techniques and facial
expression with nineteenth century American Afro -
Americans (Montgomery, 1975). In some communities there
are taboos regarding cosmetics related to religion, health and
identity and in others, ritual application is related to self -
importance (Boulton, 1853; Alavi, 1993; Møller, 1996). In all
settings application is minimized with scientific invention
(Traquair, 1940). Modern functions are similar to those in
previous centuries – gender distinction (Decker, 1937;
Robinson, 1964), religious aspirations (Keightley,  1897;
Warren, 1898), beauty enhancement (Scott, 1945), and
socialization vehicle (Walton, 1960). However, the
perceptions of social reality have been changed (Sullivan,
1913). Meanings are built onto some items, making them no
longer cosmetics (Nichols, 1919), and other items emerge in
the wake of scientific and engineering advances (Magri,
1936). Current practices embody elements of large, broad
ancient treaties and textbooks, most often cited and
reproduced by modern specialties, such as G. Ugel (1776), Q.
Alfhazi (1861), F. Fortano (1880), Y. Ashraf (1907), U.
Orlandini (1935), and J. Oosaan (1935) (Halim, 2018). The
craftsmen and practitioners are two different trades (Salis,
1952), and the modern operators are considered as the
modern version of the an cient artists who consider modern
products as the return to historic essences (Rosenberg, 2000).
In the twentieth century, eye make -up is a combination of
technical achievement, knowledge and an interest in
prolonging the self, premodern emergence (Wang, 2015). In
the present day, shadows and powders are not simply used to
cover up, but to make and educate (Chaudhry, 1983). The
initial step taking heading of “drawing” links to material
movements instead of contour lines (O’Grady, 2000).

IV. METHODOLOGIES IN EYE MAKEUP
RESEARCH

Most of the eye makeup research uses qualitative
methods and fewer use existing statistics, and a variety of
methods and data sources are used. The surveys are the most
common, capturing perceptions related to color use, or
attitudes toward products, with a considerably lower being
the mixed method surveys that explore the wider meaning of
the art form (Mingzhe Li et al., 2022). The subject sometimes
allows for ethnography, which includes participant
observation and interviews typical of fieldwork, but rare ly
formal ethnographic documentation and inquiry are found.
Assessments of media and advertising imagery of the
discipline are predominantly undertaken using content
analysis. With the onset of the COVID -19 pandemic, two
studies have explored the importanc e of makeup in social
isolation, but because of the health crisis, some conclusions
may not be readily extrapolatable to the pre -pandemic era

 Synthesis of Findings and Gaps in the Literature
Eye make-up is a subject that can be studied in several
ways and is culturally relative. This is a very  obvious
diversity over several aspects (products, application styles,
supporting tools, associated gender expectations), which
narrows and gives hints of possible research directions.
Fashionable eye makeup products have proven to last a long
way despite the volatile consumer cravings, and have
continued to exist – even thrive – in an age of clean makeup
and skinimalism, which is why they deserve academic
attention. Eye make -up is also a product of an unusually
diverse consumer market, with differences not  only among
consumers but over time within single points of access in
terms of intentions and methods. Body aesthetics has long
been a scholarly interest, and cultural studies as well as
market engagement with and rejection of traditional and
mediated ways of doing eye make-up have thus shaped it as
an under-explored space of investigation. Eye makeup refers
to a variety of beauty cosmetics that are purposefully applied
to an individual's visible anatomical structures of one or more
eyelids, the epithelium surrounding the eyelids or the furrows
at these locations (Mingzhe Li et al., 2022). Many of these
objects have a great potential to alter the viewers'
interpretation of the models' identity, social qualities or
feelings. A cross -cultural investigation rev eals that while
some products (like mascara), for example, become
universally accepted as standard, each period or human group
has its own unique pattern of development and use. A review
of literature about makeup can easily list the various scholarly
perspectives taken over a wide span of time, but key questions
concerning a makeup subfield – definitional constraints,
critical points in time, product varieties and the changing
usage cultures of these products – remain poorly
circumscribed. These gaps are a ddressed and provide the
general framework of eye make-up as a cluster of individual
practices, advancing the debate in cultural analysis and
consumer economic research.

V. CONCLUSION

The art of make-up is a uniquely human art, which has
been around for more than 10,000 years. However, most
beauty research does not consider it. Even the use of
cosmetics has been little documented and most of the
evidence dates back more than a century. The focus has been
on face, skin, and hair—an area that has been neglected in the
study of beauty practices. In comparison, eye make up is
common in all cultures, is still very present in the personal
and advertising beauty, and still has strong sociocultu ral
reactions; in spite of all this, it has been largely neglected.
There is very little empirical research and even less theory to
be found in the literature. In contrast, little has been written
about its early use in the Ancient Near East, in Egypt or i n
classical Greece.

To understand eye makeup one must first be familiar
with the definition of the term and understand the wide -
ranging scope of the term. The most common one is to
highlight the clear eyelids, which is not something that is
regularly associated with other body cosmetic, (Biesiadecka
et al., 2023). For instance, over 90% of Japanese women
consider makeup for their eyelids to be essential for their
basic beauty, but such make-up is not typically worn only by
males (Mingzhe Li et al., 2022). Eyeliner, eye shadow, eye
