Abstract
This research offers insights to both researchers and policymakers to address major societal issues through the lens
of social marketing and consumer behavior research from the Web of Science relevant empirical evidence that may
aid in planning interventions in the future. The publication, leveraging bibliometric analysis using R-Biblioshiny,
examines research design parameters, techniques to gather data, means of analysis, visualization, and interpretation
frameworks. Using Bradford and Lotka’s laws as well as intellectual, social, and conceptual structure analyses, the study
illustrates the significance of the previous literature in sharing impactful research. Results suggest that social market-
ing and consumer behavior research, which was originally developed in the USA, has gained traction internationally
through increasing levels of collaboration and citation activity. Features covering wellness, ecology, accident pre-
vention, and community development show how the industry has the potential to address some important issues
for society. This study identifies leading authors, publications, and gaps in the literature providing a roadmap for aca-
demics and practitioners alike on the direction of future research and policies, thus enhancing the understanding
and use of social marketing and consumer behavior.

Introduction
The primary purpose of social marketing is to achieve
the “common good. ” Traditional commercial marketing
goals are generally pecuniary, though they can also have
positive societal consequences. One of the social mar -
keting purposes is to encourage people to change their
bad habits [7, 46]. Rather than talking about the product,
social marketing talks to the customer about their senti -
ments, psychology, and objective [5]. The phrase “social
marketing” often causes confusion among individuals,
since it is sometimes misunderstood and conflated with
other marketing strategies rooted in economic behavior,
Internet networking and advertising, commonly referred
to as “social media marketing” [18]. Social marketing
promotes behavior change for a social good [25], aligned
with public health, environmental sustainability [43],
or community well-being. It employs various types of
behavioral science, and psychological and marketing
principles to solve social problems. On the other hand,
social media marketing is a business strategy that uses
social media platforms such as Facebook, Instagram, and
Twitter to promote products, services, or brands. Social
media (marketing) is not social marketing. Social mar -
keting puts social outcomes first, whereas social media
marketing is most often an economic endeavor designed
to improve the performance of brands (increased visibil -
ity, engagement, sales, etc.). Nonetheless, social media
is a tool in social marketing campaigns designed to help
spread messages to wider audiences.
Social marketing has effectively altered behavior [10,
14, 38, 44]. However, social marketing efforts have also
been widely criticized, particularly in terms of the strate -
gies used [10], adverse outcomes [1], competing interests,

Literature review
Social marketing utilizes marketing principles to pro -
mote behavior change for societal benefit, addressing
health, safety, and sustainability issues [1, 3, 7, 10, 51].
Over the years, social marketing has been found to posi -
tively impact the behavior of consumers [13]. Studies
by Alhraiwil et  al. [2] mentioned social marketing cam -
paigns significantly increasing vaccine uptake, particu -
larly for children. In another study in Maryland, such
campaigns improved COVID-19 testing and vaccination
rates by addressing structural barriers [48]. Social mar -
keting campaigns have effectively tackled HIV/AIDS
awareness [53] and even successful video campaigns for
health workers [36]. Social marketing campaigns are also
associated with safety-oriented campaigns. A systematic
review of safety campaigns at the workplace revealed that
social marketing inventions in healthcare and construc -
tion reduced workplace injuries [27]. Research has even
identified social marketing campaigns influencing con -
sumer behavior at biomedical facilities [28] improving
personal well-being and lead mitigation in Madagascar
[8]. Social marketing campaigns were even successful in
promoting sustainable diets discouraging the reduction
in red meat consumption [52]. In Mexico, these cam -
paigns have promoted sustainable consumption through
awareness campaigns targeting eco-friendly products
[34]. In Europe, social marketing approaches promote
the idea of sustainable food labeling [17]. Research even
promotes the integration of social marketing to promote
workplace health initiatives and improve employee well-
being [4]. It is also found that educational social cam -
paigns in universities have integrated occupational health
and safety into sustainability curricula to promote safer
workplaces [24]. Studies even show that campaigns dur -
ing COVID-19 relied heavily on social media to engage
public audiences and spread health-related informa -
tion effectively [40]. With the impact of social market -
ing campaigns, social media is becoming an important
tool to engage consumers. Anjorin et al. [6] emphasized
the role of social media as a game-changer in influenc -
ing consumers’ purchase behaviors, brand adherence,
and engagement via influencer partnerships and content
genuineness. Likewise, Kanaveedu and Kalapurackal [23]
presented a systematic review of influencer marketing
by suggesting integrative frameworks that could be used
in future campaigns to drive consumer action. Future-
focused social marketing campaigns emphasize the role
of CSR and innovation in promoting environmentally
conscious consumer behavior as well [42].
Recent papers highlight the value of psychology for
marketing strategies. The key drivers of consumer deci -
sion-making are emotions, cognitive biases, and brand
story. According to Putra and Sheyoputri [44], “market -
ers recognized that an integrated framework of psycho -
logical theories captures the unique interconnections
between classical and contemporary marketing con -
cepts. ” The literature has shown that psychological theo -
ries play a vital role in determining consumer behavior in
social marketing. Some of the more successful behavio -
ral frameworks include but are not limited to the theory
of planned behavior, social cognitive theory, and health
belief model which provide a lot of insights into types of
pro-social behavior mostly focused on health [10]. The
SHIFT framework developed by White and others [35]
emphasizes psychological components such as social
influence, habit formation, and cognitive engagement to
drive sustainable consumer behavior. Social marketing
faces challenges unique to the heterogeneity of consumer
behavior across income levels and cultures. Contre -
ras-Velásquez et  al. [11] examined the behavior of low-
income consumers, who have previously been neglected
in marketing, and called for more inclusive marketing
approaches that embrace socioeconomic heterogeneity,
emphasizing the need for joint targeting strategies. As
an illustration, Shaw [49] explained that cultural values
and societal norms play a significant role in shaping con -
sumer preferences, emphasizing the necessity for cultur -
ally relevant campaigns. Rethinking social marketing, the
concept of sustainability emerged. Chea [9] highlighted
the growing consumer interest in sustainable products
and the importance of marketing in promoting environ -
mentally conscious choices. Efendioglu [15] reported on
the increasing importance of digital consumer behavior,
on trends such as mobile commerce, online shopping,
real-time engagement. On the other hand, Mou and
Benyoucef [39] investigated social commerce, identifying
trust as a key driver in consumer choices.
Although social marketing predominantly has much
scope in public health, public administration, and envi -
ronmental studies, there is also a lack of social mar -
keting as a separate discipline in the academic field.
Policymakers also lack awareness and understanding of
the social marketing option for changing citizens’ behav -
ior [30]. Consumer behavior is changing and increasingly
requires a multidisciplinary approach in social market -
ing. Marketers have the opportunity to design impactful

underexplored in the field. Bibliometric analysis that

and emerging themes can help us understand the evolu -

consumer behavior over the years. This approach helps
researchers identify linkages between different fields,
assess the influence of seminal theories, and pinpoint
areas of demographic or methodological underrepre -

exploration may reveal a gap in the literature around
low-income consumers or a lack of application of digi -

can inform future investigations by illuminating impact -

contributes to a comprehensive understanding of the pre-
sent landscape of social marketing research but also gen -

effectively tackle multidimensional social issues.
Thus, it is now imperative to know how social mar -
keting and the consumer have impacted the past to
shed more light on the future and a bibliometric analy -
sis will help us to visualize the picture. This study does
a bibliometric examination of the previous literature on
consumer behavior connected to social marketing in
business, management, science, and engineering. The
study examines the literature and identifies the most
important countries, authors, affiliations, and sources in
social marketing and related consumer behavior. Further-
more, this research intends to identify research streams
and themes by examining social marketing and related
consumer behavior literature. The utilization of topics
and research streams by policymakers, scholars, and aca -

Methods
Bibliometric analysis is  a quantitative method that uses
statistical tools to analyze academic publications.  It can
be used to understand how research is produced, organ -
ized, and interrelated [25]. Bibliometric analysis is used
across several peripherals. In psychology, it tracks evalu -
ation and discourse studies [33], business it identifies,
intelligent techniques for bankruptcy prediction [50]. In
medicine, it has helped in evaluating impactful research
[21]. In technology, it has helped to estimate the impact
of emerging innovations like nanotechnology [31], in
education it has identified influential works to shape cur -
ricula in research [45] and so on.
A tool called Biblioshiny was utilized for bibliometric
analysis. The tool is an R package that runs on the web
(Bibliometrix 3.0). The process for collecting bibliomet -
ric data was designed in two principal stages. First, we
defined the source of articles, and in this instance, the
Web of Science database was chosen due to the inclusion
of a lot of high-impact, peer-reviewed journals with solid
bibliometric analysis tools, making it consistent with
the scope of our research. The second step was building
the search query using the keywords “Social Marketing”
AND “Consumer Behavior” and applying further filters.
Among those were the selection of categories (business,
management, psychology), publication year, and docu -
ment type. This first dataset underwent a further exami -
nation of all its metadata where we filtered out duplicate/
irrelevant entries (as needed, we also manually checked
relevant keywords, abstracts, and even full texts). Stud -
ies were included (1) if they were peer-reviewed journal
articles that both included the term “social marketing”

The chosen methodology is a key part of effectively
answering the research question because it aligns with
the study’s goals and the type of data being analyzed. Bib-
liometric analysis was selected as it provides a system -
atic way to explore patterns, trends, and connections in
the existing literature, offering a clear picture of how the
field has evolved. This approach is ideal for identifying
influential authors, important publications, and emerg -
ing themes, all of which are central to understanding the
subject in depth. Additionally, bibliometric methods are
well suited for handling large volumes of data, making
the analysis thorough, reliable, and easy to replicate. Thus
this methodology was chosen because it is the best fit
for providing meaningful insights and comprehensively
addressing the research question.
The procedure for conducting bibliometric analysis is
narrated as a five-step process [54]. It starts with estab -
lishing a theme for the study (step 1). This stage urges the
researcher to craft the research questions and suggests
identifying the tools and techniques for the study. In step
2 (data collection), the researcher determines the source
to be selected for the study. Bibliometric analysis is car -
ried out in step 3, wherein the emphasis is on software
selection and analysis. In the visualization stage (step 4),
the researcher selects the tools and techniques for appro-
priate visualization. Finally, in the interpretation stage, a
discussion on the findings is reported.
The period of the study fell in between 1993 and 2021.
Several critical considerations guided the choice of the
time span (1993–2021) for the study. The year 1993 was
chosen as the earliest point because that was when aca -
demic research on social marketing started to gain openly
recognized legitimacy as an area of academic study with
the first publications in the mainstream academic mar -
keting journals. There are several reasons, however, why
we opted for 2021 as a cutoff year. First, the year 2021
experienced an unprecedented increase in research about
social marketing and consumer behavior, largely insti -
gated by the global effects of the COVID-19 pandemic.
Few areas received more attention than topics relevant to
social marketing including behavior change campaigns,
health marketing, and digital engagement strategies for
consumers during the pandemic. Further, the inclusion
of publications up to only 2021 values the capture of this
unassailably important body of research, representing a
crucial inflection point in the field. Secondly, it facilitates
the inclusion of studies with ample time for citations to
stack up and also face the rigors of academic peer review,
since studies newer than 2022 might not have the cita -
tion maturity required for a solid bibliometric analysis.
Third, the cutoff is in harmony with the time frame of our
study, allowing enough time to gather and systematically
analyze data without compromising the dataset integrity.

A thematic map is a pictorial representation of the
fields under investigation under four grids: basic theme,
motor theme, niche theme, and emerging or clinical
theme. Motor themes are topics that are both well devel -
oped and highly connected to other themes in a research
field. These themes are central to driving research for -
ward because they are important and extensively studied.
On the other hand, niche themes are also well developed
but less connected to the broader field. These themes are

Discussion
As a bibliometric analysis of social marketing and con -
sumer behavior research, this research provides impor -
tant insights into the trajectory and development of
the field, including trends in publication, topic areas of
focus, and key contributors to that body of work. Using
Biblioshiny application to analyze indexed articles in
the Web of Science database, this study depicts emerg -
ing trends as well as highlights future research and rel -
evant applications potential.
Our findings indicate that the study of social mar -
keting and consumer behavior is much more special -
ized but growing in strength. Peer-reviewed journals
like Journal of Business Research and Social Marketing
Quarterly have positioned themselves as the leading
outlets, and this is certainly a reflection of both their
interdisciplinary focus and their repute to publishing
behavior-based research. This emerging trend suggests
a maturing field, but also calls for diversity in policy
and setting. This dominance suggests that research -
ers or practitioners appreciate models that are empiri -
cally validated and interventions that are well verified.
This underlines the importance of publishing impactful
studies that should support actionable insights to the
social marketing strategies.
