Abstrak

This study investigates how location accessibility and the quality of the physical service
environment (servicescape) shape perceived value for money, customer satisfaction, and
customer loyalty within traditional micro -scale culinary businesses, particularly warung  soto.
The intensifying competition in the food industry has increased the importance of creating
meaningful dining experiences, making both spatial accessibility and environmental design
crucial determinants of customer evaluations. A quantitative research design was employed
by distributing an online questionnaire to 100 individuals who had previously visited a warung
soto. The data were analyzed using the SEM -PLS approach via SmartPLS  4.0 to assess
measurement validity, construct reliability, and the structural relationships among variables.
The findings reveal that both location accessibility and servicescape exert significant positive
effects on perceived value for money and customer satisfaction. Furthermore, perceived
value for money enhances customer satisfaction but does not directly lead to customer
loyalty, indicating that positive value perceptions must be accompanied by satisfying
experiences to encourage repeat patronage. In contrast, customer satisfaction emerged as
the strongest predictor of customer loyalty, emphasizing that a consistently positive dining
experience is essential for building long -term commitment. Overall, the study highlights the
strategic importance of enhancing accessibility, improving physical service environments,
and ensuring pleasant customer experiences to strengthen perceived value and foster loyalty
within traditional micro food enterprises. Future research is encouraged to broaden the scope
of variables and contexts to enrich insights into consumer behavior in the culinary sector

Abstract

Penelitian ini mengkaji bagaimana aksesibilitas lokasi dan kualitas lingkungan fisik layanan
(servicescape) membentuk persepsi nilai terhadap uang (perceived value for money),
kepuasan pelanggan, dan loyalitas pelanggan pada usaha kuliner skala mikro tradisional,
khususnya warung soto. Di tengah intensitas persaingan  industri makanan yang semakin
ketat, aksesibilitas spasial dan desain lingkungan menjadi determinan krusial dalam evaluasi
pelanggan. Penelitian ini menggunakan desain kuantitatif dengan menyebarkan kuesioner
daring kepada 100 responden yang pernah mengunjungi warung soto. Data dianalisis
menggunakan pendekatan Structural Equation Modeling -Partial Least Square (SEM -PLS)
melalui SmartPLS 4.0 untuk menguji validitas pengukuran, reliabilitas konstruk, dan
hubungan struktural antar variabel. Temuan menunjukkan bahwa aksesibilitas lokasi dan
servicescape berpengaruh positif dan signifikan terhadap persepsi nilai dan kepuasan
pelanggan. Lebih lanjut, persepsi nilai meningkatkan kepuasan pelanggan, namun tidak
berpengaruh langsung terhadap loyalitas pelanggan; hal ini mengindikasikan bahwa
persepsi nilai yang positif harus disertai dengan pengalaman yang memuaskan untuk
mendorong kunjungan ulang. Sebaliknya, kepuasan pelanggan muncul sebagai prediktor
terkuat bagi loyalitas pelanggan, yang menegaskan bahwa pengalaman bersantap yang
konsisten sangat penting untuk membangun komitmen jangka panjang. Secara keseluruhan,
penelitian ini menyoroti pentingnya strategi peningkatan aksesibilitas, perbaikan lingkungan
fisik layanan, dan jaminan pengalaman pelanggan yang menyenangkan untuk memperkuat
persepsi nilai dan menumbuhkan loyalitas dalam usaha kuliner mikro tradisional.

INTRODUCTION
The rapid growth of Indonesia’s culinary industry has driven significant shifts in
consumer dining preferences and intensified competition among local food businesses
(Suhartanto, 2024).  Amid this evolving landscape, micro -enterprises offering traditional
foods, such as warung soto, continue to play a vital role in preserving culinary heritage while
contributing to local economic development. Nevertheless, the rise of modern cafés, digital -
based food delivery services, and franchise restaurants has elevated consumer
expectations, compelling traditional food businesses to enhance their functional attributes
and service experience. Recent studies highlight that both location accessibility and the
physical service environment are key determinants of customer satisfaction and loyalty (Tran
et al., 2020a) . Location accessibility —reflected in proximity, visibility, and ease of access
significantly shapes perceived value and visit intention. Meanwhile, servicescape
components such as spatial layout, cleanliness, and ambience have been shown to elicit
emotional responses and influence customer satisfaction, as evidenced by both foundational
research (Wakefield & Blodgett, 1996 ) and recent systematic reviews (An et al., 2023) . The
importance of experiential attributes is further underscored by the substantial market size of

 To examine how location accessibility and servicescape quality affect customer
satisfaction and loyalty, this study integrates three theoretical perspectives, Servicescape
Theory (Bitner et al., 1992) , Perceived Value Theory (Zeithaml, 1988), and the Satisfaction–
Loyalty Paradigm (Oliver, 1999) into a cohesive conceptual framework. Servicescape Theory
asserts that the physical and atmospheric attributes of a service setting, including layout,
cleanliness, lighting, and design, serve as environmental stimuli that shape customers’
cognitive and emotional reactions, ultimately influencing their behavioral responses (Bitner et
al., 1992) . This viewpoint is supported by Al Halbusi et al. (2020) , who showed that
environmental cues significantly affect customers’ perceptions and satisfaction. Likewise,
Jeon et al. (2021) found that ambient conditions and spatial organization enhance customers’
emotional experiences, which subsequently drive higher satisfaction and loyalty. The theory
has been extensively applied in various service contexts such as restaurants (Tran et al.,
2020b), retail (Harris & Ezeh, 2008) , and tourism (An et al., 2023). In parallel, Perceived
Value Theory describes how consumers assess the balance between the benefits they
receive and the costs they incur, positioning perceived value as a key predictor of
satisfaction and loyalty (Dodds et al., 1991; Zeithaml, 1988). Within micro-enterprises, where
affordability and authentic experience are central perceived value functions as a cognitive
mechanism that links environmental cues, such as authenticity, ambience quality, and
interpersonal interaction, to customer satisfaction. Consistent findings are reported by Yang
et al. (2023) , who noted that authenticity and environmental features elevate perceived
value, which then fosters customer satisfaction. Moreover, the Satisfaction–Loyalty Paradigm
highlights satisfaction as a fundamental antecedent of customers’ repurchase intentions and
word-of-mouth behavior (Cronin et al., 2000; Oliver, 1999) . By integrating these three
theoretical perspectives, this study develops a dual -path conceptual model in which
servicescape and location accessibility act as stimuli shaping customers’ cognitive
evaluations of perceived value, which subsequently drive emotional satisfaction and loyalty
toward traditional culinary micro-enterprises.
 This study seeks to address gaps in the existing literature by developing an
integrated conceptual model that combines Servicescape Theory, Perceived Value Theory,
and the Satisfaction –Loyalty Paradigm to explain customer behavior in traditional culinary
micro-enterprises. The proposed model offers a novel perspective by examining how
environmental stimuli and spatial factors, namely location accessibility and servicescape
quality are cognitively processed through perceived value, which subsequently generates
emotional satisfaction and long -term loyalty. Prior research has seldom explored such a
multi-layered mechanism, particularly within informal, low -cost service settings where
economic efficiency and experiential authenticity jointly shape customer evaluations
(Suhartanto, 2024) . Accordingly, this study is expected to deliver significant theoretical

RESEARCH METHOD
The study applies a quantitative, descriptive research strategy to assess the
proposed conceptual model and its associated hypotheses using data provided directly by
respondents. It draws on both primary and secondary sources. Primary information was
obtained through an online survey distributed to individuals who had previously visited
traditional micro -scale food outlets, specifically warung soto. Participants were selected
through purposive sampling, limited to those aged 18 –60 who had prior dining experience at
such venues. In total, 100 valid responses were collected and prepared for subsequent
analysis.
 This study utilized a non -probability sampling approach with a purposive sampling
technique, in which participants were selected based on their alignment with the research
objectives. The targeted respondents were consumers in Indonesia aged 18 –60 years who
had previously dined at traditional culinary micro -enterprises, specifically warung soto. The
minimum required sample size was determined following the guideline of Hair Jr. and Ringle
(2021), who recommend a ratio of at least five respondents per indicator. With 28 indicators
included in this study, the minimum sample requirement amounted to 140 respondents, while
also adhering to the general rule that sample size should not be fewer than 100. Data were
gathered through a self -administered online questionnaire disseminated across multiple
social media platforms over a four -week period, from September to October 2025, yielding
100 valid responses that fulfilled the eligibility criteria.
 The dataset was processed using SmartPLS  (PLS-SEM) through a comprehensive
sequence of measurement assessments. Convergent validity was evaluated by reviewing the
magnitude of each indicator’s loading and the Average Variance Extracted (AVE). Indicators
were retained when their loadings were positive and sufficiently strong, generally sought to
be above 0.708, while a construct was considered to satisfy convergent validity when its AVE
surpassed the 0.50 threshold. Reliability was then examined using Cronbach’s alpha and
Composite Reliability (CR), where alpha values greater than 0.60 and CR values exceeding
0.70 signified adequate internal consistency under PLS-SEM guidelines. Discriminant validity
was confirmed using the Heterotrait–Monotrait (HTMT) ratio, employing the widely accepted
cutoff of <0.90. To evaluate issues of multicollinearity, the Variance Inflation Factor (VIF) was
also inspected, and values below 3 indicated that collinearity was not problematic.

RESULT AND DISCUSSION
Respondent Characteristics
 The sample comprised 100 participants, with women representing a slightly higher
proportion (58%) compared to men (42%). The age profile was primarily concentrated in the
20–29 age range (49%), while respondents aged 30 –39 and 40 –49 each accounted for
19.5%. Only a small fraction were below 20 years old (7.5%) or above 50 years old (4.5%).
Regarding educational attainment, more than half of the respondents were graduates of
senior high school or vocational school (51.5%), followed by bachelor’s degree holders
(30%) and diploma graduates (18.5%). In terms of occupational background, students
formed the largest segment (52.5%), whereas 30% were employed in government or private
institutions, 16.5% identified as entrepreneurs, and 1% worked in other fields. Patterns of
visitation also varied: 46.5% reported visiting a warung soto one to two times per month, 30%
visited three to five times monthly, and 23.5% visited approximately once per week.Spending
behavior per visit further reflected consumption diversity. The majority spent less than IDR
20,000 (39.5%), while 41.5% typically spent between IDR 20,000 –50,000, and 19%
exceeded IDR 50,000 per visit. Collectively, these descriptive attributes portray a
heterogeneous consumer base and offer a comprehensive depiction of customer
characteristics within the traditional warung soto context.

Descriptive Analysis
 The descriptive analysis offers a general depiction of the respondents who comprise
the sample for assessing consumer behavior in traditional warung  soto micro-enterprises. A
total of 100 individuals took part in the survey, with the majority falling within the 20 –29 age
bracket (65%). This distribution suggests that the primary consumer group consists of young
adults in their economically active stage. Participants younger than 20 years old accounted
for 15%, whereas those aged 30 –39 and 40 –49 made up 9% and 6%, respectively.
Additionally, 5% of respondents were over the age of 50.
With respect to occupational background, the sample was predominantly composed
of students, who accounted for 69% of respondents. This was followed by individuals
employed in government or private institutions (21%), entrepreneurs (8%), and homemakers
(2%). This composition indicates that warung soto patrons are largely drawn from groups
characterized by high mobility and a preference for convenient, easily accessible meal
options. In terms of visitation patterns, 55% reported dining at a warung  soto one to two
times per month, while 32% visited three to five times monthly, and 13% indicated a
frequency of more than five visits per month.
In terms of expenditure, the largest proportion of respondents reported spending IDR
20,000–50,000 per visit (46%), followed by those who typically spent less than IDR 20,000
(42%). Only a smaller segment, amounting to 12%, indicated spending above IDR 50,000
per visit. These patterns suggest that warung  soto is generally viewed as an affordable food
option, particularly appealing to younger customers.

then analyzed to determine the relationships among variables by examining the path
coefficients, R² values, T -statistics, and significance levels derived from the bootstrapping
process.

indicators achieved loading factors between 0.721 and 0.903, confirming that each item
meets the criteria for convergent validity, and no indicator removal was required. A graphical
