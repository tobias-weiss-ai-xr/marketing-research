Abstract
The competition between electric vehicles (EVs) and internal combustion engine vehicles is fundamentally shaped by
how heterogeneous consumers respond to technological and market change. This study develops a decision-based
framework that links micro-level adoption behavior with macro-level ﬂeet dynamics. Using China as the core case,
we identify three consumer segments: new, replacement, and renewal buyers, and show that they react differently to
purchase cost, usability, and charging conditions. Embedding these segments in a dynamic simulation suggests a
conditional phased transition under the baseline scenario: EV stock may overtake ICEVs around 2030, and the
expansion of the secondhand EV market after 2035 could reshape buyer composition and strengthen the role of
replacement demand. A cross-national comparison of 51 countries further shows that economic development stages
and institutional capacity mediate how these behavioral logics translate into market outcomes, from infrastructure and
affordability constraints in emerging EV markets to norm-driven, autonomous adoption in advanced EV markets. The
ﬁndings highlight that EV transitions are driven by the interaction between consumer heterogeneity and market
development, providing a behavioral basis for stage-speci ﬁc policies and infrastructure strategies worldwide.
Introduction
The transition toward electric vehicle (EV) represents a fundamental strategy to address global climate change and
enhance energy security. The rapid pace of EV market diffusion is profoundly reshaping the passenger vehicle
industry landscape (Link et al., 2025; Sun et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2024a). From 2015 to 2024, global passenger
vehicle sales increased from 71 million to 80 million units, with EV sales share surging dramatically from 0.7% to
22%, signaling rapid substitution dynamics (International Energy Agency, 2025). This transition embodies techno-
logical competition between EV and internal combustion engine vehicle (ICEV). The success of either technological
pathway mainly hinges on satisfying differentiated consumer demands across multiple dimensions, including per-
formance, cost, and convenience (Victoria et al., 2022). The European Union exempli ﬁes proactive engagement in
this transition through comprehensive policy frameworks, such as phase-out timelines for ICEV and EV purchase
subsidies. Nevertheless, the effectiveness of these policy interventions critically depends on consumer purchasing
behavior and market response dynamics (Lagomarsino et al., 2023; Wolske et al., 2020). Under market-driven

conditions where consumer demand in ﬂuences production, changes in consumer preferences become central drivers

Although global EV sales penetration already reached 22%, it should be noticed that EV stock penetration is still
around the 4.5% level (International Energy Agency, 2025). Therefore, the competition between internal combustion
engine vehicles (ICEVs) and EVs is expected to persist for decades, driven by evolving consumer preferences across
all groups (Nguyen-Tien et al., 2025). Passenger vehicle consumers can generally be categorized into three groups:
(1) new consumers, who purchase an additional vehicle —either as their ﬁrst car or as an extra vehicle without
replacing an existing one; (2) replacement consumers, who are upgrading or switching out an existing vehicle with a
new one; and (3) renewal consumers, who replace a vehicle that has reached the end of its service life or is being
decommissioned. These groups differ signi ﬁcantly in terms of their purchasing motivations, decision-making
behaviors, and responsiveness to policy measures. As EV market penetration deepens, behavioral distinctions across
consumer groups become more nuanced and complex, requiring increasingly precise and targeted policy interven-
tions (Sun and You, 2024; Trutnevyte et al., 2022).
Existing research on the EV transition has predominantly emphasized supply-side factors, particularly technolo-
gical advancements as the primary driver of market diffusion (Link et al., 2024; Zeng et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2023).
Ren et al. ( 2023) employed logistic functions to project EV diffusion across cities at various development stages,
whereas Li et al. ( 2018) extended the Bass diffusion model to assess hydrogen vehicle market penetration under
different infrastructure development scenarios. While technological and policy developments play a pivotal role in
shaping the transition, demand-side dynamics are equally crucial in determining its speed and trajectory (Zielonka
and Trutnevyte, 2025; McCollum et al., 2018; Fisch-Romito et al., 2025; Speizer et al., 2024). Edelenbosch et al.
(2018) argue that technological progress and consumer preferences jointly shape the technological transition, whereas
Verrier et al. ( 2022) highlight consumer demand factors as essential drivers of technological evolution. However,
many existing transition models tend to oversimplify or neglect dynamic consumer behavior due to challenges related
to complexity and parameter sensitivity, thereby constraining bottom-up forecasts from the consumer perspective
(Assis et al., 2023). Previous research on consumer behavior has primarily concentrated on individual-level decision-
making processes. For instance, Greene et al. ( 2014) utilized nested multinomial logit models to capture consumer
vehicle choice behavior, while Cirillo and Xu ( 2011) integrated multinomial logit and hidden Markov models to
reﬂect dynamic decision-making components. Although such studies offer valuable micro-level insights, they seldom
explore how decision-making varies across different consumer types in the context of evolving market structures.
From the perspective of consumer behavior research, the adoption of new technologies is not determined solely by
price or performance, but rather results from the joint in ﬂuence of socio-psychological mechanisms and external
contextual factors. In addressing the mechanisms underlying consumer technology adoption, the technology
acceptance model (TAM) and the theory of planned behavior (TPB) provide important theoretical foundations for
understanding how consumers accept new technologies. The TAM proposed by Davis ( 1989) emphasizes that
perceived usefulness (PU) and perceived ease of use (EOU) are the core variables shaping individuals ’ adoption
intentions. Subsequent studies have further broadened the explanatory scope of technology adoption by comparing
and integrating TAM with TPB and related theories (Venkatesh et al., 2003; Feng et al., 2021). However, TAM still
has limitations in explaining technology adoption in the real world, particularly in its insuf ﬁcient attention to social
context, institutional environment, and speci ﬁc usage situations (Scherer and Teo, 2019). Therefore, it is necessary to
combine socio-psychological factors with external in ﬂuencing factors under more realistic market and social con-
ditions in order to provide a more comprehensive analysis of consumer technology acceptance behavior (Putri and
Kresnanto, 2026). In addition, consumer preferences for electric vehicles also exhibit signi ﬁcant heterogeneity,
meaning that the same vehicle attribute may carry different meanings and exert different effects across consumer
groups. Consumers with different income levels (Zhao et al., 2026), those living in different climatic regions (Han
and Sun, 2024), and those located in different city tiers (Huang et al., 2021) all show different willingness to adopt
electric vehicles. Furthermore, preferences for electric vehicles are not static, but evolve dynamically with market
diffusion and the accumulation of consumer experience, and the differences in preferences across consumer groups
may also change over time (Jia and Chen, 2023). Therefore, from a perspective that is closer to real-world contexts,

incorporating socio-psychological mechanisms, changes in the external environment, and consumer heterogeneity

China, as the world ’s largest passenger vehicle market, offers a compelling context in which to examine these
evolving dynamics. In 2024, China ’s EV market penetration rate has reached 48%, marking a rapid transition from
the introductory to the growth phase. The Chinese consumer market demonstrates substantial heterogeneity, not only
in terms of consumer segments (new, replacement, and renewal), but also in income distribution, offering rich
empirical data for behavioral studies across income levels (Ye et al., 2025). China ’s comprehensive EV policy
framework, including purchase subsidies, infrastructure development, and vehicle scrappage and renewal programs —
offers valuable insights for policy design and implementation (Zhang et al., 2024). Recent initiatives like the “Action
Plan for Large-Scale Equipment Renewal and Consumer Goods Trade-In ” highlight signiﬁcant policy advancements
aimed at accelerating EV adoption. This evolving context provides invaluable empirical grounds for evaluating policy
effectiveness and investigating the mechanisms of EV technological transition.
Drawing on China ’s extensive empirical data, this study addresses the following key research questions: (1) How
do decision-making characteristics vary across different passenger vehicle consumer segments? (2) How do con-
sumers respond differently to EVs and ICEVs under varying policy and market conditions? (3) What transition
pathways and policy optimization strategies can be proposed to accelerate global EV adoption?

Methods
Research framework
This study focuses on China ’s passenger vehicle market as the primary case and further extends the analysis through a
cross-national comparative framework covering 51 countries. To guide the research, we develop a comprehensive
analytical framework consisting of ﬁve interrelated components: (1) decision-making analysis across consumer
segments, to capture heterogeneity in behavioral responses among new, replacement, and renewal consumers; (2)
evolutionary trend analysis of China ’s passenger vehicle market based on consumer choice dynamics, enabling
projection of structural shifts in vehicle stock composition; (3) scenario-based analysis of consumer choices under
different policy and market conditions, including the effects of subsidies and key product attributes; (4) multi-group
analysis of income-strati ﬁed consumer responsiveness, identifying variation in EV adoption potential across different
socioeconomic groups; and (5) cross-national cluster analysis of global EV market development trajectories, enabling
typological classi ﬁcation of countries based on transition patterns. The research framework is shown in Fig. 1.
These ﬁve components are conceptually and empirically interconnected. The analysis of consumer decision-
making forms the behavioral foundation of the study, offering a detailed understanding of how new, replacement, and
renewal consumers differ in their adoption preferences and sensitivities. These behavioral insights directly inform the
market evolution analysis, where shifts in consumer preferences are translated into projected changes in the com-
position of China ’s passenger vehicle stock over time. Scenario-based analysis builds on both behavior and market
projections to simulate consumer responses under different policy and market conditions providing a dynamic
assessment. The income-strati ﬁed analysis further enhances these insights by revealing how adoption behavior varies
across socioeconomic groups, enabling more granular assessments of market potential and distributional policy
impacts. Finally, the cross-national clustering synthesizes the cumulative ﬁndings from the preceding components to
classify countries into distinct EV transition trajectories, offering a comparative perspective on global diffusion
patterns and their underlying behavioral and structural drivers.
We utilize China ’s multifaceted market as the primary site for mechanism discovery, where the interactions
between micro-behavior and macro-dynamics are decoded in depth. Subsequently, the analysis is generalized through
a cross-national framework covering 51 countries, which serves to test the universal applicability of the behavioral
logics identi ﬁed in the China case across diverse socio-technical regimes.
Consumer behavior model
The behavioral model was estimated using survey data collected between August and November 2024, spanning over
20 provinces and autonomous regions in China. Respondents were recruited and surveyed online via Wenjuanxing, a
professional survey platform in China. To ensure data reliability, questionnaires with abnormally short or long
completion times, as well as those exhibiting straight-lining behavior, were excluded. This quality control process
ultimately yielded a ﬁnal sample of 3840 valid questionnaires from passenger vehicle consumers. The questionnaire
was designed to distinguish three consumer segments, namely new consumers, replacement consumers, and renewal
consumers, and to collect information on vehicle choice preferences, psychological perceptions, and key socio-
demographic characteristics. Detailed questionnaire items and sample structure are provided in Section A of the
Supplementary Information.
We construct a mixed multinomial logit model based on random utility theory to analyze the purchasing decision
mechanisms of different consumer types. The model assumes that consumer behavior is jointly shaped by socio-
psychological evaluation and external contextual conditions.
The socio-psychological dimension is represented by PU and EOU from the TAM, together with SN from the
Theory of TPB. PU re ﬂects the extent to which consumers perceive EVs as practically valuable and capable of
meeting their daily mobility needs. EOU re ﬂects the extent to which consumers perceive EVs as easy to use, operate,

and integrate into everyday travel routines. SN captures the in ﬂuence of social opinion, peer evaluation, and the
attitudes of family members and friends toward EV adoption.
The external contextual dimension captures the concrete market and policy conditions under which vehicle choices
are made, including purchase cost, fuel cost, charging time, driving range, and policy variables such as vehicle
purchase restrictions. By combining these two categories of factors, the model is designed to capture how socio-
psychological evaluation and real-world usage conditions jointly shape consumer decisions. Detailed questionnaire
items and parameter settings are reported in Section A of the Supplementary Information. Following the IEA
statistical convention and China ’s new energy vehicle classi ﬁcation, the EV category in this study is treated as an
aggregated category that includes battery electric vehicles and plug-in hybrid/extended-range electric vehicles.
Therefore, the model does not separately distinguish BEVs from plug-in hybrids, but focuses on the behavioral
contrast between EVs and conventional ICEVs.
Based on this theoretical framework, we propose the following research hypotheses.
H1. Perceived usefulness positively affects consumers ’ preference for EVs.
H2. Perceived ease of use positively affects consumers ’ preference for EVs.
H3. Subjective norms positively affect consumers ’ preference for EVs.
H4. External contextual factors, including purchase cost, fuel cost, charging time, driving range, and policy
conditions, signi ﬁcantly in ﬂuence consumers ’ vehicle choices.
H5. The effects of socio-psychological and external contextual factors differ across new, replacement, and renewal
consumers.
These hypotheses allow us to examine not only whether consumers prefer EVs or ICEVs, but also how different
consumer groups respond to social-psychological perceptions and market conditions in distinct ways.

EV evolution dynamics model
To represent the changing psychological dimensions of EV adoption over time, this study models the evolution of
three consumer perception variables: SN, EOU, and PU. Rather than treating these perceptions as ﬁxed, we assume
that they change as EV markets expand and as consumers accumulate more social exposure and practical knowledge.
This approach allows us to connect micro-level consumer perceptions with broader market development in a more
interpretable way. Speci ﬁcally, we use observable market conditions as proxies for the social and practical envir-
onments in which these perceptions are formed. The behavioral rationale is as follows.
(a) Subjective norms (SN).
SN captures the extent to which consumers perceive EV adoption as socially supported or socially expected. In
this study, we link SN to the cumulative penetration rate of EVs. The underlying assumption is that, as EVs
become more visible in everyday life, consumers are more likely to encounter positive signals from peers, social
networks, and the broader public environment. This process can be understood as a bandwagon effect or
positive network externality, as EVs become more visible and more widely adopted by peers, perceived
uncertainty decreases, and subsequent adoption becomes more likely. This visibility strengthens the perception
that choosing an EV is no longer a marginal decision, but an increasingly legitimate and socially recognized
one. The implication is that EV adoption is shaped not only by private cost-bene ﬁt evaluation, but also by the
social normalization of the technology over time.
(b) Perceived ease of use (EOU).
EOU reﬂects the extent to which consumers perceive EVs as easy to operate and convenient to incorporate into
daily routines. We link this construct to the BEV-to-charging-point ratio, which serves as an indicator of
charging accessibility. The assumption here is that better charging availability reduces the practical burden of
EV use, including concerns about recharging and everyday inconvenience. As a result, consumers are more
likely to view EVs as manageable and compatible with regular travel needs. The implication is that ease of use
is not only a matter of product design, but also of infrastructure conditions that shape the everyday usability of
the technology.
(c) Perceived usefulness (PU).
PU captures consumers ’ belief that EVs can provide meaningful functional bene ﬁts in daily mobility. In our
framework, this construct is linked to the penetration rate of intelligent EVs, which re ﬂects the growing presence of
advanced features such as connectivity and assisted driving. The assumption is that, as these features become more
widespread, consumers increasingly perceive EVs as offering practical advantages beyond basic substitution for
internal combustion engine vehicles (ICEVs). The implication is that perceived usefulness evolves not only through
direct experience, but also through broader technological upgrading in the market, which reshapes how consumers
evaluate the value of EVs.
Taken together, these assumptions allow us to interpret psychological change as part of the broader transition
process. They imply that consumer perceptions are socially and technologically embedded, and that changes in
market development can gradually reshape the ways in which EVs are understood, evaluated, and adopted. We
predict the long-term trend changes for three key psychological variables: PU, EOU, and SN. These forecasts are
detailed in Supplementary Information Section E.

We systematically examine vehicle-purchasing behavior across three distinct consumer segments: new consumers,
replacement consumers, and renewal consumers. We explore the market scale and evolution mechanisms associated
with each consumer group through an analysis of the passenger vehicle market ’s consumption structure and dynamic
evolution. Speci ﬁcally, we determine the scale of new consumers based on calculations of changes in passenger
vehicle stock, which quanti ﬁes incremental demand for vehicles. The size of the replacement consumer segment is
estimated using transaction volumes from the secondhand vehicle market, re ﬂecting the internal circulation and
upgrading patterns within the vehicle stock. Finally, we assess the scale of renewal consumers based on vehicle
scrappage volumes, thereby capturing repurchase demand driven by vehicle retirement. Detailed methodological
descriptions are provided in Supplementary Information Section F.
The analytical bridge connecting the behavioral model to macro-level transition projections is established through a
structured four-step process. First, a mixed multinomial logit model is employed to calculate the utilities for three
mutually exclusive alternatives: purchasing an EV, purchasing an ICEV, or choosing to wait. These utilities are
estimated under time-varying scenarios de ﬁned by ﬁve critical attributes: EV purchase price, driving range, fuel
prices, fast-charging time, and purchase restriction policies.
Second, these estimated utilities are converted into individual purchase probabilities using the logit transformation.
Third, these probabilities are aggregated by integrating them with the population scales of three distinct consumer
segments—new, replacement, and renewal buyers —whose sizes are derived from the stock- ﬂow analysis. By
weighting the choice probabilities with the respective segment sizes, we calculate the aggregate EV market pene-
tration rate for each period. Finally, these behaviorally-derived market shares are fed into the stock-based framework
to update the social ownership and simulate the long-term evolution of China ’s vehicle ﬂeet. This explicit linkage
ensures that macro-level structural shifts are fundamentally rooted in micro-level consumer responses to evolving
market conditions.

scenarios, including variations in subsidy policies and modi ﬁcations to product attributes. Various policy interven-
tions, such as subsidies and product attributes like EV charging time and driving range, are incorporated into these

hypothetical scenarios. The design of these scenarios aims to simulate how different consumer segments, such as new

By systematically adjusting the parameters within these scenarios, the impact on consumer decision-making within
each segment is assessed, with particular attention given to the in ﬂuence of ﬁnancial incentives and product char-

To account for uncertainty in long-term projections, we conducted uncertainty analyses for key assumptions in the
behavioral and stock- ﬂow framework. In addition to EV price uncertainty, we further considered uncertainty related
to psychological variables, vehicle scrappage, and secondhand vehicle market development. Details are provided in
Supplementary Information Sections C, D, and J –L.
Income-based multi-group model
To investigate how income disparities in ﬂuence EV adoption decisions, consumers are segmented into three income-
based groups: low-income, middle-income, and high-income consumers. A multi-group analysis is employed to
explore and compare the decision-making processes of these distinct income groups throughout the EV adoption
journey. This model allows for the identi ﬁcation of income-driven variations in the factors in ﬂuencing vehicle
purchasing behavior. This study distinguishes consumers by purchasing stage, including new consumers, replacement
consumers, and renewal consumers, rather than by age group. Therefore, buyer age is not used as a grouping variable
in the current model.
Each income group is analyzed separately to assess its sensitivity to a variety of in ﬂuencing factors, including
ﬁnancial incentives, product attributes (such as charging time and driving range), and broader market conditions (such
as fuel prices and infrastructure development). Additionally, factors such as perceived ease of use, perceived use-
fulness, and subjective norms are considered to understand how these variables interact with income levels to shape
consumer preferences and decisions.

Results and discussion
Decision-making of various consumer groups
The decision logics of the three consumer segments-new consumers, replacement consumers, and renewal consumers
reﬂect diverse preferences at various stages of EV market evolution. New consumers make EV adoption decisions
primarily in ﬂuenced by economic and psychological factors. Among economic factors, purchase cost ( β = −0.17)
exerts the strongest negative in ﬂuence, as shown in Fig. 2. This indicates that initial vehicle purchase price constitutes
the primary consideration for new consumers (Domarchi and Cherchi, 2023). Among psychological factors, the

inﬂuence of perceived ease of use is approximately 29% stronger than subjective norms and 59% greater than
perceived usefulness. These results highlight the critical role of operational convenience and accessibility in the
decision-making process for new consumers, with a clear preference for ease of operation over advanced vehicle
performance features. Furthermore, subject norms in ﬂuence decisions more strongly than perceived usefulness by
24%, highlighting the importance of social opinions and group evaluations for new consumers. This behavioral
pattern suggests a potential reliance on external information and social references, which may be attributed to the
limited direct driving experience typically associated with new buyers.
In contrast, replacement consumers exhibit a more complex decision-making process, shaped by interactions
among economic, policy, technical, and psychological factors. Economically, purchase cost ( β = −0.25) has a
stronger negative impact on replacement consumers than on new consumers, re ﬂecting not only the investment in a
new vehicle but also the loss associated with trading in an old vehicle —a sign of heightened loss aversion. Regarding
policy factors, vehicle purchase restriction policies ( β = 0.22) emerge as a signi ﬁcant positive in ﬂuence, re ﬂecting
heightened policy sensitivity developed through long-term experience with ICEVs. Notably, replacement consumers
exhibit a strong negative response to perceived usefulness ( β = −0.27), suggesting that advertised intelligent features
and advanced technologies do not resonate well with this group. The negative coef ﬁcient for perceived usefulness
potentially indicates a form of psychological resistance among replacement consumers. On the other hand, repla-
cement consumers show an exceptionally strong positive response to perceived ease of use, with its in ﬂuence being
approximately 67% greater than other factors. This underscores their preference for simplicity and operational
convenience. Overall, replacement consumers display a dual characteristic of technological conservatism and
pragmatic orientation, favoring simplicity and ease of use over complex innovations.

surpasses the inﬂuence observed among new and replacement consumers, with an increase of about 87% compared to

key determinant in their decision-making process. Economically, purchase cost ( β = −0.21) exhibits slightly less
negative in ﬂuence compared to replacement consumers but greater than new consumers, highlighting mature and
rational pricing evaluations within this group. A key distinction for renewal consumers is their concern about
charging time, which directly stems from their daily operational needs developed through prolonged vehicle use. As
the EV market matures, the scale of renewal consumers is expected to grow substantially, and their continued focus
on operational convenience and daily usability will signi ﬁcantly shape the long-term growth and development of the
EV market.

The initial stage is marked by the continued dominance of ICEVs. During this period, the ICEV stock grows
rapidly, increasing from approximately 50 million units in 2010 to around 220 million units by 2020, with an annual
growth rate of 15.87%. Although EVs experience rapid growth, their market share remains modest. In this early

direct economic incentives such as vehicle purchase subsidies and tax exemptions. The secondhand vehicle market
during this stage is predominantly ICEV-dominated, with secondhand EV transactions remaining scarce. This is
likely due to consumer concerns regarding the residual values and battery lifespans of used EVs, which create
uncertainty about their long-term viability. Consequently, new consumers prefer purchasing new vehicles with
transparent pricing and high reliability, which aligns with their preference for low-risk investments (Pares Olguin et
al., 2025).
This second stage represents a behavior-driven acceleration. The model suggests a potential crossover point where
EV stock may overtake ICEVs, a transition contingent upon the synergetic effects of social norms and infrastructure
maturity. Rather than a deterministic forecast, this peak in ICEV scrappage serves as a strategic window that depends
on the sustained evolution of trade-in policies and consumer trust. Concurrently, the volume of ICEV scrappage
reaches its peak around 2032, with an estimated 19 million units being scrapped. This creates an optimal timing
window for implementing effective trade-in policies. Furthermore, the secondhand vehicle market begins to undergo
electriﬁcation, with EVs aged 3 –6 years expected to constitute over 30% of transactions in this segment. Policies
speciﬁcally targeting vehicle replacement within this age group are expected to signi ﬁcantly stimulate consumer
willingness to replace older ICEVs with new or used EVs.
The ﬁnal stage marks the transition to a mature EV-dominated market. ICEV stock experiences a substantial
decline, dropping from around 79 million units in 2035 to roughly 15 million units by 2050. EV scrappage volumes
surpass ICEV scrappage for the ﬁrst time around 2040, reaching an estimated 33 million units by 2050. During this
phase, the market shifts from incremental expansion to stock optimization, with renewal consumers emerging as the
dominant consumer segment. Their growing recognition of the long-term value of EVs enhances the comprehensive
electriﬁcation of the secondhand vehicle market. Notably, vehicles aged 0 –3, 3 –6, and 6 –10 years are expected to
experience signi ﬁcant growth, re ﬂecting an increased consumer appreciation for the life-cycle value of EVs (Pares
Olguin et al., 2023). In this phase, future policy directions should evolve from incentivizing initial EV purchases to
fostering improvements in the entire value chain and supporting continuous technological advancements.
Consumer behavior characteristics and evolution analysis
We examined differences across psychological factors by demographic characteristics, as shown in Fig. 4a. Actual
vehicle ownership signi ﬁcantly enhances consumers ’ evaluations of EV PU, with vehicle owners scoring approxi-
mately 2% higher than non-owners. Male consumers rate signi ﬁcantly higher than female counterparts across all
psychological dimensions, averaging 0.12 points higher. These ﬁndings suggest EV promotional strategies targeting
women should emphasize usability and driving experience. Higher educational attainment correlates positively with
all psychological factors, particularly SN (0.12 points higher), highlighting the association between educational
background and technology acceptance. Additionally, high-income consumers rate PU approximately 4% higher than
middle- and low-income groups, while low-income consumers score lowest in EOU, underscoring income ’s critical
role in shaping technology adoption preferences (Cui et al., 2025).
Our results reveal differences in consumer sensitivity to key EV attributes. Replacement consumers exhibit the
highest sensitivity to driving range (Fig. 4b), particularly within the 500 –700 km interval, indicating a psychological
threshold beyond which marginal perceived bene ﬁts decline sharply. New consumers display greater sensitivity to
fuel costs than replacement consumers, especially within the 500 –1000 RMB per 1000 km range, re ﬂecting a stronger
emphasis on long-term operating expenses. Vehicle purchase cost signi ﬁcantly in ﬂuences all segments within the
150,000–200,000 RMB range, underscoring the high price elasticity in the mainstream market. Additionally,
replacement consumers demonstrate strong negative reactions to charging times exceeding 40 min, indicating a
critical threshold for charging acceptability. This threshold can also be understood in terms of the opportunity cost of

Consumer behavior heterogeneity and policy matching mechanisms
Understanding heterogeneous consumer responses is essential for designing policy mechanisms that effectively
promote EV adoption. Among various policy instruments, trade-in programs have been widely adopted as a means to
accelerate vehicle ﬂeet renewal and stimulate market transition. In this section, the trade-in policy is examined as a
representative intervention to illustrate how policy incentives can be aligned with differentiated consumer behavior
patterns across income levels and purchasing stages.

roles, re ﬂecting their high sensitivity to resource access and usage risks. Notably, new and replacement consumers
within this group respond actively to vehicle purchase restriction policies, suggesting that institutional thresholds can
guide behavior when combined with accessible subsidies. For this group, trade-in policy effectiveness hinges on both

short-distance transportation tools. This ﬁnding indicates that trade-in policies should incorporate vehicle purchasing
stages into precise target group segmentation (Virmani et al., 2023). For new consumers and replacement consumers,
policy incentives should be strengthened alongside product education coordination. For renewal consumers, focus
should be placed on optimizing experiences, enhancing brand recognition, and community belonging to improve their
loyalty and repurchase willingness.
Cross-country divergences and the role of policy guidance
The global trajectory of EV technological transitions is deeply in ﬂuenced by national economic structures, the
strength of policy interventions, and the extent of infrastructure development. To examine these interdependencies, a
cluster analysis was conducted across 51 countries, revealing distinct transition pathways corresponding to emerging
EV markets, developing EV markets, and advanced EV markets. These patterns highlight how policy design and
infrastructure deployment interact with economic context to either enable or constrain EV adoption.
In emerging EV markets, EV market development exhibits high polarization, as Fig. 6 shows. Two archetypes are
identiﬁed: the “low-infrastructure, low-penetration ” model and the “policy-driven activation ” model. The former,
including countries such as India, Indonesia, South Africa, Colombia, and Brazil, is characterized by a near absence
of both ﬁscal incentives and public charging infrastructure, typically with fewer than 0.01 chargers per 1000 people.
This dual deﬁciency imposes systemic constraints on consumer adoption, resulting in persistently low EV penetration
levels from approximately 0.29% to 3.9%. These trends align with the behavioral tendencies of emerging consumers,
who exhibit strong dependence on external enablers such as subsidies and infrastructure access (Wang, 2025). In
contrast, a second subgroup represented by Thailand demonstrates the potential for early-stage market activation
under resource-constrained conditions. With relatively proactive policy intervention and nascent infrastructure
deployment (0.03 chargers per 1000 people), Thailand has achieved an EV penetration rate exceeding 10%, sig-
niﬁcantly outperforming its peers. This case highlights that beyond physical infrastructure, the state ’s governance
capacity in providing consistent and credible institutional signals is crucial. In Thailand, the transition was facilitated
not just by the chargers themselves, but by the building of institutional trust, which reduced the perceived risk of
technological abandonment. For emerging consumers, the government ’s role as a reliable guarantor of the new energy
ecosystem is a prerequisite for unlocking adoption under resource-constrained conditions.
Among developing EV markets, two distinct development pathways emerge: the “infrastructure-deﬁcient” model
and the “well-conditioned market ” model. As Fig. 6 shows, Countries such as Malaysia, Mexico, and Turkey
represent the former, where limited charging infrastructure (typically <0.3 chargers per 1,000 people) and weak

relative electricity-to-fuel price advantages constrain consumer appeal. Despite the introduction of purchase subsidies
in some cases, overall EV adoption remains below 10%. This re ﬂects not only economic pragmatism but also the
complex cultural meanings attached to vehicle ownership in emerging economies. In many societies, the passenger
vehicle remains a potent symbol of social status and personal identity. When the institutional environment is
perceived as unstable, consumers often retreat to ICEVs. The slow adoption in these countries is thus a re ﬂection of a
mismatch between the EV ’s current image and the traditional social identity associated with automotive success,
coupled with a lack of institutionalized norms that would rede ﬁne sustainable driving as a superior social value. By
contrast, countries like China and Portugal illustrate a structurally more favorable environment. Long-term, stable
policy frameworks, extensive infrastructure deployment, and signi ﬁcant energy cost advantages have enabled sus-
tained market growth (Gillingham, 2022). Portugal achieved a 32% penetration rate under similarly robust conditions.
These countries have gradually shifted from policy-driven adoption to market-driven growth, where improved
economic fundamentals and consumer ecosystems promote autonomous EV uptake.
Advanced EV markets present a further evolution in transition pathways, marked by increasing consumer
autonomy in adoption behavior. Based on infrastructure mat urity and market development, these countries can be
categorized into “steady-growth ” and “mature-market ” models. The former includes France, Germany, and the
United States, where EV penetration has surpassed 20%, supported by established policy regimes and moderate
infrastructure rollout. However, the availability of fast -charging facilities and infr astructure density remains
uneven, and consumer uptake is still partly dependent on ongoing infrastructure improvements. In contrast,
countries such as Sweden, Denmark, Iceland, Finland, the Netherlands, Belgium, and Norway represent the
mature-market archetype. With EV penetration rates exceeding 40%-and as high as 93% in Norway-these nations
also boast world-leading infrastructure deployment (e.g ., the Netherlands with 8.16 chargers per 1,000 people).
The high penetration rates and mature infrastructure observed in this cluster are consistent with the theoretical
proposition that adoption in such markets is increasingly in ﬂuenced by social identity and trust in technology.
While our clustering analysis primarily utilizes structural and market-level variables, the shift toward autonomous
adoption potentially re ﬂects deeper socio-cultural drivers that have been identi ﬁed in recent consumer literature
(Edelenbosch et al., 2018;F i s c h - R o m i t oe ta l . ,2025). In these mature markets, EV adoption re ﬂects a deep
alignment between individual social identity and broad er institutional trust. Consumers view EVs not merely as
transport tools, but as cultural markers of a sustainable life style. This transition is supported by high levels of trust
in public institutions and energy governance, where the state ’s long-term commitment to decarbonization provides
a stable social framework that reduces the perceived risks of technological shifts and reinforces the social
legitimacy of electric mobility (Kanberger and Ziegler, 2024). Moreover, regional integration has played a rein-
forcing role. Northern and Northwestern European countr ies have leveraged long-standing cooperation in energy
transition, grid interconnectivity, and smart mobility infrastructure to achieve synchronized market expansion. In
these contexts, EV ownership is often perceived as an expression of environmental responsibility and social
commitment, re ﬂecting broader cultural values that further accelerate market transformation.
Policy implications
The ﬁndings from both the China deep-dive and the global comparative analysis converge to suggest that EV
transitions are not idiosyncratic, but follow predictable behavioral stages.
In the early phase of the EV technology transition proce ss, heterogeneity in consumer behavior emerges as a key
determinant of market penetration. Distinct adoption patterns are observed across consumer segments. New
consumers tend to rely heavily on policy incentives and c harging accessibility, while replacement and renewal
consumers focus more on life-cycle cost, technological performance, and brand perception (Devarasan et al.,
2025). Such behavioral heterogeneity necessitates targeted and differentiated policy designs in the short term,
a l i g n e dw i t ht h es p e c iﬁc needs of each segment. In emerging EV markets, the combination of limited infrastructure
and high purchase costs results in low market penetration ra tes. As such, the effectiveness of policy incentives and
the compatibility of infrastructure development are critic al. In areas lacking basic EV infrastructure, early-stage

public investment and ﬁscal incentives can effectively stimulate con sumer adoption. Thailand provides a notable
example: despite its status as an emerging EV market, tim ely implementation of incen tive policies and initial
infrastructure deployment elevated its EV market penetra tion rate to over 10%. This demonstrates that, even under
constrained economic conditions, the interaction between policy and infrastructure can successfully stimulate
consumer demand and support the early stages of the technology transition process.
During the medium-term phase, EV market expansion increasingly relies on the development of supporting
infrastructure and the maturation of market conditions. Many developing EV markets, such as Malaysia, Mexico, and
Turkey, struggle with insuf ﬁcient charging infrastructure and a lack of meaningful economic advantages relative to
internal combustion vehicles (Shi et al., 2022). These limitations hinder EV adoption and delay the market ’s
transition. In developing economies, uncertainty in power supply and grid reliability may further weaken consumer
conﬁdence, even where charging facilities are gradually being expanded. However, improvements in policy fra-
meworks and market structures can shift this trajectory. Countries such as China and Portugal, through sustained
policy support and large-scale infrastructure investment, have entered a period of rapid EV market growth. In these
markets, consumers no longer base decisions solely on cost considerations; instead, user experience, charging
convenience, and long-term bene ﬁts play increasingly important roles (Razmjoo et al., 2022). These examples
highlight the importance of synchronized development of policy and infrastructure during the medium stage of the
technology transition process, particularly for developing EV markets seeking to reach maturity.
In the long-term phase, the EV market transition in advanced EV markets is no longer primarily reliant on
subsidies or incentives. Instead, EV adoption is shaped by social norms, environmental values, and individual identity
alignment. In countries like Norway, where EV market penetration rates exceed 40%, EVs have become a main-
stream transportation choice. In such contexts, consumer behavior is in ﬂuenced less by economic factors and more by
technological trust, brand loyalty, and personal commitment to sustainability (Gillingham et al., 2023). Furthermore,
advanced EV markets are increasingly exhibiting regional coordination effects in the EV technology transition
process (Kanberger and Ziegler, 2024). Northern and Northwestern Europe have established regional cooperation
frameworks in renewable energy integration, smart grid interconnectivity, and intelligent transport infrastructure,
fostering simultaneous market growth. In these societies, EV adoption serves not only practical mobility needs but
also re ﬂects broader cultural and environmental values. These mature-market experiences may also generate
demonstration effects by making EV ownership more visible, socially recognizable, and easier for later adopters to
evaluate. The social and cultural embedding of EV ownership signi ﬁcantly accelerates market diffusion and deepens
the technology transition process.
Beyond the technical optimization of transition pathways, the sustained ef ﬁcacy of EV policy is contingent upon
social feasibility and institutional capacity. The legitimacy of the transition framework further requires an explicit
evaluation of the trade-offs inherent in systemic shifts, such as structural labor market transitions within the internal
combustion engine value chain and the potential for regressive resource transfers during the early stages of adoption.
Cultivating social acceptance through policy frameworks that integrate infrastructure equity with comprehensive
social support systems is essential to ensure that the decarbonization trajectory remains stable and inclusive across
diverse global regimes.
Conclusion
This study investigates the evolution of China ’s EV market and conducts a cross-national comparative analysis of 51
countries to examine how consumer behavior in ﬂuences the EV technology transition process. The results provide
three key contributions:
First, we identify distinct behavioral patterns across three consumer segments: new consumers, replacement
consumers, and renewal consumers. New consumers are primarily concerned with initial purchase cost and perceived
ease of use, and their decisions are strongly in ﬂuenced by social norms. Replacement consumers prioritize driving
range, life-cycle cost, and functional utility, while demonstrating greater caution toward technological innovation, as
indicated by a negative PU coef ﬁcient ( β = –0.27). Renewal consumers leverage prior usage experience, placing
https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-026-09262-w Article in Press
Humanities and Social Sciences Communications

emphasis on operational convenience and charging accessibility. These insights suggest that consumer-segment-
speciﬁc policy incentives are necessary to drive adoption.
Second, based on a dynamic simulation of China ’s passenger vehicle market, we identify a phased transition
process. Under the baseline scenario, the model suggests that ICEV stock may decline signi ﬁcantly between 2025 and
2035, while EV stock could surpass ICEVs around 2030. The expansion of the secondhand EV market may create
new opportunities for scaling adoption and extending vehicle utility. By 2035, replacement consumers could account
for over 40% of total buyers under the assumptions of the dynamic simulation framework. This structural change
underscores the growing importance of targeted policies aimed at facilitating vehicle replacement and market
renewal.
Third, EV adoption patterns vary signi ﬁcantly across different markets, shaped by differences in policy com-
mitment and infrastructure readiness. In emerging EV markets, market entry is often obstructed by the absence of
policy and infrastructure, where EV penetration remains below 4%. Developing EV markets face delays due to
underdeveloped charging networks and weak cost advantages. Advanced EV markets, especially in Northern Europe,
are now entering a stage of autonomous adoption, where cultural values, identity signaling, and sustainability
awareness are key drivers.
In conclusion, the EV transition process is shaped not only by ﬁnancial incentives but also by the interaction between
consumer heterogeneity, technological maturity, and transition stages. Future EV policy frameworks may consider
adopting behaviorally informed, stage-speciﬁc strategies tailored to distinct consumer segments. Aligning policy instru-
ments with infrastructure development and consumer readiness will be essential to accelerate structural transformation and
achieve long-term decarbonization goals.
