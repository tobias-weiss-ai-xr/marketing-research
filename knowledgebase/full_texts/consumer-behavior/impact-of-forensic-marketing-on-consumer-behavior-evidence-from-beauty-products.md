Abstract: Marketing firms navigate a dynamic environment characterized with volatility and complexity, where survival
hinges upon adaptability and responsiveness to external forces. The pursuit of rapid growth, profit and competitive
advantage in the cosmetics market often leads some companies to engage in unlawful and unethical practices, including
copyright and trademark infringement and misleading marketing, which creates problems for honest consumers and the
entire industry's sustainability and leads to legal cases of fraud. This has led to the emergence of a specific type of
marketing ‒ forensic marketing, characterise d by a thorough check (often in a legal context) of all the facts and data
accompanying marketing policy. This study examines how forensic marketing dimension influence the consumer behavior
using the case study of 3 skincare products in Nigeria, including  the impact of copyright violation on brand awareness,
trademark infringement on consumer patronage, and the impact of misleading advertising on consumer loyalty. In solving
this problem, the article analyses the provisions of the Theory of Buyer Behaviour, the Nicosia Model and the
Psychoanalytic Model. The study is b ased on the results of an online survey of 384 Nigerian consumers of cosmetic
skincare products (a five-point Likert scale was used for answers, Bill Golden technique was used to determine the sample
size, Cronbach's Alpha test was used to confirm reliability, and SmartPLS software was used for data processing).
Descriptive (frequency, percentage, mean, standard deviation, and skewness) and inferential (structural equation
modelling (SEM) by ut ilising the partial least squares (PLS) version) analyses were carried out. The modelling
empirically confirms the validity of all three hypotheses: 1) respondents perceive copyright infringement as having a
significant relationship with brand awareness, w ith the strongest impact on a product idea, moderate impact on brand
name, and weak impact on promotion strategy; 2) trademark infringement practices have a significant impact on
consumer patronage, with design having the strongest impact, signs and symbols having a moderate impact, and colour
having a weak impact; 3) deceptive marketing practices have a significant impact on cus tomer loyalty, with advertising
having the most significant impact, product quality having a moderate impact, and labelling and pa ckaging having a
weak impact. The article emphasises the importance of regularly informing consumers about the quality and features of
products, as well as encouraging whistleblowers through reward systems. By adopting ethical marketing practices,
companies can build a reputation that will ultimately benefit society, strengthen their reputation, and influence consumer
purchasing decisions.

trademark infringement.

Introduction
The beauty industry has been experiencing consistent growth, driven by the rising importance of self-care and
wellness among consumers worldwide. Despite a temporary setback in 2020 due to the COVID-19 pandemic,
the global cosmetics market has been on an u pward trajectory since 2004, with projections indicating that
revenues could reach nearly $129 billion by 2028 (Petruzzi, 2023). However, the pursuit of rapid growth,
profit, and competitive advantage has led some companies to engage in unlawful and unethical practices. These
actions have raised societal concerns, including copyright violations, trademark infringement, and deceptive
marketing, which pose challenges to the integrity and sustainability of the industry. The advent of the internet
and technological advancements have made reproduction, imitation, redistribution, re -performance, and re -
display of copyrighted ideas, knowledge, and products, particularly in terms of packaging, colour, flavour, and
symbol, significant issues in the digital era. These practices make the company a core copyright violator.
Violation of copyright creates confusion among  customers during the selection process, complicating their
purchasing decisions due to the reproduction of known products or ideas. The violator benefits from high
customer patronage and increased turnover, while the original copyright owner loses the value attached to their
idea, work, or product (Aboyade et al., 2015). The dynamic technological environment makes product features
such as signs, symbols, flavours, and trademarks more susceptible to infringement, posing challenges to both
the owner and customers in the form of sales diversion and product confusion, as customers may believe the
products originated from the trademark owner.
Unethical business firms adopt deceptive marketing to increase their product demand because technology is
the foundation of the firm’s promotional activities and the basis for information passage. Deceptive marketing,
particularly deceptive advertising, ha s been rampant in the cosmetic industry, as on many occasions,
customers’ expectations are higher than what the product can give them. For instance, in the UK, 17.8 percent
of ad impressions were fraudulent in 2023, while in Japan, it was 1.9 percent, illustrating a wide-ranging issue
(Statista, 2023). Nigeria reported that over 60% of consumers encounter deceptive marketing and copyright
violations, eroding trust and distorting market competition (Ezeonu, 2018). Meanwhile, Taiwan witnessed a
surge in copyr ight infringement cases, representing 2,074 cases, emphasizing the need for ethical
considerations in marketing practices (Textor & Statista, 2023). Consequently, most firms that tend to produce
cosmetic products usually advertise their product with a beautiful woman after using it. However, the product
does not give the customer the expected value. Due to the firm’s misleading activities, the firm’s sales drop as
a result of the firm’s bad reputation, and this will discourage customers from patronizing the firm due to their
experience with the firm.
In contrast, as they become eminent in all consumer endeavour through pyramids including people, planet and
profit, sustainability displayed through ethical marketing practices is crucial for several reasons. Firstly, ethical
marketing practices help build trust and credibility with customers (Han, 2021). When firms prioritize honesty,
transparency, and respect for intellectual property rights as noted by accounting standard, they establish a
positive reputation that can lead to long-term customer loyalty and positive brand perception. Secondly, ethical
marketing practices contribute to the overall well-being of society by promoting fair competition and consumer
protection (Acuti et al., 2021). By adhering to ethical standards motioning by consumer protectio n agency
across the globe, firms ensure that their marketing strategies does not harm competitors or deceive consumers,
thereby fostering a more ethical and sustainable marketplace. Furthermore, ethical marketing practices align
with societal values and ex pectations (Trudel, 2019). In today's socially conscious environment, consumers
are increasingly demanding that companies demonstrate social responsibility and ethical behavior. Firms that
engage in sustainable and ethical marketing practices are more like ly to resonate with these values, attracting
socially conscious consumers and enhancing their brand image. Through this firm's ethical act, consumer
displayed sustainable behaviour.
Forensic marketing practices have resulted in legal provisions related to copyright, brand recognition, and
advertising to protect the firm, customers, and society (Ababio & Yamoah, 2016). However, it failed to provide
and deliver the desired result becaus e of its complexity and the limited availability of data to enhance its
usefulness, which has a significant adverse effect on the purchasing decision of the customer as the firm lures
and entices the customer of another firm to buy their product through de ceptiveness, copyright violations, or
trademark infringement.
Despite daily marketing orientation by the marketing firm through their integrated marketing communication
(Ahmed & Rahman, 2015), customers are strongly affected by marketing communication, particularly
advertising, which influences customers to purchase a product that is said to be imitated or copied from the
trademark owner. A firm that intends to achieve faster growth in terms of sales and profitability often adopts

Literature Review

and marketing. The word “forensic” is synonymous with “legal or related to courts” and in Latin means “before
the forum” (Mokhtar, 2015). The Merriam -Webster dictionary opined , that forensics focuses on using
scientific knowledge in solving crimes or relating to, used in, or suitable to a court of law. In brevity, forensics

is applying the scientific method and knowledge to issues related to court or legal cases, especially in the
scientific analysis of physical evidence related to the crime scene. In other words, it is the scientific knowledge
or techniques to be applied in court. Alternatively, marketing is generally acknowledged as applying a
marketing mix to solve customer requirements that result in customer satisfaction through tailored -made
products and services (Sukanta & Muis, 2022). As forensic marketing depicts a ne w legitimate and emerging

and involves employing the case study method to structure data collection and analysis.
Several studies have addressed forensic marketing issues in the Nigerian context from a legal perspective. For
instance, Ababio and Yamoah (2016) delved into the impact of advertising on the brand loyalty of cosmetic
products among college students. The st udy found that advertising does not significantly affect college
students' loyalty to cosmetic products. However, the study focused solely on advertising in a general sense
with no attention to the negative impact of deceptive advertising, which is prevale nt in the marketing
landscape. Also, Shahzad and Kausar (2016) examined the moderating influence of corporate image and
customer trust on the relationship between perceived deception and customer loyalty. The study contradicted
that perceived deception is inversely linked to customer trust and loyalty. However, this study left out
advertising, which has become rampant in the concept of deception in marketing.
Furthermore, Iqbal and Sidiqqui (2019) investigated how deceptive advertising impacts customer loyalty in
Karachi’s telecommunications industry, Pakistan. The study’s results indicated that word of mouth
significantly influences customer loyalty, with dece ptive ads having a negative impact on word of mouth.
While the study primarily prioritized providing accurate information and delivering competitive services to
enhance customer loyalty and foster long -term relationships, it did not address other factors l ike customer
satisfaction and patronage in consumer buying decisions. Similarly, Bayero (2019) explored how brand
association impacts consumer loyalty towards GSM service providers in metropolitan Kano. The study
indicated a significant correlation between perceived service quality and consumer loyalty. However, the study
overlooked other industries, such as the cosmetics sector, where deceptive practices are prevalent, especially
in Kwara State, Nigeria.
Abdulbaqi (2020) evaluated how deceptive advertising impacts consumer purchasing decisions on social
media, focusing on the University of Tabuk in Saudi Arabia. The study revealed a positive correlation between
deceptive advertising and word of mouth, influencing buying behaviour. However, the study solely considered
word of mouth as a factor in consumer buying decisions, overlooking other variables like satisfaction and
patronage. Similarly, according to Bian and Haque (2020), emotional brand attachment holds greater sway in
driving consumers towards original brand patronage compared to brand involvement. However, the impact of
brand involvement remains noteworthy. However, the study's findings underscore a gap in understanding the
relationship between emotional brand attachment and counterfeit patronage, warranting further investigation.
Goldfarb and Tucker (2011) evaluated the impact of privacy regulations in the European Union on online
advertising effectiveness. The study indicated a significant decline in the effectiveness of display advertising
in influencing purchase intent following  the enactment of EU privacy laws. However, the study's gap lies in
the failure to delve into potential alternative advertising strategies or adaptations that could mitigate the
negative effects of privacy regulations on online advertising effectiveness. Similarly, Gbandi and Isibor (2022)
researched how branding influences consumer patronage of restaurants in Benin City, Edo State, Nigeria. This
study showed a positive significant correlation between brand awareness, brand image, brand attachment, and
consumer patronage of restaurants in Benin City. However, this study neglects trademark infringement,
representing a significant unethical practice in the research landscape.
Aboyade et al. (2015) utilized a descriptive survey to investigate perceptions regarding Nigerian copyright law
and its abuse concerning published works. The study’s results revealed that while there was no significant
difference in the perceived reasons for photocopying among teachers and students, a disparity existed in their
understanding of copyright law. However, the study’s gap lies in its failure to delve into the specific strategies
or interventions needed to address the issue effectively. Similarly, Khan et al. (2020) explored the correlation
between deceptive advertising and consumer behaviour. The study unveiled the symbolic metaphors employed
by participants and elucidated the meanings they attach to consumer experiences. However, it fails to address
the specific mechanisms through which deceptive advertising influences consumer decision -making,
highlighting a notable gap in this research.

Theoretical Review.  This study’s theoretical foundation encompasses insights from the Theory of Buyer
Behavior, the Nicosia Model, and the Psychoanalytic Model. By leveraging these theories, marketers can gain
insights into factors that drive their purchasing behaviour, thereby enhancing their forensic marketing
strategies and market penetration efforts in the beauty product industry.
Theory of Buyer Behaviour. In 1963, Howard pioneered the initial consumer decision model (Roy, 2022),
which underwent further development in 1969 through collaboration with Sheth, evolving into the 'Theory of
Buyer Behaviour' or the Howard and Sheth Model (Chen & Barnes, 2007). This  model represents a
sophisticated amalgamation of social, psychological, and marketing factors influencing consumer choices,
structured within a coherent information processing sequence. It elucidates individual buyer behaviour
patterns, particularly in brand selection, by delineating the components of the consumer decision process: a
series of motives, multiple alternative actions, and decision mediators aligning motives with options. The
authors also observe how these elements e volve due to their repetitive nature and elucidate how their
combination influences search processes and the assimilation of information from the buyer's commercial and
social millennia. However, the gap in the existing literature lies in the specific elucidation of how these decision
elements dynamically interact with evolving market conditions and individual preferences over time.
Nicosia Model. The Nicosia model, developed in 1966 by Francesco Nicosia, a prominent figure in consumer
motivation and behaviour (Jisana, 2014), elucidates consumer behaviour by elucidating the dynamic between
an organization and its consumers. According to this model, communication from the organization plays a
pivotal role in shaping consumers' predispositions towards a product or service, subsequently influencing their
attitudes towards it. This process may prompt consumers to seek out the product or ev aluate its attributes
actively. Ultimately, if these preceding stages meet the consumer's expectations, it may lead to a positive
response characterized by a decision to purchase the product. However, the gap in this explanation lies in the
absence of acknowledging potential complexities or external factors that could influence consumer decision -
making beyond the scope of the organization's messages.
Psychoanalytic Model. Initially conceived by Sigmund Freud in the 1890s, the psychoanalytical model focuses
on the communication dynamics between a brand and a consumer (Stortelder & Ploegmakers -Burg, 2010).
This framework delineates stages, akin to fields, through which intera ctions occur. It acknowledges the
interplay between the conscious and subconscious realms in shaping consumer behaviour, drawing upon
Freud's conceptualization of the id, ego, and superego as agents of influence. According to this the ory, latent
symbols embedded within a company's name or logo can resonate with an individual's subconscious,
potentially swaying their purchasing decisions towards the associated product over competitors' offerings.
However, the gap in this perspective lies in the lack of concrete evidence or empirical support to substantiate
the claim regarding the influence of hidden symbols on consumer behaviour.
The study adopted the “Theory of Buyer Behavior” because it is all -encompassing and thus applies to all the
variables used in formulating the hypotheses in this study. This is ideal because it implies that the behavioural
intentions of beauty product users are influenced by thr ee primary factors: attitude toward the behaviour,
subjective norms, and perceived behavioural control (Hand & Riley, 2016). Firstly, understanding consumers’
attitudes towards beauty products is crucial. TPB suggests that consumers’ positive or negative evaluations of
these products will significantly influence their purchasing decisions (Chia et al., 2016). By investigating
consumers’ attitudes towards beauty products in Nigeria, marketers can gain insights into the factors driving
purchasing behaviour and tailor their forensic marketing strategies accordingly.
Secondly, subjective norms play a pivotal role in the TPB framework, indicating that individuals' perceptions
of social pressures and norms surrounding beauty products influence their intentions to purchase them
(Gbadamosi, 2020). Exploring the influence o f deceptiveness on consumers' buying decisions can provide
valuable insights into the social dynamics shaping consumer behaviour in Nigeria. Lastly, perceived
behavioural control refers to individuals' perceptions of their ability to perform the behaviour in question
(Ahrens et al., 2019). Exploring product availability, affordability, and accessibility may impact consumers'
perceived control over their buying decisions. Understanding these factors can inform forensic marketing
strategies to enhance consumers' perceived control and facilitate purchasing behaviour.

sampled beauty product companies. Additionally, inferential statistical methods involved using structural
equation modelling (SEM) by utilizing the partial least squares (PLS) version. This is necessary to analyze the
effect of the moderating variable. This is justified because of its advantage. Firstly, it allows for the
simultaneous analysis of multiple structural equations, accommodating numerous dependent and independent
variables (Ali et al., 2018). Secondly, PLS -SEM is valuable when exploring direct and indirect impacts,
including mediating variables (Lowry & Gaskin, 2014). Thirdly, it enables the effective evaluation of the
reliability and validity of the study construct by  calculating a series of statistical tools like the mean, median,
adjusted regression (R2) and correlation statistics of the variables. Lastly, PLS-SEM does not necessitate data
normality and can accommodate smaller sample sizes (Hair et al., 2021).

Discussion
The digitalization of daily affairs has significantly impacted the competitive environment of firms, leading to
enhanced reproduction of ideas, work, and products, particularly in product design, packaging, flavor, and
branding. To aid informed decision -making, firms provide information about their products to influence
customer purchase decisions. However, the digital age has brought about information overload and i ncreased
opportunities for reproduction or imitation, leading to forensic marketing issues such as deceptive marketing,
copyright violations, and trademark infringement. These issues can confuse customers during product selection
and complicate their purch ase decisions as firms widely adopt these practices. For example, reproducing a
product or idea known to consumers can lead to copyright infringement, benefiting the violating company
through increased customer patronage and turnover while harming the orig inal copyright owner. Therefore,
the role of forensic marketers is crucial in addressing these issues, which may lead to legal consequences,
including fines and restrictions on corporate existence. This study suggests that forensic marketing issues
significantly impact the customer buying decision process, as promotional strategies, product ideas, and
corporate names have statistically significant effects on brand recognition. The null hypothesis that copyright
violations do not significantly affect brand recognition is rejected, indicating that these factors influence brand
recognition. This contrasts with Bayero's (2019) findings, which show that using celebrities for promotional
activities does not significantly affect consumer patronage of service providers. However, this study differs as
it assesses the impact of copyright violations on brand recognition, highlighting the importance of brand
association in achieving recognition.
Also, this study revealed that all three factors, namely colour, sign and symbol, and design, have statistically
significant effects on customer patronage. Thus, the null hypothesis that trademark infringement does not
significantly affect customer patronage is rejected based on these findings, indicating that these factors do
influence customer patronage meaningfully. This finding is related to the output of Gbandi and Isibor (2022),
who found a positive and substantial association between customer patronage of restaurants in Benin City and
the three branding variables: brand awareness, brand image, and brand attachment. It follows, therefore, that
when a beauty brand is counterfeited, it will affect the consumer’s patronage of the same brand. Finally, the
study concluded that all three factors, namely advertising, product quality, and labelling and packaging, have
statistically significant effects on customer loyalty. Thus, the null hypothesis that deceptive marketing does

Practical Implication. The research significantly contributes to the existing literature for several reasons. From
a theoretical viewpoint, it expands the understanding of the impact mechanisms of forensic marketing on
consumer behaviour, particularly in Nigeria's context as an emerging economy facing competitive pressures

Despite forensic marketing being widely practiced, its comprehensive understanding remains controversial in

bridges this gap by contributing to mar keting literature in emerging economies facing competitive pressures

Conclusions
Despite being new to the academic arena due to its limited coverage, forensic marketing has become
widespread across the marketing domain, including copyright, advertising, and branding. This study's
statistical findings, supported by the customer's perspe ctive on its impact, resulted in several significant
conclusions. The study concluded that promotional strategy, product idea, and corporate name, as factors of
copyright violation, positively influence brand recognition of selected beauty products in Nige ria.
Furthermore, colour, sign and symbol, and design, as factors of trademark infringement, positively influence
customer patronage of selected beauty products in Nigeria. Finally, advertising, product quality, labelling, and
packaging, as factors of deceptive marketing, positively impact customer loyalty for selected beauty products
in Nigeria. Overall, the study concludes that forensic marketing significantly affects customer buying decisions
among selected beauty products in Nigeria. Consequently, this study recommends that:
➢ The government should foster an ethical environment by implementing policy reforms that guide firms
against unethical marketing practices. This is necessary to protect the firm’s value proposition and the
consumer’s stock requirement.
➢ To increase brand recognition of beauty products in Nigeria, marketers should develop and foster unique
and appealing promotional strategies, product ideas, and corporate names to distinguish their products from
competitors and protect their intellectual property from violation.
➢ To increase customer patronage of beauty products in Ilorin, marketers should ensure a targeted marketing
mix by incorporating essential brand features that cannot be easily imitated. This might include colour,
design, sign, and symbol that can be adequately distinctive to prevent infringement.
➢ To increase customer loyalty to beauty products in Nigeria, marketers should design and foster unique and
appealing advertising strategies, product quality, labelling, and packaging that encourage customers’
enlightenment and the firm’s brand identification. This will serve as an information channel on the qualities

➢ Even though Statista (2023) reported that the forensic marketing dimension is a global academic and
professional issue, researchers should further review the justification and service areas of forensic
marketing in Nigeria. This is necessary to determine w hether this practice and action are necessary or a
choice.
The research is valuable in today's ethical dynamics by investigating how the dimension of forensic marketing
impact the buying behaviour of Skincare consumers, specifically in the context of beauty products purchase
decisions. However, several limitations must be acknowledged to ensure the validity and reliability of the
findings. Firstly, the research is limited by relying on Partial Least Squares St ructural Equation Modeling
(PLS-SEM) assumptions, including linearity, multivariate normality, and correct model specification. While
this might not be true due to natural change, failure to meet these assumptions can lead to biased or misleading
results. To address this limitation, future research could consider using alternative statistical techniques that
do not rely on these assumptions, such as Bayesian Structural Equation Modeling (BSEM) or robust methods
for parameter estimation like systematic literature review.
Secondly, the research does not consider changes in the strategies of cosmetic companies over time, which
could potentially impact the effectiveness of the dimension of forensic marketing as a strategic initiative and
its effects on consumers. To overcome this limitation, future research could adopt a longitudinal approach to
examine how forensic marketing strategies and their impacts evolve.
Despite these limitations, the research significantly contributes to the literature on forensic marketing and
consumer behaviour in the beauty products industry. It addresses a gap in the existing literature by focusing

investigating the moderating effects of individual characteristics, such as age, gender, and income, on the
