ABSTRACT:
Artificial Intelligence (AI) has emerged as a transformative force in the financial technology (FinTech)
industry by enabling intelligent automat ion, personalized financial services, predictive analytics, fraud
detection, and enhanced customer experiences. The rapid adoption of digital payment systems, including
mobile wallets, Unified Payments Interface (UPI), internet banking, and AI -powered fina ncial
applications, has significantly changed the way consumers manage financial transactions. AI -based
personalization allows fintech platforms to analyse customer preferences, spending behaviour,
transaction history, and financial goals to provide custom ized recommendations and services.
Simultaneously, perceived ease of use plays a vital role in encouraging users to adopt AI-enabled fintech
services by reducing complexity and increasing convenience. Despite the increasing popularity of AI -
powered financial applications, several users continue to face concerns regarding privacy, transparency,
trust, and the usability of AI -based systems. The present study investigates the impact of AI -based
personalization and perceived ease of use on the adoption of AI -driven fintech services among digital
payment users. The study adopts a quantitative research approach using a structured questionnaire
administered to digital payment users. Primary data will be collected through convenience sampling, while
descriptive statistics, reliability analysis, correlation analysis, regression analysis, and hypothesis testing
will be employed using SPSS software to examine the relationships among the variables.

Keywords: Artificial Intelligence, FinTech, Digital Payments, AI-Based Personalization, Perceived Ease
of Use, Technology Acceptance Model.

2. Does Age Influence AI-Enabled Mobile Banking App Usage? (2025)
The study examined how cognitive factors influence users' continuance i ntention toward AI-enabled
mobile banking applications. The study surveyed 398 respondents and employed SPSS to analyse
relationships among perceived usefulness, perceived ease of use, perceived intelligence, and
behavioural intention. The results indicate d that perceived ease of use and perceived intelligence
significantly increase users' intentions to continue using AI -enabled banking applications. Age
moderated several relationships, suggesting that younger users adapt more quickly to AI -powered
banking features. The research emphasizes that fintech providers should simplify interfaces while
improving AI capabilities to ensure long-term customer engagement.
3. Understanding Consumer Acceptance of Artificial Intelligence in Mobile Banking Through
Perceived Ease and Usefulness (2025)
The study explored the acceptance of AI technology in mobile banking by focusing on perceived ease
of use and perceived usefulness. Using data from 210 mobile banking users, the researchers found that
both variables have a signific ant positive effect on AI adoption. The findings demonstrated that
customers are more willing to use AI -based banking services when applications are easy to navigate
and provide practical benefits. The study recommends improving interface design and simplifying AI
features to encourage greater customer adoption.
4. Perceptions of Financial AI Assistants and Intentions Toward AI -Assisted Financial Products
(2025)
This study investigated how users perceive AI financial assistants and how these perceptions influence
their intention to adopt AI-assisted financial products. Data collected from 458 respondents revealed
that perceived performance, ease of use, anthropomorphism, and trust significantly affect users'
behavioural intentions. Trust acted as a mediating va riable between AI characteristics and adoption
intention. The authors concluded that AI assistants should be designed to appear reliable, intelligent,
and easy to interact with to increase customer acceptance.
5. Artificial Intelligence: Understanding the Det erminants of Adoption and Intention to
Recommend the Technology in the Financial Sector (2025)
This study examined the determinants influencing customers' adoption of AI in financial institutions.
Structural Equation Modelling revealed that perceived usefu lness, perceived ease of use, perceived
trust, and perceived risk significantly affect users' attitudes toward AI-powered financial services. The
study also found that positive attitudes increase users' willingness to recommend AI-enabled financial
services to others. The authors emphasized that fintech organizations should improve transparency and
usability while minimizing perceived risks.

7. How Perceived Value Drives Usage Intention of AI Digital Human Advisors in Digital Finance

This research investigated the adoption of AI-powered digital human advisors in digital finance. The

8. Drivers of Banking Chatbot Adoption by Mobile Banking Customers (2025)
The study investigated the adoption of AI -powered banking chatbots among Indian mobile banki ng

customer adoption. The findings demonstrate that chatbot interfaces should be intuitive, responsive,
and capable of providing personalized assistance to improve customer satisfaction.
9. FinTech Adoption for ESG Integration Through Robo-Advisors, Personalization, and Perceived
Trust (2025)
The study examined how AI -driven robot -advisors, personalization, and trust influence fintech
adoption for sustainable investments. The study concluded that personalized investment
recommendations significantly increase users' trust and adoption intentions. Perceived trust partial ly
mediated the relationship between AI personalization and behavioural intention, highlighting
personalization as a strategic advantage for fintech firms.
10. Algorithm of Trust: The Causal Impact of AI Personalization on Consumer Adoption in Mobile
Messaging Payments (2025)
The study examined the causal relationship between AI -driven personalization and mobile payment
adoption using a natural experiment. The study found that personalized AI recommendations
significantly increased payment adoption by strengthening consumer trust. Trust served as the primary
mechanism through which AI personalization influenced behavioural intention. The findings suggest
that fintech firms should invest in personalized recommendation systems while ensuring transparency
to maximize customer adoption.

 The research is limited to digital payment users in India; therefore, findings may not be generalizable
to other countries.
 The study uses a cross -sectional survey design and captures respondents' perceptions at
one point in time.
 Responses are based on self-reported opinions, which may introduce response bias.

OVERALL FINDINGS:
The study indicate that AI-based personalization and perceived ease of use are significant determinants
of the adoption of AI -driven fintech services among digital payment users. The demographic analysis
revealed that the majority of respondents were young adults between the ages of 18 and 35, indicating that
younger consumers are more receptive to AI -enabled financial technologies. The reliability analysis
confirmed that the questionnaire was highly reliable, with Cronbach's Alpha values exceeding the
recommended threshold, ensuring the consistency of the measurement scale.
