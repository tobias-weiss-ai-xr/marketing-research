Abstract
This study aims to uncover the nature of the relationship between digital marketing and the Sustainable
Development Goals (SDGs) within the banking and digital ﬁnancial services sector in Egypt, while testing
the mediating role of consumer environmental awareness. The study is based on the main hypothesis
that digital transformation in the marketing activities of banks and ﬁnancial service companies is not
limited to improving customer reach or increasing the use of applications and ﬁnancial platforms.
Rather, it extends to inﬂuencing consumers' perception of sustainability issues, rationalizing ﬁnancial
and consumption behavior, promoting ﬁnancial inclusion, and reducing reliance on paper-based and
cash transactions. This supports several SDGs, particularly Goal 8 (Decent Work and Economic Growth),
Goal 9 (Industry, Innovation, and Infrastructure), Goal 12 (Responsible Consumption and Production),
and Goal 13 (Climate Action).
The study adopted a descriptive-analytical methodology and proposed a conceptual model linking digital
marketing as an independent variable, SDGs as a dependent variable, and consumer environmental
awareness as a mediating variable. The study concludes that digital marketing in the Egyptian ﬁnancial
sector can become a strategic tool supporting sustainable development if it goes beyond service
promotion to building genuine consumer environmental awareness, delivering transparent digital
content, adopting responsible marketing messages, and linking digital ﬁnancial usage to sustainability
values.
1. Introduction
Digital marketing has become one of the most signiﬁcant transformations reshaping the relationship
between ﬁnancial institutions and customers in recent decades. Banks no longer rely solely on
traditional branches or direct advertising campaigns; they now use mobile applications, social media
platforms, targeted ads, email, search engines, data analytics, digital user experience, personalized
messages, and interactive content to reach consumers and inﬂuence their decisions, guiding their
ﬁnancial behavior (Gharios & Abu Khalaf, 2024). At the same time, banks' objectives are no longer limited
to maximizing ﬁnancial proﬁts but have extended to include moral and environmental responsibility. With
the emergence of fourth and ﬁfth-generation technologies and the accelerating pace of digital
transformation, "digital marketing" has become not just a tool for promoting services but a strategic
channel contributing to shaping societal awareness and guiding behaviors towards sustainability.
However, 2024 United Nations reports indicate that the world remains far from the right track to achieve
the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development; only 17% of the goals are on track, while nearly half show
limited or moderate progress, and more than one-third are stagnant or regressing compared to the 2015
baseline (UN DESA, 2024).
Hence, the current study attempts to present a model linking digital marketing in the Egyptian ﬁnancial
sector to the Sustainable Development Goals, highlighting environmental awareness as the

psychological and cognitive mechanism explaining how and why digital marketing can transform from a
mere promotional tool into a tool supporting sustainability .
2. Conceptual Framework of the Research
The conceptual framework of the study is based on three main variables (see Fig. 1). The independent
variable is digital marketing, which refers to the use of digital channels and technologies such as social
media, search engines, email, and websites to promote products and services, build customer
relationships, enhance interaction, and achieve strategic marketing goals in electronic environments
(Pruthi & Bansal, 2024). The banking sector has witnessed an evolution in adopting digital marketing as
a key tool to expand customer reach and improve relationships. Financial institutions use multiple digital
channels such as mobile applications, social media campaigns, targeted ads, email, user experience
enhancement, and inﬂuencer marketing to raise awareness of digital services and stimulate usage
(Gharios & Abu Khalaf, 2024).
This aligns with what Yimer et al. (2026) found, that digital transformation in the ﬁnancial sector is
among the most important variables explaining the acceleration of digital marketing adoption. The
concept of environmental awareness refers to the degree of consumer knowledge of environmental
issues associated with their consumption or ﬁnancial behavior, and their sense of responsibility towards
protecting natural resources and reducing environmental harm resulting from daily activities (Yang et al.,
2021). It operates through three main components: knowledge (understanding environmental problems
and their impact), attitudes (positive or negative orientation towards environmental issues), and behavior
(actual practices such as reducing consumption or recycling) (Shen & Wang, 2022). Consequently,
raising environmental awareness can lead to a change in consumption patterns, especially if the
individual recognizes the environmental consequences of his choices and has a desire to modify his
behavior, making it a central variable in understanding the impact of digital marketing on sustainable
development. The study by Yang et al. (2021) across 39 countries conﬁrmed that environmental
awareness is one of the strongest predictors of pro-environmental behavioral intentions, regardless of
cultural differences.
The Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), adopted by the United Nations in 2015, represent a
comprehensive agenda comprising 17 goals and 169 targets aimed at eradicating poverty, protecting the
planet, and ensuring prosperity for all by 2030. The current study focuses on four goals most relevant to
the banking and digital ﬁnancial services sector: Goal 8 (Decent Work and Economic Growth), Goal 9
(Industry, Innovation, and Infrastructure), Goal 12 (Responsible Consumption and Production), and Goal
13 (Climate Action), in addition to the cross-cutting role of ﬁnancial inclusion as an enabling pillar for
several other goals.
3. Literature Review and Hypothesis Development
3.1 Digital Marketing and Sustainable Development Goals
Page 3/22

Recent literature has shown that digital marketing is no longer just a promotional tool but has become a
strategic channel for education, trust-building, and guiding consumer behavior towards more sustainable
choices. A study by Kim et al. (2021) demonstrated that digital marketing communication provides
extensive capabilities to reach, inform, engage customers, and build lasting relationships with them,
making it a suitable tool for conveying sustainability messages and linking them to digital services. In
the same context, a systematic review by Rosário and Dias (2025) concluded that sustainable digital
marketing, through personalized messages, social media interaction, green branding, and transparency,
contributes to shaping responsible consumption behavior. The study reviewed 84 peer-reviewed articles
related to digital marketing and sustainable consumption.
Regarding the relationship between green marketing and SDGs, a study by Amoako et al. (2020) showed
that green marketing strategies inﬂuence consumer responses to SDG initiatives in emerging markets,
supporting purchasing decisions related to environmental values. Thakur et al. (2025) found that
sustainability-oriented digital marketing communication affects sustainable purchase intention, and that
digital customer experience represents an important mechanism for translating digital messages into
sustainable behavior. For the banking sector, a study by Mir et al. (2025) showed that digital banking
services, as one dimension of green banking practices, positively affect customer satisfaction and
support digital transformation agendas and sustainable banking practices.
Based on this, it can be argued that digital marketing in banks can contribute to achieving SDGs by
highlighting the beneﬁts of digital services such as reducing paper transactions, lowering the need to
visit branches, supporting ﬁnancial inclusion, and encouraging customers to use electronic banking
channels. This supports the ﬁrst hypothesis that there is a positive impact of digital marketing on
achieving the SDGs.
Accordingly, the ﬁrst hypothesis can be formulated as:
H1: There is a signiﬁcant positive impact of digital marketing on the Sustainable Development Goals in
the banking and digital ﬁnancial services sector in Egypt.
3.2 Digital Marketing and Consumer Environmental
Awareness
Several studies have addressed the role of digital content in shaping individuals' awareness of
environmental issues. A study by Daoud et al. (2024) demonstrated that sustainability initiatives, digital
content selection, and interaction with environmental content inﬂuence environmental awareness. The
study's results conﬁrm that digital marketing strategies are capable of shaping individuals' awareness
and attitudes towards environmental issues, making them suitable for building bank customers'
awareness of green banking practices.
A study by Sun and Wang (2020) also found that the social media environment plays a signiﬁcant role in
explaining consumers' intentions to purchase green products, indicating that digital marketing messages

can affect customers' perception of environmental beneﬁts. This is reinforced by what Rosário and Dias
(2025) found: that digital messages based on transparency, emotional narrative, and personalization can
make sustainability more visible and understandable to consumers. Thus, in the banking context, digital
campaigns explaining the impact of electronic statements, digital payments, green ﬁnancing, and
reducing paper transactions can raise customers' level of environmental awareness. Therefore, the
second hypothesis can be formulated as:
H2: There is a signiﬁcant positive impact of digital marketing on consumer environmental awareness in
the banking and digital ﬁnancial services sector in Egypt.
3.3 Environmental Awareness and Sustainable
Development Goals
Environmental awareness represents a link between knowledge and behavior. An environmentally aware
consumer does not view the digital ﬁnancial service merely as an easy tool but also as an option less
dependent on traditional resources. Consequently, a rise in environmental awareness can lead to
increased use of digital channels, reduced demand for paper documents, preference for electronic
notiﬁcations, support for services that declare their commitment to sustainability, and participation in
green ﬁnancial campaigns.
A study by Daoud et al. (2024) conﬁrmed that sustainability initiatives, digital content selection, and
interaction with environmental content inﬂuence environmental awareness. The study's results
emphasize that digital marketing strategies can shape individuals' awareness and attitudes towards
environmental issues, making them suitable for building bank customers' awareness of green banking
practices.
Sun and Wang (2020) found that the social media environment plays a signiﬁcant role in explaining
consumers' intentions to purchase green products, indicating that digital marketing messages can affect
customers' perception of environmental beneﬁts. Rosário and Dias (2025) showed that digital messages
based on transparency, emotional narrative, and personalization can make sustainability more visible
and understandable to consumers. In the banking context, digital campaigns explaining the impact of
electronic statements, digital payments, green ﬁnancing, and reducing paper transactions can raise
customers' level of environmental awareness. Therefore, the third hypothesis can be formulated as:
H3: There is a signiﬁcant positive impact of consumer environmental awareness on the Sustainable
Development Goals in the banking and digital ﬁnancial services sector in Egypt.
3.4 The Mediating Role of Environmental Awareness
The importance of the mediating variable lies in explaining the mechanism by which the independent
variable affects the dependent variable; the mediator is the channel through which the impact passes. In
this study, it is expected that digital marketing affects the SDGs not only directly, but also indirectly by
Page 5/22

raising consumer environmental awareness, making environmental awareness the explanatory
mechanism that translates digital messages into development impact.
Studies have addressed the mediating role of environmental awareness in several contexts. A study by
Paul et al. (2016) conﬁrmed that environmental awareness mediates the relationship between green
marketing and green purchase intention, meaning that effective green marketing increases consumer
awareness, which in turn leads to greater intention to purchase sustainable products. A study by Daoud
et al. (2024) also found that digital content strategies inﬂuence environmental awareness, which
subsequently affects the adoption of sustainable behaviors. García-Salirrosas et al. (2023) and Yadav
and Pathak (2016) reached similar conclusions regarding the mediating role of awareness/knowledge in
shaping sustainable behavioral intentions.
Based on the above, the fourth hypothesis can be formulated as:
H4: Consumer environmental awareness mediates the relationship between digital marketing and the
Sustainable Development Goals in the banking and digital ﬁnancial services sector in Egypt.
4. Research Gap and Problem Statement
Despite efforts to promote digital transformation in the Egyptian banking sector, there remains a
cognitive and applicative gap regarding how to systematically employ digital marketing tools to support
the SDGs and raise consumer environmental awareness. While Egyptian banks have signiﬁcantly
increased their use of digital services, most digital campaigns focus on ease of use, speed, and
promotional offers, giving limited space to sustainability and environmental awareness. This raises
questions about the effectiveness of these tools in achieving more sustainable ﬁnancial and
consumption behavior.
This problem is evident through a comparison of local and international indicators:
1. Financial Inclusion: World Bank reports indicate that ﬁnancial inclusion rates in the Middle East and
North Africa region are about 53%, while in Egypt they rose to 64.8% in 2023 thanks to digital
services. However, a gap of approximately 35% remains compared to developed countries where the
rate exceeds 94% (World Bank, 2023).
2. Environmental Footprint of the Financial Sector: Globally, digital transformation contributes to
reducing paper consumption in European banks by over 70%. While digital transformation in Egypt
saves about 250 million papers annually in banking transactions, the level of environmental
awareness among Egyptian consumers remains at an "intermediate level" according to the
Sustainable Consumption Index (DataReportal, 2024).
Page 6/22

3. Digital Gap in Sustainable Marketing: Data indicates that internet penetration in Egypt reached
72.2% in 2024, yet digital content aimed at promoting sustainability constitutes only 15% of total
digital marketing campaigns of banks, compared to 45% in sustainability-leading countries like
Germany and Denmark (DataReportal, 2024).
4. International Status of SDGs: The 2024 United Nations report indicates that only 17% of global
targets are on track, while nearly half show limited or moderate progress, and more than one-third
are stagnant or regressing (UN DESA, 2024).
5. Trust Gap and Green Skepticism: Studies indicate that consumers may perceive green messages as
mere marketing tools rather than genuine commitments, a concept known as Green Skepticism or
Greenwashing. Research has shown that the absence of credibility and trust reduces the
effectiveness of environmental messages in shaping sustainable consumer behavior (Matthes &
Wonneberger, 2014; Ulusoy & Barretta, 2016).
Based on the above, a gap emerges between the signiﬁcant expansion of digital marketing and digital
ﬁnancial services in Egypt and the need to leverage this expansion systematically to support sustainable
development goals and enhance environmental awareness. The central research question focuses on
the extent to which digital marketing practices by banks and digital ﬁnancial service providers can
directly achieve sustainable development goals, or whether consumer environmental awareness plays a
mediating role in this relationship.
This problem gives rise to several sub-dimensions:
Dimension 1: Most digital campaigns focus on ease of use, speed, and promotional offers, while
sustainability and environmental awareness receive limited attention.
Dimension 2: Consumers may use digital services for convenience or cost reasons without
recognizing the environmental and social impact of these services.
Dimension 3: A trust gap exists among some consumers regarding environmental claims, which are
perceived as part of a marketing image rather than a genuine institutional commitment, thereby
reducing the effectiveness of digital marketing in inﬂuencing sustainable ﬁnancial and consumption
behavior.
Based on the above, the research problem can be formulated through the following questions:
1. What is the impact of digital marketing on the Sustainable Development Goals?
2. What is the impact of digital marketing on consumer environmental awareness?
3. What is the impact of consumer environmental awareness on the Sustainable Development Goals?
4. Does environmental awareness mediate the relationship between digital marketing and the
Sustainable Development Goals?
5. Which digital marketing dimensions most inﬂuence the formation of consumer environmental
awareness?

6 .How can banks and digital ﬁnancial service institutions employ digital marketing to support
sustainability more effectively?

6. Research Signiﬁcance
The scientiﬁc signiﬁcance of this research stems from addressing a complex relationship combining
digital marketing, environmental awareness, and SDGs within a ﬁnancial sector characterized by rapid
transformation and expansion. Despite numerous studies on digital marketing, green marketing, or
sustainable behavior, studies linking ﬁnancial digital marketing to SDGs through environmental
awareness in the Egyptian context remain limited. Thus, the research contributes to ﬁlling a knowledge
gap regarding how the impact of digital marketing transfers from the level of message exposure to the
level of supporting sustainability.
The applied signiﬁcance lies in the fact that the research results can help banks and digital ﬁnancial
service institutions in Egypt develop their digital campaigns not only to focus on attracting customers
but also to integrate sustainability messages and environmental awareness into marketing content. They
can also help policymakers...
7 .Research Methodology
The research adopts a descriptive-analytical methodology, as it is suitable for studying relationships
between variables and analyzing their trends in practical reality. The research targets consumers of
banking and digital ﬁnancial services in Egypt, especially users of banking applications, electronic
wallets, electronic payment services, digital transfers, and mobile ﬁnancial service platforms.

The sampling unit is the Egyptian consumer who uses at least one digital ﬁnancial service. The research
proposes using an electronic questionnaire directed at a sample of digital ﬁnancial service users,
including axes to measure digital marketing, environmental awareness, and SDGs, in addition to
demographic variables.

7.3 Research Population and Sample
This research was based on a research population consisting of all customers of banks and digital
ﬁnancial service institutions in Egypt who use the internet and digital banking services via mobile
applications, websites, and social media platforms. According to the Central Bank of Egypt and
DataReportal (2024), the research population at the beginning of 2024 reached 82.01 million users,
ensuring a broad base for the target population and its readiness for research application.
The sample size was determined based on the Krejcie and Morgan (1974) formula, which recommends
that the ideal sample size for a large population (> 1 million) is approximately 384 participants to obtain
accurate statistical representation with 95% conﬁdence and 5% margin of error. To ensure ease of
implementation and reduce complexity, simple random sampling will be used, where each individual in
the research population has the same probability of being selected regardless of age, gender,
educational level, or geographic location.

8.5 Discussion of Hypotheses Results
First Hypothesis H1: Supported. The direct positive effect (β = 0.312, p < 0.001) indicates that digital
marketing contributes to SDGs by highlighting beneﬁts of digital services such as reducing paper
transactions, lowering the need to travel to branches, supporting ﬁnancial inclusion, and encouraging use
of electronic channels.
Second Hypothesis H2: Supported. The strong effect (β = 0.548, p < 0.001) indicates that digital
marketing is an effective tool for shaping and enhancing consumer environmental awareness through
interactive content, personalized messages, and transparent communication about environmental
beneﬁts.
Third Hypothesis H3: Supported. The effect (β = 0.547, p < 0.001) conﬁrms that environmentally aware
consumers are more likely to recognize and support sustainable digital ﬁnancial services, prefer
paperless transactions, and adopt responsible consumption patterns.
Page 14/22

Fourth Hypothesis H4: Supported (Partial Mediation). The indirect effect (β = 0.300, p < 0.001) alongside
the remaining signiﬁcant direct effect (β = 0.312) indicates that environmental awareness partially
mediates the relationship. This means digital marketing affects SDGs both directly and indirectly by
building environmental awareness.

11. Study Limitations and Future Directions
Page 17/22

The study is limited to clients of banks and digital ﬁnancial services in Egypt, relying on self-reported
responses via an online survey, which may be inﬂuenced by participants’ digital literacy, trust in ﬁnancial
institutions, and demographic characteristics.
11.1 Temporal limitations: This study was conducted during the year 2025. Primary data were collected
via an electronic questionnaire distributed during 2025.
Future research is recommended to:
Test the model on comparative samples between traditional banks, digital banks, and ﬁntech
companies.
Include additional variables such as digital trust, ﬁnancial literacy, perception of cyber risks, and
quality of e-services.
Conduct longitudinal studies to measure the impact of digital campaigns before and after
environmental awareness programs.
Compare customer outcomes in urban and rural areas to assess equity in service access.
12. Conclusion
The study concluded that digital marketing can be a powerful tool to achieve sustainable development
goals in Egypt’s banking and digital ﬁnancial services sector, but it is effective only when it contributes to
raising consumer environmental awareness. While the expansion of digital services provides an
infrastructure conducive to sustainability, transforming this infrastructure into actual developmental
impact requires clear, credible marketing messages aimed at building knowledge, attitudes, and
responsible behaviors among consumers.
