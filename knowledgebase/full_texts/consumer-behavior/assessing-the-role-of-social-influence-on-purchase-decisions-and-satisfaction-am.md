Abstract
The fast growing of e-commerce websites has created a huge need for social influence in influencing the behavior of
consumers especially those who shop virtually. This study attempts to find out how social influences affect the
purchasing decisions and total satisfaction levels of virtual shoppers in Ernakulam District, Kerala. The main purpose
is to find out the different factors of social influence and establish their statistical link with purchase decision making
and satisfaction after the purchases. Descriptive and Analytical research approach has been used in this study and the
primary data has been gathered from 150 respondents through a structured questionnaire that followed five -point
Likert scale. Based on the results, online reviews and ratings (me an = 4.32) were found to be the top influential
variable, followed by platform trust and reliability (mean = 4.18) and social media influencers (mean = 4.05).
Correlation analysis confirms the existence of a statistically significant positive correlation b etween the variable of
social influence and purchase decision (r = 0.612, p < 0.05), showing that the greater one's exposure to the social
influence variable, the greater is their chance to make purchases. Regression analysis also reveals that all aspects of
social influence significantly account for 52.3% of the total variance in overall satisfaction (R² = 0.523, F = 25.84,
p < 0.001). Within the predictors, online reviews and ratings (β = 0.298) and platform trust (β = 0.244) were found to
be the key factors of overall satisfaction. Overall, the study finds out that social influence through information and
trust-related cues influences consumer behavior in the virtual environment to a great extent.

Introduction
Electronic commerce has transitioned from being a retail channel that's only peripheral to a retail channel that's
mainstream, especially for the digitally -savvy population in urban and semi -urban India. The district of Ernakulam
has seen a significant rise in the percentage of consumers who regularly do research, comparison, and shopping online,
on social media marketplaces, and mobile shopping apps. So-called "virtual shoppers" are those who are buying goods
online instead of in a physical store, and who are shopping in a virtual sphere where shopping cues like physical
product inspection, word of mouth in a local community, and dealing with a salesperson are replaced or complemented
by digital alternatives.
Examples of social influence in the digital domain are online product reviews and star ratings; recommendations in
family and peer WhatsApp groups or social media groups; the content produced by social media influencers; and the
active buying or using beha viors of other consumers that are visible on the platforms (for instance, ‘bestseller’ tags,
‘customers also bought’). These cues serve as a type of social proof, which lowers risk and uncertainty, especially for
products that can't be inspected before being bought.
In some international and metro -centric Indian research, the effects of these digital cues on purchase intention have
been reported, but there is a lack of empirical evidence regarding the perception and response of the virtual shoppers
in the Ernakulam di strict to these social cues, and how these social cues lead to purchase and post -purchase
satisfaction. This study aims to fill that void and empirically investigate the relationship between social influence and
purchase decision and satisfaction level for a virtual shopper in the district.
The paper is structured in the following segments. Section I gives the context of virtual shopping and social influence
in Ernakulam. A review of related literature on social media, Trust, Online communities, Kerala consumers' attitude
toward buying, and digital marketing is presented in Section II. The research gap, problem statement, objectives, and
hypotheses of the study are given in Section III. Research design, study area, sample size, method of collecting data,
Conceptual SEM model, and statistical tools used are discussed in Section IV. Analysis of data (demographic profile
of the sample, rank of different influencing social factors, and results of correlation and regression analysis) is
presented in Section V. Discussion of the findings, theoretical meaning, comparison with previous research, and
managerial implications are explained in Section VI. Conclusion of the study, marketing recommendations, and future
work are discussed in Section VII.
Review of Literature
Social Media, Online Communities, and Social Commerce
The effect of digitally mediated social interactions on consumers' purchasing behavior has been explored by numerous
researchers. According to the study, The Digital Pulse (2025) by Sujith & Aju, social media interaction and
participation in online groups can significantly influence consumers' buying decisions, and peer-generated content and
community discussions are very influential in providing product information, regardless of whether they are formal
or informal (Sujith & Aju, 2025) . Sohn and Kim , (2020) examined the factors affecting the purchase intention in a
social commerce environment and identified the social support, platform trust, and perceived interactivity as some of
the significant factors influencing the purchase intention of users on social embedded online platforms (Sohn & Kim,
2020). Taking interactive media one step further, Mehta et al. , (2022) explored the consumer's behavior with regard
to conversational commerce facilitated by messenger chatbots and discovered that conversational interfaces using AI
can provide a sense of personal, dialogue -based engagement that affects consumer decisi on-making in a similar
manner as interpersonal social influence (Mehta et al., 2022).
Trust, Service Quality, and Satisfaction in Online and Digital Retail
In the context of digital services, the link between customer satisfaction and the service -related factors has been
extensively studied. This is relevant to virtual shopping platforms, which involve similar modes of trust -based
interactions between service s and customers, as George and Kumar , (2014) found that customers' satisfaction with
internet banking is significantly influenced by the dimensions of service quality: reliability, responsiveness, and
security (George & Kumar, 2014). Ahmadova et al., (2025) highlighted that trust and reliability are essential for online
food and grocery delivery services to gain the consumer's confidence, as they are unable to inspect the products before
delivery (Ahmadova et al., 2025) . Vittayavarakorn  and Sornsaruht, (2024) study on the Relationship between the
Perceived Quality and the Marketing -mix Elements on the Brand Attitude and Customer Satisfaction in the Organic
Skin Care Market in Thailand (Vittayavarakorn & Sornsaruht, 2024). The study found that the perceived quality and
marketing-mix elements jointly affect the brand attitude and customer satisfaction. Likewise, the authors

Regional Consumer Behavior Studies in Kerala
Some studies are available on consumer behavior in the context of Kerala, which would serve as the background of
the present study. In the rural suburban area of Ernakulam district, Kumar and Joseph, (2014) examined the consumer
behavior regarding FMCG products and found that family influence, local retailer relationship, and brand loyalty had
significant influences on consumer buying behavior in this region, highlighting the importance of the role of socia l
embeddedness in consumer decision making (Kumar & Joseph, 2014). In the context of the growing demand for Khadi
textile products among the younger generation, Nair and Aithal , (2023) studied the green marketing tools and
strategies used to boost the consumption of Khadi textile products among millennials in Kerala (Nair & Aithal, 2023).
They concluded that socially and environmentally conscious messaging increased the purchase intent of the younger
population. This study by Kattali and Sarojadevi , (2025) examines the consumer perception of products made by
Kerala's famous women-led community enterprise network, Kudumbashree, revealing that their consumer perception
was found to be positively influenced by consumer trust in community, consumer ident ification with the community
and the perceived benefits of the community, thus highlighting the influence of socially rooted enterprises on
consumers' purchase-related attitudes in the Kerala market (Kattali & Sarojadevi, 2025).
The Social Dimension of Purchase Intention: Social Enterprise and Purpose-Driven Consumption
There has been a significant body of scholarly work that has investigated how perceived social value, purpose, and
community benefit of a product or seller can relate to the purchase intention of a product – a type of “social influence”
that has a value dimension but is not necessarily interpersonal in nature. Ferdousi, (2017) gave a conceptual overview
of consumer behavior towards social enterprise brands, and Ravi et al., (2022) identified emotional value as a mediator
between the consumer purchase intention and social enterprise positioning (Ravi et al., 2022; Ferdousi, 2017). Lee et
al., (2021) created a structural model of key drivers of customers' intentions to buy products of social enterprises, such
as social trust, perceived value, and ethical concern, while Ng , (2022) discovered that customers' purchase intentions
are strongly influenced by corporate social responsibility (CSR) expectations  (Ng, 2022 ; Lee et al., 2021) . These
findings have been confirmed by cross -cultural research: Ip  et al. , (2024) examined the perceptions of online
consumers of agricultural social enterprises in Taiwan, which also showed that perceived authenticity and social
impact were significant determinants of purchase intention, and Tsai  et al., (2020) provided cross -level evidence of
the effects of social enterprise consumer behaviors on organizational and individual outcomes (Ip et al., 2024; Tsai et
al., 2020). More recently, Tan and Cheah, (2025) studied their motivations (aside from good intentions) for purchasing
products from social enterprises, and Althuizen et al. , (2026) studied how consumers balance "purpose and profit"
when responding to social enterprises in retail and service settings  (Tan & Cheah, 2025 ; Althuizen et al., 2026) .
Together, this literature indicates that social perception of the brand, seller, or product (articulated via digital and
word-of-mouth modalities) is an important values -based component of social influence in purchase decision -making
processes.
Decision-Making and Digital Marketing in Retail Contexts
The present study is also guided by the broader literature on retail decision -making. From the Indian context,
Suvadarshini & Singh , (2024) reviewed the literature critically to find that numerous situational, social, and
psychological factors influence the decision of shoppers to go to the mall, both in -store and via the omnichannel
approach (Suvadarshini & Singh, 2024) . Kapri and Sharma , (2024) explored the influence of digital marketing on
homestay bookings in rural areas and the influence of destination image, concluding that digitally communicated
social proof and online information sources are important enablers in rural homestay book ings, consistent with the
previous study findings relating to social proof in the context of virtual products (Kapri & Sharma, 2024).
Research Gap and Statement of the Problem
The literature reviewed above suggests that social influence can be achieved through various and overlapping
mechanisms such as social media and online community, conversational interactions and chatbots, social
embeddedness and trust in both, and the affe ctive nature of shopping and the perceived social purpose of brands and
sellers. Most of these studies, however, do not consider social influences in isolation but are conducted in the context
of other states or the social influence on other product categories, including FMCG, banking, food delivery, homestays,
or social enterprise products. There is a lack of integrated empirical evidence regarding the influence of various

Objectives of the Study
1. To study the virtual shoppers' profile of Ernakulam district regarding their demographics and online shopping
profile.
2. To recognize and rank the primary drivers of social influence that influence the buying decision of virtual
shoppers.
3. To investigate how social influence and the buying decisions of virtual buyers relate to each other.
4. To examine the effect of social influence on the overall satisfaction of virtual shoppers.
5. To provide recommendations to e -commerce sites and digital marketers on how to use social influence to
improve customer satisfaction.
Research Questions
● RQ1: What is the demographic profile and e-commerce profile of virtual shoppers in Ernakulam district?
● RQ2: What are the sources and dimensions of social influence in the case of virtual shopping?
● RQ3: What is the association between social influence and purchase decision in the context of virtual
shopping?
● RQ4: What is the effect of social influence on the satisfaction of virtual shoppers?
● RQ5: Does demographics play any significant role in influencing social influence?
Hypotheses of the Study
Based on the objectives framed, the following hypotheses were tested at a 5% level of significance:
• H01: Virtual shoppers' purchase decisions are not significantly related to social influence.
• H02: Social influence has no significant effect on the overall satisfaction of virtual shoppers.
• H03: The demographic profile of the respondents does not significantly differ across the level of social
influence experienced.
Research Methodology
Research Design
The study uses a descriptive and analytical approach with the goal of describing the dominant social influence among
virtual shoppers, as well as examining the relationship between social influence, purchase decision, and satisfaction.
Area and Period of the Study
The study was limited to respondents who were from urban and semi -urban areas of Ernakulam district, Kerala, and
its adjoining municipalities of Kochi city. Data Collection Phase was conducted for three months from January 2026
to March 2026.
Sample Size and Sampling Technique
The respondents were sampled using convenience-cum-judgmental sampling technique to get a representative sample
of 150 respondents who actively participate in online/virtual shopping, representative in terms of age group, gender,
occupation, and income.

Data Collection
Primary data was gathered using a structured questionnaire that contained sections on demographic profile, frequency
and pattern of virtual shopping, sources of social influence (rated on a 5 -point Likert scale: "Strongly Disagree" to
"Strongly Agree"), and indicators of purchase decisions, as well as overall satisfaction. Secondary data were obtained
from peer-reviewed journals, books, industry reports, and reputable websites to complement the conceptual framework
and discussion of findings.
Tools for Analysis
The data obtained were analyzed statistically with the help of percentage analysis, arithmetic mean and ranking, Karl
Pearson's coefficient of correlation, multiple regression analysis, and Chi-square test processed in SPSS/MS Excel.

Discussion
The results of this study seem to be very convincing in showing that the social influence is an important and measurable
force in buying decisions and satisfaction after the purchase of the virtual shoppers in Ernakulam district. From a
general point of view, the outcomes demonstrate that virtual shopping conduct is not just self-centered or cost-fixing,
but has a significant impact on socially created signals, including online reviews, recommendations from others,
influence content, and trust signals on platforms.
Interpretation of Key Findings
The correlation analysis showed a positive moderate correlation between social influence and purchase decision,
which means that the more exposure to social influence, the greater the influence on the purchase decision (r = 0.612).
The findings suggest tha t external social validation is important in the digital shopping journey of virtual customers
of Ernakulam as the products purchased online are of high perceived risk and uncertain.
Secondly, the regression results showed that social influence dimensions as a whole explained the variance accounted
for in overall customer satisfaction (R²=0.523), a relatively high variance accounted for in behavioral studies of
consumer satisfaction re search. The study identified a distinct hierarchy of influence. The major drivers  defined as
those with the highest mean scores  were online reviews and star ratings (Mean = 4.32), platform trust and reliability
(Mean = 4.18), and social media influencers (Mean = 4.05). In contrast, interpersonal recommendations and
algorithmic cues acted as minor drivers (Mean scores ranging from 3.21 to 3.92). It is important to note, however, that
while Celebrity Endorsements have a low descriptive mean score (3.21, table 2), they carry high statistical weight
(𝛽 =0.91) within the SEM structural path (Fig . 1), suggesting that while respondents consciously rank them lower,
they still exert significant latent influence on the model.
This hierarchy implies that when consumers have to make satisfaction judgments following purchase, they are more
interested in informational credibility (reviews and ratings) than promotional/persuasive information. The importance
of word of mouth is still  not lost in digital contexts, however, and word of mouth is still an important and powerful
influence within the peer and family context. However, newer mechanisms, like CSR perception and online

Theoretical Implications
Results were very consistent with the Social Influence Theory, which distinguishes between two types of influence,
namely informational influence (reviews, ratings, expert feedback) and normative influence (peer pressure,
endorsement from influencers). The  electronic shopping arena of Ernakulam is revealed as a more powerful arena
than the normative influence, with informational influence being more influential, with online reviews and trust on
websites being the most influential. In addition, results furth er extend the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) by
confirming that perceived trust and social validation are important external factors impacting behavioral intention and
satisfaction. The study also confirms the literature which emphasizes the importance of trust and service reliability in
an online environment where it is impossible to physically inspect the product and there is a high level of uncertainty.
Comparison with Existing Literature
The results are similar to previous research, which has shown how vital digital word of mouth and platform credibility
have become to consumer choice. This study is similar to Sujith and Aju , (2025), in which the results indicate that
social media and online communities have a great impact on buying decisions (Sujith & Aju, 2025) . Similarly, the
significant influence of trust corroborates with George and Kumar , (2014) and Ahmadova et al., (2025), who argued
that perceived reliability is one of the essential factors that lead to user satisfaction in e -services (Ahmadova et al.,
2025).
But the more peripheral CSR perceptions and online communities do not completely align with some international
research regarding the growing importance of ‘ethical consumption’ and ‘community -based commerce’. It may be
because functional factors such as p rice, reliability of delivery, and authenticity of the product may still play a more
important role in the region of Ernakulam as compared to value-based consumption factors.
Practical Implications
There are some ideas in the study that can be used in some kind of action for e-commerce sites and digital marketers.
There should be a focus on investment in review systems, rating transparency and verified feedback mechanisms as
these are the most influential outcomes of investment and satisfaction related to purchasing. Second, establishing trust
is essential to retain customers, especially with reliable payment systems, consistent delivery, and consistent service
quality. Thirdly, while influencer marke ting is useful, it is a secondary influence after peer -generated content,
suggesting that peer -generated content may have a greater impact than influencer marketing. Lastly, peer -to-peer
influence networks, such as WhatsApp groups or informal groups of com munities of digital influence, are still very
much alive and active, and influence consumer behavior in regional markets like Ernakulam.
Finally, it confirmed social influence as a multidimensional concept with its significant effect on the cognitive
(purchase decision) and affective (satisfaction) dimensions of virtual shopping behavior. The results reflect a definite
transition from trust-based and information-based approaches to decision-making in online commerce, as well as the
ongoing importance of interpersonal influence in online settings. These insights emphasize the significance of
leveraging social proof elements strategically in d igital marketing and customer engagement initiatives to enhance
conversions and customer satisfaction.
Conclusion
The research explored the impact of social influence on the buying decision and satisfaction of virtual consumers in
Ernakulam District, and the study was carried out to identify the important factors of social influence and their
statistical significance. The results from the research prove that social influence is an important factor for consumer
decision-making and post -purchase satisfaction in the virtual shopping environment. Of all the factors of social
influence, online review and ratings, trustworthiness of the platform, and social media influencer were found to be the
dominating factors of consumer behavior. Statistical proof of these results is evident through the Karl Pearson
correlation test that indicates a moderate positive correlation between social influence and decision-making (r = 0.612,
p < 0.05). Furthermore, based on the results of the multiple regression analysis, it was found that the dimensions of
social influence account for 52.3% of the variance of total customer satisfaction (R² = 0 .523; F = 25.84; p < 0.001).
In terms of predictor variables, online reviews and ratings (β = 0.298) and the trust in the website (β = 0.244) accounted
for the largest contributions to the level of satisfaction, followed by social media influencers and pee r
recommendations. In terms of demographic effect, the findings revealed the lack of any significant correlation between
the consumers’ demographics and the degree of social influence felt. Thus, the null hypothesis H03 was accepted,

satisfaction compared to promotional and entertaining ones in the case of virtual shopping environment. Although the
influence of peer recommendations and influencer marketing is significant, the primary driving factor for the
consumers' reliance on the signals is their credibili ty, transparency, and reliability. In general, the paper shows that
social influence affects the cognitive and affective dimensions of consumer behavior.
