Abstract: This study aims to examine the effect of green marketing and brand image on
purchase decisions through consumer perception as a mediating variable among Kopi
Kenangan consumers in Jakarta. The study is motivated by the increasing importance of
environmentally responsible marketing practices and strong brand image in influencing
consumer behavior within the competitive food and beverage industry. A quantitative research
approach is employed, with data collected from Kopi Kenangan consumers in Jakarta through
a structured questionnaire. The data are analyzed using statistical methods to examine the direct
effects of green marketing and brand im age on purchase decisions, as well as their indirect
effects through consumer perception. The conceptual framework assumes that effective green
marketing and a positive brand image can shape favorable consumer perceptions, which may
subsequently encourage purchase decisions. Consumer perception is therefore positioned as an
important mechanism linking marketing activities and consumer decision-making. The findings
are expected to provide insights for Kopi Kenangan and similar businesses in developing
environmentally oriented marketing strategies, strengthening brand image, and improving
consumer perceptions to support purchasing decisions.

Keyword: Green Marketing, Brand Image, Consumer Perception, Purchase Decision,
Consumer Behavior, Kopi Kenangan.

INTRODUCTION
Competition in the f ood and beverage industry —particularly among coffee shops and
ready-to-drink coffee brands—is increasingly determined not only by taste, price, and location
but also by brand experience, reputation, and the alignment of brand values with consumer
values. Consumer purchase decisions are formed through a process of evaluating the functional,
emotional, social, and symbolic values offered by a company (Kotler et al.,

2022). Changes in consumer behavior are also influenced by growing concern for
sustainability issues; therefore, marketing strategies that convey environmental messages must
be perceived as valuable, credible, and relevant to consumers (Nekmahmud & Farkas,
2020).
Green marketing is one strategy companies can use to build brand differentiation in a
competitive market through the management of products, pricing, distribution, and promotion
that prioritize sustainability (Majeed et al., 2022). However, green m arketing does not always
directly lead to a purchase decision because consumers must first form perceptions regarding
the sincerity, benefits, quality, and credibility of the green messages they receive (Correia et
al., 2023). In addition to green marketing, brand image is also a key factor in shaping purchasing
decisions because it reflects consumers’ impressions, associations, and beliefs regarding a
brand (Reddy et al., 2023).
Kopi Kenangan is a local coffee brand widely recognized by urban consumers tha t has
grown through a concept centered on easily accessible local coffee, affordable prices, digital
channels, menu innovations, and enhanced customer experiences. In the Jakarta market, Kopi
Kenangan faces competition from local cafés, national coffee chains, international brands, and
new entrants. The initial findings of this study are evident in the 2023–2024 sales data for Café
Kopi Kenangan Jakarta, as presented in Table 1.

Total sales decreased from Rp52,480,000,000 in 2023 to Rp46,320,000,000 in 2024 ,
while the number of transactions fell from 4,160,000 cups to 3,680,000 cups, a decline of
11.73 percent. This decline indicates issues with consumer purchasing decisions,
particularly regarding purchase frequency and brand choice amid increasingly intens e
competition in the coffee shop industry. A decline in sales is not always caused by a single
factor; purchase decisions can be influenced by product appeal, brand image, price perception,
quality of experience, promotional communication, and perceived value, including consumers’
assessments of how well a company’s eco-friendly claims align with its actual practices (Szabo
& Webster, 2021).
This study draws on three theoretical frameworks. Marketing management theory
explains how companies create, communic ate, and deliver value to consumers to drive
purchasing decisions (Kotler et al., 2022). The Stimulus -Organism-Response (S-O-R) Theory
positions the stimulus as an environmental cue, the organism as the individual’s internal
process, and the response as th e action that follows the individual’s interpretation of the
stimulus (Mehrabian & Russell, 1974); in this study, Green Marketing and Brand Image are
positioned  as  marketing  stimuli,  Consumer  Perception  as  the  consumer’s internal process,
and Purchase Decision as the purchasing behavior response. The Theory of Planned Behavior
serves as an additional framework for understanding that purchase decisions are influenced by
beliefs, norms, and perceived behavioral control (Ajzen, 1991).
Green Marketing is understood as consumers’ perceptions of a company’s efforts to
manage products, prices, places, and promotions with a focus on sustainability, as articulated

through the dimensions of Green Product, Green Price, Green Place, and Green Promotion
(Majeed et al., 2022; Nekmahmud & Farkas, 2020). Brand Image is understood as the set of
associations, impressions, beliefs, and consumer evaluations of a brand formed through strong,
beneficial, and unique associations, which are elaborated through the dimensions  of Brand
Identity, Brand Personality, Brand Association, Brand Behavior and Attitude, and Brand
Competence and Benefit (Keller & Swaminathan, 2020). Consumer Perception is understood
as the process by which consumers select, organize, and interpret inform ation regarding
products, brands, prices, promotions, and service experiences through personal factors and
stimulus factors (Solomon, 2020). Purchase Decision is understood as the consumer’s decision
to select, purchase, and use a product based on the alignment with needs, product benefits, and
the appropriateness of the product choice (Kotler et al., 2022).
Previous research has demonstrated a relationship between green marketing, brand
image, consumer perception, and purchasing behavior. Nekmahmud and Far kas (2020) found
that environmental concern, the benefits of green products, and perceptions of green marketing
are associated with green purchasing decisions. Majeed et al. (2022) identified green brand
image and consumer beliefs as mechanisms explaining the influence of green marketing on
green purchase intentions, while Correia et al. (2023) emphasized the importance of green
marketing communication in influencing green purchase behavior . Reddy et al. (2023)
demonstrate that consumer perceptions of gree n marketing are related to preferences for
environmentally friendly products, and Efendioğlu et al. (2022) show that
brand prestige, reputation, and reliability influence purchasing decisions. Nevertheless,
research that specifically positions Consumer Pe rception as a mediating variable in the
relationship between Green Marketing and Brand Image on the Purchase Decision of local
coffee consumers in Jakarta still needs to be developed. The novelty of this study lies in testing
this mediation model using the empirical phenomenon of the decline in sales of Kopi Kenangan
Jakarta from 2023 to 2024 as a contextual basis.
Based on this description, the research framework positions Green Marketing (X1) and
Brand Image (X2) as marketing stimuli that influence Consumer Perception (M), which in turn
influences Purchase Decision (Y). Green Marketing and Brand Image are also assumed to have
a direct effect on Purchase Decision, while Consumer Perception is tested as the mediating
variable between these two effects. The research model is presented in Figure 1.

Based on this framework, the research hypotheses are formulated as follows. Green
Marketing evident in product, price, place, and promotion provides consumers with information
regarding sustainability values and is expected to influence how consumers assess a brand’s
benefits, credibility, and relevance (H1). Brand Image, formed through brand identity,
reputation, experience, and associations, is expected to influence consumers’  perceptions of a
brand’s quality, benefits, and suitability for their needs (H2). Positive Consumer Perception
regarding a brand’s benefits, credibility, and suitability is expected to strengthen purchase
decisions (H3). Green practices that are perceived  as valuable and credible are expected to
directly drive purchase decisions (H4), and a strong brand image is also expected to directly
reinforce purchase decisions (H5). Since purchase decisions are not always automatically
formed in response to marketing stimuli, consumer perception is expected to mediate the effects
of green marketing (H6) and brand image (H7) on purchase decisions. The seven hypotheses
are as follows:
H1: Green Marketing has a positive and significant effect on Consumer Perception. H2: Brand
Image has a positive and significant effect on Consumer Perception.
H3: Consumer Perception has a positive and significant effect on Purchase Decision. H4: Green
Marketing has a positive and significant effect on Purchase Decision.
H5: Brand Image has a positive and significant effect on Purchase Decision.
H6: Consumer Perception mediates the effect of Green Marketing on Purchase Decisions. H7:
Consumer Perception mediates the effect of Brand Image on Purchase Decisions.
Based on this background and hypothesis, this study aims to analyze the influence of
green marketing and brand image on consumer perception, the influence of consumer
perception on purchase decisions, the direct influence of green marketing and brand image on
purchase decisions, and t he mediating role of consumer perception among Kopi Kenangan
customers in Jakarta. The research findings are expected to provide a theoretical contribution
to the study of marketing management and consumer behavior, as well as practical insights for
Kopi Kenangan in strengthening its green marketing strategy, brand image management, and
the cultivation of a more positive consumer perception.

METHOD
This study employs a quantitative approach using explanatory research because it aims
to explain causal relationships among variables through hypothesis testing (Bougie & Sekaran,
2020; Creswell & Creswell, 2023). Data analysis employs Structural Equation Modeling based
on Partial Least Squares (SEM-PLS) using the SmartPLS 4 software, as this approach is suitable
for pre dictive models involving latent constructs, mediating variables, and both direct and
indirect relationships (Hair et al., 2022).
The study population consisted of Kopi Kenangan consumers in Jakarta who had
purchased Kopi Kenangan products and were aware of  or had received information regarding
the brand’s marketing activities. The sample was selected using purposive sampling based on
the following criteria: (1) residing in or active in Jakarta; (2) at least 17 years of age; (3) having
purchased Kopi Kenangan products at least once in the past six months; (4) being aware of or
having received information about Kopi Kenangan’s marketing activities; and (5) willing to
complete the questionnaire in full. The minimum sample size was calculated using the
Lemeshow formula for large or unknown populations (Riyanto & Hatmawan, 2020), namely n
= (Z² × p × q) / d², where Z = 1.96, p = q = 0.50, and d = 0.05, resulting in n = 384.16, rounded

Six of the seven hypotheses were accepted, and one hypothesis was rejected. The direct
path with the largest coefficient was Consumer Perception on Purchase Decision (0.537),
followed by Brand Image on Consumer Perception (0.527). The path from Green Marketing to
Purchase Decision was the only direct effect that was not significant (H4 rejected). In the
mediation test, the indirect effect of Green Marketing on Purchase Decision via Consumer
Perception was significant (0.079; p = 0.001), while the direct effect was not significant; thus,
the resulting pattern was indirect-only mediation (full mediation). The indirect effect of Brand
Image on Purchase Decision through Consumer Perception was also significant (0.283; p <
0.001), and since its direct effect remained significant in the same direction, the resulting pattern
was complementary mediation (partial complementary mediation). The total effect of Green
Marketing on Purchase Decision was 0.114 (p = 0.011), and the total effect of Brand Image on
Purchase Decision was 0.507 (p < 0.001).

Discussion
Green Marketing has a positive and significant effect on Consumer Perception (H1
accepted), consistent with Nekmahmud and Farkas (2020), who demonstrated the role of green
product benefits in  shaping green purchasing tendencies, as well as Majeed et al. (2022) and
Correia et al. (2023), who emphasized the role of green marketing communication in shaping
consumer beliefs and perceptions. However, the coefficient of influence is relatively small (β =
0.147; f² = 0.029), which aligns with the descriptive findings that digital services and the
alignment of price with environmental benefits received relatively low ratings . Within the
framework of Stimulus -Organism-Response Theory, green marketing f unctions as a stimulus
that has not yet become the primary driver in shaping consumer perceptions of Kopi Kenangan;

therefore, green communication needs to be accompanied by more concrete and measurable
evidence.
Brand Image has a positive and significant effect on Consumer Perception with a
much larger coefficient (β = 0.527; f² = 0.368; H2 accepted), consistent with Keller and
Swaminathan (2020), who explain that brand image is formed through strong, beneficial, and
unique associations, as well as Efendio ğlu et al. (2022), who demonstrated the influence of
brand name and reliability on consumer evaluations. These findings indicate that the brand’s
identity, experiences, and benefits —which consumers have directly experienced — serve as
stronger stimuli compa red to environmental programs; therefore, strengthening brand image
and ensuring service consistency across outlets should be a priority.
Consumer Perception has a positive and significant effect on Purchase Decision with the
largest coefficient in the model (β = 0.537; f² = 0.369; H3 accepted), confirming that consumer
perception is the primary psychological mechanism bridging marketing stimuli and purchase
decisions, as explained by Solomon (2020) and supported by Reddy et al. (2023). Conversely,
Green Marketing does not have a significant direct effect on Purchase Decision (β = 0.035; p =
0.365; H4 rejected), indicating an attitude -behavior gap between consumers’ support for
environmental issues and their actual purchasing behavior, consistent with Szabo and Webster’s
(2021) warning regarding perceptions of greenwashing. Nevertheless, the total effect of Green
Marketing on Purchase Decision remains positive and significant (0.114) after accounting for
the pathway through Consumer Perception,  which  reinforces the  pattern  observed  in  Majeed
et  al.  (2022)  that  green marketing operates through psychological mechanisms and brand
image before driving purchase decisions.
Brand Image has a positive and significant effect on Purchase Decision both directl y (β
= 0.224; H5 accepted) and through Consumer Perception (β = 0.283; H7 accepted), with a
complementary  mediation  pattern indicating that  brand  image  operates through  two channels
simultaneously: directly facilitating consumer choice and shaping e valuations of a product’s
quality, benefits, and risks. The total effect of Brand Image on Purchase Decision reached
0.507, confirming the central role of Brand Image in this model, consistent with Efendioğlu et
al. (2022). Meanwhile, Consumer Perception fully mediates (indirect-only mediation) the effect
of Green Marketing on Purchase Decision (β = 0.079; H6 accepted), meaning that Kopi
Kenangan’s green practices only influence purchase decisions after consumers interpret them
as assessments of benefits, credibility, and value alignment.
Overall, the research results support the Stimulus-Organism-Response Theory, as Green
Marketing and Brand Image act as stimuli, Consumer Perception as an internal process
(organism), and Purchase Decision as a behavioral response. The Theory of Planned Beh avior
serves only as an additional framework because the constructs of attitude, subjective norm, and
behavioral control were not explicitly measured. , these findings suggest that the success of
Kopi Kenangan’s green marketing program should not be judged solely by a direct increase in
transactions, but rather by its ability to shape consumer perceptions regarding the program’s
benefits and credibility, while strengthening brand image and ensuring consistent customer
experiences remain the primary drivers of increased purchase decisions.

CONCLUSION
This study concludes that the purchase decisions of Kopi Kenangan consumers in Jakarta
are explained by a combination of Brand Image, Consumer Perception, and Green Marketing,
which operate through distinct mechanisms. Green Marketing and Brand Image were found to
have a positive and significant influence on Consumer Perception, with Brand Image (β =

0.527) serving as a far stronger stimulus than Green Marketing (β = 0.147). Consumer
Perception was found to have a positive and significant effect on Purchase Decision (β = 0.537)
and served as the strongest direct predictor in the model. Brand Image has a positive and
significant effect on Purchase Decision both directly (β = 0.224) and through Consumer
Perception (β = 0.283; c omplementary mediation), whereas Green Marketing does not have a
significant direct effect on Purchase Decision (β = 0.035; p =
0.365) and only has an indirect effect through Consumer Perception (β = 0.079; indirect-
only mediation). The research model exp lains 35.9 percent of the variance in Consumer
Perception and 50.0 percent of the variance in Purchase Decision, with good predictive
relevance and model fit.
Based on these findings, Kopi Kenangan’s management is advised to clearly
communicate concrete an d measurable evidence of eco -friendly practices on packaging, the
app, and social media; link pricing to the sustainability benefits perceived by consumers, for
example through incentives for reusable container users; improve service consistency across
outlets and channels, as this indicator received a relatively low rating in Brand Image; and
translate the brand image into evidence of actual experiences consistent with marketing
messages. Consumer Perception is recommended as a leading indicator through br ief post -
transaction pulse surveys to monitor the effectiveness of marketing strategies before it becomes
apparent in sales data.
For future research, it is recommended to use probability sampling or a more controlled
quota sample, expand the geographic scope to the Greater Jakarta area (Jabodetabek), develop
a longitudinal or experimental design to test changes in perception over time, and include
variables such as perceived value and customer experience, given that
50.0 percent of the variation in Purchase Decision is still explained by factors outside the
model. This study has limitations, including its cross -sectional design, a relatively short data
collection period, the use of nonprobability sampling techniques, potential common method
bias in self -reported data, and a sample scope limited to a single brand and a single region;
therefore, the results should be generalized with caution.
