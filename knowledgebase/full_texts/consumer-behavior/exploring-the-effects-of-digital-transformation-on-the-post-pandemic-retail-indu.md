Abstract. Digital transformation has profoundly reshaped the retail sector during the last decade. Retail
evolved from a predominantly physical industry into a hybrid ecosystem characterized by platform -based
models and omnichannel strategies. The COVID-19 pandemic further accelerated the transition, acting as
a major external shock . It forced retailers to rapidly ad opt digital solutions to maintain operations and
respond to changing consumer behaviors. Understanding the evolution of retail business model is critical,
as firms must adapt to remain competitive while maintaining profitability and consumer loyalty. This study
explored the influence of digital transformation on contemporary retail business models through a
Systematic Literature Review (SLR) guided by the PRISMA framework. Analysing 67 peer-reviewed articles
published between 2020 and 2026 retrieved from the Web of Science database, the research identifies key
trends in post-pandemic retail. The findings indicate that the pandemic catalysed the shift towards seamless
omnichannel retailing and contactless commerce, making digital adoption a matter of survival rather than
strategic choice. The review highlights that business model innovation is essential for organisational
resilience. As the firms integrate online and offline capabilities, they achieve greater operational flexibility
and efficiency. Furthermore, the study demonstrates that technological integration, specifically through AI,
IoT and blockchain, supports sustainability by minimizing waste and enabling circular economy practices.
This research provides a n overview of how digital transformation drives value creation, resilience and
environmental responsibility in the global retail landscape.

Keywords: retail, digital transformation, business mode, e-commerce, omnichannel.

Introduction
The retail industry plays a central role in modern economies, as it directly connects producers and
consumers, generates significant levels of employment and contributes substantially to national
gross domestic product (GDP). According to the European Commis sion website : “the retail
ecosystem encompasses various sectors, including grocery and non -grocery retail, wholesale and
online platforms. It is closely interconnected with manufacturers, importers, packaging,
transportation and logisti cs service providers, postal and courier services providers, fulfilment
centres, marketing, financial and payment services and retail real estate.” (European Commission,
2025) retail also influences consumption patterns, distribution systems and overall ma rket
dynamics, making it a key sector for economic growth and social development. Due to its
interaction with consumers and rapid exposure to market changes, retail is inherently dynamic and
continuously adapts to evolving economic, technological and social conditions.
 In recent decades, digital technologies have increasingly reshaped economic activities
across most industries, transforming how firms operate, interact with customers and create value.
Technologies such as e- commerce platforms, mobile applications, big data analytics, arti ficial
intelligence and could computing have altered business processes, customer experiences and
competitive strategies. The retail sector has been particularly affected by these developments, as
digitalization has changed traditional models of distribution, communication and service delivery.

landscape is characterized by increased reliance on digital channels, omnichannel  strategies and

 Against this background, this paper aims to explore the effects of digital transformation on
the post-pandemic retail industry through a systematic literature review. By synthesizing existing
academic research, the study seeks to identify trends , impacts and challenges associated with
digitalization in retail and to provide a n overview of how the retail sector is evolving in response

Literature review
Digital transformation has become one of the most discussed topics in contemporary management
and information systems research  over the last decade. Despite being widely used, researchers
define the concept in different ways depending on the level of technological, organizational or
strategic changes examined. Vial (2019) defines digital transformation as a process in which
organizations improve their performance by integrating digital technologies into business models,
operational activities and customer interactions. Similarly, Verhoef et al. (2021) argue that digital
transformation should not be understood merely as the implementation of new technologies, but
rather as a broader organizational shift involving structural and cultural changes as well as strategic
reorientation.
Within the retail sector, digitaliz ation refers to the integration of digital technologies into
retail operations and customer relevant activities with the aim of increasing customer engagement
and creating additional value  (Hagberg et al., 2016). As digital tools become more and more
integrated in retail operations, they influence the way retailers communicate with customers ,
organize the supply chains and deliver products and services across both physical and online
environments. Reinartz et al. (2019)  note that digital retail has gradually evolved from a
predominantly brick store -based industry into a hybrid ecosystem shaped by omnichannel
strategies and digital platform interactions.
A large number of technologies currently support digital transformation in retail. Artificial
Intelligence (AI) and Machine Learning (ML) support functions such as  personalized
recommendations, demand forecasting, and automated decision- making (Davenport et al., 2020).
Big data analytics allows retailers to process large volumes of customer and transaction data in
order in order to better understand consumer behavior and optimize operations (Wamba et  al.,
2017). At the same time, Internet of Things (IoT) technologies contribute to the development of
smart retail environments through devices such as RFID systems, sensors, and intelligent shelves,
improving inventory control and in- store experiences. Cl oud computing further supports digital
transformation by offering scalable infrastructure and real -time access to data and services. In
addition, digital platforms facilitate interactions between retailers, customers, and external
partners, contributing to the emergence of new business models and transforming market dynamics
(de Reuver et al., 2018).
Together, these technologies form the technological foundation of digital transformation in retail
and support more flexible, data-driven and consumer-oriented business strategies.
Sorescu et al. (2011) note that business models consist of interconnected elements related
to format, activities, and governance. From this systems perspective, innovation in retail business
models involves changes not only in individual , isolated activities, but also in the relationship

Methodology
Following the review of the theoretical foundations concerning retail evolution and digital
transformation, the research continues toward a systematic examination of the ways in which
digital transformation has reshaped the retail sector in the post-pandemic environment. To address
this objective, the study adopts a Systematic Literature Review (SLR) methodology with focus on
contemporary retail business models and their transformation through digital technologies. The
review process follows the PRISMA fram ework, which supports a transparent and replicable
procedure for identifying, screening, and selecting relevant academic sources (Page et al., 2021).
 The Web of Science (WoS) database was selected as the primary source of literature due to
its strong acad emic reputation and extensive coverage of peer -reviewed journals. Another
important consideration was the database’s suitability for systematic review procedures, especially
because it offers standardized indexing and reliable citation metadata. Previous m ethodological
studies have highlighted the advantages of WoS for systematic reviews, especially in terms of
consistency and reproducibility of search results (Gusenbauer et al., 2020). Compared with broader
databases, WoS provides stricter journal selection standards . This helps reduce the inclusion of
low-quality or non-academic sources. Although expanding the search to additional databases could
have increased the number of retrieved studies, the present research prioritizes methodological
rigor, quality control, and transparency in the selection process.
 The study aims to explore how digital transformation influences retail business models in
the period following the COVID -19 pandemic. Based on this objective, the systematic review is
guided by the following research question:  How has digital transformation reshaped retail
business models in contemporary retail environments?
To address this question, the search strategy was developed around three interconnected
thematic dimensions: digital transformation, business models, and retail.
The first group of keywords included the terms “digital transformation,” “digitization,” and
“digitalization.” These concepts were selected in order to capture the variety of expressions used
in academic literature to describe  technology-driven organizational change. Even though “digital
transformation” is the dominant concept in recent research, the other terms are frequently used
across different disciplines and contexts, making their inclusion necessary for a broader and mor e
accurate literature coverage.
The second thematic dimension focused on business models and included the keywords
“business model,” “value creation,” and “innovation.” Existing literature often discusses
transformations in retail through concepts related to innovation and value creation without
explicitly referring to the term “business model.” Including these additional concepts therefore
increased the methodological nature of the search process and reduced the possibility of excluding
relevant studies addressing similar phenomena from different theoretical perspectives.
The third thematic dimension focused on the retail sector itself and included the keywords
“retail,” “retailing,” “retailer,” “online retail,” “e -commerce,” and “omnichannel.” These terms

For the literature search, the Topic (TS) field available in Web of Science was used instead
of limiting the search exclusively to article titles. The TS field includes titles, abstracts, and author
keywords, allowing for broader coverage of the literature. This approach was considered more
appropriate for a systematic review because many studies discuss digital transformation or business
model innovation extensively within the abstract or keywords without necessarily mentioning these
concepts directly in the title.
The PRISMA framework has become one of the most widely used approaches for
conducting systematic literature reviews due to its emphasis on transparency and methodological
consistency. Previous studies in retail and related research fields have successfully adopted
PRISMA-based methodologies, including the works of Yang et al. (2017), Melacini et al. (2018),
and Risberg (2023). These studies demonstrate the us efulness of PRISMA for organizing the
literature selection process . It lead to  identifying dominant research directions, and analyzing
theoretical and methodological patterns across existing publications. The continued use and
citation of these reviews further supports the academic relevance and methodological credibility of
PRISMA as a framework for evidence synthesis.
The initial search generated 434 records. Since the research focuses specifically on post -
pandemic developments in retail, the dataset was first filtered by publication year. Only studies
published between 2020 and 2026 were retained, as this period captures both the outbreak of the
COVID-19 pandemic and the accelerated digital transformation that followed. After applying this
criterion, the number of records decreased to 367.
A second filtering stage focused on document type. To maintain academic rigor and ensure
the inclusion of validated research findings, only peer -reviewed journal articles were retained.
Conference proceedings, editorials , book chapters, and other publication types were excluded at
this stage, reducing the dataset to 293 articles.
The next step involved applying a language filter. Articles published in English and German
were included in the review. English -language public ations were expected to dominate the
international literature, while German-language studies were included in order to broaden the scope
of the review and reduce potential language bias. Since the researcher is proficient in German,
these sources could be assessed and interpreted accurately. Following this step, the sample
consisted of 286 studies.

The dataset was then refined further using Web of Science research categories. Only
categories directly related to the research topic were retained, including Bu siness, Management,

During the eligibility stage, all remaining articles were assessed through full-text review in
order to verify their relevance to the research objective. This process reduced the number of eligible

concentrated primarily on corporate social responsibility (CSR) topics without establishing a direct
connection to retail business model transformation or digitalization. The remaining 67 articles

Results and discussions
The analysis of the 67 selected studies provides a n overview of the ways in which digital
transformation and emerging technologies are reshaping both the retail sector and its associated
supply chains. Across the reviewed literature, researchers discuss several major strategic
developments. These include mainly the expansion of omnichannel retailing, the growing use of
artificial intellig ence, and the integration of Internet of Things (IoT) technologies aimed at
improving operational performance and customer interaction. One of the most frequently recurring
themes identified in the review relates to the impact of the COVID -19 pandemic. This topic was
explicitly discussed in approximately 18% of the analyzed papers. The pandemic is widely
described as a turning point that accelerated the transition toward digital platforms . Contactless
shopping solutions and new forms of retail interaction were also included were also addressed. In
addition, many studies emphasize the importance of business model innovation in helping retailers
balance efficiency, flexibility, and customer expectations in rapidly changing market conditions.

COVID-19 pandemic as a catalyst for retail digital transformation
The COVID-19 pandemic began at the end of 2019. It  was officially declared a global pandemic
by the World Health Organization in March 2020. The health crisis, together with the restrictions
introduced to limit the spread of the virus, generated substantial disruptions across economic
sectors w orldwide. In retail, lockdowns, social distancing measures, and mobility restrictions
significantly affected both consumer behavior and the day-to-day functioning of businesses. These
events and social measures created pressure for rapid operational adaptation.
Among the 67 articles included in the review, 15 studies explicitly identified the pandemic
as a major accelerator of digital transformation within the retail sector. These studies describe
COVID-19 as an external shock that forced retailers to accel erate digital adoption . The
digitalization was not simply part of long -term strategic development, it was more an immediate
response of the activity sector  necessary for business continuity and survival (Allagiannis et al.,
2025; Ean et al., 2022). The tra nsformation occurred through multiple interconnected processes,
including the rapid implementation of digital technologies, the redesign of customer interaction
channels, and the restructuring of existing business models.
One of the strongest drivers of this transformation was the temporary closure of physical
stores during lockdown periods, which led to fast  declines in customer traffic and in -store sales
(Allagiannis et al., 2025). As a result, many retailers redirected their activities toward digital
channels and accelerated investments in online operations (Baldivia et al., 2024). In parallel, some
companies introduced service robots. Other automated solutions were also adopted in an effort to
better integrate digital and physical retail environments while remaining competitive within
increasingly technology-driven markets (Ean et al., 2022).
Retailers that had previously relied primarily on physical stores were compelled to
implement digital alternatives such as e -commerce platforms, mobile applications,  and online
marketing tools (Palmié et al., 2022). In South Africa, for instance, major food retailers adopted
“click-and-collect” services and mobile-based purchasing systems, contributing to a reported 50%
increase in online sales during 2020 (Ean et al., 2022).
The pandemic also accelerated the adoption of omnichannel retail strategies among small
and medium-sized retailers. These strategies aimed to create more integrated customer experiences
by connecting websites, mobile applications, social media pla tforms, and physical stores into a
unified retail ecosystem. Implementing such systems required retailers to overcome important
operational and technological challenges. Among the basic steps was the integration of information
technologies capable of supporting customer engagement, sales optimization, and feedback
management (Baldivia et al., 2024).
At the same time, concerns regarding physical interaction and public health increased
demand for contactless retail solutions (Wang et al., 2024). As a conseque nce, retailers expanded
the use of technologies such as contactless payment systems, automated vending solutions, and
digitally enabled self-service stores (Heins, 2023).
Finally, Joshi et al. (2023), in their analysis of the Czech retail sector, demonstrate that the
pandemic also encouraged the adoption of more advanced digital technologies, including Artificial
Intelligence (AI), Machine Learning (ML), and Augmented Reality (AR). Their findings indicate
that the crisis accelerated the development of voice- assisted shopping systems and intensified the
use of digital marketing practices aimed at improving customer interaction and personalization.

Business Model Innovation and Resilience
The findings show that 28 of the 67 reviewed studies describe the pandemic as a major driver of
business model innovation in retail. Many retailers had to rethink how they create value and interact
with customers. Studies suggest that companies needed to balance efficiency -focused strategies,
which support quick adaptati on, with more innovative approaches that encourage deeper
transformation (Hu et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2023). Research from China shows that retailers using
“New Retail” models, which combine online and offline operations, performed better during the
pandemic than traditional retailers in terms of efficiency and productivity (Yang et al., 2023).
Operational flexibility was also an important factor in business resilience. Companies that
adapted their products and services while expanding digital sales channels achieved better sales
results and adjusted more easily to market changes (Shekarian et al., 2023). In contrast, businesses
that relied only on physical stores faced greater difficulties and were less adaptable.
The pandemic also changed consumer behaviour and accelerated digital engagement. More
consumers turned to online shopping because of health concerns and movement restrictions
(Neiberger et al., 2020). At the same time, customers developed new expectations, including a
preference for contactless shopping and “phygital” experiences that combine physical and digital
retail. In Germany, for example, the pandemic created new opportunities for online grocery
shopping, although this growth slowed once restrictions were lifted (Dannenberg et al., 2020).

Sustainability
The literature shows that technology integration helps retailers respond more effectively to external
challenges such as the COVID -19 pandemic and market instability. Organizational resilience is
strongly connected to digital flexibility, which allows businesses to adjust sales channels and
product offerings during crises (Shekarian et al., 2023). Companies that combined product or
service changes with digital sales channels achieved better results, while businesses that depended
only on physical stores struggled more.
Digitalization also improves supply chain flexibility by helping firms balance efficiency
and innovation. Research suggests that digital supply chains strengthen overall business resilience
(Poku et al., 2025). In unstable and uncertain business environments, digital transformation also
supports both gradual and major innovations (Su et al., 2025). In addition, retailers can improve
long-term resilience by managing multiple business models and integrating physical and digital
operations through technology (Casciani et al., 2022).
Technology also helps reduce environmental impact and supports sustainable business
practices. Many studies highlight the importance of reducing waste and improving resource
management. For example, Ou et al. (2021) found that digital tools in the fresh food sector help
retailers predict consumer demand and adjust prices more effectively. This reduced food waste
while also increasing profits. Other studies show that combining blockchain and Internet of Things
(IoT) technologies improves supply chain management, reduces waste, and lowers carbon
emissions (Nazir et al., 2024).
Technology-based strategies are also important for the growth of online second- hand and
resale platforms. These platforms encourage sustainable consumption and support the circular
economy by making it easier and safer for consumers to buy and sell used products.

Conclusion
This research paper examines how digital technologies are changing the global retail industry,
especially after the disruptions caused by the COVID-19 pandemic. Using a systematic review of
academic literature, the study shows that major crises, including global health emergencies, often
accelerate the adoption of e- commerce and omnichannel business models. The fi ndings point to
technologies such as artificial intelligence, blockchain, and the Internet of Things as important
tools for improving operational efficiency and strengthening customer engagement. The reviewed
studies also indicate that innovation in business models can help companies become more resilient
while supporting sustainability by reducing waste and more efficient supply chains.
Overall, the literature suggests that retail is mainly  moving toward hybrid environments
where physical and digital experiences are closely connected in order to respond to changing
consumer expectations and purchasing habits.
This study contributes to the existing literature by bringing together and organizing current
research on digital transformation in post-pandemic retail. It also provides useful insights for both
researchers and practitioners by highlighting major trends, identifying gaps in the literature, and
outlining possible directions for future research.
Future studies could build on these findings by examining how digital transformation
affects retail performance, resilience, and competitiveness in real business settings. More research
is also needed on consumer behaviour in hybrid and omnichannel retail environments, particularly
regarding digital trust, customer engagement, and changing purchasing patterns. In addition,
technology-focused research on artificial intelligence, the Internet of Things, and other digital
innovations could provide a cl earer understanding of how these technologies contribute to
operational efficiency and supply chain optimization. Comparative studies across different
countries and markets would also be valuable, as the pace and nature of digital adoption can vary
significantly depending on the context. Finally, long -term studies are needed to evaluate whether
the digital transformations adopted after the pandemic remain sustainable over time and how they
continue to influence organizational resilience and sustainable retail practices.
Although this study followed a systematic and structured research approach, several
limitations should be considered. First, the literature review relied exclusively on the Web of
Science database. While Web of Science is recognized as a reli able and high- quality academic
source, limiting the search to a single database may have excluded relevant studies available
through platforms such as Scopus, PubMed, or Google Scholar. Second, the review applied specific
inclusion and exclusion criteria, including restrictions related to language and publication period,
which may have resulted in the omission of relevant research published in other languages or
outside the selected timeframe. Third, like most systematic literature reviews, the conclusions of
this study depend on the quality and methodological rigor of the studies included in the analysis.
Finally, the thematic analysis and categorization of findings involved a degree of interpretation,
which may introduce some level of researcher bias despi te efforts to maintain consistency and
transparency throughout the process.
These limitations should be taken into account when interpreting the results and may serve
as a basis for future research. Expanding the review to multiple databases, incorporating  grey
literature, and combining different methodological approaches could provide a broader and more
systematic understanding of digital transformation in the retail sector.
