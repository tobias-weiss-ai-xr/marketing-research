ABSTRACT
As a supplier of energy services, PT. PLN (Persero) strategically supports social and economic endeavors. This
study looks at how communication service quality affects customer satisfaction, which in turn affects perceived
risk, trust, and intention to utilize prepaid power services. Purposive sample of 150 clients of PT. PLN (Persero) UP3
Sanggau was used in a quantitative study. PLS-SEM with SmartPLS 4.1.1 was used to evaluate the data. The findings
demonstrate that communication service quality significantly improves customer satisfaction (β = 0.484, p < 0.001),
customer trust ( β = 0.639, p < 0.001), and perceived risk ( β = 0.197 , p = 0.015). Additionally, the intention to utilize
prepaid power services is significantly increased by customer satisfaction (β = 0.323, p = 0.002) and communication
service quality ( β = 0.441, p < 0.001). On the other hand, customer happiness is significantly impacted negatively
by perceived risk ( β = -0.147, p = 0.035) and favorably by customer trust ( β = 0.235, p = 0.019). Customer trust and
communication service quality are shown to have the highest correlation. 47% of the variation in intention to use
is explained by the model (R2 = 0.470). By including perceived risk and communication service quality into the
model of consumer behavioral intention in prepaid power services, this study advances the Theory of Reasoned
Action (TRA). Practically speaking, the results give PT. PLN (Persero) guidance on how to enhance communication
tactics to increase customer happiness, reduce perceived risk, and build trust in order to increase customer
uptake of prepaid power services.

1. INTRODUCTION
Technological advancements in the era of globalization
have significantly transformed the way societies fulfill
their daily needs, with electricity emerging as one of the
most essential resources supporting household activities,
education, business operations, and broader economic
development. In Indonesia, electricity services are
provided by PT Perusahaan Listrik Negara (Persero),
a state-owned enterprise responsible for ensuring the
availability and reliability of the national electricity
supply (Persero, 2024).
In its service operations, PT PLN (Persero) offers two
primary electricity service schemes: postpaid and
prepaid systems. While postpaid electricity allows
customers to consume electricity before making
payments, it is often associated with issues such as billing
uncertainty, meter reading inaccuracies, late payment
penalties, and disconnection risks (Khumaidi & Dahda,
2018; Hartawan, 2023). In contrast, prepaid electricity
provides greater transparency and control over
consumption, enabling customers to manage usage
based on their financial capacity (Banurea & Aisyah,
2022). Despite these advantages, the adoption of
prepaid electricity services remains uneven, indicating
that technical superiority alone is insufficient to drive
customer acceptance.
Previous research indicates that customer satisfaction
and behavioral intention are significantly influenced
by service quality (Abideen et al., 2018). However,
most prior research has primarily focused on general
service quality dimensions, with limited attention given
to communication-specific aspects, particularly in
utility service contexts. This represents an important
gap, as electricity services involve complex procedures,
including token purchases, tariff understanding, and
outage handling, which require clear, accurate, and
responsive communication between service providers
and customers.

Moreover, previous studies have shown that customer
satisfaction is influenced not only by ser vice per formance
but also by psychological factors such as trust and
perceived risk (Jin & Lim, 2021). Trust reflects customers’
confidence in the reliability and integrity of service
providers, while perceived risk relates to uncertainties
regarding system reliability, payment security, and
potential service disruptions (Resa & Andjarwati,
2019). Although these variables have been widely
examined, prior findings remain fragmented, as they
are often analyzed separately or without integrating
communication service quality as a central antecedent.
From a theoretical perspective, the Theory of Reasoned
Action (TRA) explains that behavioral intention is shaped
by individuals’ evaluations and beliefs. In the context of
prepaid electricity services, customers’ evaluations may
be influenced by communication quality, which shapes
trust and perceived risk, ultimately affecting satisfaction
and intention to use. However, empirical applications
of TRA in utility service settings, particularly in prepaid
electricity services in Indonesia, remain limited.
Based on a preliminary survey conducted among
postpaid electricity customers in Sanggau Regency, it
was found that although customers are familiar with the
postpaid system, concerns regarding billing uncertainty
and system reliability have encouraged interest in
switching to prepaid electricity. Nevertheless, customers
still express doubts related to information clarity and
perceived risks, indicating that communication plays
a critical role in shaping their evaluation of prepaid
electricity services.
In order to fill the highlighted research vacuum, this
study creates an integrated model that looks at how
communication service quality affects customer
satisfaction, perceived risk, and trust, as well as how
these factors affect the propensity to utilize prepaid
power services. By integrating perceived risk and
communication service quality in the context of public
utility services, this study adds to the body of literature by
expanding the application of the Theory of Reasoned
Action. Practically speaking, the results should help PT
PLN (Persero) create more successful communication
plans to increase customer happiness and increase the
use of prepaid power services.
2. LITERATURE REVIEW
2.1 State of the Art
The growing significance of service quality in influencing
customer satisfaction and behavioral intention has been
highlighted by recent research in service management
and consumer behavior. However, previous research
has mostly concentrated on generic aspects of service
quality, such as tangibles, assurance, responsiveness,
empathy, and dependability, without specifically
identifying communication service quality as a distinct
concept (Abideen et al., 2018; Qalati et al., 2021).
Empirical studies have shown that service quality
significantly influences customer trust, perceived risk,
and customer satisfaction, which subsequently affect
behavioral intention (Jin & Lim, 2021). Nevertheless,
findings across studies are not always consistent. For
instance, while Jin and Lim (2021) found that service
quality reduces perceived risk, other studies indicate
that the relationship may vary depending on the
context and the nature of the service (Lainamngern &
Sawmong, 2019). This inconsistency suggests that more
context-specific investigation is required, particularly in
utility services such as prepaid electricity.
In the context of prepaid electricity services,
communication plays a critical role because customers
rely heavily on accurate and timely information
regarding token usage, payment procedures, and
service disruptions. Despite this, limited studies have
specifically examined communication service quality
as a central variable, especially in developing countries
like Indonesia. Furthermore, previous research tends to
analyze customer trust, perceived risk, and satisfaction
separately, rather than integrating them into a
comprehensive model.
Therefore, this study fills up these gaps by combining
customer happiness, perceived risk, communication
service quality, and customer trust into a cohesive
framework to explain consumers' intentions to utilize
prepaid power services.
2.2 Theory of Reasoned Action (TRA)
According to the Theory of Reasoned Action (TRA),
an individual's attitudes and subjective norms, which
are based on beliefs and assessments of a behavior,
determine their behavioral purpose (Ajzen & Fishbein,
1980). In service contexts, these beliefs are shaped by
customers’ perceptions of service attributes, including
service quality, trust, and risk.
Previous studies have successfully applied TRA to
explain consumer behavior in various service settings,
demonstrating that positive beliefs about a service
lead to favorable attitudes and stronger behavioral
intentions (Jin & Lim, 2021). In this study, communication
service quality is conceptualized as a key antecedent
influencing customer beliefs, which are reflected
in customer trust and perceived risk. These factors
subsequently affect customer satisfaction and ultimately
determine customers’ intention to use prepaid electricity
services.

2.3 Communication Service Quality
Communication service quality refers to the ability of
a service provider to deliver clear, accurate, timely,
and responsive information to customers. It is a critical
component of overall service quality, particularly in
services that rely heavily on information exchange
(Abideen et al., 2018).
Prior research has established that effective
communication enhances customer understanding,
reduces uncertainty, and strengthens the perceived
reliability of a service (Qalati et al., 2021). However, most
studies treat communication as part of general service
quality rather than examining it as an independent
construct. This limitation highlights the need to explore
communication service quality more specifically,
especially in contexts where customer decisions depend
on information clarity, such as prepaid electricity
services.
2.4 Customer Trust
Customers' faith in a service provider's skill, honesty, and
dependability is referred to as customer trust. In order to
lower uncertainty and promote enduring connections
between clients and service providers, trust is essential.
(Maharsi et al., 2021).
Empirical studies indicate that service quality positively
influences customer trust, as clear and reliable
communication enhances customers’ confidence in
a service (Jin & Lim, 2021). However, trust formation
is highly context-dependent, particularly in services
involving financial transactions and system reliability,
such as prepaid electricity. Therefore, examining the
role of communication service quality in building trust
is essential.
2.5 Perceived Risk
Perceived risk refers to customers’ subjective assessment
of uncertainty and potential negative consequences
associated with using a service (Santoso & Lestari, 2022).
In service contexts, perceived risk is closely related to
information asymmetry and lack of transparency.
Previous studies suggest that high service quality
can reduce perceived risk by providing clear and
reliable information (Jin & Lim, 2021). However, in
certain contexts, perceived risk may persist despite
high service quality, particularly when customers lack
prior experience or trust in the system (Lainamngern
& Sawmong, 2019). This indicates that the relationship
between service quality and perceived risk is complex
and warrants further investigation.
2.6 Customer Satisfaction
Customer satisfaction is defined as the evaluation of a
service based on the comparison between expectations
and actual performance. It is widely recognized as a
key outcome of service quality and a determinant of
behavioral intention (Qalati et al., 2021).
Studies consistently show that higher service quality
leads to increased customer satisfaction. Additionally,
customer trust and perceived risk have been found to
significantly influence satisfaction, either positively or
negatively, depending on the context (Jin & Lim, 2021).
Therefore, customer satisfaction serves as a mediating
variable linking service quality, trust, and perceived risk
to behavioral intention.

2.7 Intention to Use
Intention to use refers to customers’ willingness and
tendency to adopt or continue using a service based on
prior experience and evaluation. In this study, intention
to use is defined as customers’ intention to continue
using prepaid electricity services.
Customer happiness is one of the best indicators of
intention to use, according to earlier research, as happy
consumers are more likely to promote and use a service
again (Qalati et al., 2021). In addition, trust enhances
intention by increasing confidence in the service, while
perceived risk may weaken intention due to uncertainty
and potential negative outcomes (Jin & Lim, 2021).
2.8 Research Gap
Customer happiness is one of the best indicators of
intention to use, according to earlier research, as happy
consumers are more likely to promote and use a service
again. First, most studies focus on general service quality
dimensions, with limited attention to communication
service quality as a distinct construct. Second, previous
research tends to analyze customer trust, perceived
risk, and satisfaction separately rather than integrating
them into a comprehensive model. Third, empirical
findings regarding the relationship between service
quality and perceived risk remain inconsistent across
different contexts.
Furthermore, there are still a few studies that specifically
address the quality of communication services in
prepaid electricity services, especially in developing
nations like Indonesia. As a result, this study suggests
an integrated model that looks at how communication
service quality affects customer satisfaction, perceived
risk, and trust, as well as how it affects consumers'
intentions to utilize prepaid power services.
2.9 Hypothesis Development
Communication service quality plays a crucial role
in shaping customer perceptions in service contexts,
particularly in services that rely heavily on information
exchange. High-quality communication enables
customers to receive accurate, timely, and clear
information, which reduces uncertainty and enhances
their confidence in the service provider. Previous studies
have shown that effective service quality positively
influences customer trust, as customers tend to rely more
on service providers who deliver consistent and reliable
information (Abideen et al., 2018; Jin & Lim, 2021). As a
result, the following theory is put forward:
H1: Communication service quality has a positive effect
on customer trust.
In addition to building trust, communication service
quality is also expected to influence perceived risk. Clear
and transparent communication reduces ambiguity
and helps customers better understand the service,
thereby lowering their perceived uncertainty and
potential negative outcomes. Prior research indicates
that higher service quality can reduce perceived risk,
although the strength of this relationship may vary
across contexts (Lainamngern & Sawmong, 2019; Jin
& Lim, 2021). Accordingly, the following hypothesis is
formulated:
H2: Communication service quality has a negative
effect on perceived risk.
Furthermore, Customer happiness is directly impacted
by the quality of communication services. Customers
are more likely to see a service as dependable and
good when they receive prompt, clear communication.
According to empirical data, consumer happiness in a
variety of service sectors is significantly influenced by
service quality (Qalati et al., 2021). Consequently, the
following theory is proposed:
H3: The quality of communication services has a
favorable effect on customer satisfaction.
Another significant element affecting client happiness
is consumer trust. Customers are more likely to have
favorable opinions of a service provider when they trust
them, which raises satisfaction levels. In service contexts,
prior research has repeatedly shown that trust has a
favorable impact on consumer happiness (Jin & Lim,
2021; Maharsi et al., 2021). Thus, the following hypothesis
is proposed:
H4: Customer trust has a positive effect on customer
satisfaction.
On the other hand, perceived risk is expected to
negatively influence customer satisfaction. Higher
perceived risk increases customer uncertainty and
reduces confidence in the service, which may lead to
dissatisfaction. Prior research has demonstrated that
perceived risk negatively affects customer satisfaction,
particularly in services involving financial and technical
uncertainty (Lainamngern & Sawmong, 2019; Santoso
& Lestari, 2022). Therefore, the following hypothesis is
formulated:
H5: Perceived risk has a negative effect on customer
satisfaction.
It is often acknowledged that a major factor influencing
behavioral intention is customer satisfaction. Customers
who are happy with a service are more inclined to stick
with it and refer others to it. Customer satisfaction has
a favorable impact on intention to use, according to
several empirical research (Qalati et al., 2021). Thus, the
following theory is put forth:

3. METHODOLOGY
In order to investigate the strength and direction of the
interactions between independent and dependent
variables, this study uses a quantitative research
technique using an associative design. This study's
primary goal is to examine how communication service
quality affects customers' propensity to utilize prepaid
energy services at PT PLN (Persero) UP3 Sanggau as well
as their perceptions of risk, trust, and satisfaction.
Because it enables researchers to get data directly
from respondents via standardized questionnaires,
the research method employed is a survey method.
Survey-based quantitative research is widely applied
to measure perceptions, attitudes, and behavioral
intentions using numerical data that can be statistically
analyzed (Saputra, 2021).

3.1 Unit of Analysis and Research Location
Customers who use prepaid power services were the
study's unit of analysis. The study was conducted at
PT PLN (Persero) UP3 Sanggau and covered several
administrative regions in West Kalimantan, Indonesia,
including Sanggau Regency, Sintang Regency, Kapuas
Hulu Regency, Sekadau Regency, and Melawi Regency.
3.2 Time Horizon
This study used a cross-sectional temporal horizon,
which means that information was gathered all at
once. Because of time and resource limitations, a
cross-sectional approach is appropriate for studying
phenomena in a short amount of time (Herdiani, 2021).
Data collection for this study was carried out in March
2025.
The population of this study consists of all prepaid
electricity customers registered at PT PLN (Persero) UP3
Sanggau, West Kalimantan, totaling 341,209 customers.
A sample is defined as a subset of the population that
represents the characteristics of the population being
studied (Imron, 2019). To determine the sample size, this
study applies the Slovin formula, which is commonly
used when the population size is known (Adnyana, 2021).

In this study, intention to use refers to customers’ intention
to continue using prepaid electricity services based
on their prior experience. A structured questionnaire
that was circulated online using Google Forms was
used to collect the data. This approach was chosen
because it is effective at reaching respondents in a
large geographic region and can enable quick data
gathering in a constrained amount of time. A five-point
Likert scale, from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly
agree), was used in the questionnaire's design to
gauge respondents' opinions. This method enables the
conversion of subjective opinions into quantitative data
for statistical analysis (Joshi et al., 2015).
To guarantee the constructs' content validity and
reliability, the measuring items were modified from
previously validated investigations (Hair et al., 2021). The
primary analytical technique employed in this paper
is partial least squares structural equation modeling
(PLS-SEM). PLS-SEM was selected because it does not
require an exact normal data distribution, is suitable
for evaluating complex research models, and performs
well for exploratory and predictive research (Hair et al.,
2021). Assessment of the measurement model (outer
model) and assessment of the structural model (inner
model) were the two primary phases of the data analysis
process, which was carried out using SmartPLS software.
The purpose of the measurement model evaluation is
to evaluate the constructs' validity and reliability. Factor
loadings (> 0.70) and Average Variance Extracted (AVE
> 0.50) were used to assess convergent validity (Hair et
al., 2021). The Fornell-Larcker criterion, cross-loading
analysis, and the Heterotrait-Monotrait Ratio (HTMT), a
reliable technique for evaluating construct uniqueness,
were used to assess discriminant validity (Henseler et
al., 2015). Composite Reliability and Cronbach's Alpha
were used for reliability testing; values higher than 0.70
imply acceptable internal consistency.
The examination of the structural model looks at the
connections between latent variables. The model's
explanatory power is measured by the R-squared
value, and each independent variable's impact size
is evaluated by the F-square test (Hair et al., 2021). The
model's predictive capacity was assessed using the
Q-squared predictive relevance test; scores larger than
zero imply sufficient predictive relevance (Shmueli et
al., 2019). The variance inflation factor (VIF) was used to
evaluate any common method bias. The model is free
of significant multicollinearity problems and common
method bias if the VIF score is less than 3.3 (Kock, 2015).
The bootstrapping method was used for hypothesis
testing in order to provide t-statistics and p-values.
When the p-value is less than the significance level, a
hypothesis is deemed supported (Hair et al., 2021).

Second, the relationship between communication

communication is essential to the service experience,

interaction. Rather than functioning as a supporting
element, communication quality emerges as a primary
determinant of satisfaction by shaping customers’
understanding, expectations, and perceived control
over service usage.

enhances post-consumption evaluation but also plays

enhances satisfaction, supporting the view that
trust functions as a cognitive foundation for positive
service evaluation. Conversely, perceived risk is found
to negatively affect satisfaction, highlighting the

This study examined the role of communication service

a negative relationship between perceived risk and
behavioral intention (Resa & Andjarwati, 2019), the

context of prepaid electricity services. Customers who
are aware of potential financial or operational risks may
become more cautious in managing their electricity
consumption. As prepaid electricity enables users to
monitor and control their usage directly, higher risk
awareness may encourage customers to continue
using the service as a means of reducing uncertainty
regarding electricity expenditure.
From a theoretical perspective, this study extends
the application of the Theory of Reasoned Action
(TRA) within the electricity service sector. The findings
demonstrate that communication service quality plays
an important role in shaping customer perceptions and
behavioral intentions. In addition, this study contributes
empirical evidence to the growing body of literature on
service quality, customer satisfaction, perceived risk,
and usage intention in utility services, an area that has
received relatively limited scholarly attention compared
with banking, e-commerce, and mobile payment
services (Jin & Lim, 2021; Qalati et al., 2021).
From a managerial perspective, the findings suggest
that PT PLN should continue enhancing communication

communication regarding prepaid electricity services.
Clear and timely communication can improve customer
satisfaction and strengthen customers’ confidence
in the service. Furthermore, PT PLN should implement
educational programs and digital communication
initiatives that help customers better understand
prepaid electricity mechanisms and encourage the
continued use of prepaid electricity services.
6. CONCLUSION
This study shows that consumer behavior in prepaid
power services is significantly influenced by the quality
of communication services. The findings confirm that
communication service quality significantly influences
customer trust, perceived risk, and customer satisfaction,
which subsequently affect customers’ intention to
continue using prepaid electricity services. These results
highlight that communication is not merely a supporting
element of service delivery but a strategic factor that
directly and indirectly drives customer behavioral
intention.
From a theoretical standpoint, by incorporating
perceived risk and communication service quality into
the model of consumer behavioral intention, this study
advances the Theory of Reasoned Action (TRA). The
results imply that communication-driven assessments,
such as perceived risk and trust, influence behavioral
intention in service situations in addition to attitudes
and subjective standards. This offers a more thorough
understanding of the ways in which emotive and
cognitive elements combine to influence consumer
choices, especially when it comes to public utility
services.
Practically speaking, the results suggest that PT PLN
(Persero) should give top priority to creating clear and
efficient communication methods, accessibility, and
responsiveness. Strengthening communication quality
can enhance customer trust, improve satisfaction,

This study has a number of drawbacks, despite the fact
that it makes a contribution. The first limitation is that
the study only covers one location (UP3 Sanggau), so

consumer characteristics. Second, as this study only
collects customer impressions at one given instant in
time, the cross-sectional technique may not correctly
reflect changes in behavior over time. Thirdly, there are
a lot of variables that were not considered in the study;
for example, perceived value, service innovation, and
digital literacy are additional potential influences on

that future research broaden the study's scope by
incorporating greater sample sizes and broader
geographic locations. Additionally, longitudinal

consumer attitudes and behavior over time. To provide
a more thorough knowledge of consumer behavior in

studies may also include other factors such as perceived
value, customer experience, or technical preparedness.
