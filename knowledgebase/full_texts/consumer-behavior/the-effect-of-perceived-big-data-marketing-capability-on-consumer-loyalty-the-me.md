This study analyzes the effect of Perceived Big Data Marketing Capability (PBDMC)
on Consumer Loyalty by positioning Personalized Customer Experience, Customer
Trust, and Perceived Value as serial mediating variables and Privacy Concern as a
moderating var iable. The study was conducted in Banten Province, Indonesia,
involving 324 respondents who had interacted with data -driven digital marketing
practices, such as personalized product recommendations, targeted advertising,
search-history-based promotions, and digital services tailored to user preferences.
This research employed a quantitative explanatory design. Data were collected
through a structured questionnaire using a five -point Likert scale and analyzed
using Structural Equation Modeling-Partial Least Squares (SEM-PLS). The findings
show that PBDMC positively influences Personalized Customer Experience and
Consumer Loyalty. Personalized Customer Experience significantly enhances
Customer Trust and Perceived Value, while Customer Trust positively affects
Perceived Value and Consumer Loyalty. Perceived Value is also an important
predictor of Consumer Loyalty. The moderation result indicates that Privacy
Concern weakens the relationship between Personalized Customer Experience and
Customer Trust. This means  that data -driven personalization does not always
generate trust when consumers perceive that their privacy is at risk. This study
contributes theoretically by developing a serial mediation and moderation model
within the data-driven marketing literature. Practically, the findings emphasize the
importance of managing consumer data transparently, securely, ethically, and value-
oriented so that data -based marketing practices can build sustainable Consumer
Loyalty.

retain consumers. Consumer interaction is now recorded through product searches, ad clicks, online
reviews, purchase history, social media engagement, location -based behavior, and responses to
personalized promotions. These digital traces provide firms with the opportunity to transform
consumer data into more relevant marketing actions. In this study, the construct is defined as Perceived
Big Data Marketing Capabil ity (PBDMC), namely consumers' perception that a firm is able to collect,
analyze, and utilize consumer data to deliver relevant, responsive, and personalized marketing

As the digital economy continues to grow, companies increasingly use consumer data to create more
personalized and relevant experiences. Prior research shows that big data analytics and marketing
analytics help firms identify hidden consumer insights, supp ort segmentation, improve targeting
accuracy, and develop more responsive marketing decisions (Erevelles et al., 2016; Wedel & Kannan,
2016). However, the value of data -driven marketing depends on whether consumers perceive such
practices as useful, fair, and trustworthy.
Personalization is a central mechanism in data -driven marketing. Through analysis of search
history, purchase behavior, and preference patterns, firms can design product recommendations,
promotional messages, and service interactions that match consumer ne eds. Personalized Customer
Experience (PCE) is therefore expected to become the first psychological outcome of PBDMC because
consumers directly experience the relevance and convenience generated by data -based marketing
actions.
Privacy concern arises when consumers worry about the collection, storage, sharing, or misuse of
their personal data. A 2024 PwC Asia -Pacific survey reports that data protection is a major element of
consumer trust, while academic studies on information pr ivacy show that privacy concern can shape
trust and behavioral responses in online environments (Malhotra et al., 2004; PwC, 2024). Thus, firms
must balance personalization with transparency, control, and responsible data governance.

can easily move between platforms when they find better promotions, smoother applications, more

2. LITERATURE REVIEW AND HYPOTHESES DEVELOPMENT
2.1. Perceived Big Data Marketing Capability
Perceived Big Data Marketing Capability (PBDMC) refers to consumers' perception that a firm is
capable of collecting, integrating, analyzing, and utilizing consumer data to support accurate,
personalized, and responsive marketing decisions. The construct i s adapted from the broader idea of
perceived big data marketing capability, but it is framed at the consumer -perception level because the
data were collected from consumers rather than from internal organizational records. From the dynamic
capability persp ective, firms that sense consumer needs, seize data -based opportunities, and
reconfigure marketing resources are more likely to create relevant customer experiences (Teece, 2007).
Big data consumer analytics also enables firms to extract hidden insights fr om consumer behavior and
translate them into marketing actions (Erevelles et al., 2016; Wedel & Kannan, 2016).
2.2. Personalized Customer Experience
Personalized Customer Experience refers to consumer experiences shaped by product
recommendations, promotions, communication, and services tailored to individual needs. In data-driven
marketing, personalization emerges when firms use behavioral data to design interactions that are more
relevant to consumer preferences and decision stages. Prior research shows that personalization can
improve advertising effectiveness and customer response when it is relevant, timely, and not perceived
as intrusive (Aguirre et al., 2015; Bleier & Eisenbeiss, 2015). Therefore, PCE becomes an important
bridge between perceived data capability and relational outcomes because consumers evaluate data use
through the quality of experiences they receive.
2.3. Customer Trust
Customer Trust refers to consumers' belief that a firm is honest, reliable, competent, and
responsible in delivering services and managing personal data. In digital and data -driven marketing,
trust becomes critical because consumers evaluate not only servi ce performance but also the risks
associated with information disclosure. Trust reduces perceived uncertainty and supports long -term
relational exchange (Doney & Cannon, 1997; Morgan & Hunt, 1994). In the context of personalization,

2.4. Perceived Value
Perceived Value is consumers' overall evaluation of the benefits received compared with the
sacrifices made, including money, time, effort, risk, and personal data disclosure (Zeithaml, 1988). In
this study, perceived value includes information convenience , relevance of recommendations, time
efficiency, transaction comfort, economic benefits, and emotional satisfaction. The PERVAL perspective
also emphasizes that consumer value is multidimensional, including functional, emotional, social, and
price-related dimensions (Sweeney & Soutar, 2001). Thus, perceived value becomes a central pathway
toward loyalty because consumers are more likely to remain with a brand when the benefits of
interaction exceed the perceived costs and risks.
2.5. Privacy Concern
Privacy Concern refers to consumers' concerns about the collection, storage, analysis, sharing, and
use of their personal data by firms. This variable is important because personalization is not always
perceived positively. Consumers may welcome relevant recommendations while simultaneously feeling
uncomfortable when firms appear to know too much about their digital behavior . Studies on information
privacy show that privacy concern is closely related to perceived vulnerability, control, and trust in
online environments (Malhotra et al., 2004; Tucker, 2014). By positioning Privacy Concern as a
moderator, this study captures the ethical and psychological boundary of personalization.
2.6. Consumer Loyalty
Consumer Loyalty reflects consumers' commitment to continue using, repurchasing,
recommending, and resisting competitors' offers. Loyalty is commonly understood as a staged process
that includes cognitive, affective, conative, and behavioral dimensions (Oliver, 1999). In digital markets,
loyalty is dynamic because consumers can compare alternatives easily and switch platforms quickly.
Therefore, loyalty must be examined not only through satisfaction, but also through personalized
experience, trust, perceive d value, and perceptions of data security (Chaudhuri & Holbrook, 2001;
Morgan & Hunt, 1994).
2.7. Relationships Among Variables and Model Development
Theoretically, PBDMC is expected to influence Personalized Customer Experience because
consumers who perceive a firm as capable of using data effectively are more likely to experience relevant
recommendations, timely promotions, and smoother service intera ctions. PCE may then increase
Customer Trust when personalization is perceived as helpful, transparent, and non-intrusive. Customer
Trust contributes to Perceived Value because trust reduces perceived risk and increases the perceived
net benefits of intera ction. Ultimately, Perceived Value strengthens Consumer Loyalty through
repurchase intention, recommendation, and continued use. Privacy Concern is positioned as a
moderating variable that may weaken the relationship between Personalized Customer Experience and
Customer Trust when consumers perceive higher data vulnerability.
Based on the literature review, this study proposes a series of hypotheses that explore the
relationship between Perceived Big Data Marketing Capability (PBDMC) and Consumer Loyalty through
Personalized Customer Experience (PCE), Customer Trust (CT), and Perceived Value (PV), with Privacy
Concern (PC) as a moderating factor.
Hypothesis 1 (H1): Perceived Big Data Marketing Capability (PBDMC) has a positive effect on
Personalized Customer Experience (PCE). This hypothesis is supported by dynamic capability theory
and marketing analytics literature, which explain that data -based sensing and analytics capabilities

3. METHOD
This study employed a quantitative approach with an explanatory research design to examine the
effect of Perceived Big Data Marketing Capability (PBDMC) on Consumer Loyalty through Personalized
Customer Experience, Customer Trust, and Perceived Value, whil e also testing the moderating role of
Privacy Concern. The quantitative approach was selected because this study aims to measure
relationships among latent variables based on respondents' perceptions. The explanatory design was
considered appropriate because the study does not merely describe the phenomenon, but explains the
mechanisms through which variables are connected within the proposed conceptual model.
The study was conducted in Banten Province, Indonesia. Banten was selected because it represents
a region with dynamic digital economic development, supported by its proximity to national business
centers, increasing use of digital platforms, growing e -commerce adoption, and broader exposure to
data-based marketing practices. The population of this study consisted of consumers in Banten who had
interacted with data-driven digital marketing practices, such as personalized product recommendations,

A total of 324 respondents participated in this study. Respondents were selected using purposive
sampling based on several criteria: (1) residing in Banten Province, (2) being at least 17 years old, (3)
having purchased or interacted with digital platforms, and (4) having received data-based personalized
marketing, such as product recommendations, targeted advertisements, or customized promotional
offers. This technique was used because the study required respondents with direct experience of big
data marketing practices.
Data were collected using a structured questionnaire measured with a five -point Likert scale,
ranging from 1 = strongly disagree to 5 = strongly agree. The instrument was developed based on the
main constructs of the study, namely Perceived Big Data Market ing Capability, Personalized Customer
Experience, Customer Trust, Perceived Value, Privacy Concern, and Consumer Loyalty. The collected data
were analyzed using Structural Equation Modeling -Partial Least Squares (SEM -PLS) because the
proposed model includes multiple latent variables, serial mediation paths, and moderating effects (Hair
et al., 2017). The analysis was conducted in two stages: measurement model assessment and structural
model assessment. The measurement model was evaluated using outer loading , Cronbach's alpha,
composite reliability, average variance extracted, discriminant validity, and common method bias
assessment. The structural model was assessed through path coefficients, t -statistics, p -values, R -
square, Q-square, collinearity, mediation, and moderation testing.
In this study, PBDMC is explicitly measured from the consumer's perspective. Therefore, the
construct does not claim to measure a firm's internal big data infrastructure directly. Instead, it captures
how consumers perceive a firm's ability to use data for  relevant recommendations, personalized
promotions, predictive service responses, and preference -based communication. This definition is
consistent with the operational indicators used in the questionnaire and avoids treating consumer
perceptions as objective organizational capability data.
For this reason, the acronym PBDMC is used consistently throughout the manuscript. The original
term Big Data Marketing Capability is retained only as the theoretical root of the construct, while the
empirical construct is labeled Perceived Big Data Market ing Capability to clarify that the unit of
measurement is consumer perception.
To assess common method bias (CMB), this study used the full collinearity test recommended in
PLS-SEM. All latent constructs were evaluated using full collinearity VIF values, and a VIF below 3.3 was
used as an indication that common method bias was not a serious threat to the model (Kock, 2015). The
CMB results are reported separately in Table 5 so that the assessment is not mixed with convergent
validity or structural collinearity diagnostics.
Before data collection, a pilot test was conducted to ensure the quality of the measurement
instrument and to identify potential issues in the questionnaire. The pilot test was carried out with 30
respondents who had similar characteristics to the target p opulation, to test the reliability and validity
of the instrument. All research procedures adhered to the applicable research ethics guidelines. Ethical
approval was obtained from the university ethics committee, and participant consent was obtained after
they were provided with clear information about the research objectives and their rights as participants.

5. DISCUSSION
The Effect of Perceived Big Data Marketing Capability (PBDMC) on Personalized Customer
Experience (PCE)
The findings show that PBDMC has a significant effect on PCE, with a path coefficient of 0.642 and
a p -value of 0.000. This result is consistent with dynamic capability theory and big data consumer
analytics literature, which explain that the ability to sense, analyze, and use consumer data can improve
the relevance of marketing actions (Teece, 2007; Erevelles et al., 2016; Wedel & Kannan, 2016). Because
this study measures capability from the consumer side, the result indicates that consumers are more
likely to perceive personalization positively when they recognize that firms use data to understand
preferences, predict needs, and respond quickly to changing expectations.
The Effect of Personalized Customer Experience on Customer Trust (CT)
The results of this study indicate that PCE has a significant effect on CT , with a path coefficient of
0.417 and a p -value of 0.000. This finding supports the personalization literature, which shows that
relevant and non -intrusive personalization can stren gthen trust when consumers perceive that data -
based interactions are useful and transparent (Aguirre et al., 2015; Bleier & Eisenbeiss, 2015). However,
personalization does not build trust automatically. Trust grows when consumers interpret
personalization as a service improvement rather than as covert surveillance or manipulative targeting.
The Effect of Customer Trust on Perceived Value (PV)
Customer Trust is proven to have a significant positive effect on Perceived Value, with a path
coefficient of 0.536 and a p -value of 0.000. This result indicates that trust reduces perceived risk and
increases the psychological value of data -driven interac tion. When consumers trust that a firm is
reliable, secure, and honest, they are more likely to evaluate personalized recommendations, digital
services, and promotional offers as beneficial. This finding is consistent with relationship marketing and
buyer-seller trust literature (Doney & Cannon, 1997; Morgan & Hunt, 1994).
The Effect of Perceived Value on Consumer Loyalty (CL)
Perceived Value is proven to have a significant positive effect on Consumer Loyalty, with a path
coefficient of 0.491 and a p -value of 0.000. This finding confirms the logic of perceived value theory,
which explains that consumers compare received benefits with sacrifices such as cost, time, effort, risk,
and personal data disclosure (Zeithaml, 1988; Sweeney & Soutar, 2001). In this context, loyalty is
strengthened when consumers perceive that personalized services save time, increase convenience,
provide relevant offers, and generate benefits that exceed the risks of data use.

The Effect of Perceived Big Data Marketing Capability on Consumer Loyalty
Although PBDMC has a significant direct effect on Consumer Loyalty, its effect is smaller than its
indirect effects through PCE, CT , and PV, with a path coefficient of 0.184 and a p -value of 0.003. This
finding is theoretically important because it shows t hat data capability alone is insufficient to create
strong loyalty. Consumers do not become loyal simply because a firm possesses data or analytics
capability; they become loyal when that capability is transformed into relevant experiences, trust-based
relationships, and value perceptions. The significant serial mediation results support this mechanism:
PBDMC first shapes PCE, PCE strengthens CT and PV, and these relational-value outcomes subsequently
drive loyalty. Thus, the dominant pathway is experientia l and psychological rather than purely
technological.
The Moderating Role of Privacy Concern (PC) in the Relationship Between Personalized
Customer Experience and Customer Trust
The moderation analysis shows that Privacy Concern significantly weakens the relationship
between PCE and CT , with a path coefficient of -0.173 and a p -value of 0.007. This result confirms the
personalization-privacy paradox, where the same personalization  that improves relevance can also
trigger vulnerability when consumers perceive excessive data collection or limited control over personal
information (Aguirre et al., 2015; Malhotra et al., 2004; Cloarec, 2022). For managers, this finding means
that personalization should be supported by privacy notices, consent mechanisms, transparent data-use
explanations, and visible security practices. Without these safeguards, personalization may lose its trust-
building effect.
6. CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS
This study concludes that Perceived Big Data Marketing Capability (PBDMC) plays a crucial role in
enhancing Consumer Loyalty through Personalized Customer Experience, Customer Trust, and
Perceived Value. Based on data from 324 respondents in Banten, the fi ndings indicate that consumers'
perception of a firm's ability to use data not only has a direct effect on loyalty but also works more
strongly through relevant personalized experiences, consumer trust, and perceived value.
This study also confirms that Privacy Concern weakens the relationship between Personalized
Customer Experience and Customer Trust. This means that data-driven personalization does not always
generate trust when consumers feel that their privacy is at risk . Theoretically, this study enriches the
data-driven marketing literature through a serial mediation and moderation model. Practically, firms
need to develop data-based marketing strategies that are personalized, transparent, secure, and ethical
to build sustainable Consumer Loyalty.
However, this study has several limitations that should be considered. First, the study is
geographically limited to Banten, which may not fully represent the consumer population in other
regions of Indonesia or other countries with different cultural and market contexts. Second, the study
design is cross -sectional, meaning that the analysis of relationships between variables cannot fully
capture changes in consumer perception over time. Third, the measurement relies on a single source,
namely respondents' perceptions, although the full collinearity test indicates that common method bias
is not a serious threat. Fourth, PBDMC remains a perceived construct and should not be interpreted as
direct evidence of firms' internal analytics infrastructure.
Future research could expand on these findings by using longitudinal designs to examine changes
in PBDMC, PCE, CT , PV, and Consumer Loyalty over time. Future studies could also combine consumer
survey data with objective firm -level data, such as analytics infrastructure, campaign performance, or
platform usage records, to strengthen construct validity. Geographically, expanding the research to other
regions of Indonesia or countries with different levels of digital penetration could provide deeper
insights into contextual differences. Future research may also examine data protection regulation,
