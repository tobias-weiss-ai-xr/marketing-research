ABSTRACT
The aim of this study is to analyze how Yüz Numaralı Adam anticipates and critically reflects
the mechanisms of persuasion, mediated influence, and consumer behavior that underpin
modern influencer marketing practices, by employing the conceptual frameworks of
influencer marketing and the sales funnel model. The study employs a qualitative thematic
analysis. The findings reveal that the protagonist, Şaban, functions as a “proto-influencer,”
whose trajectory from an ordinary citizen to an advertising icon anticipates the logic of
credibility, authenticity, and parasocial relationships that drive digital influencer culture
today. The application of the sales funnel model illustrates how the film satirizes the
sequential process of consumer persuasion. Moreover, the film critiques the
commodification of selfhood and d ramatizes the fragility of trust. When the defective
products lead to consumer disillusionment, the film models the reversal of the sales funnel
via negative word -of-mouth. Şaban’s eventual ethical resistance and public confession
highlight that moral accountability is the most precarious yet redemptive aspect of the
influence economy. Ultimately, the film illustrates that persuasion is relational and fragile;
when authenticity is commodified and trust is betrayed, ethical reflexivity represents a
critical foundation for credibility and long-term engagement.
Keywords: Influencer marketing, Sales funnel, Proto-influencer, Trust, Parasocial relation.

Introduction
Consumption, especially since the second half of the 20th
century, has become a central element in shaping individual
identity and social relationships. In this process, advertising
emerged as a c ultural production field that not only
promotes products but also constructs lifestyles, value
systems, and desires. In Türkiye, with the expansion of mass
media in the late 1970s and the penetration of capitalist
market logic into cultural representations , media
productions functioned both as carriers and critics of
consumption ( Gedik, 2024, p. 138 ; Kıran, 2019). As Öncü
(1999) argues, television broadcasting, introduced in 1968,
had by the mid -1970s turned advertising into a significant
cultural force shaping ideals of modern living. Turkish
cinema, often referred to as Yeşilçam, responded to these
transformations. While melodramas and comedies
remained dominant, a subgenre of satirical cinema
emerged, led by filmmakers such as Ertem Eğilmez, that
critically engaged with modernization, class inequalities, and
cultural anxieties (Dönmez-Colin, 2008; Suner, 2010, p. 17).
Influencers are individuals who are perceived as credible in
specific areas, are followed by an audience, influence the
behavior of that audience, and share similar characteristics
with consumers (Martínez -López et al., 2020 , p. 1807 ).
Influencer marketing is considered an extension of word-of-
mouth marketing, in which individuals with influence over a
specific target audience are identifie d and engaged as part
of marketing campaigns (Sudha & Sheena, 2017, p. 14).
The rise of television and cinema facilitated the use of
celebrities in advertisements, thereby making them
significant tools in shaping consumption through these
channels (Ahmed & Rathore, 2024, p. 19). Today, in a similar
way, influencers who engage with consumers through
digital platforms play a central role in directing
consumption. Accordingly, studies on influencer marketing
have largely focused on influencers operating withi n digital
channels ( e.g., Abidin, 2016; Ki et al., 2020). However,
influencer marketing, which relies on individuals who
influence or change the behavior of others, is not limited to
social media alone. Indeed, consumers tend to perceive the
opinions of ot her consumers as more trustworthy and
valuable (De Veirman et al., 2017 , p. 800 ). From this
perspective, Yüz Numaralı Adam (Seden, 1978) satirizes how
consumer behavior was shaped through celebrity figures in
the pre -social media era, offering an early ref lection of
strategies that are today recognized as influencer
marketing.
The present research situates the film within the
interdisciplinary terrain of marketing communication and
media theory, thereby foregrounding its capacity to both
reflect and problem atize the mechanisms of modern
advertising. By employing thematic analysis, the study
demonstrates how the protagonist Şaban operates as a
proto-influencer figure whose trajectory from ordinary
citizen to advertising icon embodies the logics of credibility,
authenticity, and social proof later crystallized in digital
influencer culture ( Djafarova & Trofimenko, 2019, p. 8 ;
Freberg et al., 2011 , p.  92). In bridging the historical
conditions of Turkish advertising in the 1960s and 1970s
(Aslaner & Aslaner, 2020, pp. 23-24; Yavuz, 2006, pp. 160-
161) with the dynamics of twenty -first-century influencer
marketing, the study extends the genealogy of persuasion
strategies beyond the digital era and suggests that Yüz
Numaralı Adam  can be read as a culturally signi ficant
critique of consumer society, with insights that remain
relevant to contemporary debates.
Influencer Marketing
Influencer marketing, broadly defined as a form of
marketing that relies on individuals with perceived social
capital to promote products or services, has emerged as a
dominant force in the contemporary digital economy. Unlike
traditional celebrity endorsements, influencer marketing
operates primarily through social media platforms and
draws legitimacy from perceived authenticity, intimacy, and
peer-like trust (Abidin, 2016 , p. 5; Audrezet et al., 2020 , p.
565). These features allow influencers to act as powerful
mediators between brands and consumers, especially in
contexts where conventional advertising is increasingly met
with skepticism. Influencers function as both content
creators and laborers in a market driven by visibility,
engagement metrics, and algorithmic amplification. Their
role blurs the boundary between professional and personal
life, as they curate identities that must remain both relatable
and aspirational (Duffy, 2016, p. 7). This double bind raises
critical questions around authenticity, commodification of
the self, and the affective labor demanded by platform
economies.
The use of celebrities to promote products or brands dates
back to the second half of the 19th century (Ahmed &
Rathore, 2024, p. 19 ). However, research has shown that
influencers tend to have a greater level of influence and
credibility among consumers compared to traditional
celebrities (Schouten et al., 2 021, p. 208 ). This influence is
said to originate from opinion leadership and parasocial
relationships (Ahmed & Rathore, 2024, p. 19 ). Consumers
often place high value on the opinions of others, leading
them to perceive messages conveyed by fellow consumer s
as more authentic and trustworthy, where these parasocial
relationships—one-sided bonds where followers feel a
sense of intimacy and familiarity with influencers —make

consumers more receptive to the influencers’ opinions and
behaviors (De Veirman et al.,  2017, p. 801 ). Influencers, in
particular, are viewed as personal, credible, and sincere
sources of information ( Kaur & Siddhey, 2024, p. 5 ;
Martínez-López et al., 2020, p. 1807). Their ability to embed
brand messages into everyday life scenarios, such as
morning routines, travel vlogs, or fitness challenges,
enhances message reception and engagement (Campbell &
Rapp Farrell, 2020 , p.  8). However, the very mechanisms
that make in fluencer marketing effective also render it
ethically and socially ambivalent. The veneer of authenticity
can be carefully manufactured, leading to what Kilgour et al.
(2015) call strategic authenticity, whereby influencers
present curated vulnerability to  maintain trust while
advancing commercial objectives.
The Sales Funnel
The sales funnel, often conceptualized through the AIDA
model, remains one of the foundational frameworks for
understanding consumer decision -making in marketing
scholarship. Originall y developed in the late 19th century
(Strong, 1925), the model illustrates how potential
consumers move sequentially from becoming aware of a
product, to developing interest, forming a desire, and
ultimately taking action in the form of purchase. Despite its
simplicity, the sales funnel has proven remarkably adaptable
across different media landscapes and continues to inform
both academic research and managerial practice (Kotler &
Keller, 2016). Influencer marketing occupies a particularly
strategic role wi thin this process. By leveraging parasocial
relationships and perceived authenticity, influencers are
highly effective at the attention and interest stages, where
visibility and trust are critical (Labrecque, 2014). At the
desire stage, influencers often n arrativize consumption by
embedding products into aspirational lifestyles, thereby
framing them as symbols of identity, taste, or cultural capital
(Bourdieu, 1984). Finally, at the action stage, influencers can
directly mobilize their audience toward purch ase through
affiliate links, discount codes, and platform -specific
shopping integrations, effectively collapsing the distance
between promotion and transaction (Campbell & Rapp
Farrell, 2020, p. 5).
Beyond influencer marketing, empirical research has
demonstrated that the AIDA framework provides a robust
tool for evaluating the effectiveness of diverse advertising
strategies. Wenger (2025) emphasizes that the funnel
structure continues to anchor modern lead management by
aligning marketing activities with measurable outcomes
across customer touchpoints. Similarly, Banerjee’s (2022)
review of thirty studies shows that AIDA has been
successfully applied to contexts as varied as sports
marketing, televisi on campaigns, social media advertising,
and website usability. Even in media-related environments,
where consumer journeys are non -linear and mediated by
algorithms, AIDA continues to provide a valuable heuristic
for structuring campaigns and measuring impact.
Nevertheless, scholars have noted that the linearity of the
AIDA model requires refinement in order to reflect
contemporary consumption patterns. Modifications such as
AIDAR (adding Retention) and AIDAS (adding Satisfaction)
extend the framework to ac count for post -purchase
engagement, customer loyalty, and long -term value
creation (Banerjee, 2022). In practice, influencer marketing
again exemplifies this evolution: followers not only purchase
but also co -produce brand narratives through comments,
shares, and user -generated content, thereby blurring the
traditional boundaries of the funnel. Taken together, the
persistence of the AIDA model in both classical advertising
research and contemporary digital strategies suggests that,
while its stages may need  reinterpretation, it remains an
indispensable analytical lens for understanding the interplay
between marketing communication and consumer action.
The enduring relevance of the sales funnel lies in its ability
to provide a structured understanding of cons umer
persuasion, while its contemporary adaptations highlight
the increasingly participatory, social, and algorithmically
mediated nature of consumption.
Alongside these marketing -oriented frameworks,
psychoanalytic approaches to visual culture (Rose, 2016 )
offer a third lens, explaining how mediated figures become
objects of audience identification and desire (Freud, 2019;
Lacan, 2006). Where influencer marketing theory accounts
for the perceived credibility of a mediated figure, and the
sales funnel accou nts for how that credibility is converted
into consumer behavior, psychoanalytic theory addresses a
prior question: why audiences form attachments to such
figures in the first place.
Aim and Methodology
Rather than presenting these perspectives as separat e but
equally weighted frameworks, this study organizes them
into a hierarchical explanatory structure. Psychoanalytic
theory (Freud, 2019; Lacan, 2006) provides the foundational
account of audience identification by explaining how and
why a mediated figur e comes to be invested with trust,
desire, and emotional significance. Building on this
foundation, theories of authenticity and parasocial
relationships help explain how these psychological
investments are experienced and maintained as enduring
relational bonds between audiences and mediated figures.
Finally, the AIDA model illustrates how these relationships

are translated into commercial outcomes by tracing the
progression from initial attention to consumer action.
Viewed in this way, the three perspecti ves are interrelated
rather than independent. Psychoanalytic theory explains the
conditions under which authenticity and social proof
become persuasive, while authenticity and parasociality
describe the relational processes through which persuasion
is sustained. The AIDA framework then captures how these
established relationships are ultimately mobilized to shape
consumer behavior. Together, these perspectives offer a
coherent account of influencer persuasion, linking its
psychological foundations to its re lational dynamics and,
ultimately, its commercial consequences.  The inclusion of
psychoanalytic interpretation alongside these marketing
frameworks is warranted by the nature of the object of
study. As a work of narrative cinema, Yüz Numaralı Adam
invites analysis through frameworks long established within
film studies for theorizing spectatorship, identification, and
desire (e.g., Metz, 1981; Mulvey, 2013), a dimension of
audience response that marketing frameworks alone do not
theorize. This lens is there fore applied not as an ad hoc
addition but as a discipline-appropriate complement to the
marketing-oriented frameworks guiding the study’s primary
research questions.
The central aim of this research is to examine Yüz Numaralı
Adam through the dual frameworks of influencer marketing
and the sales funnel model, with particular attention to how
the film anticipates, satirizes, and problematizes strategies
of persuasion, consumer behavior, and mediated influence.
While American markets had already entered a p hase of
product abundance in the 1920s, Turkey experienced a
delayed version of this transition in the 1960s (Yavuz, 2006,
p. 160 ). In this sales -oriented environment, companies
prioritized marketing and promotional strategies to ensure
that production was  matched by consumption, often
neglecting consumer needs and relying on misleading tactics
(Alabay, 2010, p p. 215-216; Helmold, 2022 , p.  38; Özden,
2018, p. 13 ). The 1960s marked the institutionalization of
advertising in Turkey, while the 1970s witnessed the rise of
television commercials, which professionalized the sector
and expanded its cultural presence (Aslaner & Aslaner,
2020, p. 24 ; Karşu Cesur, 2022 , p.  322). It is within this
context that Yüz Numaralı Adam satirizes the ways in which
advertising manipulates audiences and reconfigures
consumer behavior through mediated figures, offering an
early critique of strategies later crystallized in influencer
marketing.
While studies of influencer marketing have largely
concentrated on the digital era ( e.g., Abidin, 2016; Ki et al.,
2020), Yüz Numaralı Adam  demonstrates that the cultural
logic underpinning influencer persuasion was already
operative in earlier media environments. Retrospective
application of contemporary frameworks thus enables a
dual contribution: to film studies, by positioning the film as
a culturally significant critiq ue of consumer society, and to
marketing research, by extending the genealogy of
persuasion strategies beyond digital platforms.
Emerging as a significant approach in the social sciences in
the late 20th century, qualitative research aims to uncover
the underlying causes of social reality and human behavior
(Gürbüz & Şahin, 2016, p. 112), and within this framework,
films are regarded as a form of visual document (Merriam,
2018, pp. 136-137; Rose, 2016). In the marketing literature,
the use of film as a sou rce of data is neither new nor
uncommon. For example, Giroux (2000) explores the politics
of consumerism through David Fincher’s Fight Club (1999),
while Varul (2018) interprets The Lego Movie (2014) as a
modern consumer -capitalist myth that channels viewe rs’
frustrations and expectations under late capitalism into a
narrative that is both reassuring and readily marketable.
Similarly, Başaran (2025) examines consumerism and class
dynamics through the lens of The Menu (2022). In this
tradition, the present study examines Yüz Numaralı Adam, a
unique cultural artifact in Turkish cinema that explicitly
addresses advertising and audience manipulation, making it
particularly relevant for discussions on influencer marketing
and sales funnels.
For this purpose, them atic analysis was employed as the
research method. Thematic analysis is a flexible qualitative
approach that enables researchers to systematically
identify, analyze, and interpret patterns within a data set
(Boyatzis, 1998; Braun & Clarke, 2006, pp. 77-78). It allows
both explicit and implicit meanings to be uncovered,
offering a detailed and rich understanding of complex
phenomena (Clarke & Braun, 2014, p. 1948). By coding and
categorizing the narrative, characters, and sales strategies
depicted in Yüz Numaralı Adam, the method provides an in-
depth interpretation of how advertising practices and
consumer manipulation are represented in the film.
Therefore, the research questions are as follows:
RQ1: How does the portrayal of Şaban as the face of
advertising in the film illustrate his transformation from an
ordinary person into an influencer, and in what ways does
this process resemble contemporary influencer marketing
strategies?
RQ2: How does the film depict the role of a constructed
celebrity in shaping  consumer behavior, and in what ways
can the sales funnel (AIDA model) be traced in the film’s
representation of consumer persuasion from attention to
action?

RQ3: What insights does the film offer about the relationship
between media visibility, authentic ity, and consumer trust,
and how are these dynamics relevant from historical roots
to contemporary discussions on influencer marketing
approaches?
The analysis focused on individual scenes and dialogue
exchanges in which advertising, persuasion, or consume r
responses were either directly portrayed or explicitly
discussed. To become thoroughly familiar with the material,
each author watched the entire film several times either
separately or altogether before beginning the coding
process. Initial coding combined deductive and inductive
approaches. A preliminary set of codes was informed by the
existing literature on influencer marketing and the AIDA
model (e.g., credibility, social proof, parasocial relationships,
and the stages of attention, interest, desire, and action),
while additional codes were developed as new patterns
emerged during analysis (e.g., loss of trust and ethical
resistance).
Codes were assigned to transcribed dialogue an d detailed
scene descriptions using manual coding in a shared
document. The coded data were then examined following
Braun and Clarke’s (2006) six-phase framework for thematic
analysis. Related codes were gradually organized into
broader themes, which were repeatedly reviewed and
refined by comparing them with both the coded extracts
and the dataset as a whole.
To strengthen the credibility of the analysis, each author
independently coded the material during the initial stage
before meeting to discuss and co mpare their
interpretations. Any differences in coding were resolved
through discussion and, when needed, by revisiting the
relevant scenes until a shared interpretation was reached.
This collaborative and iterative process reflects Braun and
Clarke's (2019) understanding of reflexive thematic analysis,
where analytical rigor is established through critical dialogue
and reflexive engagement with the data rather than through
statistical measures of inter-rater agreement
Results
Thematic analysis of the film yielded four main themes, each
addressing one or more of the study’s research questions.
Theme 1, “Constructing the Proto -Influencer: Authenticity
and Reliability,” addresses RQ1 by tracing Şaban’s
transformation into a truste d, relatable figure. Theme 2,
“The Commodification of Popular Persona,” and Theme 3,
“The Erosion of Trust,” address RQ2 by mapping this
transformation onto the sales funnel and its reversal. Theme
4, “Resistance and Vulnerability of the Influencer Figure, ”
addresses RQ3 by examining the ethical dimensions of
mediated persuasion. These themes are presented
sequentially below.
The analysis is guided by two theoretical frameworks: 1) the
influencer marketing approach, and 2) the sales funnel
model. Influencer marketing approach led us to analyze the
movie concepts such as credibility, attractiveness, and
relatability of characters are evaluated to determine how
they could function as potential influencers in shaping
audience attitudes and behaviors (Brown & Hayes, 2008, p.
xiii; Freberg et al., 2011, p. 90); where the sales funnel model
is applied to assess how narrative elements and character
interactions could hypothetically guide an audience along a
sales funnel, leading to engagement or conversion in a
marketing context (Kotler & Keller, 2016). By reading Yüz
Numaralı Adam through this lens, the analysis explores how
Şaban functions as a cinematic representation of the
consumer subject and, more specifically, as a proto -
influencer figure. His transformation from a modest
everyman into an advertising icon illustrates both the
seductions a nd the instabilities of consumerist identity.
Therefore, Şaban is first identified as a proto -influencer
through a psychoanalytic reading, using Rose’s (2016)
approach, while highlighting the necessity of his figure ,
consistent with the definition outlined  above, in which
proto-influencer designates a pre-digital figure enacting the
relational logics later formalized in platform culture.
Theme 1: Constructing the Proto-Influencer: Authenticity
and Reliability
Representatives from the three holding companies  visited
the advertising agency to express their dissatisfaction with
the advertisements. In response, the agency manager, Can,
explained that they had offered all the celebrities available
and were unsure of what more could be done. The
representative, however, undermined the credibility of the
celebrities by stating:
“They are (consumers) aware that these millionaires dine on
lobster and caviar nightly, sipping champagne... It is simply not
credible for them to stand before our consumers and endorse
our products. You are creating a false narrative with these so-
called 'heroes,' lulling the public into complacency. ” (Yüz
Numaralı Adam, 09:58).
This criticism points to the limits of endorsement credibility.
When the film shows consumers doubting celebrities
because of their perceived distance from everyday life, it
anticipates what modern marketing research calls the
“authenticity problem” in influencer endorsements. Studies
consistently show that the effectiveness of endorsements is
contingent upon consumers’ perception of the influencer’s
trustworthiness, similarity, and relatability (Djafarova &
Trofimenko, 2019 , p.  5). In other words, the less the

endorser appears to share the consumer’s lived reality, the
weaker the persuasive effect. Ayşe (Oya Aydoğan) responds
to this criticism by suggesting:
“Bravo! We need to present a hero who emerges from among
the people — perhaps a minibus driver, a lathe operator, or a
street vendor. We will introduce this individual to the public
and help them grow to admire him . The people will see
themselves reflected in this ordinary citizen.” (Yüz Numaralı
Adam, 10:51).
This is echoed in source credibility theory (Hovland & Weiss,
1951), where expertise and trustworthiness are central
dimensions of persuasion. In today’s digi tal culture,
audiences are acutely aware that influencers and celebrities
are compensated for their promotions, leading to skepticism
about authenticity (Boerman et al., 2017 , p.  85). The film
illustrates this perfectly: consumers recognize the gap
between the lavish lifestyles of celebrities and the products
they promote, which reduces credibility and even provokes
resistance.  In influencer marketing literature, “authenticity”
is not merely about appearing “real,” but is also linked to
being consistent, aligned with values, and sincere. Hasan et
al. (2024) approach influencer authenticity as a multi -
component structure: consistency, passion expression, and
disclosure are components of this structure. Additionally,
Liao and Chen (2024) examine how influencers shape digital
engagement through their information sharing (how open
they are) and passion expression (connections to their
personal contexts and values) strategies. We see this conflict
depicted in the film: the tension between using a high -
budget celebrity and creating a “one of us” image reflects a
broader debate within the literature. Therefore, in the film,
Şaban, who becomes a folk hero, is portrayed as a figure
closely identified with the people. This identity is reinforced
through descriptions su ch as “a typical folk house” and “a
typical folk child.” ( Yüz Numaralı Adam , 28:58). Therefore,
Şaban becomes a credible and sincere source of information
just like the modern influencers (Kaur & Siddhey, 2024, p. 5;
Martínez-López et al., 2020, p. 1807).
On the other hand, Şaban’s confused claim that “advertising
is divided into three categories” reveals a lack of advertising
literacy, the capacity to recognize and critically evaluate
persuasive messages (Malmelin, 2010, p.  130; Sweeney et
al., 2022, p. 56), that leaves him unable to analyze, let alone
resist, the system exploiting him.
The story of Şaban, an “ordinary man” who becomes the
face of countless commercials, enables the film to question
how subjectivity, desire, and social identity are reshaped
under the influence of mass media. To analyze these
dynamics, we adopt psychoanalytic approaches to visual
culture (Rose, 2016, p. 147), drawing on Freud (2019), Lacan
(2006), and their interpreters. This perspective emphasizes
how images engage unconscious processes, mobilize desire,
and constitute subject positions through fantasy and
identification. Reading Şaban through this lens, we can see
him as a proto-influencer—one who embodies many of the
affective and psychic function s later performed by
influencers, even if the context of pre-digital film does not
literally involve social media or algorithmic feedback loops.
Although Yüz Numaralı Adam  belongs to the 1970s, its
psychoanalytic logic resonates strongly with the digital
economies of visibility that define the 21st century. Today’s
influencers on platforms such as Instagram, YouTube, or
TikTok operate through dynamics strikingly similar to
Şaban’s cinematic transformation.
Şaban’s bodily humor and unmediated w ishes initially align
him with what Freud (2019) terms the id, yet the film's
elevation of his ordinariness into something admired and
emulated moves his figure into the register of the ego-ideal:
audiences invest in him not despite his imperfections but
because of them, projecting onto him qualities they wish for
themselves.
Şaban functions for the audience as a mirror to their own

Theme 2: The Commodification of Popular Persona:
Multi-Touch Sales Funnel and Consumer Manipulation
Building on the psychoanalytic mechanism of identification
established above, this section examines how that bond is
commercially instrumentalized. A recurrent motif in Yüz
Numaralı Adam is the commodification of Şaban’s identity,
reflected in his gradual transformation from an ordinary
individual into a branded persona. Once he attains public
recognition, advertising executives systematically reshape
his image into a marketable commodity: h is gestures,
speech patterns, and clumsy mannerisms are appropriated
to serve corporate branding strategies. This process
illustrates what Hearn and Schoenhoff (2016), and Duffy and
Pooley (2019) describe as the commercialization of selfhood
in influencer economies, where personal identity becomes a
strategic asset to be packaged and monetized. Şaban thus
loses agency over his own representation, functioning
instead as symbolic capital within the advertising system. His
commodified persona operates as a “middle-of-the-funnel”
mechanism that sustains consumer engagement through
repetitive exposure to a familiar and seemingly authentic
figure. This dynamic gains further depth when examined
through the logic of persuasion underlying influencer
marketing. As Kot ler and Keller (2016) note in the AIDA
model, Attention and Interest are captured most effectively
through affective, relatable communication. Influencers,
often perceived as authentic and trustworthy ( Kaur &
Siddhey, 2024, p. 5 ; Martínez-López et al., 202 0, p. 1807 ),
achieve persuasion by embedding promotional content
within personal narratives. In the film, Şaban’s line “It’s
delicious, better than the stuffed grape leaves my mom
makes at home. Don’t bother your old mom, just open a can
of it” epitomizes this mechanism. By referencing an intimate
experience, Şaban constructs a sense of sincerity that
fosters subconscious trust in the viewer (Yakın, 2025 , p.
140). The film thus anticipates contemporary influencer
marketing’s reliance on personal branding as  both a
persuasive strategy and a mode of exploitation. Through
Şaban’s loss of narrative control, Yüz Numaralı Adam
exposes how emotional authenticity can be commodified,
turning personal relatability into a vehicle for capitalist
consumption.
In the film, Şaban’s promotional lines for various products —
“You'll stand out with Seymen” (ready -to-wear), “Üfler
nourishes, Üfler strengthens” (biscuit), “Long, wide, one
piece, no joints” (pipe), “Delicious, just like my mother used
to make” (canned food), “Don’ t say you have a refrigerator
unless you choose this lion Ö.İ.G.” (refrigerator), and “ Yüz
Numaralı Adam, sultan of hearts, Altın Mehtap Kumaşları”
(fabric), reveal the multi -pronged functioning of the sales
funnel. Each product corresponds to a different sector and
addresses a distinct category of consumer need, yet the
common strategy across all is to build consumer trust by
leveraging Şaban’s image as a folk hero. This approach
parallels what is defined in marketing literature as a multi -
touch funnel, wherein consumers are repeatedly engaged at
multiple points through a consistent figure (Court et al.,
2009). In this case, the attention stage of the funnel is
triggered simultaneously across several products, rather
than a single brand. Şaban thus function s as a persistent
bridge between consumer and brand, a reliable reference
point who anchors the message across categories. Following

Crucially, the film illustrates the sales funnel’s manipulative
and affective capacity. Audiences do not merely laugh at
Şaban; rather, they are drawn into a system where humor
and familiarity cultivate parasocial relationships that are
strategically exploited to foster consumer desire. Research
on influencer marketing highlights that parasocial
interaction, the sense of intimacy and trust developed
toward a mediated persona, functions as a persuasive
mechanism that lowers resistance to advertising messages
(Kim & Song, 2016, p. 573; Labrecque, 2014, p. 135). In this
sense, Şaban’s transformation into a “folk hero” exemplifies
how brands can convert attention into effect, and affect into
purchase intent, by leveraging perceived authenticity and
social proximity (Audrezet et al., 2020). The company in the
film capitalizes on this dynamic, aligning Şaban’s cultural
visibility with product promotion and thereby collapsing the
boundary between entertainment and commerce. Şaban’s
media presence becomes the connective tissue of the
funnel, moving consumers from attention to action through
the emotional affordances of celebrity culture. In this way,
Yüz Numaralı Adam  can be read as a cinematic allegory of
contemporary marketing strategies, foreshadowing today’s
integrated campaigns in which influencer personas operate
as full -funnel mediators between consumer attention,
desire, and conversion.

film thus offers a critical perspective on how the sales funnel
operates across multiple channels, highlighting its capacity
to shape consumer perception simultaneously across
different product categories.
Theme 3: The Erosion of Trust
The products endorsed by Şaban in the film fail to meet the
quality standards promised in the advertisements. For
instance, the canned food has a foul odor, the fabric tears
easily, and the refrigerator is defective. These shortcomings
create a sense of deception among consumers. In one
scene, while sitting in a café, Şaban is confronted by several
women who express their disappointment. They voice their
frustration at having their admiration and trust in him
betrayed, revealing the emotional con sequences of false
advertising and the fragility of the consumer -brand
relationship when mediated by a trusted figure:
“We love you very much, but we’re really upset… That canned
food you praised so much, what a filthy thing, it smells awful…
We believed in you once, son, don’t deceive us again.” (Yüz
Numaralı Adam, 52:24).
“What kind of refrigerator is that? It’s falling apart! Didn’t you
feel ashamed while advertising it? ” (Yüz Numaralı Adam ,
53:45).
“That’s no fabric — it’s rough like straw.” (Yüz Numaralı Adam,
54:11).
The above statements highlight the most critical issue in
influencer marketing: the erosion of trust. Lou and Yuan
(2019) indicate that consumer trust is a fundamental
determinant in influence r strategies, and loss of trust can
damage not only a single product but also all brand
collaborations associated with the influencer. Moreover,
this scene also represents the reverse operation of the sales
funnel. Initially, the persuasion process follows  the AIDA
model; however, due to the product’s poor quality, this
process reverses. The consumer, having experienced
dissatisfaction, shares their negative experience within their
social circle, thereby damaging the influencer’s image. This
phenomenon is known in the literature as negative word-of-
mouth (Ladhari, 2007 , p. 1093). When the influencer’s
credibility is undermined in this process, not only the
individual product but all brands they represent suffer
reputational damage. Therefore, the film illustrates how an
influencer’s loss of trust can lead to a cumulative effect: a
scandal over canned food, a broken refrigerator, or torn
fabric can completely dismantle Şaban’s heroic image. This
serves as a significant critique, demonstrating that the
continuity of the sales funnel can be secured not only
through advertising rhetoric but also by the actual
performance of the advertised product.
This reversal also illustrates a dynamic recognized in crisis
communication research; the same relational intimacy that
makes an endorser persuasive also accelerates the spread
and intensity of backlash once trust is broken, since
audiences experience the failure as a personal betrayal
rather than a neutral product complaint (Coombs, 2007, pp.
164-168). The film thus d ramatizes not only the erosion of
trust in the abstract, but the mechanism by which parasocial
closeness converts a product failure into a reputational crisis
for the endorser specifically, rather than the brand alone.
Theme 4: Resistance and Vulnerability of the Influencer
Figure
Despite his rapid rise, Şaban also embodies the inherent
fragility of the influencer role. His lack of awareness of the
corporate machinery exploiting him highlights his
vulnerability. Unlike modern influencers, who actively
manage their image and negotiate brand deals, Şaban is
completely at the mercy of executives who control his value

Communicata
and visibility. This asymmetry exemplifies what scholars
have described as the precarious labor of the influencer
economy (Duffy, 2016, p. 13). While influencers may appear
to enjoy autonomy, they remain structurally dependent on
platforms, algorithms, and brand sponsorships.
After confronting the consumers, Şaban’s lack of
information prompts him to reflect and question his actions.
As a result, d espite receiving high royalties, he refuses to
endorse the failing construction advertisement. Moreover,
he goes further by rejecting the advertising agency
manager’s final and significantly high offer —an offer that,
due to various contracts, could have ev en cost him his
home—dismissively calling it “eşoğlu eşek” (literally “son of
a donkey”). This aligns with Foucault’s (2019) approach that
resistance can emerge even within the strictest systems of
power. Under these rigid circumstances, his resistance and
press conference depict the most vulnerable point of
influencer marketing: ethical responsibility. This resistance
represents both a personal moral reckoning and an ethical
rebellion against the marketing system. Şaban:
“My dear audience, I’m just an ordinary citizen who has come
this far thanks to your love. I have neither an education nor any
special talent. One day, these bandits dressed me up, polished
me, and put me in front of you. Why? To rip you off. First, I
became famous. They told me, ‘The more the people love you,
the more they’ll believe you.’ They said, ‘People are like lambs.’
By the time I opened my eyes, the scam was already done.”
(Yüz Numaralı Adam, 1:17:24).
Şaban’s refusal of the final offer despite high financial cost
reframes the  ethical question the film raises: whether
accountability in mediated persuasion can only emerge
retroactively, after harm has occurred, rather than being
built into the persuasion process itself. This mirrors an
ongoing tension in influencer marketing eth ics between
disclosure-based regulation, which assumes audiences can
protect themselves if endorsements are labeled as paid, and
trust-based models, which recognize that parasocial
intimacy can override critical distance regardless of
disclosure (Wilson et  al., 2024, p.  72). Şaban's belated
resistance suggests the latter: his ethical rupture restores
his own credibility, but does nothing to compensate the
consumers already harmed, underscoring the asymmetry of
power between mediated figures and the audience s who
trust them. These words reveal both Şaban’s own position
and the nature of advertising. Thus, the climax of the film
reveals the fact that the sales funnel functions not only as a
tool of persuasion but also as a bridge of trust between the
consumer and the brand. Research shows that influencers’
failure to uphold ethical responsibilities damages not only
product sales but also the long -term trust in the brand
(Wilson et al., 2024 , p.  72). This confrontation transforms
Şaban into a folk hero once again. Therefore, Şaban’s return
as the beloved of the people demonstrates that an ethical
stance has the power to restore consumer trust.
Read together, these four themes demonstrate that Yüz
Numaralı Adam does not merely anticipate the mechanics
of influencer marketing in isolated moments, but dramatizes
its full arc, from the construction of trust through its
commercial exploitation to its eventual collapse and ethical
reckoning. This arc substantiates th e study’s central claim
that the persuasive power of mediated figures, and its
vulnerability to rupture, is a durable structural feature of
mediated persuasion rather than an artifact of digital
platform culture.
Discussion
This study demonstrates that  Yüz Numaralı Adam can be
productively read through the dual lenses of influencer
marketing theory and the sales -funnel model —leading to
several contributions to both film/media scholarship and
marketing studies. Although studies on influencer marketing
primarily focus on influencers operating on social media
platforms, the film Yüz Numaralı Adam  illustrates how
consumer behavior was influenced by such figures even
before the emergence of social media. Şaban’s portrayal as
a “proto-influencer” represents an early example of today’s
influencers, who are often perceived as more effective and
trustworthy than traditional celebrities (Schouten et al.,
2021, p. 208 ). Reading Şaban as a “proto -influencer”
extends influencer marketing theory beyond digital
platforms. Whereas Bishop (2021) locates proto -influencer
practices within pre -formalized digital culture (e.g., early
bloggers), this study extends the term to pre -digital mass
media, suggesting that the underlying relational logic of
influencer persuasion is medium -independent rather than
platform-specific. Although the film precedes social media,
the processes it depicts —identification, desire, and
fantasy—mirror the psychic mechanisms that drive follower
engagement today.  This continuity suggests that influencer
effects are rooted in visual and affective structures of
subjectivity as much as in algorithmic affordances (Liu &
Zheng, 2024, p. 3). As Rose (2016) and Lacan (2006) argue,
the image functions as a site of both identification and
misrecognition. Şaban’s appeal emerges precisely from this
contradiction: he is ordinary yet idealized, flawed yet
admired. His cinematic trajectory anticipa tes the
influencer’s role as a mirror for the audience’s aspirational
self (Koay & Lim, 2025 , p.  274). The study thus situates
influencer logic within longer histories of visual culture and
mediated selfhood, rather than confining it to algorithmic
economies. Trust in an influencer enhances the
effectiveness of the message they deliver and fosters a

positive attitude toward the promoted product (Kim & Kim,
2021, p. 225). In the film, the trust placed in Şaban results in
a favorable attitude toward the produ cts; however, the
products fail to deliver the expected performance, and once
broken, this trust compromises not only product appeal but
the influencer's entire network of associations. Thus, trust is
openly satirized in the film as the most fragile aspect  of
influencer marketing, and, as Wilson et al. (2024) note,
maintaining moral accountability is now a determinant of
long-term brand trust.
The film’s critique of celebrity endorsement foreshadows
what marketing literature identifies as the “authenticity
problem.” Viewers’ rejection of distant, glamorous figures
parallels contemporary audiences’ distrust of paid
endorsements ( Boerman et al., 2017, p. 86 ; Djafarova &
Trofimenko, 2019, p. 11 ). Ayşe’s insistence on choosing “a
hero from among the people” arti culates a desire for
credibility grounded in shared experience. This resonates
with recent conceptualizations of influencer authenticity as
multidimensional—comprising passion, consistency, and
openness (Hasan et al., 2024, p. 4; Liao & Chen, 2024). In Yüz
Numaralı Adam, authenticity becomes not only a rhetorical
device but a narrative necessity that underwrites Şaban’s
ordinariness and his persuasive power.
Applying the sales funnel model (Kotler & Keller, 2016) to
the film underscores how advertising logic operates
narratively. Poster slogans such as “one of us” correspond
to the attention stage; Şaban’s friendly testimonials evoke
interest and desire; and consumers’ mass purchases
represent action. Yet wh en defective products provoke
outrage, the process reverses—demonstrating what Ladhari
(2007) calls the negative word -of-mouth effect. The film
therefore exposes the funnel’s double nature: a mechanism
of persuasion and a potential circuit of backlash. As Cialdini
(2009) argues, social proof can mobilize collective behavior,
but it also amplifies disillusionment when credibility fails.
The film thus models how multi -touch persuasion can both
create and collapse consumer belief (Ünalmış et al., 2024).

Conclusion and Recommendations
This study contributes to marketing  scholarship by
demonstrating that the persuasive mechanisms of
influencer culture are grounded in enduring psychological
and cultural dynamics rather than contingent on digital
infrastructure. It contributes to film studies by reframing Yüz
Numaralı Adam  as a site where economic, affective, and
ideological dimensions of persuasion intersect. A limitation
of this study is its reliance on a single filmic case and an
interpretive methodology; the transferability of the proto -
influencer concept to other pre -digital media contexts
remains to be tested empirically.
Future research could investigate pre -digital media texts

influencer figures. Empirical audience studies might explore
whether contemporary vi ewers interpret Şaban through a
digital-influencer lens. Further research could examine
ethical refusal —when influencers reject problematic
sponsorships—as a mechanism for rebuilding consumer
trust over time.
