A B S T R A C T
In the era of e-commerce and the increasing digitalization of consumer journeys, online customer
reviews have become a decisive factor influencing potential buyers’ decisions. This study investigates
the effect of online experience sharing on consumers’ purchase intentions across six key dimensions:
emotional valence, quantity, content quality, responsiveness, reviewer reputation, and review recency.
A quantitative research design was employed using data collected from a sample of 320 online
consumers. The dataset was analyzed through multiple linear regression models to assess the relative
impact of each dimension. The findings reveal that all six dimensions exert a positive and statistically
significant influence on consumers’ purchase intentions, with emotional valence and review content
emerging as the most influential factors. These results contribute to the growing body of literature on
online consumer behavior and provide valuable managerial insights for digital marketing practitioners
aiming to optimize electronic word-of-mouth strategies.

Introduction
In the era of digitalization, consumers’ purchasing habits are undergoing a profound transformation. The rise of e-commerce and the
widespread use of the Internet have significantly reshaped the traditional buying journey, which is now structured around online
information searches, product comparisons, and the consultation of other users’ reviews. These experience-based feedbacks, available
on merchant platforms, forums, and social networks, have become a major source of information for consumers. They contribute to
building trust and directly influence purchase intention (Chevalier & Mayzlin, 2006; Lee, Park & Han, 2008; Filieri, 2015). The
phenomenon of electronic word-of-mouth (e-WOM) has thus emerged as a strategic lever for companies operating in highly
competitive digital environments. Unlike traditional commercial communication, online reviews rely on authentic experiences that
are perceived as credible and impartial (Dellarocas, 2003; Hennig-Thurau et al., 2004). According to Matute-Vallejo, Bravo & Pina
(2015), consulting customer reviews represents one of the most decisive stages of the online purchasing process, influencing
perceived quality, trust, and the consumer’s final decision. However, despite the abundance of research on e-WOM, several grey
areas persist regarding the specific dimensions of reviews that may exert a differentiated influence on purchase intention. Some
studies have focused on the emotional valence (positive/negative) of reviews (Chang & Wang, 2014; Chevalier & Mayzlin, 2006),
others on their quantity or perceived credibility (Mudambi & Schuff, 2010), while few have simultaneously integrated variables such
as responsiveness, reviewer reputation, and review recency within the same empirical approach. This theoretical and empirical gap
justifies the relevance of a synthesis aiming to jointly assess the effect of these six dimensions on purchasing decisions. This study
therefore seeks to answer the following question: To what extent does the sharing of online experience-based reviews influence
consumers’ purchase intention?
To address this issue, the article adopts a quantitative approach based on a questionnaire survey administered to 320 online consumers
and an analysis using multiple regressions. The structure of the article is organized as follows: the first section presents the literature
review, mobilizing key theories explaining purchasing behavior and e-WOM; the second describes the research methodology and

Literature Review
The rise of e-commerce has profoundly transformed consumer behavior, giving rise to a new dynamic of influence: electronic word-
of-mouth (e-WOM). This form of digital exchange has become an essential communication channel, allowing consumers to share
their experiences and guide others’ purchasing decisions. Unlike advertising messages, online reviews are perceived as impartial
information based on authentic experiences (Dellarocas, 2003; Hennig-Thurau et al., 2004). Several empirical studies have
demonstrated that the content of reviews, their tone, and the credibility of their authors significantly influence product perception
and purchase intention (Chevalier & Mayzlin, 2006; Filieri, 2015; Lee, Park & Han, 2008). Thus, this literature review focuses on
six key dimensions likely to explain the impact of online reviews on consumers’ purchasing decisions.
Review Valence and Its Effect on Purchase Decisions
The valence of reviews—whether positive or negative—is one of the major determinants of buying behavior. According to Chevalier
and Mayzlin (2006), positive reviews enhance perceived quality and increase the likelihood of purchase, whereas negative reviews
exert a stronger discouraging effect. Chang and Wang (2014) confirm that the emotions conveyed through comments shape
consumers’ cognitive responses. More recently, Qiu et al. (2024), in a meta-analysis covering 69 studies, demonstrated that valence
has t he strongest effect (r = 0.563) among the characteristics of online reviews.
These empirical findings suggest that the emotional valence of reviews positively influences consumers’ purchase intentions.
H1: Review valence positively influences consumers’ purchase intention.
Review Quantity and Perceived Social Proof
The number of reviews is a key indicator of popularity and legitimacy. Liu (2006) shows that the more comments a product receives,
the more it attracts attention and builds buyers’ trust. Similarly, Park and Lee (2008) highlight that a large volume of reviews acts as
social proof, suggesting product reliability. This relationship is explained by consumers’ natural tendency to rely on majority behavior
to6reduce6uncertainty6(Cialdini,2001).
Thus, the literature suggests that a high quantity of reviews—whether positive or simply numerous exerts a favorable effect on
purchase intention.
H2: The quantity of online reviews positively influences consumers’ purchase decisions.
Informational Content of Reviews
The quality of review content is crucial in the persuasion process. Mudambi and Schuff (2010) show that reviews rich in concrete
details (arguments, comparisons, examples) are perceived as more useful and credible. Filieri (2015) adds that the relevance and
accuracy of the message strengthen trust in both the platform and the brand. Furthermore, Matute-Vallejo et al. (2015) demonstrate
that perceived message quality acts as a mediator between source credibility and purchase intention. These findings support the
idea that the more informative a review is, the more it positively influences consumer behavior.
H3: The informational content of online reviews positively influences consumers’ purchase decisions.
Review Responsiveness and Dynamic Activity
Responsiveness understood as the speed and frequency of review publication adds an essential temporal dimension to product
evaluation. According to Balaji, Khong & Chong (2016), consistent activity with frequent and recent reviews creates a perception of
vitality and trust toward the brand. De Angelis et al. (2012) emphasize that frequent interactions foster collective engagement and
enhance6product6credibility.
These studies support the idea that strong responsiveness in reviews nurtures a sense of transparency and increases consumer interest.
H4: Review responsiveness positively influences consumers’ purchase intention.
Reviewer Reputation and Source Credibility
Source credibility is a decisive factor in shaping consumer opinions. Studies by Chang & Wang (2014) and Lee, Park & Han (2008)
reveal that reviews from verified or expert-like profiles have a stronger impact on purchase intention. Additionally, Pentina, Zhang
& Basmanova (2013) note that the digital reputation of the reviewer influences interpersonal trust and perceived reliability. Thus,
empirical literature converges toward the idea that the reviewer’s reputation is a key indicator of credibility and influence.
H5: Reviewer reputation positively influences consumers’ purchase decisions.

Synthesis and Conceptual Model
The literature review highlights six major dimensions of electronic word-of-mouth likely to influence purchase intention. These
dimensions are not independent: valence and content activate deeper cognitive processing, whereas quantity, responsiveness, and
reviewer reputation act as peripheral cues (Petty & Cacioppo, 1986). Recency, for its part, enhances the perceived credibility of the
product. Thus, the conceptual model of this research posits that these six variables exert a positive influence on the dependent variable
consumers’ purchase intention. This model, grounded in theories of planned behavior and persuasion, serves as the framework for
subsequent empirical validation.

Research and Methodology

The target population of this study consists of consumers who have previously made online purchases and consulted customer reviews
prior to making their purchasing decisions. A non-probabilistic convenience sampling method was adopted due to accessibility and
time constraints. Data were collected using an online survey administered via Google Forms. The questionnaire link was distributed
through several digital platforms, including Facebook groups, WhatsApp communities, and LinkedIn networks related to e-commerce
and digital consumption. This approach allowed the researchers to reach a diverse group of respondents with prior online purchasing
experience. A total of 320 valid responses were obtained. The sample is composed of 56% women and 44% men, with a mean age

Data Collection Instrument
Data were collected through a structured questionnaire developed based on previously validated measurement scales in the electronic
word-of-mouth (e-WOM) literature.
The questionnaire consisted of two main sections:
i. The first section captured respondents’ sociodemographic characteristics (age, gender, frequency of online purchases, and
types of products purchased).
ii. The second section measured the six dimensions of online customer reviews as well as purchase intention.
All constructs were measured using multiple items on a five-point Likert scale ranging from “strongly disagree” (1) to “strongly
agree” (5).
The measurement scales were adapted from prior studies to ensure content validity:
i. Review valence (VAL): 4 items adapted from Chevalier & Mayzlin (2006)
ii. Review quantity (QUA): 3 items adapted from Park & Lee (2008)
iii. Review content (CON): 4 items adapted from Filieri (2015)
iv. Review responsiveness (REA): 3 items adapted from Park & Lee (2008)
v. Reviewer reputation (REP): 3 items adapted from Mudambi & Schuff (2010)
vi. Review recency (ACT): 3 items adapted from Zhu & Zhang (2015)
vii. Purchase intention (PI): 4 items adapted from Matute-Vallejo et al. (2015)
A pre-test was conducted with 20 participants to assess the clarity, relevance, and comprehensibility of the questionnaire items. Minor
adjustments were made based on participants’ feedback.
Research Model
The conceptual model, presented in the literature review, is based on six independent variables that are likely to influence the
dependent variable, namely consumers’ online purchase intention.
The multiple regression equation used is as follows:
PI=β0+β1VAL+β2QUA+β3CON+β4REA+β5REP+β6ACT+ε
Where:
i. PI: Purchase intention (dependent variable)
ii. VAL: Review valence (perceived positive/negative emotion)
iii. QUA: Review quantity (total number of available evaluations)
iv. CON: Review content (level of detail and argumentation)
v. REA: Review responsiveness (frequency and speed of postings)
vi. REP: Reviewer reputation (credibility, seniority, “verified buyer” status)
vii. ACT: Review recency (timeliness of the review)
viii. ε: Random error term
Each variable was measured using items validated in the literature. For example:
i. Review valence: items adapted from Chevalier & Mayzlin (2006)
ii. Review content: items from Filieri (2015)
iii. Responsiveness and recency: indicators derived from Park & Lee (2008)
iv. Reviewer reputation: items from Mudambi & Schuff (2010)
v. Purchase intention: measures from Matute-Vallejo et al. (2015)

Data Analysis Method
The collected data were analyzed using SPSS software (version 26). The analysis followed a structured procedure:
i. Descriptive statistics were computed to summarize the sample characteristics and central tendencies of the variables (means
and standard deviations).
ii. Reliability analysis was conducted using Cronbach’s alpha coefficient. All constructs exceeded the recommended threshold
of 0.70, indicating satisfactory internal consistency.

behavior.
Discussion
The findings of this study reveal that all six dimensions of online customer reviews—valence, quantity, content, responsiveness,
reviewer reputation, and recency—have a positive and statistically significant influence on consumers’ purchase intention. These
results provide strong empirical support for the growing importance of electronic word-of-mouth (e-WOM) in shaping consumer
decision-making in digital environments.
Among the examined variables, review valence emerges as the most influential factor. This result is consistent with prior studies
(Chevalier & Mayzlin, 2006; Chang & Wang, 2014), which highlight the dominant role of emotional tone in influencing consumer
perceptions. From a theoretical perspective, this finding can be explained through the Elaboration Likelihood Model, which suggests
that emotionally charged information can strongly influence attitudes, particularly when consumers rely on heuristic processing.
Positive reviews act as persuasive cues that reduce perceived risk and enhance trust, thereby increasing the likelihood of purchase.
Review content is identified as the second most influential factor, reinforcing the importance of informational quality. This finding
aligns with Filieri (2015) and Mudambi & Schuff (2010), who argue that detailed and argument-based reviews enhance perceived
usefulness and credibility. Within the framework of the Theory of Planned Behavior, high-quality content contributes to shaping
favorable attitudes and perceived behavioral control, both of which are key determinants of purchase intention.
In contrast, review quantity and responsiveness exhibit relatively weaker yet still significant effects. This result may be explained
by the fact that while a large number of reviews signals popularity and social proof (Cialdini, 2001), it does not necessarily guarantee
informational depth or credibility. Similarly, responsiveness reflects platform dynamism but may not directly influence the cognitive
evaluation of the product. These findings differ from some previous studies where quantity played a more dominant role (Liu, 2006),
suggesting that consumers in this context may prioritize quality over volume.
Furthermore, reviewer reputation and review recency also show significant positive effects, confirming their role as credibility-
enhancing cues. Reviews from reputable sources are perceived as more trustworthy, while recent reviews reduce uncertainty by
providing up-to-date information. These results are consistent with Zhu & Zhang (2015) and Pentina et al. (2013). However, their
relatively moderate impact suggests that they act more as reinforcing factors rather than primary drivers of decision-making.
An important contribution of this study lies in its contextual specificity. The sample, primarily composed of Moroccan online
consumers, may reflect particular behavioral patterns. In emerging digital markets such as Morocco, where trust in online transactions
is still developing, consumers may rely more heavily on emotional and informational cues (valence and content) than on purely
quantitative indicators. This highlights the importance of considering cultural and contextual factors when interpreting e-WOM
effects.
Despite its contributions, this study presents several limitations. First, the use of a cross-sectional design limits the ability to establish
causal relationships between variables. Second, the reliance on convenience sampling may reduce the generalizability of the
findings. Third, the use of self-reported data raises the possibility of common method bias. Future research could address these
limitations by adopting longitudinal designs, probabilistic sampling methods, or experimental approaches.

Overall, the findings confirm that online reviews constitute a powerful mechanism of influence in digital environments. However,
their effectiveness depends not only on their presence but also on their qualitative and contextual characteristics.
Conclusion
In the digital era, online experience-based reviews have become central to consumers’ decision-making processes. This study has
examined the influence of six key dimensions of online customer reviews valence, quantity, content, responsiveness, reviewer
reputation, and recency on consumers’ purchase intention. The findings confirm that all six dimensions exert a positive and
statistically significant effect, highlighting the multidimensional nature of electronic word -of-mouth (e-WOM). In particular,
emotional valence and review content emerge as the most influential factors, emphasizing the importance of both affective and
cognitive mechanisms in shaping consumer decisions. From a theoretical perspective, this research contributes to the literature by
integrating multiple dimensions of e-WOM within a single empirical model, while mobilizing complementary frameworks such as
the Elaboration Likelihood Model and the Theory of Planned Behavior to better explain consumer behavior in digital contexts. From
a managerial standpoint, the results suggest that companies and e-commerce platforms should prioritize the management of online
reviews by encouraging high-quality, detailed, and positive customer feedback. Enhancing reviewer credibility (e.g., verified users)
and ensuring the visibility of recent reviews can further strengthen consumer trust and increase purchase intention. However, this
study is not without limitations. First, the use of a cross-sectional research design does not allow for causal inference. Second, the
reliance on convenience sampling may limit the generalizability of the findings. Third, the use of self-reported data may introduce
common method bias. Future research could address these limitations by employing longitudinal designs, experimental methods, or
probabilistic sampling techniques. Additionally, future studies could explore the moderating effects of demographic and contextual
variables such as age, culture, or product category. The increasing integration of artificial intelligence and recommendation systems
also offers promising avenues for extending research on e-WOM and consumer decision-making.
