Introduction
The rapid growth of digital technologies has transformed the way individuals communicate, interact,
and make consumption decisions. Among these technologies, social media platforms have become
an integral part of everyday life, influencing personal relatio nships, information sharing,
entertainment, and consumer purchasing behavior. Platforms such as Instagram, Facebook, TikTok,
YouTube, and X (formerly Twitter) enable users to create, share, and consume content continuously,
resulting in unprecedented level s of digital engagement. As of recent global estimates, billions of
individuals actively use social media, making it one of the most influential digital ecosystems shaping
consumer attitudes and market behavior (Kaplan & Haenlein, 2010; Kuss & Griffiths, 2017). Although
social media provides numerous benefits, including enhanced communication, knowledge sharing,
and business opportunities, excessive and uncontrolled use has led to growing concerns regarding
social media addiction (SMA). Social media addicti on is characterized by excessive preoccupation
with social networking platforms, loss of self-control over usage, withdrawal symptoms when access
is restricted, and continued use despite negative psychological, social, or occupational consequences
(Andreassen, 2015). Researchers have increasingly recognized social media addiction as a behavioral
phenomenon that affects emotional regulation, self -esteem, interpersonal relationships, and
decision-making processes. Excessive exposure to online content can crea te psychological
dependence, encouraging users to spend substantial amounts of time engaging with digitally curated
lifestyles and consumer-oriented content (Kuss & Griffiths, 2017; Sun & Zhang, 2021).
The emergence of influencer marketing, personalized advertising, and algorithm -driven content
recommendations has further strengthened the relationship between social media usage and
consumer purchasing behavior. Modern social media platforms continuously expose users to product
endorsements, promotional campaigns, luxury lifestyles, and peer -generated consumption
experiences. These digital interactions reduce the distinction between entertainment and
commercial communication, creating environments where pu rchasing decisions are frequently
stimulated by visual content and social influence rather than actual consumption needs (Djafarova &
Rushworth, 2017; Lou & Yuan, 2019). Consequently, social media has become an important driver of
both planned and unplanne d purchasing behavior. One behavioral outcome receiving increasing
academic attention is compulsive buying behaviour (CBB). Compulsive buying refers to repetitive,
excessive, and uncontrollable purchasing that is primarily driven by psychological and emoti onal
factors rather than genuine product requirements (Duroy et al., 2014). Individuals exhibiting
compulsive buying tendencies often experience temporary emotional relief following purchases,
followed by feelings of regret, financial stress, guilt, or anx iety. Previous studies indicate that
compulsive buying is associated with poor emotional regulation, materialistic values, impulsivity, low
self-esteem, and excessive digital media consumption (Maraz et al., 2016; Islam et al., 2021). With
the rapid growth  of e -commerce and social commerce, compulsive purchasing has become
increasingly accessible because consumers can complete purchases instantly through integrated
shopping features within social media platforms.

The relationship between social media addiction and compulsive buying behaviour has become
particularly relevant in today's digital economy. Individuals who spend extended periods on social
networking platforms encounter continuous streams of product -related information, influencer
recommendations, and peer -generated lifestyle content. Such repeated exposure may increase
purchase intentions while simultaneously encouraging impulsive and compulsive buying behaviors.
Several empirical studies have reported positive associations between excessive social media use and
increased purchasing tendencies, particularly among younger consumers and highly active social
media users (Dittmar, 2005; Islam et al., 2021). However, the psychological mechanisms explaining
this relationship remain insufficiently understood. Among the psychological constructs proposed to
explain consumer responses to social media environments, social comparison has emerged as one of
the most influential. Social Comparison Theory, originally propo sed by Festinger (1954), argues that
individuals naturally evaluate their opinions, abilities, and achievements by comparing themselves
with others. In digital environments, these comparisons occur more frequently because users are
continuously exposed to carefully curated images, achievements, luxury possessions, travel
experiences, and lifestyle portrayals presented by peers, celebrities, and influencers. Unlike
traditional interpersonal interactions, social media often presents idealized and selectively positive
representations that encourage upward comparisons and perceptions of relative inadequacy.
Upward social comparison can significantly influence consumers' emotional states and purchasing
decisions. Individuals who perceive themselves as less successful or less attractive than others
displayed online may experience dissatisfaction with their own lifestyles, thereby increasing their
desire to purchase products that symbolize success, status, or social acceptance. Consequently, social
comparison may function as an important psychological mechanism through which excessive social
media use translates into compulsive buying behaviour. Recent studies have suggested that social
comparison contributes to materialism, body image concerns, reduced self -esteem, and impulsive
purchasing, yet evidence regarding its mediating role between social media addiction and compulsive
buying remains limited (Appel et al., 2016; Verduyn et al., 2020). The increasing commercialization
of social media platforms has intensified these psychological processes. Artificial intelligence -based
recommendation systems, targeted adver tising, personalized content delivery, and influencer
marketing strategies continuously expose users to products aligned with their browsing behavior and
personal interests. These technologies increase consumer engagement while simultaneously creating
highly persuasive purchasing environments. Although businesses benefit from personalized digital
marketing, concerns have emerged regarding the potential impact of such environments on
vulnerable consumers who exhibit addictive social media usage patterns. Und erstanding these
behavioral consequences is therefore essential for balancing commercial innovation with consumer
well-being.
From a theoretical perspective, this study integrates Social Comparison Theory (Festinger, 1954) with
the Stimulus –Organism–Response (S –O–R) framework (Mehrabian & Russell, 1974). Within this
framework, social media addiction represents the external stimul us, social comparison represents
the internal psychological organism, and compulsive buying behaviour represents the behavioral
response. The integration of these theories provides a comprehensive explanation of how digital

environments influence consumer behaviour through psychological evaluation processes. While
previous studies have often examined direct relationships between social media use and purchasing

research gaps remain. First, much of the existing literature has examined impulsive buying rather

social media addiction and compulsive buying independently without adequately exploring the
psychological mechanisms connecting these constructs. Third, relatively few studies have

empirical model. Finally, although Structural Equation Modeling has become a widely a ccepted
analytical technique for examining complex behavioral relationships, limited research has applied
SEM to investigate the mediating role of social comparison in this context.
To address these gaps, the present study investigates the relationship between social media
addiction, social comparison, and compulsive buying behaviour using Structural Equation Modeling.
Specifically, the study examines whether social media addiction directly influences compulsive buying
behaviour and whether social comparison mediates this relationship. By empirically validating the
proposed conceptual framework, the study seeks to contribute to the literature on digital consumer
behaviour, social media psychology, and consumer decision-making.
Literature Review
The rapid expansion of social networking platforms has significantly transformed consumer
behaviour by influencing users’ psychological processes, social interactions, and purchasing
decisions. Social media platforms provide continuous opportunities for se lf-presentation, peer
observation, and social evaluation, which may contribute to excessive online engagement and
problematic consumption patterns. Andreassen (2015) conceptualized online social network site
addiction as a behavioural addiction characterized by excessive preoccupation, compulsive use, and
difficulties in controlling online activities. Further, Andreassen et al. (2017) demonstrated that
addictive social media use is associated with psychological factors such as narcissism and self-esteem,
highlighting those individual differences influence vulnerability toward problematic social media
engagement. Similarly, Kuss and Griffiths (2017) emphasized that social networking site addiction
shares several characteristics with behavioural addictions, in cluding salience, mood modification,
tolerance, withdrawal, and relapse, suggesting its potential influence on users’ emotional and
behavioural outcomes.
Social comparison has emerged as a critical psychological mechanism explaining how social media
usage affects individual perceptions and consumer behaviour. According to Social Comparison
Theory proposed by Festinger (1954), individuals naturally evaluate their abilities, achievements, and
lifestyles by comparing themselves with others. In digital environments, social networking sites
intensify these comparison processes because users are constantly exposed to carefully constructed
and idealized representations of others’ lives. Appel et al. (2016) found that Facebook usage increases
social comparison tendencies, envy, and negative emotional outcomes, demonstrating that online

interactions can influence users’ self -evaluations. Similarly, Verduyn et al. (2020) highlighted that

Recent empirical studies have further established the relationship between social comparison, social
media addiction, and psychological outcomes. Kim et al. (2021) reported that social comparison

The relationship between social media engagement and consumer purchasing behaviour has gained
increasing attention in recent years. Social networking platforms have become powerful marketing
environments where users are exposed to influencer content, peer recommendations,
advertisements, and lifestyle -based consumption patterns. Kaplan and Haenlein (2010) highlighted
that social media enables interactive communication between consumers and brands, fundamentally
changing traditional consumer decision -making processes. In this context, influencer -driven
marketing has become particularly influential. Lou and Yuan (2019) demonstrated that influencer
credibility and message value significantly affect consumer trust and purchase intention, indicating
that online p ersonalities can shape consumer attitudes and buying decisions. Similarly, Islam et al.
(2021) found that social media influencers influence purchase intention through the mediating role
of trust, emphasizing the psychological mechanisms underlying influencer-based consumption.
Compulsive and impulsive buying behaviour has also been linked with psychological factors
associated with digital engagement. Maraz et al. (2016) identified compulsive buying as a problematic
consumer behaviour characterized by excessive purchasing urges, reduced control over buying
decisions, and negative personal consequences. Duroy et al. (2014) further observed that compulsive
buying tendencies among young individuals are associated with emotional regulation difficulties and
psychological vulnerabilitie s. In online environments, social media exposure may amplify these
tendencies by increasing product visibility, social pressure, and comparison -based consumption
motivations.
Recent research has specifically examined the role of social comparison in online compulsive buying
behaviour. Zhou et al. (2020) demonstrated that upward social comparison on social networking sites
increases state anxiety, which subsequently contributes to online compulsive buying behaviour. Their
findings indicate that users who perceive others’ lifestyles as superior may experience emotional
discomfort and attempt to reduce this gap through excessive purchasing. Similarly, Ling et al. (2023)
identified that upward social comparison influences online compulsive buying through sequential
mediation involving envy and materialism. These findings provide evidence that social comparison
acts as an important psychological pathway connecting social media experie nces with compulsive
consumption patterns.

The role of social media addiction in compulsive buying behaviour has been further explored by

Out (FoMO) significantly mediate this relationship. Their findings indicate that excessive social media
users continuously compare themselves with others and experience anxiety related to missing

highlighted the protective role of self -esteem, suggesting that individuals with stronger self -

Maccarrone-Eaglen and Schofield (2023) further contributed to this research area by examining the

Additionally, Djafarova and Bowes (2021) demonstrated that Instagram usage significantly influences

Research Methodology
Research Design
This study adopts a quantitative, explanatory, and cross-sectional research design to investigate the
relationships among Social Media Addiction (SMA), Social Comparison (SC), and Compulsive Buying
Behaviour (CBB). A quantitative approach is appropriate be cause the study aims to empirically test
hypothesized relationships among latent constructs using Structural Equation Modeling (SEM). Cross-
sectional survey research is widely employed in consumer behaviour and information systems
research because it facilitates the collection of standardized data from a large number of respondents
within a relatively short period while enabling statistical validation of theoretical models (Hair et al.,
2022).

Measurement Scale
All questionnaire items are measured using a five-point Likert scale, where:
1 = Strongly Disagree, 2 = Disagree, 3 = Neutral, 4 = Agree, 5 = Strongly Agree
The Likert scale is widely accepted in behavioural research because it enables respondents to express
varying levels of agreement while producing data suitable for multivariate statistical analysis.
Data Analysis Technique
The proposed conceptual model is evaluated using Structural Equation Modeling (SEM). Depending
on the distributional characteristics of the data and research objectives, analysis may be conducted
using SmartPLS 4 (PLS-SEM) or AMOS (CB-SEM).
The analysis proceeds in two stages.
Stage 1: Measurement Model Assessment
The measurement model is evaluated using the following criteria:
Indicator loadings, Cronbach's Alpha, Composite Reliability (CR), Average Variance Extracted (AVE),
Convergent validity, Discriminant validity using the Fornell–Larcker criterion and HTMT ratio

Factor Loading ≥ 0.70, Cronbach's Alpha ≥ 0.70, Composite Reliability ≥ 0.70, AVE ≥ 0.50, HTMT < 0.85
(or < 0.90 depending on context)
Stage 2: Structural Model Assessment
The structural model evaluates the hypothesized relationships using:
Path coefficients (β), t -values, p -values, Bootstrapping (5,000 resamples), Coefficient of

Discussion
The primary objective of this study was to examine the relationship between Social Media Addiction
(SMA) and Compulsive Buying Behaviour (CBB) while investigating the mediating role of Social
Comparison (SC) using Structural Equation Modeling (SEM). The fi ndings provide empirical evidence
supporting the proposed conceptual framework and demonstrate that excessive engagement with
social networking platforms significantly influences consumers' purchasing behaviour both directly
and indirectly through psycholo gical comparison processes. The results revealed that Social Media
Addiction has a significant positive effect on Compulsive Buying Behaviour (H1). This finding suggests

Conclusion
The present study investigated the relationship between Social Media Addiction (SMA) and
Compulsive Buying Behaviour (CBB) while examining the mediating role of Social Comparison (SC)
using Structural Equation Modeling (SEM). The rapid expansion of social networking platforms has
fundamentally transformed consumer behaviour by integrating communication, entertainment, and
commerce into a single digital ecosystem. Although social media offers numerous benefits for
information sharing, social interaction, and  business promotion, excessive engagement has also
generated concerns regarding behavioural addiction and its influence on consumer decision-making.
The findings of this study provide empirical evidence supporting the proposed conceptual framework
and cont ribute to a deeper understanding of the psychological mechanisms linking social media
addiction with compulsive buying behaviour. The empirical findings demonstrate that social media
addiction significantly and positively influences compulsive buying behaviour. Individuals who spend
excessive time on social networking platforms are repeatedly exposed to influencer marketing,
personalized advertisements, peer recommendations, and aspirational lifestyle content. Such
continuous exposure increases consumers' p urchasing intentions and encourages excessive buying
beyond actual consumption needs. The findings indicate that addictive engagement with social media
extends beyond communication behaviour and substantially influences purchasing decisions within
digital environments. The study further confirms that social media addiction significantly increases
social comparison. Users who frequently interact with social networking platforms tend to compare
themselves with peers, celebrities, and influencers who present carefully curated representations of
success, luxury, beauty, and achievement. These comparisons often create dissatisfaction with one's
own lifestyle, possessions, and social status. Consistent with Social Comparison Theory, the findings
indicate that digital environments intensify upward comparison processes by continuously exposing
individuals to idealized online content. In addition, the results demonstrate that social comparison
positively influences compulsive buying behaviour. Individuals experiencing  upward comparison
frequently attempt to reduce perceived social differences through consumption. Purchasing
fashionable products, branded goods, luxury items, or technologically advanced products becomes a
symbolic means of improving self -image and achiev ing perceived social acceptance. Consequently,
