Keywords: Abstract
Emotional Engagement; Promotion;
Social Influence; Product Scarcity;
Purchase Intention
This research examined how emotional engagement, promotion, and social
influence shaped Generation Z’s purchase intention toward a local Indonesian
beauty brand, Somethinc, with product scarcity  tested as a mediating variable.
Grounded in the Theory of Planned Behavior, this study addressed
inconsistencies in previous consumer behavior research by proposing perceived
scarcity as a psychological mechanism that links marketing stimuli to purchase
intention. A quantitative causal-associative research design was applied to 230
Generation Z consumers in Denpasar City, selected through purposive
sampling based on the Slovin formula from a population of 227,000 individuals.
Data were collected through an online questionnaire and analyzed using Partial
Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM) with SmartPLS. The
measurement model confirmed convergent validity, discriminant validity, and
construct reliability. The structural model results showed that emotional
engagement, promotion, and social influence each had positive and significant
direct effects on purchase intention and product scarcity. Furthermore, product
scarcity positively influenced purchase intention and significantly mediated the
relationships between emotional engagement, promotion, social influence, and
purchase intention. The model explained 75.7% of the variance in purchase
intention. These findings extend the Theory of Planned Behavior by positioning
perceived scarcity as a mediating mechanism and provide practical implications
for local beauty brands in integrating emotional branding, promotional
strategies, and social proof approaches with appropriate scarcity marketing
strategies. Future research should examine the model acros s different cities,
product categories, and additional variables, such as brand trust and fear of
missing out.

INTRODUCTION
Indonesia’s beauty industry has expanded rapidly over the past decade, driven by a young
population and increasing public awareness of skincare and personal appearance (Nawiyah et al.,
2023). Statistics Indonesia (Badan Pusat Statistik) reports that Generation Z, born between 1997 and
2012, represents th e largest generational cohort in the country, consisting of approximately 74.93
million people or 27.94% of the total population. Therefore, producers are encouraged to develop
innovative products that respond to this generation’s changing preferences and rapid adoption of
technology (Fajrina & Yamit, 2022; Nabillah, 2023). Approximately 97.7% of Indonesian Generation

Z consumers actively engage with multiple online channels, and this cohort has been reported to drive
the fastest sales growth across nearly all beauty product categories, particularly among local brands,
surpassing Millennials in categories such as sunscreen, acne patches, hair mist, fragrance, and cushion
foundation. Consistent with this trend, a national survey found that approximately 87% of Indonesian
Generation Z and Millennial consumers use local skincare products, although international brands
continue to be perceived as sources of innovation (Populix, 2025).
Market insight data further indicate that local beauty brands currently dominat e the beauty
product category, with an estimated 74% market share compared with less than 30% for global
brands. This reflects local brands’ stronger understanding of domestic consumer needs and their
competitive pricing strategies. Somethinc, established in 2019, is among the local beauty brands that
has attracted significant interest from Generation Z consumers. The brand became Shopee’s top -
selling skincare brand for nine consecutive periods in 2021 and was subsequently included among
Indonesia’s Top 50 brands. Somethinc maintains an active social media presence through customer
service interactions on Twitter/X and educational content on YouTube. Its e -commerce sales are
concentrated on Shopee (82.40% market share), followed by TikTok Shop (17.19%) and T okopedia
(0.41%). A notable market phenomenon is that several Somethinc product launches, such as the Lip
Glaze line and the Diamond Phyto Stem Cell Serum, sold out shortly after release despite being
produced in large quantities. This pattern has strength ened the brand’s image of exclusivity and
desirability among its self-identified consumer community, the “Somethinc Squad.”
Purchase intention represents the decision-making stage in which a perceived need is translated
into a consumer’s willingness to act, reflecting the degree to which an individual intends to acquire a
particular product or service (Semuel & Setiawan, 2018). Among Generation Z consumers, purchase
intention toward local beauty brands such as Somethinc appears to be influenced not only by product
attributes but also by emotional and social factors. Consumers within this cohort tend to prefer brands
whose values align with inclusivity, transparency, and sustainability. Previous literature identifies
three important antecedents of purchase in tention: emotional engagement, p romotion, and social
influence.
Emotional engagement refers to the affective connection formed between consumers and brands
through experiences, interactions, and emotional responses (Eigenraam et al., 2018; Garg & Farooqi,
2018). Within the Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB), emotional engagement is considered a factor
that shapes consumers’ attitudes toward a behavior, which subsequently influences behavioral
intention (Ajzen, 1991). However, empirical findings regarding this relationship remain inconsistent.
Several studies have reported that emotional engagement significantly increases purchase intention
(Prentice & Loureiro, 2023; Selem et al., 2023), whereas other studies have found no significant
effect, suggesting that p urchase decisions in certain contexts may be influenced more strongly by
promotional activities or social media influences than by emotional factors (Candradewi & Marsasi,
2023; Rupianti & Nashohah, 2023).
Promotion is a marketing mix element used to inform, persuade, and remind consumers about a
brand’s offerings (Astuti & Anggresta, 2022; Kotler & Keller, 2016). Within the TPB framework,
promotional communication functions as external information that can shape favorable consumer
attitudes and increase pu rchase intention. Similar to emotional engagement, previous findings
regarding the effect of promotion on purchase intention remain inconsistent. Some studies have
reported a significant positive effect of promotion on purchase intention (Rahman et al., 20 24),

whereas others have found that promotional efforts alone may be insufficient when social influence
is considered (Ramdani et al., 2024).
Social influence refers to the extent to which an individual’s attitudes and decisions are shaped
by family, peers, reference groups, influencers, or social media communities (Effendi, 2016). Within
TPB, this construct corresponds to the subjective norm component of behavioral intention. Several
studies have confirmed a significant positive relationship between social  influence and purchase
intention (Wijaya et al., 2024). However, other studies have reported non -significant relationships,
indicating that the influence of social factors may depend on contextual conditions or specific product
characteristics (Rupianti & Nashohah, 2023).
Given these inconsistencies, this study introduced product scarcity, operationalized as brand
scarcity, as a mediating variable linking emotional engagement, promotion, and social influence with
purchase intention  (Aggarwal et al., 2011) . Perceived scarcity occurs when limited product
availability creates perceptions of exclusivity and urgency, encouraging consumers to assign greater
value to the product (Aggarwal et al., 2011; Lynn, 1992; Trope & Liber man, 2010). In the case of
Somethinc, recurring stock shortages caused by high demand may have strengthened perceptions of
exclusivity among Generation Z consumers. International studies have shown that scarcity -based
promotional appeals can strengthen the  relationship between marketing stimuli and purchase
intention by increasing perceived urgency and value (Aggarwal & Jun, 2023; Wang et al., 2022).
Recent evidence in the beauty product sector also suggests that scarcity perception can enhance the
effects of social and promotional cues on purchase intention (Nguyen et al., 2025).
Building on this evidence and the theoretical foundation of TPB, this study proposed and tested
ten hypotheses. Emotional engagement, promotion, and social influence were hypothes ized to have
direct positive effects on purchase intention (H1–H3) and product scarcity (H4–H6). Product scarcity
was hypothesized to have a direct positive effect on purchase intention (H7), while also mediating
the relationships between emotional engagement, promotion, social influence, and purchase intention
(H8–H10). By examining product scarcity as a mediating mechanism rather than merely an
antecedent variable, this study contributes to consumer behavior literature regarding local beauty
brands and pr ovides local producers with an evidence -based rationale for integrating emotional
branding, promotional strategies, and social proof marketing with appropriate scarcity strategies.
The main objectives of this study were to examine the direct effects of emo tional engagement,
promotion, and social influence on Generation Z’s purchase intention toward the local beauty brand
Somethinc; examine the effects of these three variables on product scarcity perception; examine the
effect of product scarcity on purchase  intention; and evaluate the mediating role of product scarcity
in the relationship between the antecedent variables and purchase intention. Theoretically, this study
contributes to the expansion of the Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) by incorporating per ceived
scarcity as a mediating mechanism that explains how emotional and social marketing stimuli are
transformed into purchase intentions, thereby enriching consumer behavior literature, particularly in
the context of local brands and digital -native consu mers in emerging markets. Practically, this
research provides insights for business practitioners, especially local beauty brands, in developing
more effective marketing strategies by integrating emotional engagement, digital promotion, social
influence, and proportionate scarcity strategies while maintaining consumer trust. Furthermore, the
findings may serve as input for policymakers in the creative industry and digital trade sectors to
support the strengthening of local brands amid global competition.

METHOD
This research employed a quantitative causal -associative research design to examine the
direct and mediated relationships among emotional engagement, promotion, social influence,
product scarcity, and purchase intention toward the local beauty brand S omethinc. The research
subjects were Generation Z consumers born between 1997 and 2012 who resided in Denpasar
City, Bali Province, and intended to purchase or use Somethinc products. Denpasar City was
selected as the research location due to its digitally  active population, high social media
engagement, and demographic diversity across its four districts, providing a relevant context for
examining digital consumer behavior toward local beauty brands. Based on 2023 Statistics
Indonesia population data for D enpasar City, the target population consisted of approximately
227,000 Generation Z individuals. The sample size was determined using the Slovin formula with
a 10% margin of error, resulting in a minimum sample requirement of 230 respondents.
Respondents were selected using non -probability purposive sampling based on the criteria that
they were Generation Z residents of Denpasar City who intended to purc hase or use Somethinc
products.
Data were collected using a structured online questionnaire distributed t hrough Google
Forms. The questionnaire consisted of respondent screening questions, demographic information,
and Likert-scale items measuring each research variable: emotional engagement (eight items),
promotion (five items), social influence (three items) , product scarcity (six items), and purchase
intention (three items). All 230 distributed questionnaires were returned complete and included in
the analysis, resulting in a 100% response rate. The instrument was assessed for validity using
product-moment c orrelation, with items considered valid when the correlation coefficient
exceeded 0.30, and reliability testing was conducted using Cronbach’s alpha, with a coefficient
value of ≥0.70 indicating acceptable reliability.
Data analysis was conducted in two st ages. First, descriptive statistics were used to
describe respondent characteristics and summarize the mean scores of each research variable.
Second, inferential analysis was performed using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation
Modeling (PLS-SEM) with SmartPLS software. The measurement (outer) model was evaluated
based on convergent validity through outer loadings and Average Variance Extracted (AVE),
discriminant validity through cross -loadings and the Heterotrait -Monotrait (HTMT) ratio, and
internal consistency reliability through Cronbach’s alpha and Composite Reliability. The structural
(inner) model was subsequently evaluated using the coefficient of determination (R²) to assess the
model’s explanatory power. Hypothesis testing was conducted throug h bootstrapping with 5,000
resamples to obtain path coefficients, t -statistics, and p -values for the ten proposed direct and
mediated hypotheses. A relationship was considered statistically significant when the t -statistic
exceeded 1.96 and the p-value was below 0.05.
This research did not involve vulnerable populations, invasive procedures, or the collection
of sensitive personal information. Participation was voluntary and anonymous. Respondents were
informed about the research objectives and data usage procedures on the opening page of the
questionnaire and provided consent by continuing to complete the survey, in accordance with the
ethical standards of the Undiknas Graduate School.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
This section presents the findings of the study, beginning with respondent characteristics
and descriptive variable scores derived from the online survey, followed by the measurement
(outer) and structural (inner) model evaluation obtained through PLS -SEM bootstrapping. The
results are present ed objectively before being interpreted and connected to theory and prior
research in the Discussion subsection that follows.
Results
The questionnaire was distributed to 230 respondents, all of which were returned and
usable for analysis (100% response ra te). Respondents were fairly evenly distributed across
Generation Z birth -year cohorts, with the largest group (15.7%) born in 1997 -1998 and the
remaining respondents distributed across 1999-2012 in roughly similar proportions, indicating that
the sample reasonably represents the breadth of the Generation Z age range in Denpasar City.
Descriptive analysis of the five research variables showed that all constructs fell into the
"strongly agree" category, indicating generally favorable consumer perceptions of Somethinc
across every dimension studied.

The finding that emotional engagement, promotion, and social influence each directly and
positively predict purchase intention (H1-H3) is consistent with the attitude and subjective -norm
components of the Theory of Planned Behavior: positive emotional experiences with the
Somethinc brand, informative and creative promotional content, and endorsement from peers,
family, or beauty influencers each appear to shape favorable consumer evaluations that translate
into stronger intention to buy. This pattern aligns with Prentice an d Loureiro (2023), who found
that emotional engagement significantly strengthens purchase intention in online retail contexts,
and with Rahman et al. (2024), who reported that digital promotion combined with scarcity cues
significantly increases purchase i ntention in e -commerce settings. Rather than simply
reconfirming these studies, the present findings extend them by demonstrating that all three
antecedents operate simultaneously and additively within a single local -beauty-brand context, a
configuration that has been tested comparatively less often in the Indonesian market.
More distinctively, this study found that emotional engagement, promotion, and social
influence also significantly increase perceived product scarcity itself (H4 -H6), and that product
scarcity in turn significantly increases purchase intention (H7) and significantly mediates all three
original relationships (H8 -H10). This mediation pattern indicates that scarcity functions not
merely as an independent marketing lever but as a psychologic al pathway through which
emotional and social marketing stimuli are converted into purchase urgency: when consumers feel
emotionally connected to a brand, receive persuasive promotional messages, or observe positive
social signals, they become more attenti ve to cues of limited availability, and this heightened
scarcity perception is what ultimately intensifies their intention to buy. This mechanism is
consistent with international scarcity -marketing evidence showing that scarcity appeals amplify
the effect of promotional and social stimuli on purchase intention (Aggarwal & Jun, 2023; Wang

521

et al., 2022), and with recent sector-specific findings that scarcity perception strengthens the link
between marketing cues and purchase intention specifically for skincar e and beauty products
(Nguyen et al., 2025). Rather than listing further parallel studies, this discussion focuses on the
theoretical implication of this convergence: perceived scarcity offers a parsimonious explanation
for why emotionally engaging and socially endorsed local brands, such as Somethinc, can convert
stock-outs into a competitive signal rather than a service failure.
These findings carry both theoretical and practical significance. Theoretically, the study
extends the Theory of Planned Behavio r beyond its conventional attitude -norm-control structure
by positioning perceived product scarcity as a mediating psychological mechanism that connects
marketing-mix stimuli to behavioral intention, contributing to the broader literature on consumer
behavior toward local beauty brands among Generation Z. Practically, the results suggest that
Somethinc, and local beauty brands more broadly, can strengthen purchase intention by
simultaneously investing in authentic emotional-branding content, optimizing digital promotional
tactics such as flash sales, bundles, and limited editions, deepening collaboration with beauty
influencers and content creators to stimulate positive electronic word -of-mouth, and applying
scarcity marketing proportionately, for example through limited-stock notifications or time-bound
offers, so that urgency is created without eroding consumer trust in the brand.
Beyond the local context, these findings also speak to a broader, internationally relevant
question in consumer psychology: how digitally native consumers across emerging markets
respond to scarcity -based marketing under conditions of high social -media exposure. Because
Generation Z purchase behavior is shaped by globally shared digital platforms rather than purely
local retail env ironments, the mediating role of perceived scarcity identified here is plausibly
transferable to other emerging -market contexts where local brands compete against global
incumbents on value and authenticity rather than price alone, offering a cross -culturally relevant
account of how emotional and social marketing signals are converted into urgency-driven purchase
decisions. This study is nonetheless limited by its cross-sectional design, its focus on a single city
and a single brand, and its reliance on sel f-reported intention rather than observed purchase
behavior, and these limitations should be considered when generalizing the findings to other
beauty brands, regions, or consumer segments.

CONCLUSION
This study examined how emotional engagement, promotion, and social influence shaped
Generation Z’s purchase intention toward the local beauty brand Somethinc and whether product
scarcity mediated these relationships. Using PLS -SEM on data collected from 230 Generation Z
consumers in Denpasar City, th e study found that emotional engagement, promotion, and social
influence each had significant and positive effects on both purchase intention and perceived product
scarcity. Furthermore, product scarcity had a significant and positive effect on purchase intention and
significantly mediated the relationships between all three antecedents and purchase intention. The full
model explained 75.7% of the variance in purchase intention. These findings indicate that Generation
Z’s purchase intention toward local bea uty brands is influenced not only by direct emotional and
social marketing factors but also by the psychological reinforcement generated when these stimuli
enhance perceptions of scarcity and exclusivity. Future research is encouraged to replicate this model
across different cities, product categories, and local brands, while incorporating additional constructs

such as brand trust, brand image, fear of missing out, and electronic word-of-mouth to develop a more
comprehensive understanding of Generation Z’s purchase behavior toward local beauty brands.
