Abstract:
AI-driven neuromarketing combines behavioural theory, multimodal sensing, statistical
inference and machine learning to explain how consumers allocate attention, experience
affect and translate internal states into purchase-related decisions. This research book
develops an integrated framework that treats gaze, physiological activity, interaction traces,
language and observed choice as incomplete but complementary measurements of latent
consumer states. It connects experimental design and causal inference with predictive
modelling, explainability, calibration, scalable data engineering and responsible governance.
Particular emphasis is placed on separating measurement from construct validity,
distinguishing prediction from causal explanation, and evaluating models under heterogeneity,
missingness and distribution shift. The methodological architecture extends from controlled
experiments to real-time analytics and MLOps, while sectoral applications cover retail, e-
commerce, media, entertainment, tourism and service experience. Across South Asia, Europe,
Africa and the Americas, the framework stresses cultural calibration, privacy-aware
deployment, transparent decision support and auditable personalization. The book’s central
contribution is a research-to-deployment pathway in which measurement protocols, causal
estimands, predictive models, uncertainty diagnostics, governance controls and
reproducibility artifacts operate as one coherent neuromarketing system.

Chapter 1. Foundations, Uncertainty and Research Design for AI-Driven
Neuromarketing
The first chapter establishes a measurement-centred foundation for neuromarketing in
which psychological constructs, observable signals and decision outcomes are kept
analytically distinct. Attention and emotion are treated as latent processes inferred
from imperfect measurements, not as direct readouts from a single device. This
approach accommodates the supplied work on social behaviour, multimodal inference,
physiological state mapping and intention decoding, which collectively justify a
systems view of human response rather than a sensor-specific account (Pasupuleti
2026c, pp. 60–65; Pasupuleti 2026d, pp. 142–147; Pasupuleti 2026h, pp. 1–6;
Pasupuleti 2026e, pp. 94–99). The research design therefore begins with construct
definition, stimulus logic, sampling, timing and uncertainty before any predictive
model is selected.
The chapter’s conceptual map links theory to measurement through a stimulus–state–
response structure. Assumptions are documented at each transition: what a signal is
believed to measure, how context may alter its meaning, and which forms of
missingness or heterogeneity are plausible. The principal outputs are a measurement
ontology, synchronized data protocol, experimental design dossier, uncertainty
register and integrative research map. Together these artifacts make neuromarketing
evidence more comparable across laboratories, firms and regions while preserving the
distinction between exploratory pattern discovery and confirmatory behavioural
inference.

Chapter 2. Statistical Modelling for Explanation, Causal Inference and
Behavioral Mechanisms
Chapter 2 develops explanatory and causal reasoning for neuromarketing. Predictive
associations can be commercially useful, but they do not by themselves establish that
a creative treatment, message, price frame or interface caused a change in attention,
emotion or purchase. The supplied methodological corpus on double machine
learning, streaming causal inference, causal time series and feature dependence
provides a transferable basis for formal estimands, confounding control and
sensitivity analysis (Pasupuleti 2023a, pp. 76–81; Pasupuleti 2023e, pp. 53–58;
Pasupuleti 2023b, pp. 29–34; Pasupuleti 2021a, pp. 356–361). Statistical modelling is
therefore organized around explicit questions about effects, mechanisms, trajectories
and heterogeneity.
The chapter moves from generalized response models to counterfactual designs,
functional trajectories, mediation and moderation, and robustness analysis. Each
model is accompanied by an assumptions ledger and an evaluation plan rather than
interpreted through coefficient significance alone. The research outputs include causal
diagrams, estimand statements, dynamic-state models, subgroup effect profiles and
replication envelopes. These outputs help researchers and practitioners distinguish
what the data describe, what the model predicts, and what an intervention can
plausibly be said to change.

I. Neurocognitive Attention as a Dynamic Market Signal
The attention theme treats consumer response as a temporally organized latent process
rather than a single survey answer. In AI-driven neuromarketing, observable traces
such as gaze persistence, physiological variation, interaction timing, language, and
choice are interpreted as partial measurements of an underlying cognitive–affective
state. The supplied research supports this shift from isolated indicators toward
continuous, multimodal state estimation, while also showing why dimensionality,
temporal dependence, and signal heterogeneity must be modelled explicitly
(Pasupuleti 2026h, pp. 1–6; Pasupuleti 2026e, pp. 94–99). For marketing research, the
theoretical advance is to separate the construct of interest from the sensor that
happens to measure it: attention is not eye movement alone, emotion is not a facial
score alone, and purchase propensity is not reducible to a click. This distinction
permits stronger construct validity and prevents technical convenience from becoming
a substitute for behavioural theory.

Glossary
Affective computing: Computational inference and modelling of affective states
from behavioural, physiological or contextual signals.
Attention: Selective allocation of cognitive processing resources to stimuli or tasks,
inferred through multiple imperfect indicators.
Calibration: Agreement between predicted probabilities and observed outcome
frequencies under a defined evaluation design.
Causal estimand: A precisely defined quantity representing the causal effect a study
seeks to identify.
Consumer-state estimation: Inference of latent cognitive, affective or intentional
states from multimodal observations.
Distribution shift: Change in the data-generating process between training,
validation and deployment contexts.
Double machine learning: Causal estimation strategy using flexible nuisance models
with orthogonalized effect estimation.
Ecological validity: Degree to which a study reflects behaviour and conditions in
real-world settings.
Feature dependence: Statistical or structural dependence among predictors that can
alter interpretation, stability and attribution.
Functional data analysis: Methods treating curves or trajectories as analytical
objects rather than isolated point measurements.
MLOps: Practices for versioning, deploying, monitoring and governing machine-
learning systems across their lifecycle.
Multimodal fusion: Integration of information from multiple signal modalities into a
shared or coordinated representation.
Neuromarketing: Use of neuroscience-informed, behavioural and computational
methods to study consumer attention, affect and decision processes.
Purchase propensity: Estimated likelihood of a purchase-related action under a
defined context and time horizon.
Representation learning: Automatic learning of useful latent features from high-
dimensional data.
Signal synchronization: Alignment of multimodal observations onto a common
temporal reference for joint analysis.
