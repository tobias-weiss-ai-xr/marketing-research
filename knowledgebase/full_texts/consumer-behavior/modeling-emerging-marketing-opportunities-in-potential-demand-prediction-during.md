This research aims to develop a concept of “potential demand,” a flexible
driving variable, to evaluate the role of emerging marketing opportunities
during acute crises, taking the COVID-19 pandemic as an example. It fills a
research gap in that it address es the structural shortcomings of the
traditional marketing literature that reduces disruptions to risk
management. It shifts marketing thought to an integrated approach which
views the components of the enhanced marketing mix as a flexible system
to promo te organizational sustainability. The research was descriptive,
analytic and inferential with Purposive sampling and respondents (N =
800). Data were analyzed using exploratory factor analysis and regression
models, using advanced software packages as SPSS, Python in Google
Colab environment. The measurement model had a high internal
consistency and efficiency (Cronbach's alpha coefficient 0.937). The
inference analysis results showed that there were positive relationships
that were significant. Among the f actors, the highest was the "changing
consumer behavior", followed by "digital marketing activities", "product
availability" and "trust of the customers". The model illustrates the
technological paradox with the adoption of technology: it has only a
marginal direct effect, as the flexible mix is a product of technology, not its
driver of demand. The paper concludes that bold operations changes and
re-engineering of offerings are needed in order to seize market
opportunities and predict potential demand. It suggests the need for
consumer-friendly adaptive pricing and logistics policies that are easily
scalable. The literature review contributes to the research by providing
background information and context to link the dynamics of digital
transformation and s trategic marketing flexibility in the effective
management of the new consumers' gaps.

1. Introduction
The marketing mix represents the basic pillar on which organizations rely to achieve growth and
sustainability targets, provided that it is formulated in accordance with the aspirations of consumers
and their changing requirements Sharma [1]. Since institutions do not offer an isolated product, but
rather present an integrated marketing package, the product’s distinction alone does not guarantee
its success if it is coupled with poorly thought -out pricing, inadequate promotional campaigns, or

unsuitable distribution channels. With the beginning of 2020, the world was hit by the COVID -19
pandemic as an exceptional crisis that imposed fundamental shifts in consumer behaviors and
purchasing patterns Darwish [2]. Although precautionary measures such as home quarantine and
social distancing posed extremely complex operational challenges, they simultaneously provided
promising marketing opportunities, especially in digital business environments and the logistics and
delivery support sectors. Despite the richness of previous literature that addressed this concept, the
critical review revealed a clear research gap embodied in the limited methodological integration
between theoretical grounding and quantitative analysis based on actual field data, the lac k of
structural coverage of the seven marketing mix elements (7Ps) as a single integrated system during
crises, as well as the lack of sectoral allocation to document and analyze the experiences of specific
service sectors such as the food delivery sector during the pandemic. Therefore, this current study
comes to fill this knowledge gap, by providing an integrated (quantitative and qualitative) analysis of
the reality of the food delivery sector during that period. This study aims to analyze the impact of
marketing opportunities during crises, specifically the COVID -19 pandemic, on predicting potential
consumer demand. It examines the influence of several marketing factors, including customer
confidence, pricing strategies, product availability, digital mar keting activities, and technology
adoption. The study also seeks to develop an explanatory model to help organizations develop
effective marketing strategies during crises. To achieve these objectives, the research organizational
structure was built to begin with a review of the literature and previous studies to review and discuss
what management and marketing thought has offered in this context, followed by the study
methodology to determine the research population, the sample, data collection tools and s tatistical

variables, followed by the results section and discussion to present the analyses and their
interpretation in light of the field reality, leading up to the conclusion and recommendations to
summarize the most prominent findings of the research and to provide practical suggestions for
companies and researchers in the future.

4.1 The Potential Demand Variable in Times of Crisis and Marketing Transformations
Potential Demand in Management and Marketing Literature: The Concept in Management and
Marketing Literature is defined as maximum sales volume that can be achieved by the market
segment for a product or service during a particular period of time under certain level of marketing
efforts and the existing environment. Ernestivita[18].
Normally, businesses use past data and the consistency of consumer actions to predict this demand.
Typically, businesses are able to predict this demand based on past data and the consistency of
consumer actions Mahpour [19] . But in crisis situations, the dimensions of potential demand are
extended to become more of an indicator of “emerging marketing opportunities”  Sadiq [20]. As a
consequence of crisis pressures and adapting to the new reality, it becomes latent or deferred
demand and then actual and accelerated demand Taheri [21]. Dynamics of Demand Transformation
During Crises (The COVID-19 Model). The COVID-19 pandemic is a good example of a large-scale crisis
that can disrupt markets, and in an instant, create new ones Robertson [22]. The potential demand
in a crisis can take one of three forms.

4.2 The Paths of potential demand during a crisis
1. Substitutive Demand
Lockdowns forced the closure of traditional retail (stores, restaurants, entertainment centers).
Consumer demand for products and services did not decrease, but transferred to a huge potential
demand for digital channels (e -commerce, delivery applications) Saka [ 23]. The companies who

2. Demand Driven for Safety
The pandemic has changed consumer priorities, with “safety and physiological needs” now at the top
of Maslow’s hierarchy of wants Pirzada [24], creating unprecedented potential demand for:
Health care and preventative items (masks, sanitizers, vitamins) Food with a long shelf life (stocking
vital products).
Remote work & education technologies (video conferencing tools like Zoom and Teams).

3. Postponed Demand
This is demand that gets repressed momentarily during a crisis Khan [2 5] (for example auto
purchases, real estate and overseas travel), but which is latent potential demand building up in the
background of the market, waiting for the “recovery” period to propel what is termed “revenge
buying.

  4.3 The   Strategic Importance of Studying Potential Demand During Crises
A deep understanding and early detection of potential demand gives organizations many strategic
advantages, primarily
Supporting Innovation and Product Development: This enables organizations to modify their product
lines or products as per the immediate requirement and demand of the market e.g. car
manufacturers producing sanitizers and ventilators during the pandemic. T he Digital Marketing Mix
formation: Technology adoption is directly related to the potential demand Jibril [2 6]. Digital
distribution cha nnels and promotional measures that are flexible, and do not rely on aggressive
selling, but rather trust and empathy need to be in place to anticipate potential demand.
Logistical preparedness (supply chains): Companies that can measure the potential demand, can
avoid "out-of-stock" crises, which is why you also found a strong correlation in your study between

4.4 The   Theoretical conclusion of the study model
Modern crisis management theories agree that a crisis contains within it the seeds of opportunity
Elorza [28]. The potential demand study in this research aims to monitor consumption levels but also
demonstrate how strategic flexibility and marketing vigilance can transform the environmental
threats posed by the COVID -19 pandemic into sustainable marketing and pric ing opportunities by
effectively understanding and addressing emerging sales gaps.

6. Conclusion
This study examines the effect of marketing opportunities in periods of crisis (Covid-19) on predicting
potential demand. The results confirmed the shift that crises bring about in market, priorities, and
organizations, and provide opportunities for business to those who can adapt quickly and are flexible
in their strategy. The results of the statistical analysis showed that the research model proposed has
high explanatory value and predictive value, and has a good correlation between independent
variables and potential demands. It was observed from the results that the change of behaviour of
the person is the most important variable affecting the potential demand during crises, highlighting
the importance of knowing how consumer needs, purchasing behaviour and decision-making process
change during crises. Also, the results showed digital marketing activities and pricing strategies were
mostly significant and strong, indicating the need for agility in pricing and digital communication
channels to maintain cus tomer engagement and the organization’s sustainability in times of
disruption. Furthermore, it was found that the availability of the products and the customers'
confidence is complementary but also necessary to enhance market responsiveness and product

demand stability. The direct statistical contributions of technology were limited, but the participants
had high descriptive ratings of technology adoption in regard to the potential demand. The results
indicate that tech alone isn't enough to create a dem and – it should be coupled with strategic
marketing strategies, and consumer -friendly policies and operational efficiency. Technological
infrastructure should thus be viewed as a means to enable the organization's capacity to respond at
a broader level, and not a source of market demand. The factor analysis results validated two main
factors related to the study variables. The first has to do with the readiness of the market and
consumer responsiveness; the second, with the strategic marketing actions and o perational during
crisis. These results reflect the theoretical foundation that crisis marketing involves behavioural,
operational and digital components in a strategic framework. This study adds to the existing body of
crisis marketing and literature on t he forecasting of demand, as it offers a comprehensive
methodology that explains the relationship between marketing opportunities and potential demand
in the context of an emergency. In practical terms, the study provides a basis for decision makers and
organisations to increase their resilience, develop customer relationships, develop digital skills and
establish action plans that will be able to respond to future crises. In summary, the study shows that
organizations with the ability to grasp the changes in consumers' behavior, keep the business
operating and adapt to the changing marketing environment are more likely to benefit from the
opportunities afforded by crisis situations, and to ensure that potential demand can be satisfied
under conditions of great uncertainty.
