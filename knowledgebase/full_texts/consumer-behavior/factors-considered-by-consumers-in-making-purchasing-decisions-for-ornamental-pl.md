ABSTRACT
Kelurahan Fatululi is a center for ornamental plant business actors in NTT. Success in running an ornamental
plant business requires an understanding that consumer behavior in purchasing decisions is strongly influenced by
socio-economic factors. The selection of the data analysis technique was based on the research objectives,
namely: (1) to determine the factors considered by consumers in purchasing ornamental plants, and (2) to
determine which factors are most dominant in influencing ornamental plant purchasing decisions. Therefore, the
data analysis technique used was descriptive percentage analysis. Based on this study, it can be concluded that
price (X1), occupation (X2), plant shape and pattern (X3), types of plants already owned or not yet owned (X4),
yard area used (X5), and plant care methods (X6) influence ornamental plant purchasing decisions. This study
identified three most dominant factors, namely price (X1), where the largest proportion of respondents selected
strongly agree, namely 56.7%, and 43.3% selected agree; plant shape and pattern (X3), where the largest
proportion of respondents selected strongly agree, namely 60%, and 40% selected agree; and types of plants
already owned or not yet owned (X4), where the largest proportion of respondents selected agree, namely 56.7%,
and 43.3% selected strongly agree. Based on these data, it can be concluded that these three factors are more
dominant than other factors in this study. Based on the research results and discussion, several conclusions can be
drawn as follows: price (X1), occupation (X2), plant shape and pattern (X3), types of plants already owned or not
yet owned (X4), yard area used (X5), and plant care methods (X6) influence ornamental plant purchasing
decisions. In this study, the three factors that are more dominant in influencing ornamental plant purchasing
decisions are price (X1), plant shape and pattern (X3), and types of plants already owned or not yet owned (X4).

Keywords: factors considered by consumers, purchasing decisions, ornamental plants

INTRODUCTION
 Ornamental plant agribusiness is currently developing very rapidly as part of the broader horticultural
sector. Ornamental plants constitute one category of horticultural crops that is distinguished primarily by its
function and aesthetic value. In a general context, the classification of ornamental plants is relatively broad
because vegetable plants, medicinal plants, fruit plants, plantation crops, or even forestry plants may also function
as ornamental plants when they possess visual or aesthetic characteristics that are valued by consumers.
Ornamental plants may therefore include herbaceous plants, creeping plants, shrubs, bushes, or trees that are
deliberately cultivated and arranged as components of gardens, home gardens, room decoration, religious and
state ceremonial equipment, make-up or fashion components, or flower arrangements.
 The increasing development of ornamental plant agribusiness is closely related to changes in consumer
lifestyles, preferences, and awareness of environmental aesthetics. Ornamental plants are no longer viewed merely
as decorative products, but also as products that can contribute to the creation of a more attractive, comfortable,
and environmentally friendly living space. The increasing interest in home decoration, urban gardening, and
landscaping has created wider market opportunities for ornamental plant businesses. This condition provides
opportunities for farmers, traders, and small -scale entrepreneurs to develop ornamental plant businesses as a
source of income and employment.
 According to Zulkarnain (2019), ornamental plants refer to all plants cultivated for the purpose of
enjoying their beauty. Therefore, based on this definition, the classification of ornamental plants is not limited
only to flowering plants; ornamental plants also include plantation or forestry plants that possess aesthetic value
in terms of their form and are cultivated to enhance the beauty and greenness of the environment. Ornamental
plants do not only play a role in the development of the agricultural sector, but also in the development of the
agrotourism sector and the economy for part of the Indonesian community. Their economic value is influenced
not only by production aspects but also by consumer preferences, trends, uniqueness, plant appearance, and the
ability of business actors to respond to changing market demand.
 From an agribusiness perspective, ornamental plants represent commodities with distinctive market
characteristics. Unlike staple agricultural commodities, demand for ornamental plants is strongly influenced by
consumers' tastes, purchasing power, lifestyle, aesthetic preferences, and social trends. The purchasing decision
therefore involves more than simply assessing the functional usefulness of the product. Consumers may consider
plant species, size, color, uniqueness, ease of maintenance, price, perceived prestige, environmental benefits, and
suitability for the intended location. Understanding these considerations is important for ornamental plant
entrepreneurs because changes in consumer preferences can directly affect the types of plants demanded in the
market.
 Ornamental plants in Kota Kupang have relatively high productivity compared with other kabupaten in
Provinsi NTT. This is because many ornamental plant cultivation activities use polybags, which can be arranged
efficiently and do not require extensive space. The ornamental plant with the highest production in Kota Kupang
is bougainvillea. Bougainvillea production in Kota Kupang reached 1,600 plants. Furthermore, the ornamental
plant with the second highest production is aglaonema, with a production of 600 plants. The plant with the lowest
production is Cordyline, with 60 plants produced in Kota Kupang (BPS NTT, 2024). These figures indicate that
Kota Kupang has potential for the development of ornamental plant agribusiness, although production levels
differ considerably across plant types. Such differences may reflect variations in consumer demand, market
trends, cultivation practices, availability of planting materials, and the preferences of ornamental plant producers
and buyers.
 The role of ornamental plants in beautifying the environment makes them a popular choice among the
public. Various types of ornamental plants can be found, ranging from herbs and shrubs to trees, which are

 Kelurahan Fatululi is a center for ornamental plant business actors in NTT. This area is known for various

segmentation more clearly so that product offerings, prices, and promotional strategies can be aligned with
consumer characteristics.
 Success in running an ornamental plant business requires an understanding that consumer behavior in
purchasing decisions is strongly influenced by socio -economic factors. Engel et al. (1995) stated that consumer
behavior refers to actions directly involved in obtaining, consuming, and disposing of products and services,
including the decision -making processes that precede and follow these actions. This concept shows that
purchasing decisions are the result of a series of considerations rather than a single spontaneous action. In the case
of ornamental plants, consumers may evaluate available alternatives based on their needs, preferences, financial
capacity, knowledge, and perceptions regarding the benefits of the product.
 In this context, ornamental plant purchasing decisions are not only influenced by personal preferences but
also by consumers’ socio -economic factors. These factors include a person’s position in society based on
economic activity, education, and income level (Wayan, 2014). Socio -economic characteristics can influence
consumers' access to information, purchasing power, perceptions of product quality, and willingness to pay for
particular types of ornamental plants. Consumers with different socio -economic backgrounds may therefore
demonstrate different purchasing patterns, preferences, and priorities.
 According to Darmayanti (2021), ornamental plant purchasing decisions during the pandemic were not
only influenced by aesthetic needs but also driven by psychological factors and the social environment resulting
from activity restrictions. This finding indicates that ornamental plant purchasing behavior may change according
to social circumstances and consumer lifestyles. Although conditions after the pandemic may differ, changes in
consumer awareness of home environments, gardening, and decorative plants may continue to influence the
ornamental plant market. Therefore, examining consumer purchasing considerations remains relevant, particularly
in local markets with specific socio-economic characteristics.
 Sulistyorini (2023) stated that socio-economic status includes three factors, namely occupation, education,
and income. These factors can influence how consumers perceive ornamental plant products and determine which
attributes are considered most important. The availability of quality products and good service is an attraction for
consumers, but it is also important to understand the factors underlying their purchasing decisions. Educated
consumers tend to be more sensitive to aesthetic value and the additional benefits of ornamental plants, such as
health benefits and positive impacts on the environment. On the other hand, consumers with lower economic
backgrounds may focus more on price and functionality.
 Income may determine the level of purchasing power and the ability of consumers to choose ornamental
plants of different price categories. Education may influence consumer knowledge regarding plant quality,
maintenance, environmental benefits, and aesthetic characteristics. Meanwhile, occupation may be associated
with lifestyle, social interaction, available leisure time, and preferences for particular types of ornamental plants.
These socio -economic characteristics may interact with one another and shape the final purchasing decision.

 For business owners, understanding differences in consumer characteristics is important for developing

influencing ornamental plant purchasing decisions.

METHOD
This research was conducted in Kelurahan Fatululi, Kecamatan Oebobo, Kota Kupang from November to
December 2024. The data used in this study were qualitative data. Primary data were obtained through interviews
and questionnaires, while secondary data were collected through documentation methods or written materials. The
population in this study consisted of all consumers located in Kelurahan Fatululi from November to December
2024. The sampling technique used in this study was non -probability sampling, using accidental sampling or
convenience sampling (Sugiyono, 2017). The minimum sample size in this study was 30 respondents.
The data analysis method in this study involved data processing and analysis. Data analysis is the process of
simplifying data into a form that is easier to understand for interpretation. Descriptive analysis was intended to
provide information about the observed data so that it becomes meaningful and communicative, using descriptive
percentage analysis.

level, income level, yard area, and types of ornamental plants owned.

The research results show that the community in Kelurahan Fatululi Kota Kupang strongly agrees that price

influencing purchasing decisions, and this is closely related to their background and purpose in buying the plants.
Consumers with high interest in ornamental plants, such as collectors or serious hobbyists, tend not to hesitate to
spend more money to obtain expensive ornamental plants. For them, high prices usually reflect species
uniqueness, rarity, beauty of shape or color, as well as the aesthetic and emotional value of the plant itself. In
addition, expensive ornamental plants are often considered an investment because their value can increase over
time, especially if they are well cared for. Such consumers also usually have sufficient knowledge about plant
types, care techniques, and market trends, so purchases are made with long -term considerations. Expensive plants
are also often used as premium decorative elements that reflect the taste and social status of their owners,
especially among urban communities and young professionals who make ornamental plants part of a modern
lifestyle.
On the other hand, consumers who are more functional or beginners in ornamental plants tend to look for plants at
affordable prices. This group usually purchases ornamental plants for home decoration, workplace decoration, or
as a means of relaxation and therapy without wanting major commitment or risk. Low prices are a key attraction
because they allow them to try caring for plants without fear of financial loss if the plant dies or does not grow
optimally. These consumers also often purchase larger quantities to decorate various corners of rooms, small
gardens, or even as gifts. In addition, this segment is usually more open to plant types that are easy to care for,
grow quickly, and do not require special maintenance. Affordable ornamental plants are also highly demanded by
small business actors such as florists, event decorators, or retailers who require fast stock turnover and
economical purchase prices. Thus, ornamental plant prices reflect the type of consumer purchasing them.
Consumers oriented toward collection and long -term aesthetic value prefer high -priced plants, while beginners,
functional consumers, or small business actors prefer plants that are inexpensive and easy to obtain. Both
segments are equally important in the ornamental plant market and have different motivations, expectations, and
purchasing habits, all of which are rooted in their perceptions of the value offered by plant prices.

CLOSING
Conclusion
Based on the research results and discussion, several conclusions can be drawn as follows:
1. The research results show that the price factor (X1), occupation (X2), plant shape and pattern (X3), types of
plants already owned or not yet owned (X4), yard area used (X5), and plant care methods (X6) influence
ornamental plant purchasing decisions.
2. In this study, there are three factors that are more dominant in influencing ornamental plant purchasing
decisions, namely the price factor (X1), plant shape and pattern (X3), and types of plants already owned or not yet
owned (X4).
Suggestions
1. To increase ornamental plant sales, producers are advised to prioritize prices that are in accordance with
consumer needs and to provide various types of plants that have unique shapes and patterns, because these factors
are the main considerations for consumers in determining ornamental plant purchases. In addition, producers
should continue to maintain product quality and service so as to create a positive impression for consumers.
2. For future researchers, it is recommended to use more independent variables, such as social class, small
reference groups, family, experience, personality, and consumer attitudes toward purchasing decisions.
