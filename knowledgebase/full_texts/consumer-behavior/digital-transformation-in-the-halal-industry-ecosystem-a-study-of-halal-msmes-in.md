Background: Digital transformation is a critical factor in the development of halal
micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs) in the digital economy era, as it can
improve business efficiency, expand market access, and strengthen the halal industry
ecosystem. However, its implementation still faces various obstacles, such as low digital
literacy, limited human resources, and suboptimal technology utilization.
Research Gap:  Various previous studies have examined digital transformation in
MSMEs as well as halal industry development; however, most have focused on the
impact of digitalization on business performance, digital marketing, o r technology
adoption in general. Research specifically examining digital transformation as a factor
strengthening the halal industry ecosystem, particularly among halal MSMEs at the
regional level, remains relatively limited. In addition, previous studies  have
predominantly used quantitative approaches, which have not been able to provide an
in-depth depiction of the experiences, strategies, and challenges faced by halal MSME
actors in implementing digital transformation.
Novelty: This study uniquely examines digital transformation as a factor strengthening
the halal industry ecosystem among halal MSMEs at the regional level in Pekanbaru
City, using a qualitative case study approach with the Technology -Organization-
Environment Framework as the main conceptu al framework and integrating Islamic
economic values of amanah, hisbah, and maslahah.
Method: This study employs a qualitative approach with a case study design involving
six halal MSMEs in Pekanbaru City selected through purposive sampling. Data were
collected through in-depth interviews, observation, and documentation, then analyzed
using the interactive model of Miles, Huberman, and Saldaña, with data validity tested
through source triangulation, method triangulation, and member checking.
Results: Digital transformation has been implemented through the use of social media,
marketplaces, digital payment systems (QRIS), and communication applications to
support business activities. This implementation has generated positive impacts,
including expanded marke t reach, improved transaction efficiency, easier
communication with consumers, and increased transparency of halal product
information. Factors driving digital transformation include changes in consumer
behavior, the development of digital technology, and training support. Meanwhile, the
main obstacles encountered are low digital literacy, limited human resources, and
suboptimal utilization of technology in business management.
Conclusion: Digital transformation contributes to strengthening the halal indust ry
ecosystem by expanding market access, improving business efficiency, and reinforcing
consumer trust in halal products. Therefore, optimizing digital transformation is an
important factor in enhancing the competitiveness of halal MSMEs and supporting the
sustainable development of the halal industry.

INTRODUCTION
Digital transformation has become one of the primary drivers of change in the global economic
system. The development of information, communication, and internet technology has changed the way
businesses conduct their operations, ranging from production and marketing to distribution and
consumer service processes (Faturrahman, Ega Saiful Subhan, 2025). Digitalization is no longer regarded
merely as a technological innovation, but has evolved into a b usiness strategy capable of improving
operational efficiency, expanding market access, accelerating decision -making processes, and
strengthening business competitiveness amid increasingly competitive economic conditions (F. A.
Siregar et al., 2026). In Indonesia, digital transformation holds strategic significance because the national
economic structure is dominated by micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs) (Faturrahman, Ega
Saiful Subhan, 2025). According to data from the Ministry of Cooperatives and  Small and Medium
Enterprises of the Republic of Indonesia, there are approximately 65 million MSMEs contributing
around 60% to national gross domestic product (GDP) and absorbing more than 97% of the workforce
(PEREKONOMIAN & INDONESIA, 2025). This substa ntial contribution demonstrates that the
success of MSME digitalization will directly affect national economic growth. Along with the increasing
use of e -commerce, social media, digital payment systems, and various internet -based business
platforms, digita lization has become an opportunity for MSMEs to expand markets, increase
productivity, and strengthen the sustainability of their businesses (Mirza Yuniar Isnaeni Mara, 2025)
From the perspective of sharia economics, digital transformation plays an increas ingly important
role as the halal industry develops into one of the priority sectors of national development (Puteri et al.,
2025). Indonesia has significant potential in developing the halal industry, supported by the world's
largest Muslim population and rising demand for halal products and services (Keuangan & Indonesia,
2023). According to the State of the Global Islamic Economy Report, global consumption of halal
products continues to increase in the food and beverage, Muslim fashion, cosmetics, pharma ceutical,
media, and halal tourism sectors (Hendri Hermawan Adinugraha, 2022). This condition opens
opportunities for Indonesian businesses to expand their markets through the utilization of digital
technology (Ummi Duwila, Fredy Hendry Louhenapessy, Bin R audha Arif Hanoeboen & Ridhwan
Assel, 2025). In this context, digital transformation is not only utilized as a marketing and transaction
medium, but also plays a role in strengthening the halal value chain, increasing product information
transparency, expanding market access, and building consumer trust in halal products (Cory Vidiati,
2026). Therefore, digitalization has become one of the important instruments in building a more
integrated and sustainable halal industry ecosystem (FOSSEI, 2025).
As part of the halal industry ecosystem, halal MSMEs occupy a strategic position as the primary
producers of various halal products and services consumed by the public. However, the implementation
of digital transformation among halal MSMEs still faces various chall enges (Suciati, Eva Dwi, 2025).
Some business actors have utilized social media, marketplaces, digital payment systems, and business
management applications in operating their businesses, while others still face limitations in digital
literacy, human resource quality, technology access, and capital (M. R. Siregar et al., 2026). This condition
is also found among halal MSMEs in Pekanbaru City, which is one of the economic growth centers in
Riau Province with fairly rapid MSME development, particularly in the  food and beverage, Muslim
fashion, trade, and creative industry sectors. Although the local government continues to encourage
MSME digitalization, the level of digital technology adoption still varies, meaning that not all business
actors have been able t o optimally integrate digital technology into their business processes. These
differences in readiness levels indicate that digital transformation has not yet fully strengthened the halal
industry ecosystem at the regional level.

Various previous studies have examined digital transformation in MSMEs as well as halal industry
development; however, most have focused on the impact of digitalization on business performance,
digital marketing, or technology adoption in general (Permadi et al., 2025). Research sp ecifically
examining digital transformation as a factor strengthening the halal industry ecosystem, particularly
among halal MSMEs at the regional level, remains relatively limited. In addition, previous studies have
predominantly used quantitative approaches, which have not been able to provide an in-depth depiction
of the experiences, strategies, and challenges faced by halal MSME actors in implementing digital
transformation. Based on this gap, this study aims to analyze the implementation of digital
transformation among halal MSMEs in Pekanbaru City, identify the factors that support and hinder the
digitalization process, and explain its contribution to strengthening the halal industry ecosystem. The
findings of this study are expected to enrich the disc ourse on digital transformation from a sharia
economics perspective, as well as serve as a recommendation for local governments, MSME actors, and
stakeholders in formulating sustainable digitalization strategies for halal MSMEs.
METHOD
This study applies a qualitative approach with a case study design to obtain an in -depth
understanding of the implementation of digital transformation within the halal industry ecosystem
among halal MSMEs in Pekanbaru City (Afriyanti, 2024). This approach was chosen because it is able
to reveal the experiences, perceptions, and strategies of MSME actors in utilizing digital technology as
part of business development (Rinastiti et al., 2026). The research was conducted in Pekanbaru City,
Riau Province, which  was selected due to its fairly rapid growth of halal MSMEs, supported by the
development of sharia economics and business digitalization (Afriyanti, 2025). Research informants were
determined using a purposive sampling technique, with the criterion of hal al MSME actors who had
utilized digital technology in their business activities, such as social media, marketplaces, digital payment
systems, and business support applications. The informants in this study included: Hojicha, Sambal Pecel
Khamu, Jellins Cookies, Maswook Coffee, Manggleng Mbak Esti, and Jajanan AA Eka.
The number of informants in this study was determined based on the principle of data saturation
rather than a predetermined sample size, with data collected iteratively by comparing information from
each informant until subsequent interviews and observations no longer generated substantive new
information, themes, or categories related to digital transformation implementation, organizational
readiness, human resource capacity, consumer behavior , external environmental support, and the
application of Islamic economic values; after the sixth informant, the findings showed repetition of the
key themes without the emergence of substantively different categories, indicating that data saturation
had been achieved and that the six selected cases provided sufficient depth of information to analyse
the digital transformation of halal MSMEs within the dimensions of the Technology –Organization–
Environment (TOE) Framework.
This research uses the Technology –Organization–Environment (TOE) Framework as the main
conceptual framework in the development of research instruments, data grouping, and findings analysis.
The Technology Dimension is used to explore the readiness and utilisation of digital technology by halal
SMEs. The Organization Dimension focuses on the internal readiness of the organization, including
management support, human resources, and the organization's capacity to adopt technology. Meanwhile,
the Environment dimension is used to analyse the infl uence of external factors such as competition,
customer needs, government support, and the halal industry ecosystem on the digital transformation
process. Thus, the TOE Framework not only serves as a theoretical foundation but also becomes a
systematic gui de in the development of instruments, coding processes, data categorisation, and
interpretation of research results.
Data collection was conducted through in -depth interviews, observation, and documentation.
Semi-structured interviews were used to explore informants' experiences and views regarding the digital
transformation process implemented in their business activities (Tedi Priatna, Bayu Bambang Nurfauji,
Dewi Nuraeni, Eli Setia Mukti Sari, Putri Salsabilah Agasha, 2025). Observation was conducted to
directly examine the practice of digital technology utilization in MSME business activities, while
documentation was used as supporting data, including business profiles, digital promotional media,
social media accounts, product catalogs, and other documents relevant to the research focus (Mokoagow

Data analysis referred to the interac tive model of Miles, Huberman, and Saldaña, which includes

focus, then presented in narrative form to facilitate the interpretation process (Janati et al., 2025). Data
validity was maintained through source triangulation, method triangulation, and member checking, so
that the research results possess a hi gh level of credibility and are able to provide a comprehensive
depiction of digital transformation within the halal industry ecosystem among halal MSMEs in
Pekanbaru City.

business activities, particularly for marketing, communication with consumers, payment transactions,
and business management. The forms of technology utilization identified include the use of social media
as a promotional tool, e -commerce platforms to expand markets, digital payments, and simple
applications for financial record -keeping. The level of digital techno logy utilization differs across
MSMEs, influenced by the readiness of human resources, business experience, and the characteristics
of the products marketed. Based on the results of interviews, observation, and documentation, the forms

Based on the table above, the research results show that digital transformation has been
implemented by halal MSMEs in Pekanbaru City in various business aspects, including digital marketing,
digital payments, communication with consumers, and the dissemination of halal product information.
The use of Instagram, TikTok, marketplaces, QRIS, digital wallets, and WhatsApp helps business
operators expand their market reach, increase transaction efficiency, and streng then communication
with consumers.
Changes in consumer behaviour, who more frequently seek product information through social
media before making a purchase, encourage SMEs to become more active in utilising digital technology.
However, the implementation of digital transformation still faces challenges, particularly the low digital
literacy in creating promotional content and managing advertisements, as well as the limited human
resources handling digital marketing.
The research results also show that MSME actors are starting to display halal certificates on social
media and marketplaces as a form of transparency to increase consumer trust. In addition, government

Digital transformation in halal MSMEs is also implemented while adhering to Islamic economic

compliance with halal principles and accurate information delivery, and maslahah through the use of
technology to improve services, expand market access, and provide benefits for both entrepreneurs and
consumers.
Supporting and Inhibiting Factors of Digital Transformation
The research findings indicate that digital transformation is supported by increased internet use,
changes in consumer behavior toward digital transactions, and the availability of training and mentoring
for MSMEs. In addition, ease of access to social media, marketplaces, and digital payment systems
encourages business actors to develop technology-based business models.
On the other hand, the study also identified several obstacles, such as limited digital literacy, uneven
human resource capabilities, l imited capital for technology investment, and suboptimal continuous
mentoring. These differences in capacity result in varying levels of digital transformation among
MSMEs.
Contribution of Digital Transformation to the Halal Industry Ecosystem
Digital transformation contributes to strengthening the halal industry ecosystem through improved
market access, business process efficiency, and transparency of halal product information. The use of
digital platforms enables business actors to more easily convey info rmation regarding products,
production processes, and halal certification to consumers. In addition, faster communication through
digital media strengthens the relationship between business actors and customers, thereby increasing
trust in halal products.
The research findings indicate that digital transformation is not only utilized as a promotional
medium but has become part of a business management strategy. The use of social media, marketplaces,
and digital payments shows that halal MSMEs are beginning to adapt to changes in consumer behavior
that increasingly rely on technology in the transaction process. This condition indicates that digital
transformation contributes to improved operational efficiency and expanded market access, which
ultimately supports the increased competitiveness of halal MSMEs.
DISCUSSION
Compared to previous studies, the findings from Pekanbaru  City reveal a pattern similar to the
digitalization trends of halal MSMEs in other regions of Indonesia and in countries with Muslim-majority
populations. Research in Medan indicates that the use of digital technology can boost the
competitiveness of halal MSMEs (Khairiah et al., 2025), while studies in Yogyakarta show that digital
marketing, payments, financing, and platform usage play a role in supporting their performance (Ali
Ashar Hotama, 2025). Internationally, research in Malaysia also demonstrates that technology adoption
among halal MSMEs is influenced by technological readiness, human resource capabilities, and the need
to expand markets and enhance competitiveness (Bayu ari fianto, faradillah putri fauzi, noven suprayogi,
dian filianti, cristopher gan, 2026)
These findings reinforce research results from Pekanbaru, confirming that digital transformation
is a crucial element in the development of halal MSMEs, although the extent of technology adoption
remains influenced by the specific conditions and characteristics of each region.
From a theoretical perspective, these findings can be explained through the Technology -
Organization-Environment (TOE) Framework, which emphasizes that the success of technology
adoption is influenced by technological read iness, organizational capacity, and external environmental
support. In this study, the readiness of business actors to utilize digital technology is an important
internal factor, while changes in consumer behavior, the development of digital infrastructure , and
government support are external factors that accelerate the digital transformation process.
This study also shows that digital transformation still faces various obstacles, particularly low digital
literacy, limited human resources, and minimal techn ology investment. These findings indicate that

Implementation of Islamic Economic Values on Amanah, Hisbah, and Mashlahah
The implementation of digital transformation in halal MSMEs in Pekanbaru Cit y shows that the
use of technology remains grounded in Islamic economic values, particularly amanah, hisbah, and
maslahah. The value of amanah is reflected in the commitment of business actors to convey product
information honestly, include halal certifica tes on digital media, and maintain product quality and
transactions responsibly. Meanwhile, the value of hisbah is realised through oversight of business
activities by ensuring that marketed products comply with halal regulations, prices are set fairly, and the
information provided to consumers does not contain elements of deception or manipulation. The
application of these two values strengthens consumer trust in halal products marketed through digital
platforms.
In addition, digital transformation also re flects the implementation of the maslahah value, which
brings broader benefits to both business actors and society. The use of social media, marketplaces, and
digital payment systems not only increases operational efficiency and expands market access but a lso
makes it easier for consumers to obtain information about halal products quickly and transparently.
Thus, the digitalisation of halal MSMEs is not solely orientated towards increasing economic profits, but
also supports the creation of business activit ies that provide benefits, uphold Sharia principles, and
contribute to strengthening a sustainable halal industry ecosystem.
CONCLUSION
This research shows that digital transformation has been implemented by halal SMEs in the city of
Pekanbaru through the utilisation of social media, marketplaces, digital payment systems, and
communication applications to support marketing, transaction, and customer service activities. The
implementation has provided benefits such as market reach expansion, increased operational efficiency,
ease of transactions, and transparency of halal product information. However, the level of digital
transformation adoption still varies among SMEs due to factors such as the readiness of business actors,
changes in consumer behaviour, training support, and various obstacles like low digital literacy, limited
human resources, and the suboptimal use of digital technology in business management.
The findings of this research emphasise that digital transformation plays a strategic role in
strengthening the halal industry ecosystem through increased market access, enhanced business
communication, process efficiency, and increased consumer trust in halal products. Therefore, digital
transformation not only serves as a means of adapting to technological advancements but also becomes
an important part of developing more competitive and sustainable halal SMEs. The results of this
research are expected to serve as a reference for the development of policies and strategies for the
digitalisation of hal al MSMEs, while also enriching the study of digital transformation from the
perspective of Islamic economics. This research recommends that the government and related
institutions enhance training and support for the digital transformation of halal SMEs su stainably, so
that the utilisation of technology can run optimally and remain aligned with Islamic economic values in
supporting the strengthening of the halal industry ecosystem.
In line with these findings, the government, Sharia financial institutions, MSME associations, and
training providers need to strengthen the digital transformation ecosystem for halal MSMEs through
shared digital marketing services, needs-based digital competency training, and affordable Sharia micro-
financing to support technology investment; this will help address limitations in human resources, low
