# Mapping the Future of Travel: Investigating the Impact of Customer Satisfaction on Online Travel Agency Purchase Intention Using Extended TAM and TPB

Asian Journal of Business Research, Volume 16, Issue 3, 2026 21
Research Paper
Abstract
This study investigates the determinants affecting the purchasing intentions of
individuals in Indonesia, specifically within the online travel agency context. The
study is distinctive in integrating the extended Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB)
and Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) frameworks, incorporating customer
satisfaction, perceived value, and attitude as mediating variables, and trust as a
moderating variable. By addressing the Indonesian context, the study fills a
significant gap in understanding consumer behavior in online travel agency (OTA)
transactions. The data were gathered from 400 respondents in Indonesia who had
either indicated an intention or had previously participated in online purchases
through an online travel agency. A robust analytical approach utilizing Partial Least
Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) ensures the reliability of findings.
Results demonstrated that customer satisfaction, perceived usefulness, perceived ease
of use, attitude, and perceived behavioral control significantly affect online purchase
intention. However, perceived enjoyment and subjective norms show no direct
influence, revealing nuanced consumer behavior patterns unique to the OTA market.
These findings provide theoretical insights into online consumer behavior and
practical solutions for online travel agencies to improve customer engagement.

  Introduction
The growing demands of tourists urge travel firms to deliver extensive services to
satisfy their customers’ needs (George, 2021), for example, by incorporating
technology. According to Ryu et al. (2021), technology is expected to play a more
prominent role in tourism sectors, especially in travel services. It revolutionizes the
tourism industry by digitizing customer engagement, particularly through online travel
agencies (OTAs). OTAs have rapidly transformed modern tourism ecosystems due to
their ability to provide streamlined and comprehensive services (Tam et al., 2022). They
offer easy access via websites and mobile applications, significantly changing how
consumers plan trips, purchase travel services, and interact with tourism providers.

In Indonesia, the role OTAs has become prominent with the emergence of travel
platforms such as Traveloka, Tiket.com, Agoda, and Booking.com (Angeloni and
Rossi, 2020). These platforms manage to eliminate geographical and time barriers,
while offering competitive prices and personalized experiences (Zhu et al., 2022).
According to Dogra et al. (2023), other features such as transparency, convenience, and
accessibility are also crucial in enhancing customer satisfaction and supporting the
OTA market’s future growth. Therefore, they keep growing, which is evident by the
steady expansion of online bookings, with Traveloka and Tiket.com capturing
significant market shares (Santika, 2023).

Given this growing interest, many scholars have paid attention to issues related to OTA,
such as those focusing on the purchase of goods and services via OTA or online
purchasing intention (Tam et al., 2022; Zhu et al., 2022; Dogra et al., 2023). Various
theoretical models have been frequently utilized to examine online purchase intentions,
such as the theory of planned behavior (TPB) (Leong et al., 2023), the technology
acceptance model (TAM) (Hasni et al., 2021), and the stimulus -organism-response
(SOR) model (Anubha et al., 2023). While this model can be used alone, some studies
combine two models, for example, between TPB and TAM, to provide a more thorough
explanation of online purchasing intentions (Peña -García et al., 2020; Nguyen et al.,
2023; Song and Jo, 2023).

Another research issue that is worth discussing is  customer satisfaction, a determining
factor influencing purchase intention (Tavitiyaman et al., 2022; Reddy and Thanigan,
2023; Tsang et al., 2024). Customer satisfaction emerges from a combination of
perceived value, ease of use, and trust, which differ significantly across markets.
However, findings on this topic have been inconsistent. Moreover, existing literature
merely focuses on general purchase intention. Studies investigating the impact of
customer satisfaction on OTA purchase intention still need to be explored, especially
in the Indonesian context. Scholars argue that assessing the roles of customer
satisfaction mediated by trust within a combined TPB and TAM model can contribute
to understanding online purchase behavior. Moreover, the Indonesian OTA market
presents unique challenges, such as diverse consumer preferences, trust issues, and
competitive pressures, which need further investigation.
To fill this gap, this study investigates the influence of customer satisfaction, perceived
enjoyment, perceived value, and trust on online purchasing intentions on Indonesian
OTA platforms by extending the TPB -TAM models. As one of the fastest -growing
digital ecosystems in Southeast Asia, Indonesia provides a compelling context for

  Literature Review
Online travel agencies (OTAs) have become integral to the tourism ecosystem,
providing a wide range of products and services. As digital adoption increases,
understanding the behavioral determinants influencing OTA use is critical. Earlier
studies have widely used the theory of planned behavior (TPB) for analyzing customer
purchasing intentions on OTAs due to its effectiveness in capturing the psychological
processes behind consumer decision-making through three key components (attitudes,
subjective norms, and perceived behavioral control). However, TPB alone does not
account for the unique technological features of OTAs. To address this, this research
integrates TPB with the technology acceptance model (TAM), which evaluates user
acceptance of technology by examining perceived usefulness and ease of use. The
integration of these models is illustrated in Figure 1.

TAM suggests two primary factors that influence people to use technology, i.e.,
perceived ease of use and perceived usefulness. These factors are particularly relevant
in OTAs, where user -friendly interfaces and functionality are essential drivers of
adoption. The original TAM framework found two critical factors: perceived ease of
use and perceived utility, which were essential in forecasting computer usage
acceptance (Davis, 1989). More recent studies incorporated subjective enjoyment as an
additional extended variable of TAM (Chang et al., 2015). This variable reflects the
evolving nature of consumer expectations, where hedonic factors increasingly influence
decision-making in digital environments. The three components of the extended TAM
directly affect attitudes about purchasing OTA, customer satisfaction, and the
moderating influence of trust, which is additionally included as a factor in online
purchase intention.

Online Travel Agency Purchase Intention

Ajzen (1991) asserts that intention signifies an individual’s endeavor to formulate an
action plan. In this context, behavioral intention is a fundamental element of TPB and
is a crucial predictor of consumers’ decisions whether to buy goods or services
(purchase behavior). In the OTA context, purchase intention reflects the likelihood of
consumers completing a transaction on a digital platform. Understanding these
intentions can provide valuable insights into the effectiveness of OTA features and
marketing strategies. In this study, "online purchase intention" denotes the consumers’
inclination to acquire products or services using OTA platforms. Peña -García et al.
(2020) define online purchase intention as the degree to which a consumer is inclined
to acquire a product or service via an online platform. A high level of purchase intention
in the client correlates with an increased likelihood of purchasing (Martins et al., 2019).

Customer Satisfaction

Customer satisfaction is a key determinant of purchase intention in online commerce,
particularly in high-involvement contexts such as travel bookings. Within the

framework of TPB, satisfaction can be conceptually linked to behavioral beliefs. It
reflects the outcomes of previous interactions that shape future attitudes and intentions
(Ajzen, 1991; Fishbein and Ajzen, 2010). Empirical evidence consistently demonstrates
that satisfaction mediates the relationship between service experience and repurchase
intention (García-Salirrosas et al., 2022; Koay et al., 2022). In the context of OTAs,
customer satisfaction is driven by a combination of utilitarian values (e.g., ease of
booking, competitive pricing), affective responses (e.g., trust and enjoyment), and
service quality dimensions (responsiveness and information clarity) (Dhingra et al.,
2020; Tsang et al., 2024).

However, the relationship is not always straightforward. For example, Uvet et al. (2024)
and Moslehpour et al. (2017) noted that satisfaction alone may be insufficient without
trust or emotional engagement, especially in cultures characterized by high uncertainty
avoidance. These mixed findings highlight a research gap: most existing models treat
satisfaction as a direct predictor, while few have examined its interaction with perceived
value in an integrated framework. Therefore, the following hypothesis is proposed.

H1   Customer satisfaction positively influences the purchase intention of OTA.

Perceived Value

Zeithaml (1988) defined perceived value as the customer’s overall assessment of the
benefits received from a product or service. This construct extends beyond purely
economic considerations, encompassing convenience, efficiency, service
personalization, and emotional satisfaction (Akkaya, 2021). In digital OTAs, users tend
to evaluate services based on how much the platform facilitates efficient travel
planning, offers competitive pricing, and provides features aligned with their personal
preferences (Talwar et al., 2020; Zhu et al., 2022). Within the TPB framework,
perceived value functions as an indirect determinant through affective and instrumental
attitudes that form behavioral intentions (Fishbein and Ajzen, 2010). In TAM, it is
conceptualized as a behavioral belief that influences consumer attitudes (Hasan et al.,
2023; Nguyen et al., 2023).

The relationship between perceived value and customer satisfaction has also been
extensively explored in tourism literature (Tuncer et al., 2021; Paulose and Shakeel,
2022; Saut and Bie, 2022). The findings reveal that higher perceived value is
consistently linked to increased satisfaction —particularly in digital contexts that
emphasize affordability and ease of access. This satisfaction contributes to purchase
intention (Peña-García et al., 2020; Sharma et al., 2020). However, most of these studies
have not concurrently examined the individual cognitive (attitude) and affective
(satisfaction).

Accordingly, this study adopts a distinct approach by positioning perceived value as a
central construct that influences both user attitudes and customer satisfaction. Perceived
value is a simultaneous driver of both cognitive and affective responses, which
collectively shape behavioral intention. This dual -pathway perspective is integrated
within an extended TAM –TPB framework. Based on this foundation, the following
hypothesis is proposed:

H2a  Perceived value positively influences attitudes toward purchasing OTA.

TPB Input Factors

Attitude

Attitude has long served as a central element in behavioral psychology, particularly in
understanding individual decision -making mechanisms (Fishbein and Ajzen, 2010).
Within the theory of planned behavior (TPB), attitude is defined as an overall
evaluation, positive or negative, of performing a particular behavior. Ajzen (1991),
describes attitude toward behavior as an individual’s subjective assessment of whether
engaging in a specific action is beneficial or detrimental. In OTA, attitude reflects
consumer perceptions of the usefulness, efficiency, and convenience provided by
digital platforms in facilitating travel-related transactions.

Empirical evidence has consistently demonstrated a strong positive correlation between
favorable attitudes and higher levels of online purchase intention (Peña -García et al.,
2020; Tran and Nguyen, 2022; Bilal et al., 2023;  Dogra et al., 2023) However, several
studies in Southeast Asia suggest that online purchase attitudes may be influenced by
additional contextual variables, such as social perceptions and local cultural norms
(Tran and Nguyen, 2022; Nguyen et al., 2023; Thuy et al., 2024). This study, therefore,
investigates the mediating role of attitude in bridging the effects of perceived value,
perceived usefulness, and perceived ease of use on purchase intention, within an
integrated TAM–TPB framework. Based on this theoretical foundation, the following
hypothesis is proposed:

H3   Attitude towards OTA purchase positively influences OTA purchase intention.

Subjective Norms

Subjective norms refer to an individual’s perception of social pressure regarding
whether or not a particular behavior should be performed (Ajzen, 1991). This construct
reflects the perceived expectations of significant others, such as family members, peers,
or the broader community, which may influence individual decision -making.
Subjective norms pertain to the perceived opinions of referent groups that hold
emotional closeness or significant social status (Holdsworth et al., 2019). In OTA,
subjective norms may be shaped by online reviews, peer recommendations, and the
behavior of digital communities active on social media platforms.

Existing research reveals mixed findings regarding the influence of subjective norms
on behavioral intention. While subjective norms exert a strong influence in collectivist
societies (Holdsworth et al., 2019; Rehman et al., 2019; Nguyen et al., 2023), they may
diminish when personal attitudes become the dominant determinant (Yeh et al., 2021).
Given Indonesia’s strong collectivist cultural orientation and widespread social media
penetration, understanding the role of subjective norms becomes increasingly relevant.
Taking into account the relevance of social influence on behavioral intentions, the
following hypothesis is proposed:

H4   Subjective norms positively influence OTA purchase intention.

Perceived Behavioral Control

Perceived behavioral control (PBC) is defined as the extent to which individuals believe
they possess the ability to perform a specific behavior, determined by internal resources
(e.g., self -confidence, skills) and external constraints (e.g., technological access or
system usability) (Ajzen, 1991). Individuals with high perceived control are more likely
to exhibit stronger intentions to engage in a given behavior. In the context of digital
transactions, PBC is particularly relevant as it relates directly to users’ abilities to access
platforms, overcome technical challenges, and successfully complete online purchases
(Nguyen et al., 2023).

Numerous interdisciplinary studies have consistently identified PBC as a significant
predictor of behavioral intention, particularly in purchasing decisions (Moon, 2021;
Yeh et al., 2021; Leong et al., 2023). However, findings from developing countries
indicate that infrastructure limitations and gaps in digital literacy may weaken the
strength of this relationship (Leong et al., 2023; Song and Jo, 2023). In the Indonesian
context, where digital access is uneven across population segments, it is essential to
reassess the relevance and strength of the PBC intention relationship in OTA usage.
Accordingly, the following hypothesis is proposed:

H5 Perceived behavioral control positively influences the purchase intention of
OTA.

Extended TAM Model

TAM Factors

The Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) has been widely adopted to examine
technology adoption behaviors (Lee et al., 2006; Hasni et al., 2021). Although
originally developed to explain users’ acceptance of information technology, the model
is particularly relevant to the context of online travel agencies (OTAs), as it emphasizes
two key constructs: perceived usefulness (PU) and perceived ease of use (PEOU).
Perceived usefulness refers to the belief that a system enhances task performance,
whereas perceived ease of use reflects the extent to which a system is considered free
from effort. In the OTA context, perceived usefulness is represented by the platform’s
effectiveness in facilitating travel planning and online transactions, while perceived
ease of use relates to ease of navigation and feature accessibility (Nguyen et al., 2023).

Extensive prior research has validated the critical role of these two variables in shaping
consumer attitudes and behavioral intentions in e -commerce environments. For
example, Nguyen et al. (2023) reported that both PEOU and PU significantly influence
purchase intentions on OTA platforms. However, studies in developing markets reveal
that the influence of PU may be weakened by factors such as price sensitivity and low
trust (Sharma et al., 2020; Nguyen et al., 2023). Therefore, this study revisits the  roles
of PU and PEOU within the Indonesian market, which is characterized by heterogeneity
in digital literacy and user experience. Based on this framework, the following
hypotheses are proposed:

H6a  Perceived usefulness is positively related to attitudes towards purchasing OTA.

Perceived Enjoyment

The extended TAM incorporates hedonic dimensions through the construct of
perceived enjoyment, defined as the extent to which using a technological system is
perceived as enjoyable in its own right, regardless of any performance outcomes (Lee
et al., 2006; Chang et al., 2015; Chen et al., 2018). This dimension enhances the
practical foundation of TAM by highlighting the role of emotional and intrinsic
experiences in the technology adoption process. In TAM, enjoyment reflects the degree
of pleasure users derive from interacting with e -commerce websites or applications
(Chang et al., 2015).

Numerous studies have shown that perceived enjoyment positively influences both
attitudes and purchase intentions in digital environments (Bilal et al., 2023; Nguyen et
al., 2023; Soren and Chakraborty, 2024). For example, Zhu et al. (2022) found that
perceived enjoyment enhances user attitudes toward mobile -based tourism
applications. However, other studies suggest that the effect of enjoyment becomes
particularly significant when combined with perceived value and trust (Rehman et al.,
2019; McLean et al., 2020; Nguyen et al., 2023).

To address these, the present study examines the role of perceived enjoyment within an
integrated TAM–TPB framework, focusing on OTA users in Indonesia. Accordingly,
the following hypotheses are proposed:

H8a Perceived enjoyment positively influences attitude towards purchasing OTA.
H8b Perceived enjoyment positively influences the purchase intention of OTA
Trust as Moderator
Trust plays a pivotal role in shaping online consumer behavior, particularly in
mitigating perceived risk and enhancing individuals’  confidence in engaging in digital
transactions (Sharma et al., 2020; Sadiq et al., 2022). In online travel agencies (OTAs),
trust is defined as the belief that the platform will consistently deliver reliable, secure,
and dependable services; such as offering transparent pricing, protecting personal data,
and providing responsive customer support (Shin and Park, 2019). Trust is especially
important because interactions occur without face -to-face verification, and the
platform’s reputation serves as the primary basis for consumer decision-making.

Although trust is not a core construct of TAM, it is increasingly recognized as a
situational moderator capable of strengthening or weakening the relationship between
cognitive beliefs and behavioral intentions. Studies reveal similar results regarding this
variable, with some claiming that trust moderates a positive attitude to high purchase
intention (Anantharaman et al., 2023; Anubha et al., 2023), and others argue that low
trust may undermine the positive influence of attitude on behavioral intention (Lee et
al., 2023).

However, the results are mixed when it comes to geographical area. Research conducted
in Western countries generally reports a strong moderating effect of trust (Sharma et

H9 Trust moderates the relationship between attitudes towards purchasing OTA

Customer Satisfaction as a Mediator

Perceived value of a product or service reflects the customer’s overall evaluation of the
utility offered, based on a range of personally relevant criteria. In OTAs, this perceived
value encompasses factors such as competitive pricing, ease of platform use, and the
availability of diverse travel options, all of which directly influence customer
satisfaction. Such evaluations form the basis for consumers’ decision-making processes
regarding whether to accept or reject a particular product or service (Tavitiyaman et al.,
2022). Within the extended TAM, satisfaction is conceptualized as an affective
response that arises from perceived value and user experience during interaction with
the platform, subsequently reinforcing the intention to engage in repeat transactions.

Previous research has confirmed that perceived value significantly and positively
influences customer satisfaction, which in turn serves as a key predictor of purchase
intention (Zhu et al., 2022; Dogra et al., 2023). Within the integrated TPB –TAM
framework, satisfaction operates as a mediating variable that links cognitive
components (perceived value) to conative outcomes (behavioral intention).
Nevertheless, some studies indicate that satisfaction does not always fully  mediate the
relationship between perceived value and purchase intention. In high perceived risk or
low trust digital platforms, satisfaction alone may be insufficient to generate strong
behavioral intentions (McLean et al., 2020; Prakash et al., 2023). This phenomenon is
particularly relevant in Southeast Asian markets, including Indonesia. Despite its
relevance, there remains a lack of empirical studies explicitly examining the mediating
role of satisfaction within the OTA ecosystem in Indonesia. Therefore, this study seeks
to address this gap by empirically investigating the mediating effect of customer
satisfaction in the relationship between perceived value and purchase intention through
the following hypothesis.

H10 Customer satisfaction mediates the relationship between perceived value and
OTA purchase intention.

Attitude as a Mediator

Within the theory of planned behavior (TPB), attitude is positioned as a key determinant
of behavioral intention, reflecting an individual’s overall evaluation, either favorable or
unfavorable, toward performing a particular action (Ajzen, 1991; Lebdaoui and
Chetioui, 2020). In the TAM–TPB framework, attitude also functions as a mediating

variable that bridges the influence of cognitive beliefs, such as perceived value,
perceived usefulness, perceived ease of use, and perceived enjoyment, on behavioral
outcomes such as purchase intention (Jain, 2021; Ng et al., 2021). In OTA, a positive
attitude toward the platform is shaped by users’ perceptions of value, functional
benefits, and emotional experiences during service interaction.

Prior literature has demonstrated that constructs such as perceived value and perceived
usefulness affect behavioral intentions indirectly through attitude. For instance, Kalinic
et al. (2019) and Driediger and Bhatiasevi (2019) found that perceived enjoyment
enhances attitude, which subsequently increases the intention to use digital and tourism-
related platforms. However, these findings are not always consistent across cultural and
geographical contexts. In Southeast Asia, the formation of attitudes is often influenced
by additional factors such as platform trust, users’ digital experience, and prevailing
social norms, which can dynamically reinforce or diminish the mediating role of
attitude (Lacap et al., 2024; Soren and Chakraborty, 2024).

Therefore, this study aims to empirically examine whether attitude mediates the effects
of perceived value, perceived usefulness, perceived ease of use, and perceived
enjoyment on purchase intention within an extended TAM–TPB framework. Based on
these arguments, the following hypotheses are proposed:

H11 Attitude mediates the relationship between perceived value and OTA purchase
intention.

H12 Attitude mediates the relationship between perceived usefulness and OTA
purchase intention.

H13 Attitude mediates the relationship between perceived ease of use and OTA
purchase intention.

H14 Attitude mediates the relationship between perceived enjoyment and OTA

  Methodology
To achieve the research objectives, this study integrated the theory of planned behavior
(TPB) and the technology acceptance model (TAM). The combined TPB –TAM
framework was selected due to its ability to provide a comprehensive approach to
understanding  consumer  behavior  by  incorporating  both  psychological  and

Yudhistira and Meliana, 2026
Asian Journal of Business Research, Volume 16, Issue 3, 2026 31

technological dimensions. Further, a quantitative, non -experimental research design
was employed to test the hypothesized relationships among constructs.

This approach involved numerical data from respondents recruited through purposive
sampling (Hair et al., 2021). The respondents must meet several criteria: (1) individuals
must be at least 18 years old, (2) live in Indonesia, and (2) must have experience with
or intention to use OTA services. According to Hair et al. (2022), an adequate sample
size should meet the five -to-ten ratio rule relative to the number of measurement
indicators.

To collect data, the respondents were requested to complete a questionnaire. The
questionnaire consisted of 60 items measured on a five-point Likert scale, ranging from
1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree) (Bougie and Sekaran, 2020). All indicators
were adapted from previously validated instruments relevant to the TPB and TAM
frameworks. The ten primary constructs in this study were operationalized through
indicators translated into Bahasa Indonesia and contextually tailored for OTA users.
The survey instrument was reviewed and approved by an authorized research body to
ensure its appropriateness and relevance for the target population. Clarity and
comprehensibility of the items were also verified through a pilot test embedded in the
data collection phase. To ensure broader demographic representation, the survey was
disseminated via multiple social media platforms such as Instagram, Twitter, and
WhatsApp, targeting diverse respondents from various regions across Indonesia.

Once the data were collected, this study involved advanced statistical analyses to
uncover relationships between variables. It employed a structural model analysis using
Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM). Facilitated by the
SmartPLS 4 software, this analysis is well -suited for complex models and small to
medium sample sizes (Ringle et al., 2024). PLS -SEM was used to assess both direct
and indirect relationships among latent variables. After that, convergent validity was
evaluated through outer loadings (> 0.708) and average variance extracted (AVE >
0.50), in accordance with the guidelines of Hair et al. (2022). Discriminant validity was
assessed using the Heterotrait –Monotrait ratio (HTMT < 0.90), while internal
consistency reliability was measured using Cronbach’s alpha and composite reliability
(CR), with a minimum acceptable threshold of 0.70 (Ringle et al., 2020). All assessment
results are presented in the findings section.

This study adhered to established ethical standards in social science research. All
participants provided informed consent after being clearly informed about the study’s
purpose and the voluntary nature of their participation. The right to withdraw at any
time was guaranteed, and confidentiality of the data was strictly maintained. The
research protocol was designed in accordance with institutional ethical guidelines. To
enhance geographic representation, data were collected from various provinces across
Indonesia, encompassing both urban and rural areas, to ensure national-level relevance
and coverage.

  Discussion
Achieving customer satisfaction in digital business ecosystems presents a significant
challenge due to ever -evolving user expectations (Masyhuri, 2022; Saha et al., 2023).
Digital service providers must thoroughly understand customer characteristics that were
traditionally acquired through direct interactions. Customer satisfaction is shaped by
various factors such as pricing, service performance, accessibility, usage habits, and
social pressures (Siyal et al., 2021). As such, digital providers must ensure the
consistency and quality of their systems, products, and services to meet users’
expectations. Application quality and service reliability have also been shown to
influence consumers’ willingness to engage in online transactions (Zhu et al., 2022).
This study confirmed that customer satisfaction is a significant predictor of OTA
purchase intention H1 (β1 = 0.189, t = 3.468, p < 0.05), aligning with prior research
(Cattapan and Pongsakornrungsilp, 2022; Rana et al., 2023; Saha et al., 2023).
Furthermore, customer satisfaction was found to mediate the relationship between
perceived value and online purchase intention H10 (β10 = 0.068, t = 3.037, p < 0.05),
consistent with earlier studies (Tavitiyaman et al., 2022; Tsang et al., 2024).

Further, this study discovered that perceived value significantly influences attitudes
toward online purchasing H2a (β2a = 0.260, t = 3.821, p = 0.05), echoing findings by
Arora and Manchanda (2022), Hasan et al. (2023), and Nguyen et al. (2023).
Consumers’ perceptions of service benefits contribute to forming favorable attitudes,
which subsequently enhance purchase intention. Perceived value also demonstrated a
strong positive effect on customer satisfaction H2b (β2b = 0.360, t = 6.767, p < 0.05),
underscoring the critical role of expectation fulfillment in shaping satisfaction.
Additionally, attitude toward online purchasing mediated the effect of perceived value
on purchase intention H11 (β11 = 0.070, t = 2.530, p < 0.05), reinforcing the central
role of attitude as a bridge between cognitive evaluations and behavioral outcomes.

The TPB component of the model evaluated three core constructs: attitude, subjective
norms, and perceived behavioral control. Results indicated that attitude had a
significant effect on online purchase intention H3 (β3 = 0.268, t = 3.315, p < 0.05),
supporting previous findings by Akar (2021), Nguyen et al. (2023), and Tran and
Nguyen (2022). In contrast, subjective norms did not yield a significant effect H4 (β4
= 0.051, t = 0.622, p > 0.05), consistent with studies by Bhutto et al. (2024) and Lavuri
(2022). This discrepancy may stem from cultural contexts and a higher degree of
individualism in online decision -making processes. Meanwhile, perceived behavioral
control significantly influenced purchase intention H5 (β5 = 0.229, t = 3.360, p < 0.05),
indicating that individuals’ belief in their ability to control digital transactions is a key
driver of behavioral intention (Bhardwaj et al., 2022; Sadiq et al., 2022).
Based on these findings, perceived value and customer satisfaction are confirmed as the
most dominant factors influencing OTA purchase intentions in Indonesia. These
findings reflect the characteristics of consumers in emerging markets, who tend to
prioritize functional benefits such as price and efficiency over hedonic aspects. Most
respondents exhibited high price sensitivity and emphasized seamless user experience,
underscoring the importance of value perception and operational convenience.

The core components of TAM also demonstrated significant results. Perceived

usefulness influenced both attitude toward online purchase H6a (β6a = 0.201, t = 2.721,
p < 0.05) and purchase intention directly H6b (β6b = 0.137, t = 2.010, p < 0.05). This
is in line with findings by Bilal et al. (2023) and Ventre and Kolbe (2020). Attitude was
also found to mediate the relationship between perceived usefulness and purchase
intention H12 (β12 = 0.054, t = 1.996, p < 0.05). Additionally, perceived ease of use
significantly influenced attitude directly H7 (β7 = 0.195, t = 2.615, p < 0.05). It
indirectly affected purchase intention through attitude as a mediator H13 (β13 = 0.052,
t = 2.111, p < 0.05), supporting earlier work by Palumian et al. (2021) and Majeed et
al. (2024).

Perceived enjoyment, as a hedonic component within the extended TAM –TPB
framework, significantly influenced attitude H8a (β8a = 0.282, t = 5.494, p < 0.05), but
did not directly affect purchase intention H8b (β8b = –0.076, t = 1.262, p > 0.05). This
finding is consistent with Jung et al. (2022), who argue that enjoyment alone may be
insufficient to drive purchase intention in utilitarian contexts like OTA usage. However,
the indirect effect through attitude remained significant H14 (β14 = 0.076, t = 2.827, p
< 0.05), indicating that while emotional experiences can shape positive attitudes, they
may not be strong enough to trigger behavioral action in efficiency -oriented settings
(Davis, 1989).

In particular, the finding that perceived enjoyment does not directly influence purchase
intention (H8b not supported) reinforces the assumption that OTA platforms are
primarily viewed as productivity tools rather than entertainment platforms. In a post -
crisis economic environment, consumers prioritize cost -effectiveness, informational
reliability, and transactional speed. Therefore, OTA service providers should prioritize
improving system performance, service transparency, and core functionalities before
investing in entertainment-based features or gamification.

Finally, the study found that trust did not moderate the relationship between attitude
and online purchase intention H9 (β9 = –0.039, t = 0.640, p > 0.05). This suggests that
while trust may be essential in the early stages of engagement or for new users, its
moderating effect diminishes as users gain experience and develop positive attitudes
toward digital transactions (Anubha et al., 2023). Thus, trust remains a foundational
prerequisite, but it may no longer function as a dynamic driver of behavioral intention
among experienced users. Future studies are encouraged to reexamine the role of trust
in the context of new users, older adults, or interactions with lesser -known OTA
platforms.

Conclusion
The integrative approach employed in this study enables a more comprehensive
evaluation of the roles of customer satisfaction and perceived value as key determinants
of online purchase intention. The findings suggest that the extended TPB –TAM
framework enhances the accuracy of predicting attitudes toward online purchasing and
captures the influence of perceived enjoyment, thereby offering a more holistic
understanding of the factors shaping consumers’ intention to purchase through online
platforms.

The study also confirms that customer satisfaction mediates the relationship between
perceived value and online purchase intention, while attitude serves as a mediator in

the relationships between perceived usefulness, ease of use, and perceived enjoyment
and purchase intention. Conversely, the effects of subjective norms and perceived
enjoyment on purchase intention were not statistically significant. Furthermore, trust,
as a moderating variable in the relationship between attitude and purchase intention,
did not demonstrate a significant effect.

Limitations and Future Research

Although this study provides valuable contributions, several limitations should be
acknowledged to inform future research development. First, the demographic
distribution of the sample was imbalanced, dominated by female respondents and those
aged 18 –25, most of whom were university students. Second, the exclusive use of
online surveys disseminated via social media may have introduced potential response
bias, which may have led to an overestimation of purchase intention. After that, the
cross-sectional approach used in this study limits the ability to capture behavioral
changes over time. A longitudinal design would offer a more nuanced understanding of
how OTA user attitudes, trust, and satisfaction evolve. Lastly, the study’s reliance on a
purely quantitative approach limits the depth of exploration into the psychological
motivations underlying purchase decisions. Therefore, future studies are recommended
to address these issues by adopting stratified or quota -based sampling methods across
geographic regions, considering offline data collection methods, employing a
longitudinal design, and conducting interviews or focus group discussions (FGDs) to
more deeply uncover consumers’ cognitive and emotional dynamics.

  Practical Implications for Asian Business
This study offers several practical implications relevant to online travel agencies
(OTAs) and other digital service providers operating within the Asian business
landscape. The findings highlight the importance of prioritizing customer satisfaction
as a mediating variable between perceived value and online purchase intention. To
achieve this, OTAs must deliver a satisfying customer experience by providing accurate
information, responsive customer support, flexibility in service modifications or
cancellations, transparent pricing, and personalized services based on users’ behavioral
data. Post-transaction support strategies, such as follow-up emails and exclusive offers
can enhance satisfaction, strengthen customer loyalty, and cultivate a trustworthy brand
image. Such approaches are essential for attracting new customers, retaining existing
ones, and reinforcing competitive positioning in the market.

The study’s findings on the mediating role of attitude underscore its function as a key
determinant of purchase intention. Therefore, OTAs in Asia are advised to invest in the
development of intuitive, user -friendly platforms that foster positive perceptions of
service. This can be achieved by enhancing users’ perceptions of usefulness, ease of
use, value, and enjoyment while using the platform. Features such as simplified
navigation, visually appealing interfaces, and real -time support options, such as
chatbots or live hotlines can reinforce users’ positive attitudes and, ultimately, drive
purchase intentions.

Innovation in interactive features is also critical and should be further developed. Fast
page-loading speeds, advanced search filters, and streamlined payment processes are

Yudhistira and Meliana, 2026

essential components in creating a seamless user experience. Immersive elements such
as destination videos, travel simulations, and customer testimonials have the potential
to enhance the enjoyment dimension of the user experience. While enjoyment may not
always directly influence purchase intention, it contributes significantly to the
formation of favorable attitudes toward the platform.

This study also found that subjective norms did not significantly influence online
purchase intention among Asian consumers. Accordingly, OTAs are encouraged to
focus more on internal factors and personalized marketing strategies. Initiatives such as
tailored email campaigns, behavior -based notifications, and personalized
recommendations based on transaction history are likely to be more effective in
influencing purchase intentions. These personalized strategies foster stronger user -
platform relationships and increase the likelihood of repeat purchases.

Although trust did not moderate the relationship between attitude and purchase
intention, it remains a vital factor in the initial stages of consumer interaction. To
establish a strong foundation of trust, OTAs must ensure system security through
certifications, maintain transactional transparency, and offer guarantees such as refund
policies and insurance protection options. These efforts are especially important for
first-time users and contribute to the development of long-term customer relationships.

To enhance accessibility and trust across diverse consumer segments, OTAs should also
develop transparent pricing features, such as direct price comparisons, no -hidden-fee
labels, and clearly itemized billing during the payment process. These initiatives are
particularly important for attracting price -sensitive users. Additionally, digital literacy
campaigns targeting older adults and rural communities should be expanded. Such
initiatives may include visual tutorials, local language options, and educational content
on safe online transactions. In the long term, collaboration between tourism
policymakers and OTA providers is essential to establish standardized pricing
transparency, security certification frameworks, and digital inclusion programs to
ensure equitable access to online tourism services across all societal segments.

Finally, the dominant role of perceived value underscores the urgency of implementing
differentiation strategies amid intense competition in the Asian market. OTAs can
enhance perceived value by offering flexible travel packages, superior customer
service, and exclusive deals. Examples of concrete strategies include loyalty programs,
personalized itineraries, and 24/7 customer support initiatives that not only improve
customer retention but also attract new users and increase opportunities to stand out in
the competitive OTA landscape.

In conclusion, the implementation of the aforementioned practical strategies can enable
OTAs in Asia to improve user satisfaction, shape positive attitudes, build trust, and
create a superior value proposition. Collectively, these efforts will contribute to
enhancing online purchase intention and driving sustainable business growth in a
dynamic and competitive market environment.
