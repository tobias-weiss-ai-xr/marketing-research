# Dark Tetrad Personality Traits and Willingness to Purchase Counterfeit Luxury Brands: The Mediating Role of Moral Disengagement and Status Consumption

2026, 15 (3), 1907-1930 | Research Article

Dark Tetrad Personality Traits and Willingness to Purchase Counterfeit
Luxury Brands: The Mediating Role of Moral Disengagement and Status
Consumption
Sema Küçüker 1 Neslişah Özdemir 2
Abstract
This study aims to examine the mediating roles of moral disengagement and status consumption in
the relationship between Dark Tetrad personality traits and consumers' willingness to purchase
counterfeit luxury brands (CLB). The Dark Tetrad is conceptualized as comprising nar cissism,
machiavellianism, psychopathy, and sadism. Moral Disengagement Theory provides the theoretical
framework of the study . A descriptive quantitative research design was adopted . D ata were
collected through an online survey from 464 consumers aged 18 years and older in Türkiye using
convenience and snowball sampling techniques. The data were analyzed using SPSS and PROCESS
Macro. The findings indicate that Dark Tetrad personality traits positively influence both moral
disengagement and status consumption but do not directly affect consumers' willingness to
purchase CLB. Moral disengagement positively influences willingness to purchase CLB and
mediates the relationship between Dark Tetrad personality traits and willingness to purchase CLB.
In contrast, although status consumption negatively influences willingness to purchase CLB, it does
not mediate the relationship between Dark Tetrad personality traits and willingness to purchase
CLB. This study contributes to the literature by providing a comprehensive explanation of how Dark
Tetrad personality traits are associated with unethical consumption through the psychological
mechanisms of moral disengagement and status consumption. By incorporating sadism into the dark
personality traits  and examining both mediating mechanisms, the study extends the limited
empirical literature on the relationship between dark personality traits and counterfeit luxury
consumption while offering theoretical and practical implications for promoting ethical consumer
behavior.

Introduction
Producing and selling counterfeit products has increasingly emerged as a significant
issue in both developed and developing countries (Bavar, Tahmasebifard, & Kheiry, 2017,
p. 41). The International Anti -Counterfeiting Coalition (IACC, 2025) defines
counterfeiting as the unauthorized appropriation of trademarks. Counterfeit products
imitate the designs and aesthetic features of original brands without authorization,
thereby infringing intellectual property rights (Wilson, 2017, p. 271). These products are
typically of inferior quality and differ from g enuine products in terms of performance,
durability, and reliability (Yoo & Lee, 2009, p. 282). Counterfeit products also cause
substantial economic losses for legitimate producers, consumers, governments, and
investors and pose serious health and safety risks (Sullivan & Chermak, 2013, p. 296). The
global trade in counterfeit goods continues to expand, with recent estimates placing its
annual value between USD 460 and 470 billion (OECD/EUIPO, 2025).
Beyond these legal and economic consequences, counterfeit consumption also raises
ethical concerns, as consumers generally perceive counterfeit goods as unethical (Amar
et al., 2018). Accordingly, academic research has increasingly focused on the factors
influencing consumers' willingness to purchase counterfeit luxury brands (CLB) (Tunçel,
2022; Manikoth et al., 2024). Understanding these factors requires moving beyond
demographic and situational explanations to examine the psychological dispositions
underlying counterfeit purchasing behavior.  Numerous factors have been identified as
influencing consumers' intentions to purchase CLB including attitudes, subjective norms,
perceived behavioral control, and motivations such as value consciousness, risk
perception, novelty seeking, and status seekin g (De Matos, 2007; Phau & Teah, 2009;
Harun et al., 2012; Herstein et al., 2015; Singh et al., 2021; Tunçel, 2022; Manikoth et al.,
2024). Khan et al. (2021 , p. 338 ) categorized these determinants into psychographic,
demographic, microsocial, macrosocial, marketing, behavioral, and situational factors.
Among these categories, psychographic factors have received comparatively less
systematic attention despite their po tential to explain the dispositional foundations of
ethically questionable consumption. Accordingly, this study focuses on psychographic
characteristics, specifically the Dark Tetrad personality  traits- machiavellianism,
narcissism, psychopathy, and sadism - which have been consistently associated with
unethical behavior and are therefore particularly relevant to the morally questionable act
of purchasing CLB (Jiang et al., 2019; Babamiri et al., 2020; Koay & Lok, 2025).
This study examines how Dark Tetrad personality traits influence consumers' willingness
to purchase CLB. Extending the Dark Triad by incorporating sadism alongside
narcissism, Machiavellianism, and psychopathy, the Dark Tetrad provides a broader
framework for understanding socially undesirable and ethically questionable behaviors
(Buckels et al., 2013; Paulhus et al., 2021). Th ese traits are particularly relevant to
counterfeit luxury consumption because they are associated with self-serving and norm-
violating tendencies and with motives for status, power, and social recognition.
Individuals with dark personality traits may use luxury consumption and material
possessions to pursue such motives (Razmus et al., 2023 , p. 2 ). Accordingly, CLB may
provide consumers with access to the symbolic and status -related benefits of luxury
consumption at a lower financial cost than genuine luxury products (Hussain et al., 2017;
Khan et al., 2021).

purchase CLB remains limited. Although prior studies have examined individual traits
such as narcissism (Can  ve Er, 2019; Orcan, 2022), Machiavellianism and psychopathy
(Koay & Lok, 2025) and sadism (Salimian et al., 2024), research adopting the broader Dark

psychological mechanisms. Koay and Lok (2025), for instance, identified moral

background, the present study examines the relationship between Dark Tetrad
personality traits and consumers' willingness to purchase CLB by considering the
mediating roles of moral disengagement and status consumption. Moral disengagement
and status consumption represent distinct psychological mechanisms through which
Dark Tetrad traits may relate to willingness to purchase CLB.
Grounded in Bandura's (1999) Moral Disengagement Theory, moral disengagement
refers to cognitive mechanisms that enable individuals to justify unethical behavior
without experiencing self -condemnation or guilt (Moore, 2008 , p. 131 ). In CLB
consumption, it allows consumers to rationalize counterfeit purchases and reduce
associated moral discomfort (Yakut, 2021, p. 543). Individuals with dark personality traits
are particularly prone to moral disengagement, making it an important psychological
mechanism linking these traits to unethical consumption (Koay & Lok, 2025, p. 1076).
Status consumption refers to consumers' desire to acquire products that communicate
prestige, social standing, and personal success (Eastman et al., 1997 , p. 53 ). Dark
personality traits—including narcissism, Machiavellianism, psychopathy, and sadism —
have been associated with a stronger desire for power, social dominance, and status -
signaling possessions (Davis & Vaillancourt, 2023, p. 13682). Although CLB may offer an
accessible means of signaling status when genuine luxury products are financially
unattainable, their lack of authenticity and exclusivity may also undermine the prestige
sought through status consumption. For individuals with Dark Tetrad traits, greater
tolerance for norm -violating behavior may make counterfeit consumption more
acceptable; however, stronger status consumption tendencies may work in the opposite
direction by increasing the importance attached to authenticity and prestige.
Accordingly, status consumption is proposed as a mediating mechanism linking Dark
Tetrad personality traits to consumers' willingness to purchase CLB.
This study makes several contributions to the literature on unethical consumer behavior.
Theoretically, it advances understanding of how Dark Tetrad personality traits influence
consumers' willingness to purchase CLB through the complementary mechanisms of
moral disengagement and status consumption. In this sense, it extends prior research,
which has focused primarily on the Dark Triad (Koay & Lok, 2025), by incorporating
sadism as a theoretically meaningful fourth personality dimension. Empirically, the
study provides evidence from Türkiye, a context identified by the OECD /EUIPO (2025,
p. 27) as a major production and transit hub in the global counterfeit trade. Examining
the proposed model in this context enhances the practical and theoretical relevance of the
findings.

1. Conceptual Framework
1.1. Dark Tetrad Personality Traits
The concept of the Dark Triad was introduced to conceptualize Machiavellianism,
narcissism, and psychopathy collectively as socially aversive personality traits rather
than as independent characteristics (Paulhus & Williams, 2002 , p. 557). These traits have
been associated with manipulative, antisocial, and unethical tendencies (Međedović &
Petrović, 2015, p. 229). Building on this framework, Paulhus et al. (2021, p. 208) proposed
the inclusion of sadism as a fourth personality dimension, giving rise to the Dark Tetrad.
Compared with the Dark Triad, the Dark Tetrad provides a more comprehensive
framework for understanding socially harmful and ethically questionable behaviors.
Narcissism is characterized by excessive self-admiration and a strong desire for prestige,
leading individuals to pursue symbols of social status (Morf & Rhodewalt, 2001 , p. 178).
It is generally regarded as a socially maladaptive personality trait associated with self -
enhancement and interpersonal difficulties.
Machiavellianism is characterized by manipulation, strategic calculation, and the pursuit
of personal goals with little regard for ethical principles (Ayan et al., 2013, p . 104 ).
Individuals high in Machiavellianism tend to adopt a pragmatic, outcome -oriented
approach that prioritizes personal gain over emotional or moral considerations (Zeigler -
Hill & Vonk, 2015, p. 694).
Psychopathy is generally conceptualized as a subclinical personality trait rather than a
distinct psychiatric disorder (Özsoy & Ardıç, 2017 , p. 396 ). It is characterized by
impulsivity, low empathy, emotional detachment, and poor behavioral regulation
(Kanten et al., 2015, p. 369).
Sadism, the fourth dimension of the Dark Tetrad, refers to the tendency to derive pleasure
from causing or observing the suffering of others (Paulhus et al., 2021, p. 209). In
personality research, everyday sadism is conceptualized as a subclinical trait a ssociated
with cruelty and harmful behavior in everyday contexts (Buckels et al., 2013, p. 2203).
1.2. Moral Disengagement
Moral disengagement is a key theoretical construct developed by Bandura (1999) as an
extension of social cognitive theory. It refers to the cognitive mechanisms through which
individuals deactivate their self -regulatory processes, allowing them to engage in
behaviors that violate their moral standards without experiencing guilt or self -
condemnation (Bandura, 1999, p. 206). Rat her than changing their moral beliefs,
individuals cognitively reinterpret unethical actions in ways that make them appear
acceptable or justifiable (Moore, 2008, p. 130).
Moral standards normally regulate behavior by discouraging actions  that may harm
others (Bandura et al., 1996, p. 364). Through socialization, individuals internalize these
standards, which promote self-regulation and constrain unethical conduct. When moral
standards are violated, individuals may experience psychological  discomfort. Moral
disengagement explains how these self -sanctions can be temporarily suspended,
enabling individuals to rationalize unethical behavior while avoiding feelings of shame,
guilt, or personal responsibility (Bandura et al., 1996, p. 371).

1.3. Status Consumption
Status consumption has been defined from various perspectives in the consumer
behavior literature. It has been described as a motivational process that encourages
consumers to acquire products with symbolic meanings in order to communicate their
social sta tus (Eastman et al., 199 7, p. 53 ). It also involves selecting status -signaling
products to demonstrate one's social position to oneself and others (Heaney et al., 2005,
p. 85). Building on Veblen's theory of conspicuous consumption, status consumption has
further been defined as the acquisition, use, display, and consumption of products to
attain social status (Phau et al., 2009b, p. 71).
In contemporary consumer societies, consumption extends beyond satisfying functional
needs and has become an important means of self-presentation and identity construction
(Kadıoğlu, 2013, p. 105 ). Through their consumption choices, individuals communicate
symbolic messages about themselves and their social standing. Consequently, the
psychological and symbolic value of products often outweighs their functional utility
(Gökaliler et al., 2011, p. 38). Individuals seeking prestige and social recognition therefore
tend to prefer products associated with high status, a behavioral tendency referred to as
status consumption (O'Cass & Frost, 2002, p. 69).
1.4. Willingness to Purchase CLB
Willingness to purchase CLB refers to consumers' conscious propensity to purchase
counterfeit versions of luxury products despite being aware of their inauthentic nature
(Wu & Zhao, 2021, p. 3). Unlike purchase intention, willingness to purchase emphasizes
consumers' readiness to engage in the behavior and has been suggested as a stronger
indicator of actual purchasing behavior (Phau et al., 2009a , p. 266 ). Accordingly,
consumers' willingness to purchase a specific product is considered a meaningful
predictor of its actual purchase (Liao & Hsieh, 2013, p. 411).
Stravinskiene et al. (2013) conceptualized this willingness as a combination of consumers'
attitudes, perceptions, and behavioral predispositions toward purchasing counterfeit
products. The literature generally attributes consumers' willingness to purchase CLB to
two broad motivations: economic benefits and symbolic value. Economic benefits refer to
consumers' desire to obtain products resembling luxury brands at lower prices, whereas
symbolic value reflects the desire to communicate social status through the possession of
such products (Khan et al., 2021, p. 343). Luxury consumption is fundamentally
motivated by the pursuit of status, self-expression, and social recognition (Wiedmann et
al., 2017, p. 86).
2. Literature Review and Hypothesis Development
2.1. Dark Tetrad Personality Traits and Willingness to Purchase CLB
Individuals exhibiting Dark Tetrad personality traits are generally characterized by self -
serving, manipulative, and socially aversive tendencies. Narcissistic individuals seek
attention and praise, view themselves as superior to others, and tend to pursue  objects
that enhance their social image and status  (Ülker, 2021 , p. 1017). Machiavellianism is
associated with cynical and manipulative behavior oriented toward personal gain
(Sharpe et al., 2021 , p. 663 ). Psychopathy is characterized by poor impulse control,
hedonism, and emotional detachment, which may increase the likelihood of antisocial or

and psychopathic traits are often perceived as more repulsive and antisocial (Rauthmann
& Kolar, 2012 , p. 885 ). Sadism, the fourth component of the Dark Tetrad, refers to the
tendency to derive pleasure from causing or witnessing the suffering of others (Buckels
et al., 2013 , p. 2202 ). These traits provide a psychological basis for understanding why
some consumers may be more willing to engage in ethically questionable consumption
practices.
Luxury brands serve not only functional purposes but also symbolic ones by
communicating prestige, success, and social status. In this sense, luxury consumption has
been consistently associated with impression management, social differentiation, and the
pursuit of interpersonal influence (McFerran et al., 2014; Cannon & Rucker, 2019). When
authentic luxury products are financially inaccessible, CLB may provide an alternative
means of attaining similar symbolic benefits. Dark personality traits have been linked to
the use of luxury products as symbols of status and influence (Ko et al., 2019; Zhu et al.,
2023). In the context of counterfeit luxury consumption, covert narcissism has been
associated with stronger purchase intentions, whereas Machiavellianism has been linked
to counterfeit luxury purchase intention through moral disengagement (Orcan, 2022;
Koay & Lok, 2025). Sadism has likewise been associated with willingness to purchase
CLB through schadenfreude (Salimian et al., 2024).  These theoretical arguments and
empirical findings suggests that individuals with the Dark tetrad personality trait will
exhibit a willingness to purchase CLB. Therefore, the first hypothesis is presented  as
below:
H1: Dark Tetrad personality traits positively influence the willingness to purchase CLB.
2.2. The Mediating Effect of Moral disengagement on Dark Tetrad Personality
Traits- Willingness to Purchase CLB Linkage
Moral disengagement has been widely used to explain how individuals cognitively
justify unethical behavior while avoiding feelings of guilt or self-condemnation (Bandura,
1999; Moore, 2008). Individuals exhibiting Dark Tetrad personality traits may be more
prone to moral disengagement because these traits are commonly associated with self -
serving motivations and reduced empathy. Previous research has shown that Dark Triad
personality traits promote moral disengagement by influencing individuals' moral
development and moral values (Egan et al., 2015). Likewise, individuals high in sadism
may justify morally disengaged behavior thro ugh reduced empathy and greater
acceptance of causing harm to others (Buckels et al., 2013). Individuals with psychopathic
tendencies also exhibit higher levels of moral disengagement due to diminished feelings
of shame, guilt, and remorse, making it easier for them to rationalize harmful behavior
(Spiecker, 1988; Kokkinos et al., 2016). Similarly, individuals high in machiavellianism
tend to prioritize personal gain over moral considerations and are therefore more likely
to justify unethical behavior in pursuit of their goals (Feng et al., 2023, p. 257).
Empirical evidence further supports these theoretical arguments. Psychopathy and
sadism have been positively associated with moral disengagement (Aykanat & Yıldız,
2022). Dark Triad traits have also been shown to influence unethical decision -making
through moral disengagement (Travis et al., 2024).  Collectively, these theoretical and
empirical findings suggest that individuals exhibiting stronger Dark Tetrad personality
traits are more likely to disengage from their internal moral standards when evaluating

Moral disengagement refers to the cognitive process through which individuals justify
decisions and behaviors with ethical implications. Individuals who are prone to moral
disengagement are able to engage in unethical behavior without experiencing feelings of
guilt or psychological distress (Moore, 2015, p. 199). According to Moral Disengagement
Theory (Bandura, 199 9), individuals cognitively rationalize unethical behavior in order
to avoid self-condemnation, thereby making behaviors such as purchasing CLB easier to
justify (Chen, Teng & Liao, 2018, p. 260).
Empirical evidence also supports the relationship between moral disengagement and
consumers' willingness to purchase CLB . Samaddar et al. (2024) found that moral
disengagement influenced young consumers' online purchases of counterfeit luxury
brands in India. Similarly, Gupta and Lyndem (2024) reported that U.S. consumers with
higher levels of moral disengagement were more w illing to purchase CLB as a means of

enhance the willingness to purchase CLB. Accordingly, the following hypothesis is
proposed:
H3: Moral disengagement positively influences the willingness to purchase CLB.
Previous studies have shown that the relationship between dark triad personality traits
and various unethical behaviors is not direct (Egan et al., 2015; Set, 2020; Kapoor et al.,
2021; Koay & Lok, 2025). Moral disengagement has emerged as an important mechanism
linking dark personality traits to unethical decision -making. Psychopathy and
Machiavellianism have been associated with higher levels of moral disengagement,
which in turn relates to more favorable attitudes toward unethical consumer b ehavior
(Egan et al., 2015 , p. 123 ). More broadly, unethical behavior may be facilitated by
mechanisms that allow individuals to justify morally questionable actions (Set, 2020 , p.
320). This mediating role has also been observed in consumer contexts, including
exaggerated online reviews for narcissism and psychopathy (Kapoor et al., 2021) and
counterfeit luxury purchase intentions for Machiavellianism and psychopathy (Koay &
Lok, 2025). A similar indirect pattern has been reported for sadism, which has been linked
to CLB purchase intention through schadenfreude (Salimian et al., 2024) . These findings
suggest that individuals exhibiting stronger Dark Tetrad personality traits are more likely
to disengage from their moral standards, which, in turn, increases their willingness to
purchase CLB. Accordingly, the following hypothesis is proposed:
H4: Moral disengagement acts as a mediator between dark tetrad personality traits and
willingness to purchase CLB.
2.3. The Mediating Effect of Status Consumption on Dark Tetrad Personality
Traits- Willingness to Purchase CLB Linkage
Dark personality traits have consistently been associated with a stronger desire for
power, dominance, and social status. The Dark Triad  has been shown to increase
individuals' preference for expensive and materialistic goods that signal wealth, prestige,
and social status . Likewise, sadism is positively associated with social dominance, as
individuals high in sadism derive satisfaction not only from causing harm but also from
demonstrating power and control over others (Davis & Vaillancourt, 2023 , p. 13681). In

individuals high in sadism may embrace conspicuous consumption as an accessible way

Among the Dark Tetrad traits, narcissism has received particular attention in the status
consumption literature. Narcissistic individuals tend to purchase high-status products to
reinforce their self-image, gain admiration, and communicate their perceived uniqueness
(Khan et al., 2021 , p. 340 ). Individuals with higher levels of narcissism tend to show
stronger preferences for prestigious products (Niesiobedzka & Konaszewski, 2022, p. 23).
Dark Tetrad traits, with the exception of narcissism, have been found to increase status
consumption among Chinese female university students (Zhu et al., 2023) . Psychopathy
and Machiavellianism have also been associated with a stronger pursuit of social status
and self-centered status-seeking tendencies (Konuk & Otterbring, 2024 , p. 3). Therefore,
individuals exhibiting stronger Dark Tetrad personality traits are more likely to engage
in status consumption. Accordingly, the following hypothesis is proposed:
H5: Dark tetrad personality traits positively influence status consumption.
A review of the literature suggests that status consumption is closely associated with
consumers' attitudes toward counterfeit products and their willingness to purchase CLB.
However, empirical findings regarding the direction of this relationship remain
fragmented. Some studies have reported no significant relationship between status
consumption and counterfeit luxury consumption. For example, Kassim et al. (2021)
found no significant association between status consumption and attitudes toward
counterfeit luxury products among consumers in Malaysia and Saudi Arabia. Similarly,
Harun et al. (2012) reported no direct relationship betw een status consumption and
consumers' willingness to purchase CLB in Malaysia.  In contrast, status-related motives
have been shown to encourage counterfeit luxury consumption among Pakistani and
British consumers (Hussain et al., 2017). Other studies, however, suggest that status -
oriented consumers may avoid CLB because such products can undermine the prestige
and symbolic value they seek to convey. Status-conscious consumers have been found to
avoid counterfeit products that may damage their own or oth ers’ social image, while
status consumption has also been negatively associated with attitudes and perceptions
toward CLB (Liao & Hsieh, 2013; Ting et al., 2016; Phau & Teah, 2009).
Given the conflicting findings in the literature, this study follows the stream of research
suggesting that status -oriented consumers tend to avoid purchasing CLB because
counterfeit products may weaken the status signals associated with authentic luxury
goods. Accordingly, the following hypothesis is proposed:
H6: Status consumption negatively influences the willingness to purchase CLB.
Individuals with dark personality traits exhibit manipulative interpersonal behaviors and
are driven by a desire for influence, admiration, power, and privilege. To achieve these
goals, they often seek to enhance their physical appearance and display luxury
possessions as symbols of status (Grapsas et al., 2020 , p. 155). Consistent with this view,
research indicates that individuals with high levels of narcissism, machiavellianism, and
psychopathy tend to purchase luxury brands to signal wealth, social status, and similarity
to admired individuals (Ko et al., 2019; Zhu et al., 2023). In addition, individuals high in
sadism have been found to engage in conspicuous consumption as a means of displaying

3. Methodology
This study adopts a descriptive quantitative research design. In the proposed research
model, Dark Tetrad personality traits constitute the independent variable, willingness to
purchase CLB represents the dependent variable, and moral disengagement and status
consumption serve as the mediating variables.

from Tunçel (2022). The Dark Tetrad personality traits were measured using a 21 -item
scale adapted from Aytaç (2022), whereas moral disengagement was measured using a
three-item scale adapted from Wang et al. (2019). Status consumption was measured
using a three -item scale adapted from Malik et al. (2020). All cons tructs were assessed
using a five-point Likert scale.

Conclusion, Discussion and Implications
This study examined the influence of Dark Tetrad personality traits on consumers'
willingness to purchase CLB by considering the mediating roles of moral disengagement
and status consumption. The findings demonstrate that the relationship between dark
personality traits and unethical consumption is not explained solely by direct effects but
also by underlying psychological mechanisms. This result extends previous research
linking dark personality traits to unethical consumer behavior (Buckels et al., 2013; Koay
& Lok, 2025) by examining both moral disengagement and status consumption within a
single conceptual framework. Accordingly, the study contributes to the literature both
theoretically and methodologically by providing a more comprehensive explanation of
the psychological processes underlying consumers' willingness to purchase counterfeit
luxury brands.
The findings indicate that Dark Tetrad personality traits do not directly influence
consumers' willingness to purchase CLB. This finding differs from studies suggesting that
individual dark personality traits, particularly narcissism and sadism, may increa se
consumers' tendency to purchase counterfeit products (Can  ve Er, 2019; Salimian et al.,
2024). Instead, the present study demonstrates that the relationship operates primarily
through indirect psychological mechanisms rather than direct effects.
Specifically, Dark Tetrad personality traits were found to increase moral disengagement,
which subsequently enhanced consumers' willingness to purchase CLB. This finding is
consistent with Bandura's (1999) Moral Disengagement Theory and supports previous
research showing that individuals with dark personality traits are more likely to weaken
their moral self -regulatory processes and rationalize unethical behavior (Buckels et al.,
2013; Aykanat & Yıldız, 2022). Consistent with earlier studies (Chen et al., 2018;
Türkdemir, 2022; Koay & Lok, 2025; Samaddar et al., 2024), the results suggest that
consumers use cognitive justification  mechanisms to reduce feelings of guilt associated
with unethical consumption, thereby increasing their willingness to purchase CLB.
This study further demonstrates that moral disengagement mediates the relationship
between Dark Tetrad personality traits and consumers' willingness to purchase CLB,
suggesting that the influence of dark personality traits operates indirectly through
cognitive mechanisms. This finding is consistent with the framework proposed by Koay
and Lok (2025) while extending the existin g literature by incorporating sadism into the
Dark Tetrad framework. Accordingly, the results suggest that unethical consumption is
shaped not only by dark personality traits but also by the cognitive processes through
which individuals justify morally questionable behavior.
The findings also indicate that Dark Tetrad personality traits positively influence status
consumption, consistent with previous studies (Zhu et al., 2023; Davis & Vaillancourt,
2023). Individuals exhibiting these personality traits appear to pursue status -oriented
consumption as a means of gaining social visibility, prestige, and privilege. As expected,

status consumption was found to negatively influence consumers' willingness to
purchase CLB. This finding is partially consistent with Ting et al. (2016) but differs from
the results reported by Hussain et al. (2017), who found a positive relationship between
status consumption and counterfeit luxury consumption. One possible explanation is that
consumers with strong status motives may not perceive CLB as effective status symbols.
Instead, they may avoid counterfeit products because these products threaten the
authenticity, exclusivity, and social prestige associated with genuine luxury brands.
This study contributes to the literature  by proposing a comprehensive model that
explains consumers' willingness to purchase CLB through the mediating roles of moral
disengagement and status consumption. By integrating these psychological mechanisms
into the relationship between Dark Tetrad personality traits and unethical consumption,
the study extends the existing literature and provides one of the few empirical
investigations of the interaction between dark personality traits and moral
disengagement in the consumer behavior context. The findings also offer an empirical
basis for developing policies aimed at promoting ethical consumption.
The findings suggest that strategies designed to reduce counterfeit luxury consumption
should consider not only consumers' purchasing behavior but also the underlying
personality traits and moral tendencies that shape such behavior. Accordingly, social
campaigns that strengthen moral awareness may be more effective than relying solely on
legal sanctions against counterfeit products, particularly for individuals exhibiting dark
personality traits associated with unethical behavior. In this regard, social responsibility
initiatives jointly implemented by public institutions and brands could reinforce ethical
values and strengthen consumers' internal mor al standards. Likewise, brand
communication emphasizing authenticity, honesty, and ethical values may help reduce
counterfeit luxury consumption. Social media awareness campaigns may be particularly
effective in reaching younger consumers. Finally, developing alternative status symbols
and experience-oriented marketing strategies may help satisfy consumers' status-seeking
motives without encouraging counterfeit luxury consumption. Promoting sustainability,
social responsibility, and cultural heritage  as symbols of status may further encourage
more ethical consumption choices.
From a practitioner perspective, brand managers should move beyond emphasizing
copyright infringement in their anti -counterfeit campaigns and place greater emphasis
on promoting moral awareness and ethical values. Educational programs that encourage
ethical consumption may be implemented in schools and youth centers to strengthen
consumers' moral awareness from an early age. In addition, alternative avenues for
achieving social s tatus should be encouraged to reduce unethical consumption. For
example, experience-based consumption, such as participation in cultural events, may be
promoted as an alternative status symbol, thereby reducing consumers' reliance on
counterfeit luxury brands for status signaling.
This study has several limitations. First, the use of convenience and snowball sampling
may limit the generalizability of the results . In addition, as the data were collected from
consumers in Türkiye, the findings may be influenced by the specific cultural and
consumption context of the sample. Second, the cross -sectional and self-reported nature
of the data limits the ability to draw ca usal conclusions and may be subject to potential
response biases, particularly given the ethically sensitive nature of counterfeit
consumption. Finally, this study examined Dark Tetrad personality traits as an overall
