Abstract: In the competitive market of skin care products, the uncertainty of consumer’s intention is always at the
center. The current study investigated the effects of mediation variable of brand image towards endorsers
creditability and perceived risk on intentio n to purchase skincare products. The study has analyzed a total 150
respondents’ using SmartPLS-SEM tool. The results show the credibility of the endorser has a positive effect on the
brand image and purchase intention, and the brand image has a positive e ffect on purchase intention. However,
recent evidence indicates that the perceived risk does not always directly suppress purchase intention, especially
when brand image is a psychological and perceptual barrier. Endorser credibility, risk perception and p urchase
intention are mediated by brand image. The results show that the consumers might be willing to purchase skincare
products even with some potential risks if the brand image is strong and the endorser is credible, which highlights
the link between trust, perception, and decision-making. The contribution of study applied to the theory of consumer
behavior in the beauty sector, provides cross-disciplinary insights into marketing strategy and consumer psychology,
and offers practical recommendations for marketers on how to select endorsers and improve brand image to increase
purchase intention. Limitations include the size of the product, the nature of the sample, and the single -method
approach. Future research can use the model for other product categori es. Mixed methods can also be applied to
investigate specific dimensions of risk.
Keywords: Skincare, Purchase Intention, Brand Image, Endorser Credibility, Risk Perception
1. Introduction
Companies face more intense competition to attract consumers in the dynamic socio-economic and consumer
behavior scenario, as the skincare industry is booming in countries such as Indonesia (Herdyanti & Mansoor, 2020).
The current skincare products in the market are using an effective marketing strategic. The rising interest in personal
grooming and the increasing popularity of online shopping drive this part. Indonesia has grown to be one of the
largest markets of sk in care in Southeast Asia ( Wijaya et al., 2025). Industry analysts say that its annual market
value is experiencing constant double-digit growth. But that meteoric growth has also made things more competitive.
Industries are resorting to aggressive marketing tactics as the market becomes increasingly crowded. Many skincare
brands suffer from weak brand identities, products that just fizzle out, and marketing that does not work. For
example, celebrity endorsements do not necessarily translate into consumer purchases or long -term loyalty.
This creates major management and commercial problems for skin care companies. Companies invest heavily
in celebrity and influencer endorsements in the hope that these strategies will result in higher levels of consumer
engagement and sales ( Atta et al., 2024; Kumar et al., 2025). However, if these marketing investments do not
translate into purchase intents, the firms might be looking at an inefficient allocation of the marketing budget, which
diminishes the brand’s competitiveness and the customers’ confidence. These industry-level inefficiencies can impact
market sustainability and compel enterprises to focus on sustainable marketing and brand positioning (Utami, 2024).
In the highly competitive skincare industry, businesses need to have a comprehensive understanding of the factors

the risk, marketing strategies may not have the desired behavioral outcomes (Roostika et al., 2025).
One common technique is the use of an endorser, a celebrity who promotes a product to make the consumer
more confident and pay more attention. But consumers are also becoming more aware of the possible risks of using
skincare products. Endorser credibility  and risk perception are considered important factors that affect purchase
intention, while brand image can play a mediating role that affects the relationship between these factors and
consumer decision-making (Sumartini et al., 2021). This study is signi ficant for firms to obtain the interaction of
these variables so that they can develop effective marketing strategies to capture attention and convert the interests
of consumers into actual buying behavior.
The two most important factors in consumer choice in today's competitive and sensitive market are the
endorsement credibility and the risk perception of the product. An important, yet frequently overlooked, piece of this
process is the brand image. Previous studies have studied endorser credibility, risk perception, and purchase intention
separately, but there is very limited study on brand image mediation variable in these relationships in the skincare
industry, especially in emerging markets. This is a re levant gap since poor marketing decisions in extremely
competitive industries such as skincare can result in considerable financial losses, wasted promotional spending, and
loss of consumer trust (Kumar et al., 2025). Therefore, the present study intends to fill this vacuum by studying the
mediating role of brand image between endorser credibility, perceived risk, and purchase intention in the context of
the Indonesian skincare industry.
The theoretical frameworks relevant for this study are endorser credibility theory, risk perception theory, and
brand image theory. The effectiveness of promotional efforts is determined by three important dimensions of credible
endorsement such as skills, attitude, and attractiveness (Ohanian, 1990). On the other hand, Zhu (2016) described
that perceived risks, including financial, social, psychological, and performance risks, are essential to shape
consumers’ purchasing behavior. Keller (1993) argued a strong brand reputation can increase the effect of endorser
credibility and reduce perceived risks, thus increasing consumers’ willingness to buy through his brand image theory.
The endorser credibility, purchase intention, risk perception, and consumer decision -making have a strong
relationship in the marketing research (Mookda et al., 2020 & Putri & Roostika, 2022). Most of this research, however,
deals with direct effects and neglects the possible mediating effect of brand image. Moreover, the literature on
skincare products has overlooked the connection between endorser credibility and risk perception. Previous
marketing research has extensively examined the dimensions of endor sed credibility, risk perception, and intention
to buy. The research is fragmentary and mostly about their direct effect. There is a significant gap in theoretical and
empirical studies about the relationship between endorser credibility and perceived risk  and consumer purchase
intention. Specifically, mediation of brand image has been largely overlooked. This is especially true in the skincare
industry, where health concerns and the efficacy of the product increase the perceived risk.
This research provides a more complete model of consumer decision making, contributing to current
understanding. The mediating theory of brand image in this study was based on the endorser credibility theory and
risk perception theory. Furthermore, the stu dy offers new insights by applying data from the skincare sector in a
developing market. This increases the generalizability of previous findings mostly based on developed markets. The
present study aims to fill these gaps by investigating the mediating role of brand image on the relationship between

2. Literature Review
The present study is developed on the theories of consumer behavior and perceived risk which explain how
consumers evaluate marketing cues under the conditions of uncertainty. Endorser credibility theory (Ohanian, 1990)
states that source-related cues have an impact on consumer trust and evaluation. The theory of perceived risk states
that consumers reduce uncertainty by making purchase decisions based on external cues such as brand image and
endorsement. Together these frameworks provide a rationale for the interrelationships between endorser credibility,
perceived risk, brand image, and purchase intention in the proposed research model, with perceived risk being a
central and distinct construct in this study.
Endorser credibility (EC) is widely acknowledged as a significant factor influencing consumer behavior.
Ohanian (1990) provides endorser credibility with three dimensions, which include expertise, which means perceived
knowledge and competence of the endorser in a specific domain. Then trustworthiness means the audience's
confidence in the endorser's honesty and integrity,  and lastly attractiveness involves the physical appeal and
personality of the endorser, both of which can shape consumer perceptions pos itively (Roostika et al., 2025). From
a theoretical perspective, credible endorsers serve as heuristic cues that enhance message acceptance and reduce
uncertainty, mainly in product categories requiring higher involvement. Studies by Utami (2024) demonstrate that
credible endorsers can significantly enhance consumers’ attitudes toward a product and increase their purchase
intention (PI). EC plays a significant role in shaping brand image (BI), which subsequently influences consumers’
purchasing decisions by enhancing positive brand perceptions and thereby enhancing the probability of purchase
(Atta et al., 2024; Ismail & Krishnanraw, 2025). Follow the above description, the hypotheses below show:
H1: EC has a positive effect on PI
H3: EC has a positive effect on BI
Perceived risk (PR) is particularly crucial in the cosmetics sector as the items are personal and frequently
sensitive. As per the perceived risk theory the process of risk reduction is a very critical factor that influences the
customers buy decisions when the consumers expect to have certain negative outcomes. Maziriri, & Chuchu, (2017)
divided PR into four: financial, social, psychological and performance hazards. Financial risk is the risk of losing
money. Social risk is the risk of being evaluated or disliked by other people. Psychological risk is the worry it creates.
Performance risk is the fear that the product will not do what it is supposed to do.
Nurcahya and Bastaman (2024) revealed that when the PR is high, consumers choose to postpone their
purchase or not purchase the skincare product because they are not convinced about the safety and effectiveness
of the skincare product. This study stresses the need of reducing the per ceived risks through credible endorsement
and strong brand positioning ( Roostika et al., 2025). BI is one of the key aspects to mitigate perceived risks and
increase EC impacts. BI is the core perception of a brand by consumers based on their experiences, word of mouth
and marketing communication (Keller, 1993). Strong BI generates trust and loyalty that in turn strengthen PI in high
risk environments. The description above suggests the following hypothesis:
H2: PR has a positive effect on PI
H4: PR has a positive effect on BI
Previous research has shown the importance of brand image (BI) in transforming marketing stimuli into
customer behavioral intentions. Purchase intention (PI), generally described as an individual’s subjective probability,

that PI may manifest in more general behavioral tendencies related to future purchasing decisions, such as purchase

However, existing research has not sufficiently explained the psychological mechanism of how perceived risk
affects purchase intention. The skincare industry is rife with uncertainty for consumers regarding product safety,
efficacy, ingredients, and side effects. Consumers first assess whether a brand is trustworthy, reliable, and capable
of addressing such risks, rather than directly translating these concerns into purchase decisions. This evaluation then
results in the brand image, which in turn influence s the consumers’ willingness to buy. Thus, brand image is an
important factor in the cognitive process of converting risk perception into behavioral intentions. In theory, brand
image is required in this situation, as it is the intermediate evaluation proc ess consumers experience between
perceived risk and purchase decisions.
Moreover, there is a lack of research that has studied simultaneously endorser credibility, perceived risk,
brand image, and purchase intention using a single integrated model in the skincare industry, especially in emerging
markets. Filling this gap may lead to a more holistic understanding of how endorser credibility and perceived risk are
transformed together via brand-related evaluations before they affect consumers’ purchase intentions. On the basis
of these arguments, the following hypotheses are suggested:
H5: BI has a positive effect on PI
H6: EC has a positive effect on PI through BI
H7: PR has a positive effect on PI through BI

3. Methods
3.1. Research Design
This research utilizes a quantitative and cross -sectional research design to empirically examine the
relationships among endorser credibility (EC), perceived risk (PR), brand image (BI), and purchase intention (PI) in
the market of skincare product. The ra tionale for the choice of the survey -based approach was its relevance to the
systematic measurement of hidden concepts and the measurement of the structural relations of the conceptual
model using statistical analysis. Figure 1 presents the proposed concep tual framework. The study employed a non -
probability purposive sampling technique which is appropriate where respondents are to meet specific criteria
relevant to the research objectives. Respondents were specifically selected because they were previously exposed
to skin care product ads with endorsers such as celebrities or social media influencers. The study involved consumers
who have seen advertisements for skin care products that featured celebrities and social media influencers as
endorsers.
This selection criterion was used to ensure the respondents have enough contextual knowledge to assess
the credibility of the endorsement, perceived risk, brand image and purchase intention. The questionnaire was
constructed with a screening question to al low the purposive sampling procedure to investigate whether the
respondents ever watch skincare advertisements with endorsers (i.e., celebrities or influencers). The question asked
was if respondents had seen or interacted with skincare advertisements that  included endorsers. Only those who
answered positively were allowed to proceed with the survey. Non-compliant or incomplete responses were excluded
from the final data set.

3.2 Data Collection Analysis
Data collection process through a structured self-administrated questionnaire, accessible through digital and
physical channels. The online survey was conducted through Google Forms, and the offline survey was distributed
in controlled settings to reduce the bias of the responses. The data collection period was deliberately restricted to a
particular timeframe to avoid temporal effects and to enable consistency in participants' perspectives. To reduce
common method bias and enhance the reliability of the data, participants were informed that their participation was
voluntary and anonymous and that all answers would be treated as valid. This approach was developed to minimise
social desirability bias and promote honest responses.
After the data collection phase, a thorough screening process was conducted to eliminate duplicate entries,
incomplete questionnaires and response patterns that were straight-lining. The final analysis was based on 150 valid
responses. The sample size was acceptable for Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling (PLS -SEM),
particularly given the model complexity and the mediating variable (Hair et al., 2017). In addition, this study’s method
applies a parallel analysis of the outer and inner model with mediation effects.

3.3. Measures
To ensure content validity, all constructs studied in the present study were measured by multi -item scales
derived from previous, established, and validated studies. The questionnaire contained 23 measurement items
concerning endorser credibility, perceived risk, brand image, and purchase intention. All the questionnaire items used
in the survey are listed in Table 1. Some wording was changed slightly to make the text relevant to skin care, but
the original meaning was preserved. Responses were collected on a 5-point Likert scale, with 1 representing strongly
disagree and 5 representing strongly agree. This method is popular in consumer behavior research to gauge people’s
opinions. Purchase intention was conceptualized as a broader purchase -oriented behavior al predisposition rather
than solely an immediate intention to buy. Consistent with consumer behavior literature, purchase intention can be
expressed through consumers’ willingness to buy, purchase plans, tendency to keep buying, responsiveness to
recommendations, and behavioral readiness towards market offerings (Ajzen, 1991). This broader operationalization
is particularly relevant in the skincare industry, where purchasing decisions are often shaped by personal evaluations,
social influence, and ongoing engagement with brands. Therefore, the purchase intention construct was designed to
capture multiple manifestations of consumers’ behavioral orientation toward skincare product purchasing.

5. Discussion
5.1 Endorser Creditability on Purchase Intention
The data suggest that EC has a significant influence on PI (β = 0.341, p = 0.000). This research advices
that consumers who consider the endorsers as credible, especially in terms of competence, trustworthiness and
attractiveness (Ohanian, 1990), are more likely to express higher intentions to purchase skincare products. This
finding is consistent with Source Credibility Theory which argues that credible communicators may enhance the
persuasiveness of marketing communications and positively shap e consumer a ssessments. These findings are
consistent with prior observations. Putri & Roostika (2022) found that EC is positively related to customer attitudes
and PI toward the product, as items with more credible endorsers are connected with more positive product
evaluations and higher buy intentions.

In addition, the mediation study indicates that BI mediates the relationship between EC and PI. However, due
to its cross-sectional nature, this finding should be treated as evidence for statistical mediation rather than evidence
of a causal mechanism. Managerially, the results suggest that skincare companies may want to select endorsers not
only on the basis of popularity, but also on the basis of perceived knowledge and trustworthiness, especially for
goods that are tied to personal health and skin safety  issues. “Credible endorsers may serve as information cues,
which enhance consumers’ confidence in their product evaluations” ( Erdogan, 1999). However, the management
implications should be taken with caution because the present study identifies relationships and not causative
consequences. Additional longitudinal or experimental studies are needed to confirm whether the increases in
endorser credibility lead directly to changes in consumers’ purchase behavior.

5.2 Perceived Risk on Purchase Intention
On the other hand, PR does not substantially affect PI (β = 0.052; p = 0.530). The consumers should manage
a risk of skin irritation, incompatibility, or ineffectiveness of the product. These worries are serious enough to deter
and lower the purchase inten tion. This result is in line with other research ( Nurcahya & Bastaman, 2024 ) that
increased PR led to decreased PI, and it offers useful managerial implications for skincare firms. The findings suggest
that risk -reducing techniques (i.e., credible endorsem ents, unambiguous ingredient disclosure, and third -party
certification) may negate perceived risk in the purchase choice process. Although PR is not a significant variable on
PI operations such as BPOM certification, dermatological testing and posting of user testimonials are vital to convince
the consumers and to lower the perceived risk. As a result, managers should take a proactive approach to safety
and product efficacy to sustain consumer confidence in high-involvement skincare purchases (Yee et al., 2025).

5.3 Endorser Credibility on Brand Image
EC has a positive effect on BI (β = 0.587; p = 0.000). Trustworthy endorsers can lead to better perceptions
of the advertised brand in terms of quality, emotional appeal, and social value. This supports the notion that public
figures or endorsers in promot ional activities influence BI through their connections. Erdogan's (1999) systematic
examination of the role of EC in developing BI is consistent with these findings which includes elements like expertise,
trustworthiness, and attractiveness, can really influence the way consumers perceive a brand.
EC also reinforces BI and then PI ( Sedana & Mahyuni, 2024 ). This suggests that, from a managerial
perspective, endorser selection is a strategic brand -building decision, not a promotional tactic. The managerial
implication is that EC stimulates the PI and reinforces the overall BI. Marketing campaigns should not  only focus on
product promotion but also build positive brand associations through the endorser’s persona and communication
style. A strong, attractive, and premium brand image in the eyes of consumers, which is particularly important in the
skincare sector, where the perceived safety and effectiveness of the products are tightly linked to brand trust, can
be built by keeping alignment between the core brand values and the public perception of the endorser.

5.4 Perceived Risk on Brand Image
Perceived risk (PR) was not related to brand image (BI). The effect was statistically significant (β = 0.229,
p = 0.001), but the positive direction of the association is opposite to the conventional consumer behavior theory,
which usually suggests that higher perceived risk has a negative influence on consumers’ perception of a brand. This
result should be interpreted with caution and not as a direct indication of the effect of risk on brand image. There
are several possible explanations for this observation. In some categories of risk, they may have perceived them as
indications of product efficacy rather than sources of uncertainty. In skin care, products with active ingredients, like
retinol, exfoliating acids, or concentrated formulations, are usually r elated to higher efficiency but also to the
possibility of adverse effects. Some consumers may take any level of risk as evidence of a strong or technologically
advanced product.
Second, the outcome may generate a quality heuristic, where customers infer that products that need careful
use are of high quality and efficacy. If this is the correct interpretation, then perceived danger is not a deterrent but
a sign of exclusivity or of increased performance. Third, brand trust and safety assurance may conceptually overlap.
Proactive disclosure of potential side effects and transparency about ingredients and instructions for use by brands
can increase consumer awareness of risk and impr ove perceptions of trustworthiness and brand reliability. In these

5.5 Brand Image on Purchase Intention
BI is a significant variable on PI (β = 0.333; p = 0.001). Consumers are more likely to purchase a product
that has a positive reputation, a strong brand image, and clear uniqueness. The result also supports the proposition
that brand equity is an important element in the purchase decision, particularly in competitive segments like skincare
products. The present research extends prior research that demonstrated the effect of BI on consumers’ product
perceptions, attitudes, and PI. A strong BI can boost consumer trust, decrease PR, and make a purchase more likely.
The study emphasizes the necessity for practitioners to communicate consistently brand messages that are based
on safety, quality, and reliability, especially in markets where consumers may have dou bts about the suitability of
the product. Companies need to be consistent in their brand identity across all touchpoints, including product
packaging, visual design, digital communication, and customer service.

5.6 Endorser Credibility on Purchase Intention Through Brand Image
The mediation path analysis shows a significant mediation effect of BI between EC and PI (β = 0.195; p <
0.004). Thus, EC has a direct effect on PI by improving BI. This research aligns to the studies of Putri & Roostika
(2022) and Rosli et al. (2025) that investigated the mediating influence of BI on the connections of EC, PR, and PI.
Evi Novitasari and Nabila Kharimah Vedy (2022) concluded that BI mediates both relationships significantly,
strengthening the positive effect of EC and reducing the negative effect of PR on PI. This finding has managerial
implications that companies need to strategically manage the linkage between EC a nd BI development to maximise
PI. The finding indicates that brands need to integrate endorsers into long-term strategies rather than use them only
for short-term promotions. It is important for endorsers to understand the brand’s values in order to make t heir
messages compatible with the brand identity. A helpful and consistent BI would make the impact of the endorsers
much more effective on influencing the decision of consumers to make the purchase.

5.7 Perceived Risk on Purchase Intention through Brand Image
The mediation of BI on the relationship between PR and PI is also significant (β = 0.076; p = 0.009), and
the effect remains positive. Therefore, the idea that PR decreases PI through BI is not confirmed. This is a finding
that skincare companies should ta ke seriously, for the right brand positioning can minimise or even reverse PR. A
"risk" brand can still be highly appealing if it is perceived as effective, imaginative, or excellent. PR and PI are close
to BI.
This finding shows that PR has no direct impact on PI, but a successful company in improving its BI can
offset or even reverse the negative effect of risk perception. It is found that BI significantly mediates these
interactions, strengthening the positive effect of EC and weakening the negative effect of PR on PI (Novitasari et al.,
2022; Rosli et al., 2025). This study is important for management because BI can cancel and even reverse the
negative effect of PR. Thus, companies need to convey their brand message in a way that fosters trust and positive
quality impressions ( Monfort et al.,  2025). Practical measures include labelling products with clear ingredients,
evidence-based claims, expert endorsements and instructional information to help customers understand and
manage product-related hazards. In addition to the PR, it also has quality vi suals, credible science claims and an
educational communication strategy that helps buyers to feel more informed and confident about the product.

5.8 Theoretical Implications
Overall, the results suggest that brand reputation is a significant mediating variable that converts external
marketing stimuli into consumer buying behavior. The most dominant antecedent was the endorser's credibility,
which had a direct and indirect effe ct on buy intention, while risk perception only influenced purchasing decisions
through brand image creation. The findings suggest that trust, authenticity, and positive brand relationships are
more important than risk factors in the evaluation of skin car e products by consumers. Thus, organizations must
actively integrate authentic endorsement procedures with brand -building operations to enhance consumer
confidence and buying intention in increasingly competitive digital marketplaces.
This research found several major implication to the research on consumer behavior and endorsement. First,
it reconceptualizes brand image as a central psychological process that simultaneously mediates the impact of
positive endorser credibility and perce ived risk on purchase intention. The results show that brand image has a
strategic role in the customers’ choice -making with partial and full mediation effects, respectively. Second, the
research implies to the perceived risk research by indicating that perceived risk have no direct influence on the desire
to buy skincare products but an indirect effect through the formation of a brand image. These results challenge the
prevailing assumption that risk perception translates directly into purchase behavior.
Third, this research contributes facts from Indonesia as a rising market where the process behind skincare
purchase decisions might be changed due to increasing digitalization and influencer -led consumption trends. The
present findings extend previous theories by delineating the routes through which brand-related evaluations transfer
credibility and risk cues to ultimately impact consumer behavioral intentions.

5.9 Practical Contributions
The practical contributions for skincare companies, marketers, and practitioners of digital platforms are
crucial implication to the study. First, the results indicate that endorser credibility is an important strategic resource
that directly and indirectly increases consumers’ purchase intentions. Therefore, brands should prioritize authenticity,
expertise, and trustworthiness over popularity or follower count when selecting endorsers. Long -term relationships
with reputable sponsors are likely to foster sustainable trust among consumers and brand equity (Millán et al., 2026).
Second, the study found that risk perception does not deter intent to buy directly but affects consumers via
the formation of brand image. That means companies need to shift from reducing risk perceptions to building a
trustworthy brand image. Transparent ingredient information, safety certifications, scientific evidence, customer
testimonials, and clear product claims can help consumers convert risks into positive brand evaluations. Third, brand
image is an important mechanism between both endorser credibility and perceived risk and purchase intention; thus,
it is recommended that firms implement an integrated marketing strategy that includes influencer marketing, brand-
building activities, and risk communication simultaneously. Organizations should view these activities as strategically
aligned rather than disparate initiatives, and they should leverage them to enhance consumer confidence and
purchasing behavior.
The results are of particular interest to skincare companies operating in emerging digital markets such as
Indonesia, where consumers increasingly use social media information and influencer recommendations in purchase
decisions. For firms entering similar  emerging economies, it is important to recognize that brand management and
trust building may be more effective than risk reduction alone in influencing consumer purchase intentions.

6. Conclusion
The research used PLS-SEM to test the study framework in the cosmetics business. The results found that
endorser credibility had a significant relationship with purchase intention (β = 0.341, p = 0.000) and brand image
(β = 0.587, p = 0.001). When the consumer perceives the endorser as credible, their attitude toward the brand and
buy intentions are positive. However, perceived risk was not significantly associated with purchase intention (β =
0.052, p = 0.530), and was significantly and positively associat ed with brand image (β = 0.229, p = 0.001). This
implies a context -dependent association and needs to be interpreted with caution. Brand image had a positive
influence on purchase intention (β = 0.333, p = 0.001) and served as an important mediating mechan ism for
endorser credibility (β = 0.195, p = 0.004) and perceived risk (β = 0.076, p = 0.009). Model accounts for moderate
