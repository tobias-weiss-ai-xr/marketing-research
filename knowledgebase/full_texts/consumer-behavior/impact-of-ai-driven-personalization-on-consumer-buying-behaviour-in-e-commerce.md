ABSTRACT
The rapid development of Artificial Intelligenc e (AI) has significantly transformed the e -commerce sector
by enabling businesses to understand consumer preferences and provide highly personalized shopping experiences.
AI-driven personalization uses technologies such as machine learning, recommendation systems, predictive
analytics, chatbots, personalized advertisements, and customized product displays to match products and services
with individual consumer preferences. The present study examines the impact of AI -driven personalization on
consumer buying  behaviour in e -commerce. The study focuses on major dimensions of AI personalization,
including personalized product recommendations, customized promotional offers, personalized content, AI -
powered customer assistance, and predictive product suggestions. It further examines the influence of perceived
relevance, perceived usefulness, consumer trust, privacy concerns, and perceived intrusiveness on consumer
buying behaviour. The study proposes a conceptual framework based on the Stimulus –Organism–Response (S-O-
R) framework and Technology Acceptance Model (TAM). The paper argues that relevant and useful AI
personalization can reduce consumers' search effort, improve shopping convenience, enhance engagement, and
positively influence purchase intention. However,  excessive personalization, privacy concerns, lack of
transparency, and perceived manipulation may negatively affect consumer trust and buying behaviour. The study
provides implications for e -commerce businesses regarding responsible AI implementation, tra nsparency, privacy
protection, and consumer-centric personalization.
Keywords: Artificial Intelligence, AI -driven personalization, E -commerce, Consumer Behaviour, Purchase

1. Introduction
The rapid growth of digital technology has fundamentally changed the way businesses interact with
consumers. Among the most significant technological developments influencing contemporary business is
Artificial Intelligence (AI). AI enables businesses to a nalyse large volumes of data, identify patterns in consumer
behaviour, predict preferences, automate decision -making, and deliver personalized experiences. The e -commerce
industry has become one of the major sectors in which AI is extensively applied.
E-commerce platforms generate large quantities of consumer data through browsing behaviour, search history,
previous purchases, product reviews, clicks, wish -list activity, location, and interactions with digital platforms. AI
technologies can process these da ta and identify patterns that would be difficult to identify through traditional
marketing methods. As a result, e -commerce businesses can present consumers with products, advertisements,
offers, and content that are more closely aligned with their individual interests.
AI-driven personalization refers to the use of artificial intelligence to customize the online shopping experience
according to the characteristics, preferences, interests, and behaviour of individual consumers. Product
recommendation system s are one of the most visible applications. For example, an e -commerce platform may
recommend products based on a consumer's previous purchases or browsing behaviour. Similarly, AI can
customize search results, promotional messages, product rankings, email  communication, advertisements, and
discounts.
Personalization can provide several benefits to consumers. First, it can reduce the amount of time required to
search for products. Instead of examining hundreds of products, consumers can receive recommendati ons that are
relevant to their needs. Second, personalization can improve convenience by making the shopping process more
efficient. Third, personalized offers may increase the perceived value of products and services. Finally,
personalized recommendations may help consumers discover products that they might not otherwise consider.
However, AI-driven personalization also creates challenges. Consumers may become concerned about how their
personal data are collected, stored, analysed, and used. A recommendati on may sometimes appear too accurate or
intrusive, leading consumers to feel that their privacy has been compromised. This creates the personalization –
privacy paradox: consumers appreciate relevant personalization while simultaneously becoming concerned ab out
the amount of personal information required to provide it.
Consumer trust is therefore an important factor in the success of AI -driven personalization. If consumers believe
that an AI system provides useful and relevant recommendations and handles thei r information responsibly,
personalization may strengthen trust and purchase intention. Conversely, if consumers perceive AI
recommendations as manipulative, inaccurate, intrusive, or opaque, personalization may reduce trust.
Therefore, AI-driven personalization should not be viewed merely as a technological tool for increasing sales. It is
also a consumer -behaviour mechanism that can influence perceptions, attitudes, trust, decision -making, and
purchasing behaviour. The present paper examines these relatio nships and proposes a conceptual framework for
understanding the impact of AI-driven personalization on consumer buying behaviour in e-commerce.

2. Background of the Study
The traditional e -commerce model provides similar information to large groups of con sumers. However,
contemporary digital platforms increasingly provide individualized experiences. AI allows businesses to move
from mass marketing towards individualized marketing.
AI personalization can be observed in several areas of e -commerce. Personali zed product recommendations
analyse previous consumer activities and suggest products that may be relevant. Personalized search results can
rank products according to individual consumer preferences. Personalized advertisements can be based on
browsing pat terns, previous purchases, and interactions. AI can also support customized discounts, promotions,
chatbots, virtual assistants, and predictive shopping.
These applications demonstrate that AI has shifted personalization from a simple marketing technique t o an
integrated component of digital commerce.
3. Statement of the Problem
The growth of e -commerce has resulted in an increasingly competitive digital marketplace. Consumers are
exposed to a large number of products, brands, advertisements, and promotiona l messages. Although this variety
provides greater choice, it can also create information overload and increase the difficulty of selecting appropriate
products.
AI-driven personalization attempts to solve this problem by presenting consumers with relevant  products and
information. However, the effectiveness of personalization depends on how consumers perceive AI -generated
recommendations.
A highly relevant recommendation may reduce search effort and increase purchase intention. At the same time,
excessive personalization may create concerns about privacy and surveillance. Consumers may question how an e -
commerce platform knows their preferences and why particular products are being recommended.
The challenge for e -commerce businesses is therefore to identif y the appropriate balance between personalization
and privacy. The central research problem is to determine to what extent AI -driven personalization influences
consumer buying behaviour and how perceived relevance, usefulness, trust, and privacy concerns a ffect this
relationship.

6. Review of Literature
6.1 Artificial Intelligence in E-Commerce
Artificial Intelligence has become an important component of modern e -commerce. AI enables firms to analyse
customer data, automate interactions, predict demand, perso nalize recommendations, and improve marketing
effectiveness. Madanchian (2024) highlighted the importance of AI tools such as personalization engines,
chatbots, and predictive analytics in improving e-commerce performance.
6.2 AI-Driven Personalization
AI-driven personalization refers to the use of AI technologies to customize products, services, communications,
recommendations, and shopping experiences for individual consumers. Personalization can influence the
consumer's perception of relevance. When c onsumers receive recommendations that match their preferences, they
may perceive the platform as more useful and convenient.
6.3 Personalized Product Recommendations
Recommendation systems are among the most common AI applications in e -commerce. They analyse information
about consumers and products to predict which products are likely to be attractive. Personalized recommendation
systems can reduce search costs and help consumers make purchasing decisions more efficiently.
6.4 Perceived Relevance
Perceived relevance refers to the extent to which consumers believe that an AI -generated recommendation
matches their needs, preferences, or interests. A recommendation that is perceived as relevant can increase the
usefulness of the shopping platform and encourage product exploration.
6.5 Perceived Usefulness
Perceived usefulness refers to the extent to which consumers believe that AI personalization improves their
shopping experience. AI recommendations may save shopping time, identify suitable products, simplify d ecision-
making, and improve convenience. Davis (1989) identified perceived usefulness as an important determinant of
technology acceptance.
6.6 Consumer Trust

           October 2026                                IJCMTH                               DOI :  10.67851/ijcmth.vol.1.issue.3.001                                       Page 5
Trust is particularly important in AI-based e-commerce because consumers must provide personal information and
rely on algorithmic recommendations. Trust may develop when AI systems are perceived as accurate, transparent,
reliable, secure, fair, and beneficial.
6.7 Privacy Concerns
AI personalization requires consumer data. These data may include brow sing history, purchase history, location,
preferences, and interactions. Consumers may therefore be concerned about unauthorized use of personal
information, excessive data collection, data sharing, security breaches, behavioural tracking, and lack of control.
6.8 Perceived Intrusiveness
Personalization becomes problematic when consumers feel that an e -commerce platform knows too much about
them. Repeated advertisements or highly targeted recommendations may be perceived as intrusive. Such
perceptions can reduce trust and purchase intention.
7. Theoretical Framework
7.1 Stimulus–Organism–Response (S-O-R) Framework
The S-O-R framework explains how external stimuli influence internal psychological states, which subsequently
lead to behavioural responses.
In the present study, AI-driven personalization represents the stimulus. Perceived relevance, perceived usefulness,
consumer trust, perceived value, and privacy concern represent the organism. Purchase intention and consumer
buying behaviour represent the response.
7.2 Technology Acceptance Model
The Technology Acceptance Model developed by Davis (1989) suggests that perceived usefulness and perceived
ease of use influence technology acceptance. Applied to e -commerce, consumers may accept AI personalization
when they believe it is useful and easy to interact with.

14. Expected Findings and Discussion
The study expects AI-driven personalization to have a positive relationship with consumer buying behaviour when
consumers perceive recommendations as relevant, useful, and trustworthy. Personalized product recommendations
can reduce search time and effort, potentially increasing product exploration and purchase intention.
However, personalization should not be assumed to have universally positive effects. When recommendations
become excessively intrusive, consumers may experience d iscomfort. Privacy concerns can also reduce trust.
Therefore, the quality, relevance, transparency, and ethical use of personalization are likely to be more important
than simply increasing the amount of personalization.
The proposed relationship can be re presented as: Effective Personalization → Relevance → Usefulness → Trust
→ Purchase Intention, while Excessive Personalization → Intrusiveness → Privacy Concern → Reduced Trust →
Lower Purchase Intention.
15. Managerial Implications
E-commerce businesses s hould improve recommendation accuracy, provide consumers with control over
personalized recommendations, explain why products are recommended, protect consumer data, avoid excessive
personalization, improve AI -based customer service, and build trust throug h transparent and responsible AI
practices.
16. Theoretical Implications
The study contributes to the literature by integrating AI personalization with consumer behaviour. It applies the
Technology Acceptance Model to AI -driven personalization and uses the  Stimulus–Organism–Response
framework to explain how AI personalization influences internal consumer perceptions and behavioural outcomes.
The inclusion of privacy concern recognizes that personalization can generate both positive and negative consumer
responses.

17. Challenges of AI-Driven Personalization
Key challenges include data privacy, algorithmic bias, lack of transparency, over -personalization, data security,
consumer dependence on algorithms, and manipulation concerns. Responsible AI requires tran sparency, fairness,
security, and meaningful consumer control.
18. Future Scope of the Study
Future studies can compare Generation Z, Millennials, and Generation X; compare major e -commerce platforms;
examine different product categories; and study emergin g technologies such as generative AI, AI shopping
assistants, virtual try-on, conversational commerce, predictive shopping, and autonomous shopping agents.
19. Conclusion
Artificial Intelligence is transforming e -commerce by enabling businesses to provide increasingly personalized
shopping experiences. Personalized product recommendations, customized promotions, personalized content,
predictive analytics, and AI -powered customer assistance can increase relevance and convenience. When
consumers perceive pers onalization as useful and trustworthy, it can positively influence purchase intention and
buying behaviour.
However, personalization also creates challenges. Privacy concerns, perceived intrusiveness, lack of transparency,
algorithmic bias, and concerns ab out manipulation may reduce consumer trust. Successful AI personalization
should therefore be consumer -centric rather than technology -centric. E -commerce companies should provide
relevant recommendations while maintaining transparency, privacy, security, and consumer control.
The proposed framework suggests that AI -driven personalization influences consumer buying behaviour through
perceived relevance, perceived usefulness, and consumer trust, while privacy concerns and perceived intrusiveness
may weaken th ese positive effects. Future empirical research using primary data can validate these relationships
through correlation, regression, or PLS-SEM analysis.
