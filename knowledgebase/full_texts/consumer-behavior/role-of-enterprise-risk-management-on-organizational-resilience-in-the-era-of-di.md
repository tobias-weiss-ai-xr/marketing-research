ABSTRACT

Digital transformation has changed the way organizations conduct operations, make
strategic decisions, and create business value. On the other hand, digital transformation has
also increased the complexity of risks faced by companies, such as cyber risks, operational
disruptions, changes in consumer behavior, and uncertain business environments. In facing
these challenges, Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) is a crucial approach to ensure
organizations are able to identify, manage, and respond to risks in an integrated manner. This
study aims to analyze the role of Enterprise Risk Management on organizational resilience
in the era of digital transformation using a case study of PT Unilever  Indonesia Tbk. This
research employed a qualitative approach with a case study method through document
analysis of the annual report, sustainability report, and corporate governance report of PT
Unilever Indonesia Tbk for the 2020–2024 period. The analysis was conducted based on the
five components of the 2017 COSO Enterprise Risk Management Framework: Governance
and Culture, Strategy and Objective Setting, Performance, Review and Revision, and
Information, Communication, and Reporting. The results indicate that ERM implementation
plays a role in strengthening organizational resilience by improving risk identification
capabilities, improving adaptive decision-making, strengthening governance, and enhancing
organizational capacity to address business change and disruption. Furthermore, the
integration of risk management with corporate strategy supports operational continuity and
the achievement of organizational goals amidst the dynamics of digital transformation. This
research provides theoretical contributi ons to the development of Enterprise Risk
Management and organizational resilience studies, as well as practical implications for
companies striving to build more resilient and sustainable organizations in the digital era.

Keywords: Enterprise Risk Management, Organizational Resilience, Digital

THEORETICAL BASIS

Enterprise Risk Management (ERM)
Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) is an integrated and systematic risk management
approach to identify, assess, respond to, monitor, and control various risks that can impact
the achievement of organizational goals. Unlike traditional risk management approaches that
manage risks separately across each organizational function (silo -based risk management),
ERM integrates the risk management process into all organizational activities so that every
strategic decision considers the balance between risks and opportunities (COSO, 2017).
The Committee of Sponsoring Organizations of the Treadway Commission (COSO,
2017) defines ERM as a process influenced by the board of directors, management, and all
organizational personnel, applied in the formulation of strategies and implementation of
company activities to identify potential events that could impact the organization and
manage risks within a tolerance level (risk appetite) to provide reasonable assurance in
achieving organizational goals. This definition demonstrates that ERM functions not only as
a risk control tool but also as an integral part of the organization's value creation, value
preservation, and value enhancement processes.
In addition to the COSO framework, the international standard ISO 31000:2018
explains that risk management is a coordinated activity to direct and control an
organization's risk management. ISO 31000 emphasizes that risk management
implementation must be i ntegrated with organizational governance, decision -making
processes, organizational culture, and strategic planning to create and protect organizational
value (International Organization for Standardization [ISO], 2018). Therefore, both COSO
and ISO 31000 share the same goal of improving risk management effectiveness. Although
COSO is more oriented toward achieving a company's strategic objectives, ISO 31000
emphasizes universal risk management principles and processes.
Over time, COSO updated its Enterprise Risk Management framework through
Enterprise Risk Management: Integrating with Strategy and Performance (2017). This
update emphasized that risk management must be an integral part of corporate strategy
development, d ecision-making, performance management, and long -term value creation.
This framework consists of five main, interconnected components: Governance and Culture,
Strategy and Objective Setting, Performance, Review and Revision, and Information,
Communication, and Reporting. These five components illustrate a continuous risk
management cycle that supports the achievement of organizational goals.
Governance and Culture emphasizes the importance of corporate governance,
leadership, integrity, and a risk -aware culture in building an effective risk management

recover from various disruptions without losing its ability to achieve its strategic goals. In a

resilient organization is not only able to survive crises but also able to utilize change as an

2021).
The concept of organizational resilience evolved from the engineering resilience
approach, which emphasized the system's ability to bounce back after experiencing
disruption, and then evolved into a concept that views resilience as a dynamic capability.
From this perspective, organizations are not only required to recover from disruptions but
also to learn, adapt, and transform to remain competitive amidst environmental change
(Lengnick-Hall et al., 2011).
Duchek (2020) explains that organizational resilience is a dynamic capability built
through organizational learning processes, resource management, and the ability to adapt to
change. The model consists of three main dimensions: anticipation, coping, and adaptation.
Anticipation is an organization's ability to identify, predict, and prepare for various risks
through risk identification, environmental monitoring, and contingency planning. Coping
describes an organization's ability to respond to and manage th e impact of disruptions
through resource coordination, effective communication, and rapid decision -making.
Meanwhile, adaptation is an organization's ability to learn from experience, evaluate past
processes, and adjust strategies, business processes, and technology utilization to better
prepare for future changes.

Digital Transformation
Digital transformation is a process of comprehensive organizational change through
the use of digital technology to improve operational efficiency, innovation, decision-making,
and value creation for customers and stakeholders. Digital transformation is not only related
to the adoption of new technologies, but also encompasses changes in business strategy,
operational processes, organizational culture, and business models that enable companies to
adapt to the dynamics of the business environment. Vial (2019 ) explains that digital
transformation is a process aimed at improving organizational performance through a
combination of digital technology utilization, business process changes, and continuous
organizational capability development.
Technological developments such as artificial intelligence (AI), big data analytics,
cloud computing, the Internet of Things (IoT), and machine learning have driven
organizations to accelerate digital transformation. According to Verhoef et al. (2021), digital
transformation is a multidisciplinary phenomenon that integrates aspects of technology,

Despite these benefits, digital transformation also increases the complexity of
organizational risks. Dependence on information technology exposes companies to various

The Relationship Between Enterprise Risk Management and Organizational
Resilience
Enterprise Risk Management and organizational resilience are two interrelated
concepts that support organizational sustainability. Implementing ERM enables
organizations to identify risks early, evaluate their impact on organizational goals, and
develop appropriate mitigation strategies. Through this process, organizations can increase
their preparedness in the face of various forms of uncertainty.
Fietz et al. (2021) explain that organizations with good risk governance tend to have a
greater ability to cope with environmental changes and operational disruptions. Therefore,
ERM functions not only as a risk control tool but also as a mechanism that supports building
organizational resilience.
In the context of digital transformation, ERM plays a role in helping organizations
manage various digital risks that arise from technological changes and the dynamics of the
business environment. Therefore, effective ERM implementation is believed to stre ngthen
an organization's ability to anticipate, face, and adapt to the various challenges that arise in
the era of digital transformation.

Reseach Gap
Based on previous research, it can be seen that studies on Enterprise Risk
Management, organizational resilience, and digital transformation are generally still
conducted separately. Research on ERM primarily examines its impact on company
performance, governance, or company value, while research on organizational resilience
focuses on an organization's ability to face crises and environmental changes. On the other
hand, research on digital transformation focuses more on technology adoption and business
model innovation. Therefore, studies that integrate the implementation of Enterprise Risk
Management based on the COSO Enterprise Risk Management Framework 2017 to explain
organizational resilience in the context of digital transformation, particularly throug h case
studies of consumer goods companies in Indonesia, are still relatively limited. This gap
forms the basis and primary contribution of this research.

METODE PENELITIAN

This research uses a qualitative approach with a case study method to analyze the role
of Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) in organizational resilience in the era of digital
transformation. The research object is PT Unilever Indonesia Tbk.
The data used is secondary data obtained from the Annual Report, Sustainability
Report, and corporate governance report of PT Unilever Indonesia Tbk for the 2020 –2024
period. Data collection was conducted through a documentary study of information related
to risk management implementation, digital transformation, and corporate strategy.
The data analysis technique used content analysis with reference to the five COSO
ERM components: Governance and Culture, Strategy and Objective Setting, Performance,
Review and Revision, and Information, Communication, and Reporting. The analysis results
are then interpreted based on the dimensions of organizational resilience, which include
anticipation, coping, and adaptation.
To enhance the credibility of the research results, source triangulation was conducted
by comparing information obtained from company reports, official documents, and relevant
scientific literature. This approach aims to increase consistency of interpretation and ensure
that the analysis results are supported by multiple, credible data sources.

CONCLUSION

This study examined the role of Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) in strengthening
organizational resilience in the era of digital transformation through a case study of PT
Unilever Indonesia Tbk. The findings indicate that the company has consistently
implemented the five components of the COSO Enterprise Risk Management Framework
(2017), namely Governance and Culture, Strategy and Objective Setting, Performance,
Review and Revision, and Information, Communication and Reporting. The integration of
these components into corporate governance, strategic planning, operational processes, and
performance evaluation demonstrates that ERM has become an integral part of the
company's management system rather than merely a risk control mechanism.
The study further reveals that the implementation of ERM contributes significantly to
organizational resilience by enhancing the organization's capabilities of anticipation, coping,
and adaptation. Through systematic risk identification, continuous monitoring, and adaptive

Research Limitations
This study has several limitations. First, the analysis relied solely on secondary data
obtained from the Annual Reports, Sustainability Reports, and other publicly available
corporate documents of PT Unilever Indonesia Tbk. Consequently, the findings are limited
to the information disclosed by the company and may not fully capture the actual
implementation of Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) practices. Second, this research
employed a single-case study approach focusing on PT Unilever Indonesia Tbk; theref ore,
the findings cannot be generalized to other companies or industry sectors with different
organizational characteristics. Third, the study adopted a qualitative descriptive approach,
which provides an in-depth understanding of ERM implementation but does not empirically
measure the causal relationship between Enterprise Risk Management and organizational
resilience.
Future research is recommended to incorporate primary data through interviews or
surveys involving company management and risk management practitioners, enabling a
more comprehensive understanding of ERM implementation. In addition, future studies may
adopt quantitative or mixed -method approaches to examine the effect of Enterprise Risk
Management on organizational resilience across different industries, thereby improving the
generalizability and robustness of the findings.

This study provides both theoretical and practical implications. Theoretically, the

supports the COSO Enterprise Risk Management Framework (2017) by showing that the
integration of risk governance, strategic planning, performance management, and
organizational communication strengthens the dimensions of organizational resilience,
namely anticipation, coping, and adaptation.
