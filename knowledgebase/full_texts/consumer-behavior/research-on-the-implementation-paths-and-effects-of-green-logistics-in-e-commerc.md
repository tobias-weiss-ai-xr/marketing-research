  Abstract
Under the "Dual Carbon" goals, the rapid growth of e -commerce logistics has
intensified environmental pressures, making green transformation an urgent
priority. This paper systematically investigates the implementation paths and
effects of green logistics in e -commerce enterprises through a case study of JD
Logistics. Employing literature review, case study, and comparative analysis
methods, the study examines JD Logistics' green practices across four
dimensions—green packaging, green transportation, green warehousing, and
reverse logistics —and evaluates their effects based on corporate data. The
findings show that JD Logistics has achieved notable environmental benefits,
enhanced brand image, and gradually  realized cost advantages. However,
challenges persist, including cost -benefit imbalance, insufficient supply chain
coordination, technological bottlenecks, and consumer behavioral inertia.
Countermeasures are proposed from enterprise, industry, and govern ment levels.
This study enriches green logistics research in the e -commerce context and
provides practical references for enterprises' green transformation.

1. Introduction
The Implementation Plan for Science and Technology Supporting the Goals of Carbon
Peaking and Carbon Neutrality (2022–2030) states that China aims to achieve carbon
peaking before 2030 [1]. The logistics industry is a strategic tertiary industry
underpinning national economic development, serving as a critical bridge connecting
production, distribution, and consumption [2]. In 2023, China's e -commerce
transaction volume reached 43.8 trillion yuan, accompanied by rapid logistics
expansion. However, this growth has imposed considerable environmental pressure:

substantial carbon emissions, and warehousing energy consumption demands urgent
attention [3]. Against this backdrop, how e-commerce enterprises can advance green
logistics to achieve synergistic economic and environmental benefits has become a

The concept of green logistics originated in the 1990s, focusing on reducing
environmental impacts and ach ieving efficient resource utilization. Scholars have
integrated circular economy, corporate social responsibility, and green supply chain
management theories into green logistics research, forming a "full -life-cycle
greening" framework. Following the "Dual  Carbon" goals, research has shifted
toward carbon accounting, low -carbon technologies, and policy incentives, yet
systematic case studies on e-commerce enterprises remain insufficient.
Theoretical significance: This study constructs a "paths–effects–problems" analytical
framework, enriching green logistics research in the e- commerce context. Practical
significance: It extracts replicable experiential models, offering references for small
and medium -sized e -commerce enterprises and contributing to the "Dual  Carbon"
goals.
This paper's structure is as follows: First, it reviews relevant theories; second, it
analyzes JD Logistics' green practices across four dimensions; third, it evaluates
implementation effects; fourth, it identifies problems and causes; fifth, it proposes
countermeasures from three levels.
Research Methods:  1) Literature review method —reviewing theoretical
achievements and practical experiences ; 2) Case study method —selecting JD
Logistics for its representativeness ; and 3) Comparative analysi s
method—comparing  JD Logistics' carbon emissions and operational costs before
and after its green transformation to evaluate the effects, and benchmarks domestic
and international green logistics practices to identify gaps and improvement
directions for Chinese e-commerce enterprises..
2. Theoretical Foundations
2.1. Green Logistics: Connotation and System
Green logistics refers to a logistics model that reduces resource consumption and
environmental pollution throughout the entire logistics process, achi eving
coordinated economic, environmental, and social benefits [4 -5]. Its core objectives
are resource conservation, environmental friendliness, and improved resource
utilization efficiency. The logistics industry's green transformation faces multiple
challenges [6].
The green logistics system comprises four key components:  green packaging—using
recyclable materials to achieve reduction and harmlessness;  green
transportation—reducing carbon emissions through route optimization, new energy
vehicles, and multimodal transport; green warehousing—employing energy-efficient

equipment and optimizing layouts to reduce energy consumption; and  reverse
logistics—establishing recovery systems for products and packaging waste to enable
resource recycling.
2.2. The "Dual Carbon" Strategy and Corporate Environmental
Responsibility
The "Dual Carbon" goals are a major strategic decision based on China's commitment
to sustainable development. "Carbon peaking" refers to emissions reaching their
peak and then declining; "carbon neutrality" means achieving net -zero emissions
through afforestation and emission reduction. As a key pillar of the national economy,
the logistics industry has significant environmental impacts, and its sustainable
development is crucial [7]. Enterprises must not only comply with regulations but
also proactively assume green transformation responsibilities.
2.3. Environmental Challenges of E-Commerce Logistics
E-commerce logistics faces four major environmental challenges: 1) excessive
packaging—multi-layer methods causing resource waste , 2) high transportation
emissions—road transport dominance with high empty -load rates , 3) high
warehousing energy consumption— large centers requiring substantial lighting, air
conditioning, and sorting e quipment , and 4) packaging waste recovery —single-use
packaging and low consumer recycling awareness leading to environmental
pollution.
3. Case Study: JD Logistics' Green Practice
3.1 Overview and Development Drivers
JD Logistics, established in 2007, is a leading integrated supply chain logistics service
provider in China. By end -2023, it operated over 1,500 warehouses (30 million m²)
and possessed more than 200,000 delivery vehicles, building a nationwide network
serving e-commerce, FMCG, home appliances, and apparel.
Drivers for green development:  1) Policy drivers—"Dual Carbon" goals and express
packaging policies impose clear requirements , 2) Market drivers —green
consumption trends enhance consumer identification and loyalty , 3) Economic
drivers—long-term cost reduction through reusable packaging and route
optimization, and 4)  Strategic drivers —JD.com's sustainability strategy positions
green logistics as a core business priority.
3.2. Implementation Paths
3.2.1. Green Packaging
JD Logistics launched the "Green Stream Initiative" promoting reduction, recyclability,
and harmlessness. Specific measures include: "slimming tape" to reduce usage;
electronic waybills saving over 200,000 tons of paper annually; "origi nal packaging"

collaboration with suppliers to reduce secondary packaging. The "Green Stream Box,"
a reusable plastic box with a per-use cost one-third that of cartons and over 100 uses,
significantly reduces waste. Reusable insulated boxes replace foam for fresh food
delivery. The company is also phasing out non -degradable plastics and promoting
biodegradable alternatives through industry collaboration [8].
3.2.2. Green Transportation
JD Logistics builds a green transportation system through: 1)  New energy
vehicles—primarily for urban delivery, reducing fuel consumption by over 100,000
tons and CO₂ emissions by over 300,000 tons annually, with hydrogen vehicles
explored for line -haul, 2) Route optimization —using big data and AI to optimize
delivery routes in real time, reducing empty -load rates and mileage , and
3) Multimodal transport—promoting rail-road and sea -road intermodal models to
increase low-carbon transport share.
3.2.3. Green Warehousing
JD Logistics' "Asia No.1" intelligent logistics center employs smart lighting (30%
energy saving), intelligent temperature control, and automated sorting. Rooftop
photovoltaic systems across multiple centers generate clean energy for warehouse
operations, achieving self -sufficiency. IoT -based intelligent management  optimizes
inventory layouts and reduces handling frequency [9].
3.2.4. Reverse Logistics and Recovery Systems
JD Logistics has set up packaging recycling bins at delivery stations nationwide and
launched a points -based recycling incentive program. It also  conducts recycling of
used clothing, old appliances, and mobile phones in collaboration with charitable
organizations [10].
3.3. Implementation Effects
 Environmental benefits: By end -2023, through the Green Stream Initiative, JD
Logistics reduced packaging material use by over 800,000 tons and tape use by
over 3 billion meters; new energy vehicles and route optimization reduced CO₂
emissions by over 1.5 million tons; photovoltaic projects generated over 1.5
billion kWh.
 Brand image enhancement: Over 70% of  consumers identify with JD Logistics'
green practices and prefer its services.
 Cost advantages:  Green Stream Boxes save over 1 billion yuan annually in
packaging costs; photovoltaic projects save over 300 million yuan annually in
electricity expenses.

4.1. Existing Problems
4.1.1. Cost-Benefit Imbalance
The substantial upfront capital required for reusable packaging— spanning R&D,
deployment, sanitation, and warehousing —combined with the high procurement
costs of new energy vehicles and the necessary charging infrastructure, places
significant strain on the overall cost-benefit equilibrium.
4.1.2. Insufficient Supply Chain Coordination
Upstream suppliers still use excessive packaging despite the "original packaging"
concept; downstream consumers lack green awareness, consider reusable boxes
unsanitary, and show low recycling participation. Limited collaboration with other
logistics enterprises prevents industry-wide synergy.
4.1.3. Technological and Standardization Bottlenecks
New energy vehicles have limited range and long charging times; biodegradable
materials lack sufficient strength and water resistance. China's green logistics
standards for packaging dimensions, recycling procedures, and carbon accounting
remain ununified, hindering large-scale promotion.
4.1.4. Consumer Awareness and Behavioral Inertia
Participation is further undermined by consumer- side challenges: insufficient
knowledge about green logistics, reluctance to absorb potential price hikes, cultural
or habitual preferences for decorative packaging, and the physical inconvenience of
under-serviced recycling stations.
4.2. Cause Analysis
External causes:1) The incentive framework remains underpowered,  as existing
fiscal and tax instruments have yet to reach a threshold that effectively stimulates
investment, 2) The physical infrastructure, particularly for charging and packaging
collection, suffers from both quantitative deficits and qualitative disparities across
regions, and 3) The regulatory environment is characterized by fragmentation,  with
no binding consensus on carbon accounting protocols, recyclability criteria, or NEV
performance benchmarks —a lacuna that precludes any coordinated, scalable
implementation.
Internal causes: 1)Departmental misalignment, wh erein procurement's cost -cutting
priorities override sustainability requirements , and 2) underinvestment in green
technology R&D, with JD Logistics' innovation spending (under 1% of revenue)
falling significantly short of the 5% industry benchmark set by international leaders.
5. Countermeasures and Suggestions
5.1. Enterprise Level

 Build a strategic green supply chain management system —integrate green
development into corporate strategy, set clear carbon reduction and recycling
targets, strengthen upstream /downstream collaboration, and establish
cross-departmental working groups.
 Increase green technology innovation investment —intensify R&D in
biodegradable materials and low- carbon technologies, and strengthen
university-industry collaboration.
 Design consumer incentive mechanisms—introduce green consumption points
systems, strengthen public education via social media, and improve recycling
convenience with community collection points and door-to-door services.
5.2. Industry Level
 Establish green logistics s tandards—unified standards for green packaging,
carbon accounting, and packaging recycling to provide regulatory guidance.
 Build shared reverse logistics platforms—integrate industry recycling resources,
establish rental platforms for reusable packaging, and create
information-sharing platforms for experience exchange.
 Strengthen industry self -regulation and green certification —formulate
self-discipline conventions, establish green logistics enterprise certification, and
strengthen supervision.
5.3. Government Level
 Improve incentive -constraint balance —increase fiscal subsidies for green
technologies, implement tax incentives for green logistics enterprises, and
impose environmental taxes on excessive packaging and high carbon emissions.
 Strengthen education and talent development—integrate green logistics into the
education system, promote public awareness, and support universities in
offering green logistics programs to cultivate interdisciplinary talent.
6. Conclusions and Outlook
6.1. Main Conclusions
 Green logistics implementation paths for e -commerce enterprises should cover
green packaging, green transportation, green warehousing, and reverse logistics,
achieving full -process greening through technological and managerial
innovation.
 JD Logistics has developed a comprehensive green logistics framework
anchored in four pillars: the Green Stream Initiative, new energy vehicles,
photovoltaic power generation, and packaging recycling. This systematic
approach has not only generated tangible environmental and economic returns,
but also enhanced corporate reputation, offering a referenceable pathway for
peers pursuing similar transitions.

6.2. Research Outlook
This study is confined to JD Logistics and does not encompass SMEs; consequently,
