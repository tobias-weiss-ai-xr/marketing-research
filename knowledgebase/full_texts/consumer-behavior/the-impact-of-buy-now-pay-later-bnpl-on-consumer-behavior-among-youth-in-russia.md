ABSTRACT

Buy

Now,

Pay

Later

(BNPL)

services

allow

consumers

to

receive

goods

immediately

while

dividing

payment

into

smaller

installments.

Although

BNPL

is

often

presented

as

a

convenient

payment

option,

concerns

have

emerged

about

its

effects

on

spending

behavior

and

financial

discipline,

particularly

among

young

consumers

with

limited

financial

experience.

This

exploratory

study

examines

the

relationship

between

BNPL

use

and

consumer

behavior

among

young

people

in

Russia,

focusing

on

impulse

purchasing,

perceived

affordability,

budgeting

discipline,

and

financial

stress.

It

combines

an

international

literature

review,

an

analysis

of

Russia’s

emerging

and

newly

regulated

BNPL

market,

and

an

online

survey

of

150

respondents

aged

14–25.

Awareness

of

BNPL

was

high:

99%

of

respondents

were

familiar

with

the

service,

while

15%

had

used

it.

Among

BNPL

users,

more

than

60%

reported

making

unplanned

purchases

at

least

sometimes,

80%

agreed

that

BNPL

encouraged

them

to

select

more

expensive

products,

and

47%

had

forgotten

a

future

payment

at

least

once.

Only

22%

of

the

full

sample

owned

a

credit

card,

suggesting

that

BNPL

may

play

a

credit-like

role

for

some

young

consumers.

The

findings

indicate

that

BNPL

can

provide

payment

flexibility

while

reducing

the

perceived

importance

of

the

total

purchase

cost

and

creating

challenges

for

financial

planning.

Because

BNPL

adoption

in

Russia

remains

limited,

clearer

disclosures,

stronger

payment

reminders,

financial

literacy

initiatives,

and

tools

showing

total

outstanding

obligations

could

help

mitigate

consumer

risks.

The

results

should

be

interpreted

as

exploratory

because

the

subgroup

of

BNPL

users

was

small.

Because

Research
