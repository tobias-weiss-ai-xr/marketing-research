 Abstract
The rapid expansion of Financial Te chnology has
transformed the way consumers conduct financial
transactions and make purchasing decisions in the digital
economy. This study aims to examine the effect of Financial
Technology on consumer spending behavior by investigating
how digital financi al services influence consumers'
purchasing activities. A quantitative research approach was
employed using a cross -sectional survey of active Financial
Technology users. The collected data were analyzed using
Structural Equation Modeling to evaluate the m easurement
model and examine the structural relationship between
Financial Technology and consumer spending behavior. The
findings indicate that Financial Technology exerts a
significant positive influence on consumer spending behavior
by improving transac tion efficiency, convenience,
accessibility, and consumer confidence. The proposed model
also demonstrates satisfactory reliability, validity,
explanatory power, and predictive capability, confirming its
suitability for e xplaining consumer behavior within digital
financial environments. These findings contribute to the
growing literature on digital consumer behavior while
providing practical insights for policymakers, financial
institutions, FinTech providers, and business  organizations
seeking to promote sustainable digital financial ecosystems
and enhance consumer experiences in the digital economy.
INTRODUCTION
The rapid advancement of digital technology has significantly transformed the global
financial landscape, crea ting new opportunities for individu als and businesses to
perform financial transactions through innovative digital platforms. Financial
Technology (FinTech) has emerged as one of the most influential innovations in the
financial services sector by integrat ing information technology with fin ancial
products to deliver faster, more accessible, and more efficient services. Mobile
banking, electronic wallets, peer -to-peer payment systems, digital lending,
investment applications, and QR code-based payment services have become integral
components of everyday economic activities  (Zakariya & Arifin, 2025; Vermani &
Arora, 2026) . As digital financial ecosystems continue to expand, consumers
increasingly rely on FinTech applications not only to facilitate transactions but also

The growing adoption of FinTech has substantially altered consumer spending
behavior in both developed and emerging  economies. Digital payment platforms
simplify purchasing processes by reducing transaction costs, minimizing payment
delays, and enabling instant financial transfers (Kantheti & Bvuma, 2024; Minarni,
2025; Deshpande, 2025; Sundar et al., 2026). Consumers are now able to complete
purchases within seconds using smartphones or other digital devices without relying
on physical cash (Bakar et al., 2025; Faraz & Anjum, 2025). This convenience has
accelerated the transition toward digital consumption, where financial transactions
are seamlessly integrated wit h e -commerce, online services, and physical retail
environments. Consequently, FinTech has become a fundamental driver of changes
in purchasing patterns, consumption frequency, and overall consumer decision -
making (Hakobjanyan et al., 2026; Satpute & Shukla, 2026).
The digital economy has also heightened the relevance of Financial Technology by
introducing financial services, digital commerce, artificial intelligence, and data -
driven marketing strategies (Malempati, 2022; Pazouki et al., 202 5; Mahmood &
Miah, 2025). Always in these ecosystems, they are inundated with personalized
promotions, advertisements, digital loyalty programs and cashback offers which
push them into buying more. Being able to access these features has shifted the
nature of the purchasing process from a traditional decision -making process to a
technology-enabled experience that is fast, personalized and convenient (Elgammal
et a., 2026; Wan et al., 2025; Theodore Armand et al., 2024). As a consequence,
digital financial p latforms drive the decision -making process as well as traditional
payment mechanisms.
The growth of FinTech has been especially pronounced in emerging economy
countries, where digital financial inclusion has been boosted by rising internet use,
high smartphone penetration and positive government policies. Over the last ten
years, countries like India, China, Singapore and Indonesia have seen significant
growth in the number of digital payment transactions and the use of electronic
wallets. (Putrevu & Mertzanis, 2024; Trianto et al., 2025; Widjaja, 2025; Susanto et
al., 2022). These countries are seen using digital financial services routinely in a
variety of sectors, such as transportation, food delivery, retail, healthcare, education,
and entertainment (Shaikh et al., 2023; Farinloye et al., 2024; Kowsar et al., 2025).
The ubiquity of the use of FinTech in everyday life highlights that digital financial
innovation is not just a means of technological progress but also a key part of
consumer spending habits.
Financial Technology has expanded significantly but there is still limited knowledge
of the behavioral impacts of this technology. The majority of the previous studies
have been focused on the explanation of the determinants of FinTech adoption
through the theory of Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), the Unified Theory of
Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT), or the Diffusion of Innovation Theory
(DoIT). A number of these studies highlight perceived usefulness, ease of use, trust,
security, perceived ri sk, and social influence as key factors that influence the
adoption of the technology. While these frameworks provide good explanations of
consumers' initial intentions to use digital financial services, the frameworks offer
relatively little insight into the impact of ongoing use of FinTech on actual spending
behaviour once the technology has been used.
In addition, there is mixed evidence on the effects of Financial Technology on
consumer spending behaviour. There are multiple studies that show that electronic
payment methods make users spend more and tend to buy on impulse, since the
payment itself is less perceived when it occurs electronically than when it is done in

Yet another problem in the previous literature is the conceptualization of Financial
Technology. Previous research has focused on FinTech as a technological advance
and is restricted to examining the FinTech product as a payment tool and not as part
of the digital economy. Today's FinTech platforms are multi -faceted platforms that
integrate payment services, financial management, consumer analyti cs, digital
marketing, and personalised recommendations (Baral, 2026; Pazouki et al., 2025;
Jawad et al., 2025). Thus, the adoption of digital payment services is not the only
factor that affects spending behaviours, but consumers' engagement with multiple
technological characteristics of payment platforms as well. The point of view of this
broader perspective has been relatively little studied in the empirical work so far.
The majority of the research currently conducted has focused on certain  digital
payments apps or specific sample populations, making it hard to draw broader
inferences. However, variations in digital literacy, financial inclusion, financial
status, consumer buying patterns, and technological set up between countries could
significantly affect consumers' responses to Financial Technology (Financial
Technology or Tulcanaza) (Tulcanaza et al., 2025; Ben Belgacem et al., 2024; Hasan,
2024). Thus, more specific research is needed to gain a deeper understanding of
consumers' spending habits in increasingly digital financial contexts.
To fill the identified gaps, the current study proposes an integrated framework which
explores the impact of Financial Technology on consumer's spending behavior in the
digital economy. This paper does not just examine consumers' intentions to use
digital financial services, but also how the ongoing relationship with FinTech
platforms impacts the number of purchases, decisions on spending, and consumers'
consumption behaviors. The framework takes into account consumer behaviour,
financial technology and the literature of the digital economy, and seeks to illustrate
how the mechanisms of consumer beh aviour are affected by technological
innovation, thereby shaping consumer expenditure (Barkas, 2024; Singh et al.,
2024).
There are three significant advantages to this research which are reflected in the
novelty of this research. First, this study brings the focus of analysis from technology
adoption to the behavioural impact of the continuous use of FinTech. Second, it
defines Financial Technology as a multi -faceted ecosystem that includes digital
payment services, financial accessibility, transaction eff iciency and technology -
driven consumer experiences. Third, the study offers empirical data on the latest
developments in consumer spending patterns in the evolving digital economy, where
technological innovation still drives financial transactions and mark etplace
interactions.
Therefore, the purpose of this study is to analyze the influence of Financial
Technology on the consumer spending behavior in the digital economy. The results
are anticipated to contribute to theory debates on digital consumer behavio ur, and
will also inform policy -makers, financial institutions, FinTech companies, and
business organisations on how to boost the digital financial ecosystem, enhance

METHODS
This study employed a quantitative research approach using a cross-sectional survey
design to investigate the effect of Financial Technology (FinTech) on consumer
spending behavior in the digital economy. A quantitative design was considered
appropriate because it enables the examination of causal relationships among latent
variables through statistical analysis and facilitates the testing of research
hypotheses using empirical data. The conceptual model was developed by integrating
perspectives from financ ial technol ogy, consumer behavior, and digital economy
literature to explain how the adoption and utilization of FinTech services influence
consumers' spending patterns.
The research was conducted in urban areas characterized by a high level of digital
financial inclusion and widespread utilization of FinTech services. These locations
represent environments where consumers regularly use electronic wallets, mobile
banking applications, online payment systems, QR code transactions, and other
digital financial services for daily purchases. The selected research setting reflects
the rapid expansion of digital commerce, in which financial transactions have
become increasingly integrated with online marketplaces, retail businesses,
transportation services, food de livery plat forms, and entertainment applications.
Such an environment provides an appropriate context for examining consumer
spending behavior within an increasingly technology-driven economy.
Primary data were collected through a structured online questionnaire distributed to
active users of Financial Technology services. Online data collection was selected
because it enables broad geographical coverage, efficient data acquisition, and
reduced administrative costs while ensuring accessibility for responden ts who
frequently engage in digital financial activities. Prior to the main survey, a pilot study
involving 30 participants was conducted to evaluate the clarity, readability, and
consistency of the questionnaire items. Feedback obtained during the pilot t esting
was incorporated into the final version of the instrument to improve wording and
eliminate ambiguous statements. Participation in the survey was voluntary, and
respondents were informed about the objectives of the research, confidentiality
procedures, and the anonymous treatment of their responses before providing
informed consent.
The target population consisted of individuals who had experience using at least one
Financial Technology service, including mobile banking, electronic wallets, online
payment platforms, digital investment applications, or QR code payment systems.
Eligible respondents were required to be at least 18 years old and to have conducted
digital financial transactions within the previous six months. A purposive sampling
technique was employe d because the study required respondents with sufficient
experience in using FinTech services to evaluate their influence on spending
behavior. Based on methodological recommendations for Structural Equation
Modeling (SEM), a minimum sample size  of approxi mately 250 respondents was
considered adequate to achieve reliable parameter estimation and sufficient
statistical power. A total of 300 questionnaires were distributed, and after the data
screening process, 286 valid responses were retained for subsequent analysis.

The questionnaire consisted of two sections. The first section collected respondents'
demographic information, whereas the second measured the latent construct s
associated with Financial Technology and consumer spending behavior.
Measurement items were adapted from previously validated instruments in the fields
of financial technology adoption, digital payment systems, and consumer behavior.
All indicators were assessed using a five-point Likert scale ranging from 1 ("strongly
disagree") to 5 ("strongly agree"). To enhance content validity, the questionnaire was
reviewed by academic experts in financial technology and consumer behavior before
being distributed to respondents.
The collected data were analyzed using Structural Equation Modeling (SEM), which
enables simultaneous assessment of measurement quality and structural
relationships among latent constructs. The analysis began with descriptive statistics
to summarize respondents' demographic characteristics and provide an overview of
the observed variables. Subsequently, the measurement model was evaluated by
examining internal consistency reliability and construct validity. Cronbach's Alpha
and Composite Reliability (CR) were used to assess reliability, with values above 0.70
indicating acceptable internal consistency. Convergent validity was examined using
the Average Variance Extracted (AVE), where values exceeding 0.50 demonstrated
adequate construct converg ence. Discriminant validity was assessed through the
Fornell–Larcker criterion and the Heterotrait–Monotrait Ratio (HTMT) to ensure that
each latent construct represented a concept distinct from the others.
Following confirmation of the  measurement model, the structural model was
evaluated to examine the proposed causal relationship between Financial Technology
and consumer spending behavior. Model adequacy was assessed using several
goodness-of-fit indices, including the Comparative Fit  Index (CFI), Tucker –Lewis
Index (TLI), Root Mean Square Error of Approximation (RMSEA), Standardized Root
Mean Square Residual (SRMR), and the Chi -square to degrees of freedom ratio
(χ²/df). Statistical significance for the hypothesized relationship was d etermined at
the 5% significance level.  To improve the robustness of the empirical findings,
additional diagnostic procedures were conducted prior to hypothesis testing. Missing
values, multivariate outliers, and normality assumptions were examined during the
data screening process. Common method bias was evaluated using Harman's single-
factor test, while multicollinearity among predictor variables was assessed through
Variance Inflation Factor (VIF) values. These procedures ensured that the dataset
satisfied the assumptions required for SEM analysis and strengthened the reliability,
validity, and credibility of the study's conclusions regarding the influence of Financial
Technology on consumer spending behavior in the digital economy.

Financial Technology and Consumer Spending Behavior in the Digital Economy
The results of this study show that Financial Techn ology or FinTech has a positive
and significant effect on consumers' spending behavior in the digital economy. This
relationship indicates that digital financial services have become more than just a
payment tool and are now used as a strategic enabler for consumers' buying activity.
The strong structural linkage found in this study suggests that higher usage of the
FinTech services is linked to higher frequencies, faster and more convenient
spending. Indeed, consumers are increasingly adopting digital financial services into
their everyday lives, and their purchase of these services increasingly hinges on
technological availability, transaction efficiency, and the overall financial services
experience.
The results obtained in the present study align with a growing stream of recent
literature that suggests that the utilization of FinTech has a positive impact on
consumer purchasing behavior, with this relationship being manifested in terms of
greater convenience of transactions, less friction in transactions,  and increase in
accessibility to financial services (Yamin & Abdalatif, 2024; Danisan, 2025). Mobile
banking apps, digital wallet, QR code payments, and online payment integration have
been found to streamline financial processes and save time and effort in the
purchasing process in previous studies. This makes consumers more likely to make
purchases, especially in the digital space where convenience is a key factor. The
present results corroborate these insights with further empirical evidence of the
important role of digital financial innovation for consumer expenditure in modern
digital economies.
The findings also confirm some behavioural theories which propose that
technological innovations alter the process of consumer decision -making by
enhancing service efficiency and perceived convenience. As such, FinTech is not only
affecting purchasing behavior by giving consumers an alternative payment method,
it's also changing the way they engage with financial services. Combined with
payment systems, eCommerc e platforms, digital marketplaces, and personalized
promotions, the whole process of buying and selling goods and services through
digital channels is made easy, leading to more active consumer engagement in digital
transactions. This interpretation aligns with recent research that highlights the role
that digital financial ecosystems play in both transactional efficiency and the
consumer experience (Mavlutova et al., 2022).
The results are usually consistent with those reported in the literature, but a few
differences are worth mentioning. Previous research often focused on consumers'
attitudes toward FinTech adoption and have used th eory that included the
Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), and the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use
of Technology (UTAUT). The majority of these investigations focused on some of the
reasons why people choose to use digital financial services, but not o n the
behavioural impact of ongoing FinTech use. The present study, by contrast, takes
the discussion further by providing ev idence that, once adopted, ongoing use of
Financial Technology has an impact on actual expenditures. This view extends
current know ledge as it highlights behavioral results, rather than concentrating
solely on technology acceptance.
The results are also at  odds with research indicating that, as a result of the
diminished psychological awareness of cashless transactions, they inevitably lead to
excessive or impulsive spending. Digital payment technologies certainly ease the
shopping process, but the present study shows that an rise in spending behavior
does not necessarily mean irrational consumption. Many modern FinTech platforms

On a theoretical level, this study is a valuable addition to the growing body of work
on consumer behavior and digital finance, as it combines Financial Technology with
a wider behavioral framework. The study does not view FinTech as a standalo ne
technological development but rather as an ecosystem with an impact on consumer
spending in several different and interdependent  ways: ease of use, ease of access,
trust, and efficiency of transactions. This holistic view builds on earlier theoretical
work by showing that while technology acceptance is a key determinant of consumer
spending patterns, so are ongoing interactions wit h digital financial systems
(Anagreh et al., 2024; Raut & Kumar, 2024). Based on this, the results contribute to
the ongoing debates on the behavioral aspects of financial digitalization in
increasingly technology-driven economies.
The study also has impor tant practical implications that are of value to different
stakeholders. These findings can be used by policymakers to develop a strategy that
supports inclusive digital financial ecosystems that are also consumer-protected and
financially stable. Enhanced implementation of digital payment infrastructure, better
cybersecurity laws, and better financial education programmes could help to ensure
responsible use of FinTech services among various population groups. For financial
institutions and FinTech provide rs, the insights from these findings could further
enhance consumer trust in the platforms and drive sustainable engagement b y
prioritizing service reliability, user experience, transaction security, and
technological innovation. Moreover, business enterpr ises can incorporate various
digital payment methods into their commercial plans to boost the degree of customer
contentment, streamline transactions and refine the shopping experience of digital
marketplaces.
The findings from this research are applicable  to the wider digital economy
development. With the advent of new financial technologies, consumer behaviour is
increasingly driven not so much by some specific payment system, but by the
combination of different digital ecosystems. Working more closely to gether between
governments, financial institutions, technology producers, and business groups will
become more significant if technological innovation is to be a catalyst for sustainable
economic growth, while ensuring responsible consumer spending. A strategic priority
must continue to be improving digital financial literacy, so that consumers can use
FinTech services effectively, whilst making good financial choices.
One of the key strengths and novelty of this paper is its analytical attention to
behavioral implications of ongoing use of FinTech instead of only the technology
adoption. In contrast to most previous studies, which tended to focus on consumers'
intentions to use digital financial services, this study shows the impact of continued
engagement with FinTech services on actual spending in the digital economy.
Moreover, Financial Technology is defined as a behavioral ec osystem in the form of
a unified framework that includes payment innovation, the accessibility of finance,
the efficiency of the transaction, and consumer experience. This view provides a
broader context of the impact of digital financial transformation on  consumer
behavior in today's markets.

the research precludes the study of changes in consumer spending over time as
Financial Technology keeps developing. Second, the structural model emphasizes the
direct effect of Financial Technology only, and excludes other factors that might
influence spending behavior including financial literacy, perceived risk, self-control,

CONCLUSION
This study found that Financial Technology has a positive and statistically significant
effect on consumer spending behavior. The increasing use of digital financial services
improves transaction convenience, accessibility, efficiency, and consumer
confidence, thereby encouraging purchasing decisions and participation in digital
commerce. These findings indicate that FinTech has developed beyond a payment
mechanism into an important component of contemporary consumer behavior.
The study contributes to the literature by highlighting the behavioral consequences
of continuous FinTech use and p roviding practical implications for policymakers,
financial institutions, FinTech providers, and businesses in strengthening digital
financial ecosystems. However , the cross -sectional design and limited variables
restrict the broader interpretation of the findings. Future research should consider
longitudinal designs and incorporate psychological, socioeconomic, and cultural
factors to improve the generalizability of the results.
