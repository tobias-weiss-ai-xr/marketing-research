Abstract: As digital transformation begins to influence tourism practices, the importance of exploring
consumer behavior evolution in terms of the regenerative approach is growing. The purpose of this
paper is to investigate the phenomenon of digital transformation and its influence on consumer behavior
as it occurs within the domain of regenerative tourism. The study applies the qualitative interpretivist
paradigm and combines netnography as the main technique for collecting empirical data and grounded
theory as a tool of analysis. Empirical data were gathered using Instagram and included 312 posts and
1,248 comments made from 1 October 2025 until 31 March 2026.  Results show that the process of
consumers' evolution toward regenerative tourism behavior includes f ive stages. The transformation
starts from digital exposure, followed by value formation, social validation, and cognitive
transformation, eventually resulting in behavioral changes  and adoption of regenerative tourism
behavior. The present study contributes to the knowledge about digital transformation by providing a
process-oriented conceptual framework to explain the relationship between digital transformation and
regenerative behavior of consumers in tourism.

Keywords:  Digital Transformation; Regenerative Tourism; Value Construction; Consumer Behavior;
Netnography; Social Influence

1. Introduction

Digital transformation has revolutionized the tourism industry through changes in how tourism
experiences are made, consumed, managed, and distributed. Innovative technologies including
AI, big data analytics, virtual/augmented reality, mobile apps, and social media have altered the
decision-making process of tourists while redefining interactions among tourists, tourism
companies, destination management organizations, and locals. Thus, tourism has become a
well-connected digital ecosystem where experiences are constantly generated, evaluated, and
distributed through digital interaction. While improving operations, digital transformation has
turned out to be not just an operational tool but a strategic factor that shapes tourists '
expectations, perceptions of value, decisions, and experience of destinations altogether
(Bondarenko et al., 2025; Kumar et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2025). On the other hand, digital
technology has made tourists not just the consumers of tourism experiences but their creators
and distributors as well, thus, user -generated content has become a growing fac tor of
information and inspiration for travelers (Sigala, 2018; Buhalis et al., 2011; Blanco-Moreno et
al., 2024).
These opportunities, notwithstanding, the fast pace of technological development has  brought
some new challenges for the tourism sector. In particular, the intensive usage of social media
has enhanced the visibility of destinations, the pace of tourism consumption, and led to
overtourism by promoting concentrated flows of visitors and con sumption-based travelling
behavior (Basarangil , 2022; Milano et al., 2024). Through constant exposure to digital
platforms where travel experiences are curated and promoted, people become interested in
visiting popular destinations, which leads to additional strain on the environment, resources,
and socio-cultural systems of the host communities (Agyeiwaah, 2020; Bisht et al., 2025). This
situation has shown some deficiencies in traditional approaches to sustainability, as these
approaches aimed at minimizing the negative consequences of tourism rather than restoring the
ecological and social systems affected by tourism activities. As a result, more attention is paid
to regenerative tourism, a revolutionary approach that involves both minimization of harmful
effects and generation of positive environmental, social, cultural, and economic outcomes of
tourism activities for destinations and their communities ( Bellato et al., 2022; Bellato &
Pollock, 2025; Buckton et al., 2023).

A significant amount of research has been done on the relationship between social media and

understanding of how digitally mediated interactions on social media platforms transform
tourists’ perceptions, values, and behavioral orientations toward regenerative tourism. Existing
research has predominantly examined digital transformation from technological, marketing,
and operational perspectives, while the behavioral mechanisms through which user -generated
content contributes to the internalization of re generative tourism values remain insufficiently
explained.
Likewise, despite increasing academic interest in regenerative tourism, current research has
mostly concentrated on theoretical explorations, governance, destination management, and
regenerative development (Bellato et al., 2022; Bellato & Pollock, 2025; Buckton et al., 2023).

Thanks to advancements in digital technologies, ecosystems encompassing the integrated
interaction of all stakeholders in tourism supply and demand have also undergone revolutionary
changes. The widespread adoption of digital technologies across all business processes has not
only increased the efficiency of tourism services but also enhanced their attractiveness and
sustainability (Sigala, 2018). Through this digital transformation, the tourism sector has

Technological developments, referred to as digital transformation, which are changing the

The adoption of information and communication technologies by the tourism sector began with
the emergence of computer reservation systems in the 1970s and global distribution systems in

tourists and service providers in the tourism sector takes place through digital tools, making
tourists key determinants in decision-making processes.
According to recent research, advancements in smart technologies are significantly
transforming and influencing how tourists search for information, evaluate alternative options,
decide what to buy, assess the quality of their travel experiences, and share  these experiences
with their environment (Kumar et al., 2024). Advances in smart technologies have reinforced
the role of digital environments in defining tourist behavior, supporting personalized travel
experiences. One of the key outcomes of this digita l transformation is the shift in tourism
experiences from a passive, consumption-oriented structure to an active structure that enhances
value creation (Zhang et al., 2025). Thanks to advancements in these technologies, consumer
behavior has also become more interactive, participatory, and experience-oriented.

3. Integrating Digital Transformation and Regenerative Tourism

Technological advancements have created interconnected tourism environments where tourists
can interact instantly through numerous digital applications. These interactive tourism
experiences have reshaped consumption patterns and become a significant driving force behind
behavioral change. Parallel to these changes, tourism systems are undergoing a paradigmatic
shift towards more sustainable and regenerative forms of development, and digital technologies
offer significant opportunities to facilitate this tr ansformation towards regenerative tourism.
The regenerative tourism approach represents a transformative and proactive approach that
aims to support the constructive development of tourism behavior and ecosystems by taking
traditional sustainability arguments further (Becken & Loehr, 2024), and to minimize and
transform negative tourism-related impacts in tourism destinations.
While there has been increasing research interest in the transformative paradigm of regenerative
tourism, previous studies have considered this notion mostly from the angles of destination

4.1. Research Design

The interpretive paradigm is used within the current study because the goal is to explore the
meanings and experiences associated with the emergence of regenerative tourism perceptions
among users of digital media. Interpretive research allows to identify and analyze meanings and
experiences associated with the phenomena examined (Gichuru, 2017:3).  Netnography serves
as the methodology of choice within the study due to the nature of digital interaction in the
process of interpretation. This particular method is an extension of ethnographic research to
online communities and communication processes (Kozinet s, 2015:26).  In methodological
terms, netnography provided the overall approach for examining naturally occurring

4.2. Research Context and Data Source

The empirical setting for this research is Instagram, an influential platform for the production,
distribution, and consumption of tourism experiences via images, videos, and reels. Instagram
is highly pertinent for the purposes of this paper owing to its focus on visual storytelling,
influencer culture, and user engagement, all of which are central when it comes to developing
tourism perceptions (Yang, 2021:19).  Instagram was chosen over the rest because of its
visually-oriented nature, extensive usage of tourism-related user-generated content, higher level
of user interactions through comments and hashtags, as well as its popularity among tourism
companies and tourism influencers. All these factors made Instagram an especially appropriate
platform to study how regenerative tourism stories are constructed, understood, and negotiated
via digital interaction.

4.3. Data Collection

As part of the process of data collection, there was a purpos ive selection strategy to collect
relevant data. Publicly available Instagram posts, including the associated user comments, were
collected for a period of six months from 1 October 2025 up to 31 March 2026 in order to cover
diverse tourism content as well as  seasonality dynamics. In particular, such  a chosen period
aimed at eliminating any potential seasonal biases and providing a larger set of data reflecting
different perspectives on tourism and its relation to environmental sustainability (Bisht et al.,
2025:447).
The study employed a two-stage sampling strategy. Initially, purposive hashtag sampling was
used to identify Instagram content directly related to regenerative and sustainability -oriented
tourism practices. Subsequently, theoretical sampling guided the selection of additional posts
and comments that could elaborate, challeng e, or refine emerging categories during the
grounded theory analysis (Corbin, 2017). This iterative strategy enabled the researchers to
capture both variation and conceptual depth within digitally mediated regenerative tourism
discussions.

hashtags. As a result, 520 publicly available posts were initially identified. Every post was then
evaluated based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria set in ad vance. Posts that had only

6. Discussion

The findings point out that digital transformation in tourism has expanded from merely being
technological to becoming a tool that affects behavioural change in the direction of regenerative
tourism. Digital transformation is not only used as a tool of com munication and destination
promotion but also creates an interactive environment where the tourist constantly interprets
and reinterprets their perception of traveling based on online activity. The above perspective
expands on prior research which has been  focused mainly on the technological and marketing
roles of digital transformation in tourism (Sigala, 2018; Kumar et al., 2024; Schönherr et al.,
2023). Prior literature has shown how the application of digital technology has improved the
efficiency of operations and increased customer engagement in destinations, but it can be
argued that its role also encompasses influencing touri sts’ perceptions of the environment and
values.
Instagram has proven to be an important space where the interpretation and reinforcement of
regenerative tourism values take place through continuous interactions between users. Similarly
to the findings in earlier research, the platform offers users the ability to exchange experiences
about their travels, build the image of destinations, and interact with the tourism-related content
(Filieri et al., 2021; Blanco -Moreno et al., 2024). Nevertheless, what is further found in the
analysis is that repeated consumption of regenerative tourism content stimulates the
reinterpretation of messages and gradual assimilation of regenerative values through  social
interactions. This finding supports earlier literature on the impact of social media in developing
consumer attitudes (Ali et al., 2023; Wang, 2017) in showing that discussions and peer
validation support regenerative tourism narratives and lead to  behaviour change. The findings
extend previous research by showing that behavioral transformation toward regenerative
tourism may emerge as a sequential process involving awareness, value construction, social
validation, cognitive reframing, and behavioral change. The five-stage sequence should not be
interpreted as a temporally verified trajectory experienced by every individual user; rather, it
represents a processual model derived from recurring patterns across digitally mediated
interactions. Unlike studies that primarily emphasize the technological or promotional functions
of digital transformation, this study highlights the role of digitally mediated social interaction

transformation takes place in a sequential way within a digital environment.
The next interesting finding relates to the significance of user -generated content in supporting

does not influence regenerative tourism behavior directly; rather, it operates through a socially
mediated process of meaning construction.  These observations form a solid background for
further theoretical and practical reflections on digitally mediated regenerative tourism.

7. Implications

The findings from this research have theoretical as well as practical implications regarding the
association between digital transformation and regenerative tourism. In terms of theory, this
research contributes to the body of knowledge by bringing togethe r digital transformation,
consumer behavior and regenerative tourism into one common framework. Although previous
studies have explored the concept of digital transformation from the viewpoints of technology
and marketing (Sigala, 2018; Kumar et al., 2024) and regenerative tourism from the
perspectives of destination management and sustainability (Bellato et al., 2022; Bel lato &
Pollock, 2025), this research illustrates how digital interactions can result in behavioral
transformation through increased awareness, value creation, peer validation, cognitive
transformation and behavior change.

8. Conclusion

In this study, the relationship between digital transformation and regenerative tourism
behaviour of consumers was analysed using the posts and interactions of Instagram. According
to the findings, digital transformation goes beyond its technological role by helping consumers
undergo a process of behavioural transformation which includes the following steps: awareness,
value creation, peer validation, cognitive restructuring and finally, regenerat ive tourism
behaviour. These results enhance the literature regarding the way digital environments affect
tourists’ perception and facilitate the development of regenerative tourism.  The study
demonstrates that regenerative tourism behavior in digital environments emerges not as an
immediate consequence of information exposure, but as a socially mediated and sequential
transformation process in which awareness, value construction, peer  validation, cognitive
reframing, and behavioral adaptation mutually reinforce one another.
There are various limitations to this research which should be pointed out. Firstly, the research
has been carried out only for Instagram, as there is no access to any other platform; therefore,
the results can be used only for this social network and are not relevant to other platforms.
Secondly, demographic data could not be confirmed. Thirdly, the exposure of posts might
depend on the Instagram algorithm.
