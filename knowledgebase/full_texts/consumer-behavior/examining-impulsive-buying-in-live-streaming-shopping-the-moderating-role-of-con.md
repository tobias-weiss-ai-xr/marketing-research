Examining impulsive buying in live streaming

Abstract
This study integrates the Stimulus–Organism–Response (SOR) theory and Technology Acceptance
Model (TAM) to examine how Playfulness and Convenience influence impulsive buying through
Perceived enjoyment and Perceived usefulness, while testing the moderating role of Consumer
engagement, a relationship that remains underexplored in the live streaming e-commerce context.
Survey data from 343 TikTok Shop consumers in Batam City were analyzed using Structural
Equation Modeling–Partial Least Squares (SEM-PLS). The research findings indicate that
Perceived enjoyment and Perceived usefulness are positively and significantly influenced by the
elements of fun and ease.  impulsive buying is also strongly influenced by these two mediating
factors, with perceived usefulness being the most important factor. Interestingly, there are varied
patterns in the moderation results. Whilst H8 regarding the moderating effect of consumer
involvement on the relationship between perceived usefulness and impulsive buying was accepted,
H7 regarding the moderating effect of consumer involvement on the relationship between
Perceived enjoyment and impulsive buying was rejected. The effect of Perceived enjoyment on
impulsive buying is not strengthened by consumer involvement, whereas the effect of perceived
usefulness on impulsive buying is, in fact, significantly strengthened. This suggests that cognitive
evaluations are more susceptible to involvement-based reinforcement than emotional reactions.
These findings highlight the importance of pla tform functionality, an enjoyable shopping
experience and consumer engagement in driving Impulsive buying in live-streaming-based e-
commerce. This study makes a practical contribution by emphasising the importance of enhancing
functional value and interactive engagement techniques to encourage  impulsive buying behaviour,
as well as a theoretical contribution by extending the integration of SOR –TAM through a
differential moderation mechanism.

Keywords: Impulsive buying; live streaming commerce; Perceived usefulness;
Perceived enjoyment; Consumer engagement; SOR theory; TAM; TikTok

1. Introduction
Alongside the rapid growth of the digital economy, live streaming commerce has emerged
as a significant innovation that is increasingly influencing consumers’ purchasing
decisions in Indonesia. Shopping is no longer viewed merely as an economic transaction,
but also as a social, interactive and emotionally engaging experience. Indonesia ranked
second after Thailand in the Southeast Asian live -commerce sector, which reached
US$19.7 billion, according to Statista (2025). Platforms like Shopee Live and TikTok
Shop, whose active user base has grown by 48% in the last two years, are the main drivers
of this growth (DataReportal, 2024). These platforms' growing popularity has changed
how buyers engage with goods and vendors, leading to more impulsive and experience-
driven purchases. This pattern is in line with research by Lee and Chen (2021), who found
that live streaming environments' interactivity, flash deals, and entertainment value
greatly encourage impulsive purchases, particularly when customers are emotionally
stimulated while shopping.
From a psychological standpoint, live streaming also strengthens customers'
decisions to buy by fostering an interesting and participatory buying environment.
According to Chen et al. (2022), real-time, two-way communication increases customer
engagement and promotes impulsive buying by fostering an emotional bond between
buyers and sellers. Similarly, Lukmanto and Pribadi (2025) discovered that live streaming
hosts use humor, body language, and verbal communication to affect viewers' emotional
reactions, which raises the possibility of impulsive purchases. These results imply that
consumers' psychological reactions and purchasing behavior in live streaming commerce
are significantly influenced by both technological aspects and social interactions.
Nonetheless, live streaming business adoption is still inconsistent throughout
Indonesia. According to preliminary findings, a large number of Batam companies,
especially those in the fashion and cosmetics industries, have not yet completely included
live streaming sales techniques into their digital marketing campaigns. Compared to
major metropolitan areas like Jakarta, Bandung, and Tangerang, the adoption of live
streaming commerce among local businesses is still relatively low, despite Batam being
one of Indonesia's strategic economic zones and a Free Trade Zone (FTZ) with robust
economic growth and a thriving digital business sector. Jakpat (2025) reports that 62% of
Indonesia's Gen Z customers have used live shopping, demonstrating the swift uptake of
live commerce throughout the nation. Batam's economy, meanwhile, reached Rp 233.05
trillion in 2024, demonstrating the city's increasing significance as one of Indonesia's
economic centers (BPS Kota Batam, 2025). The uptake of live commerce has lagged
behind its economic potential. Batam is a developing digital ecosystem where live
commerce is still in its infancy, in contrast to more established digital markets like Jakarta.
This context offers a chance to investigate if customers' emotional and cognitive reactions
to live streaming features vary in an immature digital world. Additionally, Batam's cross-
border economic features and its quickly expanding digital economy offer a theoretically
significant framework for examining impulsive buying outside of Indonesia's main cities.
A substantial research gap is shown by this disparity between the anticipated and
actual adoption rates of live streaming commerce. Theoretically, both consumers and
businesses should adopt live streaming commerce more widely as digital technology
becomes more widely used. Preliminary data, however, indicates that many MSMEs in
Batam continue to encounter a number of difficulties, such as a lack of digital capabilities,
a lack of expertise in creating interactive content, and the belief that live commerce mostly

This study uses the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) and the Stimulus–
Organism–Response (SOR) framework, two complementary theoretical stances, to
explain this occurrence. According to the Stimulus–Organism–Response (SOR)
hypothesis, which was put forth by Mehrabian and Russell (1974) and examined by
Hochreiter et al. (2022), an individual's behavioral reactions are shaped by internal
cognitive and emotional states that are influenced by external environmental stimuli. This
perspective is supported by Siregar and Firdausy (2024), who identified playfulness and
enjoyment as important psychological drivers of impulsive buying in digital commerce.
In the context of TikTok Shop Live streaming, external stimuli including host playfulness,
real-time interaction, product visualization, and promotional activities such as flash sales
can influence consumers' internal evaluations through Perceived enjoyment and
Perceived usefulness, ultimately encouraging impulsive buying behavior. Since
playfulness and Convenience are two complementing aspects of consumers' buying
experiences, this study concentrates on them among the several stimuli analyzed in earlier
live streaming studies. Convenience represents the effectiveness and usefulness of the
purchasing process, while playfulness captures the hedonistic and amusing elements of
live streaming interactions. A more thorough grasp of how hedonic and utilitarian values
work together to influence customers' psychological assessments during live streaming
commerce can be gained by looking at these complementing stimuli.
In addition to the SOR framework, this study employs the Technology Acceptance
Model (TAM) developed by Davis (1989). TAM proposes that technology adoption is
primarily influenced by Perceived usefulness and Perceived Ease of Use (Schorr, 2023).
In the context of TikTok Shop Live streaming, Perceived usefulness reflects consumers'
beliefs that the platform facilitates efficient, practical, and beneficial purchasing
decisions. Although TAM traditionally includes Perceived Ease of Use, this study focuses
on Perceived usefulness because TikTok Shop users generally possess sufficient digital
literacy to operate live streaming features. As a result, purchasing decisions are more
likely to depend on the perceived value and benefits obtained from the platform than on
operational ease, which has become a basic expectatio n in contemporary mobile
commerce applications. Although previous studies have integrated SOR and TAM to
explain consumer behavior in digital commerce, limited attention has been given to how
consumers' cognitive and affective evaluations jointly influence impulsive buying under
different levels of Consumer engagement. Accordingly, this study positions Playfulness
and Convenience as complementary stimulus variables, Perceived enjoyment and
Perceived usefulness as organism variables, and Consumer engagement as a moderating
mechanism that influences the relationship between these internal evaluations and
impulsive buying behavior. Rather than emphasizing the integration of SOR and TAM as
the primary novelty, this study seeks to explain how Consumer engagement shapes the
relative influence of utilitarian cognition and hedonic emotion on impulsive buying. This
theoretical configuration provides a more nuanced understanding of consumers'
purchasing behavior in TikTok Shop Live streaming, particularly within the context of an

Despite the growing body of literature on live streaming commerce, several
important research gaps remain. Previous studies have employed the Stimulus–
Organism–Response (SOR) framework and the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM),
either independently or in combination, to explain impulsive buying behavior in digital
commerce. These studies generally demonstrate that both hedonic factors, such as
Playfulness and Perceived enjoyment, and utilitarian factors, such as Perceived
usefulness, positively influence consumers' purchasing decisions. For example, P. Zhu et
al. (2023) examined the influence of live streaming features on Perceived usefulness and
purchase intention, Zhu and Vijayan ( 2025) investigated the relationship between
Perceived enjoyment and impulsive buying, and Li et al. (2022) explained impulsive
buying behavior using the SOR framework. However, previous findings remain
inconclusive regarding the relative importance of utilitarian cognition and hedonic
emotion in driving impulsive buying. While several studies emphasize the dominant role
of emotional enjoyment in live streaming commerce, others suggest that functional value
and shopping efficiency are stronger predictors of purchasing behavior. These
inconsistent findings indicate that the psychological mechanisms underlying impulsive
buying have not yet been fully explained. Furthermore, limited attention has been given
to the conditions under which consumers' cognitive and emotional evaluations become
stronger or weaker, particularly through the role of Consumer engagement. Addressing
this issue is theoretically important because impulsive buying in live streaming commerce
is not driven by isolated direct relationships but by interactions among hedonic,
utilitarian, and psychological factors operating simultaneously.
Accordingly, this study proposes a moderated research model that integrates the
complementary perspectives of the SOR framework and TAM. Specifically, Playfulness
and Convenience are conceptualized as complementary stimulus variables, while
Perceived enjoyment and Perceived usefulness represent consumers' internal evaluations
as organism variables. In addition, Consumer engagement is conceptualized as a
boundary condition that influences the extent to which these cognitive and emotional
evaluations are translated into impulsive buying behavior. Consumers who are more
actively engaged in live streaming interactions are expected to respond more strongly to
both utilitarian and hedonic evaluations than less engaged consumers. Therefore, the
contribution of this study lies not merely in combining SOR and TAM, but in providing
a more comprehensive explanation of how Consumer engagement shapes the relative
influence of utilitarian cognition and hedonic emotion on impulsive buying within live
streaming commerce. This study advances our knowledge of impulsive buying behavior
in the quickly changing digital marketplace by empirically investigating this theoretical
framework among TikTok Shop live streaming customers in Batam City. The findings are
expected to contribute to the advancement of consumer behavior theory while offering
practical implications for platform providers, livestream sellers, MSMEs, and digital
marketers seeking to develop more effective live streaming commerce strategies.

Response (SOR) framework and the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) to explain

have applied SOR and TAM in digital commerce contexts; therefore, the contribution of
this study does not rely solely on combining these two theories. On the other hand, by
elucidating how cognitive and affective mechanisms concurrently impact  impulsive

differentiates between these two mechanisms, this work adds to the body of knowledge.
This study specifically looks at how platform -related experiences, which are

According to Mehrabian and Russell's ( 1974) Stimulus–Organism–Response
(SOR) theory, environmental stimuli affect a person's internal psychological state, which
in turn sets off a behavioral reaction. In the context of digital commerce, consumers'
perceptions and emotions are shaped by technology features, interactive experiences, and
marketing components in addition to the physical surroundings. Customers are exposed
to a variety of stimuli in live streaming commerce, including amusing material, interactive
communication, product demos, and an easy way to make a purchase. These factors affect
customers' mental states, which in turn affect behavioral outcomes like impulsive
purchases. These mental states include cognitive assessments and emotional experiences
(Lee & Chen, 2021). Because it covers the process by which digital experiences are
converted into purchase decisions, recent research has proven that the SOR paradigm is
still applicable for describing customer responses in live streaming commerce contexts
(Feng et al., 2024).
This study also incorporates TAM proposed by Davis (1989) to explain consumers’
cognitive evaluations of technology usage. While TAM originally emphasizes Perceived
usefulness and Perceived Ease of Use as determinants of technology acceptance, this
study focuses on Perceived usefulness because TikTok Shop users are generally familiar
with social commerce platforms. Therefore, consumers’ decisions are more likely
influenced by the perceived benefits obtained from the platform rather than basic
operational ease. In addition, Perceived enjoyment is incorporated as an affective
dimension because live streaming commerce combines functional shopping activities
with entertainment, interaction, and experiential consumption (Wardana et al., 2022).
The integration of SOR and TAM allows this study to examine two complementary
pathways of impulsive buying behavior. The cognitive pathway explains how consumers’
perceptions of usefulness encourage faster purchase decisions, while the affective
pathway explains how enjoyment and positive emotions stimulate spontaneous purchases.
Previous research has explored these factors separately; however, limited attention has
been given to how cognitive and emotional mechanisms operate simultaneously and how
Consumer engagement influences the strength of these relationships.
Consumer engagement is positioned as a moderating variable because consumers
with different levels of involvement may respond differently to live streaming
experiences. Highly engaged consumers are expected to process product information
more deeply, strengthening the influence of Perceived usefulness and Perceived
enjoyment on impulsive buying. Rather than being modeled as a mediator, Consumer
engagement is not assumed to be a consequence of Playfulness and Convenience that

2.2 The relationship of playfulness to perceived usefulness
A person's propensity to see an activity as joyful, impromptu, and captivating is referred
to as "playfulness," and it might promote pleasurable encounters with technology (Proyer
& Brauer, 2023). Engaging shopping experiences, dynamic communication, and
captivating content that entices customers to learn more about products and the platform's
capabilities are all examples of how this playfulness is mirrored in live streamed
commerce.
From a SOR perspective, the element of excitement acts as a stimulus that shapes
consumers’ cognitive evaluations. An exciting live streaming environment can encourage
consumers to actively process product information, as the shopping process becomes
more engaging and less psychologically taxing. When consumers experience curiosity
and enjoyment whilst interacting with a platform, they tend to perceive that the platform
provides useful benefits to support product evaluation and purchasing decisions.
The expanded TAM perspective, which posits that users’ assessments of a
technology’s value can be influenced by their interactive experiences, is also consistent
with this relationship. Engaging elements in TikTok Shop Live streams, such as interaction
with the host, product demonstrations and real-time interaction, may enhance viewers’
perceptions of the platform’s effectiveness in conveying relevant information and
facilitating the purchasing process.
Previous research supports this relationship. P. Zhu et al. ( 2023) found that
interactive and enjoyable experiences in live streamed trading enhance Perceived
usefulness by improving information processing and consumer evaluation. Wardana et al.
(2022) also demonstrated that positive digital experiences contribute to a stronger
perception of the benefits of technology. However, previous research has largely
emphasised the emotional impact of the fun element, whilst its role in shaping cognitive
evaluations within a live streaming shopping environment remains under-explored.
Therefore, this study examines the influence of playfulness on Perceived usefulness
among TikTok Shop consumers in Batam City. Based on these arguments, the following
hypothesis is proposed:
H1: Playfulness has a positive effect on Perceived usefulness in consumers live streaming
TikTok Shop in Batam City.

2.4 The relationship of convenience to perceived enjoyment
According to Zeqiri et al. (2023), Convenience is the idea that a platform makes tasks
simple, effective, and unrestricted. Easy access to live broadcasts, speedy product
information retrieval, seamless host engagement, and streamlined transaction procedures
are all examples of Convenience in live streaming commerce. During live shopping
sessions, consumers must absorb multiple information sources in a short amount of time,
which makes these features crucial. Because simple interactions lessen cognitive load and
let customers concentrate on the experience elements of purchasing, Convenience can
affect Perceived enjoyment. Convenience is seen by SOR as a technical stimulus that
influences consumers' internal emotional reactions. Customers are more likely to be
satisfied and enjoy the purchasing process when they believe a platform is simple and
comfortable to use. Convenience, thus, enhances both functional efficiency and makes
online purchasing more pleasurable.
In TikTok Shop Live streaming, convenient features such as integrated product
links, real-time product information, and simple purchasing procedures can enhance
consumers’ comfort and engagement. When consumers can obtain information and
complete transactions without difficulties, the shopping experience becomes smoother
and more enjoyable. Therefore, Convenience is expected to increase Perceived enjoyment
in live-commerce environments.

2.5 The relationship of convenience to perceived usefulness
Convenience refers to the extent to which consumers perceive that a digital platform
enables activities to be completed efficiently with minimal time, effort, and complexity
(Zeqiri et al., 2023). In live streaming commerce, Convenience includes easy access to
product information, efficient interaction with sellers, quick product evaluation, and
simplified purchasing procedures. These characteristics allow consumers to make
purchase decisions more effectively during dynamic live-shopping sessions.
From the perspective of TAM, Perceived usefulness reflects consumers’ beliefs that
a technology provides benefits and improves task performance ( Davis, 1989).
Convenience can strengthen Perceived usefulness because platforms that reduce effort
and simplify shopping processes are more likely to be perceived as valuable and
beneficial. Customers may view TikTok Shop Live streaming as more beneficial when its
features enable them to easily complete purchases, evaluate options, and acquire product
information.
By considering Convenience as a technology stimulus that influences customers'
cognitive assessments, the SOR perspective also clarifies this relationship. Customers are
more likely to identify the useful advantages of live streaming commerce when they
encounter fewer challenges during platform interactions. Convenience thereby enhances
the efficacy of consumers' buying actions, which strengthens feelings of usefulness.
This association has been validated by earlier research. Convenience has a
favourable impact on perceived usefulness in online buying, according to Bansah &
Darko Agyei (2022), since customers' views of platform advantages are raised by quick
processes. Additionally, Wardana et al. (2022) showed that Convenience plays a major
role in assessments of the usefulness of technology. Furthermore, Schorr (2023) clarified
that users' cognitive views of technical worth are strengthened by useful and effective
technology encounters.
Convenience's position as a stimulant influencing perceived usefulness in live
streaming commerce needs more research, even though prior studies have mostly looked
at Convenience in relation to adoption intention and satisfaction. Thus, this study looks
at how Convenience affects Batam City TikTok Shop customers' perceptions of utility.
The following theory is put out in light of these arguments:
H4: Convenience has a positive effect on Perceived usefulness in consumers live
streaming TikTok Shop in Batam City.

2.6 The relationship of perceived enjoyment to impulsive buying
According to Liu et al. (2025), impulsive buying is defined as spontaneous purchasing
behaviour that is marked by little prior planning, quick decision-making, and significant
influence from instant emotional reactions. In digital commerce settings, consumers'
psychological experiences during shopping activities have an impact on impulsive buying
in addition to product attributes. Emotional reactions play a significant role in promoting
impulsive buying because live streaming commerce creates an environment where
entertainment, conversation, and purchasing activities take place concurrently.
The degree to which users of a digital platform feel joy, excitement, and
contentment is known as Perceived enjoyment (X. Zhu & Vijayan, 2025). Perceived
enjoyment serves as an internal affective state that converts digital buying experiences
into behavioural reactions, according to the SOR approach. Customers may get more
emotionally invested, less likely to think things through logically, and more likely to make
quick purchases when they enjoy live streaming events.
TikTok Shop Live streaming, in contrast to traditional e-commerce, blends product
details with entertainment components including host interactions, real -time
conversation, and interesting content. Customers are more likely to make impulsive
buying when they are in an immersive shopping environment created by these
experiences. Thus, in live-commerce settings, reported delight is a significant emotional
pathway that explains  impulsive buying behaviour.
Previous research supports this relationship. X. Zhu and Vijayan (2025) found that
the perception of enjoyment positively influences impulsive purchase intentions amongst
live streaming commerce consumers, as an enjoyable experience strengthens emotional
attachment to the shopping activity. Paraman et al. (2022) also demonstrated that the
experience of ‘flow’ during live streaming encourages  impulsive buying behaviour.
Furthermore, Feng et al. (2024) showed that the affective responses arising from
interactions in live streaming commerce significantly influence consumers’ propensity for
impulsive buying.
Although previous research has highlighted the emotional factors driving
impulsive buying, few studies have examined how affective and cognitive mechanisms
interact in live-stream shopping. Therefore, this study examines the perception of
pleasure as an affective pathway within the broader SOR–TAM framework to explain
impulsive buying amongst TikTok Shop consumers in Batam City. Based on these
arguments, the following hypotheses are proposed:
H5: Perceived enjoyment has a positive effect on impulsive buying among TikTok Shop
live streaming consumers in Batam City.

alternatives, understand product features, and complete transactions efficiently.

(2023), the perceived advantages of live streaming platforms increase consumers’
propensity to purchase by reinforcing their product evaluation process. Furthermore, Feng
et al. (2024) demonstrate that  impulsive buying behaviour is influenced by cognitive
reactions to cues in live streaming commerce. Similarly, Li et al. (2022) found that digital
stimuli influence consumers’ internal evaluations, which in turn drive  impulsive buying.
However, previous research has largely focused on the emotional aspects of
impulse buying, with less attention paid to the impact of cognitive judgements. This study
argues that consumers may rely on perceived usefulness to support quick purchasing
decisions in an increasingly familiar digital retail environment. Therefore, a more in-
depth explanation of  impulsive buying behaviour on TikTok Shop’s live streams can be
obtained by examining cognitive pathways in addition to emotional ones. The following
theory is proposed based on these arguments:
H6: Perceived usefulness has a positive effect on impulsive buying among TikTok Shop
live streaming consumers in Batam City.
2.8 Consumer engagement as a Moderator in the Relationship
between Perceived enjoyment and Impulsive buying
The term "consumer engagement" describes how customers connect with digital
platforms, content, vendors, and other users on a cognitive, emotional, and behavioural
level (Zheng et al., 2022). Watching live broadcasts, leaving comments, communicating
with hosts, and taking part in in-the-moment purchase experiences are all examples of
participation in live streaming commerce. Consumer engagement, as opposed to basic
platform usage, is the level of psychological involvement that affects how customers react
to digital buying stimuli.
This study positions Consumer engagement as a moderator because consumers with
different levels of involvement may experience different responses to similar shopping
experiences. While previous studies have frequently examined engagement as an
outcome of digital experiences, this study considers engagement as a boundary condition
that explains variations in the translation of internal psychological states into purchasing
responses.
From the SOR perspective, Perceived enjoyment represents an affective state
generated from consumers’ experiences during live streaming. Consumer engagement
may strengthen this relationship because highly engaged consumers are more emotionally
connected with live content, hosts, and shopping communities. As a result, enjoyable

2.9 Consumer engagement as a moderator on the relationship of

Consumer engagement influences how consumers process and respond to the value
provided by digital commerce platforms. In live streaming commerce, highly engaged
consumers tend to pay greater attention to product demonstrations, seller explanations,
promotional information, and interactions during live broadcasts. Therefore, engagement
may determine the extent to which consumers transform perceived platform benefits into
actual purchasing responses.
From the TAM perspective, Perceived usefulness represents consumers’ cognitive
evaluation of whether a technology provides meaningful benefits and improves shopping
effectiveness (Davis, 1989). However, the impact of perceived usefulness on impulsive

engagement customers are more likely to fully comprehend product details, identify the
advantages provided by TikTok Shop, and grow more confident while making snap
selections.
This regulatory mechanism is further explained through the SOR framework.
Whilst consumer engagement determines the level of cognitive processing prior to the
emergence of a behavioural response, Perceived usefulness represents an internal
cognitive state. Customers will pay greater attention to useful information offered during
live streams, such as product demonstrations, reviews and recommendations, when their

3. Method
This study employed a quantitative research approach to examine a specific population
and test the proposed hypotheses ( Syahroni, 2022). A quantitative approach was
considered appropriate because it enables the objective measurement of the direction and
significance of causal relationships among the research constructs. The study examined
the relationships among playfulness, Convenience, Perceived enjoyment, Perceived
usefulness, Consumer engagement, and impulsive buying among TikTok Shop live
streaming consumers in Batam City.

The criteria for respondents in this study are people who live in Batam City, are at
least 17 years old, actively watch TikTok Shop Live streaming at least 1 time in the past
week, and have purchased products through the TikTok Shop Live streaming feature at
least 1 time in the past week. The products purchased may include categories such as

based on respondents' preferences and firsthand experiences led to the selection of this
primary data.
To ensure the validity of the content, data were collected using a structured
questionnaire adapted from a recognised measurement scale. The indicators for
playfulness were adapted from Proyer and Brauer (2023) and Pan et al. (2023). The
Convenience, Perceived usefulness, and Perceived enjoyment constructs were adapted
from Schorr (2023), while Consumer engagement was measured using the scale
developed by Siregar and Firdausy (2024). The concept of impulse buying was adapted
from Wirawan and Syahputra (2025). To maintain semantic equivalence, the original
questionnaire was translated into Indonesian using a forward -backward translation
process. To ensure item clarity and content validity, the instrument was reviewed by
academic experts and pilot-tested prior to the main survey. A five-point Likert scale,
where 1 represents ‘strongly disagree’ and 5 represents ‘strongly agree’, was used to rate
each item.
Both statistical and procedural methods were employed to reduce common-method
bias (CMB) and improve the quality of measurement. To minimise response pattern bias,
the order of questions in the questionnaire was randomised, the wording was kept
straightforward and non-leading, and respondent anonymity was guaranteed. To reduce
social desirability bias, respondents were also informed that there were no right or wrong
answers. The Variance Inflation Factor (VIF) in SmartPLS and Harman’s Single Factor
Test were used to evaluate CMB statistically. According to Podsakoff et al. (2003), these
techniques reduce the likelihood that common-method variance exaggerates the observed
relationships. Furthermore, non-response bias was assessed by comparing initial and final
respondents, and no clear variations were found across any of the main dimensions.
In selecting the sample, the sampling technique used was non-probability purposive
sampling, in which sample elements are selected based on specific criteria relating to the
research objectives. Purposive sampling was deemed appropriate because the target
audience consists of consumers with specific experience of shopping via TikTok Shop
Live streams, making probability sampling difficult to carry out. However, the use of
purposive sampling may limit the generalisability of the findings beyond customers with
similar characteristics. The minimum sample size estimate follows the recommendations
of Hair & Sabol (2025) for determining the indicator-to-sample ratio in Structural
Equation Modelling–Partial Least Squares (SEM-PLS) modelling. The recommended
minimum ratio is 1:10, indicating that the minimum sample size must be ten times the
number of indicators. This rule of thumb is supported by the latest PLS-SEM guidelines,
which emphasise that the sample size must be sufficient to ensure successful model
estimation (Hair et al., 2022). With regard to the structural modelling in this study, which
integrates 23 operational variables, the minimum sample size is 230 respondents. In this
study, a total of 343 respondents were successfully recruited via Google Forms.

5. Discussion
5.1 The effect of playfulness on perceived usefulness
The findings confirm H1 by showing that playfulness has a positive and significant impact
on Perceived usefulness (β = 0.316, T = 5.480, p < 0.001). According to this research,
consumers' opinions of TikTok Shop Live as a helpful resource for learning about products
and making decisions about purchases are improved by a more entertaining and engaging
live streaming environment. Playfulness helps customers to see the practical advantages
of engaging in live streaming retail activities rather than just providing amusement.
One explanation could be that consumers can more effectively get pertinent
information thanks to interactive elements including real-time discussion with hosts,
captivating product demos, and prompt responses to customer enquiries. Customers'
opinions of the platform's utility are strengthened by these experiences, which lessen
information uncertainty and streamline product evaluation. As a result, playfulness
enhances customers' cognitive assessments of the platform in addition to fostering a
pleasurable purchasing environment.
This result is in line with Zhu et al. ( 2023), who found that in live streaming
commerce, playfulness increases Perceived usefulness by making product inquiry more
interesting and educational. Similarly, Fasha et al. ( 2025) showed that good online
experiences and high-quality information boost consumers' confidence in making
purchase decisions, while Wardana et al. (2022) discovered that happy digital experiences
positively influence users' perceptions of platform utility. All of these research provide
credence to the idea that consumers' functional assessments of digital purchasing
platforms can be simultaneously strengthened by pleasurable encounters.
This discovery offers a more sophisticated theoretical explanation of the connection
between the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) and the Stimulus–Organism–
Response (SOR) framework. According to the integrated framework, playfulness is an
external stimulation that influences customers' purchasing behaviour by stimulating their
cognitive reactions through Perceived usefulness. This study shows that good live
streaming experiences can convert hedonic stimuli into utilitarian evaluations instead of
only combining SOR and TAM. In the context of TikTok Shop Live in particular, this
process helps us better understand how interactive live-commerce settings influence
consumer decision-making.
5.2 The effect of playfulness on perceived enjoyment
The findings confirm H2 by demonstrating Playfulness has a positive and significant
effect on Perceived enjoyment (β = 0.320, T = 6.191, p < 0.001). According to this
research, live streaming sessions that feature engaging product presentations, interactive
communication, and impromptu host interactions are more enjoyable for viewers.
Customers' emotional reactions when utilising TikTok Shop Live are enhanced by these
enjoyable buying experiences.
Playfulness may inspire customers to view live streaming shopping as more than a

transactional activity. The shopping experience is made more immersive and enjoyable
by real-time interactions, witty communication, and captivating product demos.
Customers are therefore more likely to have favourable emotional experiences, which

The current results support the claim made by Kiverstein and Miller (2023) that

Similarly, Proyer and Brauer (2023) highlighted that playfulness-related spontaneity and

From a theoretical standpoint, these results show that a single exterior stimulus can
elicit several psychological reactions, supporting the integrated SOR–TAM framework.
While playfulness directly enhances consumers' emotional experiences, it also influences
cognitive assessments, suggesting that while live streaming shopping, emotional and
cognitive processes function concurrently. By demonstrating that pleasurable shopping
experiences are not discrete emotional reactions but rather are a component of a larger
psychological system that influences purchasing decisions, this finding offers a more

8. Limitations and suggestions for further research
This study has several limitations. First, this study only involved TikTok Shop customers
in Batam City, thereby limiting the relevance of the findings to other regions,
demographics and live-commerce markets. To determine whether the proposed
relationship holds true in various contexts, future research should involve customers from
different regions across Indonesia or conduct cross-cultural comparisons.
Second, the sample composition is relatively concentrated on male respondents and
younger customers, which may influence the relationships found, as different
demographic groups may have different motivations for making purchases. Future
research could employ multi-group analysis to investigate whether the proposed model is
influenced by customer attributes, product categories, gender or age.
Third, this study used self-reported data and a cross-sectional survey design, which
may add common method bias and restrict causal interpretation. To give more convincing
proof of causal linkages, future studies could use experimental methods, longitudinal
designs, or behavioural data from real-world purchase actions.
Fourth, the study only looked at live streaming on TikTok Shop. In order to
determine whether platform features affect consumer psychological mechanisms
differently, future study may examine several live-commerce platforms, such as TikTok
Shop, Shopee Live, and Lazada Live.
Last, by adding more variables such perceived risk, trust, social presence, scarcity
perception, pricing perception, or deal proneness, future research may broaden the model.
In order to gain a deeper understanding of why consumers turn live streaming encounters
into Impulsive buying decisions, qualitative or mixed-method techniques are also advised.
