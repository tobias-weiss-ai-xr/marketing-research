Abstract. In the context of global green, the traditional fast fashion mode has been criticized for its
high resource consumption and serious environmental pollution. Facing the huge wave of the era
responsibility, how to effectively and high-quality to long-term sustainable development has become
the core challenge of fast fashion brands. Based on the theory of values attitude behavior hierarchy,
this study uses the experimental design between groups to compare and analyze the response
differences of two circular fa shion strategies (Upcycle vs.  Recycle) implemented by consumers in
the sustainable transformation of fast fashion brands, aiming to reveal the psychological mechanism
and relative effectiveness of the two in driving consumer behavior. The results show that for fast
fashion products, th e Upcycle type of environmental protection strategy has a stronger positive
impact on the purchase intention than the Recycle type of environmental protection strategy, and
the perceived value plays an intermediary role in the Upcycle type of environmental  protection
strategy. The conclusion of this paper enriches the research of sustainable marketing strategy,
widens the development direction of fast fashion field, and provides practical guidance for fast
fashion brands to develop differentiated sustainable marketing strategies.
Keywords: Fast Fashion Brand, Circular Fashion Strategy, Upcycle, Recycle, Perceived Value.
1. Introduction
Driven by the global imperative for a circular economy, the fashion industry, as a significant source
of resource consumption and environmental pollution, is facing transformation challenges  [1].
According to research, the global apparel sector is responsible for the annual consumption of 93
billion cubic meters of water and discharges about 12 billion tons of carbon dioxide every year, more
than 98 million tonnes of non-renewable feedstocks are channelled into the production of textiles and
apparel [2]. Among them, in terms of both the magnitude of waste generated and the velocity of its
expansion, the textile industry remains a critical global concern, especially fast fashion has a dramatic
adverse effect on the clothing industry and the environment. In response to this challenge, the concept
of circular economy has been widely introduced by fast fashion brands, of which recycling and
upcycling are two core resource reuse modes, which are adopted by more a nd more fast fashion
brands as key components of sustainable development strategies (such as the famous fast fashion
clothing brand H&M has already invested in Renewcell and secured an offtake agreement with a pulp
mill translating to a conversation of saving 50 million garments) [3,4]. Although upcycling and
recycling belong to the category of circular economy, many existing studies call them "green
economy" or "sustainable initiatives", ignoring the differences between the two in consumer
psychological mechanisms, especially within the domain of fast fashion, the influence of these two
types of environmental protection products on consumers' evaluation of fast fashion products has not
yet been explored.
Therefore, this study seeks to systematically examine, through a comparative lens, the mechanisms
by which upcycling and recycling strategies influence consumer purchase intention within the fast
fashion sector. The research will focus on the differences of psychological action path behind the two
types of environmental protection strategies and use the literature analysis method to search and read
the relevant field data and literature. The advantage of this me thod is that it can better analyze the
psychological mechanism differences between the two types of environmental protection strategies

and the driving force of consumers' purchase intention. The use of a single variable group comparison
experiment can effectively compare the subtle differences between the two sustainable environmental
protection strategies for consumers, reaction to the intermediary role of perceived value, and the two
methods are conducive to the experiment. The ultimate goal of this study is to clarify the distinct
psychological processes involved, which not only contributes to the literature on sustainable
consumption and the circular economy but also informs strategic decision -making for fast fashion
brands pursuing sustainability.
2. Literature review
With the change of the environment, some companies began to take green initiatives such as
sustainable marketing and social responsibility as their core business philosophy to follow the times.
Among them, slow fashion and second-hand and retro types of clothing gradually entered the public
vision and received more and more support [2]. Fast fashion brands can't escape the torrent and step
into a sustainable pace, using upcycling and recycling strategies for transformation. Upcycling is a
relatively new sustainable production mode, which is characterized as "transforming discarded
materials into new products that possess superior quality or value relative to their initial state ",
because of its increasing demand, it has created a popular trend [5]. And recycling is to maintain the
surplus value and make use of it, such as through new process recovery or remanufacturing for
reconstruction, to introduce it into the market again [6].
Based on some previous studies, consumers are usually positive about products manufactures from
recycled materials  [6]. They found that consumers in different regions, i.e. developed countries,
exhibit a higher level of acceptance towards products manufactured from recycled materials, while
some developing countries have a lower understanding of this category [7]. Other researchers found
that the identifiability of goods crafted from reclaimed materials affects consumer recognition and
purchase intention to a certain extent  [7]. For example, within an Asian sample, 22% believed that
the ecological label as a sign determines the subsequent response of consumers to the product  [8].
Some scholars also deeply analysed the behaviour mechanism of the impact on consumers' wishes,
because the products made of recycled materials are regarded as "Green", "recycling" and
"Environmental Protection" by consumers, the perceived 'greenness' of a product is significantly and
positively associated with consumer trust and attitude [7,9]. For example, some researchers have
shown that the sense of pride and novelty brought by green products greatly improves consumers'
purchase willingness [9].
In summary, at present, a substantial body of literatures mainly focus on the impact of products
made of recycled materials on consumers' purchase intention from the perspective of environment
and cognition. Compared with the overall discussion, fast fashion brands need more detailed research
to di scuss the subtle differences between the two types of environmental protection strategies of
upgrading and recycling and consumers' wishes. Therefore, this paper focuses on the differences of
psychological action path behind the two types of environmental protection strategies to fill in the
gap in the field of circular economy.
3. Theoretical analysis and research hypothesis
3.1. Environmental protection strategies and consumers' purchase intention
Both the upcycled products and recycled products are based on the existing materials, and the
distinction lies in whether they decompose the original products. The upcycled products made from
materials need more creativity, aesthetic quality and exquisite workmanship, which also means that
consumers will think that the upcycled items as more creative and novel than recycled ones [9]. And
fast fashion consumers are mainly young groups, most of them have higher demand for "trend",
"personality" and "expression". Because consumers who buy green products can show their green
environmental protection attitude, and the upcycled products ca n provide additional benefits related

to consumers' unique needs, so the upcycled products is more suitable for this demand (for example,
the "Join Life" series created by sustainable materials and the upgrading workshop of UNIQLO
launched by ZARA in recent years, as shown in Figure 1)  [2,9]. In contrast, recycled products are
mostly in the form of single recycling, lacking novelty and creativity. Therefore, for fast fashion
products, compared with the Recycle type of environmental conservation strategy, the Upcycle type
of environmental protection strategy may have a more positive impact on consumers' purchase
intention. Thus:
H1: For fast fashion products, the positive impact of the Upcycle type of environmental protection
strategy on the purchase intention is significantly stronger than the Recycle type of environmental
protection strategy.

3.2. Perceived value
Nowadays, when facing sustainable products, consumers not only pay attention to functional value,
but also increasingly attach importance to their social value and environmental responsibility. Studies
have shown that function, social morality and emotiona l values exert a positive influence on
consumers' attitudes toward sustainable products, and at the same time, these values foster favourable
attitudes toward consumers' green purchase intention  [11]. Under the premise above, consumer
perceived value is of paramount importance in the formation of consumer purchase intention. In the
past, many scholars defined perceived value as the total perception of consumers on product utility
based on their own and product perception [12]. Later, more multidimensional explanations were put
forward, and different scholars' definitions of perceived value were mentioned. The basic definition
is that green, function, emotion and social value are important factors of perceived value  [5].
Upcycling will not reduce the original quality and composition of the product and upgrade it  [13].
Compared with other products, the upcycled products are cost-effective, which means that consumers
may more easily perceive the value of the upcycled products [14]. Upcycled products play a privotal
role in the field of environmental protection. The production process requires less energy and
resources. Consumers will recognize the green characteristics of upcycled products and think that
they have environmental protection value [5]. In addition, when fast fashion brands produce products
quickly, they can take the story background of upcycled products as a unique selling point, so that
consumers can easily feel the emotional value  [15]. Recycled products, especially upcycled ones,
serve as a vehicle for self -expression and identity construction. Consumers feel status and pride in
buying two types of products [5,9]. Therefore, perceived value may play an intermediary role between
Upcycle type environmental protection strategies and consumers' purchase intention. To sum up,
make the following assumptions (as shown in Figure 2):
H2: Perceived value plays an intermediary role between Upcycle type environmental protection
strategy and purchase intention.

4. Research
4.1. Data collection
This study adopts the experimental design between single factor groups, the main purpose is to
test the impact of Upcycle type environmental protection strategies and Recycle type environmental
protection strategies on consumers' purchase intention (H1). A t the same time, verify the
psychological mechanism of Upcycle type environmental protection strategies on consumers'
purchase intention. A total of 107 Chinese Credamo users participated in this study. In the past
literature, experiments between subjects were used to obtain the sample survey [9]. This study refers
to this method and manipulates a sustainable fast fashion product (Recycle vs. Upcycle). In the
experiment, the interviewees will read two sustainable products (Recycle vs. Upcycle) launched by a
fast fashion brand. In this study, the k nown fast fashion brand is not used. In addition, no product
pictures will be put in the experiment, to avoid the interviewees' aesthetic preference affecting the
product evaluation. The interviewees read two kinds of recycled or upcycled products launched  by
fast fashion brands. In the experiment, the text content length of fast fashion products is the same,
and the recycling and upcycling are explained.
4.2. Measures
All the measured values are from past literature, using the seven-point scale to measure, with high
reliability and more accurate comparative analysis. The dependent variable is the attitude of
consumers towards the two products. The perception of new product purchase intention was measured
through two items. The interviewees were asked that their overall purchase intention for the product
was high (1= very low, 7= very high). The interviewees were willing to recommend such products to
their friends (1= ext remely reluctant, 7= extremely willing). The perceived value of new products
was measured through seven projects. For the green perception of products, the interviewees were
asked to think that the products can help reduce environmental pollution (1= extre mely disagree, 7=
extremely agree), and that such products conform to the concept of environmental protection (1=
extremely disagree, 7= extremely agree). For product function perception, the interviewees were
asked to pay a slightly higher price for such products (1= extremely reluctant, 7= extremely willing).
For the emotional perception of the product, the interviewees were asked to think that the product has
a unique design and aesthetic (1= very disagree, 7= very agree), that this kind of product is cr eative
and refreshing (1= very disagree, 7= very agree), that buying this kind of product is a kind of self -
expression (1= very disagree, 7= very agree), and that buying this kind of product is a kind of self -
expression (1= very disagree, 7= very agree). For the social perception of products, the interviewees
thought that such products were more meaningful than ordinary fast fashion products (1= extremely
disagree, 7= extremely agree).
In the design among the interviewees, study allocated 107 participants from the Credamo platform
to one of two experimental conditions: the recycling scenario and the upcycling scenario. After
excluding 24 respondents who have never bought fast fashion products due to environmental
protection, this study analyzes the data of the remaining 83 respondents.

4.3. Results
After collection and integration, the samples cover various regions in China, with a high
concentration in North China and East China, which may affect the comprehensive inference of the
cognitive differences of national consumer environmental protection s trategies. Among the
interviewees, there were 67 female interviewees, accounting for 62.6%, and most of them were 21 –
30-year-olds, accounting for 54.2%. In the sample, most of the respondents (70.1%) included
environmental protection strategies or sustainable information into the scope of fashion consumption.
This information provides an important premise for this study to explore sustainable strategies in fast
fashion products - consumers are potential responders with cognitive basis and attitude. Among th e
respondents, 83 (accounting for 77.6%) said that they had bought fast fashion products due to the
environmental protection properties of products. It shows that the environmental protection attribute
has been effectively transformed into the actual purch ase motivation of some consumers and has a
significant consumer purchase intention driving force in the fast fashion consumption scene.
In the two situations, the attitude of the subjects to the two products changed significantly. The
willingness of subjects to purchase Upcycle type products (M=5.56) was higher than that of Recycle
type products (M=5.36). It can be seen that in addition to the perceived value of the interviewees, the
interviewees generally believe that the two products have environmental protection attributes
compared with ordinary fast fashion products. They believe that the environmental protection
attributes of Upcycled products are significantly outperform that of Recycled products
(Mupcycle=6.26, Mrecycle=5.93, P < 0.05). They believe that Upcycle products are more unique and
creative than Recycle products (P < 0.05).
In order to test H2, this study ran the model again, the consumer's purchase intention served as the
outcome variable, and the two circular strategies (Upcycle vs. Recycle) acted as the predictor. In the
above case, when the "Green" and "Emotion" in the in termediary perceived value are added, the
product type failed to show a significant effect on the purchase intention (B=-0.64, P=0.751), but both
perceived values emerged as significant positive predictors of purchase intention (Bgreen=0.408, P <
0.01; Bemotion=0.282, P < 0.05).
5. Conclusion
This study confirmed that, compared with general fast fashion, the two sustainable fast fashion
products have quite significant environmental protection properties, and have a positive attitude
towards sustainable fast fashion products. Consumers show a mo re positive attitude and willingness
to buy upcycled products, because they perceive more obvious value of such products, especially in
green perception and emotional perception. The results confirmed that upcycled products are more in
line with the concept of consumer environmental protection than recycled products, and more unique
and creative - that is, corresponding to "Green" and "Emotional" perception. There was no significant
difference in other perception.
