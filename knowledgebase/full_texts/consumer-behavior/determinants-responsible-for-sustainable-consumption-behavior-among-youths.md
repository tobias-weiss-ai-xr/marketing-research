Abstract: Environmental awareness and concern for the natural environment determine purchasing habits, forming
sustainable consumption patterns, motivating the population to prefer ecological goods and services. This issue is especially
relevant for young people since they will be the consumers of the future, and the progress of humanity in achieving
Sustainable Development Goals depends to  a large extent on the sustainability and eco -orientation of their consumer
behaviour. The article aims to determine the factors that influence the sustainable consumer behavior of young people. The
basis of the study was the results of a survey of 89 young people (aged 20 to 36 years, almost half of whom are 23 to 26 years
old) in the city of Jaipur (India, the capital of the state of Rajasthan), which is a rapidly growing urban center, so the problems
of urban development here are typical of many rapidly developing cities around the world." The questionnaire consists of 34
questions, of which 27 are aimed at determining the determinants of sustainable consumer behavior, and seven are aimed at
evaluating the most sustainable consumer behavior. A five-point Likert scale was used for evaluation. Partial Least Squares
Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM), which helps to identify the relationships between determinants and sustainable
consumer behavior, is performed using Smart PLS. Descriptive statistics, reli ability analysis and correlation analysis are
performed using SPSS. Data suitability for analysis was confirmed using Bartlett's sphericity test and sampling adequacy
measurement results (Kaiser -Meyer-Olkin). The reliability and internal consistency of the  data were confirmed using the
Alpha-Cronbach coefficient, Composite Reliability coefficients and convergent validity / Average variance extracted (AVE)
Values. The discriminant validity and suitability of the data for structural modeling was confirmed using the Fornell-Larker
criteria. The study proposed and empirically tested 10 hypotheses regarding the positive influence of 5 factor variables
(perception of consequences, behavioral control, response efficiency, sensitivity to the environment, and environ mental
knowledge) on 2 resulting variables: behavioral intentions and sustainable consumption behavior. According to the study
results, 6 out of 10 hypotheses were confirmed: ecological knowledge, behavioral control, response efficiency, and
perception of consequences were determinants of behavioral intentions, while sensitivity to the environment was not. At the
same time, only behavioral control and perception of consequences were determinants of sustainable consumer behavior.
The results of the study can be used to develop and implement measures aimed at promoting sustainable consumer behavior
among young people (for example, educational programs and political initiatives), as well as to create marketing plans that
motivate this particular customer group to make sustainable purchase decisions.

Introduction
As the globe struggles to fulfil the demands of a rising population while protecting natural resources, sustainable
consumption has grown in importance i n recent years. Given that they make up a sizeable section of the global
population and will be the consumers of the future, young people must be specifically targeted when advocating
for sustainable purchasing behaviour. Therefore, it is crucial to do study on the factors that influence young
people's consumption behavior.
The aim of this study is to identify the dete rminants responsible for sustainable consumption behavior among
youths in Jaipur, India. “Jaipur, the capital city of the state of Rajasthan, is a rapidly growing urban center and is
representative of the challenges faced by many rapidly developing cities around the” world.
To achieve the study's objective, a structured questionnaire was administered to a sample of 89 youths from Jaipur.
The questionnaire included questions about environmental knowledge, environmental attitude, environmental
concern, and environmental behavior, which are the key determinants of sustainable consumption behavior.
The study's findings will contribute to the growing body of knowledge on sustainable consumption behavior
among youths and inform the development of effective strate gies to promote sustainable consumption behavior
in this demographic. Additionally, this study's findings may have practical implications for policymakers and
practitioners seeking to promote sustainable consumption behavior among youths. The study's results can inform
the design and implementation of interventions aimed at promoting sustainable consumption behavior, such as
education programs and policy initiatives.
In conclusion, this study on the determinants responsible for sustainable consumption behavior among youths in
Jaipur, India, is an essential step towards understanding how to promote sustainable consumption behavior in
rapidly developing urban centers. By identifying the key determinants of sustainable consumption behavior, this
study can infor m the development of effective interventions aimed at promoting sustainable con sumption
behavior among youths.
Literature review
Numerous studies have been done on the connection between environmental awareness and sustainable
consumption practices. Environmental awareness was a strong predictor of sustainable consumption behaviour,
according to previous researcher study on the factors influencing university students' consumption habits (Bölük
et al., 2018). This result supports the idea that know ledge and education are crucial elements in encouraging
sustainable consumption habits.
It is also widely known that environmental attitudes can encourage sustainable consumption patterns.
Environmental attitude has a strong beneficial impact on green purc hase behaviour study on the factors
influencing young consumers in China who make green purchases (Wang et al., 2016) . The significance of
encouraging young people to have positive environmental attitudes as a strategy of encouraging sustainable
purchasing behaviour is highlighted by this research.
Concern for the environment has also been noted as a key factor influencing sustainable consumer behaviour.
Reseracher discovered that environmental concern was a major predictor of pro-environmental behaviour in their
study on the factors influencing pro-environmental behaviour among Malaysian adolescents (Cheah et al., 2020).
This research implies that encouraging young people's environmental awareness is essential for encouraging
sustainable consumption behaviour.
Environmental behaviour and sustainable consumption practises have a complicated relationship that has
generated a lot of discussion. Previous study discovered that environmental behaviour did not substantially predict
green consumer behaviour in their study on the factors influencing young consumers in India (Jindal & Verma,
2020). This research implies that although environmental behaviour may be relevant, other variables may have a
greater impact on young people's sustainable consumption behaviour.

A generation is defined as "a group of people with certain attitudes and behaviors in common  that are different
from other generations" (Kim et al., 2015, p. 278 ). These differences stem from a set of historical events and
related phenomena" (Parry and Urwin, 2011, p. 84), with influences from parents, peers, media, social and
economic events, an d popular culture accumulating to shape common life experiences and value systems that
distinguish one generation from another (Smola and Sutton, 2002).
Despite ongoing discussions among experts, there is no consensus on the generational boundaries of Gene ration
Y. Dolot (2018) suggests that Generation Y includes individuals born between 1980 and 1995, while others
narrow it down to those born between 1977 and 1994/1995 (Huang and Petrick , 2010, Robinson, V.M. and
Schanzel, H.A., 2019). The boundaries of Generation Z also vary, with Turner (2015) specifying individuals born
between 1993 and 2005, while other experts indicate those born after 1995 (Veiga et al., 2017; Dolot, 2018).
During this period, marked by significant global economic and political change s and a massive development of
information and communication technology (ICT), historical experiences, technological advancements, and
behavioral patterns contribute to the distinctive intergenerational characteristics (Dimock, 2019; Wong et al.,
2013). For the purpose of this study, Generation Y encompasses individuals born between 1982 and 1996, while
Generation Z includes those born between 1997 and 2004 (BPS, 2021). Despite both generations experiencing
profound shifts in the external environment and th e pervasive influence of social media (Dimock, 2019), they
exhibit relatively different behavioral characteristics, including travel behavior.
Individuals belonging to Generation Y were in the process of growing up when instant communication
technologies, such as email, short message service, or WhatsApp, ga ined popularity. (Taherdoost, 2016). They
exhibit comfort with an immediate and practical communication style, gravitating towards less formal, friendlier,
and more familiar forms of interaction (Bolton et al., 2013). In terms of travel preferences, Generation Y is drawn
to challenging activities and values quality experiences.
In contrast, members of Generation Z were born and raised in a world dominated by the Internet, smartphones,
video games, and  other advanced technologies. Their worldview is profoundly influenced by the rapid
development of technology (Dolot, 2018; Haddouche and Salomone, 2018; Betz, 2019). Parry and Urwin (2011)
describe Generation Z as a well -suited workforce for the digital a ge. Gen Z individuals lean towards social
activities, display a strong concern for the environment, and express significant interest in voluntourism activities
(Veiga et al., 2017; Cho et al., 2018; Haddouche and Salomone, 2018). They are particularly mind ful of global
warming issues and energy crises that may impact their lives. Consequently, it can be inferred that the exposure
to information technology and the inherent environmental differences experienced from birth are key determinants
influencing the distinct behaviors of both Generation Y and Generation Z.
Methodology
The current study intends to investigate the factors influencing young people's sustainable consumption
behaviour. The study incorporates a survey -based research design and a quantitativ e research methodology. 89
young people from Jaipur, India, who participated in the study as respondents, provided the study's data.
Convenience sampling is used to choose the sample, and respondents are chosen based on their interest in taking
part in the study.
A systematic questionnaire is used to gather the study's data. 34 items make up the questionnaire, comprising 27
items to assess the factors that influence sustainable consumption behaviour and 7 items to gauge sustainable
consumption behaviour itself. A five-point Likert scale, from strongly disagree (1) to strongly agree (5), is used
to evaluate the items.” For all statistical tests used in the study, a significance threshold of 0.05 is used.

Discussion
The study's findings on the determinants of sustainable consumption behavior among Jaipur consumers open
avenues for insightful discussions and strategic considerations. The identified factors – environmental awareness,
perceived behavioral control, consequences perception, and response efficacy – collectively form a
