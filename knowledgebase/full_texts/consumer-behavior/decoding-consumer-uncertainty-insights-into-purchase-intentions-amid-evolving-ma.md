ABSTRACT

Purpose: This study conducts a systematic literature review of 759 Scopus -indexed
publications (2014 –2024) to analyze the evolving role of consumer uncertainty in
shaping purchasing decisions.  Consumer uncertainty has become a pivotal factor
influencing purchase intentions, particularly in the wake of dynamic market
conditions, technological advancements, and socio-political disruptions.

Design and Methodology: Employing descriptive bibliometric analysis and an input-
process-output model of consumer behavior, this research identifies macro -
environmental (economic, technological, political, and socio -cultural), micro -
environmental (social media influence, marketing strategies and trust mechanisms),
and psychological drivers of consumer uncertainty.

Findings: Findings indicate that uncertainty significantly alters consumer risk
perceptions, leading to delays in purchase decisions, increased preference for trusted
brands, and heightened reliance on online reviews. Furthermore, marketing strategies
that emphasize transparency, trust-building, and digital transformation play a critical
role in mitigating uncertainty and fostering positive purchase intentions.

Implications: This study contributes to consumer behavior literature by offering a
structured framework for understanding uncertainty’s impact on purchasing behaviors
and proposes strategic implications for businesses navigating uncertain market
landscapes.

Keywords: Consumer Uncertainty, Purchase Intentions, Bibliometric Analysis,
Input-Process-Output Model, Systematic Literature Review

significantly influences their behavior. Similarly, online consumer buying behavior is more prone to
consumer uncertainty (Alrousan et al., 2022) . Uncertainty is defined as “the extent to which
environmental situations cannot be predicted because of incomplete or inappropriate information”
(Grote, 2009). An individual feels psychological stress and feels him/herself in threat in uncertain

uncertainty in online marketplace context as “the extent to which they cannot anticipate the result of

Consumer uncertainty also plays a vital role in online purchase willingness (Wu & Wu, 2022) .
Customers are unable to make rational decisions if the face choice uncertainty  (Zhang et al., 2020).
Some researcher explain that the spatial and temporal distance between the retailer and consumer
lead to uncertainty and psychological distance (Zhang et al., 2022) . Consumer uncertainty has also
been found to be a potential mediator between brand cues (Islam & Hussain, 2023) , customer
assessment (Horvath et al., 2021) and purchase behavior.
In prior consumer behavior literature, a vast number of studies have emerged especially after the
inception of e-commerce platforms and then in covid -19 period. However, the knowledge in such a
fast growing and diverse field remains fragmented, thus there is a need for systemization and
organization of this vast body of knowledge (Snyder, 2019; Mehmooda et al., 2018). This study fills
this gap by identifying 759 relevant studies in Scopus database, indexed up to 30 September 2025,
for systematic processing. Past literature has created a general perspective of consumer purchase
intentions with respect to consumer uncer tainty. These studies have identified several types of
uncertainty faced by consumers and also compared the offline and online consumer behavior patterns
regarding uncertainty.
Prior studies have created a general and global perspective of consumer uncertainty and online
consumer purchase intentions, even before and after covid -19. Such studies have sought to identify
various antecedents and behavioral outcomes of consumer uncert ainty, its influence on culturally
different consumers and different products/services. However, this study is required because the
online marketplace differs fundamentally from previous buying contexts in a number of ways. First,
it is truly global in nat ure (Brem et al., 2021 ; Taseer, 2022 ). Second, it aligns with the accelerated
emergence of diverse disruptive technologies, collectively referred to as ‘digital transformation
(Abdel-Basset et al., 2021; Said et al., 2026).

2. Literature Review
Consumer uncertainty, as defined by Al-Adwan et al. (2022), encompasses various aspects, including
concerns about the seller's reliability, the quality and safety of the product, and the potential risks
associated with the purchase. This uncertainty can stem from a lack of information, conflicting
information, or  the rapid evolution of the market. In the digital age, consumers are constantly
bombarded with information, making it challenging to discern reliable sources and accurately assess
product value. This information overload can exacerbate uncertainty and hin der decision -making
processes (Aljanabi, 2023). Furthermore, the rise of e -commerce has introduced new dimensions of
uncertainty, such as concerns about online security, delivery reliability, and the authenticity of
products (Horvath et al., 2021).
The impact of uncertainty on consumer behavior is multifaceted. Studies have shown that high levels
of uncertainty can lead to a decrease in purchase intentions, increased risk aversion, and a shift towards
safer, more familiar choices (Alsoud & Othman, 2018; Islam & Hussain, 2023; Kanani & Glavee -
Geo, 2021). Consumers may delay purchases, reduce spending, or opt for lower-priced alternatives to

where individuals tend to be more risk-averse and seek stability (Han et al., 2021; Said et al., 2026).
Understanding the specific factors that contribute to consumer uncertainty in the current context is
crucial. The ongoing digital transformation, characterized by rapid technological advancements and
evolving consumer behaviors, presents unique challenges. The rise of social media and the increasing
influence of online reviews and social media influencers can both inform and mislead consumers (Wen
et al., 2020) , further complicating the decision -making process. Additionally, the global economic
landscape, marked by geopolitical instability and economic volatility, can significantly impact
consumer confidence and spending patterns.
By comprehensively examining the factors that contribute to consumer uncertainty and their impact
on purchase intentions, businesses can develop effective strategies to address these concerns. This
includes providing consumers with transparent and accurate  information, building strong brand trust,
and offering flexible and convenient return policies. By effectively managing consumer uncertainty,
businesses can enhance customer satisfaction, build long -term relationships, and gain a competitive
advantage in the dynamic marketplace.
This addition provides further insights into the nature of consumer uncertainty, its impact on consumer
behavior, and the key factors that contribute to it in the contemporary context. It highlights the
challenges posed by the digital age and the evolving economic landscape, emphasizing the need for
businesses to adapt their strategies to effectively address consumer concerns and navigate the
complexities of the modern marketplace.
3. Methodology
Although meta-analytic or statistical synthesis methods were considered, they were not applicable to
the present study. The reviewed literature on consumer uncertainty and purchase intentions is highly
heterogeneous in terms of theoretical models, research designs, and outcome variables, which prevents
the quantitative aggregation of effect sizes required for meta -analysis. Therefore, a bibliometric and
content analysis approach was adopted to identify publication trends  and thematic clustering using
bibliometric mapping tools . This method enables a descriptive mapping and conceptual synthesis of
the literature rather than statistical generalization, aligning with the study’s objectives.  A systematic
literature review approach comprising of several steps is implied in this study, as shown in figure 1.

3.1. Research Objectives:
This study describes the systematic literature review on consumer uncertainty and consumer purchase
intentions during the last decade. Therefore, following are the objectives of this study:
RO1: To identify thematic connections/relationships in past research on consumer uncertainty and

3.2. Inclusion Criteria
A well-defined inclusion criterion has been developed for this study inspired from previous literature
(Cruz-Cárdenas et al., 2021). Journal articles from 2014 to 2025 (dated:09/30/2025) and must address
uncertainty and purchase intentions have been included in this study. Moreover, articles should be
from Scopus, written only in English, and having an appropriate quality level.
3.3. Search Strategies
The inclusion criteria were operationalized to develop research strategies. Scopus database was used
for drawing literature as Singh et al. (2020)  suggest that Scopus maintains an excellent balance
between coverage and quality. Scopus was selected because it offers extensive coverage of business,
management, marketing, psychology, and technology -related journals while maintaining strict
indexing standards. Previous bibliometric studies have demonstrated that Scopus provides broad er
journal coverage than Web of Science in social sciences and consumer behavior research. Although
reliance on a single database may introduce database bias, this approach e nsured consistency in

and alignment with the research objectives.

5.1. Macro-environmental Forces
The micro -environment is affected by the macro -environmental forces (Kotler & Keller, 2015) .
Customer is the centre of attention for the microenvironment in this study. The people or organizations
closer to consumer make up its microenvironment such as family, friends, media and company (Cruz-
Cárdenas et al., 2021) . Regarding consumer uncertainty and purchase intentions: five major
macroenvironmental forces are: economic, political, technological, socio -cultural and legal
environments. Socio -cultural, economic, political and technological environments are of utmost
importance. Numerous studies indicate how consumer uncertainty influence the purchase intentions
of consumers and induces them not to buy high tech products (Hussain et al., 2024; Islam & Hussain,
2023). Specifically, political instability leads to economic downturns which increases consumer
uncertainty and ultimately affects the purchase behavior of consumers particularly in developing
countries such as Bangladesh (Rahman & Sloan, 2017) , Saudi Arabia (Azam, 2017) , Russia
(Rebiazina et al., 2019).
Economic performance is closely linked to political-legal landscape. Evidence suggests that monetary
policies of the government influence inflation, tax rates, disposable and discretionary income levels
that ultimately affects consumption and spendings (Olusola et al., 2022). Another aspect of political-
legal landscape is the trust in government bodies. The increased consumer trust in government bodies
leads to a sense of security and lowers their uncertainty leading to favorable purchase intentions (J.
Wang et al., 2022) . Empirical research across diverse contexts -including UK (Hanna et al., 2021) ,
European economies (European Commission, 2018), and BRICS countries such as Brazil (Fundação
Getulio Vargas, 2014)- illustrates how political and economic factors influence consumer confidence,
uncertainty and purchasing behavior.
Socio-cultural factor is also important as a culture of high uncertainty avoidance might lead to
increased consumer uncertainty (Bugaje et al., 2023), and negatively influence the purchase intentions

consumer behavior, affecting both uncertainty perceptions and consumers’ response to risk in purchase

5.2. Micro-environmental Forces
As discussed, economic environment characterized by inflation and market volatility influences
consumer confidence and spending behavior (Hussain et al., 2024) , while the monetary policy shifts
and regulatory changes marks the political-legal environment. The combination of these forces creates
a dynamic microenvironment through which consumers and businesses weave in trying to understand
political landscapes an d legal frameworks that aggravate uncertainties and influence purchase
decisions. Section 5.3 covers the interactions between businesses and consumers.
Amid economic and political uncertainty, consumers rely heavily on information to make informed
purchase decisions (Davvetas et al., 2021). Social media serves as an important source of information
for this purpose (Kwahk & Kim, 2017) . Commonly, social media discussions revolve around
economic policies (Muna & Ausat, 2023), market directions (Ortiz, 2023), and changes in regulations
(Tian & Chunara, 2020) . However, social media also induces consumer uncertainty, especially in
developing countries (Malik & Choi, 2021; Shin et al., 2017) . Rising inflation, dynamic nature of
monetary efforts, official announcements and global news promote this attitude. Moreover, social
media and news significantly influence the way consumers interact with online platforms and websites
of businesses while making purchase decisions  (Mahmood & Sismeiro, 2022) . The covid -19
pandemic is the recent example where the scarcity news spread from social media that leads to
increased uncertainty among consumers and they indulged in panic buying Arafat et al., 2021; Naeem,
2021), and impulsive behavior (Elisa et al., 2022; Nuseir, 2020).
Therefore, despite making shopping easier, the social media and news channels have also contributed
to increased consumer uncertainty (Naeem, 2021) . Previous studies focusing on social media have
greatly contributed to the extensive knowledge on the micro -environmental effects on consumer
behavior (Choufani, 2023; Panda & Mishra, 2023) , these studies have laid a strong foundation for
understanding the interaction between political, legal, and economic factors and consumer decision -
making.

Companies can develop and control the marketing tools and strategies so they are of optimal
significance. Therefore, consumer behavior models typically consider them independently of their

the other 3P’s (price, place and promotion) in the marketing mix (Kotler & Armstrong, 2018).
The main elements of marketing strategies in consumer uncertainty context are mitigating perceived
risks and enhancing perceived benefits for the customer. Two main risks that marketing strategies need
to be addressing upon is trust in online transactions and economic uncertainty (Al-Adwan, Al-Debei,

purchase decisions when they perceive economic uncertainty, this association is moderated by the
consumers’ perceived risk of online transactions (Islam & Hussain, 2023) , emphasizing the need for

During periods of heightened economic uncertainty, consumer demand for high -tech products has
shifted, with a focus on essential items such as home office equipment, personal gadgets, and advanced
health monitoring devices (Anderson et al., 2021) . Consequently, implementing or increasing
promotions for these high-tech items is a recommended strategy. Additionally, for small businesses in
the high -tech sector, the speed of adaptation and digital transformation is crucial. Many small
businesses have  survived by adopting elementary digital transformation strategies, including
leveraging social media for sales and incorporating home delivery services (Khurana et al., 2022) .
These efforts help mitigate consumer uncertainty and enhance purchase intentions by providing
convenience and reliability.
Therefore, even though the results are intriguing, more research is necessary due to the critical
necessity of studies on marketing techniques in the context of consumer uncertainty and purchase
intentions. Because the political and economic landscapes are  ever-changing, businesses need to
constantly adjust their tactics. Interestingly, case studies and experimental methodologies—which are
especially useful for analyzing how marketing techniques affect consumer behavior —are not often
used in research (Alsharif et al., 2022; Baek & Kim, 2022) . By using these approaches more widely,
companies may be able to gain a better understanding of how to reduce consumer apprehension and
increase purchase intentions, particularly for high-tech products.

5.5. Purchasing Behavior
Purchase behavior is the out of consumer behavior models. This output is decided after deciding with

observed worldwide (Ali et al., 2023; Sharma, 2024) .  Another important aspect is the mode of

consumers now seek information online before buying the products. No doubt that online purchases
have made it easier for consumers to purchase (Purwanto, 2022), but still it has also complicated the
purchase process by elongating the duration of purchase as customer takes more time to in

Guthrie et al. (2021)  examine the increase in online shopping during the pandemic and the potential

behavior in the post -pandemic era will likely be a dynamic blend of pre -existing habits and new,
technology-driven practices. However, given that consumers will face new difficulties in the post -
pandemic future (Goolsbee & Syverson, 2021), this fusion of traditional and modern consumer habits
is probably going to be dynamic (in different proportions) and innovative .

6. Discussion, Implications and Limitations
As the global marketplace continues to confront rapid changes and economic fluctuations,
understanding the undercurrents that drive consumer behavior becomes increasingly crucial for
businesses aiming to thrive. In this context, the correlation between con sumer personality traits and
their purchasing intentions gains significant attention. Larsen and Buss (2024) suggest that individual
characteristics, particularly traits such as agreeableness, profoundly influence how consumers navigate
uncertainty. A heightened level of agreeableness often leads individuals to be more receptive to the
opinions and suggestions o f others (Graziano & Tobin, 2024) , thereby shaping their purchasing
decisions during times of ambiguity.
In this intricate web of psychological and economic dynamics, gender and racial factors further
complicate the landscape of consumer behavior. The work of Garcia and Harithsa (2024) exemplifies
this complexity, as their study examines how different demographics adapt their payment preferences
under economic uncertainty. By integrating insights from personality psychology with a focus on
demographic variables, we glean a more nuanced understanding of how uncertainty impacts purchase
intentions across diverse consumer categories.
6.1 Theoretical Implications
This study advances the consumer behavior literature by systematically synthesizing fragmented
evidence on consumer uncertainty and purchase intentions within an Input –Process–Output (IPO)
framework. The findings suggest that consumer uncertainty functions  as a cross -level mechanism
linking macro-environmental conditions with individual purchasing behavior. Economic instability,
political uncertainty, technological disruptions, and socio -cultural influences shape consumers’
perceptions of risk and subsequen tly influence their purchase intentions (Braun & Zenker, 2022;
Davvetas et al., 2021; Hussain et al., 2024). Rather than operating as an isolated psychological
construct, consumer uncertainty translates external environmental changes into behavioral outcom es
such as purchase delays, increased risk aversion, and stronger preference for trusted brands.

A second theoretical contribution emerges from the dual role of digital transformation in consumer
decision-making. The reviewed literature demonstrates that digital technologies simultaneously
reduce and generate uncertainty. On one hand, online reviews, social media interactions, and e -
commerce platforms provide consumers with greater access to information and facilitate decision -
making (Kwahk & Kim, 2017; Purwanto, 2022). On the other hand, information overload, privacy
concerns, misinformation, and perc eived transaction risks create new forms of uncertainty that
influence purchase intentions (Aljanabi, 2023; Horvath et al., 2021; Malik & Choi, 2021). This finding
extends existing understanding of digital consumer behavior by highlighting the paradoxical nature of
technology as both an uncertainty-reducing and uncertainty-generating force.
The review further identifies trust as a central mechanism through which consumers cope with
uncertainty. Across diverse contexts, trust consistently emerged as a factor that mitigates perceived
risk and strengthens purchase intentions. Trust generated thr ough brand reputation, institutional
credibility, online reviews, secure transaction systems, and transparent communication was found to
reduce consumers’ uncertainty and facilitate decision -making (Al -Adwan, Al -Debei, et al., 2022;
Islam & Hussain, 2023; Kanani & Glavee-Geo, 2021). This finding reinforces the importance of trust
within contemporary consumer behavior theories and suggests that uncertainty and trust should be
examined as interconnected constructs rather than independent phenomena.
Another important theoretical insight concerns the contextual nature of uncertainty. The review
indicates that consumers’ responses to uncertainty vary across cultural and demographic settings.
Studies conducted in high uncertainty -avoidance environments suggest stronger risk-averse behavior
and greater sensitivity to uncertainty -related information (Al-Adwan, Al-Debei, et al., 2022; Bugaje
et al., 2023; Han et al., 2021). Similarly, variations in age, gender, and personality traits influence how
consumers perceive and respond to uncertain purchasing situations (Garcia & Harithsa, 2024; Jani et
al., 2014; Karpinska-Krakowiak, 2021). These findings suggest that future consumer behavior theories
should incorporate greater consideration of cultural and individu al-level differences when examining
uncertainty-driven purchase decisions.
Finally, the study contributes methodologically by demonstrating the suitability of the IPO framework
for organizing and synthesizing literature on consumer uncertainty. Unlike narrower theoretical
perspectives that focus primarily on technology adoption o r specific behavioral outcomes, the IPO
framework enables a holistic understanding of how environmental stimuli, consumer characteristics,
decision-making processes, and behavioral outcomes interact under conditions of uncertainty (Cruz -
Cárdenas et al., 20 21; Schiffman et al., 2015). By integrating evidence across these dimensions, the
framework provides a descriptive bibliometric analysis  for advancing future research on consumer
behavior in increasingly uncertain market environments.

6.2 Practical Implications
This study highlights consumer uncertainty as an important mediator in consumer behavior outcomes.
Therefore, marketers need to consider this factor as a significant contributor to consumer behavior and
shape their marketing strategies and tactics accordin gly. The need for transparency, authenticity and
secure transaction mechanisms for customers is needed to mitigate their uncertainty in digital
environments. Responsive customer support and personalized communication can also address the
anxiety of customers in uncertain political, economic and sociocultural conditions. Readily available
information and use of AI technology by firms for handling customer queries aids in decision making
and boost consumer confidence in online marketplaces.
Businesses need to implement omnichannel strategies to ensure seamless digital experiences and shape
their offerings reflecting both hedonic and utilitarian needs, given the increasing traffic on e-commerce
sites. Uncertainty avoidance behavior varies acro ss cultures, as the results depict that most of the
uncertainty-based studies are from south Asian region because of highly risk averse people. Hence,
businesses (particularly multinational companies) need to customize their marketing communication
with local customer communities. By decoding the multifaceted influences of personality, gender, and
race on purchasing decisions, this study paves the way for more effective consumer engagement and
ultimately improves business outcomes amidst evolving market realities. As businesses strive to better
connect with their target audiences, the synthesis of psychological insights and demographic analysis
stands as a powerful tool in addressing the challenges posed by a fluctuating economic landscape.
Understanding how uncertainty influences purchase intentions across various customer categories is
made easier by the correlation between personality and consumer behavior. As suggested by Garcia
and Harithsa (2024) , a higher level of agreeableness indicates acceptance of others' opinions, which
can significantly affect purchasing decisions during uncertain periods. Therefore, by leveraging
insights into consumer personality traits, firms can better tailor their mark eting strategies to address
the nuanced behaviors of different customer segments, ultimately fostering stronger connections and
enhancing purchase intentions amid evolving market dynamics.
Second, firms need to think about how to handle customers’ risk and uncertainty perceptions. This
danger has been divided into two main categories: fraud and data misuse in e-commerce transactions,
as well as the risk of false information. Businesses can use security procedures, technology integration
and combination, and communication and marketing strategies to mitigate the perceived risk of fraud
in online transactions. In the latter case, using the company image to inspire customer confidence will
be the most effective tactic (Lv et al., 2020; Troise et al., 2021) . Additionally, during the pandemic,
promotions that lowered costs and increased benefits were helpful for consumers who were still unsure
about making purchases online (Lv et al., 2020; Troise et al., 2021).

6.3. Limitations and future research directions
This study has some limitations. Although the literature review was conducted using the Scopus
database, which is known for providing a solid mix of quality and extensive coverage (Singh et al.,
2020), it is important to note that certain articles not indexed in Scopus may reflect a variance in
quality. Consequently, while this choice was pivotal for ensuring a manageable overview of literature
within a constrained timeframe, it may also result in the omission of potentially relevant studies. This
study has adopted IPO model for literature systemization, while beneficial for structuring intricate
relationships, necessarily enforces a linear representation of consumer behavior. Consumer decision -
making u nder uncertainty is often iterative and recursive, with feedback loops between inputs,
processes, and outcomes that the IPO model may only partially capture. Future studies should explore
other frameworks like S -O-R models to enrich theoretical understandi ng. This study is based on
secondary articles review than primary data thus reflecting methodological bias as most of the research
articles used cross-sectional or survey designs limiting the ability of this study to capture the evolving
consumer behavior trends. New studies can utilize different methodologies and research designs
(longitudinal studies or qualitative designs) to better capture the reality. This study focuses on digital
transformation and post-pandemic consumer behavior; however, these pheno mena are still unfolding
and require more research as they evolve in future. Moreover, the results indicate that scholars from
China and USA are more active in consumer uncertainty research, which highlights the need of
studying this concept along with its  antecedents and outcomes in other contexts as well. To measure
consumer groups in technology marketplaces prior to the pandemic, technology ready index scale
(Parasuraman & Colby, 2014) , was one of several measures used in numerous studies conducted in
different countries. Strong findings on customer segments and their sizes were obtained from the
studies. Therefore, it is reasonable to wonder how up -to-date this knowledge is given the C OVID-19
pandemic's quick technological adoption. Future research can therefore ascertain how the COVID -19
pandemic altered the ways in which consumer segments used technologies, how they evolved in terms
of their significance, and whether an update to the current measuring tools (scales) is required to
appropriately capture the new dynamics of consumer behavior in post-pandemic markets.
