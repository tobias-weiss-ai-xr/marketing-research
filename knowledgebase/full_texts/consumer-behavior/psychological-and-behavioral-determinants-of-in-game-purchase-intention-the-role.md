Abstract
The main purpose of this study is to determine the antecedents of online game purchase intention. The theoretical
background of the research is based on the Flow Experience Theory. In this context, the study examines the
relationships between ﬂow experience, online game addiction, smartphone addiction, stickiness, e-WOM, and in-
game purchase intention. Data were obtained from 870 participants via an online survey using the convenience
sampling technique. To test the hypotheses of the research, PLS-SEM was employed using the SmartPLS software.
The results of the study show that ﬂow experience and e-WOM are the variables that directly affect in-game purchase
intention. In addition, ﬂow experience was found to have a signi ﬁcant effect on online game addiction, smartphone
addiction, and stickiness. Furthermore, the effects of ﬂow experience, online game addiction, smartphone addiction,
and stickiness on e-WOM were statistically con ﬁrmed. By identifying the determinants of in-game purchase
intention, this study makes a theoretical contribution to the literature on consumer (user/player) behavior in digital
gaming environments. The ﬁndings speci ﬁcally demonstrate how ﬂow experience, different forms of addiction,
stickiness, and e-WOM are associated with in-game purchase intention, thereby advancing scholarly discussions on
consumer behavior within the digital gaming context. From a practical standpoint, the results provide guidance for
game and application developers, marketing professionals, and managers in designing strategies aimed at enhancing
user engagement and purchase-related outcomes. Finally, future research is encouraged to examine the indirect effects
of the variables included in this study on in-game purchase intention.

technology by integrating them into their daily lives. A wide range of opportunities are constantly within reach,

they also give rise to the concept of Technology Addiction when viewed from another perspective. Similar to other
forms of addiction (such as gambling or substance dependence), technology addiction can negatively impact daily life
and lead to behavioral issues. Activities such as gaming, engaging with social media, and online shopping may result
in detachment from real life and can transform the individuals into irresponsible and dependent users. Individuals that

This current study is based on the Flow Experience Theory. This theory, according to Csikszentmihalyi ( 1997),
refers to the optimal psychological state in which an individual is fully engaged in an activity. It is often characterized
by a balance between challenge and skill, leading to deep immersion and focused attention on a given task. The ﬂow
state reﬂects positive experiences in which individuals operate at their full potential. These positive experiences may
include being alone with one ’s thoughts, deriving enjoyment, losing track of time, experiencing self-ef ﬁcacy, and
demonstrating goal-oriented attitudes. However, it may also bring about certain drawbacks such as inattention and
low self-awareness (Engeser, 2012; Partington et al., 2009). One of its most notable consequences is that the feeling
of happiness experienced during the activity, may potentially lead to depression, repetitive behaviors, and addiction
(Partington et al., 2009; Wanner et al., 2006). Eisenberger et al. ( 2005) indicated that the ﬂow experience is a strong
predictor of individual performance in online environments. The literature shows that ﬂow, encompassing an indi-
vidual’s mental and psychological state, has been examined in contexts such as shopping, gaming, communication
within virtual communities, and smartphone use (Bilgihan et al., 2015; Chang & Zhu, 2012; Hamari et al., 2016).
Additionally, in a study conducted by Tuncer ( 2021) using the affordance lens, information capabilities such as
visibility, voice, and guided shopping are positioned as stimulants, ﬂow experience, and trust organisms, while it is
concluded that the ﬂow and trust feelings created by technological features in users trigger social commerce intention.
Similarly, in a study by Yu et al. ( 2025) using ﬂow theory to examine the factors shaping purchase intention on live
streaming e-commerce (LSC) platforms, the ﬂow experience created by factors such as interaction and entertainment
positively affects purchase intention through trust and continuous viewing intention.
Hsu & Lu ( 2004) conﬁrmed the effect of ﬂow experience on the behavior of online game users in their study. Flow
experience increases the player ’s enjoyment of the game ’s natural challenges, causing them to perceive in-game
purchases as elements that negatively affect the experience and leading to purchase resistance. However, the pleasure
the player derives increases the time spent in the system and their attachment to the game, reversing resistance and
transforming it into a long-term consumption habit. In other words, although ﬂow suppresses the immediate intention
to purchase, it binds the individual to the game through the continuity effect it creates and strengthens the tendency to
spend (Hamari, 2015). Similarly, Goli & Vemuri ( 2021) found in their study based on ﬂow theory and consumption
values theory that users ’ game ﬂow experience and satisfaction have a positive effect on the intention to purchase in-
game products in mobile games.
Addiction, which can be described as the uncontrolled and excessive use of games, can cause individuals with high
levels of addiction to make irrational expenditures in order to maintain their status within the game or increase their
rate of progress. Fear of losing and the game occupying an important place in the individual ’s life are factors that
increase the amount spent. Chou & Ting (2003) revealed the correlation between game addiction and loyalty and
purchasing behavior. The relationship between in-game purchases and addiction is explained through ﬂow and habit
loops. According to the authors, it is not just the game itself that is addictive, but the “ﬂow” state it creates; in-game
purchases are repetitive consumption actions that feed this ﬂow state or are performed while in that state. Li and
Wang ( 2013)d e ﬁned online game addiction in terms of duration, scale scores, and behavioral outcomes, drawing
attention to the tendency to incur debt for in-game items and the withdrawal symptoms experienced when quitting the
game. Furthermore, the authors positioned game addiction as a driving factor triggering uncontrolled purchasing
behavior, emphasizing that it leads to consequences such as debt and psychological stress.
Based on the loyalty that arises from a player ’s long-term emotional or cognitive relationship with a speci ﬁc game,
a player may make purchases in an online environment to support the game or feel part of the community. According
to Kim & Son ( 2009), in addition to being revisited by users so that online service providers can continue their
activities, attachment is important in behavioral outcomes such as willingness to pay and resistance to alternatives.

Researchers suggest that loyalty refers to keeping consumers in the system through dedication or constraints, and the
stronger the loyalty, the greater the likelihood of consumers shopping on the site (purchase intent) and recommending
it to others (word-of-mouth).
In today ’s world, where the impact of traditional advertising has signi ﬁcantly diminished and digitalization has
increased, consumers have begun to place greater trust in consumer-to-consumer communication, such as blogging,
mobile messaging, and word-of-mouth marketing, rather than brands ’ one-sided mass messages (Lin et al., 2014).
Especially in the mobile gaming category, experiences shared between users (e-WOM) directly in ﬂuence the player ’s
purchase intent by increasing their loyalty to the game.
In this integrated model, the organismic process is represented by the psychological and cognitive changes created
in the individual by the ﬂow experience, smartphone and gaming addiction, and emotional attachment (Jacoby,
2002). The intense focus and state of enjoyment created by ﬂow lead the individual to psychological attachment and,
further, to addiction, thereby in ﬂuencing purchasing decisions. Another stimulus, e-WOM, triggers the purchase
intention by reinforcing the individual ’s internal state with the help of the trust factor. Therefore, bringing these
variables together in a single integrated model is important in terms of explaining, in a theoretical whole, how
environmental stimuli ﬁlter through the individual ’s psychological state and transform into a behavioral response,
beyond “how much ” is purchased.
The primary aim of this study is to identify the antecedents of online in-game purchase intention. In this context,
the study examines the relationships among ﬂow experience, online gaming addiction, smartphone addiction,
stickiness, e-WOM, and in-game purchase intention. Accordingly, the study seeks to address the following research
questions:
1. Do e-WOM and ﬂow experience have an effect on in-game purchase intention?
2. Does ﬂow experience in ﬂuence online gaming addiction, smartphone addiction, stickiness, and e-WOM?
Literature review and hypothesis development
Flow experience and addiction
Flow experience is a cognitive process in which individuals experience feelings such as concentration, enjoyment,
skill, and immersion during human-computer interaction (Csikszentmihalyi, 1975; Ghani & Deshpande, 1994). It also
serves as a precursor to the development of addiction and user attachment. Among the variables included in this
study, online gaming addiction, smartphone addiction, and stickiness are unlikely to occur in online environments
without the presence of ﬂow experience. Online gaming addiction is regarded as a psychological dependency on
playing online games (Lu & Wang, 2008). This form of addiction is characterized by an intense craving to play and a
sense of withdrawal after discontinuing gameplay (Qin et al., 2007). Both online gaming addiction and smartphone
addiction are considered subtypes of internet addiction. In online environments, excessive attachment, often referred
to as stickiness, is de ﬁned by users ’ repeated returns to the platform, frequent visits, and extended duration of stay
(Wu et al., 2008). Online gaming addiction is de ﬁned as a behavioral pattern in which an individual exhibits
symptoms such as an intense desire to engage in gaming at all times, a sense of obligation, inability to resist, and
repeated participation. These symptoms often result in the deterioration of physical, psychological, and social
functioning in individuals ’ lives (Kiatsakaret & Chen, 2022). In cases of gaming addiction, players tend to repeatedly
engage in gameplay due to the happiness, concentration, and sense of belonging they experience as part of the ﬂow
state. This tendency to repeat the activity leads to addictive behavior (Chou & Ting, 2003). A player who frequently
experiences ﬂow while playing online games is more likely to develop stronger attachment and addiction to the game
(Choi & Kim, 2004; Hsiao & Chen, 2016; Lu & Wang, 2008; Su et al., 2016). Based on this, the following
hypothesis is proposed:
H1: Flow experience positively in ﬂuences online gaming addiction .

Smartphone addiction is de ﬁned as the uncontrolled and excessive use of smartphones (Lian et al., 2016; Noe
et al., 2019). In terms of technological advancements, the most signi ﬁcant growth has occurred in the mobile gaming
industry (Liao et al., 2020). The mobile gaming market reached 77.2 billion USD in 2020, with approximately 3.5
billion players, equivalent to nearly half of the global population (Newzoo, 2021)For example, India, which is
frequently highlighted in the literature as a case study due to its large user base, rapid smartphone penetration, and
high engagement with mobile gaming platforms, and is cited as a representative example of emerging digital gaming
markets, is one of the leading markets for mobile gaming applications and accounted for 60% of global online gaming
revenue in 2019 (Williams, 2020). The rapid expansion of mobile technologies and digital entertainment platforms
has intensi ﬁed individuals ’ engagement with smartphones, thereby increasing concerns regarding excessive and
uncontrolled usage behaviors. The uncontrolled and excessive use of smartphones, which negatively affects indi-
viduals’ ability to ful ﬁll their responsibilities by altering their lifestyle, is de ﬁned as smartphone addiction (Otsuka
et al., 2023). A positive relationship has been reported between smartphone addiction and ﬂow experience in contexts
involving internet and media use (Khang et al., 2013; Hernández-Neuta et al., 2019) as well as in cases of social
network addiction (Kaur et al., 2020). Other studies have also identi ﬁed a signi ﬁcant connection between the ﬂow
process and smartphone addiction (Chen et al., 2017; Lee & Shin, 2016; Wang et al., 2020). Based on this, the
following hypothesis is proposed:
H2: Flow experience positively in ﬂuences smartphone addiction .
Stickiness is considered a precursor to a form of user or consumer loyalty. Moreover, it is regarded as a foun-
dational element for the pro ﬁtability and sustainability of retailers and brands (Kim et al., 2016). The primary factor
that triggers addiction, however, is the ﬂow experience (Chou & Ting, 2003). In online environments, stickiness
refers to a user ’s prolonged engagement with a platform, repeated visits, and continued use even when confronted
with alternative options or distractions (Li et al., 2006). In other words, it is described as the development of a sense
of loyalty by users after experiencing a particular product or service (Bao, 2017). In their study, Zhu et al. ( 2018)
found that ﬂow experience signi ﬁcantly in ﬂuenced user loyalty and continued usage intention on internet-based
platforms. Other studies have similarly reported a substantial and positive effect (Sui, 2015; Barchino et al., 2012).
Duan et al. ( 2017) also demonstrated that, in the gaming industry, ﬂow experience positively impacts users ’
stickiness. Accordingly, it is assumed that ﬂow experience has a positive effect on stickiness. Based on this, the
following hypothesis is proposed:
H3: Flow experience positively in ﬂuences stickiness .
Addiction and e-WOM
Electronic Word of Mouth (e-WOM) is de ﬁned as “the sharing of opinions expressed by potential, former, or current
customers regarding a product, service, or brand with a large audience through internet-based platforms. ” e-WOM is
a dynamic process that represents the continuity of the shopping journey (Ismagilova et al., 2017). On online
platforms, downloading an application marks the initial point of engagement. Through user reviews and interactions,
social communities are formed within these platforms, with e-WOM serving as a key driver of this dynamic structure.
These social communities, in turn, contribute to user addiction and stickiness. According to Huang et al. ( 2017),
e-WOM enables users to share their thoughts and experiences and communicate with others in the context of
smartphone or online gaming addiction. Across all types of applications, such as gaming, social media, and shopping,
e-WOM acts as a driving force behind information sharing, addiction, and the formation of social communities.
Moreover, addiction and stickiness can also lead to negative e-WOM (Israeli et al., 2019). Positive and negative
reviews on games and applications can signi ﬁcantly impact the sustainability of user engagement (Teng et al., 2024).
Therefore, within the scope of this study, the following hypotheses (H4, H5, and H6) are proposed concerning the
relationship between E-WOM and online gaming addiction, smartphone addiction, and stickiness:
H4: Online gaming addiction positively in ﬂuences e-WOM .
H5: Smartphone addiction positively in ﬂuences e-WOM .
H6: Stickiness positively in ﬂuences e-WOM .

Flow experience, e-WOM and in-game purchase intention
Flow experience is assumed to be continuously active as long as there is interaction between humans, computers,
phones, and the internet. Furthermore, due to advancing technology, individuals can easily access all the information
sources they seek. In this context, e-WOM (Electronic Word of Mouth) emerges as a communication tool that
facilitates information exchange, allowing consumers to share their opinions and reviews about a product or service
they have used (Gruen et al., 2006). Games have become an indispensable part of daily life and hold strategic
importance for businesses as well ( Şahin & Karahan, 2021). With the advancement of the Internet, word-of-mouth
communication has emerged as a signi ﬁcant phenomenon, prompting marketers to incorporate online promotional
games into their viral marketing campaigns (Renard, 2013). As e-WOM marketing is low-cost and capable of rapid
dissemination, it can in ﬂuence evaluators (Sun vd., 2024). In a study conducted by Sami, Hashim, and Yusof ( 2016),
it was found that the dimensions of ﬂow experience, namely enjoyment, perceived control, and concentration, have an
effect on e-WOM. Similarly, Yoon ( 2012) reported that shopping experience in ﬂuences e-WOM communication.
Furthermore, numerous studies in the literature have demonstrated the effect of ﬂow experience on e-WOM
(Rodríguez- Torrico et al., 2023; Sun vd., 2024; Şahin & Karahan, 2021). Based on these ﬁndings, the following
hypothesis is proposed:
H7: Flow experience positively in ﬂuences e-WOM .
Purchase intention refers to the willingness, desire, and effort demonstrated by an individual or consumer to
purchase a product or service (Wang et al., 2013, p. 361). Intention serves as a key precursor to behavior, meaning the
action is formulated and planned prior to its execution. According to the literature, there is a documented link
between addiction and purchase intention (Andreassen et al., 2015; Duroy et al., 2014; Lee et al., 2016; Wake ﬁeld
et al., 2008; Weinstein et al., 2016). In-game purchases are de ﬁned as the willingness to pay premium prices for in-
game activities and features, and are considered a manifestation of purchase intention (Cheung et al., 2021; Hamari
et al., 2017). According to Kuss and Grif ﬁths ( 2012), in-game purchases tend to occur when players engage with
games that foster long-term, high-quality relationships and strong emotional bonds, which can be interpreted as
outcomes associated with addiction and stickiness. The literature also suggests that addiction positively in ﬂuences
purchasing behavior (Andreassen et al., 2015; Balakrishnan & Grif ﬁths, 2018; Hsiao & Chen, 2016; Liao et al.,
2020). Furthermore, addiction and stickiness are unlikely to occur in the absence of ﬂow experience. Webster et al.
(1990) argue that individuals or players must engage with the application and possess a degree of skill; otherwise,
anxiety arises, and the ﬂow experience diminishes. Interaction is considered the primary source of ﬂow experience
(Novak et al., 2000). An individual in a state of ﬂow experiences feelings of happiness, along with a sense of
conﬁdence and exploration related to the activity being performed (Chou & Ting, 2003). Furthermore, players who
frequently experience ﬂow while engaging in online games tend to develop stronger attachment to the game (Choi &
Kim, 2004; Hsiao & Chen, 2016; Lu & Wang, 2008; Su et al., 2016). In their study, Kim and Han ( 2014) emphasized
the signi ﬁcant role of ﬂow experience in the decision-making process regarding whether to purchase products and
services advertised on smartphones. Additionally, the literature contains numerous studies demonstrating the in ﬂu-
ence of ﬂow experience on purchasing behavior (Berk et al., 2024; Gur ău, 2008; Hoffman & Novak, 2009). In this
context, the following hypothesis is proposed:
H8: Flow experience positively in ﬂuences purchase intention .
As in other industries, the gaming sector is characterized by intense competition. Within this context, e-WOM is
considered a critical factor for the sustainability and success of the industry. Game revenues are largely generated
from features that consumers or players are required to purchase in order to acquire speci ﬁc items or unlock levels,
such as out ﬁts, appearances, or virtual currency (Hamari, 2015). E-WOM enables consumers to share their opinions
(reviews and ratings) about the applications they purchase. These shared opinions in ﬂuence the purchasing decisions
and intentions of other consumers (Genc-Nayebi & Abran, 2017). Moreover, negative e-WOM has been shown to
affect consumers ’ purchase evaluations more strongly than positive e-WOM (Cheung et al., 2008). It is also likely
that addicted users may contribute to negative reviews (Putra et al., 2024). Based on these ﬁndings, the following
hypothesis is proposed:

Methodology
Research model and variables
In this study, a research model was developed and tested based on the determinants of in-game purchase intention.
The research model presented in Fig. 1 was analyzed using a two-stage process consisting of the measurement model,
which evaluates the extent to which latent variables explain observed variables, and the structural model, in which
hypothesis testing is conducted. A structural equation model (SEM) was constructed and tested using latent variables
identiﬁed through a review of the literature. The study includes nine hypotheses (H1 –H9) that test direct relationships
and nine hypotheses that assess mediation effects. In the research model, the latent variables OGA (Online Gaming
Addiction), SPA (Smartphone Addiction), and STC (Stickiness) were de ﬁned as exogenous variables, while FEXP
(Flow Experience), EWOM, and IGPI (In-Game Purchase Intention) were designated as endogenous variables. The
OGA variable consists of seven items, SPA of three items, STC of three items, FEXP of four items, EWOM of three
items, and IGPI of four items. The research model was tested with a total of six variables and twenty- ﬁve items.
Scales
In measuring the variables used in the study, a review of the relevant literature was conducted, and scales with
established validity and reliability were employed. To measure Online Game Addiction (OGA), the scales developed
in the study by Khazaal et al. ( 2018) were utilized. For the Smartphone Addiction (SPA) variable, scales from the
studies conducted by Caplan ( 2010) and Olivencia et al. ( 2018) were adopted. The Stickiness (STC) variable was
measured using scales from Lee et al. ( 2018), Li et al. ( 2021), and Xu et al. ( 2018). To measure Flow Experience
(FEXP), the study relied on scales used by Ghani and Deshpande ( 1994). For Electronic Word of Mouth (EWOM),
the scales developed by Goyette et al. ( 2010) were used. Finally, to measure In-Game Purchase Intention (IGPI),
scales from the study by Hsu and Lin ( 2016) were employed. The full list of scales representing each variable is
provided in Appendix.
The scales used to measure the variables were adapted into Turkish by the researchers. Prior to ﬁnalization, expert
opinions were sought to evaluate the face validity of the scales (Davis et al., 2009; Edward et al., 2010). Using the
translation and back-translation method, the items were ﬁrst translated into Turkish, which is the target language, by
academically pro ﬁcient individuals ﬂuent in both the source and target languages. The translated items were sub-
sequently reviewed by subject-matter experts. (Bouguerra et al., 2023). Based on the feedback from both academic
and ﬁeld experts, the items were translated back into English. The back-translated versions were then compared with
the original items and found to be equivalent. Following feedback from three subject-matter experts and three
academics, a pilot study was conducted with individuals from the target sample to assess the clarity of the scale items.

Research universe and sampling
The population of this study consists of young individuals aged 15 –24 who reside in various regions of Türkiye and
engage in online gaming. Individuals within the 15 –24 age range are among the most active users of the digital
gaming industry. This demographic is known for being early adopters of technology and for exhibiting high levels of
in-game purchasing behavior (Ho & Wu, 2012). Particularly during adolescence and early adulthood, young indi-
viduals are considered to be at higher risk for digital gaming addiction and smartphone addiction (Kuss & Grif ﬁths,
2012). It is well established that the digital gaming industry in Türkiye is growing rapidly, with the youth population
representing the largest consumer segment in this ﬁeld (Gaming in Turkey, 2022). The in-game spending tendencies
of Turkish youth align closely with global trends, making this age group one of the most critical segments in terms of
both addiction and purchasing behavior (We Are Social, 2023). Since it is not feasible to include the entire population
in a study, or because it is not always possible to identify all members of the target population, a sample was selected
to represent the target population (Stratton, 2021). Due to the dispersed nature of individuals comprising the study
population and their residence in various locations, as well as time and ﬁnancial constraints, it was deemed suf ﬁcient
that selected individuals possess the relevant characteristics related to the research topic. Since participants in the
sample are expected to have experience and insight into the subject matter, provide in-depth information, and
contribute to the clarity of the research, convenience sampling was employed (Golzar et al., 2022). This sampling
method allows the inclusion of anyone who is both willing and eligible to participate and is particularly suitable when
the goal is to conduct an in-depth investigation of a social phenomenon. In addition to enabling the selection of a
homogeneous sample for focused analysis, it also facilitates the exploration of various aspects of the topic and the
identiﬁcation of common patterns (Ritchie et al., 2003).
In convenience sampling, the primary goal is not necessarily to obtain a sample that statistically represents the
population, but rather to identify individuals who can provide meaningful responses to the research questions.
Therefore, it is suf ﬁcient that the selected participants possess relevant experience and knowledge, are capable of
offering in-depth insights, and can contribute to clarifying the research topic. In this study, convenience sampling was
chosen for several reasons: it requires less effort in participant selection compared to other non-random sampling
methods, it allows participants to be selected at very low cost, and it enables faster data collection due to the ease of
accessing the sample from the target population (Alvi, 2016). Lastly, convenience sampling was preferred because it
does not require a prede ﬁned sampling frame for all members of the target population, namely young individuals
aged 15 –24 living in various regions of Türkiye and engaging in online gaming (Acharya et al., 2013; Alvi, 2016).
Since the region, time, and context in which participants would be selected were clearly de ﬁned in the research
design, the sample size was determined from among young individuals aged 15 –24 who play online games. When
evaluating quantitative analyses in terms of sample size requirements, Boomsma ( 1985) recommends a minimum
sample size of 100 or 200. Bentler and Chou ( 1987) and Bollen ( 1989) suggest having 5 –10 observations per
estimated parameter. Similarly, Nunnally ( 1967) proposes that there should be 10 observations per variable. Hair
et al. ( 2013) and Howard ( 2016), on the other hand, recommend 20 observations per variable as a more robust
guideline. Given that required sample size may vary depending on the research methodology employed, including
quantitative, qualitative, or experimental designs, Neuman ( 2014) states that a sample size between 30 and 500 is
generally suf ﬁcient for social science research. Accordingly, considering the dispersed nature of the population, the
geographical distribution of individuals, as well as time and ﬁnancial constraints, and given that the total number of
items representing the main variables of the study is 24, it was concluded, based on the 20 observations per item rule
(Hair et al., 2013; Howard, 2016), that a sample size of 500 or more participants would be suf ﬁcient.

Data collection
The data for this study were collected using a survey method. Data were gathered through an online questionnaire
from young individuals aged 15 –24 who live in various regions of Türkiye and engage in online gaming. The ﬁrst
section of the questionnaire provided a brief explanation of the study ’s purpose and stated that participation was
voluntary, that participants could withdraw at any time without any pressure, and that there were no right or wrong
answers. Also, the average completion time of the survey was speci ﬁed and explained that no personal or identifying
information would be requested, and that all responses would remain con ﬁdential and used solely for academic
research purposes, without being shared with any individual or institution. Data were collected between January 15
and March 23, 2025, through an online survey distributed via convenience sampling, using internet and social media
platforms (e.g., WhatsApp, Instagram, Telegram). A total of 957 responses were received. After eliminating 87
incomplete or partially ﬁlled surveys, responses from 870 participants were included in the analysis. The response
rate was approximately 91%. Based on various criteria from the existing literature concerning adequate sample size, a
sample consisting of 870 participants was deemed suf ﬁcient for this research (Bentler & Chou, 1987; Hair et al.,
2013; Howard, 2016; Kline, 1998; Wolf et al., 2013).
In this study, common method bias (CMB) was assessed using multiple statistical approaches. First, Harman ’s
single-factor test was conducted using SPSS. All items representing the dependent and independent variables
included in the research model were analyzed under a single, unrotated factor structure (Fuller et al., 2016; Simmering
et al., 2015). Accordingly, a factor analysis was performed without applying any rotation method to the twenty- ﬁve
items belonging to six constructs, and the results indicated that the ﬁrst unrotated factor accounted for 51.58% of the
total variance. However, methodological literature has widely emphasized that Harman ’s test has limited sensitivity in
detecting common method bias and should not be considered a decisive criterion on its own (Podsakoff et al., 2003;
Fuller et al., 2016). Therefore, the assessment of common method variance in this study primarily relied on a
theoretically unrelated marker variable approach, and these analyses were conducted within the PLS-SEM framework
using SmartPLS. Although some of the paths from the marker variable to the substantive constructs were found to be

Discussion
Türkiye’s platform ecosystem has now become a global player, not just a local one, thanks to the signi ﬁcant presence
of young people in the digital world and the gaming industry ’s large economy. The fact that 86.5% of the population
are internet users and that usage time averages 7 h and 6 min per day is the biggest driving force determining the
ecosystem’s boundary conditions; this intensity propels Türkiye to the forefront of global rankings, creating a
signiﬁcant interaction space for platforms. In particular, the $204 billion global volume of the video game industry
clearly demonstrates the economic value of the consumption behavior of young groups, who constitute the majority.
In the context of Türkiye, the 3.99 average success rating achieved by the 10,939 games produced by Turkish
publishers, who make up 2929 of the 194,005 game publishers on Google Play, also demonstrates that the gaming
ecosystem has a capacity for high-quality production. In conclusion, with an expected annual growth rate of 6%, the
digital gaming sector in Türkiye has made signi ﬁcant progress in becoming a strategic powerhouse, replacing
traditional entertainment industries thanks to the efforts and interactions of young people. (Keskin et al., 2025; UAB,
2024).
This study explains the positive effects of ﬂow experience on both online game addiction smartphone addiction
and stickiness, as well as e-WOM and in-game purchase intention. The results obtained in the study are consistent
with the literature, as they demonstrate that ﬂow experience has a positive effect on online game addiction. Liu et al.
(2016), in their study examining the antecedents and consequences of ﬂow experience about online game addiction,
noted that their ﬁndings provide strategic insights for online providers in attracting players ’ attention and creating
interactive experiences among players. In a study conducted by Chou and Ting (2003), it was concluded that ﬂow
experience, deﬁned as an emotional state encompassing perceptual distortion and pleasure, has a very strong effect on
addiction, and that consumers who experience ﬂow are more likely to become addicted. Wu et al. ( 2013) used the
concept of recreation specialization to explore progression among online players and clarify the relationship between
ﬂow experiences and addiction tendencies. The authors concluded that specialized players are more likely to
experience ﬂow and exhibit game addiction than inexperienced players, and that recreation specialization intensi ﬁes
the effect of ﬂow experiences on addiction tendencies. Rodríguez-Ardura et al. ( 2023) examined how the immersive
experiences of presence and ﬂow shape both the utilitarian and hedonic value of products and services, as well as
their combined effect on user participation and commitment, in ﬂuenced by social norms. The ﬁndings from these
studies strongly support the assumed relationship between ﬂow experiences and addiction.
Through e-WOM, consumers share evaluations, comments, ideas, and information about products, brands, and
services on online platforms. Therefore, e-WOM plays an important role in in ﬂuencing and shaping consumer

psychological and social interaction elements together in explaining player behavior. Additionally, the ﬁndings of the
research support its theoretical validity and integrity.
Conclusion
In this study, a research model was developed and tested based on the determinants of in-game purchase intention.
The model demonstrates that the primary factors directly in ﬂuencing in-game purchase intention are ﬂow experience
and e-WOM. Among these, the effect of e-WOM on in-game purchase intention was found to be relatively stronger
than that of ﬂow experience. This ﬁnding highlights the signi ﬁcance of both the immersive experience during
gameplay and the social interaction occurring through electronic communication in shaping in-game purchasing
behavior. Moreover, the in-game experience related to ﬂow was found to have a strong impact on both e-WOM and
in-game purchase intention. This suggests that enhancing players ’ ﬂow experiences during gameplay can strengthen
electronic word-of-mouth activities and positively in ﬂuence in-game purchase intention. These results underscore the
importance of creating engaging, immersive game environments to support both social in ﬂuence mechanisms and
monetization strategies in the gaming industry.

The ﬁnding that ﬂow experience is signi ﬁcantly associated with online game addiction, smartphone addiction, and

The effect of ﬂow experience on e-WOM indicates that users ’ intense satisfaction and attachment resulting from
their game or application experiences increase their tendency to share those experiences with others. Flow experience
refers to a state in which an individual loses track of time, becomes fully immersed in the activity, and derives a high
level of enjoyment during gameplay or app use. Users who enter a state of ﬂow are more likely to evaluate the game
or application positively and communicate their experience to their social circles. In particular, users who establish a
deep connection with a game or application may perceive sharing this experience with others as a meaningful social
behavior.
Implications
Theoretical implications
This study offers valuable contributions both academically and practically by investigating the determinants of in-
game purchase intention. Overall, it is one of the few studies that analyzes the factors in ﬂuencing in-game purchase
intention through a comprehensive model. By employing Structural Equation Modeling (SEM), the study provides a
detailed analysis of how in-game purchase intention is associated with various factors. The ﬁndings reveal that in-
game purchasing behaviors are shaped not only by individual motivations but also by experiential, social, and
collective factors such as ﬂow experience and e-WOM. In this respect, the study contributes theoretically to the
literature. The measurement of relationships among latent variables and their validation through con ﬁrmatory factor
analysis enhance the scienti ﬁc rigor of the model. The use of SEM allows for a systematic and in-depth examination
of the relationships among variables, enabling the identi ﬁcation of key factors that shape the in-game purchasing
process. From a practical standpoint, the results of this study provide game developers with strategic insights to
enhance in-game purchasing behavior. In particular, digital marketing professionals can optimize their in-game
purchase strategies by leveraging e-WOM and player experience. The ﬁndings also offer a strategic perspective on
fostering customer loyalty within the gaming industry by identifying factors that drive purchase intention. Thus, the
study makes a meaningful practical contribution as well.
Practical implications
The ﬁndings of this study offer important practical implications for game developers, digital marketers, and managers
within the gaming ecosystem. The fact that ﬂow experience has a direct impact on both e-WOM and in-game
purchase intention underscores the critical importance of user experience design for game developers. Providing a
better gameplay experience can increase both player engagement and in-game spending. Therefore, elements such as
the in-game interface, graphic quality, storytelling, and user-friendly mechanics should be optimized. By focusing on
design components that ensure the game or application is easily understandable and enjoyable for players, developers

The relatively strong effect of e-WOM on in-game purchase intention highlights the critical role of peer recom-

Since ﬂow experience signi ﬁcantly in ﬂuences online game addiction, stickiness, and smartphone addiction, it is

within the technology and gaming industries and serve as a valuable reference point for researchers in the ﬁelds of
marketing and behavioral psychology.
Limitations and future research
This study was conducted using a sample of individuals aged 15 to 24 residing in various regions of Türkiye who
engage in online gaming. The sample was selected using a convenience sampling method, which, while offering cost-
effectiveness and easier access to participants, also presents certain limitations. As a non-probability sampling
technique, it does not allow for broad generalization and carries an inherent risk of selection bias. Future studies could
adopt probability-based sampling techniques to ensure better representation of the target population. Additionally, the
ﬁndings of this study are applicable to young gamers in Türkiye. Due to cultural, economic, and social differences,
similar results may not be observed in other countries. The purchase habits, intentions, and behaviors of Turkish
youth are shaped by various country-speci ﬁc factors, such as economic conditions, cultural values, and the dynamics
of the domestic digital gaming industry. Consequently, future research could incorporate cross-cultural comparisons
by including participants from different countries with varying socio-economic contexts. Moreover, the study ’s focus
on the 15 –24 age group limits its ability to explain in-game purchase intention among younger (e.g., under 14) or
older (e.g., over 25) individuals. Future research should examine in-game purchase behaviors across a broader range
of age groups to gain a more comprehensive understanding. Given these limitations, the ﬁndings cannot be directly
generalized to other age groups, cultural settings, or player types. Nevertheless, future studies with broader and more
diverse samples may provide opportunities for comparative and more generalizable results.
In this study, the direct effects of ﬂow experience, addiction, stickiness, and e-WOM on in-game purchase
intention were examined within the framework of the Flow Theory. In line with this approach, the research model
was deliberately designed in a parsimonious and focused manner, and more complex mediation structures among the
variables were excluded from the scope of the study. Nevertheless, the potential indirect effects of ﬂow experience on
in-game purchase intention through addiction and stickiness represent a theoretically relevant direction for future
research. Similarly, a more detailed examination of the relationships between different types of addiction, stickiness,
and purchase intention offers substantial potential for further scholarly investigation. Therefore, these relationships
and possible mediating pathways constitute important directions for future studies. Within this scope, it is also

and user loyalty varies across cultures. Understanding how cultural factors in ﬂuence gaming habits and the intensity
of ﬂow experiences can offer valuable insights for shaping global digital marketing strategies. Additionally, research
exploring how in-game mechanisms, virtual reality, and AI-powered interactions in ﬂuence ﬂow experience can
contribute to the development of innovative strategies in digital game and application design.
