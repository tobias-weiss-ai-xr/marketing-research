Abstract This research aimed to examine the influence of content formats and online media exposure channels
on consumers’ purchase decision-making processes regarding red algae in Bangkok. This study employed a
quantitative research methodology. An online questionnaire was used as the instrument for data collection
from a sample of 404 individuals residing in Bangkok who had previous exposure to information about red algae,
using a multi-stage sampling method. Data were analyzed using descriptive statistics (frequency, percentage,
mean, and standard deviation) and inferential statistics, specifically one-way ANOVA and Chi-square tests, to
test the hypotheses at a .05 level of statistical significance. The findings revealed that the majority of consumers
(70.8%) received information about red algae through online media channels such as Facebook, YouTube, and
TikTok. The most frequently accessed content format was video reviews or short clips (51.2%). The content
factor most influential on purchase decision-making was consumer reviews (23.74%), reflecting the importance
of credibility, followed by media featuring easily comprehensible images or graphics (22.03%). Hypothesis testing
results indicated that media content format was the sole factor significantly related to both aspects of consumer
behavior: purchase frequency (p-value = .029) and purchase decision-making (p-value = .003). Meanwhile, media
exposure channels were also significantly related to purchase frequency (p-value = .008). These findings indicate
that selecting appropriate online media channels and content formats tailored to the target audience directly
influences consumers’ purchase decision-making process regarding red algae in the digital era, providing
beneficial insights for marketers in planning future communication strategies.
Keywords: consumer behavior, content format, online media channels, purchase decision-making, red algae
