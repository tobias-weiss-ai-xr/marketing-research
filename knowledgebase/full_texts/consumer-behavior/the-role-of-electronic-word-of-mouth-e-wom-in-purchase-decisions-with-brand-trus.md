ABSTRACT
The rapid development of information and communication technology has shifted the
dynamics of consumer behavior from conventional interactions to the digital realm. This study
aims to analyze the role of Electronic Word of Mouth (e-WOM) on Purchase Decisions with Brand
Trust as a moderating variable. Using an explanatory quantitative approach, data were collected
through an online survey of consumers who active ly make transactions on e -commerce
platforms. Data analysis was conducted using Partial Least Squares - based Structural Equation
Modeling (PLS -SEM). The results show that e -WOM has a positive and significant effect on
Purchase Decisions. Furthermore, moderation analysis proves that Brand Trust significantly
strengthens the positive relationship between e -WOM and Purchase Decisions. This finding
indicates that although digital reviews are a primary source of information, consumers' level of
trust in a brand acts as a critical filter that validates the impact of that information in driving
purchase actions. The practical implications of this study emphasize the importance for
marketers to not only actively manage online review reputations but also build a solid foundation
of Brand Trust to maximize sales conversions in the digital marketplace.

Keywords : Electronic Word of Mouth (e -WOM), Purchase Decision, Brand Trust, Moderating
Variable, E-commerce.

INTROCUTION
The massive digital transformation over the past decade has fundamentally
altered the global trade landscape, shifting the center of economic activity from
physical markets to e -commerce ecosystems (Al -Debei et al., 2015). This paradigm
shift has not only impacted transaction modes but also restructured consumer
information-seeking and decision -making patterns. Before the internet era, product
information relied heavily on one- way promotions from manufacturers and
traditional, geographically limited word -of-mouth (WOM) recommendations
(Goldsmith & Horowitz, 2006). However, in today's digital era, consumers are faced with
an abundance of digital information that can be accessed in real time  from anywhere
and at any time. This phenomenon has created a highly c ompetitive digital
marketplace, where geographical boundaries have become irrelevant, but
competition for consumer attention and trust has become increasingly fierce (Cheung
& Thadani, 2012).
Amidst the flood of digital information, the existence of Electr onic Word of
Mouth (e-WOM) has emerged as one of the most dominant factors in influencing
digital consumer behavior (Henning -Thurau et al., 2004). e- WOM is defined as
positive or negative statements made by potential consumers, current consumers, or
former consumers about a product or company, which are made available to many

corporate promotions which are often viewed skeptically, e -WOM is considered a
more independent, objective, and trustworthy source of information because it comes
from fellow experienced users (Babić Rosario et al., 2016). This phenomenon
encompasses various forms of online communication, including product reviews on
e-commerce platforms, posts on social media, discussion forums, and personal blogs.
The impact of e -WOM on Purchase Decisions has  been widely demonstrated
in various digital marketing literature (Gvili & Levy, 2016). Consumer purchasing
decisions are complex processes involving problem reco gnition, information search,
alternative evaluation, and the actual purchase (Kotler & Keller, 2016). In a digital
context, e-WOM serves as a risk reduction tool for  consumers who cannot physically
touch or try a product before purchasing (Kudeshia & Kumar , 2017). When online
reviews provide consistently positive assessments of product quality, service
efficiency, and aesthetic value, the probability of consumers completing a transaction
through to the payment stage increases significantly (Zhang et al., 2014).
However, the relationship between e- WOM and Purchase Decisions is not
always linear or uniform across market conditions (Erkan & Evans, 2016). A
discrepancy is often found where abundant and positive e- WOM does not
automatically transform into high sa les. This phenomenon can be explained by the
issue of information credibility in cyberspace, where the rise of fake reviews ,
astroturfing, and bot accounts has fueled consumer skepticism towards digital
reviews (Filieri, 2016). Modern consumers no longer a ccept e -WOM at face value;
they tend to evaluate the validity of these reviews based on their internal perceptions
of the credibility of the reviewed entity (Cheung et al., 2009). The inconsistency of
findings in previous empirical research suggests the ex istence of conditional or
qualifying variables that act as moderators in this relationship.
Within this framework, Brand Trust plays  a crucial role as a potential
moderating variable (Chaudhuri & Holbrook, 2001). Brand Trust  represents
consumers' willingness to rely on a brand with the belief that the brand is capable of
consistently fulfilling its promised functions (Delgado -Ballester & Munuera-Alemán,
2001). Brand trust is built through a track record of consistent product performance,
company reputation, and clarity of marketing communications over time (Morgan &
Hunt, 1994). When a brand has a high level of Brand Trust, consumers' perceived risk
decreases, so that positive information from e- WOM becomes more convincing and
accelerates the conversion process to purchasing decisions (Albert & Merunka, 2013).
The integration of Brand Trust  as a moderating variable in the relationship
between e-WOM and Purchase Decisions is based on the Elaboration Likelihood Model
(ELM) develo ped by Petty and Cacioppo (1986). According to the ELM, attitude
formation or change can occur through the central route or  the peripheral route . e-
WOM acts as message arguments processed by consumers, while Brand Trust
functions as a basic cognitive and e motional bond that filters the processing of this
information (Bhattacherjee & Sanford, 2006). If Brand Trust is high, consumers are

Brand Trust is low, even positive e -WOM reviews are potentially suspected of being
marketing manipulation, so their impact on purchase decisions is weakened (Ladhari

The Signaling Theory perspective developed by Spence (1973) also provides
a strong theoretical basis for explaining this phenomenon. In digital markets
characterized by information asymmetry , consumers often lack direct information
regarding the actual quality of products (Kirmani & Rao,  2000). e-WOM acts as an
external signal from the social environment, while Brand Trust  is an internal
credibility signal emitted by the brand itself (Erdem & Swait, 1998). The effectiveness
of external signals (e- WOM) in driving purchasing decisions will depend heavily on
the strength of internal signals ( Brand Trust). The combination of positive external
signals and strong internal signals creates a synergistic effect that significantly
accelerates consumer purchasing decisions (Wells et al., 2011).
Although e-WOM and Brand Trust have been widely studied separately in the
marketing discipline, most previous studies have positioned Brand Trust  only as a
mediating variable or a direct independent variable (e.g., Abubakar & Ilkan, 2016;
Tariq et al., 2013). There is still a significant research gap regarding how Brand Trust
acts as a moderator that strengthens or weakens the influence of e-WOM on Purchase
Decisions, especially in the highly dynamic and disinformation -rich e-commerce
context (Ladhari & Michaud,  2015). Testing Brand Trust  as a moderator is important
to provide a finer - grained understanding of the specific conditions under which e -
WOM reaches its peak effectiveness.
Practically, a deep understanding of the moderating dynamics of Brand Trust
is vital for marketers and brand managers . In the modern business landscape,
companies often allocate significant marketing budgets to e-WOM campaigns, such as
using influencer marketing  or user review incentive programs (Lu et al., 2014).
However, without an e qual investment in building Brand Trust  through improving
product quality and customer service, these e- WOM strategies risk being ineffective
or even triggering backlash if they are not trusted by consumers (De Veirman et al.,
2017). Therefore, this study is designed to address these theoretical gaps and
practical needs by empirically testing the moderating role of Brand Trust  in the
relationship between e-WOM and Purchase Decisions.

that aims to test and analyze the causal relationship between variables and confirm
the role of moderation in the research model. The population in this study is all
consumers who actively use e -commerce platforms and have read product reviews
before making online purchases. Given the infinite population size , the sampling
technique used is non- probability sampling  with a purposive sampling  method. The

analysis criteria was 220 respondents, active users of e -commerce platforms who
regularly read reviews before making purchases.

Discussion
The Influence of Electronic Word of Mouth (e-WOM) on Purchasing Decisions
The empirical findings in this study prove that Electronic Word of Mouth  (e-
WOM) has a direct, positive, and significant influence on consumer purchasing
decisions. The direction of the positive relationship ( 𝛽𝛽 = 0,385) indicates that the
higher the quantity, quality, and credibility of online reviews received by consumers,
the higher their decision to make a purchase transaction on an e -commerce platform
will be. These results confirm that digital consumers currently use e-WOM as the main
information base to reduce purchasing risks due to the inability to evaluate physical
products directly (Babić Rosario et al., 2016).
Theoretically, these findings reinforce Social Information Processing Theory
(Walther, 1992), which states that individuals can effectively process interpersonal
information through electronic media to form perceptions and take concrete actions.
In the digital ecosystem, customer reviews, user recommendations, and ratings act as
powerful social proof (Cialdini, 2007). Consumers tend to perceiv e information
shared by fellow consumers as more objective, less commercially biased, and
reflective of real experiences than official company advertisements (Filieri, 2015).
The results of this study are in line with previous findings from Cheung and
Thadani (2012) and Kudeshia and Kumar (2017), who found that online information
interactions have a conclusive impact on purchasing intentions and actions. In

The Influence of Brand Trust on Purchasing Decisions
The results of statistical tests prove that Brand Trust  has a positive and
significant influence on Purchasing Decisions ( 𝛽𝛽 = 0,312This means that consumer
trust in a brand's reputation, reliability, and integrity is an important independent
driver in determining their purchasing choices. The stronger a consumer's level of
trust in a brand, the greater their willingness to make  transaction decisions without
the worry of mismatched expectations (Chaudhuri & Holbrook, 2001).
Conceptually, this finding is very consistent with the Commitment -Trust
Theory developed by Morgan and Hunt (1994), which emphasizes that trust is the
main key in building long -term relationships and triggering profitable transactional
behavior. Brand trust contains two main dimensions: trust in brand
expertise/reliability (brand reliability) and trust in brand goodwill (brand intentions)
(Delgado-Ballester & Munuera-Alemán, 2001). In digital transactions that are rich in
technical and privacy risks, Brand Trust  acts as a quality assurance that  assures
consumers that the brand will not disappoint them (Erdem & Swait, 2004).
These findings also support previous empirical studies by Albert and Merunka
(2013) and Sirdeshmukh et al. (2002), which suggest that brand trust triggers a
positive preference bias in consumers' minds. Consumers who have developed a high
level of trust in a brand experience lower mental frict ion when executing payment
decisions. Brand trust eliminates cognitive doubt and fear of financial loss, thus
smoothing the path from mere interest to actual purchase (Lau & Lee, 1999).

The Moderating Role of Brand Trust in the Relationship between e -WOM and
Purchasing Decisions
The most crucial theoretical contribution of this study lies in proving the role
of Brand Trust as a moderating variable that  strengthens the influence of e -WOM on
Purchasing Decisions. With a positive moderating interaction coefficient ( 𝛽𝛽 =
0,214,𝑝𝑝 = 0,000), these results confirm that the impact of e -WOM on consumer
purchasing decisions will be much stronger and more effective wh en supported by a
high level of Brand Trust. Brand Trust  has been shown to function as a quasi -
moderator variable, meaning it not only interacts with the independent variable, but
also has a direct influence on the dependent variable (Sharma et al., 1981).
This moderation dynamic can be explained through the lens of the Elaboration
Likelihood Model (ELM) (Petty & Cacioppo, 1986). In this model, e-WOM is processed
by consumers as a persuasive message. However, the effectiveness of the message is

CONCLUSION
This study successfully proves that Electronic Word of Mouth  (e-WOM) and
Brand Trust have a positive and significant direct influence on consumer purchasing
decisions on e -commerce platforms. The presence of online reviews,
recommendations, and ratings acts as an objective source of information that
effectively reduces uncertainty and risk perceptions of potential buyers. At the same
time, Brand Trust functions as an internal foundation that provides a sense of security
and assurance of reliability, so that it can independently trigger consumer
transactional impulses without significant cognitive barriers.
The main theoretical contribution of this study lies in examining the role of
Brand Trust as a moderating variable ( quasi-moderator). The empirical test results
confirm that Brand Trust significantly strengthens the positive influence of e-WOM on
Purchase Decisions. This finding explains that the abundant information push of e -
WOM will reach its peak effectiveness when infused with a high level of brand trust.
Conversely, without an adequate foundation of Brand Trust , even positive e -WOM
reviews risk being responded to skeptically by consumers, thus limiting their impact
on purchasing decisions.
In terms of managerial implications, this study confirms that digital marketing
strategies cannot solely rely on massive e -WOM campaigns such as in fluencer
marketing or review incentives. Brand managers and digital business practitioners
must balance e- WOM strategies with equal investment in building and maintaining
brand trust  through consistent product quality, information transparency, and
reliable after-sales service. The synergy between spreading positive external signals
(e-WOM) and strengthening internal credibility (brand trust) is the key to winning the
competition and maximizing sales conversions in the digital market.
