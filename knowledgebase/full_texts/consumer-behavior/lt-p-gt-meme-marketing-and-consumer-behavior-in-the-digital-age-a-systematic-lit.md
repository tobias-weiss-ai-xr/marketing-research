Abstract
Digital communication environments have fundamentally changed the way consumers today communicate with brands,
facilitating participatory, emotional, and culturally responsive online communication. In this changing scenario, meme
marketing has emerged as a powerful digital communication tool by which brands use humor, symbolic elements, social
responsiveness, and social relatability to connect with social media users. Although the subject of meme marketing has
been increasingly studied in both academic and management contexts, the literature is conceptually disjointed and lacks
theoretical consistency, especially when it comes to the cognitive, emotional, and behavioral processes that underlie
meme marketing's impact on consumer behavior. For this purpose, a systematic literature review is used to synthesize
existing research on meme marketing and consumer behavior in terms of the Stimulus-Organism-Response (S-O-R)
framework. Searching in accordance with the principles of PRISMA, the relevant studies were retrieved from Scopus,
Google Scholar, and ScienceDirect from the available years 2016-2026. Thematic synthesis and conceptual analysis were
conducted on 30 studies following a structured screening and eligibility assessment process. The insights reveal that the
eﬀectiveness of meme marketing is dominated by stimulus factors such as humor, relatability, familiarity with culture,
relevance to trends, and platform adaptability. They engender organismic reactions like emotional involvement,
cognitive attention, entertainment satisfaction, authenticity assessment, and interpretive interaction, which in turn
impact conduct such as sharing behavior, electronic word-of-mouth, purchase intention, and electronic consumer
involvement. The review also points to the context- and culture-specific dynamics of the eﬀectiveness of memes, which
include the impact of platform culture, symbolic meaning, audience demographics, and algorithmic visibility in
participatory digital spaces. In relation to the S-O-R framework, the review conceptually proposes an extension to
contemporary digital communication contexts and interprets meme marketing as a culturally embedded and
emotionally mediated form of symbolic brand interaction. This proposed extension is theoretical in nature and requires
future empirical validation. In addition, practical implications for marketers, digital strategists, and social media
practitioners are explored, especially in relation to authenticity, ethical sensitivity, and adapting communication for
diﬀerent platforms in meme-based branding.

Introduction And Background
The creation and development of participatory digital ecosystems have revolutionized the nature of today's marketing
communication and consumer-brand interaction. In algorithmically mediated social media environments, consumers are
not only recipients of brand messages but also creators, distributors, interpreters, and amplifiers of branded content
through sharing practices, remix culture, and networked digital participation (Jenkins, 2006) (Kietzmann et al., 2011).
With the rise of platforms like Instagram, TikTok, X, and Reddit, emotionally charged, short-form, and culturally adept
forms of communication, which can now create quick interaction, symbolic participation, and algorithmic visibility in
highly saturated digital spaces, are increasingly dominating the market (Teng et al., 2022), (Nasution and Silalahi, 2025),
and (Ashley and Tuten, 2015). Emotional, social, and participatory aspects of communication are coming to the fore in
digital marketing, more so than traditional persuasive advertising mechanisms.
In this dynamic communication environment, meme marketing is one of the most prominent and culturally resonant
forms of digital brand communication. In general, an Internet meme is considered to be a replicable unit of digital culture
that is imitated, adapted, reinterpreted, and circulated participatively and collectively within online communities
(Shifman, 2013) (Miltner, 2018). In today's marketing world, however, memes serve as more than just a form of humorous
entertainment or Internet phenomena. Meme-based communication has become a symbolic, emotionally driven
communication mechanism that is used by brands to engage in the continuous digital conversation; to react to new
cultural trends and to build online identities that can be understood in the social sphere (Miltner, 2018) (Teng et al., 2022).
A typical characteristic of meme marketing is that it tends to use more informal communication styles; that is, an
advertising message that is communicated in a manner that is similar to the peer-to-peer communication style, and that
adapts to cultural elements, reducing the perception of intrusiveness and making it more likely that consumers will
engage in it, through sharing, commenting, remixing, and electronic word-of-mouth (eWOM) behavior (Gretry et al.,
2017), (Muntinga et al., 2011), and (Erkan and Evans, 2016).
The rise in the strategic use of meme marketing is a result of the larger structural shifts in the digital consumer culture.
Recent investigations also suggest that meme-based communication can strengthen brand visibility and appears to be
associated with favorable consumer purchase outcomes in digitally connected environments (Tsai, 2025). In an
environment of brief attention spans and uninterrupted content consumption, a brand's success in earning audience
attention is increasingly dependent on humor, the ability to respond to trends, emotional appeals, and symbolic
appeal.  This is why companies like Netflix, Duolingo, Swiggy, or Zomato are increasingly adopting meme marketing
methods for their digital communication strategies as these methods help reinforce their online presence, foster
emotional connections, build platform-specific brand personas, and enhance brand engagement through viral
communication (Kim and Kim, 2024). Familiar research consistently links meme-based communication to greater levels of
audience interaction, emotional reactions, brand recall, online involvement, and purchase-related behavioral results,
notably among younger audiences who are adept at engaging in informal and culturally relevant communication forms
(Yadav and Rai, 2017), (Bowo et al., 2024), (Hollebeek et al., 2014), and (Calder et al., 2009).
This increased attention by academics and managers does not imply that the body of knowledge around meme
marketing is well-integrated and cohesive. However, there is a large body of previous research that focuses on either
cognitive (e.g., humor), aﬀective (e.g., virality) or behavioral (e.g., online engagement, purchase intention) components of
meme-based communication and their eﬀects on consumer responses, without systematically integrating those
mechanisms (Malodia et al., 2022). The results from existing studies are also dependent on the context and are
sometimes contradictory. Some research, however, suggests that too much fun, too much trend following, too much
symbolizing, or too much culturally inappropriate usage of a meme can lead to "holes in the credibility" of the brand and
negative consumer reactions (Miltner, 2018), (Gretry et al., 2017), and (Walsh et al., 2024). These inconsistencies indicate
that the value of the meme is not as easily measured by engagement or virality alone; instead, an integrated explanation
is required to understand how consumers cognitively process, emotionally react to, and behave with respect to meme-
based communication in participatory digital environments.

The existing literature also shows a few significant methodological and theoretical shortcomings. Much of the current
meme marketing research is focused on cross-sectional quantitative methods with a primary emphasis on short-term
metrics like likes, shares, and the visibility of the meme online. In comparison, less focus has been paid to the deeper
psychological mechanisms of emotional attachment, interpretive engagement, authenticity perception, trust formation,
and long-term consumer-brand relationships related to meme-based communication (Hollebeek et al., 2014), (Dessart et
al., 2015), and (Vivek et al., 2012). Moreover, although meme marketing now takes place in an algorithmically dominated
and platform-specific digital landscape, few studies have investigated the impact of platform culture, symbolic meaning,
participatory communication, and audience expectations on consumer response patterns in a systematic manner.
Although highly relevant to digitally networked communication contexts, ethical issues surrounding meme
communication, such as stereotyping, cultural appropriation, manipulative virality, misinformation, and oﬀensive humor,
are relatively under-researched.
Such constraints stress the need for a more theoretically grounded approach to consumer behavior and meme marketing
in participatory digital ecosystems. In terms of this, the Stimulus-Organism-Response (S-O-R) model oﬀers a relevant
conceptual perspective for exploring  how consumer responses are influenced at various cognitive, emotional, and
behavioral levels in meme-based communication. The S-O-R framework, originally proposed by Mehrabian and Russell,
explains how environmental stimuli aﬀect individuals’ cognitive and aﬀective states, which subsequently influence
behavioral responses. Meme features like humor, cultural references, feelings of being associated with or understood,
topicality, and platform fit can serve as ambient cues that aﬀect consumers' emotional involvement, their interpretation
of the input, their amusement, and their brand-related perceptions in meme marketing settings. The organismic
responses can then influence various behavioral consequences, such as online interaction, sharing behavior, eWOM,
intensity of interaction, and purchase intention (Eroglu et al., 2001), (Islam and Rahman, 2017), (Chu, 2011), and (Kumar et
al., 2016). Most important, the S-O-R model also allows for the understanding of meme marketing as a process of
communication rather than just content to be promoted, as it is an interpretive, participatory, and symbolic process
influenced by audience interpretations, digital culture participation, and relations with the context.
Therefore, this research work conducts a systematic literature review to compile the existing literature related to meme
marketing and consumer behavior within the S-O-R perspective. The study uses the  Preferred Reporting Items for
Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) approach to conduct a systematic literature review that examines
literature on meme communication, emotional response, cognitive response, symbolic interaction, and behavioral
response in digital marketing settings. The aim of the review is to discover the key trends, theories, methodologies, and
gaps in the field of meme marketing. The study is significant to the literature on digital marketing and consumer behavior
in several ways as it integrates the diﬀerent findings into a conceptual framework based on theory. First, it oﬀers a
thorough synthesis of meme marketing research, both in stimulus and organismic and behavioral responses. Second, it
conceptually proposes an extension of the S-O-R framework to participatory and algorithmically mediated digital
communication settings. This proposition is derived from the thematic synthesis of the reviewed literature and should be
interpreted as a theoretical direction for future empirical research rather than as an empirically validated extension. Third,
it helps further the theoretical discussion of meme marketing as a culturally rooted and emotionally charged symbolic
brand communication strategy as opposed to simply the use of humor as an engagement tool. Lastly, the study provides
practical guidance for marketers to craft culturally responsive, emotionally appealing, and ethically sound strategies for
meme marketing in the rapidly evolving landscape of social media.
Research objectives
To address the identified theoretical and methodological gaps in the literature, this review is guided by the following
research objectives:
RO1: To systematically examine the existing literature on meme marketing and consumer behavior in digital
communication environments.
RO2: To identify the key stimulus factors of meme marketing that influence consumer cognitive and emotional responses.

Review
Review approach
The present study employs a systematic literature review method in synthesizing existing studies on meme marketing
and consumer behavior using the  S-O-R  model. A systematic review method was deemed to be suitable because the
research dealing with meme marketing is scattered across various interdisciplinary fields such as digital marketing, social
media communication, consumer psychology, online engagement, and participatory digital culture. Systematic literature
reviews are a transparent and structured approach that can be replicated to identify, evaluate, and synthesize scholarly
evidence in a specific research area (Tranfield et al., 2003) (Snyder, 2019). The review also takes place according to
the  PRISMA  guidelines for methodological transparency in identifying, screening, eligibility assessment, and final
inclusion (Page et al., 2021). The review focuses on the cognitive, aﬀective, and behavioral aspects of consumers'
responses in digital environment, and how their responses may be shaped through meme-based communication.
Because there is conceptual diversity in terms of terminology, the focus of the study, and the theoretical perspective in
the current literature on meme marketing, a thematic synthesis approach was used to capture conceptual themes,
methodological patterns, and emerging gaps in the literature from the selected works. The S-O-R framework also was
used as an interpretative lens to organize the synthesized findings into stimulus, organism, and behavioral response
dimensions. The review aimed to include research published between 2016 and 2026; however, some seminal theoretical
studies published prior to this time period were selectively included to provide conceptual context for participatory
digital culture, Internet meme theory, and the S-O-R framework. These foundational studies were used only to provide
theoretical context and were not part of the empirical synthesis.
Database selection and search strategy
Studies were retrieved from Scopus, Google Scholar ,and ScienceDirect to ensure a thorough coverage of relevant
research in the field of academic literature. The selection of these databases was based on the fact that they cover
interdisciplinary research areas such as marketing, communication studies, consumer behavior, social media research,
and digital culture. Multiple databases also minimized the risk of missing relevant studies related to emerging meme
marketing scholarship. The database search and screening were conducted between December 2025 and March 2026.
The review centered on literature published from 2016 to 2026, as scholarly and industry interest in the use of memes for
brand communication increased significantly with the growth of participatory social media culture, the rise of
influencers, and short-form content driven by platforms like Instagram and TikTok. Previous research has concentrated on
internet culture and non-commercial meme use while since 2016, research has been more focused on meme
communication in branding, consumer engagement, eWOM, and online behavioral settings. To find relevant studies, a
combination of keywords and Boolean operators was used. As meme marketing literature is still conceptually scattered
among related communication and engagement categories, several keyword combinations were adopted to increase the
comprehensiveness of the search results and to align the concepts. The literature was retrieved using the search
keywords and Boolean combinations as shown in Table 1.

Screening and study selection process
The study selection process was conducted in accordance with the PRISMA methodology, which includes identification,
screening, eligibility evaluation, and final inclusion (Page et al., 2021). The first database search returned 235 records. A
total of 186 records were included for abstract and title screening after the removal of duplicate records and filtering of
inaccessible records. At this stage, studies that were not directly related to the field of consumer engagement through
digital media and/or consumer behavior were excluded.
The remaining articles subsequently underwent full-text eligibility assessment based on conceptual relevance,
methodological clarity, and alignment with the objectives of the review. Studies focusing primarily on political memes,
non-commercial Internet culture, or unrelated social media phenomena were excluded during the eligibility stage.
Following the final screening process, 30 studies were included for thematic synthesis and conceptual analysis. Although
the primary review period covered publications from 2016 to 2026, selected foundational studies published between
2009 and 2015 were retained because of their significant theoretical contributions to consumer engagement, social
media interaction, Internet meme research, and the S-O-R framework. These studies provided the conceptual foundation
for interpreting contemporary meme marketing research. The PRISMA-based study selection process is illustrated in

Data extraction and thematic analysis
After the selection of the studies, the articles included in this research were analyzed systematically to identify common
themes, theories, methodology, and consumers' reactions to meme marketing. The data extracted focused on the goals
of the study, research methods, major findings, consumer behavior variables, and theories used in the selected literature.
A thematic analysis approach was used to carry out a synthesis of the recurring themes within the reviewed studies. The
classification of concepts was mainly inductive, with the themes emerging from  recurrent  conceptual patterns in the
literature. The S-O-R framework, however, also guided the organization of the themes identified into stimulus, organism,
and response dimensions. This integrated approach allowed the review to be conceptually flexible while also being
theoretically coherent in the synthesized findings. Studies examining humor, emotional resonance, cultural relatability,
virality, online engagement,  eWOM, and purchase intention were then compared to with one another to determine
broader thematic connections between meme marketing studies. Accordingly, humor, trend relevance, visual symbolism,
and cultural familiarity were seen as environmental stimuli that impacted consumer reactions; these were considered to
be meme characteristics. These were interpreted as meme characteristics that impacted consumer reactions: humor,
trend relevance, visual symbolism, and cultural familiarity. Internal responses were assessed using organismic dimensions
(emotional involvement, cognitive attention, amusement, and brand perception) and the behavioral outcomes were
interpreted as response variables (online interaction, content sharing, eWOM, and purchase intention) in digital
consumer behavior contexts. The characteristics of the studies included in the final synthesis are summarized in Table 4.

Methodological limitations
Despite the structured and transparent review process, several limitations of the study should be acknowledged. First,
only English-language publications were included in the review. Consequently, relevant studies published in other
languages may have been excluded, potentially limiting the representation of culturally specific meme practices,
consumer behavior, and digital communication patterns across diﬀerent linguistic and regional contexts. As meme
interpretation is often shaped by cultural norms, language, and local social media ecosystems, the findings may not fully
reflect meme marketing practices in non-English-speaking environments. Future systematic reviews incorporating
multilingual databases and cross-cultural evidence could provide a more comprehensive understanding of meme
marketing across diverse cultural settings. Second, not all papers were directly related to meme marketing, and some
were found in what could be called “indirect”  literature databases, so it is possible that some indirectly related studies
were missed due to the interdisciplinary and dynamic character of the field of meme marketing research. Third, the
review was predominantly based on peer-reviewed academic articles and may not fully reflect the changes in industry
practices related to using memes for communication on new social media platforms. Lastly, the conceptual judgment
needed during categorization and synthesis of thematic interpretation can create a degree of interpretive subjectivity,
even if it was maintained that there was analytical consistency throughout the review process.
The review primarily relied on peer-reviewed academic publications retrieved from the selected electronic databases.
Consequently, relevant grey literature, including industry reports, conference papers, doctoral theses, working papers,
and unpublished studies, was not systematically included. This may introduce publication bias, as studies reporting
statistically significant or positive findings are generally more likely to be published than studies reporting null or
contradictory results. Although the selected databases provide broad coverage of high-quality scholarly literature, the
exclusion of grey literature may have limited the comprehensiveness of the evidence base. Future systematic reviews
could incorporate grey-literature sources to further strengthen the robustness and completeness of the synthesized
evidence.

Findings and discussion
Stimulus Factors Influencing Meme Marketing Eﬀectiveness
The literature identifies several stimulus factors that influence the eﬀectiveness of meme marketing in digital
environments. The reviewed literature consistently identifies humor, relatability, cultural relevance, trend responsiveness,
and platform adaptability as the primary stimulus factors influencing the eﬀectiveness of meme marketing in digital
environments. These attributes may function as environmental stimuli that are associated with consumers' cognitive
attention, emotional involvement, and behavioral responses. In addition to the advantages of the meme as a
communication tool, meme marketing often integrates informal, participatory, and culturally embedded communication
styles, which are more similar to the communication patterns found in users of today's social media (Miltner, 2018)
(Gretry et al., 2017). This makes consumers more likely to interact with brand content, share it online, and feel a social
connection with it, making brand communication more palatable and relatable due to being meme focused. Of the
stimulus dimensions identified, humor was the most widely discussed stimulus dimension found in the reviewed
studies. Recent empirical evidence also suggests that meme marketing activities are positively associated with consumer
engagement and purchase-related outcomes, particularly through enhanced online interaction and emotional
resonance  (Kim and Baek, 2025) (Rathi and Jain, 2024). Humor has been consistently associated with higher levels of
attention, emotional accessibility, and online engagement among audiences toward branded content, as it lowers the
persuasive barriers to the message and encourages audiences to engage with it voluntarily (Malodia et al., 2022), (Ashley
and Tuten, 2015), and (De Vries et al., 2012). In addition, there are several recent studies indicating that humor-based
meme communication appears particularly relevant within short-form social media contexts, where entertainment-
related content features predominantly shape patterns of consumer communication (Xiao et al., 2026). A recent study on
meme engagement with India's youth also stated that funny meme formats have been found to boost digital
engagement and sharing of content among the Indian young audiences, who are generally more inclined towards
informal digital communication styles (Bowo et al., 2024). Similar findings have been reported in social media
engagement research, where creative and interactive content characteristics significantly influence user participation
and content popularity (Cvijikj and Michahelles, 2013) (De Vries et al., 2012). A recent study on consumer interaction with
brands through memes has also indicated that humor in brand communication can lead to higher memorability and
emotional engagement with the content, as it combines entertainment with familiar social interactions (Walsh et al.,
2024). However, humor is not always seen as a universally good thing in the literature. Some studies have warned that the
eﬀectiveness of memes is highly dependent on context - when a meme goes too far in terms of humor, lack of cultural
sensitivity, political content, or brand identity (Miltner, 2018) (Gretry et al., 2017). In some instances, too performative a
meme communication can lead to the loss of a professional image and the loss of brand credibility, particularly when the
audience is used to information-based seriousness or institutional trust. Overall, these conflicting results indicate that the
value of humor is not solely defined by entertainment value, but by the appropriateness of the content, cultural
sensitivity, and audience expectations in specific digital settings.
Another key stimulus factor that aﬀects the success of a meme marketing campaign is relatability. The currently available
research indicates that consumers respond positively to meme-based communication when the message resonates with
them as a shared online experience, generational humor, social frustrations or culturally recognizable situations (Teng et
al., 2022) (Nasution and Silalahi, 2025). This relatable factor appears to strengthen the psychological connection with
content and may increase the likelihood of online engagement and peer-to-peer sharing. In addition, various studies on
younger digital audiences have found that communicating through memes that resonate with online youth can help
give the impression that brands are attuned to the current online culture and shifts in audience behavior, leading to
greater perceptions of authenticity (Teng et al., 2022) (Bowo et al., 2024). As such, meme marketing has become a kind of
marketing tactic and a symbolic means by which brands become a part of continuous digital dialogue and a shared
cultural experience. The relevance of the trend and platform adaptability were also found to be important factors
influencing the eﬀectiveness of the meme. Memes exist in rapidly changing digital contexts, where the relevance of the
content is often short-lived and algorithm-driven, unlike static advertising formats. Recent research has highlighted that
timing, context, and rapid brand reactions to new trends and conversations online are vital to the success of meme

Organism Responses: Cognitive and Emotional Consumer Reactions
In the S-O-R approach, organism responses are the internal psychological and emotional responses that occur as a result
of external marketing stimuli. A number of the studies reviewed indicated that meme marketing has a substantial impact
on the emotional involvement, attention to the content, entertainment value, and perception of the brand among
consumers in digital settings. Meme communication often evokes aﬀective responses, including aspects of humor, irony,
emotional relevance, and cultural familiarity, enabling consumers to psychologically process and emotionally engage
with branded content, which is not the case with traditional promotional advertising. Across the literature, emotional
engagement was one of the most widespread organism-level reactions.  This finding is consistent with consumer
engagement literature, which conceptualizes engagement as a multidimensional construct comprising cognitive,
emotional, and behavioral dimensions (Hollebeek et al., 2014), (Brodie et al., 2013), and (Dessart et al., 2015). There are
some studies which indicate that using memes in communication can improve emotional connection because it makes
the communication between brands and customers entertaining and friendly (Teng et al., 2022) (Nasution and Silalahi,
2025). Consumers find meme-based communication more engaging, verbal, and "natural" than advertising, which
enhances the emotional impact and decreases the feeling of "pushy" advertising (Vivek et al., 2012) (Hollebeek et al.,
2014). Studies in recent years on how younger digital audiences engage with memes also suggest that when the meme is
emotionally relatable, it can enhance the sense of social connectedness and digital intimacy between the brand and
consumer (Xiao et al., 2026) (Bowo et al., 2024). The results indicate that increasingly, meme marketing is making use of
emotional familiarity and participatory interaction instead of simply promotional persuasion. Cognitive attention and
interpretative engagement have also been attributed as important organismic responses to meme marketing, as found in
the literature. Within an increasingly saturated social media ecosystem characterized by fragmented consumer attention,
memes frequently attract users through visual simplicity, symbolic compression, humor, and culturally resonant
references (Jenkins, 2006), (Shifman, 2013), and (Teng et al., 2022). Some researchers suggest that memes evoke higher
order thinking because viewers are required to interpret more complex ideas, expressions of irony, and socially relevant
references included in the content.  This observation aligns with studies demonstrating that meaningful engagement

appropriately balanced with the brand identity and communication goals. The studies examined in this report

engagement. Emotional resonance, interpretive engagement, authenticity, amusement, and cognitive attention are the

however, also suggests that these organismic reactions are still context sensitive and culture-specific, shaped by platform
appropriateness, cultural authenticity of communication, cultural interpretation patterns, and the evolving social media
environments.
Theoretical and practical implications
Theoretical Implications
This study contributes to the digital marketing and consumer behavior literature by providing a conceptual integration of
meme marketing through the S-O-R  framework. Previous meme marketing research has tended to be siloed into
separate dimensions such as humor, engagement, virality, emotional reaction and eWOM with little theoretical linkages
between these dimensions (Hollebeek et al., 2014) (Dessart et al., 2015). The present review provides a synthesis of results
obtained in cognitive, aﬀective, and behavioral areas, thereby adding to the existing literature and establishing that
meme marketing is a multidimensional communication medium and not just a humorous promotional tool. The results
suggest that meme eﬀectiveness is created through the mediation of symbolic communication, emotional processing,
cultural familiarity, and participatory digital engagement in rapidly changing digital environments. The main theoretical
contribution of the study is the extension of the S-O-R model in the context of participatory digital communication. The
S-O-R theory has been used extensively in consumer studies, online retailing, and digital service environments, but its
application in the case of meme-driven communication is still relatively undeveloped. This present review illustrates that

keeping up with the trends can help build emotional connection and online engagement, while at the same time
compromising credibility or creating an ambiguity of interpretation in other instances. The diﬀerences suggest that some
context-related factors are aﬀecting the success of memes: platform culture, demographics, symbolic familiarity, and
communicative appropriateness. The review consequently indicates that future theoretical models that investigate
meme marketing should be more context-sensitive and culturally adaptive so as to better reflect the fluid and
interpretive dynamics of digital communication environments. Lastly, the study adds methodologically to the growing

synthesis. The current literature on meme marketing is largely cross-sectional in nature, quantitative, and largely centered
on measures of engagement and short-term behavioral reactions. The present review integrates literature in a

symbolic interpretation, digital interaction, and long-term consumer-brand relationships can be studied in meme-driven
communication environments.

There are several practical lessons for marketers, digital strategists, social media managers, and any brand that relies on
meme communication in competitive digital spaces. When done in a culturally relevant and contextually appropriate
manner, the reviewed studies have all shown that meme marketing has a significant impact on improving online
visibility, audience engagement, emotional resonance, and eWOM (Godey et al., 2016) (Hudson et al., 2016). The results,
however, suggest that the success of a meme is highly dependent on the ability to adapt, awareness of platforms, and
how the audience interprets the content, which means that using internet humor in brand messages is not as simple as it
sounds. Perhaps the most meaningful implication for practice is that of cultural relevance and knowledge of the audience
in the use of meme-based communication strategies. The interpretation of the meme is very context-specific - depending
on the social experiences of the viewer, their level of familiarity with the symbol, and their engagement with digital
culture, it is important that marketers understand how their audience will react to a meme campaign on each individual
platform before implementing it. Brands that try to catch the "meme wave"  without the knowledge of the changing
online conversations might come across as performative, dated, or unengaging to the audience. Therefore, it is essential
to continuously listen to social media, monitor trends, and analyze audiences to ensure that meme marketing strategies
are relevant and authentic. The findings further suggest that humor should be viewed not merely as an engagement
strategy but as a strategic communication tool capable of shaping brand perceptions and consumer relationships. While
memes can help boost engagement and reach, overuse of humor, inappropriate cultural references, or an attempt to
jump on a trend can hinder brand credibility and consumer trust. This is especially vital for brands that are found in
industries that demand institutional gravity, moral awareness, or informational accuracy. However, brands must strike a
balance between the entertainment value and maintaining consistency with the brand, so that meme communication
can be in line with the broader strategic positioning of the brand. A second implication is related to the platform-specific
communication adaptation. The literature reviewed shows that the eﬀectiveness of memes varies significantly across
digital platforms, with consumer interactions, algorithms, and expectations varying between diﬀerent platforms like
Instagram, TikTok, X, and Reddit (Ashley and Tuten, 2015) (Tsai and Men, 2013). Brands should therefore not use a one-
size-fits-all approach to memes and instead adapt their communications so that they fit the culture of each platform and
the method of engagement of that digital ecosystem. Depending on the conditions of the platform and the
demographics of the audience, the performance of each short-form video meme, conversational meme thread, reaction-
based format, and image caption meme might vary. The results also show that memes are more eﬀective with younger
audiences, particularly Gen Z consumers, who tend to prefer informal, participatory, and culturally attuned
communication (Yadav and Rai, 2017) (Chu, 2011). Brands targeting the younger crowd might thus be better served with
a more meme-like approach focused on authenticity, humor, emotional relatedness and social engagement, rather than
an all-too-corporate message. But the review also shows that younger people can be very aware of inauthenticity,
opportunistic involvement with a trend, and overcommercialization. That means brands need to be a part of memes in a
way that is not fake, but relevant and sincere. The review also emphasizes the emerging role of ethic sensitivity in
environments of communication based on memes. However, some of the studies analyzed in the review warn that meme
marketing can also lead to backlash when messages are based on stereotypes, oﬀensive jokes, cultural appropriation,
and/or insensitive to social issues. In networked digital environments, the transmission of problematic memes is fast, and
the repercussions of the negative reputation of problematic meme communication can rapidly build up through online
amplification and public criticism. Organizations should, therefore, enhance their ethical review processes and culturally
appropriate content evaluation processes before launching campaigns that are based on memes, especially in a diverse
or international audience settings. Lastly, the study reveals that marketers need to more seriously consider meme
marketing as a component of a wider participatory digital communication ecosystem, not just as a viral content
approach. Creativity is not the only determinant of a successful meme communication, but its responsiveness, cultural
timing, symbolic relevance, and co-participation by the audience alike. Brands that can seamlessly incorporate these
elements into their strategy have a better chance of enhancing emotional connections, community engagement, and
sustained online presence in the crowded social media landscape.

Despite providing a comprehensive synthesis of the literature on meme marketing and consumer behavior, this review
has several limitations. First, the review was restricted to English-language publications and selected academic
databases, which may have resulted in the exclusion of relevant studies published in other languages or indexed in
alternative sources. Second, meme marketing is a rapidly evolving field influenced by changing platform algorithms,
digital cultures, and communication trends; therefore, the findings reflect the state of knowledge available during the
review period. Third, the existing body of literature is dominated by cross-sectional quantitative studies, limiting current
understanding of long-term consumer responses, relationship development, and behavioral changes associated with
meme-based communication.
The review also identified several avenues for future research. Greater attention should be devoted to examining the
long-term eﬀects of meme marketing on brand trust, customer loyalty, and consumer-brand relationship quality. Future
studies may employ longitudinal and mixed-method research designs to better capture how consumer interpretations
and engagement with meme-based content evolve over time. In addition, comparative studies across diﬀerent social
media platforms may provide deeper insights into how platform characteristics, algorithmic visibility, and community
norms influence the eﬀectiveness of meme marketing strategies.
Further research is also needed to explore cross-cultural variations in meme interpretation and consumer responses. As
memes derive meaning from cultural references, language, and social context, their eﬀectiveness may vary significantly
across regions and demographic groups. Moreover, the increasing use of artificial intelligence, automated content
generation, and personalized recommendation systems presents new opportunities and challenges for meme-based
communication. Future research should therefore investigate the implications of AI-assisted meme creation, authenticity
perceptions, and ethical concerns related to misinformation, cultural appropriation, stereotyping, and responsible digital
communication practices.
Conclusions
This systematic literature review examined the relationship between meme marketing and consumer behavior through
the S-O-R framework. The findings suggest that meme marketing has evolved beyond a humorous communication tactic
and appears to function as a culturally situated and emotionally resonant form of digital brand communication. The
review identifies humor, relatability, trend relevance, cultural familiarity, and visual symbolism as recurring stimulus
factors that are consistently associated with consumers' cognitive and emotional responses, which may subsequently
contribute to behavioral outcomes such as engagement, eWOM, sharing behavior, and purchase intention.
The review further indicates that the eﬀectiveness of meme marketing is contingent upon contextual factors including
platform culture, audience expectations, authenticity, and cultural sensitivity. While meme-based communication
appears to support consumer engagement and brand interaction, its eﬀectiveness may depend on the ability of brands
to balance creativity, relevance, and responsible communication practices.
The primary contribution of this review lies in integrating fragmented findings into a comprehensive S-O-R framework
that provides a structured perspective for understanding meme-based communication within participatory digital
environments. By synthesizing existing evidence and identifying recurring mechanisms underlying consumer responses,
the study contributes to the growing literature on digital marketing and oﬀers practical insights for marketers seeking to
develop authentic, audience-oriented, and ethically responsible meme marketing strategies. Recent evidence also
indicates that branded memes may facilitate stronger aﬀective consumer-brand relationships and contribute to the
development of brand attachment within social media environments, particularly among digitally engaged audiences.
Overall, the review suggests that meme marketing represents an increasingly important communication approach within
contemporary digital ecosystems and warrants further empirical investigation across diverse cultural and platform
contexts.
