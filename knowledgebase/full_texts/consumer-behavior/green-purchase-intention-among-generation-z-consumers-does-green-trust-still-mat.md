Abstract: The increasing pollution caused by plastic waste, along with the rise of the
green consumerism trend, has encouraged businesses to develop more
environmentally friendly products by targeting the Generation Z consumer segment.
However, the high awareness of  environmental issues is not necessarily reflected in
consistent green product purchasing behavior among consumers. This research set
out to assess how far green perceived value, environmental concern, and green trust
shape the green purchase intention of Generation Z consumers of Eiger tumbler
products in Surabaya. A quantitative survey design was used, drawing on a purposive
sample of 160 participants, with data gathered via an online questionnaire and
processed through PLS -SEM in SmartPLS. Results show t hat both green perceived
value and environmental concern exert a positive and meaningful effect on green
trust as well as on green purchase intention. By contrast, green trust did not
significantly affect purchase intention and was unable to mediate the re lationship
between the two predictor variables and purchase intention. These outcomes suggest
that Generation Z places greater weight on a product's tangible benefits and their
own environmental commitment than on confidence in a brand's sustainability cla ims.
The study further contributes to the body of knowledge on green consumer behavior,
particularly regarding locally produced reusable goods within developing  country
markets.

INTRODUCTION
Among the various environmental challenges faced globally, the accumulation of plastic waste
stands out as particularly urgent, a concern that Indonesia also grapples with. The National Waste
Management Information System (SIPSN) recorded a total national waste volume of 33.79 million
tons for 2024, within which approximately 7.8 million tons were plastic materials that remained
inadequately handled (GoodStats 2025 a; E nviu Indonesia 2024) . This condition causes marine
pollution, waste disposal in landfills, and air pollution due to uncontrolled waste burning. The impact
is not only felt on the environment but also affects the social and economic conditions of the
community. This condition places Indonesia among the countries with the highest contribution o f
plastic waste globally, so concrete steps are needed to encourage a shift in people's consumption
patterns to be more responsible and oriented towards long -term sustainability  (CNBC Indonesia,
2024).
In this context, Generation Z is a group with great potential to drive change towards
sustainable consumption. This generation, born between 1997 and 2012, represents approximately
27.94% of Indonesia's total population and was raised in an era of digital technological
advancements that opened up widespread  access to a variety of information regarding global
environmental issues (BPS, 2025). Concern for the future of the Earth and a strong understanding of

modern global challenges make Generation Z a highly promising consumer segment for sustainable
products (Hidayat et al., 2024). According to a bibliometric study conducted by Sholihah et al., (2025)
research on the role of Generation Z in sustainable consumption has become a prominent academic
trend. These findings position sustainable consumption as a key pillar closely linked to consumer
behavior, eco-friendly marketing, and the advancement of the SDGs.
Generation Z's awareness of environmental issues is beginning to be evident in their daily
behavior, one example being the habit of carrying their own water bottles as an effort to reduce
dependence on single -use plastic packaging. GoodStats (2025) noted that carrying their own water
bottles is one of the most common actions taken by Gen Z to reduce plastic waste. However, this
high level of environmental awareness is not always followed by consistent green product
purchasing behavior. Findings Nguyen et al. (2019) show that intentions to purchase environmentally
friendly products often do not align with actual purchases. This presents a unique challenge for
businesses in identifying factors that can motivate consumers to actually realize their int entions to
purchase environmentally friendly products.
One local brand striving to address the green consumerism trend is Eiger Adventure. Founded
in 1989, this Bandung -based outdoor gear company offers a variety of eco -friendly tumbler
products. Eiger uses materials such as food-grade stainless steel with double-wall vacuum insulation,
BPA- and PVC-free Ecozen (Tritan), and sustainable teak wood in its Timberlake Tumbler series. The
company's commitment to sustainability is further strengthened through  its ESG reports, published
regularly since 2021. The percentage of renewable material use has increased from 4% in 2021 to
around 20-21% in 2023 (Eiger, 2025).
Several previous studies have discussed the factors influencing green purchase intention.  Li &
Shan (2025)  found that green perceived value and green trust mediate the influence of green
advertising on organic food purchase intention in China. Research  Cahyadi & Widagda (2023)
showed that environmental concern and green perceived value influence Starbucks tumbler
purchase intention in Bali through attitude toward green as a mediating variable. Another study by
Siringoringo & Harsoyo (2025)  proved that environmental concern an d green perceived value
significantly influence organic food purchase intention, while  Wibowo et al., (2022)  found an effect
of green trust on purchase intention for bottled drinking water products in Surabaya. However, most
of these studies still focus on product categories such as organic food, green cosmetics, or eco -
friendly electronics, and are conducted primarily in the context of global brands and developed
country markets.
There are research gaps that need to be filled by future studies. First, st udies on reusable
products based on local Indonesian brands, particularly tumblers, in relation to Gen Z consumer
behavior, are still relatively rare. Second, the three main variables, namely Green Perceived Value,
Environmental Concern, and Green Trust, a re generally studied separately, so there is no complete
picture of how they simultaneously influence Green Purchase Intention. Third, the role of Green
Trust as a mediating variable in the context of reusable products from local brands among the
younger generation has not received adequate attention in the existing literature. Thus, this study
aims to address this gap, namely how Green Perceived Value, Environmental Concern, and Green
Trust together shape the Green Purchase Intention of Gen Z consumers tow ards local Indonesian
brand tumblers in emerging markets, an issue that has not been comprehensively answered by
previous studies.

This research is based on the Value Belief Norm (VBN) as the primary theoretical framework.
Stern (2000) formulated VBN as a macro model that links an individual's value orientation with their
commitment to environmental sustainability; environmentally friendly behavior will be realized when
individuals embrace nature -centered values, recognize real threats to the ecosystem, and feel
personally responsible for responding to these negative impacts.
Within this framework, Green Perceived Value refers to consumers' evaluation of the
advantages and benefits derived from environmentally friendly products, measured  by the balance
between benefits received and sacrifices incurred, taking into account the needs and expectations
for ecological sustainability  (Rakhmawati et al., 2019 ; Jalu et al ., 2023). Environmental Concern is
defined as the level of individual concern for ecological issues that encourages more responsible
consumption behavior (Dunlap & Jones, 2002; Sharma & Foropon, 2019). Meanwhile, Green Trust is
consumers' belief in the credibili ty and ability of a product to fulfill its promised environmental
claims; this trust is formed when consumers perceive a brand's sustainability statements as verifiable
and trustworthy  (Lee, 2020; Wibowo et al., 2022 ). Green Purchase Intention is defined  as the
tendency of consumers to choose and purchase environmentally friendly products that have
sustainable features, where ecological factors are actively considered in purchasing decision making
(Wulandari & Fikri, 2025; Rismayanti & Harsoyo, 2025).
RESEARCH METHOD
This research adopted a quantitative survey design to test the relationships among the study
variables. Primary data were gathered electronically via a structured questionnaire distributed
through Google Forms, with each item rated on a Lik ert scale to translate respondents' subjective
perceptions into numerical values. The resulting raw data were then processed statistically using
structural equation modeling in SmartPLS. Because of the specific requirements of the data needed,
sampling was  carried out using a non -probability, purposive technique, yielding 160 respondents
considered relevant and representative for the study.
The determination of the sample size of 160 respondents was based on the inverse square root
method developed by Kock & Hadaya (2018), where this number is the minimum size recommended
for PLS-SEM analysis. This number also exceeds the 10 times rule proposed by  Hair et al. , (2017),
which requires a minimum of 10 times the number of the largest structural paths leading  to one
construct in this model of 3 paths, so the minimum required is 30 respondents.
The respondent group in this study was strictly screened based on three main criteria. First, the
demographic age limit targeted Generation Z, aged 17 to 29. Second, the  geographic limitation,
requiring participants to reside in the city of Surabaya. Third, the cognitive criterion required
respondents to have knowledge or awareness of Eiger tumbler products. These qualifications were
implemented to ensure that the opinion s collected through the questionnaire accurately reflected
local market dynamics.

 Verification of all hypotheses in this study can be summarized based on the data processing
results presented in the table above:
1. Green Perceived Value (X1) is shown to exert a positive and significant effect on Green Trust (M),
confirming that consumers who place greater value on a product's environ mental benefits
develop stronger trust in that green product, H1 is therefore supported.
2. Environmental Concern (X2) likewise exerts a positive and significant effect on Green Trust (M).
This result suggests that individuals who pay close attention to sustainability issues are more
inclined to extend trust toward offerings associated with the concept of sustainability, supporting
H2.
3. Green Perceived Value (X1) also has a positive and significant effect on Green Purchase Intention
(Y), indicating that a favorable perception of a product's green benefits can directly heighten
consumers' interest in completing a purchase, thereby supporting H3.
4. Environmental Concern (X2) is found to positively and significantly affect Green Purchase
Intention (Y), with this internal motivation emerging as one of the strongest drivers of consumers'
intention to buy environmentally friendly products, confirming H4.
5. Green Trust ( M) shows no significant effect on Green Purchase Intention (Y), implying that
consumer confidence in a product's green attributes does not by itself generate a strong purchase
intention; H5 is therefore not supported.
6. The pathway from Green Perceived Value (X1) through Green Trust ( M) to Green Purchase
Intention (Y) is also not significant, confirming th at Green Trust does not function as a mediator
linking these two constructs. Consumers' perceptions of a product's environmental benefits
appear capable of driving purchase intention directly, without first passing through a trust -
formation stage, leading to the rejection of H6.
7. Similarly, Environmental Concern (X2) channeled through Green Trust (Z) does not significantly
affect Green Purchase Intention (Y), confirming that Green Trust fails to mediate this relationship.
Consumers' environmental concern app ears able to independently spark their purchase interest
without requiring trust as an intermediary step, so H7 is not supported.

Discussion
The Influence of Green Perceived Value on Green Trust
 The first hypothesis test confirms that Green Perceived Value positively and significantly
shapes Green Trust. This suggests that consumers who rate the ecological and practical advantages
of the Eiger tumbler highly are more likely to develop firmer convi ctions about the credibility of the
brand's environmental claims. In other words, the more substantial the sustainability  related benefits
consumers perceive, the more solid their green trust becomes.
 Theoretically, this finding can be explained through V alue Belief Norm Theory, which states
that an individual's value orientation toward the environment gradually shapes ecologically informed
normative beliefs. In the context of this study, Generation Z consumers who directly experience the
superiority of product materials, such as the use of food -grade stainless steel, BPA -free design, and
the transparency of Eiger's ESG reporting, tend to interpret the consistency between the brand
promise and actual experience as concrete evidence that strengthens their green trust. This suggests
that utilitarian evaluations of product benefits serve as cognitive antecedents for the formation of
green trust.
 These findings align with research by  (Wicaksono & Darpito 2023) , which found that
perceived value of environmentally friendly products positively contributes to consumer trust in Le
Minerale gallon bottles, and research by Marques & Dewi (2022)  on Tupperware products. The
similarity of these results across product ca tegories strengthens the argument that green perceived
value is a consistent predictor of green trust, regardless of product type. Differences in research
contexts, such as cultural background, product category, and age segment, do not diminish the
consistency of this relationship, indicating that the cognitive mechanisms between perceived value
and green trust are relatively universal among ecologically oriented consumers.
 The implications of these findings suggest that, theoretically, these results stren gthen the
relevance of Value Belief Norm Theory in explaining the formation of green trust among young
consumers in emerging markets. Managerially, Eiger needs to consistently communicate concrete
evidence of product sustainability, such as material certif ication information, measurable
environmental impact reports, and supply chain transparency narratives, as key strategies in building
and maintaining green trust among the Generation Z segment.
The Influence of Environmental Concern on Green Trust
 The second hypothesis test likewise confirms a positive and significant influence of
Environmental Concern on Green Trust. This indicates that individuals who care deeply about
environmental matters tend to apply a more discerning evaluative lens when assessin g a brand's
sustainability claims, which in turn makes it easier for them to trust products perceived as genuinely
committed to green practices.
 This outcome can be situated within Value Belief Norm Theory, which proposes that
recognizing environmental threats leads individuals to believe that ecosystem damage carries direct
consequences for human life, thereby shaping personal norms oriented toward ecology. Here,
Generation Z respondents' concern over the scale of Surabaya's plastic waste problem appears to
sharpen their scrutiny of how authentic a brand's environmental claims really are. Those with
stronger environmental concern tend to seek out additional information about a brand's

The Influence of Green Perceived Value on Green Purchase Intention
 The third hypothesis is supported, with Green Perceived Value found to have a direct and
significant bearing on Green Purchase Intention. This indicates that, beyond evaluating the Eiger
tumbler purely on functionality and looks, Generation Z consumers actively weigh how meaningfully
the product reduces environmental harm. The ecological benefits they perceive directly appear to act
as a catalyst that speeds up their decision to purchase green products.
 This finding can be explained through Value Belief Norm Theory, which states that
internalized pro-environmental values encourage responsible consumption behavior. In the context
of this study, consumers' positive perceptions of the Eiger tumbler's contr ibution to reducing daily
plastic waste, a highly relevant ecological issue for Generation Z, serve as a substitute value that
integrates utilitarian and ethical considerations into a single purchase decision. This is consistent with
the notion that young consumers increasingly view purchasing green products as an expression of
identity and moral commitment, rather than simply an economic transaction.
 This research finding aligns with several previous studies that have examined the relationship
between gre en perceived value and green purchase intention.  Wicaksono & Darpito (2023)
examined the Le Mineral disposable gallon product.  Li & Shan (2025)  in the organic food segment,
and Cahyadi & Widagda (2023)  in the Starbucks tumbler in Bali, demonstrated t he positive and
significant role of green perceived value on green purchase intention. The consistency of these
findings across different green product categories strengthens green perceived value's position as a
reliable predictor of green purchase intention.
 The implications of these findings suggest that green perceived value in the context of green
products has dual utilitarian and ecological dimensions, both of which contribute to purchase
intention. Managerially, Eiger needs to communicate the produc t's ecological advantages more
specifically and measurably, for example, through quantitative claims of plastic reduction per tumbler
sold or avoided carbon footprint, to further strengthen consumer perceived value in driving purchase
intention.

The Influence of Environmental Concern on Green Purchase Intention
 The fourth hypothesis test reveals that Environmental Concern positively and significantly
shapes Green Purchase Intention, and does so more strongly than any other predictor in the model.
This points to environmental concern as the leading driver behind Generation Z's intentions to
purchase green products, with internal moral motivation exerting a more powerful pull than purely
utilitarian assessments of product benefits.
 These findings are rel evant to the Value Belief Norm principle, which states that personal
norms formed from ecological concern and an awareness of personal responsibility will directly guide
pro-environmental consumption behavior without the need for complex mediation. In the context of
this research, Generation Z, who grew up amidst massive information about the plastic crisis and its
environmental impacts, feels a moral obligation to contribute through sustainable consumption
choices. Reusable products like the Eiger tumbler serve as a concrete instrument for expressing this
moral responsibility, resulting in relatively instinctive purchase intentions without the need for
lengthy benefit calculations.
 These research results also strengthen the consistency of findings from sev eral previous
studies. Cahyadi & Widagda (2023) examined Starbucks tumblers, and Siringoringo & Harsoyo (2025)
demonstrated a positive and significant influence of environmental concern on green purchase
intention. This contrasts with the findings of  Anggraini et al. , (2023) who found a positive but
insignificant influence. This difference is likely due to differences in the ecological relevance of the
products studied. Tumblers, as a direct substitute for single -use plastic, are more explicitly link ed to
the plastic waste issue, a primary concern for Generation Z. Therefore, environmental concern
translates more directly into purchase intention than products like eco -friendly homes, which have a
larger perception gap.
 The implications of these findi ngs suggest that the dominance of environmental concern
strengthens the relevance of Value Belief Norm Theory in explaining green consumption behavior
among Generation Z, while also providing empirical evidence that moral motivations are more
dominant than  utilitarian evaluations in this segment. Managerially, a marketing communications
approach that activates moral awareness through educational content based on environmental
impact data and community -based social campaigns will be more effective in driving  purchase
intention than advertising that only emphasizes product features.
The Influence of Green Trust on Green Purchase Intention
 Contrary to expectations, the fifth hypothesis test shows that Green Trust does not
significantly influence Green Purchase Intention. This suggests that trusting a brand's green claims is
not a major factor behind Generation Z's intention to buy reusable products. For items in this
category, such as tumblers, consumers seem to lean more on functional benefits and visi ble
environmental outcomes than on symbolic, trust-based judgments of the brand.
 This finding can be explained by Generation Z's characteristics as digitally informed
consumers who have extensive access to product information and are more responsive to ta ngible
environmental benefits than abstract brand claims. In the context of this study, reusable products
like tumblers have utilitarian value that can be directly observed and evaluated, for example through
material durability and the reduction in plastic  waste that is evident in everyday life. This condition

The Influence of Green Perceived Value on Green Purchase Intention through Green Trust
 The sixth hypothesis, which tested the mediating role of Green Trust in the pathway of Green
Perceived Value toward Green Purchase Intention, was rejected. This means that Generation Z's
perception of green value in Surabaya is capable of independently driving purchase intention without
first having to go through the process of trust formation. This pattern suggests that Generation Z
operates with a more concise and direct decision mechanism, where the evaluation of a product's
ecological benefits is strong enough to trigger purchase intention w ithout requiring an additional
layer of belief as an intermediary.
 Theoretically, these results can be explained through Value Belief Norm Theory, which states
that pro -environmental behavior can directly drive green consumption behavior through personal
norms without always requiring the construct of trust as an intermediary. In the context of this
research, the psychological mechanisms at work differ between green trust and green purchase
intention. Trust is a long -term cognitive construct formed through  repeated evaluations, while
purchase intention in Generation Z is more driven by the relevance of a product's value to their
current ecological identity and personal commitment. When perceived value is strong enough, for
example because a product clearly reduces plastic waste, the decision process becomes more
efficient without going through the trust stage.
 This finding contradicts research by  Wicaksono & Darpito (2023) , which found that green
trust mediates the relationship between perceived green val ue and purchase intention for Le
Minerale gallons. This difference in results is likely due to differences in product characteristics and
consumer involvement. Water gallons are FMCG products with short purchase cycles and low
involvement, so brand trust acts as a heuristic to simplify decisions. Conversely, tumblers are durable
products with a higher level of involvement, so consumers conduct a more in -depth self-evaluation

The Influence of Environmental Concern on Green Purchase Intention through Green Trust
The seventh hypothesis was also not supported, meaning Green Trust failed to mediate the
effect of Environmental Concern on Green Purchase Intention. This finding indicates that consumer
concern for environmental issues is a sufficient driving force to directly trigger purchase intention for
green products, without requiring the construct of trust as a bridge between the two.
Theoretically, these results can be explained thro ugh Value Belief Norm Theory, which states that
individuals who feel a moral responsibility towards the environment will act directly based on their
personal norms without necessarily requiring the mediation of trust. In the context of this study,
Generation Z in Surabaya who believe they have a personal obligation to reduce their contribution to
plastic waste tend to make more instinctive purchasing decisions for substitute products.
Environmental concern, internalized within their personal value system, a cts as a stand -alone
behavioral trigger, making it more efficient in generating purchase intention than the pathway
mediated by brand trust.
 This study's results differ from the findings of  Khan & Qureshi (2025), who found green trust
to be a significant mediator between environmental concern and purchase intention. This difference
is likely due to differences in cultural context and product characteristics. In the Pakistani consumer
context studied by Khan & Qureshi, brand trust may play a larger role a s a determining factor due to
varying levels of skepticism toward green claims. Conversely, in Indonesia, particularly among
Generation Z, who are highly exposed to sustainability content on social media, consumers are more
accustomed to evaluating environmental benefits independently rather than relying on formal brand
trust. This research finding aligns with research conducted by  Wahyumar et al., (2023)  which found
similar results for green personal care products in Indonesia.
 The implications of this finding strengthen the argument that the failure of green trust as a
mediator is not an anomaly, but rather reflects a paradigm shift in young consumer behavior, which
increasingly relies on evidence -based evaluations rather than symbolic trust. This contr ibutes to the
development of green consumer behavior models that are more contextual and sensitive to the
characteristics of Generation Z. Managerially, marketing strategies that integrate ecological
community narratives, data -driven environmental educatio n content, and activation of consumers'
green identities will be more relevant for targeting Generation Z than strategies that rely solely on
building brand trust through conventional green claim communication.
CONCLUSION
This study offers empirical eviden ce on how green perceived value and environmental
concern shape green trust and purchase intention toward environmentally friendly products among
young consumers in Surabaya, using the Eiger tumbler as the object of study. The data analysis
reveals that both green perceived value and environmental concern play significant roles in fostering
green trust while also directly driving green purchase intention, with environmental concern
emerging as the most influential factor. Notably, green trust was not found to be a direct
determinant of purchase intention, nor did it function as a mediator between the two predictor
