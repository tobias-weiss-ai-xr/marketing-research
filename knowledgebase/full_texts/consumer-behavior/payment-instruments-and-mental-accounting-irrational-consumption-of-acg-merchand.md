Abstract. Payment methods now play an increasing role in reshaping mental accounting—
especially with regard to ACG merchandise—within a fast-growing part of the cultural
economy. A case study of Bilibili Membership Mall is used here to represent Gen Z ACG
consumer behavior in China; the analysis relies on both transactional data and survey
responses to explore, in some detail, the interaction of digital payments with fandom-related
psychology. This study applies mental accounting ideas to the pain-of-paying concept and
analyzes specifically, with regard to ACG merchandise, how mobile or credit payment
instruments may lead to irrational consumption choices. The work identifies positive roles
played by such payment methods: they help to lower consumption barriers, to make
consumption more complete, and to promote second-hand use, for these mechanisms also
run the risk of creating system-wide problems. With regard to mental accounting distortions
or overindebtedness, speculative leveraging on secondary markets, and socially sustained
irrational spending. To counter such risks, the present analysis advocates a coordinated
governance model—based on platform-level control of payments, secondary-market rules,
and localized digital literacy efforts. It examines how modes of payment affect mental
accounting and thus expands behavioral finance in ways useful for thinking about the long-
term viability of the ACG consumer market.
Keywords: ACG Merchandise Consumption, Payment Instruments, Mental Accounting,
Pain-of-Paying, Consumer Over-Indebtedness
1. Introduction
1.1. Research context and motivation
The global market for anime, comics, and games (ACG) merchandise has expanded rapidly in recent
years. ACG merchandise refers to physical products derived from anime, comic, and game
intellectual property. It has become an important category of consumption for young people. In
2024, China's anime goods market reached roughly 597. 7 billion yuan (about 82 billion US dollars).
Items such as badges, blind boxes, and acrylic stands have emerged as prominent categories within
China's consumer economy. China's goods economy reached 168. 9 billion yuan in 2024, up 40.
63%from the year before [1]. The market for second-dimensional intellectual property goods
jumped from 5.3 billion yuan in 2016 to 102.4 billion yuan in 2023, almost 20 times bigger [1].

Mobile payment has fundamentally altered consumers' mental accounting and their cognitive
processing of money. Mental accounting is the way people put money into different mental accounts
and track it. According to Thaler's mental accounting theory, people often separate gains and
combine losses. They put money into different mind accounts based on where it comes from, how it
is stored, and what it will be used for [2]. Mobile payments, such as WeChat Pay and Alipay, and
credit tools, such as Huabei and Baitiao, make paying much less painful. Studies reveal that people
spend 15% to 25% more when they use digital payments instead of cash [3]. Limited items, hidden
items, and IP collaborations push up prices in the second-hand market. Some products even sell for
39 times their original price [4]. Driven by fear of missing out (FOMO) and reduced payment pain,
consumers frequently exceed predetermined budgets.
Such a consumption pattern is now responsible for certain money troubles among young people
and for peer comparison or other social issues. It is therefore necessary to analyze—on an individual
level—how payment methods affect mental accounting in consumption, and young people may be
helped to spend sensibly and to preserve the health of the IP products market.
1.2. Literature review
Mental accounting theory—originally proposed by Thaler—suggests that people classify their
money into separate mental accounts (with particular budgets) and distinguish between acquisition
and transaction utility [2]. Prelec and Loewenstein found substantial differences in the pain of
paying according to method, while Soman, Raghubir, and Srivastava proved that credit cards help to
ease such pain compared to cash [5-7]. With regard to mobile payments, Shah et al., working with
over 25,000 US households, have linked digital forms of payment to both increased spending and
costly credit-taking [3, 8]. From a neuroscientific standpoint, it is clear that mobile payments relieve
pain-of-paying and may trigger a "pleasure-of-paying" effect (via reward-linked brain activity) [9].
Still, even though many of these findings are quite solid, they leave out the emotional, social, and
speculative character of ACG merchandise consumption.
With growing interest in the subject, the literature is still somewhat limited. Many investigations
deal with the relation between payment and spending on general goods. Very little attention has been
given to the effect of such methods upon mental accounting in Goods consumption. Most of the
studies treat mental accounting as something unchanging or rigid. They neglect that in ACG
consumption, people's minds are highly emotional and elastic. With regard to Goods consumption,
spending tends to be associated with emotional accounts. The budget ceiling applicable here is far
greater than for standard spending accounts. Also, little is known about how credit instruments may
relate to restricted releases or to second-hand speculation in general. The question of whether credit
promotes misleading mental accounts — and thus enables overspending under an illusion of
affordability — has not been sufficiently examined. Among practical measures aimed at improving
digital literacy for ACG consumers, few have been discussed in the literature. A focus on broad
retail contexts has tended to ignore niche cultural practices, with consequent limitations on the
usefulness of findings for the ACG sector.
1.3. Theoretical framework and research design
The present work treats Bilibili Membership Mall as a case, and develops a model that relates
payment methods, mental accounting (or its breakdown), irrational expenditure, and post-purchase
results. Using both secondary material and Internet discussion, it outlines the main dangers involved
and offers ideas for governance of sustainable ACG consumption.

2. Case background: Bilibili Membership Mall
Bilibili Membership Mall has become one of the main platforms for licensed ACG merchandise in
China, starting in 2017. As of 2023, it has sold IP derivatives totaling CNY 510 million, and its
platform GMV is close to USD 300 million by 2025 [1]. The Mall accepts not only cash (in the form
of WeChat or Alipay) but also credit-linked items — Huabei or Baitiao — with ~45%of transactions
being credit or installment - based. One typical example is the 2023 limited - edition Keqing Qipao
badge (Genshin Impact), now priced at CNY 59 and sold out in three seconds. On Xianyu, resale
values have gone beyond CNY 2, 000 (≈ 34 ×), with many buyers using Huabei installments. Such
speculation has normalized credit over – reliance, as shown by expressions such as "eating Goods
debt." Secondary-market fluidity helps the primary market but may encourage wild speculation.

3. Mechanisms and effects of payment methods on mental accounting
3.1. Positive externalities of digital payment
3.1.1. Lowering transaction barriers and expanding access
Mobile payments and credit instruments have considerably reduced the payment barrier for Goods
consumption. More young consumers with limited disposable income can now take part in IP culture
consumption. In the cash era, buying a high-priced luxury good required paying a large amount of
cash right away. This caused severe pain in the paying. Many fans gave up because they did not
have enough money. Mobile payments' one-click function and credit payment's buy now, pay later
feature reduce payment pain to a minimum. This facilitates the allocation of ACG spending to
discretionary entertainment accounts.
According to research, spending behavior is significantly impacted by digital payments. Digital
payment has a bigger effect than cash payment [3]. The cashless effect indicates that individuals
tend to spend more when using digital payment. Compared with cash, digital payment reduces the
mental anguish of paying [7]. In Goods consumption, this effect is amplified by consumers'
emotional attachment to ACG characters. Platform data shows that after Huabei installments were
introduced, average order value climbed by roughly 35%. Products priced above 200 yuan grew the
most [8]. This payment convenience not only expands the consumer base for IP goods. It also gives

content creators and rights holders a more stable income. This helps ACG culture grow and become

3.1.2. Emotional integration and consumption immersion
A frictionless mobile payment system encourages immersion and emotional involvement on the part
of the consumer. It leaves their emotional condition undisturbed. From the standpoint of mental
accounting, separation of payment from consumption—whether by gift or prepaid card—helps to
isolate spending in a prepaid account. That isolation reduces sensitivity to individual transactions.
Mobile payment features such as "password - free payment" and "print - and - pay" make it hard to
distinguish between payment and consumption mentally. As a result, Goods buying tends to be
experienced as emotional, not as mere money spending.
New research demonstrates that mobile payment may create a pleasure of paying effect. Its
smooth and easy procedure can trigger positive feelings during buying. EEG studies give neural
evidence that mobile payment not only reduces the pain of paying but also generates the pleasure of
payment. This "emotionalization" of mental accounting makes consumption smoother. It increases
consumer loyalty to IPs and their sense of belonging to the community. It also gives emotional
power to the deeper development of ACG culture. Some research on AI payment systems also
demonstrates this effect among Gen Z [10].
3.1.3. Liquidity enhancement and investment mental accounting
The convenience of mobile and credit payments also helps the second-hand goods market grow. On
platforms like Xianyu, customers can use Alipay's escrow service to complete high-value resales
fast. This low-friction experience leads some consumers to put Goods into an investment collection
mind account instead of a pure consumption spending account.
Xianyu's 2023 report demonstrates that second-hand ACG goods transaction volume increased by
250%year over year [1]. The second-hand market makes rarity evident and gives liquidity to high-
value collectibles. This can strengthen primary demand [1]. About 30%of sellers said they bought
Goods hoping to resell them for a profit. This "investmentization" of mental accounting carries
speculative risk. But it also gives Goods liquidity. Consumers can sell when they need money. This
reduces the danger of eating dust (buying and not using). It also gives the IP goods market a price
discovery mechanism.
3.2. Systemic risks and behavioral distortions
3.2.1. Mental accounting imbalance and over-indebtedness
The cashless nature of mobile payment and the delayed pain of credit payment greatly disturb
consumers' mental accounting balance. This leads to self - control failure and too much debt. In
mental accounting theory, consumers frequently set budget limits for each account. But when the
pain of paying is substantially reduced, this mental budget controller runs functioning [11]. Research
indicates that mobile payment use increases delinquency in credit cards, mortgages, and student
loans [8]. Shah et al. found that current and future payment methods significantly affect spending
behavior. Digital payment has a bigger effect than cash payment [3]. Also, frictionless payment adds
a new mind account that did not exist before. It makes people feel they have more free money than
they actually do.

With respect to Goods consumption, these effects are stronger. Survey evidence tells us that about
62%of ACG consumers have spent money on Goods not originally planned due to easy payment.
Some 28%have had "eating Goods debt". Of greater concern is the fact that tools such as Huabei
employ interest - free installments to obscure the boundary between now and later debt. That
boundary is weakened when one uses future earnings to fund present pleasures. This gives rise to a
"debt spiral" [8]. Such arrangements take advantage of consumers' inability to control themselves,
calling for both regulation and education.
3.2.2. Speculative bubbles and payment instrument misuse
Consumer mental accounting is disturbed by the interplay of payment forms with second - hand
speculation. Such disturbances lead to speculative bubbles. On sites such as Xianyu, one sees
scalpers using Huabei cash - out options for limited Goods. They sell them—often many times—
above the initial price. That form of leverage converts ordinary cultural goods into objects of
financial speculation.
Research on speculative bubbles in collectible markets has found similar patterns. The scarcity of
some collectibles often causes second-hand prices to rise [4]. Some consumers have paid over
80,000 yuan for a team-badge-themed mahjong set on a second - hand trading site. Driven by
FOMO, consumers use credit payments to buy at inflated prices. They put the spending into an
investment mind account. But they ignore the real cultural value. Second-hand prices fall sharply
when the bubble bursts, for example, when IP popularity declines. Many high-priced buyers face big
losses. Also, payment tool misuse leads to a vicious borrow-to-repay cycle. Some consumers, trying
to repay old debt, use new credit lines to buy more goods, hoping to recover losses. In the end, they
fall into a financial crisis [8]. This market disorder under distorted mental accounting not only
damages consumer rights. It also disrupts the healthy development of the IP goods market.
3.2.3. Social comparison and community-driven overspending
In Goods communities, social pressure and community comparison are exacerbated by painless
payments. This results in mental accounting's socialization and hijacking. Residents of good
communities are viewed as leaders and are respectful of the community. They own rare goods, such
as hot-selling items or sea-view items. People who own common goods are pushed to their
advantage.
Research on fan consumption has found that FOMO is the strongest driver of impulse buying [4].
Under this social pressure, consumers keep using mobile payment to buy high-priced goods to keep
"face." They put spending into a "social investment" mindset, even when it is beyond their financial
means. Survey data show that about 40% of Goods community consumers have bought Goods
beyond their budget because they did not want to be looked down on. This "socialization" hijacking
of mental accounting not only increases financial pressure. It also leads to anxiety, depression, and
other mental health problems. It seriously harms the well-being of young consumers.
4. Governance strategies for sustainable ACG consumption
4.1. Platform-level payment interventions and quota mechanisms
This paper proposes a three-part payment intervention system: platform, payment institution, and
user, in order to address mental account imbalance and excessive debt. First, secondary-market

platforms and credit providers should establish a mandatory cooling-off period for ACG
merchandise purchases.
The rational consumption reminder should be demonstrated by force when a user's monthly
goods spending exceeds a predetermined limit (eg, 2,000 yuan). The payment should be delayed by
24 hours. Research indicates that making credit card transactions more visible helps people spend
less [5]. Second, payment platforms should allow users to set mental account budgets for different
spending categories. When Goods spending gets close to the budget limit, the system should send a
warning and limit credit payment use. Using smartphones to help with mental accounting can help
consumers spend less [12]. Third, for minors and university students, credit payment functions like
Huabei and Baitiao should be turned off by default. Or they should have per-transaction or daily
restrictions. This can stop them from falling into debt traps due to weak self-control. Shah et al.
found that digital financial literacy significantly and positively moderates individual spending. This
means education and institutional protection work together [13]. Fourth, platforms should provide a
"consumption bill visualization" feature. It should show users Goods spending as a share of monthly
income in chart form. This helps users rebuild the boundaries of their mental accounts.
4.2. Regulation of secondary markets and speculative activities
To deal with second-hand speculation and payment tool misuse, this paper proposes a multi -
pronged approach to stop speculative bubbles.
First, second-hand trading platforms should set up a Goods transaction price monitoring
mechanism. Items with a premium over 500%of the original price should be automatically marked
as high - risk speculative items. The use of credit payment methods like Huabei to buy them should
be limited. The second-hand market's positive role in enhancing primary demand should be kept.
But it should not become a speculative casino. Second, platforms should stop scalpers from using
automated scripts to buy in bulk. They should use real-name purchase limits to make sure Goods go
to real fans. Third, when it comes to consumer financial protection, financial regulators ought to
incorporate good speculation. Payment institutions ought to be prohibited from offering installment
payments or leveraged financing for second-hand speculation. This prevents credit tool misuse.
Fourth, a self-regulatory body for the IP derivative second-hand market should be established by
industry associations. It ought to prohibit harmful speculation. Additionally, it ought to frequently
issue market risk warnings. Customers should also make a routine statement about the importance of
goods in a cultural consumption mind account rather than an investment mind account.
4.3. Digital financial literacy and community-based guidance
To deal with irrational spending from community comparison and social pressure, this paper
proposes a school-family platform digital financial literacy education system. First, universities and
secondary schools should include digital financial literacy in general education courses. They should
teach basic concepts like mental accounting and delayed gratification. This helps students see the
traps of painless payment. Research has proven that enhancing digital financial literacy can
encourage responsible spending through digital payment [14]. Second, parents should pay attention
to their children's consumption of goods. They should guide them to develop healthy consumption
values. They should avoid using Goods as the only way of emotional compensation [15]. The
emotional attachment consumers feel toward ACG characters should be recognized. But it should
not lead to financial harm. Third, ACG platforms such as Bilibili have an obligation to guide
communities. They may issue formal guides about rational spending practices. They may bring in

5. Conclusion
5.1. Key findings
This study uses Goods consumption on the Bilibili Membership Mall as a case. It deeply explores
how payment methods affect mental accounting in Goods consumption. Mobile and credit payments
facilitate ACG consumption by reducing payment pain, enhancing immersion, and supporting
secondary-market liquidity. However, they also cause deeper problems: mental account imbalance
and too much debt, second-hand speculation and payment tool misuse, and irrational spending
driven by community comparison. These problems seriously threaten the financial health and
psychological well - being of young consumers.
Through mental accounting, this study demonstrates that digital payment has a significant impact
on spending behavior. A bank account can move money from a secure savings account to a spending
budget by moving it to a payment app. This reduces the mental barrier to spending. These impacts
are exacerbated by emotional attachment, community dynamics, and second-hand speculation in the
consumption of goods. Beyond conventional mental accounting frameworks, the emotionalization
and socialization of mental accounting in ACG consumption are a unique phenomenon.
5.2. Research significance
The present study has considerable value both theoretically and practically. From a theoretical point
of view, it applies mental accounting ideas to the new niche of ACG Goods consumption. It gives an
account of how mobile or credit-based payment eases the formation of mental account boundaries
(i.e., pain of paying). This enlarges the conceptual scope of studies on digital behavior.
Practically, the study provides decision-making evidence for ACG platforms to improve payment
design and prevent user over indebtedness. It gives direction for financial regulators on how to
regulate credit payment instruments in cultural consumption. It also gives social value by
encouraging rational youth consumption and healthy growth of the ACG cultural business. The
tactics suggested platform payment intervention, second-hand market regulation, and digital
financial literacy education offer actionable paths for many stakeholders.
5.3. Limitations and future research
The present work is mainly based on secondary data and public case analysis—not on direct
consumer surveys. Future investigations may profit from questionnaires, controlled experiments, or
physiological methods such as EEG for an examination of how cash or mobile/credit payments
relate to mental accounting and budgeting in ACG consumption. Comparative studies of IP types
(domestic vs. Japanese) and of virtual items (NFTs)—whether in the metaverse—are certainly worth
pursuing. Given the strong moderating role of digital literacy with respect to spending/payment
linkages, it is timely to design and test literacy-specific remedies. Finally, with AI-driven
