Abstract
Background and Aim:  The global market f or lab-grown diamonds (LGDs) is expanding, yet the
drivers of consumer acceptance in key markets like China remain underexplored. This study
investigates the factors influencing Shanghai consumers' purchasing behavior, with a specific focus
on the mediating role of product differentiation.
Materials and Methods: Drawing on the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) and Social Value
theory, a conceptual model was developed. Data were collected via an online questionnaire
from 561 consumers in Shanghai who had pur chased LGDs. Structural Equation Modeling (SEM)
was employed to test the hypothesized relationships and mediation effects.
Results: The results confirm that Perceived Ease of Use (β=0.28, p<0.001), Perceived Usefulness
(β=0.31, p<0.001), and Social Value ( β=0.40, p<0.001) significantly influence Purchase Behavior.
Crucially, Product Differentiation was found to partially mediate these relationships, with a
significant indirect effect ( β=0.12, p<0.01). Demographic analysis revealed that h igher education
levels correlated with lower perceived usefulness, suggesting a more critical consumer segment.
Conclusion: This study empirically validates a TAM -based model enhanced with social value,
demonstrating that product differentiation is a pivot al mechanism through  which cognitive and
social perceptions translate into purchase behavior. The findings offer actionable insights for LGD
marketers in Shanghai, emphasizing the need for targeted communication that highlights ethical,
environmental, and innovative product differentiators to appeal to a diverse consumer base.
Keywords: Lab-grown Diamond; Consumer Behavior; Product Differentiation; Structural Equation
Modeling; Technology Acceptance Model; Shanghai Market

Objectives
 Based on the above research issues, the purpose of this study is as follows:
  (1) Analysis of strategies for fostering the behavioral intention of diamonds.
  (2) Analyze the influence of social value theory on the cultivation of the behavioral
intention of diamonds.
  (3) Analyze the impact of the technology acceptance model on the behavioral intention of
cultivated diamonds.

Literature review
  Technology Acceptance Model (TAM): Originally developed to understand technology
adoption, TAM is highly applicable to LGDs, which are a product of technological innovation. We
propose that Perceived Usefulness (PU), defined as the consumer's belief that using an LGD will
enhance their social status or self -expression, and Perceived Ease of Use (PEOU), the degree to
which purchasing and owning an LGD is free from effort (e.g., easy to buy online, easy to justify),
are fundamental antecedents of purchase intention (Li, 2022).
  Social Value Theory: Consumption is increasingly an expression of social and environmental
values. Social Value (SV) in this context refers to the perceived utility derived from the product's
ability to enhance social self-concept through environmentally friendly and ethical choices (Zhang
& Bunchapattanasakda, 2025). LGDs, with their conflict -free and lower environmental impact
profile, are potent carriers of social value.
  Product Differentiation (PD) and its Mediating Role: In competitive markets, produ ct
differentiation is a key strategy. For LGDs, differentiation is not based on physical scarcity but on
attributes like sustainability, innovat ion, and cost -effectiveness. We integrate Signaling Theory
here, suggesting that these differentiated attributes serve as signals that reduce perceived risk and
increase value (Pellegrino, as discussed in the original manuscript). We hypothesize that posit ive
perceptions (PEOU, PU) and social value (SV) strengthen the perception of LGDs as a distinctly
superior category (PD), which in turn directly influences Purchase Behavior (PB). Thus, PD acts as
a crucial mediator.
  H1: Perceived Ease of Use (PEOU) has a positive effect on Purchase Behavior (PB).
  H2: Perceived Usefulness (PU) has a positive effect on Purchase Behavior (PB).
  H3: Social Value (SV) has a positive effect on Purchase Behavior (PB).
  H4: Product Differentiation (PD) has a positive effect on Purchase Behavior (PB).
  H5: Product Differentiation (PD) mediates the relationship between (a) PEOU, (b) PU, (c) SV,
and Purchase Behavior (PB).

Methodology
   Research Design and Structural Equation Modeling Justification
 This study employs a cross-sectional survey design. Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) was
selected as the primary analytical technique because it allows for the simultaneous estimation
of multiple interrelated dependence relationships and the inclusion of latent constructs that are
not directly observable (e.g., Social Value, Perceived Usefulness). This is ideal for testing our
complex model with both direct and mediating effects in a single, comprehensive analysis
(Cheung et al., as in the original).
  Questionnaire Design and Measurement Constructs
 The questionnaire used a 5 -point Likert scale. Constructs were adapted from established
scales to fit the LGD context. For example, items for PEOU and PU were adapted from classic
TAM scales (Li, 2022), while Social Value items were drawn from scales measuring sustainable
consumption behavior (Zhang & Bunchapattanasakda, 2025). A pre-test (n=28) ensured clarity and
content validity. The final instrument measured six constructs: PEOU, PU, SV, PD, Consumer
Behavioral Intention (CBI), and Purchase Behavior (PB).
  Data Collection and Sampling
 Data were collected via online surveys distributed through social media and professional
networks in Shanghai, using a convenience and snowball sampling approach. We acknowle dge
that this non-probability sampling method limits the generalizability of our findings beyond the
specific sample. However, it was deemed appropriate for this exploratory study to reach a
targeted population of LGD purchasers. A total of 561 valid respo nses were obtained from 584
distributed.
  Common Method Bias (CMB) Assessment
  To address the potential for Common Method Bias, both procedural and statistical remedies
were employed. Procedurally, we ensured respondent anonymity and counter-balanced question
order. Statistically, Harman's single-factor test was conducted. The results showed that the first
factor explained only 33.54% of the variance, well below the 50% threshold, indicating that CMB
is not a pervasive issue in our data.

3. Relationship Between Demographics and Model Constructs
Using Analysis of Variance (ANOVA), the study investigated how demographic characteristics,
including gender, age,  education level, and personal spending capacity, influence  key latent
variables within the model. The results indicated that Consumer Perception was significantly
shaped by both education level and gender, suggesting differing levels of awareness or attitudes
based on these factors. Product Differentiation was notably influenced by education and personal
spending capacity, implying that individuals with higher educational backgrounds and greater
financial resources may perceive more nuanced product attributes. Social Value was significantly
associated with gender, age, and spending levels, reflecting that these demographics impact how
individuals internalize or respond to societal and environmental values. Lastly, Purchase Intention
was significantly affected by both gender and income level, indicating that economic standing
and gender-based preferences play critical roles in shaping consumer willingness to engage in
actual buying behavior. These findings underscore the importance of considering  demographic
diversity when analyzing consumer decision-making processes.
These findings suggest that consumer perceptions and behavioral inclinations toward lab -
grown diamonds vary across demographic segments. Notably, higher education levels
corresponded to lower perceived usefulness, possibly due to critical evaluation of technological

4. Correlation Analysis
To assess the interrelations among the study variables, Pearson correlation analysis was
conducted using SPSS 28.0. The constructs analyzed included Consumer Perception, Social Value,
Product Differentiation, Consumer Behavioral Intention, and Actual Purchase Behavior.
Results indicated statistically significant and po sitive corre lations among all major
constructs. For instance, the correlation between Consumer Behavioral Intention and Purchase
Behavior was 0.845 (p < 0.001), and between Product Differentiation and Purchase Behavior was
0.685 (p < 0.001). This suggests that stronge r consumer intention and clearer product
differentiation are associated with higher purchasing likelihood. As shown in Table 13.

5. Structural Equation Modeling
  5.1 Model Construction and Hypotheses
  The Structural Equation Modeling framework was employed to investigate the
hypothesized relationships among five latent co nstructs: Consumer Perception, Social  Value,
Product Differentiation, Consumer Behavioral Intention, and Purchase Behavior. This model
comprises both measurement and structural components. The measurement model assesses the
validity and reliability of the observed variables in representing their respective latent constructs,
ensuring accurate conceptual representation. Meanwhile, the structural model examines the
directional and causal relationships among the latent variables, thereby offering insights into how
consumer perception and social v alue influence product differentiation and ultimately affect
behavioral intention and purchase behavior. As shown in Figure 1.

 5.2 Model Fit Evaluation
 The goodness-of-fit of the revised Structural Equation Model was assessed using multiple
indices, demonstrating excellent model adequacy across all dimensions. For absolute fit, the Root
Mean Square Error of Approximation (RMSEA) was 0.024, with a Goodness -of-Fit Index (GFI) of
0.964 and an Adjus ted Goodness -of-Fit Index (AGFI) of 0.949, indicating a strong alignment
between the hypothesized model and the observed data. Incremental fit indices further
supported this conclusion, with a Comparative Fit Index (CFI) of 0.985 and a Tucker -Lewis Index
(TLI) of 0.955, both well above the recommended threshold of 0.90. In terms of parsimonious fit,
the Chi-square to degrees of freedom ratio (CMIN/DF) was 1.026, suggesting minimal discrepancy
between the model and the sample data. Additionally, the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) value
of 437.46 was substantially lower than that of the independence model, further confirming the
revised model's superiority and parsimony.
 5.3 Path Coefficients and Hypothesis Testing
 The results of the structural equation model confirm that all hypothesized relationships
are statistically significant at the p < 0.001 level, supporting the proposed theoretical framework.
Specifically, Consumer Perception exhibited a strong positive in fluence on Purchase Behavior (β
= 0.375), indicating that consumers' attitudes and awareness toward lab -grown diamonds
significantly shape their purchasing actions. Social Value had an even stronger effect ( β= 0.395),
suggesting that consumers' concern for  societal and environmental va lues is a key driver of
sustainable purchase behavior. Product Differentiation also significantly influenced Purchase
Behavior ( β = 0.344), highlighting the importance of perceived uniqueness and innovation in
consumers' evaluations. Additionally, Consumer Behavioral Intention had a statistically significant,
though relatively smaller, effect on Purchase Behavior (β = 0.162).
 Together, these results validate the hypothesized model and establish the integral role
of all four co nstructs in shaping consumer p urchasing behavior in the context of lab -grown
diamonds.

 6. Mediation Effect of Product Differentiation
To further examine the model structure, the study explored the mediating effect of Product
Differentiation using a three-step mediation analysis. The total effect of Consumer Perception on
Purchase Behavior was found to be significant (β = 0.63, p < 0.001), confirming a direct influence.
However, when Product Differentiation was introduced as a mediating variable, the direct effect
of Consumer Perception on Purchase Behavior decreased to β = 0.375, while the indirect effect
through Product Differentiation was statistically significant. Specifically, the path from Consumer

Discussion
 This research has found that product differentiation affects consumer behavior in the lab-
grown diamond market by mediating the effect of perception on purchase behavior. The SEM
results demonstrate that ease of use and usefulness affect behavior, whereas social value has a
significant and positive impact on attitude. These findings support Pellegrino’s (2018) theory that
the differentiation of products allows for solving the problem of consumer heterogeneity in
oligopoly markets by differentiating value and uniqueness.
 Furthermore, product differentiation was shown  to have an indirect impact on consumer
behavior in this study, which it mediated via the relationships between technological and social
variables and behavioral constructs. This suggests a mediating function that is similar to
omnichannel decision -making behavior, which Mishra et al. (2020) state occurs due to a
complicated physical and psychological flow of information through which a consumer makes a
purchasing decision. Ran et al. (2021) also found that the provision of transparent and trustworthy

 Product differentiation in the lab -grown diamond market is defined by characteristics
including clarity, a carbon-neutral footprint, and ethical sourcing, among others. As a result, it not
only affects consumer behavior by mediating the relationship between constructs but also
strengthens the positive effect of favorable perceptions of technology and social values. These
results also support Lopes et al.’s (2019) argument that environmental and social value serve as
psychological mediators only if expressed by product characteristics.
 The validity of the measurement model is supported by its high AVE value for all constructs
and acceptable discriminant validity, as well as the statistically significant Bartlett’s Test of
Sphericity. The SEM process is similar to that which Cheung et al. (2017) detail, in which reliability
and discriminant validity must be established before inter preting p ath coefficients, and the
correlations between demographic characteristics and the perceived value that was found in this
study suggest that individual characteristics cause variation in consumer perception. This result is
in agreement with the su ggestion of Redman and Wiek (2015), who stated that sustainability is
determined by “context-dependent cognitive competencies”.
 Thus, this study’s finding that product differentiation has a significant effect on consumer
behavior in the lab-grown diamond market, serving as a mediator of the relationship between the
technology acceptance model’s core variables and the consumer’s behavioral intention, may be
used as the basis for strategies. With these in mind, firms producing lab -grown diamonds could
use product differentiation to establish a position in the highly competitive market in which they
operate. At the same time, the diffusion of innovation, in which the technology acceptance model
is involved, is not enough to increase purchase likelihood and frequency.
 However, we found that product differentiation had a positive impact on the consumer
behavior related to the purchase of lab -grown diamonds. The SEM also showed that there is a
significant positive impact on behavioral intention from the two most important  factors of TAM,
i.e., perceived ease of use and perceived usefulness, with the added factor of social value
significantly affecting individual consumer attitude. This builds on Pellegrino’s (2018) finding that
product differentiation in oligopoly allows f irms to eff ectively respond to heterogeneous
consumer preferences by raising product uniqueness and value.
 In our study, however, product differentiation had a significant direct effect on consumer
behavior, but it also had a full mediating effect on the path of consumer behavior. This is in line
with Mishra et al. (2020) 's finding that the omnichannel decision-making process involves buyers
overcoming complex information and psychological barriers to reach a purchase decision. In

Conclusion
  This study provides robust empirical evidence that product differentiation is not merely an
outcome of marketing but a pivotal psychological mediator in the consumer decision -making
process for LGDs in Shanghai. By integrating TAM with Social Value theory and contextualizing the
findings within Shanghai's dynamic market, we offer a nuanced understanding that extends existing
literature.
  The main call to action for the LGD industry is clear: success hinges on the ability to
strategically articulate and communicate a diffe rentiated product identity that aligns with the
cognitive and social values of target consumers. Future research should explore cross -cultural
comparisons with other major markets, such as Thailand or Germany (Schüler & Tapachai, 2025),

Recommendation
  1. Emphasize Distinctive Attributes in Marketing . Companies should strategically highlight
the unique attributes of lab -grown diamonds, such as ethical sourcing, environmental
sustainability, and cost -effectiveness. Marketing campaigns should focus on differentiators that
resonate emotionally with consumers, particularly eco-conscious and value-driven buyers.
  2. Develop Strong Brand Identity Around Differentiation . Firms should build brand stories
that clearly distinguish lab -grown diamonds from mined diamonds. Educational content about
the production process, benefits, and certifications of lab-grown diamonds can increase consumer
trust and perceived value.
