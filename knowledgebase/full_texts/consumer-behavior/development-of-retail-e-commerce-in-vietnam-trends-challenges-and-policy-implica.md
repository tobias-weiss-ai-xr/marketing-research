Abstract
Vietnam's retail e-commerce sector has emerged as a cornerstone of the nation's digital economy,
experiencing exponential growth amid rapid technological adoption and shifting consumer
behaviors. As of 2025, the market is projected to surpass $45 billion in cross-border transactions
alone, contributing significantly to the overall digital economy's 20%+ annual growth rate. This
paper provides a comprehensive analysis of the development of retail e -commerce in Vietnam,
exploring key trends such as the domina nce of social and mobile commerce, AI -driven
personalization, and cross-border expansion. It also delves into persistent challenges, including
regulatory compliance under the new Draft E -Commerce Law, logistical bottlenecks,
cybersecurity threats, and regional inequalities. Drawing on secondary data from sources like the
Vietnam E-Commerce White Book (2022 -2025 updates) and primary survey insights, the study
offers policy implications for government, businesses, and consumers. Recommendations
emphasize stre ngthening legal frameworks, investing in infrastructure, and fostering SME
inclusion to ensure sustainable, inclusive growth. This research underscores Vietnam's potential
as a Southeast Asian e -commerce leader while highlighting pathways to mitigate risks  in an
increasingly competitive landscape.

Keywords: Retail e -commerce, Vietnam, Social commerce, Cross -border trade, Digital policy,
Sustainable growth

1. Introduction
The advent of the digital era has revolutionized global commerce, with retail e -commerce
serving as a pivotal driver of economic transformation. Worldwide, online retail sales reached
approximately $5.8 trillion in 2024, projected to exceed $8 trillion by 2027, according to
UNCTAD's Digital Economy Report (2024). In Southeast Asia, Vietnam stands out as a high -
growth market, where e -commerce has not only accelerated post -COVID recovery but also
become integral to the national GDP. By mid-2025, Vietnam's e-commerce sector is valued at over
$25 billion, with retail e-commerce accounting for nearly 12% of total retail sales—surpassing the
government's 2021-2025 target of 10% (Ministry of Industry and Trade, 2025). This surge is fueled
by a youthful population ( over 60% under 35), widespread smartphone penetration (over 80
million users), and internet access rates exceeding 77% nationwide.
Historically, Vietnam's e -commerce journey began modestly in the early 2010s, with
platforms like Shopee and Lazada entering the market around 2015. The COVID -19 pandemic
acted as a catalyst, boosting online adoption from 53% of internet users in 2019 to 74.8% by 2022,
a trend that has sustained into 2025 (Vietnam E-Commerce and Digital Economy Agency, 2025).

2. Methodology
This study adopts a mixed -methods framework to ensure comprehensive coverage.
Secondary data forms the backbone, sourced from authoritative reports including UNCTAD
(2024), Vietnam E -Commerce White Book (2022 -2025 editions), and ASEAN Briefing
publications. These provide quantitative metrics on market size, growth rates, and trends.
Qualitative insights draw from academic journals (e.g., Journal of Business Research) and policy
documents like the National E-Commerce Development Program 2021-2025.
Primary data was collected via an online survey in early 2025, targeting 150 respondents
aged 18-45 who had shopped online in the past year. Using convenience sampling through email
and social media, the questionnaire covered demographics, purchase habits,  satisfaction levels,
and future expectations. Questions employed Likert scales for experiences (e.g., "Rate
convenience of mobile shopping: 1 -5") and multiple -choice for categories (e.g., top products
bought). Responses yielded a 68% completion rate (102 valid), analyzed descriptively in Excel for
frequencies, means, and cross -tabulations. Comparative analysis juxtaposed Vietnam's data
against global benchmarks, with expert consultations from three e -commerce practitioners
validating interpretations.
Ethical considerations included anonymity and informed consent. Limitations —such as
sample bias toward urban youth —are mitigated by triangulating with secondary sources. This
approach ensures reliability while capturing 2025's dynamic landscape.

3. Result
3.1. Trends in Retail E-Commerce Development
Vietnam's retail e-commerce in 2025 is characterized by vibrant, multifaceted trends that
underscore its maturation. Market projections indicate a total value of $30 -35 billion, with retail
B2C segments growing at 25% year -over-year, driven by domestic con sumption and export -
oriented platforms (JUSDA Global, 2025). Social commerce has exploded, accounting for 40% of
transactions; platforms like Facebook Marketplace and TikTok Shop enable seamless buying via
livestreams, appealing to Gen Z (who represent 30%  of shoppers and prioritize interactive,
influencer-led experiences) (NSQ Vietnam, 2025). For example, 57% of consumers now discover
products through social media, up from 39% in 2022.
Mobile commerce remains dominant, with 70% of sales via apps—bolstered by 5G rollout
covering 90% of urban areas. Digital payments have normalized, with non -cash transactions at
85%, facilitated by wallets like Momo and ZaloPay (WeAreSocial, 2025). Cross -border e -

3.2. Challenges Facing Retail E-Commerce
Amidst optimism, Vietnam's e -commerce faces entrenched challenges that could impede
long-term viability. Regulatory uncertainty looms large with the Draft E -Commerce Law (2025),
which imposes stricter obligations on platforms for data protection, counterfe it prevention, and
cross-border compliance. Non -adherence risks fines up to 10% of revenue, deterring SMEs
(Vietnam Briefing, 2025). Cybersecurity threats are acute: phishing and data breaches affected
15% of users in 2024, eroding trust—40% of survey respondents cited privacy fears as a deterrent.
Logistical hurdles persist, with delivery times averaging 3 -5 days in rural areas versus 1
day urban, inflating costs to 15-20% of orders. The urban-rural digital divide exacerbates this: only
40% rural penetration versus 90% urban, limiting market inclusi vity (Aquis Capital, 2025).
Competition from global players like Amazon and Alibaba intensifies pressure on local firms;
SMEs, comprising 70% of sellers, struggle with only 26% having dedicated e-commerce staff (EBI
Report, 2025).
Environmental concerns are rising: e -commerce packaging waste surged 30% in 2024,
prompting calls for "green logistics." Skill gaps in digital literacy affect 50% of the workforce,
while economic factors like inflation (4 -5% in 2025) squeeze margins. Surve y data reveals 35%
dissatisfaction from poor service, including returns (20% complication rate). Globally, parallels in
China's supply chain strains post -2023 underscore the need for Vietnam to invest in resilient
infrastructure.
These challenges, if unaddressed, risk stalling growth; however, they also present
opportunities for innovation.

4. Policy Implications
Navigating these dynamics demands robust, multi -stakeholder policies. For government,
the Draft E -Commerce Law should be finalized by late 2025, balancing innovation with
protections - e.g., mandatory AI audits for personalization and subsidies for rural broadband
(target: 100% coverage by 2030). The National Program 2021 -2025 extension could allocate $2
billion for SME digital training and green incentives, like tax breaks for electric delivery fleets
(DNP Law, 2025). Enhancing FDI through streamlined visas for tech talent would attract $20
billion in e-commerce investments.
Businesses must prioritize compliance and agility: Adopt omnichannel tools (e.g., Lazada's
AI analytics) and partner for logistics (e.g., with Viettel Post). SMEs should leverage government
grants for app development, focusing on social commerce to reach G en Z. Consumers require
empowerment via awareness campaigns on secure shopping and rights under the new law —e.g.,
7-day returns mandates.
Internationally, Vietnam can draw from Singapore's data sovereignty model and China's
cross-border hubs. Collaborative frameworks, like ASEAN Digital Economy Framework, could
harmonize standards. These policies, if implemented, could elevate e -commerce's GDP share to
15% by 2030, fostering equitable prosperity.

5. Conclusion
Vietnam's retail e-commerce in 2025 embodies a narrative of triumph and tenacity. Trends
like social-mobile integration and AI personalization herald a vibrant future, with $35 billion in
retail value signaling global competitiveness. Yet, challenges from regulation to logistics demand
urgent action. By enacting forward -thinking policies —refining laws, bridging divides, and
promoting sustainability  - stakeholders can unlock inclusive growth. This study illuminates
