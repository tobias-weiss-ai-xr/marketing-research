This	study	examines	the	effects	of	AI	transparency	and	privacy	control	on	purchase
intention	through	consumer	trust	in	omnichannel	retail.	Methods:	An	explanatory
quantitative	design	was	applied	to	survey	data	from	100	omnichannel	consumers
in	 Yogyakarta	 City	 selected	 through	 purposive	 sampling.	 The	 reflective
measurement	 and	 structural	 models	 were	 evaluated	 using	 partial	 least	 squares
structural	equation	modeling	in	SmartPLS	4.	Findings:	AI	transparency	and	privacy
control	 positively	 influence	 consumer	 trust	 and	 purchase	 intention.	 Consumer
trust	 also	 positively	 affects	 purchase	 intention	 and	 partially	 mediates	 both
antecedent	relationships.	The	model	explains	58.8%	of	the	variance	in	consumer
trust	 and	 64.3%	 of	 the	 variance	 in	 purchase	 intention.	 Research	 Implications:
Retailers	 should	 provide	 understandable	 explanations	 of	 AI -based
recommendations	 and	 meaningful	 controls	 for	 consent,	 personalization
preferences,	 and	 data	 withdrawal.	 These	 mechanisms	 can	 reduce	 uncertainty,
strengthen	 trust,	 and	 support	 purchase	 intention	 across	 integrated	 channels.
Originality:	 This	 study	 jointly	 positions	 AI	 transparency	 and	 privacy	 control	 as
ethical	 antecedents	 of	 consumer	 trust	 and	 tests	 their	 direct	 and	 trust-mediated
effects	on	purchase	intention	in	an	emerging-market	omnichannel	context.

1. INTRODUCTION
Omnichannel	retail	has	developed	as	a	service	model	that	integrates	online	and	offline	channels
to	 create	 a	 smoother,	 more	 consistent,	 and	 more	 personalized	 shopping	 experience.	 In	 this
context,	 purchase	 intention	 is	 understood	 as	 consumers’	 psychological	 tendency	 to	 choose,
evaluate,	 and	 plan	 the	 purchase	 of	 products	 or	 services	 through	 available	 retail	 channels.
Therefore,	 this	 variable	 is	 often	 used	 as	 an	 early	 indicator	 of	 actual	 purchasing	 behavior	 in
digital	 and	 omnichannel	 retail	 environments	 (Shi	 et	 al.,	 2020;	 Mishra	 et	 al.,	 2021).	 Purchase
intention	 is	 not	 only	 influenced	 by	 product	 quality	 and	 price,	 but	 also	 by	 consumers’
experiences	 when	 moving	 across	 applications,	 marketplaces,	 websites,	 social	 media,	 and
physical	stores	in	an	integrated	manner	(Gao	et	al.,	2021;	Verhoef	et	al.,	2021).	Such	integration
enables	 consumers	 to	 search	 for	 information,	 compare	 products,	 receive	 AI -based
recommendations,	 and	 complete	 transactions	 through	 the	 channels	 that	 best	 suit	 their
preferences	(Flavián	et	al.,	2020;	Shi	et	al.,	2020).
This	 phenomenon	 is	 increasingly	 relevant	 in	 Indonesia,	 as	 modern	 retail	 has	 become

purchase	intentions	(Huang	&	Rust,	2021;	Puntoni	et	al.,	2021).
However,	the	use	of	AI	in	personalization,	product	recommendations,	chatbots,	dynamic
pricing,	and	consumer	behavior	analytics	not	only	provides	benefits	but	also	creates	perceptual
risks	for	consumers.	Consumers	may	feel	assisted	when	AI	makes	offers	more	relevant,	but	they
may	also	feel	uncertain	when	they	do	not	understand	how	data	are	collected,	how	algorithms
process	information,	and	why	the	system	produces	certain	recommendations	(Davenport	et	al.,
2020;	 Puntoni	et	 al.,	 2021).	 Issues	 of	 algorithmic	 transparency,	 privacy	 protection,	 and
consumer	trust	 are	 becoming	 increasingly	 important	 because	 automated	 decisions	 may	 be
perceived	 as	 unfair,	 difficult	 to	 explain,	 or	 overly	 invasive	 when	 companies	 fail	 to	 provide
adequate	 explanations	 (Barredo	 Arrieta	 et	 al.,	 2020;	 Larsson	 &	 Heintz,	 2020).	 The	 main
research	 gap	 lies	 in	 the	 limited	 studies	 that	 simultaneously	 examine	 AI	 transparency	 and
privacy	 control	 in	 relation	 to	 purchase	 intention	 through	 consumer	 trust	 in	 the	 context	 of
omnichannel	 retail,	 particularly	 in	 emerging	 digital	 markets	 such	 as	 Indonesia	 (Bleier	 et	 al.,
2020;	Quach	et	al.,	2022).

Privacy	control	refers	to	consumers’	perception	that	they	have	the	ability	to	know,	choose,
limit,	modify,	or	withdraw	consent	regarding	the	collection	and	use	of	their	personal	data	by
retail	companies	(Bleier	et	al.,	2020;	Quach	et	al.,	2022).	In	omnichannel	retail,	privacy	control
becomes	 crucial	 because	 consumer	 data	 can	 be	 collected	 from	 applications,	 search	 history,
physical	 store	 transactions,	 loyalty	 programs,	 location	 data,	 social	 media,	 and	 chatbot
interactions,	which	are	then	integrated	to	form	cross-channel	consumer	profiles	(Verhoef	et	al.,
2021;	Quach	et	al.,	2022).	Consumers	may	accept	personalization	when	the	benefits	obtained
are	perceived	to	outweigh	privacy	risks,	but	such	acceptance	decreases	when	data	collection
feels	 excessive,	 unclear,	 or	 uncontrollable	 (Acquisti	 et	 al.,	 2020;	 Bleier	 et	 al.,	 2020).	 Strong
privacy	control	can	reduce	perceptions	of	intrusion	because	consumers	feel	that	they	have	the
right	 to	 determine	 what	 data	 are	 shared	 and	 how	 such	 data	 are	 used	 in	 AI -based
recommendations,	promotions,	and	services	(Quach	et	al.,	2022;	Puntoni	et	al.,	2021).
Consumer	 trust	 refers	 to	 consumers’	 belief	 that	 the	 company,	 technology,	 and	 retail
channels	used	possess	competence,	integrity,	and	goodwill	in	meeting	consumer	needs	without
exploiting	their	vulnerabilities	(Glikson	&	Woolley,	2020;	Choung	et	al.,	2023).	In	the	context	of
AI,	trust	becomes	an	important	mediating	variable	because	consumers	often	make	decisions
based	on	algorithmic	recommendations	that	they	do	not	fully	understand,	so	trust	helps	reduce
perceived	risk	and	decision	complexity	(Shin,	2021;	Puntoni	et	al.,	2021).	AI	transparency	can
enhance	 consumer	 trust	 because	 clear	 explanations	 make	 consumers	 feel	 that	 the	 system
works	 fairly,	 responsibly,	 and	 non-manipulatively	 (Barredo	 Arrieta	 et	 al.,	 2020;	 Liao	 et	 al.,
2020).	Privacy	control	can	also	strengthen	consumer	trust	because	consumers	who	feel	able	to
manage	 their	 personal	 data	 tend	 to	 perceive	 companies	 as	 more	 ethical,	 safer,	 and	 more
respectful	of	consumer	interests	(Acquisti	et	al.,	2020;	Quach	et	al.,	2022).
The	 development	 of	 omnichannel	 retail	 has	 transformed	 consumer	 behavior,	 as
customers	 now	 move	 dynamically	 across	 physical	 stores,	 mobile	 applications,	 websites,
marketplaces,	social	media,	and	AI-based	services	such	as	chatbots,	product	recommendation
systems,	 dynamic	 pricing,	 and	 personalized	 promotions.	 Previous	 studies	 have	 shown	 that
channel	 integration,	 omnichannel	 customer	 experience,	 and	 consumer	 trust	 play	 important
roles	in	shaping	purchase	intention	(Rahman	et	al.,	2022;	Sombultawee	&	Wattanatorn,	2022).
However,	 existing	 research	 still	 tends	 to	 examine	 omnichannel	 retail	 mainly	 from	 the
perspectives	 of	 service	 integration,	 convenience,	 customer	 experience	 quality,	 and	 general
trust.	Limited	attention	has	been	given	to	how	AI	transparency	influences	consumer	trust	and
purchase	 intention	 in	 retail	 environments	 where	 online	 and	 offline	 channels	 are	integrated.
Studies	 on	 artificial	 intelligence	 in	 marketing	 have	 emphasized	 that	 AI	 can	 provide	 benefits

2. METHOD
This	study	employs	an	explanatory	quantitative	approach	because	it	aims	to	examine	the	causal
relationships	among	AI	Transparency	and	Privacy	Control	as	independent	variables,	Consumer
Trust	as	a	mediating	variable,	and	Purchase	Intention	as	the	dependent	variable	in	the	context
of	 consumer	 behavior	 in	 omnichannel	 retail	 in	 Yogyakarta	 City	 (Sarstedt	 et	 al.,	 2022).	 The
object	of	this	study	is	consumer	behavior	in	omnichannel	retail,	referring	to	consumers	who
have	interacted	with	or	purchased	products	through	more	than	one	integrated	retail	channel,
such	as	marketplaces,	retail	applications,	websites,	social	media,	or	digitally	connected	physical
stores	 (Sombultawee	 &	 Wattanatorn,	 2022).	 The	 population	 of	 this	 study	 consists	 of
omnichannel	retail	consumers	in	Indonesia,	with	a	specific	focus	on	respondents	who	live	or
conduct	 activities	 in	 Yogyakarta	 City,	 while	 the	 sample	 is	 determined	 as	 100	 respondents
because	 this	 number	 is	 considered	 adequate	 for	 a	 simple	 PLS-SEM	 model	 consisting	 of	 four
latent	 constructs	 and	 a	 mediation	 relationship,	 particularly	 when	 the	 study	 is	 predictive	 in
nature	and	uses	consumer	survey	data	(Sarstedt	et	al.,	2022).	The	sampling	technique	used	in
this	 study	 is	 non-probability	 sampling	 with	 a	 purposive	 sampling	 approach,	 in	 which
respondents	 are	 selected	 based	 on	 specific	 criteria,	 namely	 consumers	 who	 have	 used	 or
purchased	products	through	omnichannel	retail	channels	within	the	last	six	months	and	are
familiar	 with	 AI-based	 features	 such	 as	 product	 recommendations,	 chatbots,	 promotional
personalization,	or	personal	data	management	(Cattapan	&	Pongsakornrungsilp,	2022).	Data
are	collected	using	a	structured	questionnaire	and	analyzed	using	SEM-PLS	with	SmartPLS	4,
because	PLS-SEM	is	appropriate	for	examining	predictive	models,	latent	constructs,	mediating
effects,	and	survey	data	that	do	not	necessarily	meet	the	assumption	of	multivariate	normality
(Hair	et	al.,	2021;	Cheah	et	al.,	2024).	The	suitability	of	SEM-PLS	for	this	study	is	also	supported
by	 the	 ability	 of	 SmartPLS	 to	 evaluate	 the	 measurement	 model	 through	 outer	 loading,
Cronbach’s	alpha,	composite	reliability,	AVE,	and	HTMT,	as	well	as	to	evaluate	the	structural

The	 PLS-SEM	 analysis	 results	 indicate	 that	 all	 research	 constructs,	 namely	 AI	 Transparency,
Privacy	Control,	Consumer	Trust,	and	Purchase	Intention,	meet	the	eligibility	criteria	for	the

DISCUSSION
The	 results	 support	 H1	 and	 H4,	 showing	 that	 AI	 Transparency	 plays	 an	 important	 role	 in
shaping	 Consumer	 Trust	 in	 the	 context	 of	 omnichannel	 retail.	 This	 means	 that	 the	 clearer	 a
company	explains	the	use	of	AI,	data	sources,	the	reasons	behind	product	recommendations,
and	the	way	automated	systems	make	decisions,	the	higher	the	level	of	consumer	trust	in	the
retail	service.	This	finding	is	consistent	with	the	literature	on	explainable	AI,	which	states	that
transparency	and	system	explanations	can	reduce	the	perception	of	AI	as	a	“black	box”	and	help
users	understand	the	basis	of	technological	decisions	(Barredo	Arrieta	et	al.,	2020;	Shin,	2021).
In	 omnichannel	 retail,	 transparency	 becomes	 increasingly	 important	 because	 consumers	 do
not	interact	through	only	one	channel,	but	move	across	marketplaces,	applications,	websites,
social	 media,	 chatbots,	 and	 physical	 stores.	 When	 product	 recommendations,	 personalized
promotions,	or	price	offers	appear	without	sufficient	explanation,	consumers	may	perceive	the
system	 as	 manipulative	 or	 overly	 invasive.	 Conversely,	 when	 companies	 explain	 that
recommendations	 are	 provided	 based	 on	 search	 history,	 product	 category	 preferences,	 or
previous	 interactions,	 consumers	 are	 more	 likely	 to	 perceive	 the	 system	 as	 reasonable,
relevant,	and	trustworthy.	This	study	also	strengthens	the	view	that	AI	in	marketing	should	not
only	be	efficient	and	personalized,	but	also	understandable	to	consumers,	so	that	the	benefits
of	personalization	do	not	turn	into	psychological	uncertainty	(Davenport	et	al.,	2020;	Puntoni
et	al.,	2021).	Therefore,	AI	transparency	does	not	merely	function	as	a	technical	element,	but
also	as	an	ethical	mechanism	that	connects	technological	capability	with	consumer	acceptance.
In	 practice,	 retailers	 need	 to	 provide	 simple	 explanations	 such	 as	 “why	 this	 product	 is
recommended,”	“what	data	are	used,”	and	“how	consumers	can	modify	their	personalization
preferences.”	 Such	 explanations	 can	 increase	 consumers’	 sense	 of	 security	 and	 reduce
hesitation	in	the	purchasing	process.	In	addition,	the	significant	effect	of	Consumer	Trust	on
Purchase	Intention	supports	H3,	confirming	that	trust	is	a	central	psychological	mechanism	in
AI-enabled	omnichannel	purchasing	decisions.
The	results	support	H2	and	H5,	demonstrating	that	Privacy	Control	has	a	positive	effect
on	 Consumer	 Trust	 and	 Purchase	 Intention.	 This	 finding	 confirms	 that	 consumers	 are	 more
likely	to	trust	retailers	when	they	feel	they	have	control	over	their	personal	data,	including	the
right	to	choose	what	data	are	shared,	modify	personalization	preferences,	limit	data	use,	and

4. CONCLUSIONS
This	study	concludes	that	AI	Transparency	and	Privacy	Control	positively	influence	Consumer
Trust	and	Purchase	Intention,	while	Consumer	Trust	positively	influences	Purchase	Intention
and	partially	mediates	both	antecedent	relationships.	Thus,	H1,	H2,	H3,	H4,	H5,	H6a,	and	H6b
are	 supported.	 Theoretically,	 the	 study	 integrates	 explainable	 AI,	 privacy	 calculus,	 and
omnichannel	trust	by	demonstrating	that	transparent	and	controllable	AI	practices	operate	as
ethical	foundations	of	consumer	purchasing	decisions.	Practically,	retailers	should	disclose	the
use	and	general	logic	of	AI,	explain	the	data	used	for	recommendations,	and	provide	accessible
consent,	modification,	and	withdrawal	mechanisms.	The	findings	are	limited	by	the	sample	of
100	 purposively	 selected	 respondents,	 the	 single-city	 setting	 of	 Yogyakarta,	 and	 the	 cross-
sectional	design.	Future	research	should	use	larger	probability	samples,	include	second-	and
third-tier	 Indonesian	 cities,	 and	 employ	 longitudinal	 or	 experimental	 designs	 to	 strengthen
causal	inference	and	generalizability.
