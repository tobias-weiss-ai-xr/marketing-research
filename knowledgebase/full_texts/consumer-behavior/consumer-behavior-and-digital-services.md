# Consumer Behavior and Digital Services

Consumer Behaviour and Digital Services: Competence, Rights and
Protection is a timely and thought-provoking exploration of today’s rapidly
evolving digital services landscape, placing the consumer firmly at the heart
of the discussion on digital transformation. Drawing on technological, legal
and behavioural perspectives, and grounded in empirical research, the
authors offer a rich and accessible understanding of digital skills, cybersecu -
rity awareness, consumer rights, and the emerging risks linked to technolo -
gies such as artificial intelligence.
In an age of accelerating digitalisation, the book addresses some of the
most urgent challenges facing consumers, regulators and service providers.
It moves beyond purely technical or business-focused narratives, showing
how people actually experience and navigate increasingly complex digital
environments. By bridging theory with real-world implications, the authors
provide practical, evidence-based insights into strengthening digital literacy,
improving cybersecurity awareness, and enhancing consumer protection
frameworks.
The book stands as a highly relevant and valuable reference for research -
ers, policymakers, and professionals engaged in shaping safer, more inclusive,
and more resilient digital markets.
—Dr. Les Dolega, Senior Lecturer in GIS and Human Geography at the
University of Liverpool, UK
Consumer Behavior and Digital Services: Competence, Rights and Protection
is an exceptionally timely and highly relevant contribution to contemporary
research on digital transformation and consumer functioning in technology-
driven environments. At a moment when digital services, artificial intelli -
gence, platform ecosystems, and data-driven technologies are rapidly
reshaping everyday life, this book addresses some of the most pressing chal -
lenges faced by modern consumers and societies.
What makes this publication particularly valuable is its strong focus on
the consumer perspective in increasingly complex digital environments. The
book offers an insightful and up-to-date discussion of critical issues such as
digital competences, cybersecurity awareness, misinformation, trust in online
information, AI-generated content, and the growing risks associated with the
expansion of advanced technologies. These topics are not only academically
important but also socially and politically urgent.
The authors successfully connect technological development with ques -
tions of consumer rights, protection, awareness, and responsible participa -
tion in digital ecosystems. Especially noteworthy is the discussion of emerging
AI-related risks and the difficulties consumers face in assessing the credibility
of information and navigating digital environments safely and confidently. In
this regard, the book makes a significant contribution to ongoing debates
surrounding digital literacy, ethical technology adoption, and the future of
consumer protection in the era of artificial intelligence.

This publication will be of great value to academics, researchers, policy -
makers, educators, and practitioners interested in digital services, consumer
behavior, cybersecurity, and the societal implications of technological change.
It is a thoughtful, well-structured, and much-needed contribution to under -
standing how individuals interact with rapidly evolving digital environments
and how safer, more transparent, and more human-centered digital ecosys -
tems can be developed.
—Joanna Krywalski Santiago, PhD, ISEG – Lisbon School of Economics
and Management, University of Lisbon, Lisbon, Portugal

Alicja Fandrejewska-Nowakowska  is Assistant Professor at the Institute of
Management at Warsaw University of Life Sciences, Warsaw, Poland. Her research
interests focus on consumer behavior, digital transformation, knowledge and learning
processes in contemporary organizations, and the role of digital technologies and
cybersecurity in education, management, and sustainable development.
Monika Eisenbardt is Assistant Professor at the University of Economics in Katowice,
Poland. Her research interests focus on business informatics, knowledge manage -
ment, business–consumer knowledge exchange in digital environments, and the appli-
cation of artificial intelligence in education and organizations.
Tomasz Eisenbardt  is Assistant Professor at the University of Warsaw, Warsaw,
Poland. His areas of interest include computer science and business informatics. In
particular, his research focuses on virtual learning environments, digital transforma -
tion, management information systems, the role of digital technologies in education
and organizational development, applications of AI, data engineering, and informa -
tion systems modeling.
This book analyzes the conditions for the development of e-services and digital services
in the European Union. It covers the technological, legal, ethical, and regulatory perspec-
tives, including the present and anticipated EU regulations and directives, as well as the
broader digital environment in which contemporary consumers operate. Special atten-
tion is devoted to user security, digital competences, levels of knowledge, and awareness
of various cyber threats encountered in everyday use of e-services and digital services.
Theoretical chapters, based on a comprehensive review and secondary research,
provide a solid conceptual framework for an in-depth analysis of developments in digi-
tal services markets. They are complemented by an extensive empirical section that
draws on highly detailed research conducted among a large cohort of Generation Z,
encompassing a wide range of e-services and digital services, technologies used, and
consumer security and digital competences of service users. Each chapter is enriched
with the authors’ empirical findings to confront existing research and literature with
the results of their study, thus offering an up-to-date, multidimensional diagnosis and
highlighting key implications for policy, education, and business practice.
This innovative book will appeal in particular to scholars and postgraduate stu -
dents in the fields of consumer behavior, consumer law, marketing, technology, and
innovation management.

Despite the rapid digitization of markets and the growing prevalence of
 e-services and digital services, existing research and regulatory practices nei-
ther sufficiently address the structural asymmetries of information and power
between digital service providers and consumers nor the practical effective -
ness of existing consumer protection mechanisms in the digital environment.
Consumer safety in the e-services and digital services market, which is becom-
ing increasingly widespread, remains a significant research issue. Additionally,
a proper analysis and assessment of the level of awareness, knowledge, and
competence among users of e-services and digital services, particularly in
e-commerce, e-health, e-education, e-culture, and e-government, can yield
valuable conclusions and practical recommendations.
This monograph explores several key areas related to consumer competen-
cies in the context of using e-services and digital services. E-services refer to
services delivered electronically through digital platforms, such as  e-commerce,
e-banking, e-finance, e-education, e-health, e-transport, e-travel, e-govern -
ment, and e-administration. They are typically aimed at supporting individu-
als and communities in meeting their essential social needs, including food
and financial security, healthcare, education, safety and protection, and wel-
fare. This monograph considers both commercial and social e-services, the
latter often provided or regulated by public institutions.
Moreover, this monograph analyzes the broader context of service use in
the digital environment, encompassing both e-services and digital services.
E-services are usually understood as services delivered through electronic net-
works and information and communication technologies (ICTs), such as the
Internet, mobile channels, or self-service kiosks. Their defining feature is the
electronic mode of delivery rather than the nature of the service itself. Digital
services, in turn, are defined as service offerings that are natively digital in
their design, production, and consumption. They are embedded in digital
environments and advanced digital technologies. They also usually exist in

From a practical perspective, particularly in terms of the design and imple-

how they assess risks, interpret information, and build trust in increasingly
complex digital environments.
This monograph examines consumer safety under the circumstances of the
rapid development of technologies driving the expansion of both e-services
and digital services. Particular attention is given to consumer behavior in
digital environments, as well as the evolving legal framework and regulatory
mechanisms designed to protect users. The publication also addresses how
consumers access information, acquire knowledge, and develop competen -
cies, with a special focus on younger generations. Their attitudes, behaviors,
and proactive approaches to improving their knowledge and skills are ana -
lyzed as important factors shaping the future of responsible and informed
participation in digital and social service ecosystems, as well as their ability
to assess information credibility, perceive digital risks, and build trust in
online environments.
Current European Union initiatives and legislation increasingly recognize
new forms of consumer vulnerability in the age of digital transformation,
including the widespread deployment of advanced technologies such as gener-
ative and agentic AI. Policymakers and administrators are aware of the risks
posed by pervasive digital solutions to service provision. However, a signifi -
cant gap persists between the scope of protection declared in regulations and
the actual experiences, awareness levels, and competencies of digital service
users. The associated risks concern misinformation, the credibility of digital
content, and the growing presence of AI-generated information and recom -
mendations. Unfortunately, legal regulations often fail to keep pace with tech-
nological development and the scale of implementation of technological
solutions, such as e-services and digital services available to consumers.

Still,  these technologies are widely used and made available to citizens.
Expectations arise that citizens will quickly embrace the new opportunities
related to remote solutions in commerce, education, healthcare, and adminis-
tration and adapt to the pace of change. If they fail to do so, they may face the
risk of digital exclusion. Against this backdrop, this monograph makes an
original contribution by shifting the focus from the dominant technological
and business perspectives in the discourse on digital transformation, which is
exemplified in literature output, to the consumer’s perspective. The authors
present the consumer perspective with particular emphasis on competencies,
knowledge, awareness, risk perception, trust in information, and challenges
related to artificial intelligence (AI).
By integrating legal, technological, and behavioral perspectives with empiri-
cal research on young consumers, this monograph addresses a clear research
gap in understanding how consumers perceive and understand their rights,
assess risks, and evaluate the credibility of information. Moreover, the authors
analyze the strategies consumers develop to navigate complex digital service
ecosystems and whether they are effective responses to the emerging risks and
threats. Given that local governments and the European Union are strongly
interested in the broadest possible use of e-services as a response to current
challenges, such as present and projected staff shortages resulting from an aging
society and negative birth rates, market instability, and multidimensional geo-
political, economic, social, and environmental crises, consumer safety remains a
significant issue requiring in-depth analysis. The aim of this study is therefore
not only to diagnose the scale and nature of information asymmetries and
power imbalances among market actors but also to formulate evidence-based
recommendations to strengthen systemic solutions, improve regulatory frame-
works, and develop educational initiatives. The policies and initiatives aimed at
consumers present the potential to effectively empower consumers and foster
the creation of safer, fairer, and more transparent digital services markets.
Building on these considerations, the main research problem of the study
is formulated as follows: How do young consumers function in the digital
services market? To provide a more analytically precise perspective, the study
is guided by the following main research question: How do young consumers
function in the digital services market across technological, behavioral, com-
petence, protection, and perception–trust dimensions? This multidimensional
approach reflects the complexity of contemporary digital environments,
where consumer functioning is shaped not only by access to technologies but
also by competences, awareness, behavior, and the ability to assess risks and
information credibility.
In the era of digital transformation, the growing integration of ICTs into
everyday life is transforming how consumers perceive, access, evaluate, and
use services. The aim of this study is to examine competencies, with particular
emphasis on digital competencies and digital literacy, awareness and precau-
tionary behaviors in the face of cyber threats, consumer awareness of their
rights in the digital services market, as well as the perception of digital risks, the

Introduction 5
This monograph is intended for a broad audience interested in consumer
behavior in digital environments. It may be particularly valuable for mem -
bers of the academic community, including researchers in digital economics,
digital transformation, service management, communication sciences, and
digital security, as it provides a foundation for further research on consumer
behavior, digital competencies, and risk perception in the context of dynamic
technological change.
The publication is also relevant for public institutions responsible for
shaping policies in the areas of digitalization, consumer protection, and edu-
cation. The findings can support the development of more effective regula -
tory frameworks and educational initiatives to strengthen user competencies
and mitigate digital risks.
Furthermore, the monograph may be useful for market practitioners,
including companies offering digital services, system designers, and user
experience (UX) specialists. A better understanding of consumers’ risk per -
ception, trust, and competencies can contribute to the design of more trans -
parent, secure, and user-centered digital solutions.
The structure of the book  is designed to reflect the multidimensional
nature of consumer functioning in digital service environments and corre -
sponds directly to the analytical framework adopted in the study. Each chap-
ter addresses a distinct but interrelated dimension of consumer activity,
moving from contextual and theoretical foundations toward detailed empir-
ical analysis.
Chapter 1 focuses on the technological context of digital services, examin-
ing how technological conditions such as platformization, data-driven sys -
tems, and advanced digital infrastructures shape consumer functioning. It
also provides the conceptual and methodological foundations for the empir-
ical study, outlining the research design, variables, and analytical approach.
Chapter 2 addresses the behavioral dimension, analyzing patterns of con-
sumer and prosumptive behavior in digital service environments. Particular
attention is given to the use of digital services, engagement in platform-based
ecosystems, and the role of consumers as both users and content creators.
Chapter 3 is devoted to the competence dimension and examines the level
and structure of consumer competences in digital environments. It explores
issues such as lifelong learning, sources of knowledge acquisition, and the
perceived value of consumer knowledge, while also identifying existing com-
petence gaps that may limit effective participation in digital markets.
Chapter 4  focuses on the protection dimension, analyzing consumer
awareness of digital risks and the use of protection mechanisms. The chapter
examines both cognitive aspects (knowledge of rights and institutions) and
behavioral aspects (preventive and reactive actions), highlighting discrepan -
cies between declared awareness and actual practices.
Chapter 5  addresses the perception and trust dimension, providing an
in-depth analysis of how consumers perceive digital risks, assess the credibility
of information, and evaluate threats related to misinformation and  AI.
This chapter highlights the growing importance of trust, risk perception, and
