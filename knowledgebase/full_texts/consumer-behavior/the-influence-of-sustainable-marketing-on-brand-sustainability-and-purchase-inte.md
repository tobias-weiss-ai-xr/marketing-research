ABSTRACT:
This study examines how sustainable marketing practices influence consumers' purchase intention
toward sustainable fashion in Saudi Arabia. Drawing on the Stimulus-Organism-Response theory, the
study conceptualizes sustainable marketing practices as external stimuli, perceived brand sustainability
as the internal evaluative organism, and purchase intention as the behavioral response. The study
further investigates whether Vision 2030 sustainability awareness strengthens the relationship between
sustainable marketing practices and perceived brand sustainability. Data were collected through a
structured questionnaire from 127 consumers in Saudi Arabia and analyzed using reliability
assessment, descriptive statistics, Pearson correlations, and regression - based mediation and
moderation tests. The results show that sustainable marketing practices significantly enhance perceived
brand sustainability (β = 0.400, p < .001), and perceived brand sustainability significantly increases
purchase intention toward sustaina ble fashion (β = 0.340, p < .001). Perceived brand sustainability
significantly mediates the relationship between sustainable marketing practices and purchase intention
(indirect β = 0.135, 95% CI [0.040, 0.254]). However, Vision 2030 sustainability awaren ess does not
significantly moderate the effect of sustainable marketing practices on perceived brand sustainability
(β = 0.053, p = .447), although the interaction is positive. The findings contribute to sustainable
marketing and consumer behavior literatu re by demonstrating that consumer perceptions of brand
sustainability are a central mechanism through which sustainable marketing can influence purchase
intention. The study also provides practical implications for fashion brands, marketers, and
policymakers seeking to promote responsible consumption in alignment with Saudi Vision 2030.

Keywords: sustainable marketing practices, perceived brand sustainability, purchase intention, Vision 2030,
sustainable fashion, Saudi Arabia, S-O-R theory

1. Introduction

The fashion industry has become one of the most visible sectors in debates on
sustainability, ethical consumption, and responsible production. The rise of fast fashion
has expanded consumer access to affordable clothing, but it has also intensified concerns
about excessive resource use, textile waste, carbon emissions, and labor conditions. Recent
studies argue that sustainable fashion consumption is no longer a niche concern, as
consumers increasingly evaluate fashion brands according to environmental transparency,
ethical sourcing, circularity, and responsible communication (Li  et al., 2024; Neha et al.,
2024). At the same time, consumers are becoming more cautious about sustainability
claims because greenwashing has become a major reputational and regulatory issue in
fashion markets (Li, 2025).

Sustainable marketing practices refer to marketing activities that communicate a
firm's environmental and social responsibility through eco -labeling, green advertising,
transparent sustainability information, recycling initiatives, ethical sourcing
communication, and circular fashion messaging. Such practices can help consumers
recognize and evaluate a brand's sustainability commitment. However, the i nfluence of
sustainable marketing is not automatic. Consumers may react positively when sustainability
communication appears credible and transparent, but they may resist or reject it when
messages seem vague, exaggerated, or symbolic. This tension has mad e perceived brand
sustainability a crucial psychological mechanism in sustainable fashion research.
In Saudi Arabia, sustainability has become a central theme in national
transformation. Vision 2030 and associated initiatives such as the Saudi Green Initiative,
circular economy programs, and responsible consumption policies have expanded public
awareness of environmental responsibility and sustainable economic development. Recent
research on Saudi enterprises indicates that the circular economy has become increasingly
relevant to the Kingdom's sustainability agenda (Alhamad, 2025; Alkahtani  et al., 2020).
The Saudi fashion sector is also developing in a context of economic diversification, youth-
driven consumption, digital retail growth, and expanding public interest in lifestyle and
sustainability. Therefore, Saudi Arabia provides a timely context for examining how
consumers respond to sustainable marketing in fashion.
Although prior studies have examined green marketing and purchase intention,
important conceptual and contextual gaps remain. Green marketing generally focuses on
the environmental attributes of products, services, and promotional activities, whereas
sustainable marketing has a broader scope that incorporates environmental, social, ethical,
and economic responsibility across marketing practices. Perceived brand sustainability is
conceptually distinct from both, as it reflects consumers’ overall evaluation o f whether a
brand is genuinely committed to responsible environmental and social practices. Thus,
sustainable marketing practices represent the external signals communicated by the firm,
while perceived brand sustainability represents the consumer’s intern al assessment of
those signals.
Existing studies frequently examine the direct relationship between green or
sustainable marketing and purchase intention, but they provide limited explanation of the
psychological process through which marketing communication is translated into
consumer behavior. In particular, relatively  little attention has been given to perceived
brand sustainability as a mediating mechanism in the fashion sector. This distinction is
especially important in Saudi Arabia, where sustainability communication is shaped not
only by firm-level marketing practices but also by the wider national discourse associated
with Vision 2030. However, it remains unclear whether consumers’ awareness of Vision
2030 strengthens the extent to which sustainable marketing practices are interpreted as
evidence of genuine brand sustainability.
Accordingly, this study develops a moderated mediation model in which
sustainable marketing practices influence purchase intention indirectly through perceived
brand sustainability, while Vision 2030 sustainability awareness is examined as a contextual
moderator of the relationship between sustainable marketing practices and perceived
brand sustainability. This structure clarifies both the psychological mechanism and the

2. Literature Review
2.1 Theoretical Underpinning: Stimulus-Organism-Response Theory

The Stimulus-Organism-Response (S-O-R) theory provides a useful framework
for explaining how external marketing stimuli influence internal consumer evaluations and
behavioral responses. Originally developed by Mehrabian and Russell (1974), the S -O-R
model suggests that individuals do not respond mechanically to environmental cues.
Instead, external stimuli influence internal psychological states, which subsequently shape
behavioral intentions and actions.
Although alternative theoretical perspectives, such as the Theory of Planned
Behavior (TPB) and Signaling  Theory, have frequently been applied in sustainable
consumption research, the Stimulus –Organism–Response (S -O-R) framework is
particularly appropriate for the present study. TPB primarily explains behavioural intention
through attitudes, subjective norms,  and perceived behavioural control, whereas the
current study seeks to explain how external sustainability communication is cognitively
interpreted before influencing behavioural intention. Similarly, Signaling  Theory focuses
on how firms communicate quality or credibility under conditions of information
asymmetry but provides limited explanation of the internal psychological evaluation that
transforms external sustainability signals into consumer responses. In contrast, the S-O-R
framework explicitly captures this internal evaluative process, making it well suited to
examine how sustainable marketing practices influence purchase intention through
consumers' perceptions of brand sustainability.
Within the S-O-R framework, perceived brand sustainability is conceptualized as
the principal organism because it represents consumers' overall evaluation of whether a
brand genuinely demonstrates environmental and social responsibility. Although
constructs such as trust, attitude, and perceived authenticity are also relevant in sustainable
marketing research, they represent more specific affective or cognitive responses.
Perceived brand sustainability provides a broader evaluative construct that integrates
consumers' judgments regarding environmental commitment, ethical responsibility,
transparency, and long -term sustainability performance. Consequently, it serves as the
primary psychological mechanism through which sustainable marketing practices are
translated into purchase intention within the Saudi fashion context.

2.2 Sustainable Marketing Practices and Perceived Brand Sustainability
Sustainable marketing practices communicate a firm's commitment to
environmental protection, ethical production, transparency, and social responsibility. In
fashion, these practices may include eco -labels, recycled material claims, circular fashion
campaigns, ethical labor communication, sustainable packaging, and transparent reporting
on sourcing and production. Neha et al. (2024) argue that sustainable marketing initiatives
have become important tools through which fashion apparel brands attempt to encourage
sustainable purchase behavior.

Marketing signals are important because consumers usually cannot directly
observe the complete sustainability performance of a fashion brand. Instead, they rely on
visible cues such as eco -labels, advertising, online content, certification, recycling
messages, and sustainability storytelling. When these signals are consistent and transparent,
consumers are more likely to perceive the brand as genuinely sustainable. Conversely,
vague or exaggerate sustainability communication may create skepticism and greenwashing
concerns. Recent evidence shows that greenwashing has become a major challenge in
fashion because consumers increasingly scrutinize whether brands' environmental claims
are substantiated (Li, 2025; Rehman et al., 2022).
In the Saudi context, sustainable marketing may also benefit from wider national
attention to environmental responsibility under Vision 2030. However, consumer
perceptions still depend on the credibility and clarity of brand communication. Therefore,
sustainable marketing practices are expected to positively influence perceived brand
sustainability.
H1: Sustainable marketing practices positively influence perceived brand sustainability.

2.3 Perceived Brand Sustainability and Purchase Intention
Perceived brand sustainability refers to consumers' belief that a brand is genuinely
committed to environmental and social responsibility. This perception is important
because consumers increasingly prefer brands that align with their personal values, ethical
concerns, and environmental expectations. Gidaković et al. (2022) show that brand
sustainability can positively influence purchase intention through favorable  brand
impressions and attitudes. More recent work on sustainable clothing also shows that
perceived sustainability can strengthen perceived quality, value, and willingness to
purchase sustainable apparel (Li et al., 2024; Khan et al., 2023).
In sustainable fashion, purchase intention reflects the likelihood that consumers
will buy from sustainable brands, choose them over conventional alternatives, recommend
them to others, or pay a premium for genuinely sustainable products. When consumers
perceive a fashion brand as sustainable, they are more likely to consider the purchase as
consistent with their values and social identity. Recent studies on consumption value and
sustainable fashion further suggest that perceived value, environmental benefits, and social
responsibility can increase consumer intention to purchase sustainable products (Almalik
et al., 2025; Wu, 2025).
Therefore, perceived brand sustainability is expected to positively influence
purchase intention toward sustainable fashion.
H2: Perceived brand sustainability positively influences purchase intention toward
sustainable fashion.
2.4 Mediating Role of Perceived Brand Sustainability
According to S -O-R theory, external marketing stimuli influence behavior
through internal psychological evaluation. In this study, sustainable marketing practices
may not directly lead to purchase intention unless consumers interpret those practices as
evidence of genuine brand sustainability. Perceived brand sustainability therefore explains
how and why sustainable marketing practices influence purchase intention.
This mediating logic is supported by recent sustainable fashion literature
emphasizing the importance of consumer perceptions, trust, and brand credibility.

Sustainable marketing campaigns may attract attention, but consumers are more likely to
form purchase intentions when they believe that the brand's sustainability claims are
credible and consistent. Albakri and Ahmed, (2021) and Nguyen-Viet et al. (2023) similarly
emphasize that green marketing effects depend on non-deception, transparency, and green
brand trust. In the fashion sector, this mechanism is especially important because
consumers often question whether sustainability claims are authentic or mere ly
promotional.
Thus, perceived brand sustainability is expected to mediate the relationship
between sustainable marketing practices and purchase intention.
H3: Perceived brand sustainability mediates the relationship between sustainable
marketing practices and purchase intention toward sustainable fashion.

2.5 Moderating Role of Vision 2030 Sustainability Awareness
Vision 2030 has significantly shaped the sustainability discourse in Saudi Arabia.
The national agenda emphasizes environmental responsibility, diversification, quality of
life, innovation, and sustainable development. The Vision 2030 Annual Report (2025)
highlights the continuing importance of sustainability -oriented national transformation,
while studies on Saudi circular economy adoption show that knowledge and awareness are
important factors in achieving sustainability-related goals (Alhamad, 2025).
Consumers who are more aware of Vision 2030 sustainability goals may be more
attentive to sustainable brand practices. Such consumers may also evaluate sustainability
communication more seriously because it resonates with a broader national transformation
agenda. Therefore, Vision 2030 awareness may strengthen the influence of sustainable
marketing practices on perceived brand sustainability.
However, this moderating effect may not always be strong. Awareness of national
sustainability goals does not automatically mean that consumers trust brand -level
sustainability messages. If consumers are skeptical of fashion brands or if sustainable
marketing claims lack specificity, Vision 2030 awareness may not significantly amplify the
effect of marketing communication. Nevertheless, based on the logic of contextual
awareness, this study proposes the following hypothesis:
H4: Vision 2030 sustainability awareness positively moderates the relationship between
sustainable marketing practices and perceived brand sustainability, such that the
relationship is stronger at higher levels of Vision 2030 awareness.

3. Methodology

3.1 Research Design and Context
This study employed a quantitative, cross-sectional survey design to examine the
impact of sustainable marketing practices on purchase intention toward sustainable
fashion in Saudi Arabia. The design is appropriate because the study aims to test
theoretically derived relationships among latent constructs and assess mediation and
moderation effects using consumer survey data.
The empirical context is the Saudi fashion industry. This context is relevant
because Saudi Arabia is experiencing rapid changes in retail, lifestyle consumption,
sustainability awareness, and digital consumer engagement. The fashion sector is
increasingly influenced by young consumers, online shopping, brand communication, and
national sustainability priorities. The study therefore provides a contextually meaningful
examination of sustainable fashion consumption in a Vision 2030 environment.
3.2 Population, Sampling, and Data Collection
The target population consisted of consumers residing in Saudi Arabia who
purchase fashion products. The unit of analysis was the individual consumer. Data were
collected using a structured questionnaire distributed to consumers through online and
personal networks. A convenience sampling approach was used due to the exploratory and
consumer-oriented nature of the study.
A total of 127 responses were received. Item -level missing values were limited,
with 20 missing observations across the item -level dataset. Composite scores were
calculated using valid item responses for each construct. The final regression -based
analysis used 125 complete cases across the four main constructs.
3.3 Measurement of Constructs
The questionnaire contained five sections. Section A captured demographic
information, including gender, age, education level, income, and fashion purchase
frequency. Section B measured sustainable marketing practices using six items adapted
from sustainable marketing and green marketing literature. Section C measured perceived

3.4 Data Analysis Procedure
Data were analyzed  using descriptive statistics, reliability analysis, Pearson
correlation analysis, and regression - based mediation and moderation tests. Cronbach's
alpha was used to assess internal consistency. Pearson correlations were used to evaluate
bivariate relation ships among the constructs. Hypothesis testing was conducted using
standardized regression coefficients. The mediation effect was tested using a bootstrapping
procedure with 5,000 resamples and bias-corrected confidence intervals. The moderation
effect was  tested by creating an interaction term between standardized sustainable
marketing practices and standardized Vision 2030 awareness.
This approach is suitable for the available sample size and supports a clear test of
the proposed S-O-R model. Because the available data analysis file contained composite
scores and reliability outputs rather than a full covariance-based SEM or PLS-SEM report,
the empirical article reports regression-based mediation and moderation results rather than
fabricating unobserved SEM model fit or PLS path outputs.

a significant positive effect on purchase intention (β = 0.340, t = 3.772, p < .001). The
direct effect of sustainable marketing practices on purchase intention became non -
significant in the presence of perceived brand sustainability (β = 0.131, p = .147).

5. Discussion
 The findings provide important insights into sustainable fashion consumption in
Saudi Arabia. First, the significant effect of sustainable marketing practices on perceived
brand sustainability confirms that consumers use sustainability communication as a signal
for evaluating brand responsibility. This supports recent sustainable fashion research
suggesting that eco-labels, sustainable materials communication, recycling initiatives, and
ethical production messages can shape consumer perceptions when they ar e clear and
credible (Li et al., 2024; Neha et al., 2024).
Second, perceived brand sustainability significantly influenced purchase intention.
This finding reinforces the argument that consumers are more likely to purchase from
fashion brands when they believe those brands are genuinely committed to environmental
and social responsibility. The result is consistent with research showing that perceived
sustainability increases consumer preference, value perceptions, and purchase intention in
clothing contexts (Li et al., 2024; Wu, 2025).

Third, the mediation result confirms the core logic of S -O-R theory. Sustainable
marketing practices do not simply produce purchase intention directly; rather, they operate
through consumers' internal evaluation of brand sustainability. This finding is imp ortant
for fashion brands because it shows that sustainability campaigns must build credible
perceptions of sustainability before they can effectively influence purchase behavior . In
other words, the marketing message matters, but the consumer's belief in the brand's
sustainability matters even more.
Fourth, the moderation result shows that Vision 2030 awareness did not
significantly strengthen the relationship between sustainable marketing practices and
perceived brand sustainability. This result should not be interpreted as indicating that
Vision 2030 is unimportant. Instead, it suggests that national sustainability awareness may
create a supportive background context, but it does not automatically make consumers
believe every fashion brand's sustainability communication. Brand -level credibility,
transparency, evidence, and avoidance of greenwashing remain critical. This is particularly
relevant because recent evidence shows that fashion greenwashing is increasingly
recognized by consumers and regulators (Li, 2025).
Although the findings provide valuable insights into sustainable fashion
consumption in Saudi Arabia, they should be interpreted in light of  the sample
characteristics. The respondents were predominantly older, highly educated, and relatively
higher-income consumers, who may possess greater environmental awareness and
purchasing power than the broader Saudi population. Consequently, these consumers may
be more receptive to sustainable marketing practices and more capable of translating
sustainability perceptions into purchase intentions. In contrast, younger consumers, lower-
income groups, or consumers from different regions may prioritize factors such as price,
fashion trends, or product availability over sustainability considerations. Therefore, the
findings should be generalized with caution beyond the demographic profile represented
in this study.
Overall, the study suggests that Saudi consumers are aware of sustainability and
show willingness to consider sustainable fashion, but fashion brands still need to
strengthen the credibility, clarity, and substance of their sustainability marketing.
Sustainable marketing should therefore move beyond general claims and provide specific,
verifiable, and transparent information about materials, sourcing, circularity, labor
practices, and environmental impact.
5.1 Theoretical Implications
This study contributes to the literature in several ways. First, it extends S -O-R
theory to the Saudi sustainable fashion context by showing that sustainable marketing
practices act as stimuli that influence purchase intention through perceived brand
sustainability. This supports the idea that internal consumer evaluations are central to
translating sustainability communication into behavioral intention.
Second, the study contributes to sustainable marketing literature by empirically
confirming the mediating role of perceived brand sustainability. The finding that perceived
brand sustainability significantly mediated the relationship between sustainable marketing
practices and purchase intention is consistent with previous studies demonstrating that
consumers respond to sustainability initiatives primarily through their perceptions of
brand credibility, environmental commitment, and green brand image. Rather than directly

influencing purchasing behaviour, sustainable marketing appears to strengthen consumers'
evaluations of a brand's sustainability performance, which subsequently enhances purchase
intention. This finding supports the S -O-R framework by demonstrating that pe rceived
brand sustainability functions as the internal psychological mechanism linking external
sustainability communication to behavioural responses.
In contrast, Vision 2030 sustainability awareness did not significantly moderate
the relationship between sustainable marketing practices and perceived brand
sustainability. Although this finding differs from the initial expectation that greater
awareness of national sustainability initiatives would strengthen consumers' responses to
sustainable marketing, several explanations are plausible. First, the Vision 2030 awareness
measure may primarily capture general awareness rather than consumers' personal
engagement with sustainability initiatives. Second, relatively high awareness among
respondents may have produced restricted variability, reducing the statistical power to
detect moderation effects. Finally, awareness of national sustainability policies may remain
psychologically distant from consumers' day -to-day fashion purchasing decisions, where
product quality, price, style, and brand reputation continue to exert stronger influence than
broader policy awareness. Future research should therefore examine alternative contextual
moderators, including environmental concern, sustainability involvement, or perceived
consumer effectiveness.
 Prior studies often examine green marketing, brand image, trust, or attitude, but
the present study highlights perceived brand sustainability as a specific evaluative construct
that connects marketing practices with consumer intention.
Third, the study contributes to Saudi consumer behavior  research by examining
the contextual role of Vision 2030 sustainability awareness. Although the moderation
effect was not significant, the finding is theoretically useful because it shows that national-
level awareness does not automatically intensify bran d-level sustainability perceptions.
Future studies should examine additional boundary conditions, such as green trust,
environmental concern, consumer skepticism, and perceived greenwashing.
5.2 Practical Implications
The findings provide several practical implications for fashion brands operating
in Saudi Arabia. First, brands should design sustainability communication that is specific,
transparent, and evidence-based. Consumers appear responsive to sustainable marketing,
but only when such communication strengthens perceived brand sustainability. Brands
should therefore avoid vague expressions such as 'eco-friendly' without clear explanation.
Second, sustainable fashion brands should prioritize credible signals such as
certifications, material transparency, recycling programs, circular fashion options, and clear
reporting on ethical production. These signals can strengthen perceived brand
sustainability and, in turn, increase purchase intention.
Third, marketers should align brand sustainability messages with the broader
sustainability direction of Vision 2030, but they should not rely on Vision 2030 awareness
alone. Consumers may know that Vision 2030 supports sustainability, but they still need
convincing evidence that a particular fashion brand is sustainable.

5.3 Limitations and Future Research
This study has several limitations. First, the sample size was 127 respondents,
which is adequate for exploratory regression -based analysis but smaller than what would
be preferred for complex PLS -SEM or covariance -based SEM with multiple latent
constructs. Future studies should collect larger samples across multiple Saudi cities and
consumer segments.
Second, the study used convenience sampling, which limits generalizability.
Future research should use probability or stratified sampling to better represent Saudi
fashion consumers. Third, the study relied on self -reported measures, which may be
affected by social desirability or common method bias. Future studies may combine survey
data with experimental designs, behavioral data, or actual purchase records.
Fourth, Vision 2030 awareness was examined as a moderator, but the non -
significant result suggests that other contextual factors may better explain variations in
consumer response. Future studies should test environmental concern, sustainability
knowledge, green trust, consumer skepticism , perceived greenwashing, and social
influence as moderators. Finally, future research may compare local Saudi brands,
international fast fashion brands, luxury brands, and online-only fashion retailers to better
understand category-specific sustainability responses.
In addition to the use of convenience sampling, the demographic composition of
the sample limits the generalizability of the findings. The sample was largely composed of
older, highly educated, and relatively higher -income consumers, which may not fully
represent the diversity of fashion consumers across Saudi Arabia. Future research should
employ larger and more representative samples, including younger consumers, lower -
income groups, and participants from different regions, to examine whether the observed
relationships remain consistent across demographic segments.

6. Conclusion

This study examined the impact of sustainable marketing practices on purchase
intention toward sustainable fashion in Saudi Arabia, with perceived brand sustainability
as a mediator and Vision 2030 sustainability awareness as a moderator. Drawing on S -O-
R theory and using survey data from 127 consumers, the study found that sustainable
marketing practices significantly improve perceived brand sustainability, and perceived
brand sustainability significantly increases purchase intention. The mediation effect was
supported, confirming that perceived brand sustainability is the key mechanism through
which sustainable marketing influences consumer intention.
However, Vision 2030 awareness did not significantly moderate the relationship
between sustainable marketing practices and perceived brand sustainability. This finding
suggests that while Vision 2030 provides an important sustainability context, fashion
brands must still establish their own credibility through transparent and authentic
sustainability communication. The study concludes that sustainable marketing in the Saudi
fashion industry can influence purchase intention, but its effectiveness depends largely on
whether consumers perceive the brand as genuinely sustainable.
