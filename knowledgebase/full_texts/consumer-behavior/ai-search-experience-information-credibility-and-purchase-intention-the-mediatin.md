A B S T R A C T
The emergence of Artificial Intelligence (AI) has transformed digital commerce by enabling Zero Click Commerce,
where consumers increasingly rely on AI -generated recommendations and search results to make purchasing
decisions without visiting multiple websites. This study examines the effects of AI Search Experience, Information
Credibility, and AI Personalization Quality on Purchase Intention, while investigating the mediating role of Brand
Trust among Pakistani online consumers. Grounded in the Stimulus –Organism–Response (SOR) theory, the study
employed a quantitative, cross -sectional research design. Data were collected from 380 Pakistani online
consumers using a structured questionnaire comprising 25 measurement items adapted from established scales.
The collected data were analyzed using SPSS 27 and Hayes' PROCESS Macro (Model 4). The findings revealed that
AI Search Experience, Information Credibility, and AI Personalization Quality each have significant positive effects
on Purchase Intention, with AI P ersonalization Quality emerging as the strongest predictor. Furthermore, Brand
Trust significantly mediates the relationships between all three independent variables and Purchase Intention. The
study contributes to the growing literature on AI-enabled consumer behavior by extending the SOR framework to
the context of Zero Click Commerce and offers practical insights for organizations seeking to enhance AI -driven
customer experiences and strengthen consumer trust.
INTRODUCTION
Artificial intelligence (AI) has become a transformative force in digital commerce by reshaping how consumers
search for information, evaluate products, and make purchasing decisions. AI -powered technologies, including
intelligent search engines, recommend er systems, and conversational assistants, enable businesses to analyze
consumer preferences and deliver personalized shopping experiences in real time. These capabilities have

enhanced customer engagement, improved decision -making efficiency, and created new opportunities for
organizations to strengthen consumer relationships through data -driven marketing strategies (Prentice &
Nguyen, 2020; Desai et al., 2026).
The advancement of generative AI has accelerated the emergence of Zero Click Commerce, a digital environment
where consumers receive product information, comparisons, and recommendations directly within AI -powered
search interfaces without navigating multi ple websites. Unlike traditional search engines that function as
gateways to external content, AI -powered search platforms increasingly provide comprehensive responses that
satisfy consumers' information needs in a single interaction. Consequently, AI has shifted from being a tool for
information retrieval to becoming an intelligent decision -support system that actively influences consumer
purchase decisions (Dharani & Lavanya Latha, 2025; Gong & Zhang, 2026).
Within this evolving environment, consumers increasingly rely on AI -generated search results to evaluate
products and services. The quality of consumers' interactions with AI search systems, referred to as AI search
experience, influences how effectively t hey obtain relevant information and complete purchasing decisions.
Likewise, information credibility has become essential because consumers' willingness to rely on AI -generated
recommendations depends on their perceptions of the accuracy, reliability, and trustworthiness of the
information presented (Flanagin & Metzger, 2000). At the same time, AI personalization quality enables
businesses to deliver customized product recommendations that improve shopping convenience and reduce
consumers' search effort by matching products with individual preferences (Pu et al., 2011; Hu et al., 2025).
Despite these technological advancements, purchasing decisions remain strongly influenced by psychological
factors. Among these, brand trust plays a central role by reducing uncertainty associated with AI -generated
recommendations and increasing consumers'  confidence in online transactions. Consumers are more likely to
accept AI -generated suggestions and complete purchases when they trust the associated brand (Chaudhuri &
Holbrook, 2001; Wen & Li, 2025). Consequently, AI technologies influence purchase inte ntion not only through
technological capabilities but also by strengthening consumers' trust in brands.
Pakistan has experienced rapid growth in internet penetration, smartphone usage, digital payment systems, and
e-commerce adoption, resulting in greater consumer interaction with AI-powered search technologies. However,
empirical evidence explaining how AI search experience, information credibility, AI personalization quality, and
brand trust jointly influence purchase intention remains limited within the Pakistani context. Therefore, this study
investigates these relationships in the context of Zero Click C ommerce to provide both theoretical and practical
insights into AI-enabled consumer behavior.
Problem Statement
The increasing adoption of AI -powered search technologies has fundamentally changed how consumers search
for information and make purchasing decisions. However, existing research has primarily focused on AI chatbots,
recommendation systems, or technology adoption, while relatively limited attention has been given to Zero Click
Commerce and AI-powered search experiences (Prentice & Nguyen, 2020; ElSayad & Mamdouh, 2024). Moreover,
previous studies have generally examined AI search experience, information cre dibility, and AI personalization
quality independently, despite evidence suggesting that these technological factors collectively shape consumer
behavior (Hu et al., 2025; Mani et al., 2025). Additionally, although brand trust has been recognized as an
important determinant of online purchasing behavior, its mediating role within AI-enabled Zero Click Commerce
remains insufficiently explored, particularly in emerging economies such as Pakistan (Liang, 2026; Khyareh &
Khairandish, 2026). Consequently, there is a need for empirical research that investigates the combined influence
of these factors on consumers' purchase intention to support both theory development and AI-driven marketing
practice.

Significance of the Study
Theoretical Significance
This study contributes to the growing literature on AI -enabled consumer behavior by integrating AI search
experience, information credibility, AI personalization quality, brand trust, and purchase intention within a single
conceptual framework. It extends the emerging concept of Zero Click Commerce by providing empirical evidence
from Pakistan, where research on AI-driven consumer behavior remains limited. Furthermore, the study enhances
understanding of the psychological role of brand trust in explaining how AI-related technological factors influence
consumers' purchase intentions, thereby enriching AI marketing and consumer behavior literature (Chaudhuri &
Holbrook, 2001; Wen & Li, 2025).
Practical Significance
The findings of this study provide valuable insights for digital marketers, e-commerce organizations, AI platform
developers, and policymakers. Understanding the importance of AI search experience, credible information, and
personalized recommendations can assist organizations in designing AI -powered search systems that improve
consumer trust and encourage purchasing behavior. The study also offers practical guidance for businesses
seeking to optimize AI-driven customer experiences, strengthen brand trust, and enhance marketing effectiveness
in increasingly competitive digital marketplaces (Mani et al., 2025; Mousa et al., 2026).
Scope of the Study
This study focuses on Pakistani online consumers who use AI -powered search technologies during online
shopping. It examines the effects of AI search experience, information credibility, and AI personalization quality
on purchase intention while considering  brand trust as the mediating variable within the context of Zero Click
Commerce. The study adopts a quantitative, cross -sectional research design using data collected from 380
respondents and analyzes the proposed relationships through SPSS and PROCESS Macro mediation analysis.
LITERATURE REVIEW
This study is primarily grounded in the Stimulus –Organism–Response (SOR) Theory, originally proposed by
Mehrabian and Russell (1974). The SOR framework explains how environmental stimuli influence individuals'
internal psychological states, which subsequen tly shape behavioral responses. Although initially developed
within environmental psychology, the theory has been widely applied in consumer behavior, digital marketing,
and e -commerce research to explain how technological environments influence consumer d ecision-making
processes.

AI Search Experience
Artificial intelligence has significantly transformed online search by enabling intelligent systems to understand
user intent, analyze contextual information, and generate personalized responses. Unlike conventional keyword-
based search engines, AI -powered search technologies employ natural language processing, machine learning,
and predictive analytics to provide relevant information that supports consumer decision making. Consequently,
consumers increasingly depend on AI -generated search results during on line shopping because these systems
simplify product discovery and reduce information overload.
AI search experience refers to consumers' perceptions regarding the efficiency, responsiveness, relevance, and
ease of interacting with AI -powered search systems (Prentice & Nguyen, 2020). A positive AI search experience
enables consumers to locate desired  products quickly while reducing the cognitive effort required to compare
multiple alternatives. Recent developments in generative AI have further enhanced search experiences by
allowing conversational interactions in which consumers receive comprehensive recommendations instead of
simple lists of hyperlinks. These improvements have fundamentally changed the online shopping journey by
providing intelligent decision support throughout the purchasing process.
Existing literature consistently demonstrates that positive AI search experiences improve customer satisfaction,
engagement, and behavioral intentions. Prentice and Nguyen (2020) found that AI-enabled customer interactions
enhance consumer engagement by delivering efficient, personalized, and responsive services. Similarly, Armutcu
(2026) reported that high -quality AI communication positively influences consumers' purchase intentions by
strengthening their relationships with brands. Becan and Çeber (2026) f urther demonstrated that positive
experiences with AI-driven consumer chatbots improve consumers' attitudes toward AI technologies and increase
their willingness to adopt AI -assisted purchasing platforms. These findings indicate that AI search experience
represents an important technological determinant of consumer behavior in digital commerce.
Information Credibility
Information credibility has long been recognized as one of the most important determinants of online consumer
behavior. As consumers increasingly rely on digital information to evaluate products and services, the perceived
credibility of available informat ion directly influences purchasing decisions. Information credibility refers to
consumers' perceptions regarding the accuracy, reliability, trustworthiness, and authenticity of information
obtained through online sources (Flanagin & Metzger, 2000).
The growing adoption of AI -powered search systems has increased the importance of information credibility
because consumers frequently rely on AI -generated summaries rather than consulting multiple independent
sources. Although AI systems provide faster ac cess to information, concerns regarding algorithmic bias,
misinformation, hallucinated content, and limited transparency have raised questions regarding the reliability of

AI Personalization Quality
Personalization has become a defining characteristic of AI -powered digital marketing because it enables
organizations to deliver customized shopping experiences based on consumers' individual preferences and
behavioral patterns. AI personalization quality refers to the effectiveness with which intelligent systems generate
relevant recommendations, personalized search results, and customized product suggestions according to
consumers' interests and previous online activities (Pu et al., 2011).
Modern recommender systems employ artificial intelligence algorithms to analyze browsing histories, purchasing
behavior, demographic characteristics, and contextual information in order to identify products that closely match
consumers' needs. Such persona lized recommendations reduce search costs while improving shopping
convenience and decision -making efficiency. As AI systems continuously learn from consumer interactions,
recommendation quality improves over time, resulting in increasingly relevant shopping experiences.
Previous research consistently demonstrates that AI -driven personalization positively influences consumer
attitudes and purchasing behavior. Hu et al. (2025) reported that AI -powered personalized recommendations
significantly increase spontaneous buying in tention among Generation Z consumers by improving perceived
usefulness and satisfaction. Likewise, Hasim and Nazri (2025) found that AI -driven personalization positively
affects purchase intention by enhancing mobile application experiences and strengthening customer engagement.
Khyareh and Khairandish (2026) further concluded that AI personalization contributes to digital trust and
consumer engagement, highlighting its strategic importance within contemporary e-commerce environments.
Brand Trust
Brand trust represents consumers' confidence that a brand will consistently fulfill its promises and deliver reliable
products and services. According to Chaudhuri and Holbrook (2001), trust develops when consumers perceive a
brand as dependable, honest, and capable of meeting their expectations over time. Within digital commerce, trust
reduces consumers' perceived risks associated with online transactions and increases their willingness to engage
with brands.
The increasing integration of AI into consumer decision -making has expanded the importance of brand trust
because consumers frequently rely on AI -generated recommendations when evaluating unfamiliar products.
Although AI technologies improve search efficie ncy and recommendation accuracy, purchasing decisions
ultimately depend upon consumers' confidence in the brands being recommended. Liang (2026) argued that
effective AI recommendation systems strengthen consumer trust by providing transparent and relevant
recommendations that improve decision quality. Similarly, Wen and Li (2025) demonstrated that trust mediates
the relationship between AI responsiveness and purchase intention, suggesting that technological characteristics
influence consumer behavior through psychological mechanisms rather than direct effects alone.

The Mediating Role of Brand Trust
Brand trust represents consumers' confidence that a brand will consistently provide reliable products, accurate
information, and satisfactory experiences. In AI-enabled commerce, trust becomes particularly important because
consumers frequently depend upon  AI-generated recommendations when evaluating products. Although
technological characteristics directly influence consumer experiences, their ultimate impact on purchasing
decisions is often explained through consumers' psychological evaluations of brands.
Positive AI search experiences enhance consumers' confidence in digital interactions, thereby strengthening trust
toward brands recommended through AI -powered platforms. Likewise, credible AI -generated information
reinforces perceptions of honesty and reliability, encouraging consumers to develop greater confidence in brands.
Similarly, accurate and personalized recommendations demonstrate that brands understand consumers'
preferences and are capable of delivering relevant solutions, thereby fostering stron ger trust relationships.
According to Chaudhuri and Holbrook (2001), trust serves as an important psychological mechanism linking
marketing activities with favorable consumer outcomes. More recently, Wen and Li (2025) demonstrated that
trust mediates the relationship between AI responsiveness and purchase intention, while Liang (2026) found that
trust plays a central role in explaining how AI recommendation systems influence consumer purchasing decisions.
Based on the SOR framework, AI search experience, information credibility, and AI personalization quality
function as external stimuli that influence consumers' internal psychological state in the form of brand trust,
which subsequently affects purchase in tention. Therefore, brand trust is expected to mediate the relationships
between the technological characteristics examined in this study and consumers' behavioral intentions.

Research Gap
Although AI -powered technologies have received considerable attention within digital marketing and e -
commerce research, several important gaps remain. First, existing studies have primarily examined AI chatbots,
recommender systems, search engine marketing, and technology adoption independently, while limited research
has specifically investigated consumer behavior within the emerging context of Zero Click Commerce (Dharani &
Lavanya Latha, 2025; Duffett & Charles, 2025; Gong & Zhang, 2026). Second, previou s studies have generally
focused on individual technological factors such as AI personalization, website quality, information credibility, or

Credibility, AI Personalization Quality, Brand Trust, and Purchase Intention within a single conceptual framework
(Hu et al., 2025; ElSayad & Mamdouh, 2026; Mani et al., 2025). Third, although Brand Trust has been widely
recognized as a critical determinant of onlin e purchasing behavior, empirical evidence examining its mediating
role between AI-enabled technological characteristics and Purchase Intention remains limited, particularly within
AI-powered search environments and developing economies (Liang, 2026; Wen & Li, 2025). Furthermore, existing
AI-related studies have predominantly focused on developed markets, while empirical evidence from emerging
economies such as Pakistan remains scarce despite the country's rapidly expanding digital commerce ecosystem.
Recent studies have also highlighted the need for greater contextual evidence to understand how AI -driven
technologies influence consumer behavior across diverse markets and industries (Ashraf et al., 2025; Ghanad,
2023). Accordingly, this study addresses these theoretical and contextual gaps by examining the direct effects of
AI Search Experience, Information Credibility, and AI Personalization Quality on Purchase Intention while
investigating the mediating role of Brand Trust among Pakistani online consumers within the context of Zero Click
Commerce. This integrated framework contributes to the growing body of AI -enabled consumer behavior
literature by providing empirical evidence from an emerging economy where research on AI-assisted purchasing
decisions remains limited.

METHODOLOGY
Research Design
This study adopted a quantitative research approach to examine the relationships among AI search experience,
information credibility, AI personalization quality, brand trust, and purchase intention within the context of Zero
Click Commerce. A quantitative design was considered appropriate because it enables the collection of
standardized data from a large number of respondents and facilitates the statistical testing of hypothesized
relationships among variables (Creswell & Creswell, 2018). Furthermore, a cr oss-sectional survey design was
employed, whereby data were collected from respondents at a single point in time. This design is widely used in
consumer behavior and digital marketing research because it allows researchers to investigate behavioral
intentions and perceptions efficiently while minimizing time and resource constraints (Bloomfield & Fisher,
2019).
Population and Sampling
The target population of this study comprised Pakistani online consumers who have experience using AI-powered
search technologies while searching for products or services online. The study focused on individuals familiar
with AI -assisted search environment s because they possess the necessary experience to evaluate AI search
quality, information credibility, personalization, and brand trust.
A non-probability convenience sampling technique was employed to recruit respondents due to its suitability for
online consumer research and its effectiveness in reaching digitally active participants. Convenience sampling has
been extensively used in e -commerce and technology adoption studies because respondents can be accessed
efficiently through online platforms and social media networks (Etikan et al., 2016). A total of 380 valid responses
were collected, which was considered adequate for mediation analysis and hypothesis testing.
Instrument Development
Data were collected using a structured questionnaire comprising two sections. The first section gathered
respondents' demographic information, including gender, age, and education level. The second section measured
the study constructs using previously validated measurement scales adapted from established literature to ensure
content validity and reliability.
The questionnaire consisted of 25 measurement items distributed across five constructs. AI Search Experience
was measured using five items adapted from Prentice and Nguyen (2020). Information Credibility was measured
using five items adapted from Flanagin and Metzger (2000). AI Personalization Quality was measured using five
items adapted from Pu et al. (2011). Brand Trust was assessed through five items adapted from Chaudhuri and
Holbrook (2001), while Purchase Intention was measured using five items adapted from Dodds et al. (1991). All
measurement items were assessed using a five -point Likert scale ranging from 1 = Strongly Disagree to 5 =
Strongly Agree.
Data Collection Procedure
Primary data were collected through an online survey using Google Forms. The questionnaire link was distributed
through social media platforms, email, and online communication channels to reach consumers who regularly
engage in online shopping and AI -powered search activities. Before completing the questionnaire, respondents
were informed about the purpose of the study and assured that their participation was voluntary. Confidentiality
and anonymity were maintained throughout the data collection process, an d respondents provided informed
consent before participating. Only fully completed questionnaires were included in the final dataset for statistical
analysis.
Data Analysis
The collected data were analyzed using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) Version 27 and Hayes'
PROCESS Macro Version 4.2. Initially, descriptive statistics were computed to summarize respondents'

demographic characteristics. Reliability analysis was subsequently performed using Cronbach's alpha to assess
the internal consistency of each measurement scale, where values exceeding 0.70 were considered acceptable
(Hair et al., 2022).
Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA) was conducted to evaluate the construct validity of the measurement
instrument through the Kaiser–Meyer–Olkin (KMO) measure and Bartlett's Test of Sphericity. Pearson correlation

Finally, mediation analysis was conducted using Hayes' PROCESS Macro (Model 4) with bootstrapping procedures

Purchase Intention through Brand Trust. Statistical significance was determined at the 95% confidence level (p <
.05).
Ethical Considerations
The study adhered to established ethical principles governing academic research involving human participants.
Participation was entirely voluntary, and respondents were informed about the objectives of the study before
completing the questionnaire. No pers onally identifiable information was collected, and all responses were
treated confidentially and used solely for academic purposes. Participants were informed of their right to
withdraw from the survey at any stage without any consequences. The collected d ata were securely stored and
analyzed in aggregate form to protect respondents' privacy and ensure compliance with accepted research ethics
standards.

The findings of this study provide strong empirical support for the proposed conceptual framework and reinforce

The significant positive effect of A I Search Experience on Purchase Intention suggests that consumers who
experience efficient, responsive, and user-friendly AI search systems are more likely to purchase products online.
This finding is consistent with Prentice and Nguyen (2020), who reported that AI-enabled customer interactions
enhance consumer engagement and behavioral intentions, and with Armutcu (2026), who found that AI -
supported customer experiences positively influence purchasing behavior. Similarly, research on AI -enabled
immersive marketing and emerging digital technologies suggests that enhanced digital experiences positively
shape consumer attitudes and behavioral intentions in online environments (Gill et al., 2026). These findings
collectively indicate that positive AI -assisted s earch experiences strengthen consumers' confidence in digital
commerce and increase their willingness to make online purchases.
Information Credibility emerged as one of the strongest predictors of Purchase Intention, emphasizing the
importance of accurate and trustworthy AI-generated information in digital commerce. This finding supports the

CONCLUSION
Artificial intelligence (AI) has fundamentally transformed digital commerce by redefining how consumers search
for information, evaluate alternatives, and make purchasing decisions. The emergence of Zero Click Commerce
has further accelerated this transfor mation by enabling AI -powered search platforms to provide personalized

recommendations and comprehensive information without requiring consumers to visit multiple websites. As AI
increasingly assumes a central role in online shopping, understanding the technological and psychological factors
influencing consumer purchase inte ntion has become essential for both researchers and practitioners.
Accordingly, this study examined the effects of AI Search Experience, Information Credibility, and AI
Personalization Quality on Purchase Intention while investigating the mediating role of  Brand Trust among
Pakistani online consumers.
The empirical findings demonstrate that all three AI -related technological factors significantly influence
consumers' purchase intentions. Among the independent variables, AI Personalization Quality emerged as the
strongest predictor of Purchase Intention, indicating that consumers highly value personalized recommendations
that align with their preferences and shopping needs. Information Credibility also exhibited a strong positive
influence on Purchase Intention, h ighlighting the importance of reliable and trustworthy AI -generated
information in reducing uncertainty during online purchasing decisions. Although AI Search Experience
demonstrated a comparatively smaller effect, it remained a significant determinant of Purchase Intention,
suggesting that efficient and user -friendly AI search systems contribute positively to consumers' willingness to
purchase products through AI-enabled platforms.
The mediation analysis further revealed that Brand Trust significantly mediates the relationships between AI
Search Experience, Information Credibility, AI Personalization Quality, and Purchase Intention. These findings
indicate that technological capabilities alone are insufficient to maximize consumers' purchasing intentions unless
they also contribute to the development of trust toward the associated brands. The results therefore support the
Stimulus–Organism–Response (SOR) theory by demonstrating that A I-enabled technological characteristics
function as external stimuli, Brand Trust represents consumers' internal psychological state, and Purchase
Intention constitutes the resulting behavioral response. Overall, this study provides empirical evidence that  AI-
driven technological experiences and psychological trust jointly influence consumer behavior within the emerging
context of Zero Click Commerce.
Theoretical Implications
This study makes several important theoretical contributions to the growing body of literature on AI -enabled
consumer behavior and digital marketing. First, it extends the application of the Stimulus –Organism–Response
(SOR) theory by demonstrating how AI Search Experience, Information Credibility, and AI Personalization Quality
function as technological stimuli that influence consumers' behavioral intentions through Brand Trust. This
integrated perspective enhances understanding of the psychological processes underlying AI-assisted purchasing
decisions.
Second, the study contributes to the emerging literature on Zero Click Commerce by empirically examining
consumer behavior in AI -powered search environments. While previous research has primarily focused on AI
chatbots, recommender systems, or technology adoption independently, this study integrates multiple AI-related
constructs into a single conceptual framework, providing a more comprehensive explanation of purchase
intention.
Finally, the study contributes to the limited empirical literature on AI -powered digital commerce in developing
economies, particularly Pakistan. By examining AI-enabled purchasing behavior within the Pakistani context, the
findings broaden the geographical scope of AI marketing research and provide evidence that may support future
cross-cultural comparisons.
Practical Implications
The findings provide several practical implications for businesses, digital marketers, AI developers, and
policymakers. First, organizations should prioritize the development of intelligent search systems that provide
accurate, relevant, and responsive search experiences. Improving AI search functionality can enhance consumers'
shopping experiences and encourage stronger purchasing intentions.

Limitations of the Study
Despite its contributions, this study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, the study
employed a cross-sectional research design, which captures consumer perceptions at a single point in time and
therefore limits the ability to establ ish causal relationships. Longitudinal studies could provide greater insight
into how consumer perceptions of AI technologies evolve over time.
Second, data were collected using a convenience sampling technique from Pakistani online consumers. Although
this approach was appropriate for the research objectives, the findings may not be fully generalizable to all
consumer populations or different cultural contexts.
Third, the study focused exclusively on AI Search Experience, Information Credibility, AI Personalization Quality,
Brand Trust, and Purchase Intention. Other potentially important variables, such as perceived risk, privacy
concerns, AI transparency, consum er innovativeness, or perceived usefulness, were beyond the scope of this
research and may further explain consumer behavior within AI-enabled commerce.
Recommendations for Future Research
Future research should extend the present study by employing longitudinal research designs to examine changes
in consumer perceptions and purchasing behavior as AI technologies continue to evolve. Researchers may also
investigate AI -enabled consumer behavi or across different countries and cultural settings to compare the
generalizability of the proposed conceptual framework.
Future studies could incorporate additional variables such as perceived privacy, algorithm transparency,
perceived risk, AI literacy, consumer innovativeness, technology readiness, or customer satisfaction to develop a
more comprehensive understanding of AI-assisted purchasing behavior. Researchers may also compare different
AI-powered shopping platforms, including conversational AI assistants, AI chatbots, social commerce platforms,
and voice commerce applications.
Finally, future research may employ mixed -method or qualitative research designs to explore consumers'
experiences with Zero Click Commerce in greater depth. Such approaches could provide richer insights into the
cognitive and emotional processes underlying trust development and purchasing decisions in AI-powered digital
environments, thereby contributing to the continued advancement of AI marketing and consumer behavior
research.
