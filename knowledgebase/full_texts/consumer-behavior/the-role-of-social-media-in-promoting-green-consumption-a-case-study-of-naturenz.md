This research investigates the role of social media in
consumers’ green consumption behavior in Can Tho City. This
study surveyed 349 customers in the city using Naturenz
products. This study employs the Theory of Planned Behavior
(TPB) and Media Dependence Theory (MDT) to examine the
relationships among social media, media content, attitudes, and
subjective nor ms related to green consumption behavior. The
analytical methods used to analyze the data include measures of
scale reliability (Cronbach’s Alpha), EFA, CFA, SEM, and
ANOVA. Four determinants of green consumption were evident:
(1) social media, (2) media content, (3) attitudes towards green
consumption, and (4) subjective norms. Social media
particularly affects consumers’ awareness of green consumption
the most (β = 0.548). Research results indicate that attitudes and
subjective norms mediate green consumption behavior. This
research adds to the academic literature by developing a
consistent measurement scale for social me dia and green
consumer behavior. Furthermore, the paper offers managerial
implications to guide customers in Can Tho City to use Naturenz
products through their social media activities to promote green
consumption behavior.
1. Introduction
In 2021, Vietnam generated 8,021 tonnes of plastic waste per day (2.93 million tonnes
per year) according to the World-Wide Fund (WWF). E-commerce has increased the volume of
plastic packaging by 171,000 tons (World-Wide Fund [WWF], 2023). Waste statistics for these
numbers illustrate limited awareness of green consumption ( Ogiemwonyi et al., 2019 ). Thus,
for consumers and businesses , the importance of promoting green consumption has become
more recognized (Peattie, 2010). Green consumption is thus characterised by environmentally
responsible consumption, such as the use of organic products, clean energy, and recyclable
materials (Codini et al., 2018; Peattie, 2010). It has generated severe imbalances in ecosystems,
particularly in a time of ever -growing global economic expansion, lacking an appropriate
sustainable development orientation.
Among consumers, social media is an influential tool that can provide support for the
promotion of environmentally friendly products and business branding (Ottman, 2017). Vietnam

already had 78.44 million Internet users as of early 2024, and 92.7% of these users used at least
one social media platform. It indicates that with there being a large number of users, social
media becomes an important channel of communication in which businesses can communicate
and spread information and products widely (Rao, 2022). Therefore, social media is important
for communicating product and business information to consumers, helping them to decide on
what products will be recommended and make green decisions.
In addition, according to Wagdi et al. (2022), social media communication is considered
the most effective channel for consumers to know about businesses ’ products. In the case of
Naturenz, a DHG Pharma (Hau Giang Pharmaceutical Joint Stock Company) product that
emerged from a research effort to identify organic compounds with the potential to support
detoxification in contaminated individuals over the past  20 years, with assistance from the
Institute of Biotechnology (Nguyen, 2017). Naturenz is an innovative product that promotes the
eco-friendliness of green consumption among Vietnamese consumers. Thus, for an entry and a
competitive position in the market, strategic marketing on social media is required.
Although social media has increasingly played a pivotal role in facilitating sustainable
consumption, few studies have empirically examined the effects of social media and media
content on consumers’ attitudes, subjective norms, and green consumption behav ior in
Vietnam. While the majority of previously published studies have investigated green products
in general, only a small number have examined this relationship using combination theories.
In light of the literature gap, there is a need for research to understand the relationship between
social media and green product usage behavior. To address this, this research employs two
theoretical perspectives: the Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) and Media Dependence
Theory (MDT). The TPB describes the behavioral  framework used to account for attitudes
and subjective norms toward environmentally friendly consumption, and Media Dependence
Theory (MDT) provides a framework to explain the social impact of media exposure and
dependence on the public’s perceptions, att itudes, and actions, and how this influences
consumers. This combined approach could further elucidate the impact of social media on
perceptions and norms of green consumption by incorporating both theoretical perspectives.
Thus, this study assesses the ef fects of social media and media information on respondents’
attitudes and subjective norms toward green consumption. Concurrently, this paper discusses
implications for management regarding the enhancement of social media interventions to
promote green act ion. Naturenz is selected as a case study because it illustrates what green
consumption is meant to manifest in organic recipes, sustainable branding, and long -term
consumer trust. In addition, Naturenz’s active participation on social media is an appropriate
and practical platform for investigating the influence of social media in promoting sustainable
consumption in Vietnam and Can Tho City.
2. Literature review and theoretical model
Researchers have recently paid increasing attention to green consumption. Accordingly,
Bailey et al. (2016)  showed that green consumption values correlate to consumer attitudes
toward green marketing messages. Jalali and Khalid (2019)  and Wagdi et al. (2022)  also
examined the effect of social media marketing activities on green consumption behavior on
Facebook, TikTok, Twitter, YouTube, and Instagram. Rao (2022), Xie and Madni (2023), and
Armutcu et al. (2024)  also investigated and stressed the importance of social media in
stimulating the behavior toward green food consumption. Studies on green consumption are also

increasingly interested in Vietnam due to growing awareness of environmental protection and
sustainable development, especially in recent research circles. The majority of research on the
drivers of green consumption is concentrated on examining factors in fluencing green
consumption, as cited in Hoang et al. (2018).
Although this study provides important insights into green consumption, most of them
focus on analyzing the influence of behavior and media independently. Therefore, this paper
partially addresses the gap in previous studies by combining the Theory of Plan ned Behavior
(TPB) and Media Dependence Theory (MDT) to create a comprehensive picture that integrates
cognitive, normative, and media factors. Thereby, facilitating the joint exploration of cognitive,
normative, and media factors on green consumption decisions.
2.1. Social media
Social media has become an essential tool, which helps companies to improve the impact
of environmental communication, and consequently change consumers’ attitudes towards and
awareness of green products (Chen & Madni, 2023; Wagdi et al., 2022). Social media has also
facilitated the formation of online communities where individuals with environmental concerns
can connect ( Jalali & Khalid, 2019).  This connection helps increase the potential efficacy of
green consumer message delivery to consumers in the shortest, most efficient way. This may
affect consumers ’ positive perceptions of environmentally friendly consumption behaviors.
Combining the Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) and Media Dependence Theory (MDT), it
can be explained that social media serves as an information source and influences consumers’
beliefs (especially about sustainable products). Since consumers increasingly depend on social
media for environmental information and support from friends, exposure to social media will
amplify their beliefs, perceptions of value, and trust in eco -friendly brands like Naturenz. And
based on this, we propose the following hypothesis:
H1: Social media positively affects attitudes towards green consumption
Based on the above, social media plays an important role in the development of green
consumption norms. In this way, the information that environmental messages promote
environmentally responsible behaviours is reinforced by encouraging consumers to pursue
environmentally friendly habits ( Nekmahmud et al., 2022 ). Social media is thus an important
tool for increasing awareness of the current situation of globalisation, and it is both effective
and cost-effective for developing sustainable lifestyles (Chen & Madni, 2023; Jalali & Khalid,
2019). Based on the theories of TPB and MDT, it can be determined that individuals ’ reliance
on social media technology increases their exposure to social values and perceived social
expectations. Hence, subjective environmental norms emerge gradually. This is because
consumers may see environmental campaigns for Naturenz products when i nteracting with
social media, which may help them confirm consumption behaviours in line with green
behaviour. This hypothesis is formulated from the following information:
H2: Social media positively affects subjective norms
Furthermore, social media is considered an effective tool for spreading norms of
environmentally friendly behavior and for shaping consumer behavior towards green products.
According to the Protection Motivation Theory ( Rogers, 1975), providing information about
environmental issues through social media can arouse fear and motivate individuals to seek
solutions that contribute to environmental protection ( Cox, 2013). Based on TPB and MDT, it

can be seen that social media not only raises environmental awareness but also strengthens the

H4: Social media positively affects green consumer behavior
2.2. Media content
Subjective norms reflect the social pressure an individual feels from others regarding
whether to engage in a particular behavior ( Ham et al., 2015). Once consumers are exposed to
media content that emphasizes the benefits of green consumption and social media support, it
can strengthen individuals’ perceptions of green product use (Lin & Hsu, 2015). TPB and MDT
also explain that clear, credible media can help shape and reinforce beliefs about normative
values. Therefore, this study expects that when consumers are exposed to Naturenz content on
sustainability that highlights health and environmental benefits, they  are more likely to think
that green consumption is a good thing for society. Based on this rationale, the following
hypothesis is proposed:
H3: Media content positively affects subjective norms
Furthermore, in order to catch consumers’ attention on social media sites, media content
must align with target audiences’ interests ( Lee et al., 2018 ). This is beneficial because media
content stimulates consumer engagement by telling the product journey story. As one can see,
this is further enhanced when the media content focuses on positive aspects while also
contributing to sustainable societal dev elopment (Hoang et al., 2018).  Using TPB and MDT,
this study will explain how trustworthy, well -designed media content encourages consumers’
trust and emotional connection with the media message. This will improve consumers’ positive
attitudes and awareness of supporting green consumpt ion behaviors, which, in turn, drive
environmentally friendly actions. For Naturenz, regular exposure to clear, compelling
environmental messages enables consumers to turn their sustainability aspirations into actual
purchase behavior. Thus, by these lines of reasoning, the analysis posits that the media content
is expected to positively impact green consumer behavior:
H5: Media content positively affects green consumer behavior
2.3. Attitude towards green consumption
Consumer attitudes are the degree to which a specific behavior is perceived as good or
bad (Armutcu et al., 2024).  Green products come to the fore when consumers have a positive
attitude toward the importance of environmental protection (Bailey et al., 2016). From the TPB
perspective, attitude is a significant determinant of behavioral intention and a key contributor to
the likelihood of performing sustainable behaviors. The implication for Naturenz implies that
when customers perceive the product to be both b eneficial to the environment and improving
their health, their positive attitude will enhance the intention of purchase and support for green
products. As a result of these arguments, this study states the following hypothesis:
H6: Attitude towards green consumption positively affects green consumption behavior

2.4. Subjective norms
Social pressure can stem from many individuals surrounding a consumer, including
family members, friends, colleagues, or other groups they are likely to listen to (Armutcu et al.,
2024). Such individuals can nudge a person towards or away from green products. Some
research suggests that subjective norms lie between what consumers think about their own
benefits and limitations and their intention to buy green products. In TPB, subjective  norms
define the idea that important others want us to behave in a certain way. MDT adds that when
people see the same type of messages in the media, they may begin to believe that this is the
normal or expected choice. For Naturenz, if consumers frequent ly see positive messages about
green living and perceive that many people support sustainable products, they are more likely
to translate this social support into actual green purchases. From this idea, the following
hypothesis is proposed:
H7: Subjective norms positively affect green consumer behavior

3. Research method
3.1. Data collection methods
Data for this study were obtained from a consumer survey using a five-point Likert scale.
Because the study employed exploratory factor analysis (EFA), the sample size had to satisfy
basic requirements. Tabachnick and Fidell (2001) recommended at least 50 observations, ideally
100, and a sample of respondents for each measurement item of approximately 5:1. A minimum
number of approximately 130 respondents was observed with 26 variables. The analysis method
applied was also SEM. A sample size of 300, sufficient to maintain the minimum  level of this
methodology, is broadly appropriate for SEM (Tabachnick & Fidell, 2012).
Before starting the main survey, a small pilot test was conducted with 18 Naturenz
product consumers and 3 experts (Naturenz product managers at DHG Pharma) to assess the
clarity and appropriateness of the questions in the questionnaire (Table 1), ensuring  that they
were appropriate to the research context. The study then used a convenience sampling method
and focused only on people who had actually used Naturenz. The screening was based on
purchase history , and only those who had used the product within the previous six months
were included in the study. The direct survey was distributed at pharmacies, some retail stores ,
and some households in Can Tho City. For the online survey, the questionnaire was shared via
Zalo, email , and Facebook to reach current users. The results collected 349 valid responses
for use in this study .

3.2. Analytical methods
The study used data from 349 customers who used Naturenz products in Can Tho City
to apply data analysis methods. First, Cronbach’s Alpha coefficient was used to assess the
consistency and reliability of the items in each scale, including social media, media content,
attitude towards green consumption, subjective norms, and green consumption behavior.
Second, Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA) was performed to see how the observed variables
were grouped into factors. Then, Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA) was used to confirm the
measurement model and test convergent and discriminant validity. The proposed framework
applies Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) to test a set of hypotheses (H1 -H7). Finally,
analysis of variance (ANOVA) is used to determine whether green consumer behavior differs
across demographic groups such as gender, age, income, and education level.

Thus, the research results of the article have found that content and social media factors
have a strong impact on consumers’ attitudes, subjective norms , and green consumption
behavior towards Naturenz products in Can Tho City. First, the strong positive impact of social
media on consumers ’ attitudes towards green consumption (H1: β = 0.754, p < 0.01). This
result is consistent with the results of Wagdi et al. (2022), Chen and Madni (2023) , who stated
that social media is an important factor in raising environmental awareness and positive
attitudes of consumers towards sustainable products. The findings of this study confirmed that
social media use for Naturenz products not only contributes to increased environmental
awareness, but also promotes beliefs and perceived values through repeated exposure to
sustainability -related information.
Second, social media has a positive effect on subjective norms (H2: β = 0.760, p < 0.01).
This result is consistent with what Nekmahmud et al. (2022) found , that social media enables
collective norms, support, and even social acceptance of conscious behaviors. At the same time,
Jalali and Khalid (2019), Chen and Madni (2023) pointed out that shared environmental values
are cultivated in today’s digital communication environment. The current investigation adds
evidence to the findings that social media with which Naturenz products are linked provide a
sense of belonging to green purchases. This result supports MDT’s media dependence theory in
that users rely on social media for social validation and norm reinforcement.
Third, the study results also indicate that social media has a strong influence on green
consumer behavior (H4: β = 0.548, p < 0.01), similar to studies by Cox (2013) and Rogers (1975).
This study further adds to the behavioral motivation findings based on TPB-MDT theory integration,
finding that consumers who use more Naturenz online content are also more likely to convert
perceptions into actual purchase behavior. These findings provide empirical evidence supporting the
idea that engaging in digital activities can help bridge the gap between people’s pro-environmental
intentions and their actual behavior, an issue that has been little addressed in previous studies. The
effect of media content was smaller but still statistically significant for both subjective norms (H3:
β = 0.104, p < 0.05) and green consumption behavior (H5: β = 0.084, p < 0.05).

The findings also reveal that those with more positive attitudes toward green

As in Bailey et al. (2016) and Armutcu et al. (2024), individuals with more positive attitudes are
more likely to adopt sustainable behaviors. In this study, consumers who are convinced Naturenz
is healthy for them and not harmful to the environment are more likely to select it as a green
product. This aligns with the TPB, which proposes that positive assessments of environmental
conditions are directly related to intentions and green behavior.
Lastly, subjective norms also positively influence green consumer behavior (H7: β =
0.216, p < 0.01), compatible with Armutcu et al. (2024). The results of this study emphasize that
social influence has a significant effect on decisions to consume sustainably. This case study
extends previous work by linking social influence mechanisms to Social Influence Theory and
MDT, suggesting that rep eated exposure to Naturenz -related content on social media raises
consumers’ perceptions of social expectations around green consumption.
A one-way ANOVA was used to examine whether basic demographic factors influence
green consumption behavior. Results from the tests indicated that, for each variable tested, there
was no statistically significant difference between the groups with regard to green consumption
behavior (p > 0.05). The users of Naturenz products have comparable green consumption habits
by gender, age, income, and educational level. This indicates that perceptions about the
environment and attitude toward sustainable lifestyle a re influenced more by social media,
media messages, and personal beliefs than by demographic background. In reality, perhaps the
focus should be on the health and environmental messages and the social media communication
rather than primarily on demography.
5. Conclusion and managerial implications
The Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) integrated with Media Dependency Theory
(MDT) enables this study of how social media affects green consumer behavior. The findings
indicated that social media and media content have a positive effect on attitudes and subjective
norms, thereby promoting the use of environmentally friendly products. This way the paper can
convey that social media is not just a channel of communication, but something that can support
changes in consumer behavior. If we consider TPB and MDT together, the combination of the
two also implies that heavy use of social media platforms may improve consumers’ attitudes
towards green product adoption, which can favor the use of TPB to elucidate sustainable
consumer behavior. On the whole, the study presents a general insight into the relationship
between digital communication and media use and green consumption, and how social media
has a positive influence on environmental consciousness, as well as the purchase intention for
green products. From a practical perspective, Naturenz is a product that fits green consumption
as it uses organic ingredients and environmentally friendly packaging. The results identified
four primary antecedents that affect green consumption: (1) social media, (2) media content, (3)
attitudes toward green consumption, and (4) subjective norms. Based on these findings, the

report offers managerial suggestions for selecting Naturenz as a green product and for
encouraging green consumption among customers in Can Tho City.
First, the findings showed that social media contributes to affect consumers’ attitudes,
subjective norms (the extent to which they feel that important others expect them to behave in a
certain way), and green consumption habits for products of Naturenz. G iven this situation,
product communication should emphasize two primary aspects: Naturenz’s health benefits and
its role in environmental sustainability. Such messages need to mainly be promoted in the digital
channels, such as Facebook, Instagram, and TikTok, where customers tend to use the most. To
build consumer trust, the content can even emphasize the product’s unusual combination of
natural ingredients, its scientific testing, and the strict standards for production.
Second, the research reveals that Naturenz’s communication process significantly
affects the consumers’ subjective norms pertaining to green consumption. Communication
should not only inform but create a good connotation of the company’s social responsibil ity
among consumers, so that their choice of green products is perceived as encouraged for them.
Design of campaigns should be intentionally simple in nature, visually appealing and
stimulating as well as emphasizing Naturenz ’s clear green values. Credibility of messages
leads to higher levels of trust in the brand and higher levels of confidence in green
consumption behavior among consumers.
Third, the communication program should help consumers see Naturenz as a good green
health care option. Messages can say clearly that Naturenz is made from natural enzyme
ingredients and that it is safe and gentle to use. This links the product to health care choices that
are better for both people and the environment. The company can also work with environmental
or health organizations to make the information more trustworthy. One simple activity is to
collect used Naturenz packaging at pharmacies and give small health gifts in return. This shows
care for the environment and can encourage consumers to support green values.
Fourth, social influence and community activities can be used to support green
consumption. A referral program with small rewards for both current users and new users can
motivate people to talk about the product with friends and family. In addition, an on line group
for Naturenz users can be created. In this group, people can share their experiences, give each
other health tips, and discuss how they use Naturenz. Such simple interactions help users feel
closer to the brand and can support long-term loyalty.
Limitations and future research: This study examined how social media and media
content influence consumers’ green behavior through attitudes and subjective norms, but the
study still has certain limitations. First, the sample was collected only in Can Tho  City, so the
results may not be generalizable to other regions of Vietnam or to markets with different levels
of digital engagement. Second, the research focused on a single green product, Naturenz.
Consumers’ perceptions and behavioral responses may be d ifferent for other types of green
products. Future studies could extend the geographic scope to include several provinces or
regions and compare different green product categories. Doing so would help test whether the
TPB-MDT model remains consistent acros s contexts and would improve the generalizability
and theoretical strength of the findings.
