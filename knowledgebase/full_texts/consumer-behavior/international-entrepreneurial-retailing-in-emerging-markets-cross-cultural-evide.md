Abstract
Physical smartphone stores remain essential because consumers often examine devices, compare
models, test features, and evaluate retailer credibility before making a purchase. This research
investigates whether visual merchandising cues differently influence consumer purchase
intention and behavior in China and Bangladesh. Using the Stimulus-Organism-Response
framework, servicescape theory, cue-utilisation logic, and cross-cultural consumer behavior
insights, the study assesses how window display, floor layout, store exterior, and interior design
predict two key consumer outcomes. Data were collected from November 2025 to March 2026.
After screening, scale harmonization, and removing duplicates, 696 valid responses remained
248 from China and 448 from Bangladesh. Structural modeling with bootstrapped inference and
multi-group comparison was employed. Findings indicate that window display and interior
design influence purchase intention, while window display, exterior design, and interior design
impact buying behavior. Interior design has a stronger effect in Bangladesh; exterior design is
more relevant to behavior in China.

Keywords:  international entrepreneurship, entrepreneurial retailing, emerging markets, cross-
cultural consumer behaviour, smartphone specialty retailing, visual merchandising

1. Introduction
Digital retail has transformed the role of physical stores without diminishing their value. This
shift is especially clear with smartphone retailers. As high-involvement products, smartphones
prompt consumers to compare brands, examine screens, test cameras, ask about warranties, and
verify seller credibility before purchasing (Alexander & Varley, 2025; Breugelmans et al., 2023;
Hagtvedt & Chandukala, 2023). Therefore, physical stores serve more than just sales; they
provide reassurance and a space for assessment. Visual merchandising plays a crucial role: window
displays, store layout, exterior design, and interior design can influence consum er approach, store entry,
ease of shopping, perceived trust, and buying decisions (Basu et al., 2022; Szocs et al., 2023; Valentini et
al., 2024).  These cues go beyond aesthetics; they help consumers assess product availability,
professionalism, modernity, convenience, and credibility.
The initial study in China found that merchandising cues vary in their impact on different
consumer outcomes. The study showed that window display and interior design were more closely
associated with purchase intention, whereas exterior design was more closely  associated with buying
behaviour. This pattern is noteworthy both theoretically and practically, as it suggests consumers
rely on different cues during various stages of their shopping journey (Basu et al., 2022; Huang
& Wu, 2024).
This study aims to extend previous research by comparing Chinese and Bangladeshi retail
environments. The aim is not just to replicate the model elsewhere but to assess if the same
visual merchandising cues carry similar meanings in Asian contexts. This is important because
consumers’ interpretations of store cues can vary depending on factors such as market
development, service expectations, shopping habits, perceived risks, and trust in retail formats.
The study offers three main contributions. First, this present study integrates visual
merchandising into the context of physical smartphone retailing, where visual cues alone might
not suffice and consumers often prefer to physically experience the product. Second, this study
differentiates between purchase intention and actual buying behaviour, emphasising that not all
positive store cues lead to the same downstream effects (Banik, 2021; Pangarkar et al., 2022).
Third, the current study uses the country as a comparative variable to determine whether cue
effects are consistent across cultures or are context-dependent.

This study contributes to international entrepreneurship by examining how entrepreneurial
smartphone specialty retailers can adapt physical retail cues across emerging market contexts.
For small and growth-oriented retailers operating in international or cross-border retail
environments, store-level cues such as window display, exterior design, and interior design serve
as market-facing resources that help attract consumers, reduce uncertainty, and build credibility.
By comparing China and Bangladesh, the study shows that retail internationalisation requires
more than transferring a standardised store format across markets; it requires culturally
responsive adaptation of visual merchandising strategies.
Overall, the introduction frames the study within visual merchandising research and portrays it as
a cross-cultural extension rather than a replication. The core argument is that not all cues in
physical retail stores carry the same meaning across different markets. The core argument is that
physical-store cues may not carry the same meaning across different retail market s, especially in a high-
involvement product category such as smartphones (Basu et al., 2022; Hagtvedt & C handukala, 2023).
The paper examines how visual cues in China and Bangladesh impact consumers, assessing
whether they draw attention, aid product evaluation, and convey store credibility. The remaining
sections develop the theory, establish the conceptual framework and hypotheses, describe the
data and methods, present empirical findings, and explore the implications for theory and
practice.
2. Literature Review and Theoretical Background
2.1 Visual merchandising in smartphone physical retailing
Visual merchandising involves the strategic use of visible retail cues to direct attention,
communicate the store’s image, facilitate movement, and influence consumer decisions (Basu et
al., 2022). This study examines four key aspects: window display, floor layout, exterior design,
and interior design. This is particularly important in smartphone stores, where customers often
compare various features before making a choice. An eye-catching display can attract interest, a
well-organized layout can simplify browsing, a credible exterior can signal legitimacy, and a
comfortable interior can encourage product testing and evaluation (Hagtvedt & Chandukala,
2023; Raza et al., 2024; Szocs et al., 2023).
2.2 Stimulus-Organism-Response framework

The study is primarily guided by the Stimulus-Organism-Response (S-O-R) approach, adopted
from environmental psychology by Mehrabian and Russell (1974). The idea is that
environmental stimuli affect inner organismic states, which in turn shape approach or avoidance
responses (Shamim et al., 2024). In the current model, window display, store exterior design,
store interior design, and floor layout are defined as retail stimuli. These stimuli can trigger
internal assessments such as curiosity, comfort, trust, perceived store quality, perceived order,
product-evaluation confidence, and reduced uncertainty. While the study does not directly
measure organism-level mediators, S-O-R logic remains appropriate, as purchasing a smartphone
is not usually a purely automatic decision. Users typically evaluate and compare models, explore
features, and understand the store environment before forming purchase intentions or engaging
in buying behaviour.
2.3 Servicescape theory
Bitner (1992) introduced the servicescape concept to describe how the physical environment
affects customer and employee responses (Valentini et al., 2024). This theory suggests that
physical settings communicate both symbolic and practical messages before, during, and after a
service interaction. A smartphone store serves as an example of a servicescape, where  customers
observe products, try devices, and assess factors such as display arrangement, cleanliness,
service credibility, and the overall trustworthiness of the retail space. Consequently, both exterior
and interior design act as communication tools (Roggeveen et al., 2020; Szocs et al., 2023). A
well-maintained, professional exterior can convey legitimacy and competence, while a clean,
welcoming interior can foster product testing, lower perceived risks, and enhance the in-store
evaluation process.
2.4 Cue-utilization logic
Cue-utilization logic draws on consumer information-processing research, especially Cox (1962)
and Olson and Jacoby (1972), who proposed that consumers depend on available cues to assess
product or seller quality when they lack full information. This logic is relevant to retailing
smartphones because buyers cannot verify all technical, warranty, and service features before
purchasing. Before entering, they can rely on extrinsic cues such as window displays, signage,
storefront quality, and entrance layout (Basu et al., 2022; Breugelmans et al., 2023; Raza et al.,
2024). Inside, they focus more on layout, cleanliness, display coherence, product accessibility,

and comfort. The theory also explains the difference between purchase intention and actual
buying behavior, as some cues evoke interest and thought, while others serve as credibility
signals prompting action. Overall, cue-utilization supports viewing visual merchandising as a
multidimensional system rather than a single atmospheric factor.
2.5 Cross-cultural consumer behaviour
Hofstede’s comparative cultural research (1980, 2001) highlighting differences in values,
expectations, and social norms across countries is often linked to cross-cultural consumer
behavior (Blodgett et al., 2008). In marketing and retail, this suggests that the signals a store
sends may be interpreted differently across countries. Retail cues are adapted to local retail
culture, market maturity, trust levels, service quality, and knowledge of branded retail concepts
(Ameen et al., 2021; Banik & Gao, 2023). For example, exterior design might serve as a stronger
legitimacy cue in one market, while interior comfort and product organization might be more
persuasive in another. The China-Bangladesh comparison examines whether visual
merchandising effects are cross-cultural or universal.
The four theoretical perspectives are integrated to provide a coherent explanation of the proposed
model. S-O-R outlines the basic stimulus-response process; servicescape theory explains how the
physical retail environment communicates; cue-utilisation logic describes how consumers rely on
visual cues under uncertainty; and cross-cultural consumer behaviour explores why the
importance of cues may differ between China and Bangladesh. This theoretical framework
supports the hypotheses and cross-group comparisons.
Overall, these theories justify a model in which visual merchandising cues are not treated as
simple decorative elements. Instead, they operate as diagnostic signals that consumers interpret
during different stages of the shopping journey. S-O-R explains the broad stimulus-response
mechanism, servicescape theory explains why the physical environment communicates
credibility and comfort, cue-utilisation logic explains why consumers rely on visible cues under
uncertainty, and cross-cultural consumer behaviour explains why the strength of these cue effects
may vary between China and Bangladesh.
3. Hypothesis Development
3.1 Window display

The window display is a key visual merchandising element that many customers first encounter.
In a smartphone speciality store , it can showcase the latest products, the store’s image,
promotions, and brand relevance. The display should be both clear and eye -catching to spark
interest and curiosity, creating an impression of a modern, well-managed store and encouraging
customers to consider purchasing. It also influences buying behavio urs, such as enticing
customers to enter, prompting them to examine  devices closely, and transforming interest into
action (Basu et al., 2022; Hagtvedt & Chandukala, 2023; Raza et al., 2024).

H1: Window display has a positive effect on consumer purchase intention.
H5: Window display has a positive effect on consumer buying behaviour.
3.2 Floor layout
Floor layout represents the store ’s spatial organisation, including aisles, product zones, shelving,
counters and movement pathways. A functional layout will minimise search time, facilitate
comparison, and help consumers navigate the store without confusion or congestion. Because
smartphone shopping involves extensive searching, comparing, and engaging with multiple
models, an improved layout is likely to positively influence purchase intention (via convenience)
and purchasing behaviour (via more efficient product evaluation) (Breugelmans et al., 2023;
Roggeveen et al., 2020).
H2: Floor layout has a positive effect on consumer purchase intention.
H6: Floor layout has a positive effect on consumer buying behaviour.
3.3 Store exterior design
Store exterior design involves frontage, entrance organisation, signage and outward appearance.
These cues are understood before consumers fully engage with the retail outlet. Exterior design
can signal professionalism, legitimacy and trustworthiness, particularly for smartphone products,
where consumers are uncertain about authenticity, warranty or after-sales service (Breugelmans
et al., 2023; Valentini et al., 2024). Thus, a compelling exterior appearance can influence pre-
entry assessment and, potentially, consumers’ motivation to engage in purchase-related
behaviour.
H3: Store exterior design has a positive effect on consumer purchase intention.
H7: Store exterior design has a positive effect on consumer buying behaviour.

3.4 Store interior design
Store interior design involves cleanliness, layout, product presentation, comfort, and atmosphere.
This cue can be particularly significant for smartphone counters, as consumers will normally
spend a few minutes browsing, checking out various options, and talking to staff about the
phones and their features. Good interior design can reduce uncertainty, increase comfort during
evaluation, and enhance the feeling of value in shopping, which can lead to greater purchase
intention and buying behaviour (Hagtvedt & Chandukala, 2023; Scholdra et al., 2023; Szocs et
al., 2023).
H4: Store interior design has a positive effect on consumer purchase intention.
H8: Store interior design has a positive effect on consumer buying behaviour.
3.5 Cross-cultural differences
The final argument is that the strength of these relationships may vary between China and
Bangladesh. At the country level, variations in retail experience, trust cues, consumer
expectations, and shopping habits can change which aspects of visual merchandising are most
persuasive. That is, the store cue can have different diagnostic value across markets. The cross-
cultural hypotheses are thus used to examine the stability of the visual-merchandising model and
the possibility that some paths are stronger in one country than in the other (Alexander & Varley,
2025; Ameen et al., 2021; Banik, 2021).
H9: Country moderates the relationships between visual merchandising dimensions and consumer
purchase intention.
H10: Country moderates the relationships between visual merchandising dimensions and consumer
buying behaviour.
4. Conceptual Framework
The conceptual framework is constructed as a direct-effects model with a layer of cross-cultural
comparison. The four dimensions of visual merchandising are considered as environmental
stimuli and purchase intention and buying behaviour of consumers are considered as two
different response outcomes. H1 - H4 are the pathways between visual merchandising cues and
purchase intention, H5 - H8 are the pathways between visual merchandising cues and buying
behaviour and H9 - H10 are the cross-cultural comparison between China and Bangladesh.
Therefore, a country is not just a control variable but also a grouping variable that can influence
the strength of the structural relationships.

5. Methodology
5.1 Research design and setting

This study uses a quantitative cross-sectional survey design. The empirical context was physical
retailing with smartphones in China and Bangladesh. Data collection took place in and around
smartphone specialist retail spaces between November 2025 and March 2026. This design is
suitable because the study examines relationships between a number of latent perceptions and
two consumer response outcomes. A cross-cultural comparison necessitated keeping the same
basic visual merchandising items in both country data sets.
5.2 Data preparation
For this analysis, two country datasets were used. The China file contained the smartphone-store
indicator variables directly. The Bangladesh file included the same main blocks of indicators but
used different column naming conventions for constructs. Before the analysis, these names were
harmonised. No structural model was used that included non-comparable contextual fields. The
Bangladesh data were collected on a seven-point response scale and the China data were
collected on a five-point scale. To make the construct scores comparable, Bangladesh items were
linearly transformed to a one-to-five metric before construct scores were calculated.

5.3 Questionnaire construction and administration procedure

The questionnaire was structured sequentially for empirical reporting. The first page featured a
brief introduction to the study, a voluntary participation statement, and a note that respondents
should answer only regarding physical retail stores for smartphones. Screening questions
confirmed recent visits to or interest in smartphone specialty stores (Lewin et al., 2026). The
main questionnaire was divided into four sections: demographic profile, perceptions of visual
merchandising, purchase intention, and buying behavior. The question wording was customized
for the retail context of smartphones, avoiding references to fashion or online shopping.

Ambiguous language was removed, and the wording was kept straightforward for cross-country
administration. Multiple reflective indicators were utilized for each construct. The window
displays highlighted store image, attraction, promotions, and unplanned purchases. Floor layout
centered on product search, movement, and shelving zones. Exterior design elements included
storefront creativity, signage, and entrance setup. Interior design encompassed smartphone
model displays, cleanliness, spacing, and comfort. The constructs of purchase intention and
buying behavior were preserved as separate entities.
For the intended comparative questionnaire, all construct items should be measured on a five-
point Likert agreement scale ranging from 1 = strongly disagree to 5 = strongly agree. In the
available empirical files, China’s responses were already recorded on a 1 -5 metric, whereas
Bangladesh’s responses were recorded on a 1 -7 metric. For comparability, Bangladesh scores
were linearly transformed to the 1-5 metric using X5 = 1 + (X7 - 1) x (4/6) (Guenther et al.,
2023; Podsakoff et al., 2024). This transformation preserves the rank order and aligns the lower
and upper endpoints of the scales, but it should be reported as a harmonisation procedure rather
than as evidence that the original questionnaires used identical scales.
The scores for each construct were calculated as the average of the harmonised items. Likert
indicators, considered as ordered agreement responses, were combined into composite scores, a
common practice in survey-based marketing research. Since Likert-type data may not follow a
normal distribution, structural estimates were interpreted using bootstrapped standard errors and
confidence intervals, rather than relying solely on normal-theory assumptions. To maintain
consistency and avoid rescaling, the same five-point response scale used during data collection
should be employed in future replication studies in both countries (Chabrawi et al., 2025).
5.4 Measures
There are six reflective constructs in the measurement model: window display, floor layout, store
exterior design, store interior design, consumer purchase intention and consumer buying
behaviour. Construct scores were calculated after scale harmonisation as the mean of the retained
indicators. In this way, the scoring remains transparent and weights are not assigned that change
depending on the distribution of responses in a cross-country data set.

5.6 Analytical Strategy and uses Software

The empirical analysis followed a composite-based structural modelling workflow using
harmonised construct scores. Data preprocessing involved screening for missing values,
duplicate records, and scale inconsistencies (Esaki & Ikeda, 2026; Hair et al., 2012). Next,
descriptive statistics and sample profiles were generated. The quality of measurements was
assessed using Cronbach’s alpha, composite reliability, average variance extracted, item
loadings, Fornell-Larcker diagnostics, and HTMT. Standardized structural paths were then
estimated with Statsmodels’ ordinary least squares (OLS) using standardized construct scores,
non-parametric bootstrap standard errors, and confidence intervals (Vasileiou et al., 2018).
Multi-group path-difference tests were performed to compare country-specific models. The
workflow utilized pandas and NumPy for data handling, SciPy for statistical tests, Scikit-learn
for component loadings, Statsmodels for structural paths and VIF diagnostics, Matplotlib and
Seaborn for static visualizations, Plotly for interactive diagnostics, and a recorded, reproducible
Python environment via Requests. Web data were not included (Dybro Liengaard, 2024;
Guenther et al., 2023; Podsakoff et al., 2024).

Equation (1). Scale harmonisation
x* = 1 + (x - 1) × 4 / 6
Equation (1) presents the linear transformation used to place Bangladesh’s seven-point item s on the same one-
to-five metric as the China items. The transformation preserves item order and relative spacing while making
country means comparable.
Equation (2). Composite construct score
ηᵢⱼ = (1 / kⱼ) Σₘ x*ᵢⱼₘ
Equation (2) defines the construct score for respondent i and construct j. The score is the arithmet ic mean of

xᵢⱼₘ = λⱼₘηᵢⱼ + δᵢⱼₘ
Equation (3) presents the concept of reflective measurement. In this model, every observed item serves as an

CPIᵢc = β₀c + β₁cWDᵢc + β₂cLOᵢc + β₃cSEDᵢc + β₄cSIDᵢc + εᵢc

Equation (5). Buying-behaviour structural equation by country
CBBᵢc = γ₀c + γ₁cWDᵢc + γ₂cLOᵢc + γ₃cSEDᵢc + γ₄cSIDᵢc + νᵢc
Equation (5) uses the same approach to model consumer buying behaviour. Estimating the two

Equation (6) represents the moderation logic. BD equals 1 for Bangladesh and 0 for China. The

CR = (Σλₘ)² / [(Σλₘ)² + Σ(1 − λₘ²)]
Equation (7) calculates composite reliability from standardized item loadings. Values near or

Equation (8). Average variance extracted
AVE = Σλₘ² / k
Equation (8) provides the average variance extracted (AVE), which assesses if a construct
accounts for enough variance in its indicators. Typically, values near .50 are deemed acceptable.

HTMTₐᵦ = mean(|rᵢⱼ|, i ∈ a, j ∈ b) / √[mean(|rᵢⱼ|, i ≠ j ∈ a) × mean(|rᵢⱼ|, i ≠ j ∈ b)]
Equation (9) contrasts cross-construct item correlations with within-construct item cor relations, where lower

VIFₘ = 1 / (1 − R²ₘ)
Equation (10) assesses multicollinearity among predictors. R†m is obtained by regressing predi ctor m on the

SE (β̂ ₘ) = SD (β̂ ₘ¹, β̂ ₘ², ..., β̂ ₘᴮ)
Equation (11) defines the bootstrap standard error used for structural inference. Bootstrap

Δₘ = β̂ , Bangladesh − β̂ , China
Equation (12) shows the country difference on a structural path. A positive value indicates the pat h is more

showed higher but still interpretable predictor overlap (4.499-6.918) (Podsakoff et al., 2024).

7.3 Measurement model assessment
The measurement model was appropriate for an exploratory cross-cultural retail study (Batat,
2024; Wang et al., 2023). In the pooled sample, Cronbach’s alpha ranged from .826 to .891, the
composite reliability ranged from .897 to .932, and the AVE ranged from .733 to .821. In China,
alpha ranged from .552 to .705, composite reliability from .771 to .837, and AVE from .490 to
.631, indicating acceptable but somewhat modest measurement strength. For Bangladesh, alpha
ranged from .887 to .937, composite reliability from .930 to .960, and AVE from .816 to .889,
demonstrating very strong internal consistency. These metrics are adequate for proceeding to

8. Discussion
The findings offer a more detailed understanding of visual merchandising in physical smartphone
retailing. The pooled model indicates that window displays and interior design play a more
significant role in influencing purchase intentions. This supports the idea that consumers’
intentions are formed when they are attracted to the store and feel comfortable evaluating
products. Additionally, exterior design becomes more important regarding buying behavior,
suggesting that outward legitimacy and visible store credibility can influence consumers,
especially as they move closer to making a purchase decision (Basu et al., 2022; Hagtvedt &
Chandukala, 2023; Valentini et al., 2024).
The results are more evident at the national level. In China, store interior design significantly
predicted purchase intention (beta = .196, p = .025), whereas store exterior design was the
strongest significant predictor of buying behaviour (beta = .338, p < .001). This suggests that the
store interior can influence product evaluation, while the exterior may serve as a cue to trust or
legitimacy during purchase. In Bangladesh, interior design was the most influential predictor of
purchase intention (beta = .741, p < .001), and it also remained the key factor in buying behavior
(beta = .448, p < .001); additionally, window display was significant in both contexts. This
indicates that the internal shopping environment may be more significant for Bangladeshi
consumers, possibly because it provides comfort, cleanliness, space, and effective product
presentation, thereby fostering a more trustworthy and easier-to-evaluate shopping atmosphere.
The main structural findings were not statistically significant with respect to floor layout.
However, this doesn’t imply that layout isn’t important; it can be a crucial enabling element.
Although a poor layout might affect customer experience, when considering window displays,
exterior, interior design, and overall store arrangement, layout alone may not fully explain
customer perceptions. In smartphone stores, more emphasis might be placed on visible
professionalism, store comfort, and product presentation beyond just the layout.
The results also highlight the importance of distinguishing between purchase intention and actual
buying behavior. Without considering intention, exterior design might appear less impactful.
However, focusing only on buying behavior would give an incomplete view, as it would miss the
influence of attitudes toward window and interior design. Therefore, the dual-outcome model

offers a more accurate understanding of how visual merchandising affects shoppers during the
entire shopping process.
9. Implications
9.1 Theoretical implications
This study advances S-O-R theory by showing that stimuli in physical stores can have outcome-
specific effects in high-involvement technology retailing. Window displays and interior design
primarily influence intention formation, whereas exterior design is more closely linked to
behaviour-oriented responses. This suggests researchers should be careful not to treat all retail
cues as equally impactful or assume that the same stimuli affect both intention and behaviour.
The findings also enrich the theory of servicescape in the context of technology retail by
demonstrating that the smartphone store is not only a transaction space but also an evaluation
space. The servicescape conveys professionalism, product authenticity, convenience for
comparison, and the retailer’s credibility. This takes the servicescape concept away from
hospitality and general retail and into a product category where consumers try and compare a
product’s technical attributes before buying (Breugelmans et al., 2023; Hagtvedt & Chandukala,
2023; Shamim et al., 2024).
The results support the cue-utilisation theory because consumers use different cues at various
points in their shopping journey. Window displays and exterior design serve as pre-entry cues
that shape consumers’ expectations, whereas interior cues play a more significant role in
evaluating smartphones inside the store. This distinction is useful for future research that
separates attraction, evaluation, and trust cues.
The cross-cultural findings suggest that visual merchandising effects vary by culture. Evidence
from Bangladesh and China shows that interior design has a stronger influence in Bangladesh,
while exterior design plays a more significant behavioral role in China. These differences
indicate that retail cues are shaped by local market contexts, shopping habits, and trust
expectations. This highlights the need for multi-group retail studies, rather than assuming that
store-atmosphere models can be directly applied across different countries.
The findings extend international entrepreneurship research by showing that market adaptation in
international retailing is not limited to product, price, or distribution decisions. Store-level visual

cues also operate as entrepreneurial market-entry resources because they help new or expanding
retailers gain consumer trust in culturally different environments.
9.2 Managerial implications
Store design should be tailored to the specific needs of smartphone retailers, rather than a one-
size-fits-all approach. The findings show that different visual merchandising cues serve diverse
strategic purposes: window displays attract attention, store layout aids navigation, exterior design
builds credibility, and interior design support product evaluation. Retailers should invest in
design elements aligned with their desired retail impact. Focus should be placed on window
displays and interior design to foster customer intention. Window displays need to highlight
newness, relevance to ongoing promotions, product availability, and brand appeal. Meanwhile,
interior spaces should enable comparison by maintaining cleanliness, providing appropriate
lighting, ensuring sufficient spacing, ensuring good product visibility, and ensuring comfort.
These aspects are especially important in smartphone sales, as purchase decisions are often
preceded by careful consideration and confidence-building (Iyer et al., 2020; Pappas et al., 2023;
Scholdra et al., 2023).
Exterior design should also be considered critical for influencing customer behavior, especially
in China. Trust signals such as storefront appearance, signage, entrance layout, and visible
professionalism can build credibility. Before customers enter, the store’s exterior should
communicate reliability, authenticity, and a commitment to after-sales service, which is
particularly vital for high-involvement technology products.
Retailers in Bangladesh should place particular emphasis on the in-store shopping experience.
The results show that cleanliness, product visibility, spacing, and comfortable display
arrangements are important for both intention and behaviour. A crowded or poorly organised
store may make smartphone evaluation difficult and reduce consumer confidence. Therefore,
retailers should ensure clear product zones, visible model displays, adequate testing space, and a
tidy environment that supports comparison and reassurance. Hence, layout should be treated as
an enabling part of the overall store experience rather than as a standalone driver of purchase
response.

For entrepreneurial smartphone retailers expanding across emerging markets, the findings
suggest that standardised retail design may be insufficient. Retail entrepreneurs should adapt
interior and exterior cues according to local trust expectations, consumer evaluation habits, and
market maturity.
10. Limitations and Future Research
The study’s data are cross-sectional survey data, so caution is needed when interpreting claims
about causal relationships. The results show associations consistent with theory but do not
establish experimental causality. The samples are not representative of the general population, so
findings should be generalized cautiously beyond the surveyed respondents. The Bangladesh
dataset had seven response points, and the China dataset had five. To compare them, linear scale
harmonization was performed, but future research should use the same response format from the
outset. Because the China dataset required substantial duplicate removal, the China-specific
estimates should be interpreted cautiously and should be verified with a cleaner independently
collected sample.Future fieldwork should ensure no repetition during data collection. The
Bangladesh data showed more evidence of the single factor tendency, which may indicate
common method variance or a general pattern of positive responses. Future research should
include procedural remedies, marker variables, or time-separated measurements. In this study,
composite scores and bootstrapped structural paths were used. Future research could re-estimate
the model with a full PLS-SEM package, as well as latent-variable multi-group analysis and
MICOM permutation testing (Dybro Liengaard, 2024; Podsakoff et al., 2024).
The mediators of the organism level, including trust, perceived product authenticity, shopping
enjoyment, perceived risk reduction, and product-evaluation confidence, should be included in
future research.
11. Conclusion
This study examined how visual merchandising influences consumer s’ purchase intentions and
buying behaviour in smartphone-speciality stores in China and Bangladesh. The findings
highlight that while visual merchandising is crucial, it varies across contexts. In the combined
analysis, window displays and interior design predicted purchase intention, whereas window
display, exterior design, and interior design influenced buying behavior. Country-specific results
showed differences in how store cues are utilized, China demonstrated more consistent

behaviour with exterior design, while Bangladesh showed stronger reliance on interior design.
Overall, the study confirms that visual merchandising strategies are culturally dependent.
Retailers should tailor their in-store environment based on the desired consumer response and the
specific market they serve.
