Qianyue Huang

Abstract. Existing studies of POP MART mainly explain purchasing and brand diffusion
through blind-box uncertainty, gamification, and platform circulation, while paying less
attention to how product form, channel arrangements, and communication environments
enable multiple consumption contexts over time. Drawing on POP MART's 2020
prospectus, annual-results announcements for 2020-2025, and external archival sources, this
study adopts a longitudinal archival case-study design and develops an integrative
framework of action, space, and visibility. Visibility is differentiated into the material
conditions for consumer-carried visibility, its realized enactment in specific settings, and
firm-orchestrated visibility. The case shows three broad periods: the digitalization of
purchasing and elevation of collection, the expansion of content and experiential spaces, and
changes in material structure accompanied by differentiated visibility. Growth in plush
revenue demonstrates the rising commercial importance of plush categories and tactile
materials, but does not by itself establish company-wide portability. Miniature pendants
indicate that attachment and portability became a visible design direction, while their
revenue contribution remains undisclosed. The study therefore offers a bounded process
explanation of consumption-context expansion without treating corporate disclosures as
direct evidence of general consumer behavior or causal revenue effects.
Keywords: POP MART, visual IP, consumption-context expansion, product affordances,
longitudinal archival case study
1. Introduction
Existing research on POP MART mainly takes three approaches. The first examines blind-box
uncertainty. Studies suggest that unknown outcomes may influence purchase intention or impulse
buying through emotional value, curiosity, and perceived luck, but the effect is not unconditional [1,
2]. The second examines digital purchasing tools such as Pop Draw. Practical, social, and
experiential motives may all relate to brand loyalty, although practical motives show more stable
explanatory power in combined models [3]. The third discusses unboxing, imitation, scarcity, and
crowd cues around Labubu on digital platforms. Some of this work is review-based and cannot
replace representative consumer surveys [4].

These studies help explain why consumers buy blind boxes, how digital platforms organize
purchasing, and how popular IPs gain attention. They say less about how product form, channel
functions, and public exposure change together over time. This study therefore asks: how has POP
MART used continuing changes in product form, channel arrangements, and communication
environments to open potential consumption contexts beyond blind-box draws and private
collection? Here, a consumption context includes what consumers may do with a product, the
physical or digital spaces an IP may enter, and the possibility that others may see and circulate the
IP. The study contributes a longitudinal process view, an action-space-visibility framework, and
explicit evidence boundaries that separate company disclosures from consumer behavior and causal
claims.
The contribution is deliberately bounded. First, the paper treats product, channel, and
communication changes as a connected process from 2020 to 2025 rather than as a list of separate
marketing tactics. Second, it separates consumption-context expansion into action, space, and
visibility and distinguishes the material conditions for consumer-carried visibility from actual
enactment and from firm-orchestrated visibility. Third, it uses source criticism, external event
checks, and negative cases to avoid turning corporate narratives into claims about general consumer
behavior or revenue causality.
2. Analytical framework and research design
Traditional brand-extension research asks whether an existing brand can enter a new category and
how category fit shapes evaluation [5]. Consumption-context expansion asks related but different
questions: what can consumers do with a new product, where can the IP appear, and does the
product change the IP's social visibility? Adding colors or similar designs may increase SKUs
without changing the consumption context. Moving from fixed figurines to small pendants that can
attach to bags or phones may change use, location, and display conditions.
The action dimension draws on product-affordance research. Materials can enable or limit actions
such as touching, moving, storing, and displaying an object, while consumers still need to realize
these affordances in specific situations [6]. Traditional figurines mainly allow viewing and fixed
display; large MEGA products increase the scale of collecting; plush materials add tactile and
movement possibilities; and small pendants further allow attachment and carrying. These design
possibilities do not mean that consumers will necessarily use products in the way the company
expects, because imagined affordances and actual experience may not match [7].
The space dimension concerns the environments where an IP appears. Phygital customer-journey
research shows that consumers may move repeatedly between digital platforms and physical spaces
[8]. Pop Draw, livestreams, stores, exhibitions, and POP LAND can therefore be treated as
connected consumption environments rather than isolated channels. The visibility dimension
concerns how an IP becomes visible to others. Platformized possessions show that photos, videos,
and other digital content can carry personal meaning while being shaped by platform functions and
commercial logics [9]. Livestream research further shows that information richness, interactivity,
visual vividness, social presence, and IT affordances can structure participation and engagement [10,
11]. In this study, these findings explain channel mechanisms rather than prove POP MART
consumer responses.
Within visibility, consumer-carried visibility and firm-orchestrated visibility are distinguished.
Consumer-carried visibility has two levels. Its material condition means that size, weight, clips, or
wearable structures make public carrying physically possible; company product information can
show this possibility but not adoption. Realized consumer-carried visibility means that consumers

actually attach an IP to bags, clothing, phones, or other personal items in public settings; individual
media cases can show that the behavior exists but not how common it is. Firm-orchestrated visibility
refers to exposure actively arranged by the company through theme parks, exhibitions, livestreams,
fashion events, public activities, and collaborations. These forms may reinforce one another but
cannot substitute for one another.
Based on these distinctions, firm-driven consumption-context expansion is defined as a process in
which a firm changes product form, channel arrangements, and communication environments to
create new possibilities for action, new consumption spaces, and new conditions of visibility for the
same visual IP. The three dimensions can change separately or reinforce one another; the framework
does not impose an untested rule that a fixed number of dimensions must change before expansion
has occurred.
The study uses a longitudinal archival case-study design covering POP MART's IP product and
channel system from 2020 to 2025. Organizational source criticism is used to distinguish reportive
facts, such as revenue figures and opening dates, from performative company statements about
consumer love or emotional connection [12]. Temporal bracketing is used to divide the process into
analytically meaningful periods [13], while case boundaries, a chain of evidence, and competing
explanations follow standard case-study principles [14]. The primary archive consists of the 2020
prospectus and annual-results announcements for 2020-2025 [15-21]. External media and platform
records are used to confirm selected product launches, public events, and channel settings [22-27].
The analysis compares changes in action, space, consumer-carried visibility, and firm-orchestrated
visibility, and uses negative or inconsistent evidence to limit the strength of the explanation.
External checks confirm events and context; they do not independently re-audit the company's
financial data or measure overall consumer behavior.
The analysis followed four steps. First, an annual timeline was built for product forms, channel
functions, membership indicators, experiential projects, and revenue structure. Second, reportive
facts and company self-descriptions were separated and linked to the relevant archival pages. Third,
changes were compared across years using the four analytical categories. Fourth, inconsistent
evidence was used to test the main explanation, including low growth in 2022, the decline of Pop
Draw in 2023, and the inability to separate portable-product revenue from total plush revenue. This
procedure keeps the process interpretation tied to evidence that can support it.
The design remains a single-company case. External records can balance some parts of the
company's own narrative, but the core timeline and product revenue structure still come from POP
MART disclosures. Selective disclosure therefore cannot be ruled out, and the periodization should
be read as an analytical device rather than an official corporate stage structure.
3. Longitudinal case analysis
POP MART's consumption-context expansion can be divided roughly into three periods: the
digitalization of purchasing and expansion of collection levels, the expansion of content and
experiential spaces, and changes in material structure together with differentiated visibility.
Digitalization of purchasing and expansion of collection levels. In 2020, Pop Draw generated
RMB 466.364 million in revenue, up 72.0% year on year [16]. In 2021, revenue rose to RMB
898.014 million, up 92.6%, while registered members reached 19.58 million [17]. These figures
show the growing importance of digital purchasing and account-based consumer access, but
membership growth cannot be treated directly as evidence of loyalty or community formation. Pop
Draw moved product selection, drawing, purchasing, and result reveal from fixed stores to mobile
devices. The clearest changes were therefore in action and space rather than public product visibility.

The MEGA COLLECTION, launched in 2021, generated RMB 178.1 million in its first year [17].
Its large size and lottery-based purchasing expanded the scale and display space of collecting rather
than everyday portability.
Expansion of content, platforms, and experiential spaces. In 2022, the company recorded revenue
of RMB 4.617324 billion, up only 2.8% year on year, while operating profit fell by 49.3% [18]. This
is an important negative case: product and experience expansion did not necessarily produce
immediate high financial growth. During this period, the company developed articulated alloy
figures, sculptures, art exhibitions, and digital immersive experiences, moving visual IPs beyond
blind boxes and display cabinets into artistic and virtual spaces [18]. Company statements about
emotional connection, however, remain managerial interpretations rather than consumer evidence.
In 2023, Douyin revenue reached RMB 283.318 million, up 431.2% year on year, while Pop
Draw revenue fell by 23.6% to RMB 729.28 million [19]. The contrast suggests a change in the role
of online channels: livestreaming became more prominent for product explanation and content-
based presentation, while the earlier digital-draw channel weakened. General livestream research
helps explain how visual vividness, interaction, guidance, and social presence can organize such
experiences [10, 11], but it does not establish that livestreaming alone caused POP MART's sales
changes. In September 2023, POP LAND opened in Chaoyang Park in Beijing. Company
disclosures describe IP-themed settings, character interaction, themed food and beverage services,
and limited products [19], while external media confirmed the opening, the approximately 40,000-
square-meter site, and its retail, dining, and interactive facilities [22]. The clearest change in this
period was firm-orchestrated visibility: livestreams, exhibitions, and the theme park placed IPs in
public content and urban experiential spaces.
Changes in material structure and differentiated visibility. In 2024, plush products generated
RMB 2.832078 billion, accounting for 21.7% of total revenue, and increased by 1,289.0% from
2023 [20]. These figures show that plush became commercially important and that soft tactile
materials gained weight in the product structure. They do not show that portable products had
become dominant or that consumers generally used plush products for public display.
In 2025, plush revenue rose to RMB 18.708074 billion, or 50.4% of total revenue, while figure
toys accounted for 32.4% [21]. This establishes a major shift in product-category structure, but not a
company-wide shift toward portability. The annual-results announcement does not disclose separate
revenue shares for large plush toys, small vinyl plush products, bag pendants, and phone pendants.
Direct evidence for portability instead comes from product form. The Mini LABUBU "Pin for
Love" series used a smaller format that the company described as lighter and easier to integrate into
daily life [21]. Associated Press independently confirmed small vinyl plush pendants and noted that
POP MART had been selling plush keychain pendants since 2023 [23]. A cautious conclusion is
therefore that miniaturization, attachment, and portability became visible design directions within
plush products, while their revenue contribution remains unknown.
For consumer-carried visibility, the material condition is clear: smaller size and clip structures
make attachment to bags, phones, or clothing possible. Actual enactment requires separate evidence.
ITV described Labubu as a fashion accessory and recorded public examples of its use as a bag
charm [24]. Such observations show that the behavior occurred in some settings, not its population-
level adoption rate. Firm-orchestrated visibility developed in parallel. The company reported THE
MONSTERS in fashion and media activities and at the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade [21];
Associated Press independently confirmed a POP MART float featuring Labubu and other characters
in the 2025 parade [25]. At the platform level, TikTok Newsroom listed a POPMART UK Labubu
restock livestream [26], and Reuters reported Pop Mart products being unboxed and sold in a

4. Discussion
The main theoretical contribution of this study is not a new theory already validated with
measurement scales. Instead, it offers an integrated process framework for examining visual IP
companies. Brand-extension research asks what categories a firm enters. Consumption-context
expansion asks what actions a product changes, what environments the IP enters, and who creates its
public visibility. The distinction between consumer-carried and firm-orchestrated visibility prevents
all public exposure from being interpreted as consumer identity expression. Within consumer-carried
visibility, separating material conditions from actual enactment also keeps product design evidence
distinct from evidence of consumer use.

The 2024–2025 evidence further shows why material change, category growth, and portability
should be discussed separately. Plush revenue rose from 21.7% to 50.4% of total revenue [20, 21],
showing a major change in the commercial importance of soft materials. Mini LABUBU pendants
show a parallel move toward smaller and attachable products [21, 23]. Because portable-product
revenue is not disclosed, however, the analysis cannot claim that portability was the main reason for
plush growth. The more precise process is that plush materials expand tactile and movement
possibilities; miniaturization and clips further expand attachment and carrying; and livestreams and
public events expand firm-orchestrated visibility.
Blind boxes remain important as an entry and amplification mechanism. They organize
anticipation, reveal, series comparison, and unboxing content, while prior research shows that the
effect of uncertainty depends on emotional value, curiosity, and perceived luck rather than operating
under all conditions [1, 2]. At the same time, the case cannot rule out alternative explanations such
as supply capacity, overseas store expansion, platform recommendations, celebrity exposure, and the
popularity of leading IPs. In 2025, THE MONSTERS alone generated RMB 14.161064 billion, or
38.1% of total revenue [21]. This concentration shows how a leading IP can scale quickly and also
makes it inappropriate to attribute company growth to consumption-context expansion alone.
From a management perspective, visual IP firms should not evaluate innovation only by the
number of new SKUs or category revenue. More useful questions are what actions a material makes
possible, whether size and structure allow products to enter new environments, how the forms of
public visibility examined here are generated through consumer carrying and firm-organized
exposure, and whether those possibilities are actually realized. Large collectibles, plush toys, small
pendants, theme parks, and livestream channels perform different functions and should not be
judged by one broad assumption that more exposure or more portability is always better.
5. Conclusion
Using POP MART's 2020 prospectus, annual-results announcements for 2020–2025, and external
archival sources, this study examines how product form, channel arrangements, and communication
environments helped expand the potential consumption contexts of visual IPs. The case shows three
broad periods: digital purchasing and expanded collection levels; content, platform, and
experiential-space expansion; and material change accompanied by differentiated visibility. The core
explanation is that product materials and structures change possible actions, channel arrangements
change the spaces in which an IP appears, and, within the forms examined here, public visibility
develops through both consumer carrying and company-arranged exposure.
This explanation remains bounded. Plush revenue proves a change in material and category
structure, not company-wide portability; the revenue share of small portable products is undisclosed.
Company archives can show that the firm provided and promoted new contexts, but cannot measure
how widely consumers adopted them or what meanings they attached to them. Product and channel
changes also occurred alongside other drivers of growth, so the study does not identify a net causal
effect. Future research could use consumer interviews, reproducible sampling of public platform
posts, or comparisons with other visual IP firms to test how action affordances and the two forms of
visibility are realized on the consumer side.
