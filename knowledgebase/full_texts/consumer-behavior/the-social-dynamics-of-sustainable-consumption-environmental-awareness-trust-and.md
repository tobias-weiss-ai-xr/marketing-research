ABSTRACT
This study examines the social dynamics of sustainable consumption by investigating the
influence of environmental awareness and customer trust on consumer loyalty in eco-friendly
markets. The study addresses the importance of sustainable consumption and how consumers’
environmental perceptions and relational evaluations contribute to loyalty toward responsible
products and brands. An explanatory quantitative research design was employed. Data were
collected through a self -administered online questionnaire from consumers of
environmentally friendly household and personal care products in Sidoarjo, East Java,
Indonesia. Using purposive sampling, 140 responses were obtained, of which 127 valid
responses were retained for analysis. The measurement instrument consisted of 23 indicators
covering environmental awareness, customer trust, and consumer loyalty, measured using a
five-point Likert scale. Data were analyzed using SPSS through descriptive statistics,
validity/reliability testing, classical assumption test ing, and regression analysis. The results
demonstrate that environmental awareness has a positive and significant effect on consumer
loyalty (β = 0.342, t = 5.480, p < 0.001). Customer trust also has a positive and significant
effect on consumer loyalty an d represents the stronger predictor (β = 0.460, t = 5.537, p <
0.001). These findings indicate that sustainable consumption is influenced by both
consumers’ environmental awareness and confidence in the credibility and environmental
responsibility of eco-friendly brands. The study contributes to sustainable consumer behavior
literature by highlighting environmental and relational dimensions of consumer loyalty.
Managerially, eco-friendly brands should strengthen environmental education, transparency,
and credible sustainability practices to foster lasting consumer relationships.

INTRODUCTION
 Environmental issues arising from climate change, pollution, and environmental degradation have increasingly
shaped consumer behavior and business practices by heightening the demand for sustainable alternatives (García-Salirrosas

& Rondon-Eusebio, 2022). Growing environmental awareness prompts consumers to evaluate the environmental and social
impacts of their consumption choices and to favor environmentally responsible products and brands (Sohaib et al., 2025) .
Consequently, consumption decisions have expanded beyond traditional considerations of price and quality to encompass
broader sustainability concerns, thereby elevating the strategic importance of green marketing in attracting consumers to eco-
friendly offerings and communicating environmental value. Environmental awareness further directly influences purchasing
behavior while motivating firms to develop sustainable products that address consumers’ ecological concerns, positioning
sustainable consumption as a reflection of evolving social values toward environmental responsibility.
 Environmental awareness constitutes a fundamental foundation for sustainable consumption, as it shapes
consumers’ recognition of environmental problems and guides their evaluation of consumption choices according to
ecological consequences (M. Cui et al., 2024). Consumers exhibiting higher levels of environmental awareness demonstrate
a greater propensity to support products perceived as environmentally responsible and to develop stronger attachments to
sustainable brands (Li et al., 2024) . Empirical evidence further confirms that environmental awareness positively and
significantly influences sustainable product purchase decisions while fostering longer -term loyalty toward eco -friendly
brands through mechanisms such as green brand image an d trust (Aly et al., 2025; Van Quang & Thi Van, 2025) .
Nevertheless, awareness alone may prove insufficient to secure enduring consumer loyalty when uncertainty persists
regarding the credibility of corporate environmental commitments, underscoring the need for transparent green marketing
practices that rein force both purchase behavior and sustained brand relationships (Islam & Ali Khan, 2024; Uikey et al.,
2026).
 Customer trust is therefore another important dimension of sustainable consumption, particularly because consumers
often cannot directly verify environmental claims made by businesses. Information concerning sustainable production
processes, environmental impacts, and corporate environmental commitments may be difficult to evaluate independently,
making transparency, credibility, and consistency essential in determining whether environmental claims are perceived as
authentic. (Isac et al., 2025)  demonstrated that consistent green transparency practices strengthen customer trust in brands.
Trust can reduce uncertainty regarding the authenticity of sustainability claims and increase consumers’ confidence that a
brand’s environmental commitments are  reflected in its actual practices. Customer trust is also closely related to consumer
loyalty because consumers are more likely to maintain relationships with brands perceived as reliable, transparent, and
consistent with their commitments. Thus, trust ca n function as an important relational dimension connecting consumers’
environmental perceptions with their willingness to continue supporting environmentally responsible brands.
 Previous studies demonstrate that sustainability -related attributes, particularly those embedded in packaging and
marketing communications, can generate favorable consumer evaluations and reinforce enduring brand relationships (Branca
et al., 2024; Rahayu et al., 2025) . Sustainable and eco-friendly packaging has been shown to elevate green perceived value
and consumer satisfaction, thereby strengthening the cognitive and affective foundations of brand attachment (H. Cui et al.,
2026; Wardani & Ratnawati, 2024). Perceived value derived from green marketing initiatives further mediates the pathway
toward customer loyalty, confirming that consumers who recognize tangible environmental benefits are more inclined to
maintain long-term support for responsible brands. Nevertheless, the effectiveness of sustainability communication remains
contingent upon perceived credibility and authenticity; when environmental claims appear inconsistent with actual practices,
skepticism arises, underscoring the indispensable role of trust in converting environmental awareness and positive
sustainability perceptions into lasting consumer relationships (Aryani et al., 2025).
 Despite the growing research on sustainable consumer behavior, previous studies have often focused on particular
marketing attributes, brands, or industries. (Riva et al., 2024)  examined green marketing and service quality in relation to
consumer loyalty through customer satisfaction in Starbucks Surabaya, while (Chauhan & Goyal, 2024)  investigated the
mediating role of brand image in the relationship between green marketing and consumer loyalty toward The Body Shop in
Malang. Although these studies provide valuable insights, their focus on specific brands and marketing mechanisms offer s
only a partial understanding of the broader social and relational factors shaping loyalty toward environmentally responsible
products. Therefore, further research is needed to examine environmental awareness and customer trust simultaneously as
factors associated with consumer loyalty across a broader category of eco-friendly products.

 This study addresses this gap by examining sustainable consumption from a broader social and relational
perspective, focusing on environmental awareness, customer trust, and consumer loyalty among consumers of
environmentally friendly household and persona l care products in Sidoarjo, East Java, Indonesia. Unlike previous studies
centered on specific brands or industries, this study considers a broader category of environmentally friendly products.
Accordingly, the study aims to examine the effects of environmental awareness and customer trust on consumer loyalty and
the effect of environmental awareness on customer trust. The study contributes to sustainable consumer behavior literature
by integrating environmental awareness and customer trust as environmental and relational dimensions of consumer loyalty.
From a managerial perspective, the findings may provide insights for eco -friendly businesses to strengthen environmental
education, transparency, credible sustainability practices, and consumer relationship s. Based on the literature review and
identified research gap, the proposed hypotheses are: H1: Environmental Awareness has a positive and significant effect on
Consumer Loyalty. H2: Environmental Awareness has a positive and significant effect on Customer  Trust. H3: Customer
Trust has a positive and significant effect on Consumer Loyalty.

METHOD
 This study employs an explanatory quantitative design to examine the relationships among Environmental
Awareness, Customer Trust, and Consumer Loyalty in sustainable consumption. According to (Sekaran & Bougie, 2016) ,
explanatory research examines relationships among variables and tests theoretically derived hypotheses. The study adopts a
social-relational perspective by focusing on environmental values and consumer trust as factors associated with loyalty
toward eco-friendly products and brands. The research was conducted over a three-month period, from March to May 2026.
The research activities included data collection, data processing, analysis, and interpretation of the findings in accordance
with the objectives an d methodological framework of the study. During this period, the researchers systematically carried
out each stage of the research to ensure that the data obtained were relevant and adequate for addressing the research
questions.
 The study involved consumers of environmentally friendly household and personal care products in Sidoarjo, East
Java, Indonesia. Respondents were required to have purchased such products at least twice during the previous six months.
Purposive sampling was  used to ensure relevant consumption experience. Of 140 responses collected through an online
survey, 13 were excluded due to incomplete responses or failure to meet the criteria, resulting in 127 valid respondents. Given
the purposive sampling and location-specific population, the findings should be generalized cautiously.
 Data were collected through a self -administered online questionnaire using a five -point Likert scale (1 = Strongly
Disagree to 5 = Strongly Agree). Three constructs were measured using established indicators: Environmental Awareness
(X1) with six indicators adapted from (Nguyen et al., 2020), Customer Trust (X2) with eight indicators adapted from (Khan
et al., 2021), and Consumer Loyalty (Y) with nine indicators adapted from (Wang et al., 2021). Thus, the instrument consisted
of 23 indicators.
 Data were analyzed using SPSS through descriptive statistics, validity and reliability testing, and classical
assumption tests. Normality was assessed using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test and normal probability plots, multicollinearity
using Tolerance (>0.10) and VIF (<10), and heteroscedasticity using the Glejser test (p > 0.05).

Discussion
The Effect of Environmental Awareness on Consumer Loyalty
 The findings demonstrate that Environmental Awareness has a positive and statistically significant effect on
Consumer Loyalty (β = 0.342, t = 5.480, p < 0.001). This result indicates that consumers with greater awareness of
environmental issues tend to develop stronger loyalty toward environmentally friendly products and brands. The significant
relationship can be explained by the value -congruence mecha nism, whereby consumers are more likely to maintain a
relationship with brands whose products and practices are perceived to reflect their own environmental concerns. When
environmental issues become part of consumers’ personal values, purchasing and repur chasing environmentally friendly
products can serve not only functional purposes but also express consistency between personal values and consumption
behavior. Thus, environmental awareness provides a cognitive basis for evaluating whether a brand is compa tible with
consumers’ sustainability-oriented values.
 From a theoretical perspective, this finding is consistent with the premise of sustainable consumer behavior that
environmental knowledge and concern can shape behavioral responses when consumers perceive that their consumption
choices can contribute to environmental objectives. Environmental awareness increases consumers’ sensitivity to issues such
as pollution, resource depletion, and environmental degradation, making environmental attributes more relevant in brand
evaluation. As a result, consumers who are more environmentally aware may attach greater importance to the environmental
consequences of their purchases and become more inclined to repeatedly choose brands that are perceived as environmentally
responsible. The positive coefficient (β = 0.342) th erefore suggests that environmental cognition is translated into loyalty
through value-based evaluation of brands rather than through product attributes alone.
 This finding is consistent with (García-Salirrosas & Rondon -Eusebio, 2022), who identified a significant positive
relationship between environmental awareness and consumer loyalty. Similarly, (Sohaib et al., 2025)  reported that
environmental awareness contributes to stronger repurchase intentions and emotional attachment toward environmentally
friendly brands. (M. Cui et al., 2024) also found that environmental awareness significantly influences brand loyalty among
cosmetic consumers, while (Islam & Ali Khan, 2024)  confirmed a positive relationship between environmental awareness
and consumer loyalty in the manufacturing sector. Furthermore, (Li et al., 2024)  identified environmental awareness as an
important antecedent of green loyalty. Rather than merely confirming these previous findings, the present result indicates
that the relationship may occur because environmental awareness changes the criteria consumers use when evaluating brands.
In environmentally oriented markets, consumers with stronger awareness are more likely to incorporate environmental
responsibility into their assessment of brand value. This can strengthen psychological attachment and behavi oral continuity
when the brand’s sustainability orientation is perceived to correspond with their environmental values.
 This mechanism also explains why environmental awareness can contribute to loyalty even though awareness itself
does not guarantee repeated purchasing. Awareness creates the cognitive foundation for environmentally oriented evaluation,
but its behavioral c onsequences depend on whether consumers encounter brands that provide credible opportunities to act
consistently with those values. Therefore, the practical implication is not simply to increase consumers’ environmental
knowledge, but to connect environmen tal education with visible and meaningful sustainability practices. Companies can
strengthen this process by communicating specific environmental benefits, providing understandable sustainability
information, and ensuring that environmental claims are refl ected in actual products and business practices. In this way,
environmental awareness can be transformed from environmental concern into sustained consumer-brand relationships.
The Effect of Customer Trust on Consumer Loyalty

 The results show that Customer Trust has a positive and statistically significant effect on Consumer Loyalty and
represents the strongest predictor in the model (β = 0.460, t = 5.537, p < 0.001). This finding indicates that consumer
confidence in the credibility, environmental responsibility, and consistency of eco -friendly brands plays a particularly
important role in developing long-term loyalty. The stronger effect of Customer Trust c an be theoretically explained by the
uncertainty inherent in sustainable consumption. Environmental attributes are often difficult for consumers to verify directly
because the environmental performance of a product may involve production processes, sourcin g, packaging, waste
management, or other practices that are not immediately observable at the point of purchase. Consequently, consumers must
rely partly on the credibility of the brand when evaluating whether environmental claims accurately represent actual practices.
 Trust is especially important in sustainable consumption because consumers may experience uncertainty regarding
the authenticity of environmental claims. Consumers may have strong environmental awareness, but such awareness does
not necessarily translate into loyalty when they lack confidence in the brand. Customer trust therefore functions as a relational
mechanism that reduces uncertainty and strengthens consumers’ willingness to maintain relationships with environmentally
responsible brands. Trust reduce s the perceived risk associated with green consumption. When consumers believe that a
brand is honest, competent, and consistent in fulfilling its environmental commitments, they have less reason to question the
authenticity of its sustainability claims. T his confidence can increase satisfaction with the brand relationship, reduce the
perceived need to switch to alternative brands, and strengthen the intention to continue purchasing from the same brand.
 From a theoretical perspective, the finding highlights that loyalty in eco -friendly markets involves not only
consumers’ environmental values but also their relational assessment of the firm. Environmental awareness answers the
question of whether environmental responsibility matters to consumers, whereas trust addresses whether consumers believe
a particular brand can be relied upon to act according to those responsibilities. The stronger standardized coefficient of
Customer Trust (β = 0.460) suggests that , within the present model, confidence in the brand’s environmental claims and
behavior provides a particularly important pathway through which sustainability considerations are converted into loyalty.
 The finding is consistent with (Isac et al., 2025) , who emphasized that customer trust develops through corporate
credibility, consistent performance, and commitment to environmental values. (Branca et al., 2024)  also demonstrated that
green trust positively and significantly influences green loyalty, while (H. Cui et al., 2026)  confirmed the positive role of
trust in strengthening consumer loyalty. Similarly, (Aryani et al., 2025)  emphasized the importance of transparency,
credibility, and environmental integrity in establishing consumer trust toward green products and brands. The convergence
of these findings suggests that trust is particularly relevant when environmental performa nce cannot be fully assessed by
consumers through direct observation. However, the present study provides an additional explanation for this relationship by
demonstrating that trust is not merely an attitudinal outcome of corporate credibility; it can oper ate as a mechanism that
enables consumers to translate environmental expectations into continued brand relationships.
 The relatively stronger coefficient of Customer Trust compared with Environmental Awareness suggests that trust
represents a critical relational dimension of sustainable consumption. This difference should not be interpreted as meaning
that environmental awareness is unimportant. Rather, it indicates that within the estimated model, confidence in the brand
has a stronger statistical association with loyalty than environmental awareness. Consumers may recognize environmental
problems and value sustainability, but their loyalty is more likely to emerge when they believe that brands genuinely fulfill
their environmental commitments. This distinction is important because environmentally conscious consumers can still reject
or discontinue relationships with brand s when sustainability claims are perceived as inconsistent, exaggerated, or
insufficiently supported.
 Accordingly, sustainable brands should prioritize transparency, consistency, and verifiability in their environmental
communication. Rather than relying on broad environmental claims, companies can strengthen trust by providing specific
information about sustainable materials, production practices, certifications, measurable environmental targets, and progress
toward those targets. Such practices reduce information asymmetry between firms and consumers and provide consumers
with stronger grounds for evaluating the authenticity of environmental commitments.

 Taken together, the findings demonstrate that Environmental Awareness and Customer Trust are both significant
predictors of Consumer Loyalty, with Customer Trust showing the stronger standardized coefficient (β = 0.460) compared

with Environmental Awareness (β = 0.342). These results support the central argument of the study that sustainable
consumption is shaped by both environmental cognition and social-relational evaluations. Environmental Awareness reflects
consumers’ recognition of environmental problems and their consequences, whereas Customer Trust reflects confidence in
the credibility and environmental responsibility of brands. The two variables therefore represent different but complementary
dimensions of the consumer dec ision-making process. Environmental awareness establishes why sustainability matters to
consumers, whereas customer trust helps determine whether consumers believe a particular brand is capable of delivering the
sustainability values they expect.
 The combination of these two dimensions suggests that consumer loyalty in eco-friendly markets is not determined
solely by environmental concern. Rather, consumers are more likely to maintain relationships with sustainable brands when
their environmental values are supported by confidence in the brands’ authenticity and consistency. This can be understood
as a progression from cognition to relationship: consumers first recognize and value environmental responsibility, then
evaluate whether brands credibly embody that responsibility. When the two conditions are present, continued purchasing can
become a way of maintaining consistency between personal environmental values and a trusted brand relationship.
The stronger coefficient of Customer Trust further indicates that the relational dimension deserves particular attention in
explaining loyalty. Environmental awareness may increase consumers’ sensitivity to sustainability issues, but heightened
awareness can also make consumers more critical of corporate environmental claims. In other words, greater awareness may
increase the importance of credibility because environmentally aware consumers are more likely to scrutinize whether brands
genuinely practice wha t they communicate. Therefore, strengthening environmental awareness without simultaneously
strengthening corporate credibility may not be sufficient to produce durable loyalty.
 This finding contributes to the sustainable consumer behavior literature by emphasizing the relationship between
what consumers believe about environmental issues and whom they trust to act responsibly. Theoretically, the findings
suggest that sustainable consumer behavior should be understood through both value -based and relational mechanisms. A
consumer’s environmental orientation provides the motivation to seek environmentally responsible consumption, while trust
reduces uncertainty about whether the sel ected brand actually satisfies that expectation. This perspective extends a purely
awareness-based explanation of green loyalty by showing that consumers’ environmental cognition must be supported by
credible firm behavior.
 In practical terms, companies should therefore combine environmental education with transparent and verifiable
sustainability practices. Environmental communication should not be treated merely as a promotional instrument; it should
be supported by evidenc e that enables consumers to verify the company’s environmental commitments. At the same time,
sustainability practices should be communicated in a clear and consistent manner so that consumers can connect the
company’s actual behavior with their own enviro nmental values. This combination can strengthen both the cognitive and
relational foundations of loyalty.
 Building environmental awareness without establishing trust may be insufficient to sustain loyalty, while trust is
more likely to generate lasting relationships when supported by consumers’ environmental values. Therefore, the findings
imply that sustainab le marketing strategies need to move beyond simply communicating that a product is “green.” They
should demonstrate why the product is environmentally responsible, provide credible evidence of such responsibility, and
consistently deliver on the environmental promises communicated to consumers. Overall, the results support the perspective
that environmental awareness and customer trust constitute important foundations of consumer loyalty in eco -friendly
markets. The findings are particularly relevant for su stainable marketing strategies because they indicate that long -term
loyalty depends not only on communicating environmental values but also on demonstrating credible and consistent
commitment to those values.

CONCLUSION
 This study demonstrates that Environmental Awareness and Customer Trust are important foundations of Consumer
Loyalty in eco-friendly markets. The findings imply that increasing consumer awareness alone is not sufficient; sustainable
brands also need to es tablish credible and consistent environmental practices that strengthen consumer trust. Therefore,

and consistently align their practices with the environmental values communicated to consumers.
 Theoretically, this study contributes to sustainable consumer behavior literature by highlighting the complementary
roles of environmental awareness and customer trust in fostering consumer loyalty. Managerially, the findings suggest that
companies should integrate environmental education with trust-building strategies to develop stronger and more sustainable
consumer–brand relationships. This study is limited to 127 consumers of environmentally friendly household and personal
care products in Sidoarjo, East Java, using purposive sampling. Future research could extend the investigation to larger and
more diverse samples, different geographical contexts, and other eco -friendly product categories to enhance the
generalizability of the findings.
