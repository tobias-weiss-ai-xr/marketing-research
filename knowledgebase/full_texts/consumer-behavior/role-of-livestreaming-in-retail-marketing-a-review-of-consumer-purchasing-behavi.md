ABSTRACT  ARTICLE INFO
Introduction/Main Objectives: This study aims to investigate the role
of live  streaming in traditional retailers’  marketing and explore the
purchasing behavior of online consumers through bibliometric reviews of
publications indexed in the Scopus and Web of Science databases.
Background Problems:  The emergence of a linked world has brought
about the development of live -stream purchasing on various platforms,
while traditional shops face difficulties in maintaining their business
operations. Novelty: Although live streaming is a prominent instrument
for online retail marketing, its significance in the marketing strategies of
traditional retailers is relatively ambiguous. Th is study sheds light on its
influential impacts on purchasing behavior in a developing country.
Research Methods: The study adopts the TCCM framework to grasp the
fundamental aspects of the research topic from pertinent research indexed
in Scopus and Web of Science, applying the SPAR-4-SLR protocol.
Findings/Results: Conclusively, the SOR theory is popular among the
retail marketing and behavioral literature, followed by game, flow,
signaling, real options, and attachment theories. There is a strong
correlation between consumer behavioral intention, live -streaming
commerce, social media, trust, and perceived value toward retailers.
Conclusion: Above all, the study provides a strong foundation  for the
experimental implementation of e -commerce and social media as
marketing tools to stimulate consumer intention and potential purchasing
behavior. The study also provides novel insights into  the retail literature
by embedding behavioral aspects into traditional retailer s' marketing
strategies.

INTRODUCTION
The advent of the Internet and technological
advancements have given rise to a series of
developments in terms of consumer patterns of
consumption (Chen et al., 2022 ) on social
networking platforms during  the past decade.
Live stream, an embedded real -time broad -
casting feature in both e -commerce and social
commerce platforms (Luo et al., 2023) , has
gained popularity recently thanks to the direct
access it provides to retailers' stores, people, and
products. Online consumption is perceived as a
new norm for consumer purchasing patterns,
especially following the COVID -19 pandemic.
The vast online community enabled by  social
media, consisting of around 4.8 billion members
worldwide, or about 59.9% of t he globe's
population, has emerged as a highly efficient
promotional and communicational instrument
for marketing purposes  ( Thuy An Ngo et al.,
2023). The level of digitalized connections in in
Asia and the Pacific  economies has more than
doubled during the last decade, leading to
rampant e -commerce sales comprising around
44% of global outputs according to a report from
the Asian  Development Bank in 2021 . With
roughly 90 million civilians and a booming
middle-class economy  (Maruyama & Trung,
2012), Vietnam's  retail industry has provided
fertile ground for digital  marketing innovation
fueled by increasing and changing customer
behaviors. Hence, online sales have revolu-
tionized contemporary marketing approaches to
consumers who favor a convenient and efficien t
shopping experience.
The rapid tempo of digitalization  has seen
retail, the largest and most diverse industry,
undergo an ever -changing transformation from
static to proactive delivery of goods and
information flow. However, t he one-stop-
shopping model, deemed commonplace in e -
commerce, may diminish the value of over
focused specialists. Retailers are actively
reaching out to customers for a personalized
shopping experience while gaining insights on
how to facilitate customer tran sactions without
pressure. A successful live -streaming marketing
strategy does just that, allowing retailers with a
large capital investment to maintain desired
performance. As in other Southeast Asian
economies, t he Vietnamese retail system is a
mixture o f modern  (e.g., convenience stores,
hypermarkets, shopping centers, and super -
markets) and traditional retail formats (e.g., wet
market retailers and “mom-and-pop” stores).
While traditional retailers remain prevalent ,
modern retailers are incorporating in cremental
development that poses a significant threat to the
integrity of traditional retailers, given the
changing nature of consumer behavior. As a
result, traditional retailers with limited resources
and only a handful of IT -capable employees are
unable to adapt to the changing environment,
especially in the marketing domain.
The discussion around the recent surge of
international shops and centralized supermarkets
is becoming increasingly heated in marketing
and e -commerce discourse. Domestic retailers ,
primarily localized and operating at the
individual level, like mom -and-pop stores, have
expressed apprehension regarding the potential
loss of market share to emerging overseas retail
behemoths  (Maruyama & Trung, 2012 ). So far,
there have been f ew comprehensive examina -
tions of digital consumer purchasing behavior
under the influence of traditional retailers' live -
stream marketing. Despite the common percep -
tion that traditional retailers are technologically
lagging and lack the ability to innovate
independently, there has been limited research
dedicated to studying how live -stream retail
marketing could transform merchants and the
drivers of consumer purchas ing behavioral
patterns in the digital age.

Since the early 21st century, intensified
market competition has led to the adoption of
win-win strategies as a guiding principle. As a
result, traditional m arketing theories, which
focus on customer demands, have become  less
relevant to modern marketing situations. In
response, American scholar Don Schulz
developed the 4R marketing theory, building
upon previous theories. This theory comprises
four primary co mponents: relationship, reward,
market reaction, and consumer relevance. It
employs the relationship marketing  (RM)
paradigm to examine business and client
interactions. RM focuses on creating, growing,
and sustaining successful relationships through
marketing endeavors  (Morgan & Hunt, 19 94).
Morgan and Hunt  (1994) identified ten types of
RM, which they categorized into four groups:
internal, lateral, buyer, and supplier partnerships.
In live streaming, these connections can be seen
as exchanges between the company  (the brand)
and its audience (the fans).
Essentially, customer relevancy strongly
emphasizes the enterprise and the customer as
the key players, highlight ting the necessity of
long-term client relationships as a crucial
component of corporate operations. Commu -
nities in live streaming can arise from
maintenance of  the size of fan base  (Liu &
Wang, 2023). These followers serve as brand
ambassadors in addition to prospective clients.
Live streaming offers a unique medium for
brand communication (Akrout & Nagy, 2018) by
enabling live product demonstrations as well as
simultaneous interaction  (Liu & Yu, 2022 ). B
usinesses can draw in consumers by creating
exciting marketing content, exhibiting their
company, goods, or services  through virtual
communities (Ayuni, 2020),  establishing a two -
way relationship with clients that motivates them
to spread the word about the brand among their
networks.
Only when the relationship between buyers
and the sellers reaches a point of mutual trust do
behavioral outcomes such as continuance of
usage intention  emerged from cognitive
evaluation  (Ansori & Nugroho, 2024) . Within
the realm of mobile live commerce, the
relationship fosters robust connections between
enterprises and customers, promoting figure
recognition of the brand endorsed influencers
(Liu & Wang, 2023) . Strengthening the relation-
ship between these two parties is a practical, but
time-consuming strategy to create unwavering
patronage once shop pers subconsciously
perceive providers’ trustworthiness  (Kalia et al.,
2021). This emphasizes how important it is for
customers to provide feedback on a business's
operations, goods, or services  (Chen et al.,
2022). As a result, a  win-win scenario arises
from the reciprocal growth and progress on both
sides.
Nonetheless, indicators of such patronage
may not always be conspicuous and are
sometimes inconsistent.  The salience of
consumers’ willingness to retain relationships
consciously and deliberately sits firmly in the
emotional and psychological compensation they
receive once they experience the offerings
firsthand  (Akrout & Nagy, 2018) . Unless the
compensation equates to the amount of effort
expended during the exchange between  buyers
and sellers, both in tangible and intangible forms
(Liu et al., 2020) , buyers will show some degree
of dissatisfaction and  resentment. This failure
significantly underscores the need for proper
management of the competing interests  of
multiple stakeholders in marketing initiatives. In
mobile live commerce, the host must engage
with consumers immediately, addressing their
inquiries and understanding their perceptions of
the company.

2. Stimulus-Organism-Response (SOR)
Stimulus-organism-response (SOR) theory was
first proposed in Mehrabian and Russell (1974)’s
study in environmental psychology and later
applied to retail in Donovan (1994)’s study. The
model suggests a linear process: environmental
stimuli influence consumers through psycholo -
gical and emotional states  (the organism), which
then shape the responses of the organism  (Ming
et al., 2021; Tak & Gupta, 2021) . Research has
since tested different environmental factors at
the psychological level to explain behavio ral
outcomes across fields  (Luo et al., 2022; Zhu &
Liu, 2023) . Internal states such as control and
pleasure are not only shaped by stimuli but also
by whether consumer behavior is active or
passive, which influences intention. While early
interpretations treated the organism as passive,
newer views recognize consumers as active,
drawing on past experiences and comparisons
before making decisions.
Beyond SOR, scholars have highlighted the
role of cognitive control in consumer behavior,
which can act to limit expected outcomes
(Zhang & Xu, 2023). Familiarity strengthens this
control when consumers match current
sensations with prior positive brand experiences.
Such positivity often extends to new stimuli.
Marketers use this by evoking emotions linked

to memory, such as nostalgia   (Musslick &
Cohen, 2021). Psychological research has further
identified core processes like awareness, recall,
retention, deduction, feelings, and motivation as
central to consumer choice. These insights reveal
decision-making as a complex system of
interrelated factors  (Paz & Vargas, 2023).
The SOR framework has been widely
applied in marketing to explain ho w external
stimuli affect consumer behavior. From a
cognitive perspective, it underpins models that
consider rationality in processing marketing
messages. Yet, inhibitors in decision -making
mean that purchase intentions do not always lead
to actual purchas es, requiring marketers to
carefully study audience reactions . In this
context, authentic communication and trust are
critical, as consumers respond positively to
genuine exchanges. Overall, the cognitive
approach provides a nuanced view of how
stimuli inf luence decision -making and
purchasing behavior.
2.1. Cognitive Model of Consumer Behavior
Cognitivism discourse  often emphasizes two
significant models of concern , one stemming
from an analytical standpoint, while the other
provides insight into  the prescriptive develop -
ment of consumer behavior. The former explains
the structural intricacy of the fundamental
elements of customer behavior, with overarching
causal relationships among components  of the
input-process-output framework . These models
often follow the conventional five -step process:
determining the issue, gathering data, evaluating
choices, deciding the behavioral outcomes, and
evaluating the result   (Jagan et al., 2023; Santos
& Gonçalves, 2021) . They provide a means  of
measuring and predicting the effects of modified
or emphasized stimuli to elicit specific customer
responses. To this end, the model s of consumer
cognitive behavior are deemed to provide a
thorough view of the grand scheme of things.
2.2. Models of the Analytic Cognitive Process
Two notable instances of the analytic cognitive
process are the theory of buyer behavior and the
consumer decision model (Darley et al., 2010;
Ming et al., 2021; Santos & Gonçalves, 2021) .
From an analytical perspective, models focus
more on the influential antecedents of marketing
factors, social impacts, and psychological
determinants (Bray et al., 2011) . These
dimensions enable researchers and marketers to
grasp the causal relationship between the
processing of intake information and consumers’
discretionary choices.
On a similar note, scholars have inferred a
notable point from the  cognitivism discourse in
extant literature . They argue that the term
“buyer” should be preferred over the more
traditional “consumer” when considering com-
mercial shopping. This notion highlights the
direct stimuli, or so -called significative input, of
a particular brand or product that is encountered
firsthand by the buyers themselves. Given
increasing digital in tegration, new technology
allows marketers to use symbolic represen-
tations, like visual advertising, to entrench
shared value s in relation to  a brand or product
(Jacoby, 2002; Zhu et al., 2020) . On top of that,
a referral from friends and family or even
advocacy groups has been identified as  another
crucial contributor to the outputs of buyer
behavior. Following an initial encounter,
consumers' cognitive control s ignals a constraint
in the flow of information while adjusting to
their experience or prejudice to form biased
decisions (Park & Lin, 2020) . These decisions
will only  be converted to  actual inten tion and
behavior if they seek validation from additional
information. Many studies have pointed out that
somewhere along the process, the recalling of
motives facilitates subsequent steps toward

behavioral outputs  (Sohaib & Kang, 2015;Wang
et al., 2019) . Once the buyer validation is
completed, immediate intention is set, prompting
positive, negative, or neutral attitudes toward a
brand or product  (Paz & Vargas, 2023) .
Eventually, the buyer's intention determines
whether they initiate purchasing behavior  or
disinvest (Darley et al., 2010) . Such a process is
susceptible to changes depending on the types of
inhibitors, for example price  (Sheth, 2021),
associated with social and cultural aspects.
3. The marketing approach of live  streaming
and customer purchasing patterns
Confronted with a slew of influential stimuli, it
can be hard to  resist the temptation of purchase
intention, let alone impulsive behavior .
Sincehuman cognitive psychology i s driven by
direct social interaction and presence, closing the
gap among people creates a sense of closeness
that promotes trust and engagement. Live
streaming sessions follow suit with real -time
interaction, where shoppers receive consultation
and advice  to overcome physical and psycho -
logical barriers faced in traditional shopping.
Marketers can optimize stimuli exclusive to
these platforms, making the shopping experience
sensational.
Meanwhile, sales promotion is an effective
marketing stimulus that bo osts the likelihood of
consumers' actual purchases by following a
time-saving information process  (Liu et al.,
2023; Zhao &  Fan, 2024) . Applying the theory
of cognitive consumer behavior, many resear -

1970; Ki et al., 2024; Loudon &  Della Bitta,

feedback systems or referral coupons as a
prominent sale -oriented approach to online
shopping (Reina Paz & Rodríguez Vargas,
2023). Influencers and endorsers, who host the
live-shopping session with enthusiasm and
resourcefulness, can  guarantee success for
marketers. The conceptualization of dimensions
in the aforementioned models provides scientific
rigor regarding direct and indirect effects across
the multiple stages  in which consumers form

METHOD, DATA, AND ANALYSIS
In a quest for in-depth analytical comprehension
of the subject matter, the authors formulate d the
review according to  the Scientific Procedures
and Rationales for Systematic Literature
Reviews protocol, known as SPAR -4-SLR,
which is widely accepted to maintain scientific
rigor (Luo et al., 2023; Paul et al., 2023) . Recent
literature has emphasized the robustness of the
protocol for explaining the relationships between
complex phenomena (Singh & Dhir, 2019 ). The
review rigorously analyze d the theoretical
foundations, contextual complexities, and
methodology choices within prevailing research
on the topic  of life -stream marketing and
consumer purchase behavior.  The result is  an
informative and complex synopsis that
delineates the current state and complexities of
extant research while upholding the highest
standards of science and integrity.
1. Assembling
The assembly phase entail ed the identification
and procurement process. The initial identify-
cation stage was used to define the breadth of the
research field and formulat e relevant research
questions. The acquisition of publications from
esteemed publishers such as Emerald, Springer,
Science Direct, and IEEE Xplore e nsured
complete coverage of relevant works, with
stringent quality assessments for source
inclusion or indexing. This approach minimized
the risk of qualified manuscripts being
inadvertently excluded from the Scopus or Web
of Science  (WoS) databases , reflecting the
study's effort to achieve the most comprehensive
and diverse coverage of the relevant literature .
The research period covered five years, from
2019 to 2024. This time frame is sufficient to
conceptualize a construct or include any
significant alteration in contextual factors. To
comprehensively elucidate the intricacies of the
research concepts, Boolean queries  were used to
combine appropriate terms and comprehending
their combination within a statement (Paul et al.,
2023; Scells et al., 2021) . For instance, the
composition of clustered terms is posited as
follows: (“live stream*” OR “live stream*” OR
“live-stream*” OR “live streaming*”) AND
(“consumer purchase  behavior” OR “consumer
purchasing behavior” OR “purchase behavior”
OR “purchasing behavior” OR “online purchase
behavior” OR “online purchasing behavior”).
The search produced roughly 1,987 articles,
including 677 in the Springer archive, 276 in
Science Direct, 998 in Emerald, and 36 in IEEE
Xplore.
2. Arranging
The arranging stage involves the processes of
refinement and organization . The articles were
filtered according to document type, source type,
and language during the purification process.
The authors  limited the ir attention to "articles"
published in "journals" due to their well -
developed exploratory ideas and causal insights.
In contrast, "books" and "book chapters" are
often explanatory, "conference proceedings" are
usually tentative, and "editorials" or "notes" may
not have undergone the scrutiny of the peer -
review process. Articles not directly related to
the connection between live streaming and
business were also removed. Additionally,
duplicate articles were eliminated, leading to the
final sample of 544 pertinent articles. During the

process of organization, these items were
systematically encoded and classified using the
theories-characteristics-contexts-methods
(TCCM) framework  (Singh & Dhir, 2019).
3. Assessing
VOSviewer software was utilized to visually
represent the prominent keywords in the studies
obtained from the aforementioned databases.
Specifically, t he authors submitted the

threshold for including a keyword was

One of  the most commonly employed
theoretical frameworks in the article sample was
S-O-R (Zhu et al., 2020) , as it delineates the
psychological and emotional internalization of
an individual, leading to a subsequent behavioral
response following environmental st imuli (Nam

advantages of using the right marketing strategy

financial duress . A thorough understanding of
the customer information assessment process
would shine some light into this oblivion.
Therefore, conceptual framewo rks should be
used only if they are supported by an underlying
theoretical framework for a linear progression of
academic discourse.
A pragmatic perspective on integrated live -
stream marketing strategies has far -reaching
implications for the general develo pment of
traditional retailers across regions. Southeast
Asian economies like Vietnam, Thailand,
Indonesia, and the Philippines are experiencing
rapid growth in terms of population, digitali -
zation, and economy. This has resulted in the
rise of numerous on line shopping behaviors,
which are mostly at the discovery -led stage,
meaning consumers are unwittingly exposed to
suggested content they encounter during their
window-shopping. In the case of the Vietnamese
market, there is a stark contrast between North
and South Vietnamese consumer behaviors. The
North Vietnamese consumers rely on social
approval, while the South prefer products that fit
their personal preference. A study from
Geiger‐Oneto et al. (2023) demonstrated that the
individual and household expenditures of the
South are higher than in the North, where the
focus is on saving for rainy -day items.
Trustworthiness, authenticity, and engagement
are essential to consumers regardless of effective
advertisement campaigns, especially for
consumers from the North. Marketing value
propositions must therefore adapt promotions,
direct consultation, and personal ization to
regional marketplace differences. This also
confirms the vital influence of national culture
on consumers’ online purchasing behavior,
according to Hofstede’s cultural dimensions.

in more collectivi st societies will have a
propensity for seeking peer recommendation,
social proof, and community trust. Similarly,

Research on live streaming, retail marketing,
and behavioral attitude has mostly taken the
perspective of operational and direct
stakeholders  (Kunz & Hogreve, 2011). Previous
findings on live streaming indicate strategies for
increasing customer engagement, perceived
trust, positive attitude, impulsive purchase, and
dependence on retailers and brands. Many
researchers sugg est that single or multiple
theoretical frameworks should be included as
antecedent, mediator, and outcome dimensions
to provide  a more dynamic discussion .
Nevertheless, numerous theoretical conside -

perspective o f SOR, antecedent studies imply
vital themes regarding environmental cues,
namely social media, influencers, messages,
communication, virtual stores, online video

flow have been identified   (Chen, 2023) . For

intention, satisfaction, market, and financial
performance are highlighted (Lin et al., 2019).
The choice of research method includes
structural equation modeling, literature review
articles, experiments, and case analysis  (Jacoby,
2002; Jayaswal & Parida, 2023; Pagáč et al.,
2025; Walsh et al., 2016).
CONCLUSION AND SUGGESTIONS
This study provides a comprehensive under -
standing of live streaming in retail marketing
using bibliometric analysis and SPAR -4-SLR to
maintain scientific rigor. The key takeaways are
listed as follows:
First, consumers are more likely to depend
on direct consultation with those they deem
genuinely supportive, thereby alleviating
uncertainty an d confirming beliefs during the
decision-making process  (Mayasari, 2012). This
applies particularly to those who rely on
salesperson assi stance to thoroughly understand
product information at brick-and-mortar stores

Journal of Indonesian Economy and Business, Vol. 41, No. 3, 2026 513
(Hwang & Youn, 2023) . There should be an
appropriate action plan or a simple checklist for
regular platform management, as consumers
may make inquiries or leave feedback that needs
to be handled.  Hands-on training for staff and
owners should also be considered to improve
effectiveness in leveraging e -commerce and
social media platforms  while containing
imminent crises. Unless shop owners or staff are
able to easily handle most situations  based on
preset protocols, distrust could arise, resulting in
a deteriorated reputation and profit loss.
Traditional retailers often take matters into their
own hands, unaware of their marke ting and
service inefficiency  (Soehadi, 20 14). To this
end, traditional retailers should recognize the
importance of acquiring outsourcing profes -
sionals in social commerce and marketing
management to improve internal capabilities
while making resource-invested returns.
Second, r ealizing the necessity of mini -
mizing uncertainty in the marketplace on the part
of the consumers, traditional retailers should
adopt a pragmatic pathway for incremental
incorporation of digital activities. However, this
requires sustainable costs in terms of upfront
investment in infrastructure and resources that
underpin the overall business operation . One
practical approach to online purchasing trends is
to utilize social media  (i.e., Facebook or
TikTok), as virtual shops can be set up for free
with affordable fees for exclusive business
features (i.e., promotional activities and
advertising purposes ). These platforms provide
retailers with live -streaming features as part of
numerous instruments of social c ommerce that
enable them to create  a substantial degree of
social presence and trustworthiness   (Susilo et
al., 2022). As a result, dynamic atmospheres are
formulated, with live-streamed open discussions
that act as social stimuli, tapping into the
subconscious need  of buyers for their concerns
and distress to be heard .
Third, traditional retailers should not take
online platform metrics for granted, as they are
telltale signs of how efficiently your business  is
performing. Many businesses initiate multiple
attempts to digitalize their general operation, but
to no avail, as essential online metrics are mostly
neglected. Embedded supporting metrics , such
as the amount of feedback , chat response time,
appraisal, complaints indices, etc., help retailers
acknowledge trends and adjust current strateg ies
when needed.  Similarly, customer centricity
should be prioritized, as it is the prime concern
when achieving marketing and customer
relationship management  (CRM) goals. Online
buyers are prone to forgetting their last visited
pages, so streamers could use coupons to let
buyers disclose contact information for further
offers. Retailers should acknowledge live
streaming as a powerful medium to promote
products/services while strengthening parasocial
relationships with customers.
Fourth, c onsumerare more likely to develop
offline purchasing intention  following live -
streaming participation, especially in -store live
streaming sessions  (Zhang et al., 2023) . Fans
and influencers create a joyful atmosphere once
streamers align their target audience characteris -
tics with real-time streaming strategies to induce
customer engagement and trustworthiness via
commonality. Traditional retailers should pay
more attention to their shop interior des ign and
merchandise demonstration , as in -store
streaming provides an immersive shopping
experience and dynamic atmosphere similar to
when visiting a physical store . Despite having to
grapple with countless difficulties, traditional
retailers, especially micro and small individual
stores, continue to outnumber modern ones  as
they pay subsidized rates or use the frontage of
their houses as an outlet. These retail outlets are
within walking distance of customers and are

able to offer lower-priced items. Because of this,
traditional retailers should optimize their
location by collaborating with brands to make
their official outlets partners. Promotion al
activities like voucher gifting or special
discounts (Atilgan et al., 2024)  could also be
implemented for frequent customers or first-time
buyers for their next visit to the nearest outlet
during strategic live -streaming sessions .
Retailers should join hands as a local community
where brands offer special collaboration between
outlets for logistical and procurement purposes.
Last, m unicipal agencies should implement
policies that favor domestic and individual
retailers in transforming their business strategy
(i.e., access to capital borrowing or tax
deduction) while imposing anti -dumping
regulations against foreign retailers . Meanwhile,
governmental support and policy remain inert  as
foreign retailers  and new entrants gain sizeable
market share. Traditional retailers should voice
their concerns with domesti c, government-
backed retail associations for prote ction against
foreign competition  (i.e., the Vietnam
Association of Retailers or AVR).
Thereafter, traditional retailers should
benefit from the intuitive selection of platforms
(i.e., TikTok or Facebook ), which is the first
phase of building readiness  and the underlying
infrastructure. These platforms enable retailers
to establish credibility with basic setups like
streaming equipment (i.e., smartphones and
internet connection hotspots), ample background
decoration (i.e., backdrops and lighting), and
supportive software (i.e., product listing and
live-streaming scheduling). Digital literacy
training programs, which are widely provided by
marketing associations and agencies, enable
receivers to maintain the overall performance of
platform management. Shop owners or staff
should improve the perceived ease of use of new
technologies, granting them the capability to
effectively deliver brand value in each live
session. In the second phase,  creating parasocial
relationships stimulates perceived trust and
engagement from online shoppers. Streaming
platforms should support most tasks, from real-
time chatting to chat moderation. Emotional
attachment and brand love are the results of staff
immediacy to stimuli  and reactions flow during
streaming sessions.  Staff training in emotional
intelligence should also be taken into account, as
it is a key driver of service quality. The last
phase is associated  with integrating affi liate
marketing and CRM to achieve optimization and
expansion, given the platforms’ online consumer
behavior metric s are provided . Data interpre -
tation and adjustment of feedback-based tactics
signify the need for training in understanding the
subtle cues of parasocial behavior.  From the
governmental perspective, standardization of
platform management via policy-making and on
boarding support ensures fair competition among
retailers. A form of stimulus package should also
be implemented to fund training programs for
traditional retailers in digital literacy or CRM .
Municipal associations should also play a bigger
role in facilitating retailer  alliances combined
with tax incentives or grants for supporting low-
incurred-risk experiments. By engaging in these
phases from building foundations to engagement
to optimization, traditional retailers can thrive
and prosper amidst social and market turbulence.

RELEVANCE AND IMPLICATION TO
INDONESIAN CONTEXT
This research finding presents similarities with
the Indonesian context in terms of the structure
of social conditions, consumption patterns, and
individual characteristics.
For social conditions, this study aligns with
Indonesia’s fast -growing e -commerce, accom -
panied by the rise in internet penetration and
smartphone usage. This is a common phenol-
menon that is occurring in the young and
thriving reg ion of Southeast Asia, and the
expansion to virtual marketplaces and live -
streaming marketing is also apparent.
For consumption pattern, it is evident from
this finding that promotional activities are still
effective tools for marketers along with real-time
interaction and direct consultation from product
presentation. Consumers often opt for e -
commerce platforms that offer the most
competitive promotions with limited deadlines
and availability, creating an urgency to rush into
these platforms to secure their choice of product.
This serves as a great contribution to the
Indonesian context, where researchers can delve
into learning how to use promotional activities in
live sessions to improve the personalization of
consumer-shared experiences.
For individual  characteristics, findings from
Cluster 1 and Cluster 8 reveal that digital or
emotional stimulus associates with trust and
perceived value precede purchase intention in
live-streaming commerce. A study by Syamsuar
and Witarsyah (2025) in the Indonesian context
found a similar connection, where consumers
perceive stimuli as positive or negative before
formulating their perception of whether it is
valuable or risky in an online environment.
Similarly, relevance in terms of entert ainment
and personalization found in this study can also
be found in Chandraa et al.  (2024) article
showcasing critical factors of live -streaming
influence on purchase intention of Indonesian
buyers. Personalization of  shared experiences
and channel recommendations found in this
article can be  considered a great addition to the
study of live-streaming related features on social
commerce platforms  (Chandraa et al., 2024).
The implications for the Indonesian context
can be narrowed down to how brick -and-mortar
stores or MSMEs in Indonesia capitalize live
streaming to create authenti city and direct but
credible customer service as a driver to build
trust. Understanding the impact of different
stimuli allows retailers to anticipate responses
from potential customers. In recent times, social
commerce platforms have live features that
benefit small retailers in reaching out to
consumers in a cost-effective manner. This study
also implies that retailers should not rely
completely on virtual stores; key strategies are to
redirect consumers to visit key physical store
locations. This is emph asized by the Indonesian
governmental regulation of protecting local
businesses from Tiktok Shop, which sends a
strong message to boost the nation ’s  economy.
At the forefront of policy making, governmental
agencies, such as the Ministry of Cooperatives
and SMEs  (or Kemenkop UKM), should
organize digital literacy, focusing on live -stream
production, analytical tools, and customer
relationship management (CRM) tools.
