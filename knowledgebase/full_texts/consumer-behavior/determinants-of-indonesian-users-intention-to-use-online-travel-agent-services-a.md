ABSTRACT
Growing competition among Online Travel Agencies (OTAs) in Indonesia, driven by economic
slowdown and shifting consumer behavior, has reduced business performance and slowed technology
adoption. This study examines Indonesian users’ interest in utilizing OTA platforms through the
Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT) model, which includes four main
determinants: performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence, and facilitating
conditions. To improve the model’s explanatory power, the study added two additional variables.
The study integrates perceived trust and user attitude to examine their influence on users’ intention
to adopt OTA services. Using a quantitative approach, the study collected data from 400 respondents
in Indonesia with prior OTA experience. The findings show that perceived trust, performance
expectancy, and effort expectancy significantly influence user attitude. Furthermore, perceived trust,
performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence, facilitating conditions, and user attitude
significantly affect intention to purchase OTA services. All nine hypotheses were supported,
confirming the relevance of the extended UTAUT framework in explaining OTA adoption
intentions. The study deepens understanding of consumer behavior in the digital tourism sector and
supports innovation in online service delivery.

Keywords: Effort Expectancy, Facilitating Conditions, Perceived Trust, Performance Expectancy,
Social Influence, UTAUT, User Attitude.

INTRODUCTION
Digital technology has transformed how people meet needs in education, employment,
commerce, research, and tourism. These needs have become more complex, while
technological advances provide faster and more convenient solutions (Donthu &
Gustafsson, 2020). Smartphones and the internet have changed how individuals access
information and address problems (Wulandari et al., 2020). The internet also enables
businesses to develop websites and applications that support service delivery and
customer interaction (Cahen & Borini, 2020; Arifah et al., 2024). This transformation
matters for transportation and tourism, where consumers need access to information,
prices, schedules, and booking services. In Indonesia, internet adoption continues to
shape consumer behavior. Although COVID-19 changed how people accessed the
internet, the shift toward home-based activities strengthened dependence on digital
services (Haryanto, 2020).
Internet and smartphone use have supported growth in e-commerce and mobile
commerce in Indonesia. HootSuite reported 323.2 million smartphones in 2019 and 338.2
million in 2020, alongside 175.4 million internet users (Arisandi, 2020). By early 2021,
Indonesia had 274.9 million inhabitants and 202.6 million internet users (Riyanto, 2021).
This shift has moved purchasing behavior from offline transactions to online channels.
Online Travel Agents (OTAs) enable consumers to search travel information, compare

Consumer interest in OTA services had also developed before the pandemic. A

slowdown substantially disrupted the travel industry. Travel agents, airlines, and tourism
businesses saw demand decline as consumers cut discretionary spending, while travel
restrictions, health inspections, and passenger-capacity limits further constrained travel
activity (Supriyatna, 2020). Financial pressures encouraged consumers to stay home and
reduce leisure-related spending (Fadila, 2020). Consequently, understanding consumers’
intention to adopt and purchase through OTA platforms became increasingly important.
Technology acceptance provides a useful framework for explaining behavioral
intention. The Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT) identifies
performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence, and facilitating conditions
as key determinants of technology adoption (Venkatesh et al., 2012; Venkatesh, 2022).
UTAUT can explain approximately 70% of behavioral intention toward information
technology, exceeding the 17–53% explained by several earlier models (Indrawati et al.,
2019). However, technology acceptance also depends on users’ perceptions and attitudes
toward specific services. Trust is particularly relevant in OTA transactions because
consumers purchase without direct physical interaction with providers. Its role in OTA
purchasing remains under-explored and may vary across cultural and contextual
conditions (Chai & Dibb, 2014; Filieri et al., 2015; Agag & El-Masry, 2017). Therefore,
incorporating trust into UTAUT may provide a more comprehensive explanation of OTA
adoption. Attitude may similarly strengthen UTAUT’s predictive capability in this
context (Dwivedi et al., 2019).
Despite increasing internet penetration, OTA adoption in emerging countries remains
comparatively limited (Mohseni et al., 2018). Cultural, educational, economic, and
technological conditions may influence adoption, suggesting that findings from developed
countries may not generalize to Indonesia (Hoehle et al., 2015). Researchers have
therefore called for empirical research on online travel purchase adoption in developing
countries (Agag & El-Masry, 2017). Although UTAUT has been applied to technology
and tourism adoption, its applicability may vary across countries and contexts (Teo et al.,
2015; Venkatesh et al., 2016). This creates a research gap concerning how performance
expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence, facilitating conditions, trust, and attitude
collectively explain consumers’ behavioral intention to use OTA services in Indonesia.
This study examines the factors influencing consumers’ behavioral intention to adopt
OTA services in Indonesia by extending UTAUT with trust and attitude. Specifically, the
study investigates how the core UTAUT factors, trust, and attitude contribute to
consumers’ intention to use OTA technology for travel-related purchases. The findings
are expected to provide empirical evidence for understanding digital travel adoption in an
emerging-market context and offer useful insights for OTA providers and tourism
stakeholders in developing services that respond to changing consumer expectations.

LITERATURE REVIEW & HYPOTHESIS DEVELOPMENT
The Effect on User Attitude
Attitude has been widely recognized as an important factor in technology adoption
because it reflects consumers’ overall favorable or unfavorable evaluation of a technology
(Tanveer et al., 2021). Attitude is closely related to the Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB)
and the Theory of Reasoned Action (TRA), which explain how individual beliefs shape
behavioral intentions (Ajzen, 2020). The Technology Acceptance Model (TAM )
emphasizes perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use as key beliefs that influence

demonstrated that perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use contribute to attitude
formation in the Technology Acceptance Model Extension (TAME). Similarly, Rana et
al. (2017) highlighted that attitude can strengthen the relationship between technology-
related perceptions and behavioral intention within the UTAUT framework. These
perspectives suggest that consumers who perceive OTA services as useful and easy to use
are more likely to develop favorable attitudes, which subsequently encourage their
intention to adopt and purchase through the platform.
Trust is a key psychological determinant of consumers’ attitudes and purchase
decisions in digital environments. Sayogo (2018) argued that perceptions of company
credibility shape consumers’ dispositions and decisions, as reputable online merchants are
more likely to generate engagement and purchasing interest. Similarly, Amaro and
Duarte (2015) identified a positive relationship between consumer trust and purchase
intention in online contexts. In OTA platforms, trust can reduce transaction uncertainty
and strengthen consumers’ confidence in digital travel services. Meanwhile, performance
expectancy influences users’ attitudes toward technology by shaping perceptions of its
usefulness and expected benefits, as supported by Khalilzadeh et al. (2017) and Dwivedi
et al. (2020). Effort expectancy also contributes to favorable attitudes and purchase
intentions, as consumers are more receptive to OTA services when searching, comparing,
and purchasing travel products are perceived as simple and easy to understand. T his
relationship is supported by Altalhi (2021), Shiferaw et al. (2021), and Jena (2022).

The Effect on Intention to Purchase OTA
Perceived trust is a determinant of behavioral intention in e-commerce because online
transactions involve uncertainty. From a TRA perspective, trust shapes consumers’ beliefs
and confidence in digital services (Altaf et al., 2019). Online transactions require trust
(Pappas, 2016; Qiu & Jin, 2021). Previous studies confirm that trust influences online
shopping intentions and tourism behavioral intention (Chen et al., 2019; Choi et al., 2019;
Sharma et al., 2021). Amaro and Duarte (2015) likewise established  a relationship
between customer trust and purchase intention. Thus, consumers who perceive OTA
platforms as reliable are more likely to develop confidence and intend to purchase travel
services online.
Performance expectancy refers to whether individuals believe that system use helps
achieve desired outcomes (Venkatesh et al., 2016). It encompasses perceived utility,
intrinsic motivation, job fit, relative advantage, and outcome expectations, while
perceived usefulness reflects expectations that technology improves productivity
(Venkatesh et al., 2003). Previous studies by Escobar-Rodríguez and Carvajal-Trujillo
(2014), Slade et al. (2015), Casaló et al. (2017), Sohn (2017), and Sirdeshmukh et al.
(2018) emphasize its role in technology adoption and behavioral intention. Similar
relationships have been identified in mobile communication technologies, mobile wallets,
and telebanking (Madan & Yadav, 2016; Tan & Lau, 2016; Marriott & Williams, 2018;
Meydanoğlu et al., 2018). Soni et al. (2019) further indicate that performance expectancy
can encourage behavioral intention and online travel purchasing.
Effort expectancy represents the perceived ease of using a system (Venkatesh, 2022).
Previous research by Hsu et al. (2014) and Yeo et al. (2017) has examined this factor in
technology adoption, with Ardiansah et al. (2020) and Haryanti and Subriadi (2022)
finding that consumers generally prefer technologies that are simple and useful. Several
studies by Oliveira et al. (2014) and Shillair et al. (2015) found a positive relationship
between effort expectancy and behavioral intention. Thus, ease of use may encourage
consumers to purchase travel services through OTA platforms.

The Effect of Social Influence and Facilitating Condition on Intention to Purchase
Social influence refers to the extent to which individuals perceive that important people
expect them to use a particular system. Previous research has demonstrated its relevance
to behavioral intention, with Mahfuz et al. (2016) identifying social influen ce as a
significant determinant of behavioral intention and Indrawati and Utama (2018)
confirming its role in users’ intention to adopt 4G technology in Indonesia. These findings
indicate that application-based services can generate behavioral interest and encourage
positive word-of-mouth. Zuiderwijk et al. (2015) also examined the relationship between
social influence and behavioral intention, while Ajzen (2020) explained that individuals
are more likely to engage in behavior when they perceive social appr oval. This
relationship is further supported by Hsu and Lin (2016) and Ozturk et al. (2016).
Venkatesh (2022) reaffirmed its role in consumer behavioral outcomes, while Al Kurdi et
al. (2021) found that electronic toll collection usage can strengthen subjective norms.
Thus, support from friends, family, or other reference groups may encourage consumers
to adopt OTA services.
Facilitating conditions represent the availability of resources and technical support that
enable system use (Venkatesh et al., 2016; Venkatesh, 2022). Miladinovic and Hong
(2016) and Indrawati and Utama (2018) found that facilitating conditions influence
behavioral intention. Nistor et al. (2014), Madigan et al. (2017), Fong et al. (2017), and
Ho et al. (2020) also support this relationship , while El-Masri and Tarhini (2017)
confirmed its association with behavioral intention. In OTA services, access to suitable
devices, internet connectivity, guidance, and online support may therefore encourage
consumers’ intention to purchase travel services.

The Effect of Attitude on Intention to Purchase OTA
Previous studies have linked attitude to technology adoption and behavioral responses
(Poushneh & Vasquez-Parraga, 2018). This role of attitude aligns with the TRA and TPB,
which explain how individual beliefs and evaluations shape behavioral intention
(Buabeng-Andoh, 2018; Ajzen, 2020). TAM is a specific application of the TRA,
emphasizing perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use as determinants of attitude.
Extending this perspective, the TAME model examines the effects of perceived utility and
ease of use on attitude. These perspectives suggest that performance expectancy and effort
expectancy can shape consumers’ evaluations of technology and, in turn, influence
behavioral intention.
Within UTAUT, attitude can function as an important mechanism connecting
technology perceptions with behavioral intention. Rana et al. (2017) found that attitude
mediates the relationships between performance expectancy, effort expectancy, and
behavioral intention. In the OTA context, consumers who perceive the platform as useful
and easy to use are likely to develop favorable attitudes, which may strengthen their
intention to purchase travel services. Dwivedi et al. (2019) and Chen et al. (2022) support
this relationship, emphasizing the influence of attitude on behavioral intention. Sharma
et al. (2021) further confirmed this relationship, indicating that favorable consumer
evaluations of technology can encourage stronger behavioral intention.

are omitted because the study focuses on Indonesian OTA users, while experience is
excluded due to the cross-sectional research design (Indrawati & Utama, 2018). Thus, the
modified framework integrates UTAUT with perceived trust and attitude to explain
consumers’ intention to purchase OTA services in Indonesia, as presented in Figure 1.

RESEARCH METHODS
This is quantitative causal research. This study uses quantitative approaches to test
hypotheses using numbers and statistical analyses. This research uses the Likert scale, a
rating measure. This tiered scale gives the data an ordinal level of measurement. This
study’s five-point Likert scale should yield reliable findings. Data were collected through
a structured questionnaire distributed to eligible OTA users in Indonesia. The population
is not merely the quantity of the entity being investigated, but also contains its qualities.
This research includes all OTA users in Indonesia, whose number is unknown. A sample
is a segment of the population that researchers select to collect  research data and
information. This research samples OTA users in Indonesia who are 17 or older and have
an ID card to examine the factors influencing OTA adoption.
Lemeshow formula is used to calculate the sample size of an unknown population.
This research will employ a minimum sample of 385 respondents (n = 384.16), although
the authors round it up to 400 to reduce questionnaire mistakes. Non-probability sampling
with accidental sampling is utilized in this study. Meetings with the researcher may be
used as a sample, provided the individual is appropriate as a data source. Respondents
were selected based on their eligibility as OTA users in Indonesia and their abili ty to
provide information relevant to the research objectives.
The study employs seven variables measured using 28 indicators. Perceived trust,
performance expectancy, and effort expectancy each consist of four indicators. Social
influence and facilitating conditions each consist of three indicators. User attitude and
intention to purchase OTA are measured using five indicators. These variables represent
the key factors examined to explain consumers’ intention to purchase or use OTA
services. The research instrument consisted of structured questionnaire items measuring

Partial Least Squares analysis is used in this paper. Partial Least Squares (PLS) was

size need not be large to use indicators with categorical, ordinal, interval, and ratio scales
in the same model. Data analysis was conducted using SmartPLS 3.2.9.

is used to gather data from 400 adult Indonesians who have used an OTA and are either

DISCUSSION
The findings demonstrate that the proposed structural model meaningfully explains
consumers’ intention to purchase travel services through OTA in Indonesia. Perceived
trust, performance expectancy, and effort expectancy positively influence user attitude,
while perceived trust, performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence,
facilitating conditions, and user attitude positively influence purchase intention. These
findings highlight the relevance of both technological and psychological factors i n
explaining online travel purchase behavior. Perceived trust positively influences user
attitude, indicating that consumers develop more favorable attitudes toward OTA services
when they perceive the platform as trustworthy. Trust can reduce uncertainty in online
transactions and strengthen consumers’ engagement with digital travel services, consistent
with Khoa (2020), Albaity and Rahman (2021), and Raza et al. (2021). Moreover,
perceived trust has a positive and significant direct effect on purchase intention, suggesting
that trust not only fosters favorable attitudes but also directly strengthens consumers’
willingness to purchase travel services online. These findings support the relevance of
incorporating trust as an extension of the UTAUT framework in the OTA context.
Performance expectancy positively influences both user attitude and intention to
purchase OTA services. Consumers are more likely to develop favorable attitudes when

by Widayat et al. (2020), Altalhi (2021), Shiferaw et al. (2021), and Jena (2022). However,

finding suggests that ease of use remains relevant in the Indonesian OTA context, both in
shaping user attitude and encouraging purchase intention.
Social influence and facilitating conditions also positively affect intention to purchase
OTA services. Social influence indicates that recommendations and expectations from
people around consumers can contribute to their intention to use OTA services, consistent
with Catherine et al. (2017), López-Pérez et al. (2019), and Yang et al. (2021). Meanwhile,
facilitating conditions indicate that the availability of supporting resources and
technological infrastructure can encourage consumers to engage in online travel
purchasing. This finding is consistent with Bongso and Dewi (2021) and Wulandari et al.
(2022).
User attitude positively influences intention to purchase OTA services, indicating that
favorable perceptions toward OTA technology can translate into stronger purchase
intentions. This finding is consistent with Rana et al. (2017), Choe and Kim (2018),
Dwivedi et al. (2019), and Kemp et al. (2019). The findings extend the UTAUT
perspective by demonstrating that technology-related expectations, social and facilitating
conditions, trust, and attitude can jointly explain OTA purchase intention in Indonesia.
This supports the argument that technology acceptance models should be examined
within specific national and contextual settings rather than generalized across countries
(Venkatesh et al., 2012; Ashraf et al., 2014; Lee, 2016; Sharma et al., 2020).

CONCLUSION
This study provides insights into the factors influencing consumers ’ intention to
purchase travel products through OTAs in Indonesia. The findings indicate that
consumers’ purchase intentions are shaped by their perceptions of platform performance,
trust, social influence, facilitating conditions, and attitudes toward OTA usage. These
findings suggest that OTA providers need to maintain reliable platform performance,
provide clear and accurate information, strengthen transaction security, and offer
convenient purchasing processes. Improving customer support, payment options, and
overall user experience may also encourage consumers to use OTAs more frequently. In
addition, positive customer reviews and recommendations can strengthen social influence
and consumer confidence.
Several limitations should be considered when interpreting these findings. The use of
convenience sampling may limit the generalizability of the results, while the demographic
characteristics of the sample may not fully represent the broader Indonesian population.
Furthermore, the study examines OTA purchases as a broad category, even though
consumers may have different decision-making processes depending on the type and
complexity of travel products purchased. The study also focuses on purchase intention
rather than actual purchasing behavior and does not distinguish between different devices
used for online transactions. The findings are limited to Indonesia.
