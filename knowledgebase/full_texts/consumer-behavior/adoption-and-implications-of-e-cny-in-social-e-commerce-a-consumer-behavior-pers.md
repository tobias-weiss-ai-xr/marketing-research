Abstract
In the context of rapid growth in social e-commerce, digital currencies face dual challenges of market
promotion and sustainable usage. To mitigate user attrition stemming from increasingly integrated
usage scenarios and safeguard the position of existing currencies, issuing authorities must develop
promotion strategies tailored to consumer habits. Drawing on a survey of 334 respondents, this study
employs a consumer behavior model to systematically examine the factors inﬂuencing the adoption of
e-CNY in social e-commerce. The ﬁndings reveal that user experience in social e-commerce signiﬁcantly
stimulates sharing behaviors and heightens consumer acceptance of e-CNY, while payment security
exerts a particularly strong impact on payment decisions. This research expands consumer behavior
theory, broadens the scope of digital currency usage studies, and offers strategic insights for issuing
institutions seeking to promote the widespread adoption of e-CNY in social e-commerce.
1. Introduction
The rapid expansion of the digital economy and continual innovations in ﬁnancial technology have
catalyzed the emergence of digital currency as a groundbreaking payment instrument, gradually
transforming the global payment landscape [1]. Beyond performing the conventional functions of
traditional currency—serving as a store of value, medium of exchange, and unit of account—digital
currencies leverage cutting-edge technologies such as blockchain and big data, thereby optimizing
payment processes, strengthening transaction security, and enhancing regulatory transparency [2]. To
foster adoption, most central banks and issuing authorities utilize policy guidance and regulatory
frameworks that safeguard privacy, security, and stability [3]. Nevertheless, some non-state actors
promote third-party virtual currency platforms to cultivate user payment habits, potentially creating
barriers to the oﬃcial use of central bank digital currencies [4].
Meanwhile, social e-commerce platforms have risen to prominence by capitalizing on their social
interaction features and convenient purchasing experiences, thereby attracting a substantial user base.
These platforms not only shape consumers’ payment habits but also inﬂuence the acceptance of digital
currencies. Consequently, investigating digital currency adoption requires addressing not only
institutional credibility, security, and privacy [5] but also the emotional and social factors that affect user
willingness to adopt [6]. In this regard, China’s rapidly evolving social e-commerce ecosystems—
exempliﬁed by platforms such as TikTok and rednote—along with the e-CNY’s status as the country’s
oﬃcial digital currency [7], highlight the need to explore how digital currencies can be effectively
promoted in these burgeoning contexts [8]. Hence, this paper focuses on e-CNY as a representative case.
Although prior research provides preliminary insights into the factors driving e-CNY uptake, few studies
have addressed its adoption in social e-commerce contexts. Existing literature often centers on
technological dimensions—such as security, privacy, and legal frameworks [9]—or examines how
perceived willingness, trust, and security shape individual payment intentions [6]. Furthermore,
Page 2/23

discussions of digital currency promotion have largely remained at the policy level, resulting in limited
guidance on how to integrate e-CNY effectively into social e-commerce platforms.
To bridge this gap, our study investigates the underlying mechanisms inﬂuencing e-CNY adoption in
social e-commerce. Drawing upon data from 334 respondents, we incorporate user characteristics and
consumption patterns to enhance sample representativeness. Speciﬁcally, we identify a series of key
factors—platform attributes, security concerns, and economic incentives—and employ a consumer
behavior model, coupled with regression analysis, to explore how habitual payment behaviors form
around e-CNY within social e-commerce settings.
The objectives and contributions of this research are multifaceted. First, we systematically analyze how
social e-commerce contexts inﬂuence users’ decisions to adopt and continuously use e-CNY. Second, we
develop a reﬁned consumer behavior framework that elucidates the interplay between selection, usage,
and sharing behaviors in socially interactive environments. Finally, by distinguishing critical elements
such as payment security and platform features, we provide actionable insights for policymakers and
platform operators. In doing so, this study both advances theoretical understanding of digital currency
adoption and offers practical strategies for optimizing platform design, user experience, and deployment
efforts to promote the widespread acceptance of e-CNY.
2. Literature Review and Research Hypotheses
The growing prevalence of social media and the rapid expansion of e-commerce have propelled social e-
commerce into the spotlight as an emerging business model. Simultaneously, the advent of digital
currency as a novel payment method is poised to reshape e-commerce platforms, business operations,
and consumer behaviors within social e-commerce contexts.
2.1. Consumer Behavior in Digital Currency Adoption
Current studies on digital currency implementation in China generally span ﬁve major domains:
technological implementation, policy implementation, industry implementation, social service
implementation, and user-level analysis. This section synthesizes key ﬁndings in each domain, providing
the basis for developing the research hypotheses on the adoption of China e-CNY in social e-commerce
settings.
2.1.1. Technological Attributes
Early discussions at the “New Developments in Digital Currency” seminar in January 2016 explored the
essence of modern currency under the inﬂuence of electronic payment systems and virtual currency
technologies, highlighting international economic challenges such as the erosion of currency sovereignty
in cross-border transactions [12]. Yet, the restructuring of the digital currency ecosystem also presents
fresh growth opportunities. From a technological standpoint, digital currency typically ﬂows from central
bank reserves through circulation channels and ﬁnally into end-user storage media (i.e., digital wallets)
Page 3/23

[13]. Although transactions appear purely digital at the user interface, the currency itself is backed by
national credit and facilitated by advanced information technologies.
In China, the People’s Bank of China (PBoC) established a digital currency research team in 2014,
carrying out forward-looking research on core technologies, issuance frameworks, circulation channels,
and international experiences. This culminated in the founding of the Digital Currency Research Institute
in 2016, marking the world’s ﬁrst oﬃcial research institution dedicated to central bank digital currencies.
The same year witnessed the construction of an initial prototype for a digital currency system. By late
2017, the PBoC gained State Council approval to collaborate with commercial institutions in developing
the China e-CNY technology infrastructure, thus aligning digital currency increasingly closely with
contemporary lifestyles. Alongside these technological advances, the PBoC continues to prioritize
security research, ensuring the robustness of China e‐CNY in broader economic applications.
2.1.2. Social Inﬂuence
In the domain of social services, China e-CNY has primarily been applied to education, healthcare, social
welfare, and other public services. Issuing institutions frequently collaborate with service organizations
to offer China e‐CNY payment options [16]. Certain municipalities encourage citizens to open and utilize
China e‐CNY wallets by distributing vouchers for public transportation, social insurance, or utility
payments [17]. These initiatives often feature promotional discounts, thereby incentivizing continued
usage and cultivating routine payment habits. Accordingly, researchers have begun to examine how
these strategies shape user preferences, potentially boosting the currency’s perceived value and
adoption [18].
Studies focusing on user-level China e-CNY implementation remain relatively limited and tend to
emphasize user surveys, experience and satisfaction assessments, as well as security awareness.
Survey-based research typically examines user acceptance, attitudes, and willingness to use China e‐
CNY, investigating variables such as familiarity, usage intentions, and existing payment habits [19]. By
contrast, research on user experience and satisfaction often draws on user feedback to improve design
features related to convenience, security, speed, and functionality, thereby increasing payment frequency,
amounts, and overall usage rates [20]. Lastly, discussions of user security awareness highlight
individuals’ perceptions of payment risks, protection measures, and privacy concerns, leading to
enhanced security settings and educational initiatives aimed at reinforcing consumer trust.
2.1.3. Policy Embeddedness
From a policy perspective, the deployment of China e-CNY involves pilot testing, promotional initiatives,
and the design of application scenarios. Beginning in 2020, the PBoC conducted closed pilots in
Shenzhen, Xiong’an, Chengdu, and certain Winter Olympics venues; these were later expanded to
Shanghai, Dalian, and other regions, forming a “10 + 1” pilot pattern. In addition, local governments such
as Changshu in Jiangsu Province have pioneered the use of China e‐CNY in public sector salary
disbursements [14]. Coupled with favorable economic policies—such as fee-free transfers between digital

wallets and bank cards—these efforts have fostered broader consumer acceptance. Internationally, the
2023 Hangzhou Asian Games provided a unique opportunity to pilot China e‐CNY in a semi-closed
international environment [15].
Thus far, the PBoC has collaborated with multiple countries, including Singapore, the United Arab
Emirates, Thailand, and Malaysia, to examine cross-border usage scenarios, international promotion, and
blockchain technology cooperation. Notably, Singapore and the UAE have already initiated pilot programs
to facilitate China e-CNY payments in their ﬁnancial systems, a move that may streamline cross-border
trade and ﬁnancial activities. The ongoing progress of these initiatives is anticipated to bolster the global
proﬁle and application of China e‐CNY.
Beyond government agencies and major banks, smaller banks, enterprises, and other institutions are
progressively contributing to an expanding China e-CNY application ecosystem. At the operational level,
banks not only offer oﬄine services but also leverage supportive economic policies to engage
businesses and individual customers. Collaborations with banks, payment institutions, and e-commerce
platforms signiﬁcantly expand the reach of China e‐CNY while enriching its application scenarios.
Furthermore, the sustained operation of China e-CNY depends on robust technical infrastructure,
including payment systems and blockchain technologies, which jointly ensure security, user
convenience, and scalability. Strategic partnerships play a pivotal role by contributing technical
expertise, conducting market outreach, and enhancing user education. To date, China e‐CNY has found
notable applications in sectors such as tourism, retail, and dining, providing concrete examples of how
widespread adoption in one industry can drive uptake in others.
Recent advancements in social e-commerce research emphasize the interplay between platform
features and user behavior. For instance, demonstrate that live-streaming commerce ampliﬁes users'
willingness to adopt new payment tools through real-time social interactions. [36] Similarly, their work on
ride-hailing drivers reveals how platform governance and incentive structures shape behavioral loyalty—a
ﬁnding particularly relevant to e-CNY's integration into social commerce ecosystems.[38][39]
Despite growing interest in the technological and policy dimensions of China e-CNY, gaps persist in
understanding how these factors interact with social e-commerce environments and user behaviors.
Building on the aforementioned studies, the subsequent section develops research hypotheses to
investigate the adoption and continuous usage of China e‐CNY in social e-commerce settings.
2.2. Theoretical Basis and Research Hypotheses
Social e-commerce is a rapidly evolving business model that integrates social media and e-commerce,
enabling the promotion and sale of products through social networking platforms. Since the introduction
of e-CNY by the Chinese government, the applicability of e-CNY within social e-commerce has garnered
signiﬁcant attention. Typically, social e-commerce platforms rely on user interactions and social
connections, which not only facilitate communication around e-CNY but also help maintain secure
transaction environments. Broadly speaking, the performance of e-CNY in social e-commerce can be
Page 5/23

divided into two core components: users’ completion of payments via QR codes or phone numbers—
highlighting payment selection and convenience—and their ability to involve other users through digital
red envelopes and coupons, emphasizing sharing and social dynamics.
2.2.1. Theoretical Basis
Drawing on the Uniﬁed Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT) and the Technology
Acceptance Model (TAM), this study extends these frameworks by incorporating social dynamics unique
to e-commerce platforms. Recent studies by Zhou further highlight the role of social capital and network
effects in technology adoption, providing critical insights for integrating social commerce contexts. [37]
The integration of digital currencies, particularly China's e-CNY, within social e-commerce ecosystems
presents a compelling synergy between technological innovation and consumer-centric platforms. As a
state-backed digital currency, e-CNY inherently provides enhanced cryptographic security, transactional
eﬃciency, and cost optimization—attributes that directly address critical requirements for scalability and
trust in social commerce environments. Scholarly investigations into its adoption dynamics have
bifurcated along two primary axes:
Consumer Behavior Perspective: Grounded in established frameworks such as the Technology
Acceptance Model (TAM) and Uniﬁed Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT), extant
research elucidates how perceived value, privacy assurance, and operational convenience shape user
adoption intentions [21].
Institutional Perspective: Organizational adoption drivers, including technological infrastructure maturity,
regulatory alignment, and incentive design, have been analyzed through lenses of stakeholder theory and
service-dominant logic [22].
Notably, the embedded social graph architecture of e-commerce platforms introduces a novel dimension
to adoption mechanics. Emerging evidence suggests that network externalities—where user adoption
propensity increases with peer participation—and social capital accumulation signiﬁcantly amplify e-
CNY’s diffusion within closed-loop communities [23]. This positions social commerce as a catalytic
environment for testing hypotheses about viral adoption patterns in central bank digital currencies.
Despite burgeoning interest from policymakers and industry consortia [24], scholarly focus remains
disproportionately allocated to:Technical implementation challenges [25]Macro-regulatory frameworks
[26]Market penetration metrics [27][28]
A critical lacuna persists in micro-level behavioral analyses, speciﬁcally: How relational embeddedness
moderates risk perception; The role of platform-native gamiﬁcation in habit formation; Longitudinal
effects of social normative pressures on sustained usage.
This study bridges these gaps by interrogating e-CNY adoption through a tripartite behavioral lens—
acceptance triggers, experience sedimentation, and network propagation. By synthesizing discrete

behavioral constructs into an integrated social commerce adoption model, we advance both theoretical
understanding of CBDC integration mechanisms and actionable strategies for optimizing platform-
currency symbiosis.
2.2.2. Research Hypotheses
Social e-commerce, merging social media and online retail, has quickly reshaped consumer behavior.
Since the rollout of e-CNY by the Chinese government, digital currency usage in social e-commerce
contexts has become a focal point for researchers. These platforms allow social interactions that
facilitate clear communication about digital payments while presenting avenues to strengthen security
mechanisms. Conceptually, e-CNY usage in social e-commerce unfolds through three sequential stages
—selection, payment, and sharing—each shaped by distinct factors. Building on consumer behavior
theory and prior research, we propose two overarching relationships:
H1: The selection of e-CNY in social e-commerce directly affects payment behavior.
H2: Payment behavior involving e-CNY in social e-commerce directly affects sharing behavior.
These propositions imply that users’ initial decision to adopt e-CNY precedes and inﬂuences actual
payment actions, while successful or positive payment experiences lay the groundwork for subsequent
sharing activities.
Although e-CNY was initially promoted for oﬄine payment scenarios, its usage for online transactions
historically required speciﬁc application environments, and many social e-commerce platforms did not
yet support e-CNY, making user choice impossible [29]. With the evolving integration of e-CNY and social
e-commerce, some social platforms have begun allowing e-CNY payments [30]. Simultaneously, certain
payment scenarios exclusive to e-CNY are now accessible through social e-commerce, giving users
genuine options. “Selection” refers to consumers’ belief that a particular payment service can boost
payment eﬃciency or service quality; such a belief is considered a key factor inﬂuencing subsequent
payment behaviors. Past studies show that when consumers perceive an evident advantage to a
payment service, their likelihood of adoption increases [31]. Social e-commerce support for e-CNY—
through, for instance, legal-restriction-friendly scenarios or economic beneﬁts—further raises consumer
acceptance and selection intentions [32]. Conversely, alternative payment tools often hinge on market
acceptance, driven by user numbers and entrenched usage habits. Within this selection stage (covering
need recognition, information search, and evaluation), four main factors emerge:
H1a: A larger existing user base for e-CNY in social e-commerce increases the likelihood of new user
selection.
H1b: Stronger platform support for e-CNY in social e-commerce raises the probability of user selection.
H1c: More favorable economic incentives (e.g., discounts, cashback) in social e-commerce increase the
probability of user selection.
Page 7/23

H1d: Heightened regulatory or practical restrictions on alternative payment methods enhance the
likelihood of e-CNY selection.
After users decide to adopt e-CNY, they proceed to the payment stage, where they scan QR codes, input
phone numbers, or engage in other digital processes. Studies suggest that users’ willingness to continue
or recommend a payment method is inﬂuenced by their prior payment experiences [33]. In social e-
commerce, perceived security and ease of operation shape those experiences. Thus:
H2a: Higher perceived security of e-CNY transactions in social e-commerce boosts the likelihood of user
payment.
H2b: Greater perceived convenience of e-CNY transactions (e.g. simpliﬁed steps, user-friendly
interfaces) enhances the likelihood of user payment.
Finally, once a payment is completed, users may engage in sharing behaviors, such as distributing digital
red envelopes or recommending e-CNY to peers. Research shows that sharing can signiﬁcantly affect
acceptance attitudes and intentions for continued use; in turn, acceptance levels can inﬂuence sharing
[34]. Social inﬂuence within e-commerce settings may also elevate a product’s perceived value, prompting
users to keep using it and advocate it to their network [35]. By fostering a positive loop of user
satisfaction and loyalty, sharing behavior can further reinforce acceptance attitudes and overall usage
experience. Hence, we propose:
H3a: Closer social relationships lead to more frequent sharing of e-CNY.
H3b: Stronger positive user experiences with e-CNY (e.g., satisfaction, trust) increase sharing frequency.
H3c: More favorable social contexts or norms (e.g., peer inﬂuence, group events) positively impact
sharing frequency.
H3d: A higher level of e-CNY usage bolsters user experience, potentially reinforcing future sharing.
These sub-hypotheses underscore the pivotal role of interpersonal networks, perceived satisfaction, and
social settings in driving broader e-CNY adoption. Altogether, the proposed framework provides a
roadmap for understanding how selection, payment, and sharing behaviors intersect within social e-
commerce, paving the way for more effective digital currency strategies.
2.2.3. Constructing a Behavioral Factor Model
By integrating insights from consumer behavior theory and prior research on digital currency, we
establish a three-stage model (Select, Pay, and Share) to capture how China e‐CNY is adopted and used
in social e-commerce. As illustrated in Fig. 1, the constructs acceptance, platform support, economy and
legal restrictions collectively shape the select stage, each path represented by sub-hypotheses (H1a,
H1b, H1c, H1d). Once users select China e‐CNY as a payment method (H1), factors such as security
(H2a) and convenience (H2b) further inﬂuence whether they proceed to the pay stage. The outcome of

pay then informs the share stage (H2), where aspects including acceptance (H3a), user experience (H3b,
H3d), and social situation (H3c) impact the frequency and willingness to share or recommend China e‐
CNY
In this framework, the construct acceptance not only drives users’ initial decision to adopt China e-CNY
(H1a) but also shapes their ongoing experiences (H3d) and sharing intentions (H3a). User experience
emerges as another key factor in promoting sharing behaviors (H3b), while social situation (e.g., peer

3. Research Design and Methodology
3.1. Sampling and Data Collection
To empirically test our research framework, we designed a survey questionnaire that strictly adheres to
the principles of clarity, purpose, and authenticity. First, we conducted a pre-survey with 30 experienced
China e-CNY users to identify any ambiguities in wording or content. The pre-test involved 30
participants, achieving Cronbach's α = 0.83 for core constructs. Based on their feedback, we reﬁned the
questionnaire structure and phrasing to minimize bias arising from unclear expressions. Subsequently,
we introduced constraint options to ensure respondent eligibility, as outlined by:
(1) Participants should possess basic Internet knowledge and be proﬁcient in online operations.
(2) All responses should reﬂect the respondents’ actual experiences with China e-CNY.
(3) Evaluations regarding China e-CNY usage must derive from direct user experience rather than
hearsay.
(4)Attention-check questions (e.g., 'Please select "Strongly Disagree" for this item') were embedded to
ﬁlter low-quality responses.
Following these reﬁnements, we distributed the questionnaire online over the course of one month,
allowing for random sampling across different regions. A stratiﬁed random sampling approach was
employed, covering six major regions in China (East, North, Central, South, Northeast, Northwest) based
on the National Bureau of Statistics' e-commerce user demographics. A total of 354 responses were
received, of which 335 remained valid after removing logically inconsistent or incomplete entries. The
response rates are shown in Table 1, indicating a 95.44% effectiveness.

4.4.1. Selection Behavior Explanation
Legal restrictions emerge as a particularly strong driver—evidently, regulations or policy constraints can
signiﬁcantly increase China e-CNY adoption. Additionally, economic incentives (e.g., discounts)
positively inﬂuence selection, while higher usage costs can deter it. Factors tied to user acceptance—
such as sharing frequency, friend ratio, payment frequency, and product variety—also bolster selection.
Finally, platform support is most prominently affected by product or service support, though merchant
endorsements and reliable hardware infrastructure further strengthen user conﬁdence.
4.4.2. Payment Behavior Explanation
In this stage, selection behavior remains the primary determinant; the decision to adopt China e-CNY
naturally fuels actual payment actions. Convenience—especially in operational simplicity—signiﬁcantly
enhances the likelihood of completing a payment. Meanwhile, Security perceptions, shaped by the
credibility of the issuing institution and the reliability of the procedural system, moderate users’
willingness to ﬁnalize transactions on social e-commerce platforms.
4.4.3. Sharing Behavior Explanation
User experience is the dominant factor shaping sharing, with positive payment experiences (e.g., ease of
use, speedy transactions) spurring stronger inclination to share. Social situation—including close social
relationships and peer inﬂuence—further motivates users to distribute digital red envelopes or
recommend China e-CNY to others. Interestingly, professional background can yield negative or mixed
effects; for instance, teachers and government oﬃcials exhibit higher caution, whereas enterprise staff

and students appear more open. Overall, frequent sharing strengthens acceptance, creates a positive
feedback loop on user experience, and encourages future sharing.
In sum, these results highlight the interplay among legal, economic, social, and experiential factors in
driving China e-CNY adoption and continuous usage. The stepwise regression approach conﬁrms the
robustness of our proposed model, thereby providing meaningful implications for policymakers, platform
operators, and users alike.
5. Discussion
With the rapid expansion of social e-commerce and the evolving role of social networks within e-
commerce platforms, China e-CNY is increasingly recognized as a promising future payment method.
Through social relationships and network effects, China e‐CNY adoption has attracted extensive
attention from researchers and practitioners. Two central questions emerge: How can China e‐CNY
provide a more convenient, secure, and eﬃcient transaction method on social e-commerce platforms?
And how can these platforms establish a governance model that is both effective and user-friendly?
Building on our empirical study and prior literature, we ﬁnd that China e-CNY, as a digital currency issued
by the People’s Bank of China, offers enhanced security and potentially reduces transaction costs
relative to other electronic payment tools. Existing research and pilot studies have focused on
technology promotion, policy and legal frameworks, industry-based use cases, and public service
campaigns. These initiatives aim to guarantee the technical soundness of China e‐CNY and ensure user-
friendly payment experiences. However, merely raising awareness of user satisfaction and acceptance
does not necessarily guarantee sustained usage. Instead, leveraging social relationships—via sharing
and word-of-mouth—can be pivotal in forming persistent payment habits and expanding user
communities.

5.2. Practical Implications and Recommendations
Our analysis underscores that government-led initiatives, policy incentives, and ecological expansions
can accelerate the digital transition. Nevertheless, sustaining user interest and habit formation over the
long term remains challenging, especially in markets with numerous competing payment tools. Social e-
commerce brings both opportunities and obstacles: while peer inﬂuence can nurture quicker
acceptance, the same networks might amplify user concerns about cost or privacy.
Considering these ﬁndings, we propose the following measures to foster robust and enduring use of
China e-CNY:
5.2.1Micro-Level
User education programs mimicking WeChat Pay's "Red Envelope" campaigns
Develop Incentive Programs Based on Social Relationships: Coordinate with local governments, industry
associations, and prominent merchants or inﬂuencers to launch campaigns featuring China e-CNY
consumption vouchers, digital red envelopes, or real-time discounts. Innovative formats—such as “friend
invitations”, shared crowdfunding, or specialized discount groups—can further spur transaction volume
and community engagement. Since many existing social platforms allow in-app payment using virtual
currencies, integrating a nationally issued digital currency can broaden its usage frequency and enhance
platform governance.
Target Speciﬁc Professional Groups for Promotion: The model veriﬁcation suggests that certain
professions correlate negatively with social interaction variables. Consequently, efforts to encourage
usage among ﬁnancial institutions, UnionPay, insurance companies, and intermediaries could be
beneﬁcial. These entities typically possess extensive social networks and can incorporate China e-CNY
into wages, social security, or employee beneﬁt programs, thereby broadening both user populations and
transactional platforms.
5.2.2Meso-Level
Standardized API interfaces between social platforms and e-CNY wallets
Continuously Expand Usage Scenarios and Strengthen Functional Expectations: By collaborating with
commercial institutions, social e-commerce platforms can diversify payment scenarios (e.g. lifestyle
services, municipal services) that stimulate frequent usage. Deepening partnerships among
governments, enterprises, and banks helps create a richer ecosystem in which users recognize tangible
value from adopting China e-CNY.
Page 18/23

Optimize Payment Processes to Enhance Convenience: Given that China e-CNY usage can be region-
speciﬁc and often relies on third-party technical infrastructures, future improvements should address
friction points. For instance, reﬁning the user interface, shortening transaction steps, and ensuring stable
connectivity can signiﬁcantly boost users’ perceived convenience and willingness to transact regularly.
5.2.3Macro-Level
Cross-ministerial regulatory sandbox for social commerce transactions
Reﬁne Legal and Regulatory Frameworks: Although China e-CNY’s issuance and circulation have
progressed rapidly, corresponding regulations still lag behind practice. Payment services like peer-to-
peer transfers are restricted by current legal provisions. Therefore, establishing updated online payment
rules for digital currency—covering issuance, circulation, and dispute resolution—is critical to ensuring
that any emerging legal issues have a transparent basis.
6. Conclusion
This study is limited by its cross-sectional design and regional sampling bias. Future research should
employ longitudinal tracking across multiple pilot cities to capture dynamic adoption patterns.
The introduction of digital currency holds the potential to signiﬁcantly advance social e-commerce. By
offering a safer, more convenient, and cost-effective payment tool, digital currency can foster payment
innovations and marketing opportunities, strengthen social relationships and information dissemination,
and increase user trust in transactions, thereby boosting product sales. Nonetheless, its effective
integration into social e-commerce hinges on resolving technical, legal, and regulatory challenges.
Policymakers therefore have a vital role in shaping digital currency adoption, making it imperative to
build a payment ecosystem that not only meets international standards but also ensures a smooth
transition for users.
From an industry perspective, shifting to digital currency confers numerous beneﬁts and opportunities:
companies can optimize payment processes, cut transaction costs, and enhance the user experience,
while expanded technical infrastructure paves the way for new market segments. Moreover, the
involvement of multiple industries in digital currency transactions promotes the continuous
improvement of the payment ecosystem. As digital currency continues to permeate social e-commerce,
growing collaboration among social e-commerce platforms, ﬁnancial institutions, and technology
providers will likely result in a more holistic and resilient payment environment. At the same time,
strengthening relevant policies and governance rules remains crucial to sustaining the stability and
integrity of this evolving ecosystem.
It is worth noting that the scope of this study was somewhat constrained by the limited sample size,
yielding only a moderately satisfactory model ﬁt. Consequently, closer cooperation among government
agencies, private enterprises, and academic researchers is needed to reﬁne empirical evaluations and
Page 19/23

theoretical models, thereby ensuring a deeper and more nuanced understanding of digital currency’s role
in social e-commerce.
