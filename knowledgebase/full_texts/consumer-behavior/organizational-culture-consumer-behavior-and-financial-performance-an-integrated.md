Abstract
Organizations increasingly seek sustainable competitive advantage through effective utilization of internal capabilities and
customer-oriented strategies. The present study examines the relationships among organizational culture, consumer
behavior, and financial performance through an integrated conceptual framework grounded in Resource-Based View and
Stakeholder Theory. The study investigates how organizational culture influences financial outcomes directly and
indirectly through consumer behavior. A quantitative research design was employed using a structured questionnaire
adapted from previously validated measurement scales. Data were analyzed using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation
Modeling (PLS-SEM) through ADANCO software. Reliability and validity assessments confirmed satisfactory psychometric
properties of the measurement model. The findings revealed that organizational culture significantly and positively
influences consumer behavior, while consumer behavior demonstrates a significant positive effect on financial
performance. The mediation analysis further indicated that consumer behavior fully mediates the relationship between
organizational culture and financial performance. The results suggest that organizational culture alone may not directly
generate financial benefits but contributes to organizational success through its impact on customer-related behavioral
outcomes. The study contributes to existing literature by integrating organizational and consumer perspectives within a
single framework and provides practical insights for managers seeking to enhance organizational performance through
customer-centered cultural strategies and long-term relationship development.

Introduction
Organizations increasingly operate in highly competitive
and dynamic market environments where sustained
performance depends on both internal organizational
capabilities and external market responses. Traditional
approaches to organizational success have often focused
on financial indicators such as profitability, return on
investment, and revenue growth as primary measures of
performance (Indawati et al., 2024). Recent
developments in management and consumer research
indicate that organizational success emerges from a
complex interaction between internal organizational
factors and customer-oriented outcomes (Mittal et al.,
2023). Among these factors, organizational culture has
gained substantial attention as a strategic asset capable
of shaping organizational effectiveness and long-term
sustainability. Organizational culture represents the
shared values, beliefs, norms, and behavioral
expectations that guide organizational members in their
daily activities (Sailan Masunka et al., 2022). A strong
organizational culture creates a work environment that
influences employee behavior, decision-making processes,
innovation capability, and service orientation (Sailan
Masunka et al., 2022). Firms with adaptive and
customer-centered cultures frequently demonstrate
superior responsiveness to market changes and
enhanced organizational performance (Reza & Silalahi,
2021). Culture extends beyond internal operational
functions and influences how organizations interact
with customers and create value in competitive markets
(Savale, 2025).
Consumer behavior has emerged as a critical
determinant of organizational success in modern
business environments. The increasing availability of
information, digital interactions, and changing customer
expectations have transformed consumer decision-
making patterns (Ibrahim et al., 2024; Sharlet Asha et al.,
2026). Consumer behavior encompasses customer
attitudes, preferences, purchase intentions, loyalty, and
post-purchase evaluations that collectively determine
market outcomes(Venkatesh, 2024) . Positive consumer

behavior contributes to customer retention, repeat
purchases, and favorable word-of-mouth communication,
all of which strengthen organizational competitiveness
(Gravite-Lapere, 2022). Firms are no longer evaluated
solely by product quality or price competitiveness;
customers increasingly assess organizational values,
service experiences, and overall engagement practices
(Nurhilalia & Saleh, 2024). Existing studies have
independently examined the relationship between
organizational culture and organizational outcomes as
well as the impact of consumer behavior on business
performance. Research has demonstrated that a strong

engagement, innovation, and operational efficiency,
while consumer behavior has been associated with
improved sales performance and market growth

literature investigates these relationships separately and
provides limited understanding of the mechanism
through which organizational culture influences
financial outcomes through customer-related processes
(Lim et al., 2020). The pathways connecting internal
organizational characteristics with external market
performance remain insufficiently integrated within a
unified framework.
This gap creates an important theoretical and practical
concern. Organizations invest significant resources in
strengthening cultural values and improving customer
engagement strategies, although the mechanisms
explaining how these investments translate into
measurable financial outcomes remain underexplored
(Connell et al., 2022). An integrated perspective may
provide a more comprehensive explanation of
organizational performance by connecting internal
organizational dynamics with market responses. The
present study addresses this gap by developing and
examining an integrated framework linking
organizational culture, consumer behavior, and financial
performance. The study proposes that organizational
culture influences consumer behavior, which
subsequently contributes to improved financial
performance. Consumer behavior is expected to act as a
mediating mechanism that explains the relationship
between organizational culture and organizational
financial outcomes. By integrating organizational and
consumer perspectives within a single model, this
research contributes to management literature by
extending understanding of how internal organizational
characteristics create financial value through customer-
drivenprocesses.
The findings of this study are expected to provide
practical insights for managers and organizational
leaders seeking to enhance performance through
strategic cultural development and customer-focused
initiatives. The proposed framework may assist
organizations in understanding the interconnected role
of organizational culture and consumer behavior in
achievingsustainable financial success.
2. Literature Review
2.1 Theoretical Foundation
The present study is primarily grounded in the
Resource-Based View (RBV) and supported by the
Stakeholder Theory. Resource-Based View proposes that
organizations achieve sustainable competitive advantage
through valuable, rare, inimitable, and non-substitutable
resources(Salsabila et al., 2022) . Organizational culture
is widely recognized as an intangible strategic resource
that influences employee behavior, organizational
processes, and decision-making patterns. Unlike physical
resources that can be replicated easily, organizational
culture develops gradually through shared values and
experiences, making it difficult for competitors to
imitate (Laksmi P et al., 2024). From an RBV
perspective, a strong organizational culture creates
internal capabilities that influence customer-oriented
activities and organizational outcomes (Lubis, 2022).
Firms possessing adaptive cultures are often better
positioned to understand customer needs and respond
effectively to market demands (Chatterjee et al., 2023).
These capabilities can eventually translate into favorable
customer responses and improved organizational
performance.
Stakeholder Theory provides an additional foundation
for understanding the role of consumers within
organizational success. The theory suggests that
organizations create value through interactions with
various stakeholders, among which customers represent
one of the most critical groups (Davila, 2024).
Organizational actions and internal values influence
customer perceptions, which subsequently affect
behavioral outcomes and financial results (Eskerod,
2020). Combining both theoretical perspectives offers a
broader explanation of how internal organizational
resources create external market value. The integrated
application of these theories suggests that organizational
culture acts as a strategic resource capable of influencing
consumer behavior, which subsequently contributes to
financial performance. Such a theoretical approach
supports the development of a mediation-based
framework that links organizational processes with
market outcomes.
2.2 Organizational Culture and Consumer Behavior
Organizational culture refers to the shared values, beliefs,
assumptions, and norms that shape employee attitudes
and organizational practices (Sailan Masunka et al.,
2022). Culture determines how employees interact with
customers, address organizational challenges, and
execute strategic objectives. Organizations emphasizing
customer orientation, innovation, adaptability, and
employee commitment often create environments that
facilitate stronger customer relationships (Sailan
Masunka et al., 2022). Previous research has indicated
that organizational culture affects service quality,
customer satisfaction, and customer experiences.
Employees operating within positive cultural
environments frequently demonstrate greater

responsiveness and commitment toward customer needs
(Rahmaddian et al., 2021). Such behaviors influence
consumer perceptions regarding product quality, service
reliability, and organizational trustworthiness (Nurdiani
&Alie, 2022).
Consumers increasingly evaluate organizations beyond
functional product attributes. Ethical practices,
customer orientation, organizational responsiveness, and
perceived organizational values have become influential
factors affecting consumer attitudes and purchasing
behavior (He, 2024). Strong organizational cultures may
create positive customer experiences that enhance
purchase intentions and customer loyalty (Lim et al.,
2020). Research in marketing and organizational
behavior suggests that customer-centered cultures
contribute to stronger emotional connections between
organizations and consumers. These connections often
lead to repeat purchasing behavior and long-term
customer engagement. Based on these arguments, the
following hypothesis is proposed:
H1: Organizational culture has a significant positive
effect onconsumer behavior.
2.3 Consumer Behavior and Financial Performance
Consumer behavior represents the process through
which individuals select, purchase, use, and evaluate
products and services. Modern consumer behavior
extends beyond purchasing decisions and includes
customer satisfaction, loyalty, trust, and post-purchase
experiences. These behavioral outcomes play a critical
role in determining organizational success (Saraf &
O’Malley, 2023). Financial performance reflects an
organization's ability to generate economic value
through indicators such as profitability, return on
investment, sales growth, and revenue generation
(Mohsin et al., 2020). Existing literature suggests that
favorable consumer responses contribute substantially to
organizational financial outcomes.
Customer loyalty and positive purchase intentions
influence repeat purchases and long-term revenue
generation. Satisfied consumers frequently engage in
positive word-of-mouth communication, reducing
customer acquisition costs and strengthening market
position (Chauhan, 2023). Organizations with strong
customer relationships often demonstrate higher levels
of profitability and sustainable growth. Studies in
consumer research have consistently found positive
relationships between customer satisfaction and
organizational financial performance. Increased
customer retention rates and stronger consumer
engagement frequently produce measurable financial
benefits (Fornell et al., 2020). Consumer behavior
therefore represents an important mechanism through
which organizations achieve performance objectives.
Based on these arguments, the following hypothesis is
proposed:
H2: Consumer behavior has a significant positive effect
onfinancial performance.
2.4 Mediating Role of Consumer Behavior Between
Organizational Culture and Financial Performance
The relationship between organizational culture and
financial performance may not occur directly in all
situations. Internal organizational values and practices
often influence external outcomes through customer-
related mechanisms (Abu Mahfouz & Muhumed, 2020).
Consumer behavior can therefore function as an
explanatory process connecting organizational culture
with financial results. Organizations with strong and
adaptive cultures create environments that encourage
customer-oriented practices and service excellence (Abu
Mahfouz & Muhumed, 2020). These practices influence
customer experiences and perceptions, which
subsequently affect purchasing decisions and customer
loyalty (Gokus, 2019). Positive consumer responses
eventually contribute to increased revenues and
organizational profitability.
Existing studies have separately examined the direct
relationships between organizational culture and
organizational performance as well as between customer
behavior and financial outcomes. Limited attention has
been given to understanding how consumer behavior
explains the relationship between internal organizational
characteristics and financial outcomes. From the
perspectives of Resource-Based View and Stakeholder
Theory, organizational culture creates strategic
capabilities that influence stakeholder responses.
Consumer behavior serves as a mechanism translating
these capabilities into tangible financial outcomes. The
mediating relationship offers a more comprehensive
understanding of organizational performance by
connecting internal resources with external market
responses. Based on these arguments, the following
hypothesis is proposed:
H3: Consumer behavior mediates the relationship
between organizational culture and financial
performance.
2.5 Conceptual Model
The present study proposes an integrated framework
examining the relationship among organizational culture,
consumer behavior, and financial performance.
Organizational culture is conceptualized as the
independent variable influencing consumer behavior.
Consumer behavior acts as both a dependent variable
and a mediating construct that transmits the influence
of organizational culture toward financial performance.
Financial performance is considered the final outcome
variable.

3. Methodology
The present study employed a quantitative cross-
sectional research design to examine the relationships
among organizational culture, consumer behavior, and
financial performance. A structured questionnaire
survey was used for data collection because of its
suitability for investigating latent constructs and
examining causal relationships through structural
equation modeling. The target respondents consisted of
consumers and employees possessing adequate
knowledge and experience regarding organizational
practices and customer interactions. A purposive
sampling approach was adopted to ensure that
participants had sufficient familiarity with the
organizational context under investigation. The survey
instrument consisted of previously validated scales
adapted from established studies. Organizational
Culture was measured using items adapted from the
Denison organizational culture framework focusing on
dimensions such as involvement, consistency,
adaptability, and mission (Denison & Mishra, 1995).
Consumer Behavior was measured through customer
behavioral intention dimensions including loyalty,
repeat purchase intention, and positive word-of-mouth
adapted from Zeithaml et al. (1996). Financial
Performance was measured using subjective
organizational performance indicators assessing
profitability, market performance, and sales growth
adapted from Venkatraman and Ramanujam (1986).
Minor wording modifications were introduced to align
the measurement items with the context of the present
study while preserving the conceptual meaning of the
original scales. All questionnaire items were measured
using a five-point Likert scale ranging from 1 = strongly
disagree to 5 = strongly agree. Data analysis was
conducted using ADANCO software employing Partial
Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM).
The analytical procedure involved assessment of the
measurement model through reliability and validity
analysis followed by structural model testing for

4. Results
4.1 Measurement Model Assessment
The measurement model was assessed through reliability,
convergent validity, and discriminant validity analyses.
Reliability was examined using Cronbach's alpha (α),
Dijkstra-Henseler's rho (ρA), and Jöreskog's rho (ρC).
The results indicate high internal consistency across all
constructs. Organizational Culture (IV) demonstrated α
= 0.9623, ρA = 0.9629, and ρC = 0.9624. Consumer
Behavior (MV) showed α = 0.9500, ρA = 0.9503, and
ρC = 0.9500. Financial Performance (DV) reported α =
0.9435, ρA = 0.9447, and ρC = 0.9435. All values
exceeded the recommended threshold of 0.70,
indicating satisfactory construct reliability.
Convergent validity was evaluated using Average
Variance Extracted (AVE). The AVE values for
Organizational Culture, Consumer Behavior, and
Financial Performance were 0.8100, 0.7602, and 0.7698
respectively. Since all values exceeded the recommended
threshold of 0.50, adequate convergent validity was
established. Discriminant validity was assessed through
the Fornell-Larcker criterion. The square roots of AVE
for all constructs exceeded their inter-construct
correlations, confirming satisfactory discriminant
validity among the constructs. The results indicate that
each construct was empirically distinct from the others.
4.2 Structural Model Assessment
The overall model fit was examined using Standardized
Root Mean Square Residual (SRMR), d_ULS, and d_G
values. The SRMR value was 0.0212, which falls below
the recommended threshold of 0.08, indicating a good
model fit. The observed d_ULS value (0.0686) and d_G
value (0.1707) were lower than their respective HI95
and HI99 bootstrap confidence values, suggesting
acceptable model fit and predictive quality. The
coefficient of determination (R²) was used to assess the
explanatory power of the model. Consumer Behavior
explained 64.6% of variance (R² = 0.646), while
Financial Performance explained 73.6% of variance (R²
= 0.736). These findings indicate substantial predictive
power ofthe proposed model.

4.3 Hypothesis Testing
Hypotheses were tested using bootstrap analysis. The
direct effect of Organizational Culture on Consumer
Behavior was positive and significant (β = 0.8035, t =
29.058, p < 0.001). Thus, H1 was supported. The result
indicates that stronger organizational culture
significantly enhances consumer behavior.
The direct effect of Consumer Behavior on Financial
Performance was also positive and significant (β =
0.7550, t = 10.192, p < 0.001). Hence, H2 was
supported. This finding suggests that favorable
consumer behavior contributes substantially to
organizational financial performance. The direct
relationship between Organizational Culture and
Financial Performance was positive but statistically
insignificant (β = 0.1242, t = 1.560, p = 0.1188). This
result suggests that organizational culture alone does not
directly influence financial performance.
The indirect effect analysis revealed that Organizational
Culture significantly influenced Financial Performance
through Consumer Behavior (β = 0.6066, t = 9.573, p <
0.001). Therefore, H3 was supported. The findings
indicate a full mediation effect, since the indirect
relationship was significant while the direct relationship
between Organizational Culture and Financial
Performance remained insignificant. This suggests that
organizational culture improves financial outcomes
primarily through its influence on consumer behavior.

5. Discussion and Implications
The present study examined the relationships among
organizational culture, consumer behavior, and financial
performance through an integrated framework
grounded in Resource-Based View and Stakeholder
Theory. The empirical findings provide substantial

Journal of Theoretical Accounting Research
Available online at: https://jtar.org 105
support for the proposed framework and contribute to a
deeper understanding of how internal organizational
resources influence organizational outcomes through
customer-related mechanisms. The findings indicate that
organizational culture exerts a significant influence on
consumer behavior, while consumer behavior
significantly contributes to financial performance. The
mediation results further reveal that consumer behavior
functions as a critical pathway through which
organizational culture translates into financial outcomes.
The results demonstrate a significant positive
relationship between organizational culture and
consumer behavior (β = 0.803, p < 0.001), supporting
H1. This finding suggests that organizations
characterized by strong cultural values, customer
orientation, adaptability, and shared beliefs are more
likely to create favorable customer experiences and
behavioral outcomes. Organizational culture establishes
behavioral norms that shape employee interactions and
service delivery practices. Employees operating within
supportive and customer-centered environments often
exhibit greater commitment and responsiveness toward
customer needs. Such organizational practices enhance
customer trust, satisfaction, and purchase intentions
(Wicaksono et al., 2025).
The findings are consistent with previous research
suggesting that organizational culture influences
customer perceptions and behavioral responses through
service quality and organizational effectiveness. The
result also aligns with the Resource-Based View
perspective, which considers organizational culture as a
valuable intangible asset capable of creating competitive
advantage (Metz et al., 2020). Culture does not merely
influence internal organizational functioning; it also
shapes external stakeholder experiences and customer
evaluations. The analysis also indicates a significant
positive relationship between consumer behavior and
financial performance (β = 0.755, p < 0.001), supporting
H2. This result implies that favorable customer
behaviors such as loyalty, repeat purchase intentions,
and positive word-of-mouth substantially contribute to
organizational financial outcomes. Customers
increasingly engage with organizations based on their
experiences and perceptions rather than solely on
product attributes ((Bian, 2024)). Organizations that
cultivate stronger customer relationships are likely to
experience enhanced sales growth, market performance,
and profitability.
The finding supports existing marketing and consumer
behavior literature emphasizing customer responses as
important determinants of organizational performance.
Positive customer behavior reduces customer acquisition
costs, increases customer retention rates, and
strengthens long-term revenue generation capabilities.
Financial performance therefore appears to depend not
only on operational efficiency but also on the quality of
relationships organizations establish with consumers.
One of the most important findings of the study
emerges from the mediation analysis. Consumer
behavior significantly mediated the relationship between
organizational culture and financial performance (β =
0.607, p < 0.001). At the same time, the direct
relationship between organizational culture and
financial performance was statistically insignificant (β =
0.124, p > 0.05). These findings indicate a full
mediation effect.
The mediation result suggests that organizational culture
alone may not directly generate measurable financial
gains. Internal cultural characteristics create value when
they influence customer-related outcomes that
eventually translate into financial benefits.
Organizations frequently assume that strengthening
culture will automatically improve profitability. The
present findings indicate that cultural investments
produce financial returns when they are reflected in
customer experiences and behavioral responses. This
finding offers a more nuanced understanding of
organizational performance and extends existing
literature by integrating internal organizational processes
with external market outcomes. Prior studies have often
examined organizational culture and financial
performance independently or have focused on direct
effects. The present research demonstrates that the
pathway connecting culture and financial outcomes
involves customer-centered processes that should not be
overlooked.
Theoretical Implications
The study contributes to theoretical development in
several ways. First, it extends the application of
Resource-Based View by demonstrating that
organizational culture functions as a strategic resource
capable of influencing organizational outcomes through
behavioral mechanisms rather than direct pathways
alone. Existing research frequently treats culture as an
independent predictor of organizational performance.
The present findings suggest that intermediate customer-
related variables may provide a stronger explanation of
how culture generates value. Second, the study
strengthens Stakeholder Theory by highlighting the role
of consumers as central actors in transforming internal
organizational capabilities into measurable performance
outcomes. Stakeholder Theory emphasizes the
importance of satisfying stakeholder expectations, and
the findings suggest that consumer responses represent
an important channel through which organizational
practices create value. Third, the study develops an
integrated framework connecting organizational
behavior and consumer behavior perspectives within a
single model. Such integration expands understanding
of organizational performance by linking internal
organizational resources with external market responses.

The findings generate several practical implications for
organizational leaders and managers. Managers should
recognize that organizational culture should not be
viewed solely as an internal management practice.
Culture influences customer experiences and can
ultimately determine organizational success. Investment
inemployee development, customer-oriented values, and
adaptive organizational practices can contribute to
stronger customer relationships. Organizations should
also develop strategies that align cultural values with
customer expectations. Employee training programs,
customer service initiatives, and communication systems
should reinforce behaviors that improve customer
experiences. Creating a customer-focused culture may
strengthen customer loyalty and increase repeat
purchasing behavior. Managers should avoid relying
exclusively on financial indicators while evaluating
organizational effectiveness. Customer-related indicators
such as customer satisfaction, loyalty, and purchase
intentions should be incorporated into performance
evaluation systems because they may function as early
indicators of future financial performance.
Organizations operating in competitive markets should
recognize that sustainable financial success emerges
through the interaction of internal organizational
capabilities and external customer responses.
Strengthening culture without improving customer
experiences may limit the realization of financial
benefits. Strategic attention toward customer behavior
may therefore serve as a critical mechanism for
transforming organizational resources into long-term
organizational performance. The findings collectively
indicate that organizations seeking superior financial
outcomes should move beyond isolated organizational
practices and adopt an integrated approach that
connects cultural development with customer-centered
strategies. Such an approach may provide a more
sustainable path toward organizational growth and
competitive advantage.
6. Conclusion
The present study developed and examined an
integrated framework linking organizational culture,
consumer behavior, and financial performance to
explain how internal organizational capabilities
influence external organizational outcomes. Grounded
in Resource-Based View and Stakeholder Theory, the
study investigated the direct and indirect relationships
among the constructs and provided empirical evidence
regarding the mechanisms through which organizational
culture contributes to organizational success. The
findings demonstrated that organizational culture exerts
a significant positive influence on consumer behavior,
indicating that organizations characterized by strong
values, customer orientation, and adaptive practices are
more likely to create favorable customer responses. The
study also established that consumer behavior
significantly contributes to financial performance,
emphasizing the importance of customer loyalty,
purchasing intentions, and positive customer
experiences in enhancing organizational outcomes. The
mediation analysis revealed that consumer behavior fully
mediates the relationship between organizational culture
and financial performance, suggesting that
organizational culture does not directly generate
financial benefits but creates value through customer-
related behavioral mechanisms.
These findings provide an important contribution to
existing literature by integrating organizational and
consumer perspectives within a unified framework. The
study extends theoretical understanding by
demonstrating that customer behavior serves as a critical
pathway connecting internal organizational resources
with measurable financial outcomes. From a managerial
perspective, the findings indicate that organizations
seeking sustainable financial success should align
cultural development initiatives with customer-centered
strategies. Building a strong organizational culture and
enhancing customer experiences may create long-term
competitive advantages and improve organizational
performance in increasingly dynamic and competitive
business environments. Although the present study
contributes to the literature, future research may
incorporate additional contextual variables, longitudinal
designs, and cross-industry investigations to further
validate and extend the proposed framework.
