Abstract
Contemporary consumer behavior is increasingly changing its course from complete rational models to bounded
rationales. In line with this change, the variables used in consumer behavior research are both changing and
differentiating its course. This research is focused on problematic consumer behavior and seeks answers for which
variables are most relevant with the topic an d how they are connected. With a systematic review following
PRISMA guidelines identified a total of 57 variables; however, due to the limited number of studies, only 35
variables were included . Then, 350 research article have been examined, and a co -occurrence matrix is formed.
Results indicate that Impulsive, Compulsive, Self-control, Materialism, and FOMO form the central core of PCB,
while variables such as Gambling, Binge Eating, Binge Watching, and Hoarding etc. are connected indirectly
through these central constructs. These findings highlight theoretical imbalances in literature and provide an
integrative framework for advancing research on problematic consumer behavior.
Keywords: Problematic Consumer Behavior, Psychological Drivers, Co-occurrence Network

INTRODUCTION
The final stage of decision -making process in consumer behavior is explained as act
(consumption, buying etc.) and has long been explained as outcome of rational and preference driven
process. Yet in this current timeline consumers buy things they do not need, spend the money they do
not have, do the same purchases repeatedly despite knowing the consequences. At first these types of
behaviors might seem rational but in academic perspectives these behavio rs are often examined with a
theoretical background of psychologically driven behaviors and reflect deeper psychological conditions.
This situation brings the question of where consumer behavior and psychology meet under which
psychological variables.
The term psychology is formed from the Greek word psyche, and as the work of Robb ( 1986)
explained it is meant to refer to the soul. Keeping this in mind, Kotler et al. (2010) explaining marketing
progressing its focus through product to customers and to the spirit. In other words, the souls, and in
marketing perspective the souls of consumers. Today, consumers are not considered just rational buyers
as economics say homo economicus, but an emotional human being. Therefore, consumer behavior is a
composition of many things including personality traits, emotions, cognitions, and psychological
conditions. Literature widely demonstrates these as consumers of today are making choic es with their
personality (Xie et al., 2025), their fears and desires to show their consumption (Dinh & Lee, 2024),
their need to belonging (Fazli -Salehi et al., 2024) etc. In short, t his meeting of psychology and
consumption is not just theoretical but is visible in behavioral data, in self -reported patterns, and in the
growing volume of consumer behavior research.
In response to this situation, a substantial body of work has emerged a t the intersection of
consumer behavior and psychology. Consumer behavior researchers form their works drawn from
personality theory, affective science, developmental psychology, and social cognition to explain
consumption patterns that fall outside the boundaries of rational behavior. Psychological vulnerabilities
(Karaosmanoglu et al., 2024), emotional dysregulation (David et al., 2024), maladaptive patterns
(Clithero et al., 2021), and socially driven psychological mechanisms (Silvera et al., 2008) have each
found their way into consumer behavior. As a cumulative result in consumer behavior literature is in
many perspective deeply connected to psychology. However, there is a side of psychology which comes
at a cost for consumers, in which psychological patterns align with what the literature increasingly state
as problematic consumer behavior ( Swaffield & Sierra Jimenez, 2024; Noh & Hasan, 2017; Silvera et
al., 2008), where consumption is driven less by rational and more by problems.
The engagement between consumer behavior research and psychology has developed within
vastly different topics. Different research streams have adopted different psychological variables.
Constructs that share theoretical roots are studied independently. Var iables that frequently co-occur in
psychological literature have been examined together within a consumer behavior context. This
intersection arises a need for collecting which variables of psychology are worked within consumer

CONCEPTUAL BACKGROUND
The concept of problematic consumer behavior  (PCB) lacks a specific definition in literature.
Therefore, this section outlines how PCB is conceptualized within the context of this study.
Problematic Consumer Behavior
Consumer behavior in modern times is not just pure rational decision but is a complex process.
Drawing on cognitive psychology, social psychology, and later behavioral economics,  researchers
demonstrated systematically that consumption decisions are shaped by heuristics, biases, emotions, and
social influences that operate largely outside conscious deliberation (Hochman & Ariely, 2015;
Kahneman, 2003). This significant theoretical advance represents the idealized consumer with a more
human one. However, within this progress these irrational behaviors were treated as cognitive quirks
rather than problems. Therefore, it needs to be underlined that consumption behavior might reflect
something more deeply rooted closer to psychological problems.
The intersection between psychology and consumer behavior is not a new area. Moreover,
consumer behavior is borrowed many constructs from psychology. Work of psychologists to explain
human behavior is duplicated within consumer behavior area. Ajzen ( 1991) and its theory of planned
behavior, Mehrabian & Russell (1974) and their model of stimuli-organism-response, Festinger’s (1957)
social comparison, Maslow’s ( 1943) hierarchy of needs etc. Early borrowings were largely cognitive
and social psychological in nature, and psychology of consumer was treated as stable, accessible, and
well-functioning system. However, within the last two decades consumers’ paradigms have been
reshaped. What changed over time was not the presence of psychology in consumer behavior research,
but the depth and characteristics of that presence. The change of paradigm showed that consumer
psychology might be troubled, unstable, dysregulated, and sensitive to changing conditions.

Consequently, consumer researchers began reaching further into the psychological sciences to explain
what cognitive models alone could not. Clinical consumer psychology (Posavac et al., 2022),
maladaptive consumer behavior (Clithero et al., 2021), neuropsychology of consumers (Shaw &
Bagozzi, 2018), emotions and consumer behavior (Williams, 2014), stress and consumer  behavior
(Moschis, 2007) etc. As a result, consumer behavior literature is remarkably engaged in psychological
sciences.
PCB is conceptualized for the purposes of this study as any observable consumer behavior pattern
that is difficult to control, causes distress or harm to the individual or others, and is shaped in meaningful
ways by psychological variables that change ordinary preference process. There is also an important
notification that must be emphasized. The difference b etween maladaptive & problematic consumer
behavior. While maladaptive consumer behavior is framed within clinical and psychopathological
contexts (Clithero et al., 2021, p. 335), the problematic consumer behavior is a wider rang e of harmful
or distressing consumer behavior patterns. This is a board definition by design but reflects the general
population of modern times’ consumer behavior.
METHODOLOGY
Research Design
This research addresses two questions. Unlike traditional systematic reviews, in this research
variables are identified and validated before the article search is conducted. Then search is structured
around those variables. Reason to choose such approach is complexity and unmanageability of searching
across hundreds of journal issues to form a structure. Research questions are as follows:
• Which variables used in consumer behavior research can reflect “problematic consumer
behavior”?
• How are the variables connected to each other?
Findings for these questions is expected to illustrate which variables are in the framework of PCB,
which variables are overused or neglected, and which variables structurally connected. Accordingly in
this research first, psychological variables are determined through review of the journals which intersect
consumer behavior and psychology (Journal of Consumer Psychology, Journal of Consumer Research,
Psychology & Marketing etc.). Second, a systematic article search is conducted for each validated
variable using a consistent search string . Finally, a dataset is formed from the retrieved articles. Then
the network analysis is applied in UCINET using Centrality Measures and Netdraw.

Preparation for Network Analysis
In this part of the research to perform network analysis a matrix is created by examining retrieved
articles. In the first matrix, created columns represented variables and rows represented articles. For
each article, variables were coded as 1 if pres ent, 0 if absent (350 rows and 35 columns). Articles
retrieved for one variable may also address others, and these multi -variable articles generate the co -
occurrence signals used to construct the network. Coding was conducted carefully for all articles
producing a final dataset of binary presence-absence matrix for network analysis. Then created article-
by-variable matrix transformed into variable -by-variable (35x35 square matrix) co -occurrence matrix.
In this process affiliations (2 -mode to 1 -mode) are app lied with mode columns, and method sums of
cross-products. This affiliation matrices serves as input for the UCINET network analysis.

The second research question of how variables are connected , is examined with centrality

network multiple centrality measures captures different aspect of each variable’s position in the network.
Table 2 is composed of frequency of variables (total count for each variables used in 350 study), degree

The frequencies in table 1 (computed with the first matrix of 350*35 using Excel) indicate which
variables are overused and which are neglected. Accordingly, Impulsive (106), Compulsive (79) and
Self-control (69) emerging as most frequently studied variables in the total 350 work. In contrast,
variables such as Gambling, Binge Eating, Dissociative, and Hoarding showed notably low frequencies.
This results of imbalance and uneven distribution highlight both do minant research streams and
neglected areas within the PCB framework. Degree centrality in table 1 indicates the structural
embeddedness within the network. Accordingly, Compulsive and Self-control (30), Impulsive (29) pose
the most centrality in the network functioning as primary hubs. On the other hand, Gambling (6), Anger
(7), and Phubbing (7) show low degree centrality indicating more isolated variables in the construct.
In the closeness centrality, the overall structural positioning of variables indic ated that despite
having low degree centrality Gambling (62), Anger (61), Phubbing (61), Binge Watching (60), Hoarding
(60) and Ego Depletion (60) demonstrate the highest closeness scores. Meaning these variables are
positioned in a way that allows indirec t but efficient access to the broader network. Finally, in
betweenness centrality its shown that which variables function as bridges connecting weakly linked
constructs. Accordingly, Compulsive (53.339), Self-control (52.595), and Impulsive (50.063) have t he
highest betweenness scores indicating that they have a role as key structural connectors within PCB
framework. In addition, variables such as Gambling, Binge Eating, Anger, Phubbing are seen as
connected through more central constructs and not playing intermediary roles.

DISCUSSION & CONCLUSION
This research examines the intersection of psychology and consumer behavior with the
perspective of problematic consumer behavior. Specifically, sought for answers for which variables can
be considered as problematic consumer behavior and how the network is among the variables. Research
results reveal that there is a complex and interconnected network of psychological variables within the
framework of problematic consumer behavior. Variables such as Impulsive, Compulsive, Self-control,
Materialism, and FOMO emerged as central and highly integrated  within framework and functions as
key nodes within the network. Their centrality indicates that these variables are both over studied and
play critical roles in shaping problematic consumer behavior. On the other h and, peripheral variables
such as Gambling, Binge Eating, Binge Watching, Anger, and Dissociative  have seen less connected,
and maintain the structural connections through central constructs. This pattern indicates that certain
behaviors are contextually d ependent on broader psychological dynamics rather than existing
independently. The uneven distribution of variables underscores research imbalances within PCB
literature, highlighting overemphasized areas alongside neglected constructs that warrant future
attention. Last of all, in the process of data curation some variables have not been included in the network
however it is important to underline that variables “Excluded from Network” (In Table 1) is also creates
an area which is less explored.
The emergence of Impulsive, Compulsive, and Self-control as the central variables of the network
is theoretically consistent with the foundational role these constructs occupy in the broader
psychological literature. Impulsivity, long established as self-regulatory failure underlying a wide range
of consumer behaviors (Sharma et al., 2014, p. 1160), appears to function in the consumer behavior
literature as a gateway construct. Similarly, the consistent co -occurrence of Compulsive behavior and

Self-control across the network reflects an established th eoretical coherence in PCB. In the literature
compulsive consumption and self-control (Horváth et al., 2015; Faber, 2004) appear together suggests
that consumer behavior researchers have organized their p sychological inquiry around this regulatory
axis. In addition, hub position of Materialism and FOMO have been theoretically linked to self -
regulatory (Ozimek & Förster, 2017) and social comparison ( Dinh & Lee, 2024), suggesting that the
network's core reflects consistency with literature.  The peripheral positioning of variables such as
Gambling & Binge Eating should not be considered as irrelevant to problematic consumer behavior .
Instead, their peripheral status within the consumer behavior network reflects a disciplinary boundary
problem. In short, consumer behavior field has not yet fully absorbed the empirical insights that adjacent
these variables.
This study makes several theoretical contributions to the literature of consumer behavior  and
practical implications to the field. First theoretical contribution is that present research  provides a
mapping of psychologically grounded variables within the framework of PCB. Second, identification of
PCB variables (both included and excluded variables) carries theoretical significance to further extend
the boundaries of PCB. Third, with co-occurrence network this research provides an area for future work
to examine which psychological might be further examined. Final and crucial, the operational definition
of PCB and distinguishing from maladaptive consumer behavior, specifically offers a conceptual
boundary. In practical implications, the network structure identified in this research signals which
psychological variables are most likely to be simultaneously activated in consumer decision contexts
characterized by problematic behavior. For policymakers, the explored network highlights overlooked
dimensions of PCB, suggesting a need to broaden policy attention and prevention efforts toward these
neglected areas. For consumer protection and clinicians working with problematic consumption, the
network map off ers a structured overview of co -occurring psychological constructs that can support
more holistic assessment and intervention.
This research purposefully used only journal articles within last two decades, this is not a
complete limitation but might be see n as a temporal bias. Therefore, given potential list might be
considered as a glimpse of the actual full range of PCB variables. Second limitation is that even though
the variable identification process was applied with a systematic approach and with doub le checks, the
process still can be considered as reflecting some subjective judgment by the researcher. Finally, this
research used only limited within a co-occurrence matrix, and results do not explain the relationship or
direct effect between variables.
Technically, this entire research serves as a guide for researchers working on PCB and opens
various paths for future research. Future research should address underexplored variables such as
Gambling, Binge Eating, Binge Watching, Dissociative tendencies and other non-excluded variables to
better understand the PCB. Additionally, due to this research’s only representing the co -occurrence of
variables, future research might examine the causality between variables with a metanalysis.
