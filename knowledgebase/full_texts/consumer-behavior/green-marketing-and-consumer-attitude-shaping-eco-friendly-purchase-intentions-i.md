Green marketing has gained significant attention globally
due to increasing environmental concerns such as pollution,
climate change, and resource depletion. Companies are
integrating eco-friendly practices into their marketing
strategies to influence consumer behavior and promote
sustainable consumption. Kathmandu Valley, being the
capital and a diverse metropolitan area of Nepal, provides
a suitable context to explore how green marketing practices
affect consumers’ green purchase intentions and behavior.
This study aimed to examine the effects of the green
marketing mix and consumer attitudes on green purchase
intentions in Kathmandu Valley. The study adopted a
quantitative research design using an explanatory research
approach based on the Theory of Planned Behavior to
assess how marketing strategies and consumer attitudes
influence green purchase intentions. A structured, closed-
ended questionnaire was administered to 406 consumers,
with convenience sampling, a non-probability sampling
technique, used to select participants. Data were collected
through the questionnaire via the KOBO Toolbox
and subsequently analyzed using Microsoft Excel for
descriptive statistics and SmartPLS 4.0 for inferential
analysis through Structural Equation Modeling (SEM).
The results revealed that most components of the green
marketing mix—product, price, promotion, place, and
packaging positively influenced green purchase intention,
which in turn significantly affected consumer behavior.
Attitude toward green products did not have a significant
direct effect on purchase intention. Mediation analysis
indicated that green purchase intention partially mediated
the relationship between green marketing mix components
and consumer behavior. The study confirms that strategic
green marketing practices strongly influence consumers’

Introduction
Green marketing has become increasingly important in today’s business environment due to growing
environmental concerns such as global warming, pollution, and non-biodegradable waste (Majeed et
al.,2022; Nevşehir Resul Öztürk, 2017). It aims to promote environmentally responsible consumption
by integrating eco-friendly practices into traditional marketing strate-gies, including Product, Price,
Promotion, and Place (Liao et al., 2020). Green marketing not only enhances environmental awareness
but also encourages sustainable consumer behavior, contributing to long-term ecological protection
and business sustainability (Maniatis, 2016). The Green Marketing Mix emphasizes eco-friendly
product development, sustainable pricing, green promotion, packaging, and distribution strategies,
all designed to reduce environmental impact (Ahmad & Zhang, 2020).
Globally, companies are increasingly adopting green marketing strategies to influence consum-er
behavior and encourage the purchase of environmentally friendly products (Cronin et al., 2011).
However, most research on green marketing has been conducted in developed countries, leaving
a gap in understanding how these practices influence consumer behavior in emerging economies,
including Nepal (Paul et al., 2016). In Nepal, green marketing has evolved from a trend to a
strategic economic approach. Both profit and non-profit organizations actively pro-mote eco-friendly
initiatives, and urban areas such as Kathmandu Valley have seen growing demand for organic and
environmentally friendly products (Bhattarai & Pyakurel, 2017). Con-sumers are increasingly willing
to pay a premium for green products, while businesses are in-corporating sustainable packaging and
promoting environmental responsibility as part of their long-term strategies (Testa et al., 2015).
Despite these advancements, challenges remain. Green products often involve higher produc-tion
costs due to sustainable materials and eco-friendly processes, which can translate into high-er prices
and limit adoption among cost-sensitive consumers (Clemons & Weber, 2017;  Ülkü & Hsuan, 2017).
Moreover, consumer skepticism regarding the effectiveness or quality of green products compared
to conventional alternatives can reduce purchase intentions (Testa et al., 2015). Nonetheless,
research indicates that green marketing initiatives such as eco-labeling, sustainable packaging,
and environmentally conscious branding positively influence consumer attitudes and intentions to
purchase green products (Yadav & Pathak, 2017).
Consumer attitudes play a crucial role in shaping green purchase intentions. Individuals may engage
in eco-friendly behavior to demonstrate altruism, enhance social status, or express per-sonal values,
while emotional attachment to brands can increase repurchase likelihood and brand advocacy (Chen
& Tung, 2014). Businesses are recognizing that aligning marketing strategies with consumers’
environmental values can strengthen customer relationships and con-tribute to sustainable growth
(Ahmed et al., 2023). Green marketing not only addresses con-sumer demands but also ensures the
preservation and improvement of the natural environment for future generations (Davari & Strutton,
2014).
In Nepal, rising urbanization and environmental challenges have highlighted the need for sus-tainable
practices. Government policies, including the Fourteenth National Development Plan, promote

Literature Review
Theoretical Framework and Hypothesis Formulation
For this study, theories reviewed include Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB), Signaling Theo-ry,
ABC Theory, Attribution Theory, and Theory of Reasoned Action (TRA). According to the Theory
of Planned Behavior (TPB), a subjective norm is the perceived social pressure to engage in or avoid
a given behavior, which is impacted by significant individuals such as friends, family, and colleagues
(Paul, 2016). Signaling theory explains the exchange of infor-mation between a sender and a recipient,
impacting stakeholders’ behavior and decision-making. It is commonly used by economists and
organizations in business and green marketing to disseminate information (Liao et al., 2020).  The
ABC theory links internal factors (beliefs and attitudes) with external factors (physical structures,
social institutions, societal standards, financial support, and institutional influences) to explain eco-
conscious behavior, emphasizing the role of contextual elements in shaping green habits and providing
a valuable framework for examining the impact of various factors on green purchase intentions. (Liao et
al., 2020). At-tribution theory focuses on cognitive processes and the consequences of causal inference
and advances have been made in understanding how attributions influence consumer responses to
product problems and affective reactions to outcomes, though research on this has been limited (Hiyab
et al.,2023). The Theory of Reasoned Action (TRA) predicts intentions to engage in various activities
based on attitudes and subjective norms, where attitudes reflect personal as-sessments and subjective
norms are shaped by societal influences and others’ perspectives, link-ing rational behavior to these
beliefs and often applied to understanding voluntary behaviors like environmentally beneficial acts
influenced by both internal and external motives (Zuriyati et al., 2014). Among mentioned these
theories, Theory of Planned Behavior seems to be more appropriate for endorsing green marketing

Conclusion
This study examined the effects of the green marketing mix and consumer attitude on green purchase
intentions in Kathmandu Valley. The findings indicate that components of the green marketing mix i.e.
product, price, promotion, place, and packaging positively influence green purchase intention, which
in turn significantly affects consumer behavior. Consumer attitude toward green products, however, did
not show a direct significant effect on green purchase intention. These results highlight the importance
of marketing strategies and product-related factors in shaping environmentally responsible consumer
behavior. The study offers practical implications for both businesses and policymakers. Companies
can enhance consumer engagement and gain a competitive advantage by emphasizing the benefits
of green products, employing strategic eco-friendly advertising, and ensuring convenient access
to sustainable products. Policymakers can encourage sustainable consumption by raising public
awareness of environmental issues and promoting cultural attitudes that value eco-friendly products.
Additionally, organizations can strengthen their reputation and increase sales of green products by
incorporating sustainability into their corporate strategies and social responsibility initiatives. The
study demonstrates that effective green marketing practices, along with consumer awareness and
value perception, play a crucial role in promoting green purchase intentions and environmentally
responsible behavior, contributing to both business competitiveness and environmental sustainability
in Kathmandu Valley.
