Abstract: Sustainable food consumption has attracted increasing scholarly attention in
contemporary consumer behavior research, particularly in emerging markets characterized by
growing environmental awareness alongside continuing sustainability skepticism and
authenticity uncertainty. However, prior studies have predo minantly emphasized
environmental concern and functional product attributes while providing limited understanding
regarding how sustainability -related perceptions become psychologically internalized into
long-term lifestyle commitment. This study therefore examines the relationships among health
consciousness, perceived environmental value, consumer trust, purchase intention, and
sustainable food adoption behavior within the context of hydroponic vegetable consumption in
Indonesia. Drawing upon symbolic con sumption theory, trust theory, and the Theory of
Planned Behavior, this study proposes an integrated psychological framework explaining how
sustainability-related perceptions influence environmentally responsible consumption
behavior through affective trus t formation. Data were collected from 385 consumers
purchasing hydroponic vegetables in urban retail markets in Indonesia and analyzed using
Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) with LISREL.  The findings demonstrate that health
consciousness and perceived en vironmental value significantly strengthen consumer trust.
Consumer trust further exerts the strongest influence on purchase intention and long -term
sustainable food adoption behavior. The mediation analysis additionally reveals that consumer
trust functio ns as an affective psychological conversion mechanism linking sustainability -
related perceptions with behavioral commitment. The findings indicate that environmentally
responsible food consumption is shaped not merely by rational product evaluation but als o by
emotional reassurance, perceived authenticity, ethical alignment, and identity -related lifestyle
processes. This study contributes to sustainable consumption and consumer behavior literature
by extending understanding regarding the affective mechanism s underlying environmentally
responsible consumption behavior in emerging markets. The findings further enrich symbolic
consumption literature by demonstrating that sustainable food adoption increasingly reflects
psychologically and symbolically embedded l ifestyle commitment rather than purely
functional purchasing decisions.

INTRODUCTION
The growing global concern regarding environmental sustainability, public health, and
ethical consumption has substantially transformed consumer behavior in contemporary food
markets. Consumers increasingly seek produc ts perceived not only as beneficial for personal
well-being but also as environmentally and socially responsible. Within this context,
sustainable food products such as hydroponic vegetables have gained increasing market
attention due to their reduced pest icide usage, efficient resource utilization, and
environmentally controlled production systems. Recent consumer research suggests that
environmentally responsible food consumption is no longer driven solely by functional product
attributes but increasingly  shaped by emotional meaning, lifestyle congruence, moral values,
and identity-related motivations associated with responsible consumption (White et al., 2019;
Schmitt et al., 2022; Scott et al., 2022).
According to symbolic consumption theory, consumers u tilize marketplace products
not only for utilitarian purposes but also as symbolic resources for constructing and
communicating identities, lifestyles, and social values (Belk, 1988; McCracken, 1986).
Arnould and Thompson (2005), through Consumer Culture T heory, further argue that
consumption practices are deeply connected to self -construction, social meaning, and moral
expression. Within sustainable consumption contexts, environmentally responsible products
may therefore function as symbolic representation s of ethical awareness, health -oriented
lifestyles, and socially responsible identities. Consumers increasingly derive emotional
reassurance and psychological satisfaction from products aligned with their personal values
and desired self -concepts (Bolton &  Reed, 2022; Griskevicius et al., 2021). Consequently,
sustainable food adoption extends beyond rational purchasing decisions and increasingly
reflects emotionally meaningful and identity-driven lifestyle behavior.
Despite increasing awareness regarding sustainability and healthy consumption, actual
adoption of sustainable food products remains inconsistent, particularly in emerging markets.
This phenomenon is widely recognized as the attitude–behavior gap, where consumers express
favorable attitudes toward sustainable products but fail to consistently translate these attitudes
into actual purchasing behavior (Carrington et al., 2021; Testa et al., 2020). Existing literature
has predominantly explained sustainable food consumption through functional product
evaluations such as price, quality, accessibility, and perceived utility (Joshi & Rahman, 2021;
Nguyen et al., 2021). Although these factors remain important, they provide limited
explanation regarding how sustainability -related perceptions become psycholo gically
internalized into long-term lifestyle commitment and environmentally responsible behavioral
adoption.
The inconsistency becomes more complex in emerging markets, where institutional
trust, sustainability verification systems, and product transparen cy mechanisms are often still
evolving. Consumers frequently encounter difficulties in verifying sustainability-related claims
because environmental and ethical product attributes cannot be directly evaluated during
purchase situations. Consequently, many consumers remain skeptical toward environmentally
friendly product claims, particularly when product authenticity and producer credibility are
difficult to assess. Under such conditions, trust becomes a critical psychological mechanism
enabling consumers t o reduce uncertainty and perceived consumption risk (Morgan & Hunt,
1994; Bhattacharya & Kumar, 2020). Contemporary trust literature further suggests that trust
extends beyond cognitive evaluation of product reliability and increasingly reflects perceptions

Recent studies in consumer psychology indicate that trust functions not only as a

moral identities and lifestyle aspirations (Bolton & Reed, 2022; Schmitt et al., 2022).
Consumers are more likely to adopt sustainable products when they perceive alignment

According to the Theory of Planned Behavior (Ajz en, 1991), behavioral intention

contexts, consumers’ intentions to adopt environmentally responsible products are influenced
not only by cognitive attitudes but a lso by psychological mechanisms shaping confidence,

because trusted products are perceived as more authentic, credible, and personally meaningful
(Prakash et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2021). Trust therefore becomes an important psychological
bridge connecting sustainability-related perceptions with actual behavioral adoption.
Health consciousness represents another important psychological driver shaping
sustainable food adoption behavior. Consumers increasingly associate sustainable food
products with disease prevention, healthy lifestyles, food safety, and long -term well -being
(Prakash et al., 2023). Following global public health concerns in recent years, awareness
regarding immunity, nutrition, and personal well -being has intensified substantially,
encouraging consumers to seek products perceived as natural, safe, and environ mentally
responsible. Prior research demonstrates that health-conscious consumers tend to evaluate food
products not only based on taste or economic value but also based on meanings associated with
self-care, responsible living, and long-term lifestyle quality (Scott et al., 2022; Rahman et al.,
2024). Sustainable food consumption therefore increasingly reflects both physical well -being
motivations and identity-related aspirations associated with healthier lifestyles.
In addition to health consciousness, pe rceived environmental value has emerged as a
critical determinant of sustainable consumption behavior. Consumers increasingly evaluate
products based not only on personal utility but also on broader environmental and ethical
implications. Sustainable food products such as hydroponic vegetables are frequently
perceived as contributing to ecological preservation, environmental responsibility, and
efficient resource utilization (White et al., 2019; Joshi & Rahman, 2021). Research in symbolic
and pro -environmental consumption further indicates that consumers derive psychological
satisfaction and moral legitimacy from purchasing environmentally responsible products
because such products reinforce socially desirable identities and ethical self -perceptions
(Griskevicius et al., 2021; Schmitt et al., 2022). Environmentally responsible consumption may
therefore function simultaneously as ethical behavior and symbolic social signaling.
Although previous studies have examined sustainable food consumption,
environmental concern, and consumer trust independently, limited research has integrated
these psychological dimensions within a unified framework explaining sustainable food
adoption behavior in emerging markets. More importantly, prior literature has predominantly
emphasized functional product evaluation while insufficiently addressing the emotional,
symbolic, and identity -related mechanisms underlying trust formation in sustainable food
consumption contexts. Consequently, the psychological processes through which cons umers

This study contributes to consumer behavior literature in several important ways. First,

Literature Review and Hypotheses Development
Sustainable Food Adoption and Consumer Behavior
Sustainable food consumption has become an increasingly important topic within
contemporary consumer behavior research due to rising environmental concern, ethical
awareness, and health -oriented lifestyle transformation. Sustainable food products are
generally defined as products produced and consu med in ways that minimize environmental
impact while supporting long -term ecological and social well -being (White et al., 2019). In
recent years, consumers have increasingly considered sustainability -related attributes such as
environmental friendliness, e thical production, food safety, and responsible sourcing when
making purchasing decisions (Joshi & Rahman, 2021).
From a consumer behavior perspective, sustainable food adoption extends beyond
rational product evaluation and increasingly reflects symbolic,  emotional, and identity-driven
consumption practices. According to Consumer Culture Theory, consumers utilize marketplace
products not only to satisfy utilitarian needs but also to communicate social identity, personal
values, and desired lifestyles (Arnould & Thompson, 2005). Holbrook and Hirschman (1982)
further argue that consumption experiences are strongly associated with emotional meaning
and experiential value rather than purely functional utility. Sustainable food products may
therefore function as  symbolic representations of responsible consumption and ethical self -
expression. Consumers increasingly perceive environmentally responsible consumption as part
of a broader lifestyle orientation associated with moral awareness, health consciousness, and
social responsibility (Griskevicius et al., 2021).
Nevertheless, despite increasing sustainability awareness, prior studies consistently
report the existence of an attitude–behavior gap in sustainable purchasing contexts (Carrington
et al., 2021). Consumer s frequently express favorable attitudes toward environmentally
responsible products while failing to consistently adopt such products in actual purchasing
situations. Existing literature has predominantly explained this inconsistency through
utilitarian f actors such as price sensitivity, product accessibility, perceived quality, and

convenience (Joshi & Rahman, 2021; Nguyen et al., 2021). Although these factors remain
important, they provide limited explanation regarding how sustainability -related perceptions
become emotionally meaningful and psychologically internalized into long -term lifestyle

Recent consumer behavior research increasingly suggests that emotionally embedded
mechanisms such as trust, authenticity perception, value congruence, a nd identity alignment
may play more central roles in transforming favorable sustainability perceptions into actual
behavioral adoption (Bolton & Reed, 2022; Schmitt et al., 2022). This perspective indicates
that environmentally responsible food consumption  should not be understood solely as a
rational decision -making process but also as a psychologically embedded and symbolically
meaningful consumption behavior associated with ethical self -expression and responsible
lifestyle orientation.

Consumer Trust in Sustainable Consumption
Trust has emerged as one of the most important psychological mechanisms influencing
sustainable consumption behavior. According to relationship marketing theory, trust reflects
consumers’ confidence in the reliability, integrity, a nd credibility of products and producers
(Morgan & Hunt, 1994). Within sustainable consumption contexts, trust becomes particularly
important because many sustainability -related product attributes cannot be directly evaluated
during purchase situations. Co nsumers therefore rely on trust to reduce uncertainty and
perceived consumption risk (Bhattacharya & Kumar, 2020).
Contemporary consumer research further highlights that trust extends beyond cognitive
product evaluation and increasingly involves affective trust, ethical alignment, and symbolic
congruence between consumers and brands (Bolton & Reed, 2022). Consumers are more likely
to trust products perceived as authentic, transparent, and aligned with their personal values and
moral beliefs. Fournier (1998) emphasizes that strong consumer relationships are built not only
through functional satisfaction but also through emotional and symbolic attachment toward
brands. In sustainable food markets, trust may therefore function as an affective mechanism
reinforcing consumers’ confidence that their purchasing decisions support both personal well-
being and broader environmental responsibility (Wang et al., 2021).
Prior studies also indicate that consumers frequently remain skeptical toward
sustainability-related product claims, particularly in emerging markets characterized by limited
product transparency and weak sustainability verification systems (Newman et al., 2020;
Sharma et al., 2022). Consumers may experience uncertainty regarding whether
environmentally res ponsible products genuinely deliver the ecological and ethical benefits
communicated by producers. Consequently, trust becomes not only a cognitive evaluation
mechanism but also a psychological reassurance process reducing skepticism and
strengthening emotional commitment toward sustainable products.
Trust additionally plays an important role in strengthening consumers’ behavioral
intention and long -term commitment toward sustainable consumption. Consumers who trust
environmentally responsible products are more likely to engage in repeat purchasing behavior
and integrate sustainable products into their daily lifestyles (Campos et al., 2022). Trust
therefore functions not only as a risk -reduction mechanism but also as an affective
psychological foundation underlying environmentally responsible lifestyle adoption.

Health Consciousness and Consumer Trust
Health consciousness refers to the degree to which consumers are concerned about
personal health, food safety, and well -being in their daily consumption behavi or (Prakash et
al., 2023). Health -conscious consumers tend to pay greater attention to nutritional value,
product safety, ingredient quality, and long -term health consequences when evaluating food
products. In recent years, awareness regarding immunity, nu trition, and preventive health

behavior has intensified substantially, encouraging greater demand for products perceived as
natural, safe, and environmentally responsible.
Prior research suggests that health -conscious consumers are more likely to trust
sustainable food products because such products are frequently associated with reduced
chemical exposure, safer production systems, and healthier lifestyles (Rahman et al., 2024).
Sustainable food products such as hydroponic vegetables may therefore reinforce  consumers’
perceptions of self-care, preventive health behavior, and responsible living. From a symbolic
consumption perspective, healthy food choices may also function as identity -signaling
mechanisms reflecting desirable lifestyles, self-discipline, and positive self-concepts (Scott et
al., 2022).
Nevertheless, prior studies also indicate that consumers may still hesitate to adopt
sustainable food products when product authenticity and credibility remain uncertain (Nguyen
et al., 2021). Consumers may per ceive healthy food claims positively while simultaneously
questioning whether such products genuinely deliver the promised benefits. Under these
conditions, trust may function as an affective psychological conversion mechanism
transforming health -related p erceptions into emotional confidence toward sustainable
products.
Empirical evidence consistently demonstrates positive relationships between health
consciousness and consumer trust toward environmentally responsible products (Prakash et al.,
2023; Rahman et al., 2024). Consumers who perceive sustainable products as supportive of
personal well-being are more likely to develop stronger emotional confidence and trust toward
those products. Based on these arguments, the following hypothesis is proposed:
H1: Health consciousness has a positive and significant effect on consumer trust.

Perceived Environmental Value and Consumer Trust
Perceived environmental value refers to consumers’ perceptions regarding the
environmental benefits and ecological responsibility associated with a product (Joshi &
Rahman, 2021). Consumers increasingly evaluate products not only based on personal utility
but also based on broader environmental implications such as ecological preservation,
sustainability contribution, and resource ef ficiency. Sustainable food products are frequently
perceived as environmentally responsible alternatives supporting long -term environmental
well-being.
According to symbolic and pro -environmental consumption theory, consumers derive
psychological satisfaction and moral legitimacy from products aligned with environmental
responsibility and ethical consumption values (Griskevicius et al., 2021). Products perceived
as environmentally friendly may therefore strengthen consumers’ emotional  attachment and
trust because they reinforce moral alignment between personal values and marketplace
behavior. Sustainable products increasingly function as symbols of ethical awareness, social
responsibility, and environmentally responsible lifestyle orientation (Schmitt et al., 2022).
However, prior literature also suggests that perceived environmental value does not
always directly translate into sustainable behavioral adoption because consumers may remain
uncertain regarding the authenticity of environm ental claims (Testa et al., 2020). Green
skepticism and concerns regarding misleading sustainability communication may weaken
consumers’ willingness to engage in environmentally responsible purchasing behavior.
Consequently, consumers may require stronger affective trust and perceived authenticity
before translating environmental perceptions into actual behavioral commitment.
Prior studies consistently indicate that perceived environmental value positively
influences trust toward sustainable products and environmentally responsible brands (Campos
et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2021). Consumers who perceive stronger environmental benefits are
more likely to believe that producers are credible, responsible, and trustworthy. Based on these
arguments, the following hypothesis is proposed:

Consumer Trust and Purchase Intention
Purchase intention refers to consumers’ willingness and tendency to purchase specific
products in future consumption situations (Ajzen, 1991). According to the Theory of Planned
Behavior, behavioral intention represents one of the strongest predictors of actual behavior.
Within sustainable consumption contexts, consumers’ intentions to purchase environment ally
responsible products are influenced not only by cognitive evaluation but also by emotional
confidence and psychological reassurance toward the product.
Trust significantly reduces uncertainty and perceived consumption risk, thereby
strengthening consu mers’ willingness to engage in purchasing behavior (Morgan & Hunt,
1994). Consumers are more likely to purchase sustainable food products when they trust
product claims, producer credibility, and sustainability -related information. Prior research
demonstrates that trust positively influences purchase intention toward environmentally
responsible products and sustainable brands (Bhattacharya & Kumar, 2020; Wang et al., 2021).
Contemporary literature further suggests that emotionally trusted products are perce ived as
more authentic, meaningful, and aligned with consumers’ ethical values, thereby strengthening
behavioral intention (Bolton & Reed, 2022). Trust may therefore function as an affective
psychological bridge connecting sustainability -related perceptions with purchasing intention.
Based on these arguments, the following hypothesis is proposed:
H3: Consumer trust has a positive and significant effect on purchase intention.

Purchase Intention and Sustainable Food Adoption
Sustainable food adoption refers to consumers’ actual and continuous engagement in
purchasing and consuming environmentally responsible food products. According to the
Theory of Planned Behavior, behavioral intention serves as a direct antecedent of actual
behavior (Ajzen, 1991). Consumers with stronger purchase intentions are therefore more likely
to consistently adopt sustainable food products in their daily consumption practices.
Prior studies demonstrate that purchase intention significantly predicts sustainable food
consumption behavi or and long -term environmentally responsible lifestyles (Nguyen et al.,
2021; Testa et al., 2020). However, several studies also indicate that consumers may fail to
maintain sustainable purchasing behavior over time due to limited trust, weak emotional
attachment, and inconsistent value alignment with sustainable products (Carrington et al.,
2021). This suggests that long -term sustainable food adoption requires not only behavioral
intention but also stronger psychological commitment toward environmentally r esponsible
lifestyles.
From a symbolic consumption perspective, repeated sustainable purchasing behavior
may gradually evolve into identity -related lifestyle behavior associated with ethical
commitment and responsible self -expression (Scott et al., 2022). Sustainable food adoption
therefore reflects not only repeated purchasing behavior but also broader lifestyle -oriented
behavioral commitment. Based on these arguments, the following hypothesis is proposed:
H4: Purchase intention has a positive and significant effect on sustainable food adoption.

who trust sustainable products are more likely to repeatedly purchase and continuou sly
consume such products because trust reduces uncertainty and strengthens psychological
commitment toward environmentally responsible consumption behavior (Newman et al.,
2020).
Recent consumer behavior literature further suggests that trusted products b ecome
integrated into consumers’ self -concepts and lifestyle identities, thereby strengthening long -

Prior studies consistently demonstrate that trust positively influences sustainable

The Mediating Role of Consumer Trust
Trust may also function as an important mediating mechanism connecting consumers’
psychological perceptions with sustainable purchasing intention. Consumers who perceive
sustainable food products as healthy and environmentally responsible may not automatically
translate such perceptions into behavioral intention unless they a lso trust the authenticity and
credibility of the product.
Within sustainable consumption contexts, trust may function as an affective
psychological conversion mechanism transforming abstract sustainability -related perceptions
into emotionally meaningful b ehavioral commitment. Health -conscious consumers may
develop stronger purchase intention when they emotionally trust sustainable products and
perceive them as authentic and beneficial for personal well -being. Similarly, consumers who
perceive strong enviro nmental value may strengthen their behavioral intention when trust
reinforces confidence in product credibility and sustainability claims.
Prior consumer research suggests that trust mediates the influence of psychological
values on sustainable purchasing behavior because trust transforms abstract perceptions into
emotionally meaningful behavioral commitment (Bhattacharya & Kumar, 2020; Newman et
al., 2020). However, limited studies have positioned trust as an affective psychological
mechanism linking susta inability-related perceptions with identity -oriented behavioral
commitment in emerging market contexts. Based on these arguments, the following hypotheses
are proposed:
H6: Consumer trust mediates the relationship between health consciousness and purchase
intention.
H7: Consumer trust mediates the relationship between perceived environmental value and
purchase intention.

Conceptual Framework
Based on symbolic consumption theory, trust theory, and the Theory of Planned
Behavior, this study proposes an integrated conceptual framework explaining the
psychological mechanisms underlying sustainable food adoption behavior in emerging
markets. The framework positions health consciousness and perceived environmental value as
important antecedents shaping consu mer trust toward sustainable food products. Consumer
trust subsequently influences purchase intention and sustainable food adoption behavior. In
addition, consumer trust is proposed to mediate the relationships between sustainability-related
perceptions and behavioral intention.
The framework further conceptualizes consumer trust not merely as a cognitive
evaluation mechanism but as an affective psychological conversion mechanism connecting
sustainability-related perceptions with long -term environmentally r esponsible lifestyle
commitment. Accordingly, sustainable food adoption behavior is understood as a
psychologically and symbolically embedded consumption process influenced by emotional
reassurance, ethical alignment, perceived authenticity, and identity-related lifestyle orientation.

METHOD
This study employed a quantitative cross -sectional research design to examine the
psychological mechanisms underlying sustainable food adoption behavior among consumers
of hydroponic vegetables in Indonesia. Drawing upon  symbolic consumption theory, trust
theory, and the Theory of Planned Behavior, the study investigated the relationships among
health consciousness, perceived environmental value, consumer trust, purchase intention, and
sustainable food adoption behavior. The conceptual relationships examined in this study are
presented in Figure 1.
A cross-sectional survey approach was considered appropriate because it enables the
systematic examination of consumer perceptions, attitudes, and behavioral tendencies within
naturally occurring consumption settings and is widely applied in consumer behavior and
sustainable consumption research (Malhotra et al., 2022). The study focused on urban
consumers because urban markets in emerging economies increasingly represent importa nt
environments for health-oriented and sustainable lifestyle consumption practices.

Population and Sample
The target population consisted of consumers who had previously purchased
hydroponic vegetables from supermarkets, modern retail outlets, and environmentally oriented
food markets located in several urban regions in Indonesia. Respondents were selected using
purposive sampling because the study specifically required individuals possessing prior
consumption experience and familiarity with sustainable food products.
To ensure respondent suitability and data relevance, the following screening criteria were
applied:

1. Respondents were at least 18 years old.
2. Respondents had purchased hydroponic vegetables at least once during the previous six
months.
3. Respondents were familiar with sustainable or environmentally responsible food
products.
Prior to the main survey, a pilot study involving 30 respondents was conducted to
evaluate item clarity, wording suitability, and contextual relevance of the measureme nt
instrument. Minor revisions were subsequently implemented to improve questionnaire
readability and respondent comprehension.
A total of 420 questionnaires were distributed using both online and offline approaches.
Of these, 397 questionnaires were returned, representing a response rate of 94.52%. After data
screening procedures and elimination of incomplete responses, 385 valid questionnaires were
retained for final analysis.
The final sample size satisfies recommended requirements for covariance-based
Structural Equation Modeling (CB -SEM), which generally recommends a minimum sample
exceeding 200 observations for complex latent -variable analysis (Hair et al., 2021; Kline,
2021). The sample size was therefore considered statistically adequate for testing the proposed
conceptual framework and mediation relationships.

Data Collection Procedure
Data collection was conducted between January and March 2026 using a structured
self-administered questionnaire distributed both online and offline. Offline questionnaires were
administered directly to consumers at supermarkets and hydroponic retail markets, while online
questionnaires were distributed through digital consumer communities and social media groups
associated with healthy lifestyle and sustainable consumption interests.
Participation in the study was entirely voluntary. Prior to questionnaire completion,
respondents were informed regarding the academic purpose of the study and assured that all
responses would remain anonymous and confidential. To minimize potential common method
bias, respondents were informed that there were no right or wrong answers and were
encouraged to provide honest responses based on their personal consumption experiences. The
study complied with general ethical sta ndards for voluntary human participation, including
informed consent, confidentiality protection, and respondent anonymity.

Measurement of Variables
All constructs were measured using previously validated scales adapted from prior
consumer behavior and su stainable consumption studies. Minor wording modifications were
implemented to ensure contextual suitability for sustainable food consumption behavior in
Indonesia.
Health consciousness was measured using indicators adapted from Prakash et al. (2023)
and Rahman et al. (2024), focusing on consumers’ awareness regarding personal health, food
safety, and preventive lifestyle orientation.
Perceived environmental value was measured using items adapted from White et al.
(2019) and Campos et al. (2022), emphasizin g consumers’ perceptions regarding ecological
responsibility, environmental contribution, and sustainability-related product value.
Consumer trust was measured using indicators adapted from Morgan and Hunt (1994),
Bhattacharya and Kumar (2020), and Wang et  al. (2021), focusing on product credibility,
authenticity, transparency, and reliability.
Purchase intention was measured using indicators adapted from Ajzen (1991) and
Nguyen et al. (2021), reflecting consumers’ willingness and future intention to purcha se
sustainable food products.

Data Analysis Technique
This study employed covariance-based Structural Equation Modeling (CB-SEM) using
LISREL 8.80 to evaluate the proposed conceptual framework and test the research hypotheses.
CB-SEM was considered appropriate because the study aimed to test theoretically grounded
causal relationships among multiple latent constructs simultaneously while accounting for
measurement error (Hair et al., 2021).
The data analysis procedure consisted of two primary stages. First, Confirmatory Factor
Analysis (CFA) was conducted to assess the measurement model in terms of construct validity
and reliability. Convergent validity was evaluated using standardized factor loadings, Average
Variance Extracted (AVE), and Composite Reliability (CR). Standardized factor loadings
exceeding 0.70, AVE values above 0.50, and CR values exceeding 0.70 were considered
acceptable indicators of convergent validity and construct reliability (Hair et al., 2021).
Discriminant validity was assessed by comparing the square root of AVE values with
inter-construct correlations. Discriminant validity was considered satisfactory when the square
root of AVE for each construct exceeded the correlations between constructs.
Second, structural model analysis was conducted to examine the hypothesized
relationships among variables and evaluate the mediating role of consumer trust. Mediation
effects were further examined using b ootstrapping procedures to evaluate the indirect effects
among constructs. The structural model was evaluated using several goodness -of-fit indices,
including Chi-square/degrees of freedom (χ²/df), Root Mean Square Error of Approximation
(RMSEA), Comparati ve Fit Index (CFI), Tucker –Lewis Index (TLI), and Goodness -of-Fit
Index (GFI).

Common Method Bias Assessment
Because the study relied on self -reported questionnaire data collected from a single
source, potential common method bias was assessed using both p rocedural and statistical
remedies. Procedurally, respondent anonymity and confidentiality were assured to reduce
evaluation apprehension and social desirability bias. In addition, measurement items were
arranged systematically to reduce respondents’ tende ncy to infer causal relationships among
constructs.
Statistically, Harman’s single -factor test was conducted to evaluate whether a single
factor accounted for the majority of total variance (Podsakoff et al., 2003). The results indicated
that no single fac tor accounted for the majority of total variance, suggesting that common
method bias was unlikely to substantially influence the study findings.

Non-Response Bias Assessment
Non-response bias was evaluated by comparing early and late respondents using ind ependent
sample t-tests following the procedure recommended by Armstrong and Overton (1977). The
results indicated no statistically significant differences between the two respondent groups,
suggesting that non-response bias was not a major concern in this study.

Discussion
This study provides important insights into the psychological mechanisms underlying
sustainable food adoption behavior in emerging markets. The findings demonstrate that health
consciousness and perceived environmental value significantly strengthen consumer trust,
which subsequently influences purchase intention and long -term sustainable food adoption
behavior. M ore importantly, the study reveals that environmentally responsible food
consumption is shaped not merely by rational product evaluation but also by affective trust
formation, authenticity perception, ethical alignment, and identity-related lifestyle processes.
The findings extend prior sustainable consumption literature by demonstrating that trust
functions not only as a cognitive evaluation mechanism but also as an affective psychological
conversion mechanism linking sustainability -related perceptions wit h long -term behavioral
commitment. While prior studies have predominantly emphasized environmental concern,
product quality, and functional utility as central predictors of sustainable purchasing behavior
(Joshi & Rahman, 2021; Nguyen et al., 2021), the pr esent findings suggest that emotionally
embedded trust formation plays a more fundamental role in transforming favorable
sustainability perceptions into meaningful consumption behavior. This finding reinforces
recent consumer behavior perspectives arguing that sustainable consumption increasingly
reflects emotionally and symbolically embedded lifestyle orientation rather than purely
utilitarian decision-making processes (Bolton & Reed, 2022; Schmitt et al., 2022).
The findings reveal that health consciousness significantly influences consumer trust
toward sustainable food products. Consumers who demonstrate stronger concern regarding
personal well-being, food safety, and preventive lifestyle behavior are more likely to develop
trust toward hydropo nic vegetables and environmentally responsible food alternatives. This
finding supports prior studies indicating that health -conscious consumers increasingly
associate sustainable food products with self -care, healthy lifestyles, and long-term quality of
life (Prakash et al., 2023; Rahman et al., 2024).
More importantly, the present study extends prior literature by demonstrating that
health consciousness contributes not merely to rational product evaluation but also to emotional
reassurance and psychologic al confidence formation. Consumers increasingly perceive
sustainable food products as symbolic representations of responsible living and healthy
lifestyle identity. Consequently, trust emerges when consumers perceive alignment between
sustainable products and their desired self -concepts. This finding aligns with symbolic
consumption theory, which argues that marketplace products function as identity -signaling
resources reflecting personal values, lifestyle aspirations, and socially desirable self -images
(Belk, 1988; Arnould & Thompson, 2005).
The findings further demonstrate that perceived environmental value significantly
strengthens consumer trust. Consumers who perceive hydroponic vegetables as
environmentally responsible and ecologically meaningful produ cts are more likely to trust
sustainability-oriented brands and producers. This finding supports previous sustainable

consumption studies suggesting that consumers increasingly evaluate products not only based
on personal utility but also based on broader environmental and ethical implications (White et

However, the present study further demonstrates that environmental value alone may
not automatically produce sustainable behavioral commitment. Consumers may perceive
environmentally friendly products positively while simultaneously remaining uncertain

The findings additionally reveal that consumer trust exerts the strongest influence on

transparent, and credible demonstrate significantl y stronger willingness to engage in
environmentally responsible purchasing behavior. This finding supports relationship marketing
theory, which emphasizes that trust reduces uncertainty and strengthens consumers’ confidence
toward purchasing decisions (Morgan & Hunt, 1994).
Nevertheless, the present findings extend prior trust literature by suggesting that trust
in sustainable food consumption contexts increasingly reflects affective and symbolic
reassurance rather than purely functional reliability. Consum ers are more likely to purchase
sustainable products when they perceive such products as emotionally meaningful, ethically
aligned, and congruent with their desired lifestyle identities. This finding reinforces recent
consumer psychology arguments suggesti ng that environmentally responsible purchasing
behavior increasingly functions as ethical self-expression and socially valued identity signaling

The results further indicate that purchase intention significa ntly influences sustainable
food adoption behavior. Consumers with stronger purchasing intentions are more likely to
integrate hydroponic vegetables into routine consumption practices and long -term
environmentally responsible lifestyle behavior. This findi ng supports the Theory of Planned
Behavior, which posits that behavioral intention represents one of the strongest predictors of
actual behavior (Ajzen, 1991).
However, the present study further demonstrates that sustainable food adoption extends
beyond re peated purchasing behavior and increasingly reflects psychologically embedded
lifestyle commitment. Consumers who continuously consume environmentally responsible
food products may gradually internalize such behavior as part of their self -concept, ethical
orientation, and responsible lifestyle identity. Sustainable food adoption therefore increasingly
represents long -term behavioral commitment associated with moral self -expression and
socially desirable lifestyle positioning.
The findings additionally demon strate that consumer trust directly influences
sustainable food adoption behavior. Consumers who trust sustainable food products are more
likely to repeatedly purchase and continuously consume such products because trust
strengthens emotional attachment an d reduces uncertainty regarding sustainability -related
claims. This finding suggests that trust contributes not only to purchasing intention formation
but also to long-term environmentally responsible lifestyle continuity.
One of the most important finding s of this study concerns the mediating role of
consumer trust. The mediation analysis demonstrates that trust functions as an affective
psychological bridge connecting health consciousness and perceived environmental value with
purchase intention. Consumer s may perceive sustainable products as healthy and

Theoretical Implications
This study contributes to sustainable consumption and consumer behavior literature in
several important ways. First, the study extends sustainable consumption literature by
positioning cons umer trust as a central affective psychological mechanism underlying
sustainable food adoption behavior. While prior studies have predominantly emphasized
environmental concern and functional product attributes, the present findings demonstrate that
emotionally embedded trust formation plays a more critical role in transforming sustainability-
related perceptions into meaningful behavioral commitment.
Second, this study enriches symbolic consumption and Consumer Culture Theory
literature by demonstrating tha t environmentally responsible food consumption increasingly
reflects identity-related and ethically meaningful lifestyle behavior. Consumers do not merely
purchase sustainable food products for utilitarian reasons but also to reinforce desirable self -
concepts, responsible lifestyles, and socially valued moral identities.
Third, this study contributes to emerging market consumer behavior literature by
demonstrating that sustainability skepticism and authenticity uncertainty remain important
contextual challe nges influencing environmentally responsible purchasing behavior. Trust
therefore becomes increasingly important in reducing uncertainty and strengthening
consumers’ willingness to adopt sustainable products.

Finally, this study proposes an integrated psychological framework explaining sustainable food
adoption behavior through the interaction among health consciousness, perceived
environmental value, affective trust formation, purchase intention, and lifestyle -oriented
behavioral commitment. This framework contributes to a more comprehensive understanding
of environmentally responsible consumption behavior within emerging market environments.

Managerial Implications
The findings provide several important managerial implications for sustainable food
producers, retailers, and marketers. First, companies should strengthen consumer trust by
emphasizing transparency, authenticity, and credible sustainability communication.
Consumers are more likely to adopt environmentally responsible food products when
sustainability claims are perceived as trustworthy, verifiable, and consistent.
Second, marketers should position sustainable food products not only as environmentally
friendly alternatives but also as symbols of healthy lifestyles, ethical awareness, and
responsible living. Emotional and identity -oriented marketing communication strategies may
therefore strengthen consumers’ psychological attachment toward sustainable products.
Third, sustainable food producers should enhance consumer education regarding t he
health and environmental benefits of hydroponic vegetables. Providing accessible information
regarding production processes, food safety standards, and sustainability practices may reduce
consumer skepticism and strengthen trust formation.
Fourth, companies should recognize that sustainable food adoption increasingly reflects
lifestyle-oriented behavioral commitment rather than purely functional purchasing decisions.
Sustainable marketing strategies should therefore focus not only on product performance but
also on symbolic meaning, ethical congruence, and long -term lifestyle positioning associated
with environmentally responsible consumption behavior.
Finally, companies should recognize that trust -building strategies require long -term
consistency and per ceived authenticity. Sustainability communication perceived as
exaggerated, inconsistent, or misleading may increase consumer skepticism and weaken trust
toward environmentally responsible brands.

CONCLUSION AND SUGGESTIONS
Conclusion
This study examined the psychological mechanisms underlying sustainable food
adoption behavior among hydroponic vegetable consumers in Indonesia as an emerging market
context. Drawing upon symbolic consumption theory, trust theory, and the Theory of Planned
Behavior, the stud y investigated the relationships among health consciousness, perceived
environmental value, consumer trust, purchase intention, and sustainable food adoption
behavior.
The findings demonstrate that health consciousness and perceived environmental value
significantly strengthen consumer trust, which subsequently influences purchase intention and
long-term sustainable food adoption behavior. More importantly, the findings reveal that
consumer trust functions not merely as a cognitive evaluation mechanism but as an affective
psychological conversion mechanism linking sustainability -related perceptions with
meaningful behavioral commitment. Sustainable food consumption is therefore shaped not
only by rational product evaluation but also by emotional reassurance,  perceived authenticity,
ethical alignment, and identity-related lifestyle orientation.
This study further demonstrates that sustainable food adoption increasingly reflects
psychologically embedded lifestyle commitment rather than purely functional purchas ing
behavior. Consumers who perceive sustainable food products as authentic, trustworthy, and
aligned with their personal values are more likely to internalize environmentally responsible
consumption as part of their long -term lifestyle identity. Sustainab le consumption behavior

therefore represents not only an economic decision -making process but also an expression of
ethical self-congruity, responsible living, and socially meaningful identity construction.
From a theoretical perspective, this study contri butes to sustainable consumption
literature by positioning consumer trust as a central affective mechanism underlying
environmentally responsible purchasing behavior. The findings additionally enrich symbolic
consumption and Consumer Culture Theory literat ure by demonstrating that sustainable food
adoption behavior increasingly reflects emotional, symbolic, and identity -oriented
consumption processes. Furthermore, the study contributes to emerging market consumer
behavior research by highlighting the critic al importance of trust formation within contexts
characterized by sustainability skepticism, information asymmetry, and authenticity
uncertainty.
From a managerial perspective, the findings suggest that sustainable food producers
and marketers should prior itize transparency, authenticity, and emotionally meaningful
sustainability communication to strengthen consumer trust and long -term behavioral
commitment. Sustainable food marketing strategies should therefore move beyond purely
functional product positioning and increasingly emphasize ethical alignment, healthy lifestyle
symbolism, and responsible consumption identity.
Despite these contributions, this study contains several limitations that provide
opportunities for future research. First, the study util ized cross -sectional data, limiting the
ability to examine changes in sustainable consumption behavior over time. Future studies may
therefore employ longitudinal approaches to better understand the evolution of sustainable
lifestyle commitment. Second, th e study focused specifically on hydroponic vegetable
consumers in Indonesia; consequently, future research may examine other sustainable product
categories and cross -cultural contexts to improve generalizability. Third, future studies may
incorporate addit ional psychological constructs such as environmental anxiety, moral
obligation, lifestyle congruence, perceived consumer effectiveness, or sustainability -related
emotional attachment to further enrich understanding regarding environmentally responsible
consumption behavior.
More broadly, the findings suggest that the future of sustainable consumption may
increasingly depend not only on product functionality or environmental performance but also
on consumers’ emotional trust, ethical identification, and psyc hological attachment toward
sustainability-oriented lifestyles. Sustainable food adoption therefore represents a broader
cultural and behavioral transformation in which environmentally responsible consumption
becomes integrated into consumers’ identities, everyday practices, and long-term perceptions
of meaningful living.

Limitations
This paper presents a conceptual System Dynamics model and does not claim that its
parameters have already been empirically calibrated. It should therefore be treated as a research
framework rather than a forecasting instrument. Future research should collect longitudinal
data on learning investment, knowledge accumulation, innovation activity, market
responsiveness, revenue, productivity, and reinvestment. These data can be us ed to calibrate
and validate the Vensim model (Arranz et al., 2023; Viswanathan & Telukdarie, 2021).

Recommendation
Future research is recommended to use longitudinal designs and expand the study to
other sustainable food products and cultural contexts. Additional psychological variables, such
as environmental anxiety, moral obligation, and perceived consumer effectiveness, may also
be included to provide a broader understanding of sustainable food adoption.
