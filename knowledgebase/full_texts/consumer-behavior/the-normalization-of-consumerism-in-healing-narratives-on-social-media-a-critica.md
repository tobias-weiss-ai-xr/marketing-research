The widespread shift in the meaning of healing across social media platforms
has transformed public perceptions of psychological recovery into practices
centered on the consumption of products, services, and experiences. This
study aims to critically exami ne the changing meaning of healing and its
implications for consumerist practices. Employing a mixed-methods approach
with a case study design, the research combines qualitative and quantitative
data to provide a comprehensive analysis. The qualitative dat a consist of
TikTok videos posted by @avian.brands, which were analyzed using Norman
Fairclough's Critical Discourse Analysis (CDA) through its three analytical
dimensions: text, discursive practice, and social practice. The quantitative
component comprises secondary survey data on social media usage collected
from multiple sources to contextualize the phenomenon. The findings
demonstrate that healing narratives on social media function as discursive
instruments that normalize consumption as a legitimate so lution to
psychological problems. The analysis further identifies a recurring cycle in
which stress is mediated through media narratives that construct product
desirability, encourage consumption, and ultimately produce only temporary
healing, thereby rein forcing repeated consumer behavior rather than
addressing the underlying causes of emotional distress. Six key data sets were
identified from the Avian Brands campaign videos, including four findings at
the textual level and two at the discursive practice level. The social practice
analysis was conducted across three interconnected dimensions—situational,
institutional, and social—revealing how healing discourse reproduces capitalist
ideology by redefining emotional recovery as a consumer practice.
1. Introduction
Within the field of mental health, healing is understood as a continuous and dynamic process of overcoming stress,
trauma, emotional exhaustion, and pressures arising from the social environment. This process can be achieved through
various non-clinical activities, such as self -reflection, journaling, or communicating with trusted individuals. The growing
popularity of the healing phenomenon is closely associated with the increasing prevalence of mental health problems
worldwide. According to the World Heal th Organization (WHO), approximately one in seven people globally experiences
mental health challenges, with depression and anxiety being the most common disorders. In Indonesia, the 2018 National
Basic Health Research (Riset Kesehatan Dasar or Riskesdas) reported that the prevalence of emotional mental disorders
among individuals aged over 15 years increased from approximately 6.0% in 2013 to 9.8% (around 19 million people) in
2018.
The phenomenon commonly referred to as self-healing has undergone a significant shift in meaning over the past
few years. Rather than being associated primarily with medical recovery, healing has increasingly become a cultural
practice emphasizing individual efforts to maintain and restore mental well-being through excessive consumption, such as
purchasing products or taking leisure trips. This transformation is closely linked to the growing consumer culture in modern
society. Consumption is no longer viewed merely as an activity that satisfies practical needs but also as a means of self -
expression and emotional regulation (Faisal et al., 2025; Pratiwi et al., 2026; Andini et al., 2022). Various industries have
strategically capitalized on this tendency by mass -producing narratives surrounding self -love, self-reward, and me time,
linking these concepts to a wide range of products and experiences. Consequently, the distinction between genuine

Academic interest in the healing phenomenon has expanded across various disciplines, particularly communication
studies, psychology, and tourism. Several studies have examined how the tourism industry utilizes the healing trend as a

focus primarily on phenomenological aspects, such as the popularity of the term healing or its relationship with recreational
activities and mental well -being. Studies specifically investigating how healing narratives are employed to normalize
consumerist practices remain limited, particularly from the perspective of Critical Discourse Analysis (CDA). Furthermore,
previous research has generally approached healing as a cultural trend, leaving the ideological dynamics embedded in its
linguistic construction largely unexplored. Another noticeable lim itation is the insufficient attention given to how the
meaning of healing is discursively constructed by various social actors, including the media, commercial industries, and
social media users.
A relevant study conducted by Eliya et al. (2026), entitled Language as a Space for Healing: The Representation of
Mental Health Discourse among Muslim Women on Instagram, investigated representations of healing discourse among
Muslim women on Instagram using Sara Mills' Critical Discourse Analysis framework. The findings revealed that language
within healing narratives functions as a psychological medium for fostering self -acceptance while strengthening social
solidarity and religious identity among users.  In this context, social media serves as a symbolic space where individuals,
particularly Muslim women, cultivate emotional support and regulate their emotions through affirmative and reflective
linguistic practices. Although the study successfully identif ied the role of language in constructing spaces for healing, its
analysis primarily emphasized the affirmative and emancipatory dimensions of healing discourse. It paid limited attention
to other ideological dimensions that may be embedded within such narratives. Consequently, healing was portrayed as a
neutral and constructive practice without questioning how the discourse itself may function as a mechanism for normalizing
particular societal values.
Numerous studies have also examined consumer behavior on social media. One notable example is research on
the phenomenon of flexing on TikTok. The study conducted by Bamazruk et al. (2025), entitled The Culture of Flexing on
TikTok: Its Impact on Consumer Behavior and Mental Health among Students at the University of Lampung, demonstrated
that exposure to flexing content encourages students to purchase products beyond their actual needs while simultaneously
triggering psychological pressures such as anxiety  and diminished self -confidence. Nevertheless, the study focused
primarily on the psychological and behavioral consequences experienced by individuals after exposure to social media
content. Its analysis positioned individuals largely as passive recipients  of media influence without examining how such
content is discursively produced and disseminated. As a result, the study did not investigate how language, narratives,
and media representations contribute to constructing and normalizing consumerist practices.
Similarly, Siregar's (2024) study, Reading the Direction of United States Policy through President Donald Trump's
Statements, demonstrated that language in President Donald Trump's public statements functions not only as a vehicle
for conveying information but also as a strategic instrument for shaping public opinion and legitimizing policy directions.
Within this framework, discourse possesses a performative nature in which metaphors and rhetorical strategies are
employed to frame reality and reinforce particular ideologies. These findings highlight the power of language in constructing
and directing broader social practices. However, because the study focused primarily on political rhetoric, its analysis
emphasized the relationship between language and pub lic policy, illustrating the ideological power of language within
structural and institutional contexts of authority.
Critical Discourse Analysis (CDA) emerged as a response to the limitations of conventional discourse analysis in
addressing issues of ideology and power. According to Ratnaningsih (2019, p. 2), CDA is a method of discourse analysis
that seeks to determine whether discourse has been influenced by particular intentions and how such discourse affects
society as readers or listeners. CDA conceptualizes language not merely as a tool of communication but as a form of
social practice intrinsically connected to context, interests, and social structures. Accordingly, CDA goes beyond surface-
level linguistic analysis by uncovering the relationships among power, ideology, and social action embedded within
language use.
Within the framework of Critical Discourse Analysis, several key characteristics provide the foundation for
understanding how discourse operates within social relations. According to Eriyanto (2008, pp. 7–13), these characteristics
include action, context,  history, power, and ideology. First, discourse is understood as an active form of social practice

Based on the background described above, this study addresses the following research questions: (1) What are
the effects of the shifting meaning of healing? and (2) How are consumerist practices constructed and interpreted as part
of the healing process? In line with these questions, this study aims to analyze the transformation and implications of the
meaning of healing in relation to consumerist practices. This research is particularly important because social media
narratives increasingly portray mental well-being as something that can be achieved through the consumption of specific
products, thereby potentially distorting the public's understanding of healing as a concept grounded in medical and
psychological principles. Over time, such representations may reinforce misinformation regarding mental health recovery.
The novelty of this study lies in its application of a mixed -methods approach. Whereas previous studies have generally
examined healing from phenomenological and cultural perspectives, this research investigates how healing narratives are
produced and disseminated while identifying the social consequences of normalizing consumerism through healing
discourse, including the growing perception that mental health recovery can be achieved primarily through consumption.
2. Methodology
This study employs a mixed-methods approach, integrating both quantitative and qualitative methods within a single
research design. According to Sugiyono (2011), mixed methods combine quantitative and qualitative approaches
simultaneously to produce more c omprehensive, valid, and reliable findings. In this study, the quantitative component
serves as supporting evidence in the form of survey data collected from various sources to objectively contextualize the
phenomenon, while the qualitative component is used to conduct an in-depth analysis of the research object. Specifically,
this research adopts a case study design. Creswell (2015) defines a case study as a research strategy that explores a
contemporary phenomenon within its real-life context through the collection of detailed and context-specific data.
Data were collected using two techniques: non -participant observation and documentation. Non -participant
observation was employed to examine patterns in the use of the term healing without the researcher's direct involvement
in the observed interactions. T his method was selected to obtain authentic data that accurately reflect existing social
practices. Documentation was conducted using electronic devices, such as smartphones, to capture and archive relevant
materials. The collected data consisted of photog raphs, screenshots, and textual records, which were subsequently
organized into descriptive narratives. Relevant texts were systematically documented through screenshots and transcribed
into tabular form for further analysis.
The primary data source consisted of social media narratives published in 2023 that explicitly or implicitly associated
healing with consumer activities. The official TikTok account of Avian Brands (@avian.brands) was selected as the primary
corpus for analysis. The selection criteria focused on texts that represented healing as a solution to social or emotional
conditions and those containing identifiable ideological or persuasive agendas.
The analytical framework adopted in this study is Fairclough's (1995, p. 28) three -dimensional model of Critical
Discourse Analysis (CDA), comprising the following stages:
a. Text Analysis
The textual dimension examines the linguistic features of the selected discourse, including grammatical structures,
lexical choices, diction, pronouns, metaphors, and other formal linguistic elements. Each linguistic feature is analyzed to
identify the values embedded within the discourse. According to Fairclough (as cited in Munfarida, 2014, pp. 9–10), formal
textual elements embody four types of values: experiential, relational, expressive, and connective values.
b. Discursive Practice Analysis
This dimension investigates the processes of text production, distribution, and consumption, particularly how Avian
Brands creates and disseminates healing narratives through the TikTok platform. The analysis considers the
communicative context, the social  media trends incorporated into the content, and the characteristics of the intended

3. Results and Discussion
The increasing use of psychological terminology to market products demonstrates how brands strategically employ
persuasive language to establish stronger emotional connections with consumers. This approach creates the impression
of empathy and relevance wi thin a society that has become increasingly open to discussing mental health. Baudrillard
(1998, p. 25) argues that consumption constitutes the fundamental basis of human social existence and serves as the
driving force shaping contemporary society. Conseq uently, many brands commodify mental health by framing product
purchases as a means of fulfilling emotional needs and providing therapeutic benefits. Commodification, in this context,
refers to the transformation of goods, ideas, or experiences into market able commodities. One of the principal criticisms
of commodification is its reductionist tendency, whereby complex meanings are simplified into exchange value. As
Fairclough (as cited in Sari, 2015) explains, commodification shifts attention toward the pro duction, distribution, and
consumption of goods and services rather than their broader social or cultural meanings.
The findings of this study indicate that healing narratives on social media often function as a form of temporary
emotional escape rather than encouraging meaningful reflection on the underlying causes of psychological distress. These
narratives operate as discursive devices that normalize and legitimize consumerist practices through emotionally appealing
frameworks. Within this discourse, the lexical item heal functions as a linguistic bridge connecting affective vulnerability
with market -oriented activiti es. Individuals are implicitly encouraged to perceive self -recovery as achievable through
purchasing products, traveling, taking staycations, visiting cafés, staying in luxury hotels, or consuming various forms of
digital content. These activities represen t carefully designed recreational experiences that support the study's primary
objectives of demonstrating how the language of recovery has been reconstructed as a discourse of consumption. From
Fairclough's perspective, this discourse redirects the meaning of recovery away from psychological or medical processes
toward consumer -oriented practices. Similarly, Van Dijk interprets this phenomenon as a manifestation of ideological
normalization, through which consumption is presented as logical, safe, and emotionally necessary.

narratives, the more likely they are to perceive them as socially normal and acceptable. Therefore, the following discussion
applies Norman Fairclough's Critical Discourse Analysis to examine the data in greater depth. This analytical framework
complements the preceding findings by exploring the interrelationship between text, discursive practice, and social
practice. Ultimately, it provides a more systematic explanation of how meaning is constructed within the selected narratives
and demonstrates the research objectives through empirically supported arguments.
3.1. Text Analysis (Description)
From Norman Fairclough's perspective, the textual dimension of Critical Discourse Analysis (CDA) views language
as a semiotic practice embedded with ideological values. To understand how reality is represented within a text, the
analysis focuses on formal linguistic features, such as vocabulary and grammatical structures, through which particular

systems of meaning and social relations are constructed. Fairclough (2001, p. 19) argues that speaking, listening, writing,
and reading are not merely linguistic activities but fundamentally social phenomena. These communicative practices are
shaped by the social environment while simultaneously influencing that environment. Consequently, every formal linguistic
feature should be examined beyond its surface meaning by identifying the values embedded within it. Fairclough (as cited
in Munfarida, 2014, p. 9) categorizes these values into experiential, relational, expressive, and connective values.
First, experiential value refers to the way text producers construct social reality through their linguistic choices. This
value explicitly reveals the ideological assumptions underlying the construction of meaning within a text. Through the
selection of p articular lexical items, writers or speakers establish specific perspectives and direct audiences toward
particular interpretations or interests. Second, relational value concerns the use of language to establish, maintain, or
regulate social relationships among participants within a discourse. Word choice, sentence structure, pronoun usage, and
modality function not only as vehicles for conveying information but also as indicators of social position, authority, and the
interactional relationships intended by the text producer. Third, expressive value refers to the judgments, attitudes, or
evaluations expressed by the text producer toward the reality being discussed. It reflects the writer's or speaker's stance
toward particular events, objects, individuals,  or social groups. Finally, connective value relates to the coherence and
cohesion that link different parts of a text into a unified whole. This value encompasses not only internal textual
relationships established through conjunctions, references, and co hesive devices but also the relationship between the
text and the situational context in which it is produced. Accordingly, the purpose of connective value is to explain how
textual elements are organized logically and coherently to generate particular meanings for readers or audiences.

Textually, the statement above demonstrates several significant linguistic strategies. First, the lexical choices
“stress” and “healing” are particularly noteworthy. From Fairclough's perspective, these terms reflect experiential value,
through which the r eality of mental health is constructed as something that can be healed through practices that are
perceived as modern, fashionable, and accessible. According to Fairclough (as cited in Munfarida, 2014, p. 9), experiential
value is embedded in the vocabular y of a text and serves to identify the relationship between textual meaning and the
discourse that underlies it, while simultaneously revealing its ideological foundation. The use of the terms “stress” and
“healing” suggests that local linguistic practices have increasingly been influenced by global discourse and popular culture
through digital media. Within this context, stress is represented as an ordinary emotional condition that can be addressed
through simple, personal, and aesthetically oriented pract ices. This interpretation is consistent with the argument of
Kupriyanov and Zhdanov (as cited in Lumban Gaol, 2016, p. 1), who describe stress as a defining characteristic of modern
life.
Second, the phrase “terapi warna” carries the presupposition that color possesses therapeutic legitimacy despite
the absence of any scientific explanation supporting such a claim. The use of terminology associated with scientific or
psychological knowledge  creates the impression that the brand possesses expertise and sensitivity regarding mental
health issues. In many cases, however, such expressions function metaphorically rather than referring to established
psychological frameworks. At this stage, the di scourse undergoes a process of semantic expansion, whereby complex
psychological concepts are simplified into everyday expressions that are easily appropriated for commercial purposes.

From the perspective of relational value, the text establishes an ongoing interpersonal relationship between the
producer and the audience. Munfarida (2014, p. 10) explains that relational value can be identified through grammatical
features, particularly (1) sentence types —including declarative, imperative, and interrogative forms —and (2) modality,
which reflects the authority assumed by the speaker or writer. In this case, the simple sentence structure and the modal
expression “bisa juga” ("can also") cre ate an informal and persuasive tone. Rather than presenting itself as a scientific
authority issuing definitive recommendations, the text producer adopts the role of a friendly adviser offering an alternative
possibility to the audience. This type of relat ionship is characteristic of contemporary social media discourse on mental
health, where emotional connection is often prioritized over scientific formality. Moreover, the particle “juga” ("also")
implicitly suggests that “terapi warna” belongs to the broa der category of accepted healing methods, thereby normalizing
it as one possible approach to emotional recovery.

Syntactically, the construction of the statement begins with the preposition “Dengan…” ("With"), forming a directive
phrase followed by the nominal expression “pilihan koleksi” ("a selection of collections"). This structure omits an explicit
subject or agent. The absence of a subject creates an impersonal effect, suggesting that the promised emotional benefits
are universally applicable rather than directed toward a specific group of consumers. The phrase “pilihan koleksi” positions
the text producer as an authority capable of offering solutions, while the audience is implicitly positioned as individuals
seeking and selecting those solutions. This reflects relational value in Fairclough's framework, whereby linguistic choices
are employed to establish, maint ain, and negotiate social relationships between the text producer and its consumers.
Furthermore, the structure softens the promotional intent of the message. Instead of issuing a direct command to purchase
the product, the text presents consumption as a matter of personal choice, thereby making the persuasive appeal appear
more subtle, voluntary, and individualized.
The statement also constructs a particular representation of reality in which emotional distress and stress are
portrayed as ordinary conditions that can be addressed through the selection of appropriate products. In this
representation, individuals are no longer depicted as active agents responsible for managing their own mental well-being;
instead, this role is transferred to commercially packaged product collections. Consequently, the discourse undergoes a
process of reification, whereby abstract psychological experiences are transformed into tangible commodities that can be
purchased. Through this process, the text implicitly equates the act of shopping with the process of healing, thereby
redefining emotional recovery as a consumer practice rather than a complex psychological or therapeutic process.

3.3. Social Practice Analysis (Explanation)
Fairclough argues that discourse constitutes a form of social practice capable of reproducing as well as transforming
social relations within society. According to Eriyanto (2001, p. 321), dominant ideologies are interpreted and disseminated
through the social forces operating within society itself. Accordingly, this dimension of analysis examines how discourse
relates to ideology, social structures, power relations, and broader societal conditions across three interconnected levels:
situational, institutional, and social.
At the situational level, the emergence of healing narratives on social media is closely associated with the increasing
pressures of modern life, particularly among younger generations. Psychological escape, as a response to emotional
distress, may arise from various factors, including academic stress, workplace pressure, social anxiety, and post-pandemic
emotional exhaustion. On the one hand, social media can function as a platform that encourages individuals to seek
appropriate recovery through professional mental health support. On the other hand, it may also promote less constructive
coping strategies by framing consumer activities as forms of emotional recovery. Through aesthetically appealing visuals
and carefully curated lifestyle representations, dig ital platforms reinforce this discourse, leading audiences to associate
self-recovery with consumption-oriented activities frequently displayed online. Within this context, both language and visual
representation contribute to constructing a new definition of healing.
At the institutional level, the normalization of consumerism within healing narratives is inseparable from the
economic interests of digital capitalist industries. Platforms such as Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube operate through
algorithms that continuousl y expose users to similar and highly popular content. Simultaneously, industries including
tourism, beauty, food and beverage (F&B), fashion, and retail strategically employ the concept of healing as a marketing
tool. Expressions such as “self -reward,” “me time,” “you deserve this,” and “therapy” function as persuasive discursive
strategies that encourage individuals to purchase products or experiences in pursuit of emotional comfort. Consequently,

At the social level, the controversy surrounding consumer -oriented healing narratives is deeply connected to the

4. Conclusion
This study demonstrates that healing narratives on social media function as discursive instruments that normalize
consumption as a means of addressing psychological problems. Through Norman Fairclough's Critical Discourse Analysis,
the findings reveal how language and visual representations construct a new understanding of healing by associating it
with consumerism and forms of temporary emotional recovery. The widespread use of social media across diverse
segments of society further amplifies the reproduction of this discourse, enabling healing narratives to circulate rapidly and
become increasingly normalized through continuous online interaction.
The findings indicate that the discourse of healing no longer primarily refers to a psychological or therapeutic
process but has increasingly been reconstructed as a consumer-oriented practice embedded within digital media culture.
As a result, emotional well-being is represented as something that can be attained through the consumption of products,
services, and curated lifestyle experiences, thereby reinforcing capitalist values while obscuring the structural and
psychological causes of emotional distress.
In total, six analytical data sets were identified from the Avian Brands campaign videos in accordance with the
research objectives. Four data sets were analyzed at the textual (descriptive) level, while two data sets were examined
within the discursive pr actice (interpretive) dimension. Finally, the social practice (explanatory) analysis was conducted
across three interrelated levels—situational, institutional, and social—to demonstrate how healing discourse is produced,
circulated, interpreted, and ultimately normalized within contemporary consumer culture.
