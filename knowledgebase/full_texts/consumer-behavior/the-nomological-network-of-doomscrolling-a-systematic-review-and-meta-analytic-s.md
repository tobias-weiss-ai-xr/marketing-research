ABSTRACT
In the last half of the decade there has been an expansion of negative issues on the
globe. Algorithmically curated digital platforms continuously deliver such content to
users, creating conditions to raise the emerging online problematic behaviors such as
doomscrolling. Despite growing scholarly interest, research on doomscrolling remains
fragmented, and the relationships among its antecedents and psychological
consequences have not been comprehensively synthesized. Therefore, this study aims
to provide an  integrated overview of the doomscrolling literature through a
systematic literature review, bibliometric analysis, and Meta -Analytic Structural
Equation Modeling (MASEM). Bibliometric analyses were conducted using 107 studies
to examine publication trends, influential sources, and thematic developments within
the field. Upon completion of PRISMA guidelines, 73 independent studies are
examined thoroughly. Accordingly, findings indicated positive association with
anxiety, depression, stress, uncertainty -related constructs, and negative association
with life satisfaction and well -being. Consequently, a final model is proposed from
digital consumer behavior perspective and tested. MASEM results showed that
doomscrolling partially mediates the relationship betwe en problematic social media
use and well -being. These findings highlight the importance of understanding
doomscrolling as a contemporary digital behavior with significant implications for
individual well-being, contributing to broader efforts aimed at promoting healthy lives
and enhancing well-being.
Keywords: Doomscrolling, Nomological Network, PRISMA, MASEM.

1. INTRODUCTION
The development of digital media platforms has changed how people reach, consume, and share
information. Through digital platforms, users can receive a near -constant stream of information
at almost any time. Whenever a crisis (such as a pandemic, earthquak e, economic turmoil,
international politics, etc.) happens in real life, many individuals turn to digital media for
information and situational awareness. This behavior serves an important function in obtaining
information; however, when it combines with individuals’ habit of using digital platforms, such as
infinite scrolling, a behavior known as “doomscrolling” emerges. Rather than simply consuming
news regularly, doomscrolling is defined as repetitive, habitual consumption of predominantly
negative online news, combined with a continuous inability to stop reading even when it causes
emotional discomfort (Sharma et al., 2022).
In today's new world order, with increasing instances of crisis-driven content, doomscrolling has
become a commonly observed behavior. Therefore, understanding doomscrolling is essential, not
only because it has become so widespread but also because of its  potentially large impact on
society. Research to date indicates that doomscrolling is associated with a wide range of variables,
including feelings of anxiety (Türk-Kurtça & Kocatürk, 2025), levels of stress (Taskin et al., 2024),
sleep difficulties and depression (Yang et al., 2024), high levels of psychological distress (Satici et
al., 2023), self -compassion and death distress (Kaya et al., 2025). This variety shows that
doomscrolling's associations are different yet similarly negative. Thus, it can be concluded that
doomscrolling is more than an independent negative behavior; i t is also associated with many
others. In addition to its potential negative associations, doomscrolling also contributes to larger
fields of research such as psychology (Satici et al., 2023), media studies (Ytre-Arne & Moe, 2021),
information systems (Chand & Mishra, 2025), and business and marketing (Mandliya et al., 2024).
As research on doomscrolling continues to expand across multiple disciplines and topics, existing
literature remains fragmented and dispersed across disciplines, theories, and empirical contexts.
Existing literature reviews tend to focus on parts of doomsc rolling rather than the behavior as a
whole. Critically, only a few studies have applied a systematic approach to consolidate
doomscrolling (Avcı et al., 2026; Sharpe et al., 2026). Consequently, despite the growing interest
and volume of research, a gap r emains in the comprehensive understanding of how the diverse
predictors and outcomes of doomscrolling interact within a broader nomological framework. This
gap is particularly important given the relevance of doomscrolling to psychology, communication,
and marketing, where understanding digital consumer behavior is a central research concern.
To address this gap, the current situation requires a methodology that goes beyond conventional
narrative synthesis. Literature reviews can provide useful overall summaries of what is known,
but they cannot quantitatively evaluate complex sets of interrela ted constructs. Therefore, this
study asks how the diverse predictors and outcomes of doomscrolling can be integrated into a
unified nomological framework and seeks answers using cumulative empirical evidence through
Meta-Analytic Structural Equation Model ing (MASEM). This approach combines the benefits of
meta-analysis and structural equation modeling to statistically evaluate complex systems using
pooled empirical research (Steinmetz & Block, 2022). Accordingly, this research synthesizes the
doomscrolling literature to identify the predictors, mediators, moderators, and outcomes of
doomscrolling, develop an integrated nomological framework, and empirically test part of the
model (from a digital consumer behavior perspective) using Meta -Analytic Structural Equation
Modeling. This approach will provide a comprehensive picture of the structural relationships

2. CONCEPTUAL FRAMEWORK AND THEORETICAL PERSPECTIVES
2.1. Doomscrolling
Doomscrolling is compulsively reading or watching a continuous flow of negative content on
social media (Sharma et al., 2022). is now a known term in society. According to Karanevych et al.
(2024), its linguistic origins appear to have developed between 2020 – 2022 and gained broad
usage in the context of pandemic information consumption. Sharma et al. (2022) identified it as a
separately identifiable type of media habit in which soci al media users engage in a pattern of
dependence on information in their newsfeed, with attention to negativity and tragedy related to
global catastrophes and crises. Satici  et al. (2023) developed a measurement instrument for
doomscrolling. They extended the definition to include consuming negative online information in
a compulsive, repetitive (vicious) pattern in which individuals become trapped in a self -
reinforcing, distressing content cycle despite experiencing adverse emotional consequences.
Doomscrolling differs from typical media consumption because it involves heavy engagement
with negative content and difficulty disengaging. Unlike regular information seeking,
doomscrolling resembles passive media engagement, where users consume content wi thout
active interaction (like, share, comment), and is further reinforced by platform features such as
personalized streams, notification systems, and infinite scrolling (Neijzen, 2024, p.  185).
Additionally, doomscrolling is not limited to social media platforms. Alternatively, continuous
consumption of negative information may occur across different media environments such as
online news websites, television coverage, and mobile news appli cations, whenever people
engage in an ongoing flow of crisis-related content (Ytre-Arne & Moe, 2021, p. 1740).
The close connection between doomscrolling and problematic internet use also requires
additional clarification. Both share a similar structure in terms of maladaptive and potentially
compulsive patterns of digital engagement. Still, doomscrolling is distin guished by its particular
focus on the repetitive consumption of negative and distressing information, as opposed to
excessive interaction with digital platforms more generally (Sharpe et al., 2026, p. 29).  Taken
together, doomscrolling is a compulsion or  repeated seeking of negative information through an
individual's repeated interaction with negative content. Despite growing consensus on the major
elements of the concept, the framework and its distinction from related forms of online behavior
are still being refined (Shabahang et al., 2025).

2.2. Theoretical Perspectives on Doomscrolling
Researchers have employed multiple theories to understand why individuals engage in
doomscrolling. This section provides an overview of theoretical perspectives on doomscrolling.
Uses and Gratifications Theory (U&G) provides a framework for understanding why individuals
use media for specific purposes or motivations. U&G helps explain how individuals choose media
based on goals such as meeting psychological or emotional needs, incl uding social interaction,
entertainment, or staying up to date with current events (Katz et al., 1973). According to Usman
et al. (2025, p. 360), the Theory of U&G explains that doomscrolling occurs because people actively
seek information and emotional comfort through digital media, especially in times of uncertainty.
Nevertheless, this quest can also result in too much exposure to negative content.
The theory of uncertainty reduction states that people try to find as much information as possible
about uncertain situations so they feel less afraid when something does happen (Berger &
Calabrese, 1975). The literature also uses this theory to explain do omscrolling ( Kesner et al.,
2025; Ulaş & İncekara, 2026; Yoon et al., 2021). Ulaş and İncekara (2026) explain that, in response
to uncertainty, people who engage in higher levels of media consumption tend to exhibit
maladaptive behaviors such as doomscrolling. Similarly, empirical evidence from Kaya and
Griffiths (2025) that intolerance of uncertai nty was positively associated with doomscrolling
further suggests that individuals may repeatedly seek negative information to reduce uncertainty.
The Stimulus -Organism-Response (SOR) model explains how environmental stimuli affect
individuals’ internal psychological states and behavioral responses (Mehrabian & Russell, 1974).
When applied to doomscrolling, negative news exposure and information over load act as stimuli
that increase the tendency and contribute to negative psychological outcomes (Mandliya et al.,
2024, p. 141).
2.3. Predictors and Outcomes of Doomscrolling
Empirical research has identified a broad range of factors that are associated with doomscrolling.
The literature review shows that the same factors associated with doomscrolling are sometimes
treated as exogenous and sometimes as endogenous. This issue ma y stem from the nature of
doomscrolling behavior, as Satici et al. (2023) explain doomscrolling as vicious. On the other hand,
some variables are seen as having a role only in predicting doomscrolling. Among these
predictors, uncertainty -related ones are common (Kaya & Bağatarhan, 2025; Kaya & Griffiths,
2025; Türk-Kurtça & Kocatürk, 2025). Individuals who are uncomfortable in uncertain situations
are likely to seek more information to reduce uncertainty and regain a sense of control (Türk -
Kurtça & Kocatürk, 2025, p.  2). Continuous exposure to negative news may thus be a coping
strategy through which individuals attempt to manage uncertainty (Kaya & Bağatarhan, 2025, p.
62). However, as Kesner et al. (2025) explain, this may lead to a vicious cycle of information
seeking and distress rather than reduced uncertainty.
Information seeking on social media is also seen as a predictor of doomscrolling. When there’s
uncertainty, people often turn to digital platforms for information to ease concerns and regain
some sense of control over what’s happening. However, the constan t search for answers may
slowly become a loop of seeing the same negative content, where people progressively depend on
following distressing news and eventually doomscrolling occurs (Koselioren  & Cakir, 2024).
Additionally, a variable such as news avoidance appears to be used only as an outcome. Ytre-Arne
and Moe (2021, p. 1740) state that, based on their findings, people became overwhelmed by
continuous exposure to negative news and responded b y intentionally disconnecting from news

PSMU has likewise been examined as both a predictor (Kaya, 2025) and an outcome (Öztürk,
2026). Finally, Akat and Hamarta (2025) examined social media addiction as both a predictor and
an outcome of doomscrolling.
2.4. Moderators and Mediators
The existing literature suggests that mediating roles have received more attention than
moderating effects for doomscrolling. Only a limited number of studies have examined alleviating
or aggravating effects; instead, numerous studies have employed indirec t effects. Additionally,
doomscrolling is also positioned as a mediator linking various predictors and outcomes.
In the doomscrolling literature, mediation models have been studied more than moderation
models. Nevertheless, existing evidence suggests that doomscrolling affects the size of the
relationships between psychological variables. For instance, Kaya and Griff iths (2025, p. 2327)
found that doomscrolling moderated the relationship between intolerance of uncertainty and
mental well -being. This means the effect of intolerance of uncertainty on well -being differed
across levels of doomscrolling. Likewise, Cengiz (2025) reported that AI anxiety was a mediating
and moderating variable in the relationship between doomscrolling and job -finding anxiety. In
general, although research on moderation is still scarce, the existing evidence suggests some
psychological variables that could moderate the effects of doomscrolling.
In contrast, the literature shows a large representation of mediation models. Mindfulness and
secondary traumatic stress have been found to mediate the relationship between doomscrolling
and well -being sequentially (Taskin et al., 2024), and psychological distress has been found to
mediate the effects of doomscrolling on life satisfaction, mental well -being, and harmony in life
(Satici et al., 2023). In addition, psychological resilience and intolerance of uncertainty mediated
the relationship between trait anxiety and doomscrolling (Türk -Kurtça & Kocatürk, 2025), and
future anxiety mediated the relationship between psychological distre ss and doomscrolling
(Kartol et al., 2023). Further evidence shows that FOMO and nomophobia serially mediate the
relationship between doomscrolling and poor sleep quality (Camadan & Uzunoğlu, 2026), and

3. METHODOLOGY
3.1. Research Design
This research uses a multi-stage design. First, it draws on studies examining doomscrolling to map
the concept and develop a comprehensive nomological network; second, it conducts a systematic
review to propose a research model using conceptual mapping. Fi nally, it tests the proposed
digital consumer behavior -oriented model using Meta -Analytic Structural Equation Modeling
(MASEM). The aim is to integrate the fragmented literature on doomscrolling, identify the
nomological network around the construct, and propose a theoretically grounded framework that
can guide future research from a marketing perspective. Accordingly, the research part addresses
the following questions:
Q1: How can the predictors, outcomes, mediators, and moderators in the doomscrolling literature be
synthesized into an integrated nomological network?
Q2: How can the nomological framework be used to create a research model from a marketing
perspective?
Q3: Can the model created be tested for future experimental research?
3.2. Stage 1: Nomological Network
In the first stage of forming a nomological network, a review is conducted to identify studies
examining doomscrolling and its associated constructs. The search for this stage was performed
only in the Scopus database, which provides a broad perspective on peer -reviewed academic
literature across the social sciences, business, ps ychology, and communication disciplines. The
search included variations of the term: doomscrolling OR "doom scrolling" OR doomscroll OR
"doom scroll". Following the search, a database was created for initial screening. To synthesize
the literature and integrate it into a nomological network. Data were analyzed using Python.
3.3. Stage 2: Proposing a Model for Marketing Research
In the second stage, a testable research model for marketing using the initial nomological network
is proposed . The identified nomological network variables were assessed from a marketing
perspective specifically from digital consumer behavior.  Although the initial network includes
psychological constructs, these constructs were considered important because they might explain
how individuals respond to and behave within digital environments. From a marketing
perspective, digital consumer behavio r is not limited to purchase -related activities but also
includes how individuals engage with, process, and respond in digital environments. Therefore,
the objective is to move beyond a general psychological understanding of doomscrolling and focus
on mechanisms directly relevant to netizens' digital information consumption behavior. Variables
conceptually relevant to information seeking, information processing, media engagement, digital

Second, a general model is constructed, and the variables of this general model are applied to a

3.4. Stage 3: Meta-Analytic Structural Equation Modeling (MASEM)
In the final stage of this research, after developing a testable model, MASEM was applied. MASEM
relies on the strengths of both meta -analysis and structural equation modeling. It allows
researchers to synthesize correlation coefficients from multiple studies and test theoretical
relationships based on the pooled evidence (Cheung & Hong, 2017, p. 265).
To perform MASEM, first a meta -analytic correlation matrix is constructed based on the
correlation coefficients of the extracted variables reported in previous stage. Then, the pooled
correlation matrix was used to estimate and assess the proposed structural model. The analysis
was conducted using the metaSEM procedures in RStudio. The model was assessed using
traditional structural equation modeling criteria, such as path significance and overall fit indices.
This procedure was chosen to assess the extent to which the proposed framework is supported
by empirical evidence.
4. RESULTS
4.1. Results of Nomological Network
4.1.1. Literature Characteristics
The bibliometric search in Scopus resulted in 107 documents related to doomscrolling. The
earliest publication on doomscrolling appeared in 2021, following the COVID -19 pandemic,
reflecting its emergence as a scholarly topic and the increased consumption o f crisis -related
digital content. The dataset covers the period 2021 -2026 (Data collection concluded on
12/06/2026) and includes documents from 96 journals. This suggests that doomscrolling
research is not concentrated in one field but is distributed across multiple fields. In addition, when
publication distribution by year is examined, the publication trend indicates rapidly growing
scholarly attention. Only four studies were published in 2021, but the number increased steadily
in subsequent years. Publication increased to 15 studies in 2022, 7 studies in 2023, 26 studies in
2024, and peaked at 33 studies in 2025. In 2026, only 12 studies were identified, but this reflects
a partial publication year and should be interpreted with caution. Overall, the trend suggests that
doomscrolling has moved from an emerging topic to a more established research field.

4.3. Results of MASEM
4.3.1. Data Preparation and Program
To examine the structural relationships among problematic social media use (PSMU),
doomscrolling (DS), and well -being (WELL), a two -stage Meta -Analytic Structural Equation
Modeling (MASEM) procedure was conducted using the metaSEM package in RStudio. First, data
were extracted manually in each documents for all correlation values and written in an excel
sheet. In the Excel sheet, the first column was the study number, the second was the study’s
sample size, the third and fourth were named Var1 and Var2, and the final column was the r value
for each correlation. The final dataset included 10 independent studies, with a cumulative sample
of 4,256 participants. In this stage, due to the limited number of studies, social media addiction,
problematic media use, and media addiction are considered as PSMU, and psychological
wellbeing, mental wellbeing, and valued living are considered as wellbeing.

5. DISCUSSION
This study synthesized the literature on doomscrolling and identified its general predictors,
mediators, moderators, and outcomes. In addition, the study proposed several models, including
a model from digital consumer behavior. To reach the final destinat ion, this research used
multiple stages to approach the subject and develop a final model.
The bibliometric results were the first step. Accordingly, the findings indicate that the number of
works published each year, as well as the research area, has increased. The rapid increase in
publications after the COVID -19 pandemic supports this finding , in which times of crisis and
uncertainty increase people’s consumption of negative online information (Ytre -Arne & Moe,
2021; Sharma et al., 2022). In addition, the keyword cloud and the respective centrality measures
indicated that the concept is examin ed with variables such as problematic social media use,
anxiety, depression, uncertainty, and psychological well -being. Finally, using bibliographic
contents and nomological networks, a general model for doomscrolling is presented.
In the second stage to initiate the meta -analytic process, a comprehensive systematic literature
review was conducted to obtain empirical evidence for doomscrolling. A PRISMA -based flow
revealed that empirical studies mostly associate doomscrolling with a variety of negative
psychological outcomes, including anxiety, depression, stress, psychological distress, and reduced
well-being. These findings are consistent with previous studies indicating that constant exposure
to negative online content might increa se levels of stress (Taskin et al., 2024) and create high
levels of psychological distress ( Satici et al., 2023). After completing the PRISMA stage in this
work, and given the frequently repeated variables, a more specific model is proposed.

In the third stage, using the PRISMA results, a more specific marketing -perspective model

applying MASEM. Accordingly, meta -analytic results showed a positive relationship between
problematic social media use and doomscrolling, and a negative relationship between
doomscrolling and well -being. These findings indicate that increased exposure to negative and
distressing online content may contribute to poorer psych ological functioning and lower
subjective well-being. This finding is consistent with recent empirical evidence suggesting that
those who doomscroll more frequently tend to report higher levels of problematic social media
use, as well as lower levels of life satisfaction and mental well-being (Soraci et al., 2025, p. 11880).
From a digital consumer behavior perspective, these studies help explain how platform
engagement behaviors translate into psychological outcomes. Social media platforms are
designed to encourage continuous information consumption, and users with problemati c usage
habits can be increasingly exposed to emotionally negative content. Doomscrolling is a particular
form of this process, where information searching becomes excessive and shifts from information
gathering to repetitive exposure to negative content. Thus, the negative effects of problematic
social media use on well -being may be partially explained by the emergence of doomscrolling
behavior. In addition, results also indicate a contrary disposition. In the literature, arguments such
as these suggest that, in times of uncertainty, individuals tend to focus on negative information to
reduce uncertainty ( Kaya & Bağatarhan, 2025; Kaya & Griffiths, 2025; Türk -Kurtça & Kocatürk,
2025). This is true on the one hand; however, constant exposure to distressing news may
eventually lead to greater emotional fatigue and adversely affect psychological well -being. Thus,
similar to İncekara's (2026) statement, doomscrolling can be understood as a maladaptive coping
strategy. Therefore, while it serves an information function in the short term, doomscrolling
ultimately harms netizens. In addition, doomscrolling should be viewed not only as a maladaptive
coping strategy but also as a digital consumption phenomenon with implications for netizens’
well-being and for sustainable development goals.
6. IMPLICATIONS
The results have several practical implications. The correlation between problematic social media
use and doomscrolling suggests that efforts to reduce unhealthy social media use may also reduce
doomscrolling. Digital literacy programs can educate users about compulsive information-seeking
patterns and help them cultivate healthier online habits. The negative association between
doomscrolling and well-being underscores the need to incorporate evaluations of digital behavior
into psychological and mental hea lth interventions. Practitioners working with people
experiencing anxiety, stress, or reduced psychological well -being may want to assess
doomscrolling behaviors.
Platforms such as social media should implement intervention strategies to reduce excessive
doomscrolling, such as reminders to limit use, suggestions to seek varied content, or algorithmic
interventions to limit extended exposure to highly negative conten t. Finally, results comply with
the United Nations Sustainable Development Goal 3, which promotes mental health and well -
being for all. As digital media plays a larger role in our lives, understanding and mitigating harmful
online behaviors like doomscrolling is an important public health priority.

7. LIMITATIONS
Several limitations should be considered when interpreting the findings. The systematic review
suggested a growing body of literature, although the number of studies available for some
variables was relatively limited. Thus, some relationships in the proposed conceptual framework
could not be tested with meta-analytic techniques. Additionally, most included studies used cross-
sectional designs. Therefore, causal interpretations should be made with caution, as the observed
associations may reflect reciprocal  relationships between specific timelines (COVID19,
earthquakes, wars, etc.) and doomscrolling behavior.
The mediation was applied based on pooled meta -analytic correlations. Although this approach
provides useful evidence of potential mechanisms, the mediation effects should not be interpreted
as definitive causal pathways. Finally, as platforms, algorithms,  and information ecosystems
continue to evolve, doomscrolling behaviors may also change as digital media environments
change rapidly. Thus, the results of the present study should be considered a snapshot of the
current literature, rather than a true representation of future online behaviors.
8. FUTURE RESEARCH
The present findings bring up multiple possibilities for future research. First, future research
should incorporate doomscrolling in broader digital consumer behavior frameworks. Further
investigation of doomscrolling in relation to online engagement, cons umption patterns, digital
addiction, and platform use behaviors could provide a more comprehensive understanding of
doomscrolling's function in today’s digital landscape. Second, the proposed model needs to be
tested with real-time research designs (e.g., experimental or survey-based designs) to capture the
dynamic nature of doomscrolling behavior better and establish temporal relationships among the
variables under study. Third, future research needs to assess the effectiveness of interventions
and platform-based strategies in reducing doomscrolling and improving digital well-being. These
efforts can help create healthier online environments and support wider societal well-being.
8. CONCLUSION
This study used bibliometric analysis and meta -analytic structural equation modeling to provide
a thorough synthesis of the emerging doomscrolling literature. The results reveal that
doomscrolling is positively associated with problematic social media use and a range of negative
psychological outcomes, and negatively associated with well-being and life satisfaction. The meta-
analytic results additionally indicated that doomscrolling partially mediated the relationship
between problematic social media use an d well -being. These findings support the idea that
doomscrolling is an important digital behavior with meaningful implications for mental and
psychological health.
In conclusion, as digital media become increasingly central to everyday life, understanding the
mechanisms and consequences of doomscrolling will be increasingly important for researchers,
practitioners, and policy -makers wishing to promote healthier forms  of online engagement and
well-being.
