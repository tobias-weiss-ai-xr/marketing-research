Abstract
Introduction: Ecopreneurship, also known as ecological entrepreneurship, has arisen as a long -term
solution to environmental issues while also promoting economic growth. With its numerous ecological
zones and growing environmental consciousness, India offers both enormous potential and challenges for
ecopreneurship. This article investigates the notion of ecopreneurship in India, stressing its challenges
including limited access to funding, an immature market for sustainable products, and consumer behavior
gaps between intentions and actual purchases, while also examining problems such as legislative
impediments, financial limits, and cultural differences.
Methodology: This study relies on secondary data sources, including government publications, journal
articles, trade journals, and other accessible online resources. Factors influencing the survival and growth
of ecopreneurial ventures were examined through quantitative data analysis, focusing on specific markets
and policy contexts. Case studies of emerging and proacti ve ecopreneurs in India were analyzed to gain
deeper insights into their experiences and knowledge, and qualitative methods were employed to identify
patterns and themes in the secondary data analysis. A comparative research strategy was used to assess
the progress of ecopreneurship in India relative to global trends. The overall efficiency and effectiveness
of ecopreneurial ventures were evaluated using common performance indicators such as environmental
responsibility, market share, and business profitability.

3. Literature Review
Ecopreneurship combines economic  value with environmental sustainability. Despite its
role in fostering a greener economy, ecopreneurs face challenges that hinder growth.
This review explores key issues based on various studies. Ecopreneurs face several
challenges that hinder their success. Financial constraints make securing funding difficult
due to perceived risks, high initial costs, and regulatory compliance burdens (Schaper,
2016; York & Venkataraman, 2010). Market barriers, such as limited consumer
awareness, skepticism about green claims, and high costs of eco -friendly products,
restrict market penetration (Belz & Binder, 2017; Hockerts & Wüstenhagen, 2010).
Regulatory challenges arise from bureaucratic hurdles, inconsistent policies, and
subsidies favoring conventional businesses, creating disadvantages for ecopreneurs
(Kirkwood & Walton, 2014; Gibbs, 2009). Technological challenges further complicate
sustainable innovation, as required technologies are often costly and inaccessible,
making integration into existing systems difficul t (Demirel & Kesidou, 2019). Supply
chain issues add to these difficulties, with ethical sourcing and eco -friendly raw materials
being expensive and hard to procure, limiting operational efficiency (Bocken et al.,
2014). Additionally, competition from esta blished businesses benefiting from economies
of scale and government support makes it harder for ecopreneurs to compete (Hall et
al., 2010). Lastly, many ecopreneurs lack essential business skills, such as marketing and
management expertise, which hampers their growth and sustainability (Volery, 2002).

5. Analysis and Discussion
Ecopreneurs in India, who integrate environmental sustainability into their business
models, encounter several challenges and problems.
Over the years, ecopreneurs in I ndia have faced evolving challenges. Between 2015 and
2017, limited consumer awareness and investor hesitation made it difficult for startups
like Phool.co and Good Dot to gain traction. From 2018 to 2019, high production costs
and unclear policies hindere d growth, as seen with Takachar and Vruksha Ecosystem.
The COVID -19 pandemic in 2020 -2021 disrupted supply chains and shifted consumer
priorities, affecting businesses like Triton Foodworks and Living Greens. By 2022 -2023,
scaling operations and competing with large corporations became major obstacles for
startups like Svante Group. In 2024 and beyond, while government policies are
improving, bureaucratic hurdles and pricing challenges persist, requiring green startups
to navigate complex regulations and ma intain affordability.
