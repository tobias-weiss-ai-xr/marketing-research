 The increasing competition among Islamic educational institutions in Indonesia has
encouraged schools to strengthen parental loyalty as a strategic factor for institutional
sustainability. Existing studies on Islamic education predominantly focus on parental
decision-making during school selection, while limited attention has been given to
post-enrollment loyalty behavior. This study examines the influence of religiosity on
parental loyalty through the mediating roles of trust and satisfaction among parents
of students enrolled in Integrated Islamic Junior High Schools (SMPIT) in Pekanbaru,
Indonesia. A quantitative explanatory approach was employed using survey data
collected from 255 respondents through purposive sampling. Data were analyzed
using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS- SEM). The findings
reveal that religiosity significantly influences parents’ trust and satisfaction toward
Islamic educational institutions. Trust positively influences satisfaction and loyalty,
while satisfa ction becomes the strongest predictor of parental loyalty. Mediation
analysis further confirms that trust and satisfaction partially mediate the relationship
between religiosity and parental loyalty. This study contributes to the educational
marketing literature by integrating religiosity theory, relationship marketing theory,
and consumer behavior theory within Islamic educational contexts. Practically, the
findings provide strategic implications for Islamic schools in strengthening
institutional credibili ty, improving service quality, and maintaining sustainable
relationships with parents. The results demonstrate that parental loyalty toward
Islamic schools is influenced not only by academic quality but also by emotional
attachment and spiritual alignment.

1. INTRODUCTION
Education has become one of the most important investments for families in modern society. In addition to improving
academic competence and social mobility, education is also expected to shape moral character, emotional maturity,
and spiritual identity among younger generations. In Muslim-majority countries such as Indonesia, educational
institutions are increasingly required to integrate academic excellence with religious values and ethical development.
During the last decade, Integrated Islamic Schools (Sekolah Islam Terpadu) have experienced rapid growth in
Indonesia. These schools combine national curricula with Islamic teachings, Qur’anic education, and character-
building programs. Among these institutions, Integrated Islamic Junior High Schools (SMPIT) have become
increasingly attractive to Muslim middle-class families seeking educational environments capable of balancing
intellectual and spiritual development.
The increasing demand for Islamic education in Indonesia cannot be separated from broader socio-cultural
transformations occurring within contemporary Muslim society. Rapid urbanization, technological development, and

globalization have significantly influenced parental perceptions regarding children’s education and moral
development. Many parents increasingly perceive modern society as exposing adolescents to various social risks,
including declining moral values, excessive digital dependency, juvenile delinquency, and weakening family

As a response to these social concerns, Islamic educational institutions have emerged as alternative environments
expected to strengthen children’s religious identity, discipline, and ethical awareness. Integrated Islamic schools are
often perceived not only as academic institutions but also as socio-religious environments capable of balancing
intellectual achievement with spiritual and moral formation. Consequently, parents’ educational decisions are
increasingly influenced by emotional and ideological considerations rather than purely rational or economic factors.
The rapid growth of Islamic integrated schools has also transformed the educational sector into a highly competitive
service industry. Educational institutions are now required to compete through service quality, institutional branding,
parental engagement, and emotional relationship management. In this context, parental loyalty becomes strategically
important because loyal parents contribute not only to student retention but also to institutional reputation and long-
term sustainability through positive advocacy and word-of-mouth communication.
Within service industries, loyalty is generally influenced by customer satisfaction and trust. However, educational
services possess unique characteristics because they involve long-term emotional interaction, uncertainty regarding
outcomes, and high parental involvement. Therefore, parental loyalty toward educational institutions may involve
deeper psychological and ideological attachment compared to conventional consumer behavior.
The growth of Islamic schools has simultaneously intensified competition among educational institutions. Schools are
no longer competing solely through academic achievement but also through institutional reputation, religious
credibility, service quality, and parental engagement. Consequently, parental loyalty has emerged as a critical factor
influencing institutional sustainability and competitiveness.
A significant phenomenon in Islamic education is that many parents remain loyal to Islamic schools despite relatively
higher tuition fees compared to public schools. This condition suggests that parental loyalty is not determined solely
by economic considerations but also by emotional attachment and spiritual values. Parents often perceive Islamic
schools as safer environments capable of protecting children from moral degradation, social deviance, and the negative
effects of digital globalization.
Another phenomenon observed in urban Indonesian society is the increasing concern among parents regarding juvenile
delinquency, weakening social values, and excessive exposure to digital culture. As a result, many Muslim families
prioritize educational institutions emphasizing Islamic discipline, moral education, and character formation. These
developments indicate that religiosity has become an important factor shaping educational behavior among Muslim
families.
Previous studies have extensively examined factors influencing parental school selection decisions, including school
image, facilities, perceived price, and service quality.
Although previous studies have extensively investigated school selection decisions in Islamic education, limited
research has explored parental loyalty after enrollment decisions have been made. Existing studies predominantly
emphasize institutional image, service quality, and tuition considerations, while the role of religiosity in shaping long-
term parental loyalty remains underexplored. Furthermore, few studies integrate religiosity with relationship
marketing constructs such as trust and satisfaction within faith-based educational institutions. Therefore, this study
attempts to fill the existing gap by examining the structural relationship between religiosity, trust, satisfaction, and
parental loyalty in Islamic integrated schools.
This study therefore aims to examine the influence of religiosity on parental loyalty through trust and satisfaction
among parents of SMPIT students in Pekanbaru. By integrating consumer behavior theory, relationship marketing
theory, and religiosity theory, this study seeks to provide a broader understanding of parental behavior within Islamic
educational institutions.

2. LITERATURE REVIEW
Consumer Behavior Theory

Consumer behavior theory explains how individuals select, evaluate, and maintain relationships with products or
services. According to Kotler and Keller (2016), consumer behavior is influenced by psychological, social, cultural,
and emotional factors that shape individual decision-making processes.
Consumer behavior theory also explains that consumers are influenced by both rational and emotional considerations
when evaluating services. In educational services, emotional attachment frequently becomes more dominant because
parents perceive education as a long-term investment associated with children’s future success and moral
development. Consequently, educational decision-making often involves psychological, cultural, and ideological
dimensions beyond economic considerations.
Within Islamic educational contexts, parents may evaluate schools not only based on academic achievement but also
on institutional values, religious atmosphere, and moral credibility. This condition differentiates Islamic educational
services from conventional educational institutions because spiritual alignment becomes an important component of
perceived value.
Within educational services, parents act as consumers evaluating schools based on perceived value, institutional
credibility, and compatibility with family expectations. Educational services differ from ordinary commercial products
because they involve long-term commitment, emotional interaction, and high uncertainty regarding future outcomes.
Relationship Marketing Theory
Relationship marketing theory emphasizes the importance of establishing sustainable relationships between
organizations and customers. Morgan and Hunt explain that trust and commitment are key determinants of long-term
relational success.
Relationship marketing theory emphasizes that sustainable organizational relationships are developed through trust,
communication quality, commitment, and mutual value creation. In educational institutions, long-term relationships
between schools and parents are particularly important because parents continuously evaluate institutional
performance during students’ educational journeys.
Effective relationship marketing practices in schools may include transparent communication, parental engagement
programs, responsiveness to complaints, and emotional interaction between teachers and families. These relational
strategies strengthen institutional credibility and positively influence parental satisfaction and loyalty.
In educational institutions, relationship marketing becomes highly relevant because schools continuously interact with
parents during students’ educational journeys. Parents who trust schools and experience satisfaction are more likely
to develop loyalty and recommend schools to others.
Religiosity
Religiosity refers to the degree to which individuals internalize religious beliefs, practices, and values into daily life.
According to Charles Glock and Rodney Stark, religiosity consists of belief, worship practices, religious knowledge,
religious experience, and moral consequences.
In Islamic societies, religiosity strongly influences educational preferences because parents seek institutions aligned
with Islamic values and moral expectations.
Religiosity has increasingly become an important factor influencing educational preferences among Muslim middle-
class families in Indonesia. The rapid development of globalization, digital media exposure, and shifting social values
have increased parental concern regarding children’s moral development. As a result, many Muslim parents prefer
educational institutions capable of integrating academic excellence with Islamic values and character education.
Highly religious parents tend to perceive Islamic schools as institutions capable of protecting children from negative
social influences while simultaneously strengthening spiritual identity. Therefore, religiosity may function not only as
a personal belief system but also as a behavioral determinant influencing parental trust, satisfaction, and loyalty toward
educational institutions.
Trust
Trust represents confidence in the competence, reliability, and integrity of an institution. Mayer et al. define trust as
the willingness to become vulnerable based on positive expectations regarding another party’s actions.

In educational contexts, parental trust develops through transparency, communication quality, and schools’
consistency in delivering educational promises.
Satisfaction
Satisfaction reflects emotional responses resulting from comparisons between expectations and actual experiences.
Oliver explains that satisfaction occurs when perceived performance meets or exceeds expectations.
Within Islamic schools, parental satisfaction may emerge from academic quality, teacher professionalism,
communication effectiveness, and successful integration of religious values.
Loyalty
Loyalty refers to long-term commitment and supportive behavior toward institutions. Griffin explains that loyal
consumers not only continue using services but also provide positive advocacy and recommendations.
In educational services, loyal parents contribute significantly to institutional reputation and sustainability.
The relationship between religiosity and loyalty has received growing scholarly attention in service marketing
literature, particularly within Islamic service industries. Previous studies suggest that religiosity influences consumers’
attitudes, preferences, trust formation, and long-term behavioral commitment. Religious individuals tend to evaluate
services not only based on functional quality but also on moral compatibility and spiritual alignment with personal
values.
Within educational contexts, religiosity may shape parental perceptions regarding institutional credibility and
emotional security. Parents with strong religious orientation frequently seek educational institutions capable of
preserving children’s Islamic identity while simultaneously providing academic excellence. Therefore, religiosity may
indirectly influence loyalty through trust and satisfaction mechanisms.
Trust plays a particularly important role in educational services because parents entrust schools with children’s
intellectual, emotional, and moral development. Unlike ordinary commercial transactions, educational relationships
involve long-term psychological investment and uncertainty regarding future outcomes. Consequently, trust becomes
an essential foundation for sustainable relationships between schools and families.
Moreover, parental satisfaction within Islamic educational institutions extends beyond tangible service quality.
Satisfaction may emerge from positive emotional experiences, alignment between institutional values and family
beliefs, teacher professionalism, effective communication, and perceptions regarding children’s moral improvement.
Therefore, satisfaction within Islamic schools possesses both functional and emotional dimensions.
Previous studies in educational marketing have primarily emphasized institutional image, facilities, and service quality
as determinants of parental behavior. However, limited studies examine how religiosity influences parental loyalty
through relational constructs such as trust and satisfaction. This study therefore attempts to extend previous literature
by integrating religiosity theory, relationship marketing theory, and consumer behavior theory within the context of
Islamic integrated schools.
Hypotheses Development
Based on consumer behavior theory, relationship marketing theory, and religiosity theory, the following hypotheses
are proposed:
H1: Religiosity positively influences parental trust toward Islamic schools.
H2: Religiosity positively influences parental satisfaction.
H3: Trust positively influences parental satisfaction.
H4: Trust positively influences parental loyalty.
H5: Satisfaction positively influences parental loyalty.
H6: Trust mediates the relationship between religiosity and parental loyalty.
H7: Satisfaction mediates the relationship between religiosity and parental loyalty.

3. METHODOLOGY
Research Design
This study employed a quantitative explanatory design using a cross-sectional survey approach.
A quantitative approach was selected because this study aimed to examine causal relationships among latent variables
and statistically test the proposed hypotheses. Quantitative methods are considered appropriate for explaining
behavioral relationships within educational service contexts because they enable researchers to measure perceptions,
attitudes, and behavioral intentions systematically.
The cross-sectional design was employed because data were collected at a single point in time from parents whose
children were enrolled in Islamic integrated junior high schools. This approach is commonly used in consumer
behavior and educational marketing research because it allows researchers to capture respondents’ perceptions
efficiently.
Population and Sample
The population consisted of parents whose children were enrolled in SMPIT institutions in Pekanbaru.
Purposive sampling was used with the following criteria:
1. Parents actively involved in school activities
2. Parents whose children had studied for at least one academic year
3. Parents willing to complete questionnaires
A total of 255 valid questionnaires were analyzed.

Data Analysis Technique
Data were analyzed using SmartPLS 4.0 through:
1. Outer model evaluation
2. Reliability and validity testing
3. Structural model evaluation
4. Hypothesis testing
5. Mediation analysis
PLS-SEM was selected because it is suitable for predictive and exploratory research involving complex relationships
among latent variables. In addition, the method can effectively accommodate relatively moderate sample sizes and
non-normal data distributions. The method has been widely used in educational management, marketing, and
behavioral research.
The analysis process included outer model evaluation to assess indicator validity and reliability, followed by inner
model evaluation to examine structural relationships among constructs. Bootstrapping procedures were subsequently
performed to test hypothesis significance and mediation effects.
The questionnaire items were adapted from previous studies related to religiosity, trust, satisfaction, and loyalty within
educational and service marketing contexts. Prior to large-scale data collection, the instrument was evaluated through
preliminary testing to ensure clarity, readability, and contextual relevance for respondents.
Data collection was conducted directly through school networks and parental communication groups. Respondents
were informed regarding research objectives and confidentiality procedures before completing questionnaires.
Participation was voluntary, and respondents were allowed to withdraw at any stage of the research process.
PLS-SEM was selected because the method is suitable for exploratory and predictive research involving multiple
latent variables and mediation relationships. According to Hair et al. (2021), PLS-SEM is particularly appropriate for
behavioral research because it can effectively analyze complex structural relationships while accommodating
relatively moderate sample sizes.
The analysis process consisted of two stages. The first stage involved evaluating the outer model to assess convergent
validity, discriminant validity, and construct reliability. The second stage involved evaluating the structural model to
examine hypothesis significance, coefficient of determination, predictive relevance, and mediation effects through
bootstrapping procedures.

DISCUSSION
The findings demonstrate that religiosity significantly influences parental trust toward Islamic schools. Parents with
strong religious commitment tend to perceive Islamic educational institutions as morally credible and spiritually
aligned with family values. This result confirms the argument that religiosity functions as an important motivational
factor shaping educational preferences among Muslim families.
This finding is consistent with previous studies conducted by Abdullah and Ismail (2022), which demonstrated that
religiosity significantly influences parental preference toward Islamic educational institutions. Similarly, Amin (2020)
argued that religiosity shapes consumer perceptions and behavioral commitment within Islamic service environments.
However, the present study extends previous findings by emphasizing parental loyalty after enrollment decisions
rather than initial school selection behavior. This distinction is important because loyalty represents a long-term
relational outcome reflecting sustainable institutional attachment.
The phenomenon of increasing parental preference for Islamic schools in Indonesia reflects broader social
transformations within urban Muslim communities. Parents are increasingly concerned about moral degradation,
juvenile delinquency, and the negative influence of digital culture on adolescents. Consequently, Islamic schools are
perceived not only as academic institutions but also as protective environments preserving children’s moral identity.
The increasing preference toward Islamic schools may also reflect broader socio-cultural transformations within
Indonesian Muslim society. The rapid expansion of social media, digital entertainment, and global cultural exposure
has generated concerns regarding adolescents’ behavioral patterns and moral vulnerability. Consequently, many

Theoretically, this study contributes to the development of educational marketing literature by integrating consumer

The strong influence of satisfaction on loyalty further demonstrates that parental commitment toward schools is shaped

PRACTICAL IMPLICATIONS
The findings of this study provide several practical implications for Islamic educational institutions, particularly
integrated Islamic junior high schools operating in increasingly competitive educational environments. First, schools
should recognize that parental loyalty is not determined solely by academic performance but also by emotional trust
and spiritual alignment. Consequently, school management should strengthen institutional transparency,
communication quality, and consistency in implementing Islamic values within daily educational activities.
Second, educational institutions should improve parental engagement programs to establish stronger emotional
relationships between schools and families. Regular communication, parenting seminars, religious activities, and
collaborative educational programs may strengthen parental trust and increase institutional attachment. Schools
capable of maintaining active parental involvement are more likely to achieve long-term sustainability and positive
institutional reputation.
Third, Islamic schools should continuously improve teacher professionalism and service responsiveness because
parental satisfaction is strongly influenced by educational experiences and interpersonal interaction quality. Parents
tend to evaluate institutions positively when teachers demonstrate competence, empathy, and effective
communication.
Finally, Islamic educational institutions should understand that religiosity represents an important strategic factor
influencing consumer behavior within Muslim societies. Therefore, schools should maintain institutional credibility
by consistently integrating religious values, moral education, and academic excellence within educational services.

THEORETICAL IMPLICATIONS
This study contributes theoretically to the development of educational marketing and consumer behavior literature by
integrating religiosity theory, relationship marketing theory, and parental loyalty within the context of Islamic
educational institutions. Previous studies predominantly examined school selection decisions, institutional image, and
service quality, whereas this study emphasizes the relational and spiritual dimensions influencing long-term parental
loyalty.
The findings also strengthen relationship marketing theory by demonstrating that trust and satisfaction function as
important mediating mechanisms within educational services. Unlike ordinary commercial transactions, educational
relationships involve emotional attachment, long-term interaction, and ideological alignment between institutions and
families.
Moreover, this study extends religiosity literature by confirming that religious commitment influences not only
personal beliefs but also behavioral outcomes related to institutional trust and loyalty. Therefore, religiosity should be
understood as a multidimensional construct capable of shaping consumer perceptions, emotional attachment, and long-
term relational commitment within faith-based service industries.

5. CONCLUSION
This study concludes that religiosity significantly influences parental loyalty toward Islamic integrated junior high
schools through the mediating roles of trust and satisfaction. Parents with strong religious commitment tend to develop
greater trust and satisfaction toward schools aligned with Islamic values, ultimately strengthening long-term loyalty.
The findings indicate that parental loyalty in Islamic schools is influenced not only by academic quality but also by
emotional attachment, moral credibility, and spiritual alignment. Islamic educational institutions should therefore
strengthen communication quality, institutional transparency, service consistency, and religious credibility to maintain
sustainable parental relationships.
This study has several limitations. First, the research was conducted only within Islamic integrated junior high schools
in Pekanbaru, thereby limiting broader generalization. Second, the study employed a cross-sectional design, which
may not fully capture changes in parental perceptions over time. Future studies are therefore encouraged to utilize
longitudinal approaches and incorporate broader geographical areas and additional variables.

LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE RESEARCH
This study possesses several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, the research was conducted only within
integrated Islamic junior high schools in Pekanbaru, thereby limiting broader generalizability across different
educational and cultural contexts in Indonesia. Second, the study employed a cross-sectional design, which may not
fully capture changes in parental perceptions and loyalty behavior over time.

Third, the study focused primarily on religiosity, trust, satisfaction, and loyalty without incorporating other potentially
influential variables such as institutional image, digital communication quality, parental engagement intensity, and
perceived educational value. Future studies are therefore encouraged to examine more comprehensive models
involving additional psychological, social, and technological variables.
Furthermore, future research may utilize longitudinal approaches and comparative analysis between Islamic and non-
Islamic educational institutions to provide broader insights regarding parental loyalty behavior within contemporary
educational markets.
