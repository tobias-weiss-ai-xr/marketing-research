Abstract
Although electronic word of mouth (eWOM) is widely recognized as an important inﬂuence on consumer
behavior, less is known about how eWOM is transformed into continued purchasing decisions through
consumers’ holistic perceptions of a brand. Addressing this gap, this study examines Brand Gestalt as
the perceptual mechanism linking eWOM with repurchase intention in the halal cosmetics market. Brand
Gestalt conceptualizes brand perception as an integrated conﬁguration of Storyscape, Sensescape,
Servicescape, and Stakeholderscape. Data were collected through a questionnaire-based survey of 371
consumers with prior experience purchasing halal cosmetic products and analyzed using partial least
squares structural equation modeling. The ﬁndings show that eWOM strongly contributes to the
formation of all four Brand Gestalt dimensions, conﬁrming its role as an important source of holistic
brand meaning. However, eWOM does not directly inﬂuence repurchase intention. Among the Brand
Gestalt dimensions, only Sensescape and Stakeholderscape signiﬁcantly predict repurchase intention,
whereas Storyscape and Servicescape do not exert signiﬁcant independent effects. The mediation
analysis further reveals that the inﬂuence of eWOM on repurchase intention operates through
Sensescape and Stakeholderscape, indicating an indirect-only mediation pattern rather than a direct
persuasion mechanism. These ﬁndings extend eWOM research by repositioning online consumer
communication as a mechanism of brand-meaning formation and advance Brand Gestalt theory by
demonstrating functional asymmetry among its dimensions: although all dimensions are strongly
shaped by eWOM, only sensory and stakeholder-related perceptions translate that inﬂuence into
repurchase intention. Managerially, the ﬁndings suggest that halal cosmetic brands should move beyond
generating positive online conversations and ensure that eWOM reinforces credible sensory experiences
and trustworthy stakeholder associations that can sustain repeat purchasing.
1. Introduction
Digital commerce and social media have transformed how consumers evaluate cosmetic brands. Online
reviews, ratings, recommendations, inﬂuencer content, and other forms of electronic word of mouth
(eWOM) increasingly shape consumer perceptions before and after purchase (Liu et al., 2024),
particularly when product quality, suitability, safety, authenticity, and brand values are diﬃcult to evaluate
prior to consumption (Wanigapura et al., 2025; Singh & Mittal, 2026). Yet a fundamental question
remains unresolved: why does favorable online communication not necessarily translate into repeat
purchasing? Positive eWOM can increase awareness, reduce uncertainty, and encourage favorable
evaluation, but continued patronage may depend on whether the fragmented cues encountered online
are integrated into a coherent and meaningful representation of the brand (Rantung et al., 2025;
Wanigapura et al., 2025).
This question is particularly relevant in Indonesia, where domestic cosmetic brands compete intensively
with international brands through e-commerce and social-media platforms (Consumer News and
Business Channel, 2024). In such digitally contested markets, generating favorable online
communication or securing an initial purchase is insuﬃcient; brands must convert these favorable
Page 2/34

evaluations into continued purchasing. The theoretical issue, therefore, extends beyond whether eWOM
affects behavioral intentions to how consumer-generated communication becomes incorporated into
brand meaning and, ultimately, whether particular forms of that meaning are suﬃciently consequential to
motivate repurchase.
Halal cosmetics provide a theoretically distinctive setting in which to examine this process. Consumer
evaluations of halal cosmetic brands encompass functional and aesthetic performance as well as
sensory experience, symbolic meaning, ethical considerations, value congruence, authenticity, and
relationships with relevant stakeholders (Anwar, 2025). Importantly, several attributes associated with
halal consumption have the characteristics of credence qualities: consumers cannot necessarily verify
religious compliance, ethical appropriateness, or authenticity solely through direct product use.
Consequently, their evaluations may depend partly on the legitimacy and assurance provided by actors
surrounding the brand. Certiﬁcation authorities, religiously credible communities, retailers, inﬂuencers,
peer consumers, and other stakeholders can serve as signals of trustworthiness, legitimacy, and
conformity with expected halal values. In this setting, therefore, stakeholders are not simply peripheral
actors communicating about a brand; they can become part of the meaning through which the brand
itself is judged.
This feature makes Brand Gestalt particularly useful for explaining halal cosmetic consumption. Brand
Gestalt conceptualizes a brand as a holistic conﬁguration of interconnected meanings rather than a
collection of independently evaluated attributes (Mandagi et al., 2021, 2026). Its 4S framework
comprises storyscape, sensescape, servicescape, and stakeholderscape, capturing respectively the
narratives surrounding the brand, consumers' sensory and experiential impressions, the environments
through which the brand is encountered, and the actors involved in constructing and validating brand
meaning (Mandagi & Centeno, 2025). Collectively, these dimensions form consumers' overall
representation of what a brand is and what it signiﬁes (Mandagi et al., 2026).
The dimensional structure of Brand Gestalt is especially pertinent to halal cosmetics. Storyscape
encompasses brand narratives, heritage, positioning, values, and communicated meanings; sensescape
reﬂects product appearance, fragrance, texture, packaging, and consumption experience; and
servicescape captures physical and digital environments such as retail outlets, e-commerce platforms,
and service interactions (Mandagi & Centeno, 2025; Walean et al., 2024). Stakeholderscape, however,
assumes additional theoretical signiﬁcance in halal consumption because the people and institutions
surrounding a brand may provide the social and institutional assurance through which otherwise diﬃcult-
to-verify claims acquire credibility. Consumers, employees, retailers, inﬂuencers, communities, and other
stakeholders therefore contribute not only social associations but also legitimacy to the holistic brand
representation (Mandagi & Centeno, 2025; Walean et al., 2024). This suggests that the four dimensions
may jointly constitute Brand Gestalt while performing different functions in converting brand meaning
into subsequent behavior.

eWOM represents a potentially important antecedent of this holistic meaning formation. Digital
consumer communication simultaneously carries informational, experiential, sensory, symbolic, and
social cues. Reviews, recommendations, photographs, testimonials, social-media discussions, and
consumer-generated videos communicate product performance as well as consumption experiences,
brand meanings, service encounters, and stakeholder behavior (Liu et al., 2024). Rather than responding
to each cue independently, consumers may organize these signals into broader conﬁgurations of brand
meaning (Mandagi et al., 2021). Favorable eWOM may therefore do more than persuade consumers
directly. It may construct or reinforce storyscape, sensescape, servicescape, and stakeholderscape,
thereby shaping the holistic representation through which subsequent purchasing decisions are made.
Prior ﬁndings provide the two sides of this theoretical process but have not yet connected them
adequately. On the antecedent side, Mandagi and Aseng (2021) found that digitally mediated social
media marketing signiﬁcantly inﬂuenced all four Brand Gestalt dimensions and that selected dimensions
transmitted its effects to loyalty. Although social media marketing is marketer-generated rather than
consumer-generated, their ﬁndings demonstrate that digital communication can participate in Brand
Gestalt formation. Wang et al. (2024) similarly showed that consumers integrate experiential,
environmental, and provider-related information when forming Brand Gestalt cognition and subsequent
choices. These ﬁndings suggest that digital communication can construct holistic brand meaning rather
than functioning solely as a direct persuasive stimulus.
On the consequence side, Brand Gestalt has demonstrated behavioral relevance across several contexts.
Storyscape and sensescape predict brand attitude and revisit intention (Walean et al., 2023), while
sensescape, servicescape, and stakeholderscape contribute to satisfaction and sensescape directly
predicts loyalty (Walean et al., 2024). Destination Brand Gestalt has also been associated with multiple
dimensions of brand equity (Pongoh & Mandagi, 2025), while evidence from city branding links Brand
Gestalt with attitude, satisfaction, and loyalty (Mandagi et al., 2025). More recent research conﬁrms that
the four 4S dimensions collectively contribute to overall Destination Brand Gestalt, which subsequently
enhances satisfaction and loyalty (Mandagi et al., 2026). Most directly, Rantung et al. (2025)
demonstrated that Brand Gestalt positively inﬂuences repurchase intention in the context of an
Indonesian traditional sustainable product.
Taken together, however, these streams leave two mechanisms unresolved. First, it remains unclear
whether eWOM affects repeat purchasing primarily through direct persuasion or through brand-meaning
formation. Conventional eWOM models frequently position consumer-generated communication as a
stimulus directly inﬂuencing attitudes and behavioral intentions, whereas a gestalt perspective implies a
more elaborate mechanism in which eWOM ﬁrst shapes the conﬁguration of meanings consumers
attach to the brand. Establishing this distinction is important because favorable online communication
may be behaviorally ineffective when it fails to become incorporated into a coherent brand
representation.

Second, existing Brand Gestalt research provides insuﬃcient explanation of whether its four dimensions
are equally consequential for behavioral conversion. The dimensions may be jointly shaped by digital
communication without exerting equivalent effects on repurchase intention. This possibility is
particularly salient in halal cosmetics. Storyscape and servicescape may organize narrative and
contextual meaning, whereas sensescape provides direct experiential conﬁrmation of product
performance and stakeholderscape provides social and institutional assurance for diﬃcult-to-verify halal
and authenticity claims. Thus, the dimensions may share a common antecedent while differing in their
capacity to convert favorable communication into continued purchasing. Examining this potential
functional asymmetry moves Brand Gestalt research beyond asking whether holistic brand perception
matters toward identifying which components of that gestalt actually carry behavioral consequences.
Addressing these issues, the present study examines eWOM, the four dimensions of Brand Gestalt, and
repurchase intention in the context of halal cosmetics. The proposed framework treats consumer-
generated eWOM as a source of heterogeneous cues from which consumers construct storyscape,
sensescape, servicescape, and stakeholderscape. It then examines whether these dimensions have
equivalent or differentiated consequences for repurchase intention and whether they constitute distinct
pathways through which eWOM is converted into repeat-purchase intentions. This dimensional approach
permits a more precise assessment of both Brand Gestalt formation and its behavioral conversion.
Accordingly, the study addresses four research questions:
1. To what extent does eWOM shape each Brand Gestalt dimension, namely storyscape, sensescape,
servicescape, and stakeholderscape, in the context of halal cosmetics?
2. Which Brand Gestalt dimensions signiﬁcantly predict consumers' repurchase intention toward halal
cosmetic brands?
3. Does eWOM directly predict repurchase intention toward halal cosmetic brands?
4. Which Brand Gestalt dimensions mediate the relationship between eWOM and repurchase
intention?
This study makes three principal contributions. First, it reconceptualizes consumer-generated eWOM not
merely as a persuasive inﬂuence on behavioral intentions but as an antecedent of holistic brand
meaning. By linking eWOM to the four Brand Gestalt dimensions, the study extends the digital
communication perspective established by Mandagi and Aseng (2021) from marketer-generated social
media communication to consumer-generated communication and clariﬁes how digitally circulated cues
become incorporated into consumers' representations of a brand. Second, it advances Brand Gestalt
theory by demonstrating that its dimensions can be jointly formed yet differentially consequential. This
distinction introduces functional asymmetry into the 4S framework: the presence of a common
antecedent does not imply that storyscape, sensescape, servicescape, and stakeholderscape contribute
equally to behavioral conversion. Third, in the halal-cosmetics context, the study identiﬁes sensory and
stakeholder-related meanings as the principal mechanisms through which favorable eWOM is converted
into repurchase intention. This ﬁnding is theoretically consequential because it differentiates experiential

conﬁrmation from legitimacy-based assurance, showing that what consumers experience through the
product and whom they associate with, trust, or regard as legitimizing the brand are particularly
important in transforming positive online discourse into continued purchasing.
From a managerial perspective, these arguments challenge the assumption that generating favorable
eWOM is suﬃcient to secure repeat customers. Digital conversations may strengthen multiple
dimensions of brand meaning, yet only particular meanings may ultimately drive behavioral continuity.
For halal cosmetic brands, this implies that online communication should not merely produce positive
sentiment but reinforce credible sensory experiences and a trustworthy stakeholder ecosystem capable
of providing assurance, legitimacy, and conﬁdence in the.
2. Literature Review
2.1 Brand Gestalt
Brand Gestalt provides a holistic perspective on how consumers construct brand meaning. Drawing on
Gestalt theory, it proposes that consumers integrate multiple brand-related cues into a coherent mental
representation in which the whole acquires meaning beyond the sum of its individual elements. Mandagi
et al. (2021) established its empirical foundation through four interrelated components, subsequently
reﬁned into the 4S framework: storyscape, sensescape, servicescape, and stakeholderscape (Mandagi &
Centeno, 2025). Conceptually, Brand Gestalt represents the overarching holistic perception of the brand,
whereas the four 4S dimensions represent distinct but interconnected domains through which that
holistic meaning is formed. Consistent with the present structural model, these dimensions are therefore
modeled separately to determine whether they have differentiated antecedents and behavioral
consequences rather than being treated as a single undifferentiated construct.
Empirical evidence supports both the holistic relevance and dimensional distinctiveness of Brand
Gestalt. Mandagi and Aseng (2021) found that social media marketing inﬂuenced Brand Gestalt
dimensions and that selected dimensions transmitted its effects to loyalty. Walean et al. (2023) showed
that story and sensescape predicted brand attitude and revisit intention, while Walean et al. (2024) found
that sensescape, servicescape, and stakeholderscape contributed to satisfaction, with sensescape also
predicting loyalty. Other studies have linked Brand Gestalt to brand association, awareness, image,
perceived quality, and loyalty (Pongoh & Mandagi, 2025), as well as attitude, satisfaction, and loyalty
(Mandagi et al., 2025). Mandagi et al. (2026) further demonstrated that all four dimensions contribute to
overall Brand Gestalt, although their contributions differ in magnitude, while Rantung et al. (2025)
established a positive relationship between Brand Gestalt and repurchase intention. Collectively, these
ﬁndings suggest that the 4S dimensions jointly constitute holistic brand meaning but may not be equally
consequential for subsequent consumer behavior.
This dimensional distinction is particularly meaningful in halal cosmetics. Storyscape captures
narratives through which brands communicate their identity, including halal values, ethical commitments,
Page 6/34

authenticity, heritage, and value congruence. Sensescape represents consumers' direct sensory and
experiential encounters with the product, including texture, fragrance, appearance, packaging, and overall
usage experience. Servicescape encompasses the physical and digital environments in which
consumers engage with the brand, including retail stores, e-commerce platforms, websites, and
customer-service interfaces. Stakeholderscape reﬂects the network of actors through which brand
meaning, credibility, and legitimacy are constructed (Mandagi & Centeno, 2025; Walean et al., 2024). In
halal consumption, this dimension is theoretically distinctive because consumers may rely on halal
certiﬁcation authorities, religiously credible or trusted communities, knowledgeable retailers, credible
inﬂuencers, employees, peer consumers, and online communities to provide assurance regarding claims
that are not always directly veriﬁable through product experience. Stakeholderscape therefore captures
not merely who is associated with the brand, but also the social and institutional sources through which
halal credibility and trust can be reinforced.
Digital communication can shape each of these dimensions. Online reviews, ratings, testimonials, social-
media conversations, and user-generated content communicate narratives about brand values, sensory
experiences, service encounters, and the credibility of relevant stakeholders. Mandagi and Aseng (2021)
demonstrate that digitally mediated communication can shape Brand Gestalt dimensions, while Wang et
al. (2024) show that consumers integrate multiple experiential and stakeholder-related cues from online
comments when forming holistic brand evaluations and subsequent decisions. These ﬁndings suggest
that eWOM may function not only as a persuasive inﬂuence but also as an informational input through
which the four dimensions of Brand Gestalt are constructed.
Despite these developments, the process connecting consumer-generated digital communication, Brand
Gestalt formation, and repeat purchasing remains insuﬃciently understood. Prior research shows
separately that digital communication can shape Brand Gestalt dimensions (Mandagi & Aseng, 2021)
and that Brand Gestalt can inﬂuence repurchase intention (Rantung et al., 2025), but limited attention
has been given to whether eWOM shapes each 4S dimension and whether those dimensions contribute
differently to behavioral conversion. This distinction is important in halal cosmetics because narrative,
sensory, environmental, and stakeholder-related meanings may all be formed through eWOM while
exerting unequal effects on repurchase intention. Accordingly, the present study treats Brand Gestalt as
the overarching theoretical framework while empirically examining storyscape, sensescape,
servicescape, and stakeholderscape as separate mediating dimensions linking eWOM with repurchase
intention.
2.2 Electronic Word of Mouth
eWOM refers to consumer-generated opinions, evaluations, recommendations, and experiences
communicated through online reviews and other digital platforms. Its inﬂuence may depend on volume,
valence, argument quality, and source credibility (Verma et al., 2023). In this study, however, eWOM
speciﬁcally reﬂects consumers' tendency to consult, read, and use online reviews when evaluating
brands and purchase alternatives, rather than the positive or negative valence of those reviews. Such
Page 7/34

reviews contain heterogeneous sensory, cognitive, affective, and social information that consumers
integrate when forming evaluations (Wang et al., 2023; Ahmed & Amritesh, 2023).
This informational role is particularly relevant to cosmetics, where consumers often consult online
reviews to assess product effectiveness, appearance, texture, ingredients, safety, authenticity, packaging,
and user experiences before purchase. Prior studies conﬁrm the relevance of eWOM to cosmetic
purchase decisions (Abdul-Talib & Vadivalu, 2023) and show that its effects can operate both directly
and through health concern, environmental concern, and consumer attitude (Nguyen et al., 2024).
More broadly, eWOM often inﬂuences behavior through intervening brand evaluations rather than direct
persuasion alone. Prior research has identiﬁed brand identity (Shams et al., 2024), brand attitude (Uyanik,
2025), brand image (Kaushal et al., 2023), perceived quality and brand trust (Hairani et al., 2024), and
perceived value and brand/store image (Liao et al., 2023) as relevant mechanisms. Online brand-
community trust and positive eWOM intention have likewise been associated with repurchase behavior
(Phan Tan, 2024). These studies demonstrate the importance of mediation but generally isolate single
evaluative mechanisms.
Brand Gestalt offers a broader explanation by capturing an integrated conﬁguration of narrative, sensory,
service, and stakeholder meanings. In halal cosmetics, consulting online reviews can simultaneously
expose consumers to information about product experience, halal and ethical values, service
encounters, authenticity, retailers, inﬂuencers, communities, and other stakeholders. Consistent with the
multidimensional information-processing perspective of Wang et al. (2023) and Ahmed and Amritesh
(2023), eWOM may therefore function as an informational input from which consumers construct
storyscape, sensescape, servicescape, and stakeholderscape. Accordingly, this study examines whether
consumers' use of online reviews shapes these four Brand Gestalt dimensions and whether they
subsequently transmit the effect of eWOM to repurchase intention.
2.3 Repurchase Intention
Repurchase intention refers to a consumer’s willingness to purchase the same product or brand again
following prior consumption experience. Unlike initial purchase intention, it reﬂects an accumulated
post-consumption judgment based on prior experience, perceived value, trust, and overall brand
evaluation (Shahid et al., 2023; Hussain et al., 2024). In halal consumption, prior purchasing experience
has been shown to strengthen subsequent repurchase intention, while knowledge and religious
considerations can also contribute to continued purchasing decisions (Putri et al., 2018; Shahid et al.,
2023).
Repurchase intention is strategically important because an initial purchase does not necessarily develop
into a lasting consumer–brand relationship. It represents an important transition from favorable
evaluation to continued patronage and, potentially, loyalty. Hussain et al. (2024) found that favorable
attitudes toward halal cosmetics strengthen repurchase intention, which subsequently contributes to
brand loyalty. More broadly, holistic brand evaluations have been associated with loyalty-related
Page 8/34

outcomes and continued behavioral commitment (Mandagi et al., 2025; Mandagi et al., 2026; Pongoh &
Mandagi, 2025), while Rantung et al. (2025) directly demonstrated that Brand Gestalt positively
inﬂuences repurchase intention.
Research on halal consumption further indicates that repurchase decisions emerge from a combination
of functional, religious, experiential, and relational considerations. Religious knowledge, commitment,
and halal certiﬁcation can inﬂuence purchasing and subsequent repurchase behavior (Shahid et al.,
2023), while product quality, religious beliefs, and trust contribute to attitudes that foster repurchase
intention (Hussain et al., 2024). Putri et al. (2018) similarly identify knowledge and religiosity as relevant
determinants. Thus, continued purchasing in halal markets is shaped not by religious compatibility alone
but by the interaction of product performance, credibility, trust, prior experience, and value-based
evaluation.
Digital information increasingly contributes to this evaluative process. eWOM can shape purchase-
related intentions through brand image, attitude, perceived quality, trust, source credibility, and review
usefulness rather than through direct persuasion alone (Kaushal et al., 2023; Filieri et al., 2023; Hairani et
al., 2024; Upadhyay & Tripathi, 2023; Uyanik, 2025). In cosmetics, Nguyen et al. (2024) demonstrate that
eWOM inﬂuences behavioral intention through underlying consumer evaluations, while Abdul-Talib and
Vadivalu (2023) emphasize the role of eWOM and social-network communication in cosmetic purchase
decisions. For experienced consumers, such information can be compared with prior product
experience, either reinforcing or challenging existing brand evaluations. Moreover, trust in digitally
transmitted information varies according to its social embeddedness, sincerity, and perceived credibility
(Beck et al., 2023; Filieri et al., 2023).
Despite these developments, much of the halal cosmetics literature explains repurchase intention
through discrete antecedents such as religiosity, halal certiﬁcation, product quality, trust, attitude, prior
purchase behavior, and digital marketing activities (Putri et al., 2018; Shahid et al., 2023; Hussain et al.,
2024). Such approaches may underrepresent how consumers integrate multiple simultaneous cues
when reassessing a brand. Brand Gestalt addresses this limitation by conceptualizing brand evaluation
as a coherent conﬁguration of interconnected meanings rather than as isolated judgments (Mandagi et
al., 2021). Its four dimensions, Storyscape, Sensescape, Servicescape, and Stakeholderscape, capture
narrative, sensory, service-environment, and stakeholder-related meanings respectively (Mandagi &
Centeno, 2025). These dimensions have been associated with attitude, satisfaction, loyalty, revisit
intention, and related relational outcomes (Mandagi & Aseng, 2021; Walean et al., 2023; Walean et al.,
2024; Pongoh & Mandagi, 2025; Mandagi et al., 2026), with Rantung et al. (2025) providing direct
evidence of a relationship with repurchase intention.
From this perspective, repurchase intention represents an accumulated post-consumption decision
arising from how consumers integrate previous experience, digital information, product performance,
and brand-related meanings. Because repeat purchasing occurs after direct brand experience, not merely
before trial, some gestalt dimensions may be more behaviorally consequential than others. In halal
Page 9/34

cosmetics, Sensescape can provide experiential conﬁrmation through texture, fragrance, packaging, and
product performance, while Stakeholderscape can provide credibility and assurance through trusted
actors, communities, retailers, and other legitimizing stakeholders. These experiential and credibility-
related cues may therefore exert stronger effects on repurchase intention than more abstract narrative
meanings represented by Storyscape, providing a basis for examining whether the four Brand Gestalt
dimensions contribute asymmetrically to continued purchasing.
2.4 Hypothesis Development
2.4.1 eWOM and Brand Gestalt
eWOM exposes consumers to heterogeneous informational, experiential, sensory, and social cues about
brands. Online reviews and other user-generated content communicate product experiences, emotions,
evaluations, service encounters, and stakeholder-related perceptions. Wang et al. (2023) show that
eWOM contains sensory, cognitive, affective, and social information, while Ahmed and Amritesh (2023)
argue that consumers integrate these cues rather than process them independently.
This reasoning is consistent with Brand Gestalt, which conceptualizes brand meaning as a coherent
conﬁguration of interconnected elements (Mandagi et al., 2021). Within the 4S framework, these
meanings are represented empirically through Storyscape, Sensescape, Servicescape, and
Stakeholderscape (Mandagi & Centeno, 2025). By consulting and using online reviews, consumers can
acquire information about brand narratives and values, product texture and sensory experience, service
and digital environments, and the credibility or behavior of actors associated with the brand. eWOM may
therefore contribute to the formation of each Brand Gestalt dimension.
Empirical evidence supports this mechanism. Mandagi and Aseng (2021) found that digitally mediated
communication signiﬁcantly inﬂuenced Brand Gestalt dimensions, while Wang et al. (2024) showed that
consumers integrate experiential, environmental, and provider-related cues from online comments when
forming holistic brand cognition. More broadly, eWOM inﬂuences brand identity, attitude, image, trust,
and perceived quality (Kaushal et al., 2023; Hairani et al., 2024; Shams et al., 2024; Uyanik, 2025). In halal
cosmetics, these online cues may concern halal and ethical values, sensory product experience, service
encounters, authenticity, and the credibility of consumers, inﬂuencers, retailers, communities, and other
stakeholders. Accordingly, greater consultation and use of eWOM are expected to strengthen each
dimension of Brand Gestalt.
H1a–d
eWOM has a positive and signiﬁcant effect on the four dimensions of Brand Gestalt (i.e., storyscape,
sensescape, servicescape, and stakeholderscape).
2.4.2 Brand Gestalt and Repurchase Intention

Brand Gestalt represents consumers’ holistic understanding of a brand, while Storyscape, Sensescape,
Servicescape, and Stakeholderscape capture distinct dimensions of that meaning (Mandagi et al., 2021;
Mandagi & Centeno, 2025). Prior research shows that these dimensions inﬂuence revisit intention,
attitude, satisfaction, brand equity, and loyalty (Walean et al., 2023; Mandagi et al., 2025; Pongoh &
Mandagi, 2025; Mandagi et al., 2026), while Rantung et al. (2025) directly link Brand Gestalt to
repurchase intention.
Each dimension may contribute differently to continued purchasing. Storyscape can reinforce halal
values, ethical narratives, authenticity, and value congruence; Sensescape provides direct experiential
conﬁrmation through texture, fragrance, appearance, packaging, and product use; Servicescape supports
convenient and reliable interactions across physical and digital environments; and Stakeholderscape
provides credibility and social assurance through consumers, inﬂuencers, retailers, trusted communities,
and other brand-related actors. These mechanisms are particularly relevant to halal cosmetics, where
repurchase decisions reﬂect accumulated evaluations of product experience, trust, quality, and brand
credibility (Shahid et al., 2023; Hussain et al., 2024). Accordingly, the four dimensions are expected to
contribute positively to repurchase intention, although their effects may differ in magnitude.
H2a-d
The four Brand Gestalt dimensions (i.e., storyscape, sensescape, servicescape, and stakeholderscape)
have positive and signiﬁcant effects on repurchase intention.
2.4.3 eWOM and Repurchase Intention
eWOM can inﬂuence future purchasing decisions by reducing uncertainty, providing social information,
and reinforcing or challenging consumers’ prior brand evaluations. Online reviews and recommendations
may therefore affect whether consumers consider continued purchasing appropriate, although the
strength and direction of this effect can depend on how the information is interpreted. Prior studies
generally support the relevance of eWOM to purchase-related intentions across digital markets (Abdul-
Talib & Vadivalu, 2023; Filieri et al., 2023; Nguyen et al., 2024). In cosmetics, Nguyen et al. (2024) found
that eWOM inﬂuenced purchase intention both directly and indirectly through consumer attitudes and
related evaluations, while Abdul-Talib and Vadivalu (2023) similarly identiﬁed eWOM and social
networking sites as important sources of purchase-related information.
Evidence regarding post-purchase behavior is more nuanced. Liao et al. (2023) showed that eWOM
operates within relationships involving brand evaluations, perceived value, and repurchase intention,
while Phan Tan (2024) linked eWOM-related processes with repurchase intention through online brand-
community relationships. Jalil et al. (2021) likewise positioned eWOM as relevant to repurchase
intention in the halal cosmetics social-media environment. However, direct effects are not always
evident. Hairani et al. (2024), for example, showed that eWOM may inﬂuence behavioral outcomes
through brand awareness, trust, perceived quality, and related evaluations rather than through a simple
direct pathway.

Thus, eWOM may contribute to repurchase intention either directly or through intervening perceptual and
relational mechanisms. Because consumers who consult and use online reviews gain additional
information for reassessing the desirability and credibility of previously purchased brands, greater
engagement with eWOM may nevertheless strengthen their willingness to repurchase. Accordingly, the
following hypothesis is proposed:
H3
eWOM has a positive and signiﬁcant effect on repurchase intention.
2.4.4 The Mediating Role of Brand Gestalt
eWOM may inﬂuence repurchase intention indirectly by shaping how consumers interpret and organize
brand-related information. Ahmed and Amritesh (2023) conceptualize eWOM as a conﬁguration of
multiple cues, while prior studies show that its behavioral effects can operate through intervening
evaluations such as brand image and consumer attitudes (Kaushal et al., 2023; Nguyen et al., 2024). This
suggests that eWOM may affect subsequent purchasing not only through direct persuasion but also by
altering consumers’ representations of the brand.
Brand Gestalt provides a multidimensional mechanism for this process. Storyscape, Sensescape,
Servicescape, and Stakeholderscape capture narrative, sensory, service-related, and stakeholder-related
meanings through which consumers organize brand information (Mandagi et al., 2021; Mandagi &
Centeno, 2025). Digitally mediated communication has been shown to shape these dimensions
(Mandagi & Aseng, 2021), while Brand Gestalt and its dimensions have been associated with repurchase
intention, revisit intention, satisfaction, and loyalty (Walean et al., 2023; Rantung et al., 2025; Mandagi et
al., 2025; Mandagi et al., 2026). Accordingly, consumers’ use of eWOM may inﬂuence repurchase
intention through each 4S dimension, although the strength of these indirect mechanisms may differ.
Thus:
H4
The four Brand Gestalt dimensions (i.e., storyscape, sensescape, servicescape, and stakeholderscape)
mediate the positive relationship between eWOM and repurchase intention.
The conceptual framework integrates eWOM, the four dimensions of Brand Gestalt, and repurchase
intention. Brand Gestalt serves as the overarching theoretical concept, while Storyscape, Sensescape,
Servicescape, and Stakeholderscape are modeled as distinct dimensions. The model examines both the
direct effect of eWOM on repurchase intention and its indirect effects through each Brand Gestalt
dimension, as summarized in Fig. 1.
3. Methodology
Page 12/34

3.1. Research Design
This study employed a cross-sectional quantitative survey design to examine the relationships among
eWOM, the four Brand Gestalt dimensions, and repurchase intention in halal cosmetics. Primary data
were collected using a structured questionnaire and analyzed using Partial Least Squares Structural
Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM). PLS-SEM was appropriate because the study examines a predictive
model involving multiple latent constructs, simultaneous structural relationships, and dimension-speciﬁc
mediation effects. It also enables joint assessment of the measurement and structural models.
3.2. Population and Sampling
The target population comprised consumers of halal cosmetic products aged 18–55 years residing in
major cities and surrounding areas in Indonesia. A non-probability purposive sampling technique was
employed because respondents were required to have prior experience purchasing or using halal
cosmetics and therefore be capable of evaluating eWOM, Brand Gestalt, and repurchase intention.
Eligibility criteria were: (1) aged 18–55 years, (2) residing in a major city or surrounding area in
Indonesia, and (3) having experience purchasing or using halal cosmetic products. The minimum age of
18 ensured that all participants were adults capable of providing informed consent independently.
The minimum sample size was determined through an a priori statistical power analysis based on the
most complex endogenous construct in the structural model. Repurchase intention was predicted by ﬁve
constructs: eWOM, Storyscape, Sensescape, Servicescape, and Stakeholderscape. Assuming a medium
effect size (f² = .15), a signiﬁcance level of α = .05, statistical power of .80, and ﬁve predictors, the
analysis indicated a minimum required sample of approximately 92 respondents. The ﬁnal sample
comprised 371 valid respondents, substantially exceeding this threshold and therefore providing
adequate statistical power for estimating the proposed PLS-SEM model and testing the direct and
indirect relationships (Hair et al., 2021).
3.3. Data and Instrumentation
Data were collected using a structured questionnaire comprising demographic information and 29
measurement items, all assessed on a seven-point Likert scale from 1 (“strongly disagree”) to 7
(“strongly agree”). eWOM was measured using ﬁve items adapted from Jalilvand and Samiei (2012),
capturing consumers’ tendency to consult, read, and use online reviews when evaluating halal
cosmetics. Brand Gestalt was measured using 19 items adapted from Mandagi et al. (2021), with
Storyscape, Sensescape, Servicescape, and Stakeholderscape modeled as four correlated ﬁrst-order
constructs rather than as a higher-order construct. Repurchase intention was measured using ﬁve items
primarily adapted from Chiu et al. (2009).
All items were contextually adapted to the halal-cosmetics setting while preserving their original
conceptual meaning. The questionnaire was translated from English into Bahasa Indonesia and
independently back-translated into English to assess semantic equivalence. Discrepancies were
discussed and resolved before ﬁnalization. The instrument was then pretested with 30 consumers who
Page 13/34

met the study’s eligibility criteria to assess clarity, comprehension, and contextual suitability, with minor
wording reﬁnements made where necessary.
3.4. Data Collection Procedure
Data were collected from March to May 2025 using an online questionnaire administered through
Google Forms and distributed through social media and other digital communication channels. Potential
respondents ﬁrst completed eligibility screening questions and provided informed consent before
accessing the survey. A total of 401 responses were received. After screening for eligibility,
completeness, and duplicate or unusable submissions, 30 responses were excluded, resulting in 371
usable responses for analysis. To minimize duplicate participation, respondents were instructed to
submit the questionnaire only once, and responses were checked for duplicate or highly similar entries.
No personally identifying information was collected, and all responses were treated anonymously and
conﬁdentially. The ﬁnal dataset was exported to Microsoft Excel for data cleaning before subsequent
statistical analysis. Ethical approval was granted by the Faculty of Economics and Business, Universitas
Klabat Research Committee (Approval No. 015/UK/FEB.RK/SK/2025). Participation was voluntary, and
respondents were informed about the study purpose, conﬁdentiality of their responses, and their right to
withdraw at any time. Electronic informed consent was obtained from all participants before they
proceeded to the questionnaire.
3.5. Data Analysis Procedure
Data were analyzed using SmartPLS 4 in three stages: data screening, measurement-model assessment,
and structural-model assessment. Prior to PLS-SEM analysis, the dataset was examined for missing
values, duplicate responses, response-pattern anomalies, and potential outliers. Following the screening
procedures described above, the ﬁnal dataset comprised 371 complete and usable responses, and no
imputation was required.
The reﬂective measurement models were evaluated using indicator loadings, internal consistency
reliability, convergent validity, and discriminant validity. Outer loadings of approximately .708 or higher
were considered satisfactory, while Cronbach’s alpha and composite reliability values between .70 and
.95 indicated adequate internal consistency. Convergent validity was established when average variance
extracted (AVE) exceeded .50, and discriminant validity was assessed using the Fornell–Larcker criterion
and heterotrait–monotrait ratio (HTMT), with HTMT values below .90 considered acceptable. Structural
collinearity was assessed using variance inﬂation factors (VIF), with values below 5.00 indicating no
critical collinearity concerns.
The structural model was assessed using path coeﬃcients, coeﬃcient of determination (R²), effect size
(f²), predictive relevance (Q²), and out-of-sample predictive performance. As general benchmarks, f²
values of .02, .15, and .35 were interpreted as small, medium, and large effects, respectively, while Q²
values above zero indicated predictive relevance. Statistical signiﬁcance of direct and dimension-speciﬁc
indirect effects was evaluated using 5,000 bootstrap subsamples, a two-tailed signiﬁcance level of 5%,
and 95% bias-corrected and accelerated conﬁdence intervals. Predictive performance was additionally
Page 14/34

examined using PLSpredict with 10 folds and 10 repetitions, comparing PLS-SEM prediction errors with
the linear-model benchmark.
Because all constructs were obtained from the same self-report survey, potential common method bias
(CMB) was addressed both procedurally and statistically. Procedurally, participation was anonymous,
respondents were informed that there were no right or wrong answers, and measurement items were
worded clearly to reduce evaluation apprehension and consistency motives. Statistically, full-collinearity
VIFs were examined, with values below 3.3 indicating that CMB was unlikely to represent a critical threat.
Discriminant-validity results were also considered alongside this assessment, particularly given the
conceptual proximity of the four Brand Gestalt dimensions.
This analytical procedure enabled the study to evaluate the measurement properties of eWOM,
Storyscape, Sensescape, Servicescape, Stakeholderscape, and repurchase intention before testing the
hypothesized direct and mediation relationships.

5. Discussions
5.1 Overview of the Principal Findings
Page 23/34

The ﬁndings reveal a coherent but differentiated process through which eWOM contributes to
repurchase intention. Favorable eWOM strongly shapes all dimensions of Brand Gestalt, indicating that
online consumer communication plays an important role in constructing how consumers understand a
brand's stories, sensory qualities, service environment, and stakeholder network. However, this strong
perceptual inﬂuence does not automatically translate into repurchase intention. Instead, the behavioral
consequences of Brand Gestalt are selective, with Sensescape and Stakeholderscape emerging as the
dimensions that meaningfully drive consumers' willingness to repurchase, while Storyscape and
Servicescape do not exert independent effects when the dimensions are considered simultaneously.
This pattern extends the original conceptualization of Brand Gestalt as a synergistic conﬁguration of
interconnected brand cues rather than a collection of isolated attributes (Mandagi et al., 2021; Mandagi
& Centeno, 2025). It is also consistent with accumulating evidence that the individual dimensions of
Brand Gestalt do not contribute equally to downstream consumer outcomes. Walean et al. (2023), for
example, found that Storyscape and Sensescape inﬂuenced behavioral responses in a destination
context, with Sensescape exerting greater inﬂuence on revisit intention. Similarly, Walean et al. (2024)
showed that several Brand Gestalt dimensions contributed to satisfaction, but only Sensescape directly
strengthened loyalty. Mandagi et al. (2026) likewise identiﬁed Sensescape as particularly inﬂuential
within the broader Brand Gestalt conﬁguration. The present ﬁndings therefore reinforce an important
theoretical distinction between dimensions that contribute to the formation of the overall brand
representation and dimensions that are suﬃciently diagnostic to inﬂuence subsequent behavior.
The results also challenge a simple stimulus-response interpretation of eWOM. Although eWOM has
frequently been linked to purchase-related intentions in cosmetics and digital consumption contexts
(Abdul-Talib & Vadivalu, 2023; Nguyen et al., 2024), other evidence suggests that its behavioral effects
depend on intermediate evaluations. Kaushal et al. (2023) position brand image as an intervening
mechanism between eWOM and purchase intention, while Hairani et al. (2024) found that eWOM did not
directly inﬂuence purchase intention despite its inﬂuence on perceived quality and brand trust. Filieri et
al. (2023) similarly demonstrate that inﬂuencer-generated eWOM affects purchase decisions through
source trustworthiness and brand attitude. Viewed together, these studies support the central ﬁnding of
the present research: eWOM appears to be more powerful in shaping the psychological representation of
a brand than in independently triggering repurchase behavior.
5.2 eWOM as a Formation Mechanism of Brand Gestalt
The strong inﬂuence of eWOM across all Brand Gestalt dimensions suggests that consumer-generated
digital communication functions as a major mechanism of contemporary brand formation. Consumers
encounter brands not only through ﬁrm-controlled advertising and direct product experience but also
through reviews, recommendations, social-media discussions, inﬂuencer content, online communities,
ratings, and shared consumption narratives. These sources collectively provide informational,
experiential, symbolic, and social cues from which consumers construct an integrated understanding of
what the brand represents.
Page 24/34

This interpretation is closely aligned with Gestalt theory, which assumes that individuals organize
multiple cues into coherent wholes rather than evaluating each stimulus independently. Mandagi et al.
(2021) conceptualize Brand Gestalt as the synergy of stories, experiences, environments, and
stakeholders, while Mandagi and Centeno (2025) further emphasize the co-created nature of this gestalt.
The present ﬁndings extend this argument by positioning eWOM as an important co-creative input into
that process. Brand meaning is therefore not produced exclusively by the organization; it is continuously
reconstructed through what consumers say about the brand and how those accounts circulate within
digital networks.
The ﬁndings are particularly consistent with Mandagi and Aseng (2021), who showed that social-media
marketing inﬂuences all Brand Gestalt dimensions. The present study extends that evidence from
marketer-generated social-media activity to consumer-generated eWOM. This distinction matters
because consumer-generated information carries a different form of legitimacy. Reviews and
recommendations are commonly perceived as reﬂections of lived experience rather than formal
promotional claims and can therefore supply consumers with cues that are diﬃcult for brands to
communicate credibly through conventional advertising. Beck et al. (2023) further demonstrate that
different forms of eWOM do not carry equal relational value, with recommendations, referrals, and social
communities having stronger implications for trust than impersonal ratings and reviews. Filieri et al.
(2023) similarly highlight the importance of sincerity and trustworthiness in determining whether
digitally transmitted brand information inﬂuences consumers.
The particularly strong formation of Storyscape is conceptually understandable because eWOM is
inherently narrative. Consumers rarely communicate only isolated evaluations; they recount experiences,
explain why products worked or failed, describe interactions with sellers or brand representatives, and
locate those experiences within personal and social contexts. Through repeated circulation, these
narratives can become part of the socially constructed story of the brand. Yet the similarly strong
formation of Sensescape, Servicescape, and Stakeholderscape indicates that eWOM does more than
transmit stories. Online discussions about cosmetics frequently describe fragrance, texture, appearance,
packaging, ease of application, digital shopping experiences, customer service, inﬂuencer credibility,
certiﬁcation, community responses, and the behavior of other brand-related actors. In this sense, eWOM
provides consumers with a distributed set of cues from which the entire Brand Gestalt can be
assembled.
This interpretation also resonates with Ahmed and Amritesh (2023), who conceptualize eWOM itself as a
gestalt of informational cues requiring cognitive integration. It further complements Wang et al. (2024),
whose large-scale analysis of online consumer comments demonstrates that consumers simultaneously
draw on environmental, experiential, and provider-related information when making brand-related
decisions. Taken together, these perspectives suggest that the relationship between eWOM and Brand
Gestalt is not merely persuasive but conﬁgurational: eWOM supplies the raw social information from
which holistic brand meaning is constructed.

5.3 Which Elements of Brand Gestalt Drive Repurchase
Intention?
The differentiated effects of the Brand Gestalt dimensions demonstrate that the elements that make a
brand meaningful are not necessarily the same elements that make consumers buy it again. Sensescape
emerges as the most behaviorally consequential dimension, suggesting that repurchase decisions in
halal cosmetics are strongly grounded in embodied and experiential evaluations. Cosmetics are
inherently sensory products. Texture, fragrance, visual appearance, packaging, application experience,
comfort, and the felt quality of the product become directly observable through consumption. These
experiences provide consumers with concrete evidence about whether the brand continues to deliver
value, making sensory evaluations particularly diagnostic for repeat purchasing.
This interpretation is strongly supported by previous Brand Gestalt research. Walean et al. (2023) found
Sensescape to be more inﬂuential than other gestalt dimensions in predicting revisit intention, while
Walean et al. (2024) reported that Sensescape was the only dimension with a direct effect on customer
loyalty in the telecommunications context. Mandagi et al. (2026) likewise identify Sensescape as
particularly inﬂuential in shaping overall Brand Gestalt and subsequent loyalty-related outcomes. The
recurrence of this pattern across different consumption domains suggests that sensory and experiential
cues may occupy a distinctive position within Brand Gestalt because they provide consumers with
personally veriﬁable evidence rather than merely communicated claims.
The importance of sensory experience also aligns with emerging evidence from digital cosmetics
markets. Chen et al. (2025) found that experiential platform characteristics and visual appeal play
important roles in post-adoption behavior among online cosmetics consumers. In the halal consumption
literature, Hussain et al. (2024) similarly demonstrate that product quality contributes to favorable
attitudes that subsequently support repurchase intention, while Shahid et al. (2023) show that prior
purchase behavior and product-related evaluations are central to subsequent halal cosmetics
repurchase. These ﬁndings collectively suggest that repeat purchasing becomes increasingly grounded
in experienced performance once consumers move beyond the initial adoption stage.
Stakeholderscape also emerges as an important driver of repurchase intention. This ﬁnding underscores
the socially embedded nature of halal cosmetics consumption. Consumers do not evaluate halal brands
solely through their direct interaction with products. Their perceptions are also shaped by inﬂuencers,
retailers, online communities, employees, other consumers, certiﬁcation authorities, and social networks
surrounding the brand. Mandagi and Centeno (2025) emphasize that stakeholders actively participate in
the co-creation of Brand Gestalt, while Wang et al. (2024) demonstrate that product and service
providers can become central components of consumer decision making. In digital environments,
therefore, the credibility and behavior of actors associated with the brand may operate as signals of
authenticity, reliability, social acceptance, and value congruence.
Page 26/34

This stakeholder mechanism is particularly salient in halal markets, where trust, certiﬁcation, social
validation, and value consistency are integral to consumer evaluation. Hussain et al. (2024) identify trust
as an important antecedent of favorable attitudes and repurchase intention toward halal cosmetics,
while Shahid et al. (2023) emphasize the relevance of halal certiﬁcation, religious knowledge, and prior
purchasing behavior. Putri et al. (2018) likewise show that knowledge and religious considerations
contribute to halal repurchase decisions. Stakeholderscape may therefore capture more than
interpersonal attractiveness or social visibility; it can represent a broader social assurance system
through which consumers judge whether a halal cosmetics brand is trustworthy, legitimate, and worthy
of continued patronage.
The absence of independent effects for Storyscape and Servicescape is equally informative. Storyscape
may strongly shape what the brand means without being suﬃciently diagnostic to determine whether
consumers buy it again. Narrative meaning can differentiate a brand, strengthen identity, and enrich
symbolic associations, but consumers with prior product experience may ultimately place greater weight
on what the product feels like and whether trusted actors validate the brand. Similarly, Servicescape may
remain relevant to the overall brand experience without independently driving repurchase once sensory
experience and stakeholder perceptions are taken into account.
These ﬁndings should not be interpreted as evidence that Storyscape and Servicescape are irrelevant.
Brand Gestalt is inherently holistic, and its dimensions share substantial conceptual territory. Their lack
of independent behavioral effects may therefore indicate functional overlap rather than theoretical
redundancy. Indeed, Rantung et al. (2025) demonstrate that Brand Gestalt as a broader conﬁguration
contributes positively to repurchase intention in a traditional-product context, while Pongoh and Mandagi
(2025) show that holistic Brand Gestalt strengthens multiple dimensions of brand equity. The more
appropriate conclusion is that Brand Gestalt dimensions perform different functions: some contribute
primarily to constructing the meaning of the brand, whereas others become more decisive when
consumers translate that meaning into behavioral commitment.
5.4 Brand Gestalt as the Mechanism Connecting eWOM
with Repurchase Intention
The central theoretical contribution of the ﬁndings lies in demonstrating that the inﬂuence of eWOM on
repurchase intention is primarily interpretive rather than automatic. Favorable online communication
does not appear suﬃcient by itself to produce continued purchasing. Instead, consumers ﬁrst
incorporate digitally transmitted information into their understanding of the brand, and it is this
reconstructed brand meaning that provides the basis for subsequent behavioral decisions. Brand Gestalt
therefore operates as a perceptual conversion mechanism that translates socially transmitted
information into consumer-relevant brand meaning.
This interpretation responds directly to an important limitation in conventional eWOM research, which
often treats online communication as a proximal predictor of purchase intention. The broader literature
increasingly suggests that this relationship is more complex. Kaushal et al. (2023) argue that brand
Page 27/34

image helps explain how eWOM becomes purchase intention. Upadhyay and Tripathi (2023)
demonstrate that review helpfulness can fully transmit the effect of review valence to purchase
intention. Filieri et al. (2023) show that source-related characteristics operate through trustworthiness
and brand attitude, while Uyanik (2025) ﬁnds that brand attitude mediates the relationship between
eWOM and brand preference and that this process depends on source credibility. Hairani et al. (2024)
likewise report that eWOM does not necessarily exert a direct inﬂuence on purchase intention even when
it shapes perceived quality and trust. Collectively, these studies indicate that consumers rarely act on
eWOM in an unmediated manner; they ﬁrst interpret what the information implies about the brand.
The present study advances this literature by replacing relatively narrow mediators such as attitude,
image, trust, or perceived value with the broader concept of Brand Gestalt. Rather than asking whether
eWOM changes one speciﬁc brand evaluation, Brand Gestalt captures how consumers integrate multiple
informational domains into a coherent representation of the brand. This provides a richer explanation of
why eWOM can have substantial perceptual inﬂuence while displaying limited direct behavioral
inﬂuence. Online reviews may simultaneously alter perceptions of brand stories, sensory qualities,
service encounters, and associated stakeholders, but those perceptions must become behaviorally
meaningful before they can sustain repurchase.
The mediation pattern is also notably selective. The pathway from eWOM to repurchase intention
operates primarily through Sensescape and Stakeholderscape rather than uniformly across the entire
gestalt. This ﬁnding closely parallels Mandagi and Aseng (2021), who found that social-media marketing
shaped all Brand Gestalt dimensions but that Sensescape and Stakeholderscape were particularly
important in transmitting its effects to brand loyalty. The replication of this structural pattern in a
different communication setting and product context is theoretically signiﬁcant. It suggests that digitally
generated brand meaning may consistently require an experiential and social conversion process before
it produces relational or behavioral outcomes.
The sensory pathway implies that eWOM becomes behaviorally consequential when consumers interpret
online information as credible evidence about what using the product will actually be like. Reviews
describing texture, fragrance, application, packaging, comfort, and visible results reduce experiential
uncertainty and create expectations that can later be conﬁrmed through use. Nguyen et al. (2024)
similarly show that eWOM concerning green cosmetics inﬂuences behavioral intention partly by
changing consumers' underlying evaluations and concerns. For existing customers, this process may
become even more consequential because externally communicated experiences can be compared
against accumulated personal experience, strengthening or weakening the coherence of the brand
gestalt.
The stakeholder pathway operates differently but complements the sensory mechanism. Here, eWOM
transforms repurchase intention by shaping perceptions of the people and institutions connected to the
brand. Recommendations from trusted consumers, inﬂuencers, communities, retailers, or credible brand
representatives can provide social conﬁrmation and reduce uncertainty. Beck et al. (2023) demonstrate
Page 28/34

that socially closer forms of eWOM are particularly important in developing trust, while Filieri et al.
(2023) show that perceived sincerity and trustworthiness are central to the persuasive effects of
inﬂuencer communication. In halal cosmetics, this social validation may be especially important
because purchase decisions involve not only performance expectations but also conﬁdence in
authenticity, legitimacy, and compliance with valued standards.
The broader implication is that eWOM should not be conceptualized primarily as a digital promotional
force. Its more fundamental role is to participate in the social construction of the brand. Consumers
absorb other consumers' experiences, narratives, evaluations, and judgments and reorganize them into a
coherent representation that subsequently guides behavior. Brand Gestalt therefore provides a bridge
between communication and consumption by explaining how dispersed digital cues acquire integrated
meaning.
This perspective also reﬁnes Brand Gestalt theory itself. The ﬁndings indicate that the dimensions of a
gestalt can be jointly shaped while remaining functionally asymmetric. Holism does not require every
component to produce identical downstream consequences. Storyscape, Sensescape, Servicescape,
and Stakeholderscape may collectively constitute the brand as perceived by consumers, yet particular
dimensions become more diagnostic at particular stages of the consumer journey. In the present setting,
eWOM broadly constructs the brand, while sensory experience and stakeholder-related meaning provide
the principal routes through which that construction becomes repurchase intention. Brand Gestalt
should therefore be understood not only as the holistic outcome of integrated brand cues, but also as a
differentiated psychological architecture through which communication is converted into consumer
behavior.
6. Conclusions
This study shows that eWOM primarily functions as a mechanism for shaping consumers’ holistic
perceptions of halal cosmetic brands rather than as a direct driver of repurchase intention. Favorable
eWOM contributes strongly to the formation of all four Brand Gestalt dimensions, but only Sensescape
and Stakeholderscape emerge as meaningful drivers of repurchase intention. The ﬁndings therefore
indicate that the dimensions of Brand Gestalt are jointly formed but behaviorally differentiated. Most
importantly, Brand Gestalt explains how eWOM becomes behaviorally relevant: online consumer
communication shapes the overall representation of the brand, while sensory experience and
stakeholder-related perceptions provide the principal pathways through which this representation
translates into repurchase intention.
The study contributes to eWOM theory by reframing online consumer communication as an antecedent
of holistic brand meaning rather than merely a direct behavioral stimulus. It also advances Brand Gestalt
theory by demonstrating that consumer-generated digital communication contributes to the co-creation
of Storyscape, Sensescape, Servicescape, and Stakeholderscape, thereby extending Brand Gestalt
beyond ﬁrm-controlled branding activities. The differentiated effects of these dimensions further reveal
Page 29/34

functional asymmetry within the gestalt: although the four dimensions constitute an integrated brand
representation, they do not necessarily exert equivalent effects on behavioral outcomes. By identifying
Sensescape and Stakeholderscape as the principal mechanisms connecting eWOM with repurchase
intention, the study reﬁnes understanding of how holistic brand perceptions are converted into consumer
behavior and extends the nomological applicability of Brand Gestalt to the halal cosmetics context.
Halal cosmetic managers should view eWOM management as a process of shaping coherent brand
meaning rather than simply generating positive online visibility. Consumer reviews, testimonials,
inﬂuencer content, and online discussions should consistently reinforce the intended brand story,
sensory promise, service experience, and stakeholder credibility. Particular attention should be given to
Sensescape by encouraging authentic communication about texture, fragrance, appearance, packaging,
application, and product experience, while Stakeholderscape should be strengthened through credible
inﬂuencers, knowledgeable retailers and employees, responsive online communities, transparent
communication, and visible consistency with halal-related values and standards. Storyscape and
Servicescape should not be neglected, but managerial efforts should ensure that all four dimensions
operate coherently, with sensory delivery and stakeholder credibility receiving greater emphasis because
they appear more consequential for repurchase.
The study is limited by its cross-sectional, self-reported design and its focus on halal cosmetics within a
speciﬁc consumer context, which restricts causal inference and broader generalizability. Future research
should employ longitudinal, experimental, and behavioral designs to examine how exposure to different
forms of eWOM changes Brand Gestalt over time and whether these changes predict actual repurchase
behavior. Replication across product and service categories would clarify whether the relative
importance of Sensescape and Stakeholderscape is context dependent. Further studies should also
examine boundary conditions such as source credibility, brand familiarity, product involvement, trust,
authenticity, skepticism, and eWOM valence, while testing alternative higher-order or conﬁgurational
speciﬁcations of Brand Gestalt to better explain how its dimensions operate jointly in shaping consumer
behavior.
