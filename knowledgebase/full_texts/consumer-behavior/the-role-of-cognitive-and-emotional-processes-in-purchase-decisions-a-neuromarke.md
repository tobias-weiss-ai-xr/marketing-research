ABSTRACT  ____________________
Neuromarketing has emerged as a transformative
interdisciplinary field that integrates neuroscience,
psychology, and marketing to better understand consumer
behavior and purchase decision-making processes. This
study aims to analyze the influence of psychological
factors on consumer decisions from a neuromarketing
perspective, emphasizing the role of cognitive and
emotional processes. A qualitative, documentary-based
methodology was employed, supported by a systematic
literature review of recent academic contributions (2020–
2026). The findings reveal that consumer decisions are not
purely rational but are strongly influenced by subconscious
mechanisms, where emotions, memory, and perception
play a central role. Cognitive processes such as attention
and memory significantly impact how consumers process
information and develop brand preferences, while
emotional responses contribute to faster and more intuitive
decision-making. Additionally, the integration of advanced
neuroscientific tools such as EEG, fMRI, and eye-tracking
has enhanced the accuracy and predictive capacity of
marketing research by capturing unconscious consumer
reactions. The study also highlights the importance of
individual differences, including personality and cultural
background, in shaping consumer responses to marketing
stimuli, reinforcing the need for personalized strategies.
Furthermore, the incorporation of emerging technologies
such as artificial intelligence and virtual reality expands the
scope of neuromarketing applications, particularly in digital
environments. Despite its benefits, neuromarketing raises
ethical concerns related to the potential manipulation
of consumer behavior, emphasizing the importance of
responsible implementation. Overall, neuromarketing
provides a comprehensive framework for understanding
and influencing consumer behavior in contemporary
markets.
Keywords:
Neuromarketing, consumer behavior, psychological
factors, purchase decision-making, cognitive processes,
neuroscience.

INTRODUCTION
In the current context characterized by globalization
and digital transformation, understanding consumer
behavior has become a key challenge for organizations.
Purchase decisions can no longer be explained
solely through traditional rational models, as multiple
studies have shown that psychological and emotional
factors play a decisive role in consumer behavior.
In this context, neuromarketing emerges as an
interdisciplinary approach that integrates contributions
from Neuroscience, Psychology, and marketing, with
the aim of analyzing the mental processes that influence
purchase decision-making.
Neuromarketing is based on the premise that a
significant portion of consumer decisions occurs at a
subconscious level, where emotions, perceptions, and
automatic responses play a determining role and are not
always captured through traditional research methods.
In this regard, Gupta et al. (2025) highlights that the
purchasing process involves a complex interaction
between cognitive and emotional variables that unfold
across different stages, from need recognition to post-
purchase evaluation. These authors point out that
the analysis of brain activity has made it possible to
understand how marketing stimuli influences attention,
memory, and emotions, key factors in the selection of
products and brands.
Similarly, Devi & Dineshbabu (2025) emphasize that
the integration of neuromarketing principles into digital
environments has transformed the way companies
design their communication strategies. In a context
where consumers are constantly exposed to large
amounts of information, capturing their attention requires
an understanding of the psychological mechanisms
that guide their behavior. The authors argue that
visual stimuli, emotional narratives, and personalized
experiences can activate neural systems associated
with reward, thereby increasing the likelihood that
consumers will develop preferences for certain
brands. This approach demonstrates that purchase
decisions are not solely based on functional product
characteristics but also on emotional experiences
generated through brand interaction.
Furthermore, Traymbak et al. (2024) provide relevant
empirical evidence by analyzing the reliability and
validity of neuromarketing constructs, demonstrating
that variables such as attention, emotion, and
memory can be consistently measured to explain
consumer behavior. This finding strengthens the
scientific foundation of neuromarketing, supporting its
consolidation as a valid tool for studying psychological
factors that influence decision-making. Additionally, it
shows that these factors are not only observable but
also predictive of purchasing behavior.
Regarding the role of emotions in decision-making,
Jbara et al. (2026) indicate that emotional responses
and brand awareness significantly influence consumer
decisions, particularly in digital environments. The
authors conclude that positive emotions generated
by marketing stimuli facilitate faster decision-making
processes, reducing the level of cognitive effort
required. This highlights the central role of emotional
components in purchasing behavior and their impact
on the development of consumer-brand relationships.
Moreover, Nagpal et al. (2026) examine the relationship
between neuromarketing and consumer impulsivity,
emphasizing the influence of personality traits on
responses to marketing stimuli. Their findings suggest
that certain individuals are more susceptible to
emotional and sensory stimuli, increasing the likelihood
of impulsive purchases. This perspective reveals that
psychological factors do not affect all consumers
uniformly but rather vary according to individual
characteristics that shape their behavior.
Overall, both empirical and theoretical evidence
suggest that neuromarketing is a fundamental tool for
understanding the complexity of consumer behavior.
Purchase decisions result from a dynamic interaction
between cognitive and emotional processes, in which
psychological factors play a central role. These
processes are also deeply influenced by external
stimuli strategically designed to capture attention and
generate specific emotional responses.
In this context, the objective of this article is to analyze
the influence of psychological factors on purchase
decisions from a neuromarketing perspective,
identifying the main cognitive and emotional processes
involved in consumer behavior, as well as their
application in the design of more effective marketing
strategies.
METHODOLOGY
The present study was developed under a qualitative,
documentary-based approach, aimed at the theoretical
and conceptual analysis of neuromarketing and
its relationship with the psychological factors that
influence purchase decisions. A systematic literature
review was conducted to identify, analyze, and
integrate relevant contributions from recent research
in the fields of neuromarketing, consumer psychology,
and neuroscience applied to marketing.
For the selection of sources, scientific articles published
in indexed journals, empirical studies, theoretical

recognized databases were considered. Recent
research (2020–2026) was prioritized to ensure the
relevance and timeliness of the analysis. The inclusion
criteria encompassed studies related to cognitive
and emotional processes, neuroscientific tools (such
as EEG, fMRI, and eye-tracking), and their impact on
consumer behavior.
The methodological process was structured into three
phases. In the first phase, a search and collection
of information was conducted, identifying relevant
studies on neuromarketing and decision-making. In the
second phase, a critical content analysis was carried
out, classifying the information into categories such as
cognitive processes, emotional factors, technological
tools, and purchasing behavior. Finally, in the third
phase, the findings were integrated and synthesized,
allowing for the establishment of relationships
among the analyzed variables and the construction
of a comprehensive understanding of the studied
phenomenon.
This methodological approach enabled a deep
understanding of the influence of psychological
factors on purchase decisions without relying on direct
experimentation, while being supported by validated
scientific evidence. It also facilitated the identification
of trends, patterns, and gaps in literature, contributing
to a critical interpretation of neuromarketing as an
emerging discipline.
DEVELOPMENT
The study of neuromarketing has significantly advanced
the understanding of consumer behavior, particularly
regarding the cognitive and emotional processes
involved in decision-making. Unlike traditional
approaches, which mainly rely on declarative data,
neuromarketing incorporates scientific tools that allow
the analysis of subconscious responses, providing a
deeper and more accurate understanding of consumer
motivations. In this regard, Gupta et al. (2025) highlights
that the purchasing process is neither linear nor
entirely rational but consists of multiple stages where
psychological factors interact and influence product
perception and choice.
One of the main contributions of neuromarketing is
the identification of key cognitive processes, including
attention, memory, and perception. Attention constitutes
the first filter in decision-making, as it determines which
stimuli are processed by the consumer in an environment
saturated with information. In digital contexts, where
users are exposed to multiple stimuli simultaneously,
capturing attention becomes a determining factor in
influencing purchasing behavior. Devi & Dineshbabu
(2025) note that the use of attractive visual elements,
colors, sounds, and impactful narratives can activate
brain areas related to sensory processing, increasing
the likelihood that the message will be retained.
Memory, in turn, plays a fundamental role in building
brand preference. Consumers tend to choose products
they recognize or that have generated positive
experiences in the past. In this sense, marketing
strategies that rely on repetition, emotional association,
and the creation of memorable experiences contribute
to strengthening brand recall. Traymbak et al. (2024)
emphasize that emotional memory has a more
lasting impact than rational memory, which explains
why advertisements that evoke emotions are often
more effective than those based solely on objective
information.
Another key element is perception, understood as
the process by which consumers interpret the stimuli
they receive from their environment. Perception is not
objective but is influenced by previous experiences,
beliefs, and expectations. This implies that two
consumers may interpret the same message differently.
In this sense, neuromarketing allows for the analysis of
how sensory stimuli influence consumer perception,
facilitating the design of more effective and personalized
strategies.
Regarding emotional processes, various studies have
demonstrated that emotions play a central role in
purchase decision-making. Jbara et al. (2026) indicate
that positive emotions, such as happiness, trust,
or satisfaction, increase the likelihood of purchase
by generating an emotional connection between
the consumer and the brand. Conversely, negative
emotions can reduce purchase intention or lead to
product rejection. This finding highlights the importance
of designing marketing experiences that not only inform
but also generate favorable emotional responses.
Neuromarketing has also contributed to understanding
the phenomenon of impulsive buying, which is
characterized by rapid and less deliberate decisions.
Nagpal et al. (2026) point out that impulsivity is
influenced by both emotional factors and personality
traits. Consumers with a higher tendency toward
impulsivity are more susceptible to stimuli such as
promotions, discounts, perceived scarcity, or urgency.
These stimuli activate psychological mechanisms that
reduce rational analysis and favor immediate decisions.
In this sense, the strategic use of these elements can
increase sales, although it also raises ethical concerns
regarding the manipulation of consumer behavior.
Another relevant aspect is the influence of the digital
environment on purchasing decisions. Currently, a large
portion of interactions between consumers and brands
occur through digital platforms, which has transformed
the way neuromarketing principles are applied. Devi
& Dineshbabu (2025) highlight that interface design,
user experience, and content personalization are key
factors in influencing consumer behavior in digital
environments. Elements such as ease of navigation,
loading speed, and clarity of information can determine

purchase.
Similarly, brand awareness plays a fundamental role in
decision-making. Jbara et al. (2026) note that consumers
tend to prefer brands they recognize and with which
they have developed an emotional relationship. This
relationship is built through consistent experiences
and coherent messages that reinforce brand identity. In
this sense, neuromarketing allows for the identification
of the elements that generate the greatest impact on
the consumer’s mind, facilitating the development of
stronger and more differentiated brands.
On the other hand, it is important to consider that
the influence of psychological factors is not uniform
among all consumers. Individual differences, such as
personality, culture, and previous experiences, shape
the way each person responds to marketing stimuli.
Nagpal et al. (2026) emphasize that segmentation
based on psychological variables allows for the design
of more effective strategies tailored to the specific
characteristics of each consumer group.
Finally, the development of neuromarketing raises
important ethical implications. Although this discipline
provides valuable tools for understanding and
predicting consumer behavior, there is also a risk of
manipulating decisions through stimuli designed to
influence the subconscious. Therefore, it is essential
that companies use these tools responsibly, ensuring
transparency and respect for consumer autonomy.
In summary, neuromarketing provides a comprehensive
framework for analyzing the influence of psychological
factors on purchase decisions. The interaction
between cognitive and emotional processes, along
with the influence of external stimuli and individual
characteristics, creates a complex scenario that
requires a multidisciplinary approach. Understanding
these processes not only improves the effectiveness
of marketing strategies but also promotes more ethical
and consumer-centered practices.
Furthermore, neuromarketing relies on various
neuroscientific tools that allow for more precise
measurement of consumer responses. Techniques such
as electroencephalography (EEG), functional magnetic
resonance imaging (fMRI), and eye-tracking provide
valuable insights into attention, emotional engagement,
and decision-making processes. According to Gupta et
al. (2025), these tools enable the identification of stimuli
that generate greater neural activation, facilitating
the optimization of advertising strategies. Similarly,
Traymbak et al. (2024) highlight that integrating these
methods enhances the reliability of consumer behavior
analysis by capturing subconscious reactions that
traditional methods, such as surveys, fail to detect.
This technological advancement has significantly
contributed to the evolution of marketing research,
making it more precise and data driven.

CONCLUSIONS
The analyzed findings allow us to assert that
neuromarketing has radically transformed the
understanding of consumer behavior, challenging
traditional models based on rationality. Rather than
logical and structured decisions, purchase choices
emerge as complex responses influenced by
subconscious processes, in which emotions, memory,
and perception play a decisive role. This paradigm shift
implies that marketing strategies must evolve toward
approaches that are more focused on experience and
emotional connection with the consumer.
One of the most impactful contributions lies in
the evidence that the consumer’s mind can be
influenced through carefully designed stimuli capable
of activating neural mechanisms associated with
pleasure, reward, and rapid decision-making. This
position is neuromarketing not only as an analytical
tool but also as a highly powerful strategic resource
for shaping consumption behaviors. However, this
same capability raises a critical challenge: the risk of
consumer manipulation, which demands the ethical
and responsible use of these techniques.
Furthermore, the integration of advanced technologies
such as EEG, fMRI, artificial intelligence, and virtual
reality has significantly expanded the possibilities
for analysis, enabling access to dimensions of
human behavior that were previously hidden. These
tools have transformed neuromarketing into a more
precise, predictive, and evidence-based discipline,
strengthening its application in digital and highly
competitive environments.
On the other hand, it is evident that consumer behavior
is not homogeneous but is shaped by individual factors
such as personality, culture, and prior experiences. This
reinforces the need to develop personalized marketing
strategies capable of adapting to different profiles and
contexts, thereby increasing their effectiveness.
Neuromarketing has thus become a key discipline for
understanding and anticipating purchase decisions
in the contemporary world. Its value lies not only in
improving the effectiveness of marketing strategies but
also in revealing the complexity of human behavior.
Nevertheless, its future development will depend on
achieving a balance between technological innovation
and ethical responsibility, ensuring that the knowledge
generated is used for the benefit of both organizations
and consumers.
