Abstract. This paper uses Labubu, a popular intellectual property (IP) character under Pop
Mart, as a case study to explore the impact of the IP economy on consumer behavior. The
research finds that the IP economy significantly influences consumer behavior in two
primary dimensions: consumers’ psychological mechanisms and the reshaping of
consumption patterns. On the psychological level, four key factors emerge—emotional
resonance and value identification, scarcity and uniqueness, social recognition and
conformity, and brand effect—which collectively drive emotional engagement, perceived
value, and brand loyalty. On the behavioral level, the IP economy encourages more
diversified purchasing decisions and broader consumption patterns, transforming the
traditional consumption model into a more interactive, emotionally charged, and
community-driven process. These dynamics not only stimulate frequent consumption but
also contribute to product premiumization and the formation of fan-based economic loops.
This study holds practical significance for IP operators, marketers, and brand strategists
seeking to leverage emotional value, community culture, and multi-touchpoint engagement
to build sustainable brand ecosystems in the age of consumer co-creation.
Keywords: IP economy, Behavioral economy, Consumer behavior, Labubu
1. Introduction
In recent years, the rise of the intellectual property (IP) economy has significantly reshaped
consumer behavior, particularly among Generation Z and Millennials. IPs have evolved from mere
legal assets into cultural and emotional symbols that drive identity expression and emotional
attachment. The popularity of collectible toys—especially those sold through blind box models—
illustrates a shift from functional consumption to behavior driven by emotion and identity. This
paper uses Labubu as a case study to examine how the IP economy influences consumer behavior. It
focuses on two dimensions: the psychological mechanisms—emotional resonance, scarcity, social
identity, and brand trust—and the restructuring of consumption patterns, including diversified
purchasing behaviors and socially interactive, experience-based consumption. The study aims to
provide deeper insight into how IPs transform consumer decision-making and to offer strategic
guidance for brands seeking to foster sustained engagement in a culture-driven market.
This study contributes to the existing literature in several key ways. First, it connects IP theory
with consumer behavior by identifying the psychological mechanisms that characterize IP-driven

2. Psychological mechanisms of consumer behavior influenced by the IP economy
The IP economy has played an increasingly influential role in shaping consumer behavior,
particularly in the realms of branding, entertainment, and technology. The underlying psychological
mechanisms driving this influence can be summarized as follows:
2.1. Emotional resonance and value identification
The IP economy influences consumer purchasing behavior through emotional resonance and value
identification. First, emotional resonance significantly shapes product perception and purchase
intention. As emotional connections with a brand deepen, they increasingly drive consumer
decisions—often surpassing rational evaluation [1]. Second, effective IP creation fosters these
emotional bonds. IP elements—such as storytelling, cultural references, and emotional depth—serve
as critical touchpoints that establish enduring psychological and social ties between consumers and
brands. In addition, value identification plays a central role. Cultural products such as films, anime,
and games enable consumers to express personal identity and a sense of social belonging [2]. These
associations reinforce personal alignment with the brand and exert a strong influence on purchasing
behavior.
2.2. Scarcity and uniqueness
Scarcity and uniqueness within the IP economy exert a strong influence on consumer decision-
making. First, marketers strategically leverage scarcity to enhance purchase intention. Limited
editions create a sense of exclusivity, thereby increasing product desirability. This effect stems less
from price or quality and more from perceptions of uniqueness, sacrifice, and value [3]. Second, the
IP economy amplifies this dynamic by emphasizing rarity. Brands employ exclusive collaborations,
seasonal product drops, and limited releases to heighten perceived scarcity, which, in turn, fuels
consumer desire and encourages impulse purchases. Finally, scarcity contributes to the formation of
social identity. Possessing rare IP products can confer a sense of belonging and elevated social status
[4]. In this way, the IP economy not only drives demand but also cultivates long-term brand loyalty
through culturally and socially valued exclusivity.
2.3. Social recognition and conformity
Conformity has long influenced consumer decision-making. As inherently social beings, individuals
base their choices not only on personal preferences but also on the observed behaviors of others. The
desire for social approval and alignment with group norms often drives consumer actions [5]. With

2.4. Brand effect, trust, and loyalty
In a competitive market, brand identity serves as a critical strategic asset that significantly influences
consumer purchasing decisions. Unlike traditional markets, where brand value is primarily built on
product quality or service, IP brands often establish consumer trust through long-term reputation,
emotional resonance, and cultural storytelling [8]. Consumers frequently perceive such brands as
symbols of reliability, which reduces price sensitivity [9] and increases the likelihood of purchase—
even in the absence of detailed product evaluation [10]. For instance, fans of IPs such as Disney are
often willing to pay a premium not for functionality alone, but for the emotional and cultural
connections they associate with the brand. This results in a phenomenon known as "fan dependency"
[11], in which consumption is driven less by rational analysis and more by identity and emotional
alignment. This dynamic underscores the strategic importance of brand trust and loyalty within the
IP economy.
3. The reshaping of consumer behavior by the IP economy
Within the IP economy, consumer behavior has evolved beyond considerations of product
functionality and quality. Purchasing decisions are increasingly influenced by emotional attachment,
anticipated value appreciation, social identity, and the pursuit of entertainment. This shift has
fundamentally reshaped consumer motivations, brand preferences, and patterns of loyalty.
3.1. More diversified purchase decisions
As basic needs are increasingly met, consumer decision-making has shifted from a focus on
functionality to a prioritization of emotional and social value [12]. First, product value is now driven
more by emotional engagement and social signaling than by practical utility. Limited-edition IP
products, for instance, combine aesthetic appeal with symbolic status [13]. Second, the IP economy
has a particularly strong impact on younger consumers. Among those under the age of 39, emotional
satisfaction and social recognition are primary drivers of purchasing behavior—especially in relation
to IP-related goods [12]. Third, some consumers view limited-edition IP products as investment
assets. The perceived rarity of such items enhances their premium value, prompting a shift from
traditional consumption to asset-oriented purchasing behavior [13].
3.2. Diversification of consumption patterns
The IP economy has transformed consumer purchasing behavior from traditional models into more
dynamic, multifaceted patterns. First, it has strengthened consumer–brand interaction. Traditional
one-way transactions have evolved into participatory experiences, where consumers engage in
entertainment, information exchange, and real-time content co-creation, thereby deepening their
connection with the brand [14]. Second, the IP economy has fostered integrated consumption

4. Case study
4.1. What is Labubu
Labubu, a signature intellectual property (IP) developed by Pop Mart, has cultivated a strong fan
base through its cute yet emotionally expressive design. Combining innocence with subtle elements
of rebellion and freedom, the character embodies themes of self-discovery and nonconformity,
resonating deeply with young consumers’ desires for emotional expression and identity formation.
Pop Mart’s “blind box” model amplifies Labubu’s appeal by incorporating elements of surprise
and scarcity, transforming each purchase into a suspenseful experience. This approach not only
stimulates collecting behavior but also strengthens emotional bonds with the brand, as the unboxing
process becomes a shared ritual within fan communities, facilitated through videos, trading, and
social media interactions.
4.2. The influence mechanism of labubu on consumer behavior
4.2.1. Emotional resonance and value identification
Labubu’s distinctive design—combining elements of rebellion and warmth—functions as more than
a mere collectible; it serves as an emotional symbol of identity and belonging, resonating with
contemporary consumers’ desire for self-expression and meaningful emotional experiences. The
blind box mechanism introduces surprise and uncertainty, transforming purchases into engaging
rituals. Activities such as unboxing, trading duplicates, and sharing experiences online have shifted
consumption into an interactive, socially driven process that reinforces emotional value and sustains
a thriving fan economy. According to data from Statista and Minsheng Securities Research, blind
boxes represent 36.6% of China’s designer toy market, with the consumer base projected to increase
from 19 million in 2015 to 40 million by 2025, reflecting an average annual growth rate of 7.7%.
4.2.2. Scarcity and uniqueness
A core operational strategy of the Labubu series is the deliberate creation of perceived scarcity to
sustain ongoing consumer engagement. The blind box mechanism inherently introduces
randomness, encouraging consumers to make repeat purchases to obtain their desired figures. Each
series typically includes a limited number of “hidden editions” or “exclusive editions,” with
appearance probabilities often as low as 1/96 or 1/144, thereby generating significant scarcity value.

4.2.3. Social recognition and conformity
The popularity of Labubu is largely fueled by herd mentality, which is amplified through social
media platforms. As of June 15, 2025, there were over 200,000 posts related to “Labubu” on
Xiaohongshu, underscoring the significant role social platforms play in shaping consumer decision-
making.
Within Labubu’s fan communities, many consumers acknowledge that their purchases are not
necessarily motivated by genuine affection for the character itself, but rather by the influence of
unboxing live streams and social media posts showcasing rare editions. For instance, several trendy
cafés have introduced Labubu-themed cakes, attracting non-fans who purchase primarily for “photo
sharing” and social check-ins. This form of “emotionally contagious” consumption exemplifies how
social influence intensifies herd behavior. Consequently, consumption is transformed from a simple
transaction into a cycle of social interaction and experiential sharing, further facilitating the spread
and resale of Labubu products while reinforcing a self-sustaining community loop and maintaining
persistent market enthusiasm.
4.2.4. Brand effect and trust
Pop Mart has effectively leveraged the power of brand effect in the successful operation of its
Labubu series. By consistently launching hidden editions, limited releases, and cross-industry
collaborations, the brand continuously delivers a sense of novelty and fosters sunk cost commitment
among fans, thereby strengthening emotional attachment. Simultaneously, the sense of belonging
and interaction cultivated within Labubu’s fan communities and secondary trading markets further
enhances consumer loyalty and trust in the Pop Mart brand. As a result, the brand functions as a
“guarantee of trustworthiness,” effectively reducing consumers’ decision-making costs and price
sensitivity, and increasing their willingness to pay a premium for emotional and cultural
identification.

5. Challenge and limitation
5.1. Intellectual property and legal protection
The success of Labubu has also rendered it vulnerable to counterfeiting and unauthorized use. Weak
enforcement mechanisms, coupled with the growth of online secondary markets, complicate the full
protection of intellectual property rights, potentially undermining consumer trust and diluting brand
value. Strengthening legal safeguards and raising consumer awareness remain ongoing challenges.
Moreover, even well-established IPs like Labubu face risks related to market fatigue and shifting
consumer interests.
5.2. IP life cycle
Over-reliance on scarcity and speculation can lead to demand volatility. To sustain consumer
engagement, Pop Mart must continuously innovate through new storylines and character designs;
however, excessive expansion risks overexposure, potentially undermining brand uniqueness and
diminishing consumers’ willingness to pay. Additionally, fluctuations in the secondary market may
cause consumer dissatisfaction if perceived value declines. Therefore, sustainable IP life cycle
management requires balancing scarcity with accessibility, while adapting to evolving consumer
preferences and competitive dynamics.

5.3. Irrational consumption and financial risks
Labubu’s blind box strategy heightens consumer excitement but also contributes to impulsive
spending and addictive behaviors, with some young buyers incurring debt while pursuing rare items.
Limited editions are subject to intense speculation, with prices soaring from under 100 RMB to
several thousand. When market hype diminishes, consumers who purchased at peak prices face
significant financial losses. Although this model enhances brand visibility, it carries substantial
financial risks.
6. Future outlook
Looking ahead, the Labubu case illustrates that successful IP operations depend not only on limited
editions or design but also on sustained emotional connection, active community participation, and a
multi-channel ecosystem. Emerging technologies such as virtual reality (VR), augmented reality
(AR), artificial intelligence (AI), and non-fungible tokens (NFTs) are poised to further transform fan
engagement with IPs, enabling immersive virtual experiences, personalized content, and innovative
digital revenue models that extend beyond physical products.
At the same time, Chinese IPs such as Labubu hold growing potential for cultural export. By
integrating traditional and contemporary elements into their storytelling and design, these
homegrown IPs can become powerful vehicles for promoting Chinese culture on the global stage.
Furthermore, the future development of the IP economy must embrace greater social responsibility.
As speculative consumption and fan-driven hype intensify, brands should encourage rational
consumer behavior through transparent mechanisms, promote positive content, and foster healthier
consumer ecosystems. In doing so, IP operators can transition from mere entertainment providers to
cultural ambassadors and stewards of ethical consumption.
