Abstract
Purpose: Retail differentiation hinges on effectively aligning
a company's internal resources with the evolving psychology
of shoppers.  The ultimate aim for retailers is to  accelerate
sales. This involves utilizing retail marketing practices that
optimize marketing efforts. T he supermarket industry in
Kenya faces significant rivalry from a vast number of formal
and informal competitors. Supermarkets often fail to achieve
sales goals because of a concentration on proprietary
marketing that overlooks what shoppers feel, think and how
they are likely to act. These challenges have prompted a
conceptual shift exploring different pathways to minimizing
the gap between attitudes, motivations, and behavior of
shoppers. The specific purpose of the research is to
investigate the extent to which shopper attitudes mediate the
relationship between retail marketing practices , specifically
transaction and relationship marketing and shopper behavior.
Methodology: A supermarket intercept using descriptive
cross-sectional research is employed. Data were collected
using the 5-point Likert scale from a sample of 372 randomly
selected respondent  were examined using  descriptive and
inferential statistical tech niques with the aid of  PROCESS
macro (model 4)
Findings: The findings indicate that retail marketing
orientations comprising transactional and relational
approaches exhibit a significant relationship with shopper
behavior. The findings reveal that Shopper Attitude partially
mediates the impact of both marketing approaches. The study
indicates th at both approaches operate primarily through
direct pathways ; transaction marketing exerts a  marginally
higher direct and indirect mechanism, hence a higher  total
effect. Conclusively, tangible operational execution is the
primary engine that drives consum er behavior far more
effectively than abstract psychological states ; however, it is
intertwined with the necessity of fostering a positive
psychological attitude among consumers  as a secondary
boost.
Unique Contribution to Theory, Practice and Policy: The
findings support various theories including marketing
exchange and consumer behavior, showing that retail
marketing practices strongly influence shopper behavior
through attitudes. Future research should adopt a longitudinal
approach with a bidimensional view of attitudes. Moreover,
while this study primarily focused on a general orientation
towards supermarkets, it acknowledges that other factors
within these environments also shape shopper behavior
beyond mere attitudes. The implications of these findings are
crucial for the retail sector  and manufacturers. To maximise
market share, a retail strategy must establish operational
environments that foster long -term loyalty during the entire
shopper decision process . By deliberately structuring
marketing chann els to complement these cognitive frames,
supermarkets can transform psychological mindsets into
predictable, highly profitable repeat purchasing behaviors.
Keywords: Retail Marketing Practices, Transaction
Marketing, Relationship Marketing, Shopper Attitudes,
Shopper Behavior

INTRODUCTION
Recent a dvancements in artificial intelligence and omnichannel strategies facilitate fluid
interactions across digital and physical retail environments. This evolution is particularly
crucial in a landscape with an abundance of comparable products and services. However, this
has engendered intense global competition among market participants and the broader
consumer goods industry, thereby eroding traditional strategic differentiators, exerting severe
revenue volatility and escalating acquisition costs  in the retail sector (Silou, 2025). To drive
sales and fulfil buyers' expectations , Richter and Hanif (2023)  portend that differentiation  is
necessary and, in the retail environment , entails aligning a company's internal resources with
the psychology of consumers to bridge the gap between shoppers' attitudes and their actual
purchasing decisions.
Supermarkets in Kenya exhibit structural ch aracteristics akin to those found globally. These
establishments are delineated into a three -tier system based on branch networks (Neven &
Reardon, 2008) and are primarily engaged in the distribution of consumer-packaged goods and
grocery products. Alongside other formal retail sectors, supermarkets generate an estimated
$4.62 billion per annum, contributing approximately 2.34% to 3% of the national Gross
Domestic Product (GDP). This segment constitutes 30% of the retail industry and is the third-
largest private sector employer in the country (Kenya National Bureau of Statistics, 2026) .
While trading occurs mainly through traditional brick -and-mortar establishments, online
shopping is gradually gaining a foothold despite still being in its nascent phase of development
(UNCTAD, 2023). The supermarkets operate through a razor-thin margin of stability, currently
navigating the third -highest competitive intensity index in Africa ( Kearney Global Retail
Development Index, 2022) . Market data further emphasizes that formal supermarkets face
fierce, fragmented rivalry from over 250,000 small traditional retail shops that dictate an
astounding 77% of total market share (Boston Consulting Group, 2022).
Recent global market reviews estimate that the retail industry generated $30 trillion to $31.5
trillion in total sales and project that this figure will reach $32.9 trillio n by the end of 2026
(MarkNtel Advisors, 2026). The food and grocery segment is the largest component of the
global retail market, generating approximately $13.11 trillion annually  (Fortune Business
Insights, 2026). This makes retail a major contributor to national economies. Research on the
global retail economy also highlights differences between developed and emerging markets
(Rastogi, 2025). While studies in industrialized countries primarily examine automation and
market saturation, studies in Africa and Latin America focus on the shift from informal retail
structures to formalized supply chains.
Emerging markets, particularly in sub-Saharan Africa, are becoming the focal point for modern
retail marketing since this region is experiencing rapid urbanization and changing consumer
behaviors, leading to fast -paced changes in the market. In these dynamic environments, the
relationship between marketing strategies and business pe rformance is intricate  and largely
shaped by local factors. To understand how th ese factors operate under pressure, researchers
must look to sub-Saharan Africa's primary retail hubs. Nairobi is a key player in sub -Saharan
Africa's formal retail sector, rep resenting about 70% of Kenya's modern grocery market.
Nairobi serves as an ideal empirical context for study; furthermore, it is a growing modern hub
where formal corporate retail interacts with established informal networks, creating a complex
landscape t hat requires adaptive marketing strategies for success  (Deloitte & Planet Retail
2012).

Understanding market dynamics  entails adopting marketing practices that fulfil  shopper
expectations. Sanbonmatsu et al. (2014 ) contend that one of the keys to  achieving effective
business management is a critical understanding of the multiple roles’ shopper attitudes play
in decision-making. Solomon and Russell (2024)  emphasize that attitudes are a response to
stimuli and  significantly influence individuals' thoughts, emotions, and behaviors. Kinoti
(2015) further elaborates that business survival is enhanced by addressing the factors that
impede shoppers' purchasing decisions, which can only be comprehensively understood
through attitudes. Specifically, it enables supermarkets to predict shopping preferences and
influence purchasing patterns.  Jeromo (2022)  shows that attitudes  are perceived as either
appealing or undesirable, and in a supermarket environment, encompass the diverse reactions
toward products, brands, services, and experiences that ultimately drive consumer behavior.
The supermarket is a significant retail distributio n channel in Kenya  and utilizes similar
strategies across the industry despite selling undifferentiated goods, thus generating significant
rivalry from a vast number of formal and informal outlets (The Boston Cons ulting Group,
2022).  Because informal retail outlets leverage transaction and interaction marketing to attract
and retain shoppers (Kimani et al., 2012), understanding shoppers' psychological mechanisms
is necessary for leveraging supermarket practices for enhanced differentiation in a crowded
marketplace.
Statement of the Problem
The expansion of supermarkets in Nairobi has not come without challenges, particularly due
to the unpredictable nature of shoppers’ behavior (Boston Consulting Group, 2022). Shoppers
view supermarkets positively, appreciating their convenience for larger, less frequent
purchases, such as household supplies. However, they do not view supermarkets as exclusive
shopping destinations. They typically split their shopping outlets and prefer to buy fresh
produce from smaller vendors, highlighting a disconnect between their positive attitudes
towards supermarkets and their actual shopping behavior. This attitude–behavior gap suggests
that, while consumers may favor supermarkets, this preference does not always translate into
supermarket purchases. For retailers, understanding these market dynamics is crucial to
crafting marketing strategies that drive sales. Shoppers ’ attitudes manifest as positive or
negative evaluations, which in turn affect the responses of both sellers and buyers (Solomon,
2024).  However, businesses worldwide fail to sustain sales because they overemphasize
proprietary mar keting strategies while neglecting consumers ’ perspectives. To address this
issue, which remains relevant in consumer research, understanding shoppers’ attitudes can help
identify factors likely to impede sales and the fulfilment of shoppers ’ expectations (Munro et
al., 2023).
Additionally, while marketing effort is known to directly influence consumer actions, there is
a notable lack of empirical research examining the indirect effects, particularly how marketing
efforts must alter consumer attitudes first to affect behaviors. In the Kenyan retail market, there
is a significant gap in the execution and understanding of real-time in-store interventions.
A key issue is whether rational, practical considerations (utilitarian values) or emotional,
sensory experiences (hedonic values) drive shopping decisions. Additionally, there is a notable
lack of empirical research on how hedonic and utilitarian attitudes mediate the relationship
between marketing strategies and shopper behavior. Retailers often implement su permarket
tactics based on the incorrect assumption that these strategies affect all shoppers in the same

Research Objectives
The primary objective of the research is to investigate the relationship between retail marketing

LITERATURE REVIEW
Empirical Review
Research into shopper attitudes has yielded significant empirical insights. Kraus's (1995) meta-
analysis examined 88 separate studies which focuse d on whether there was a connection
between attitudes and behaviour. Similarly, Kinoti and Kibeh (2015)  studied how customer
relationship marketing (CRM) impacts customer loyalty among 382 telecommunication
subscribers in Kenya. T heir study reveal ed that factors like trust, service quality, customer
satisfaction, effective communication, and a strong brand image enhance attitude, leading to
repeat purchases and relational long -term behaviours. These studies indicated that attitude is
primarily viewed as a predictor of behaviour, emphaisizing its direct effect in this relationship.
However, there is a notable gap in research regarding the role of attitude when considered as a
mediating variable. This perspective warrants further expl oration, as understanding attitude's
mediating influence could provide deeper insights into the relationship between attitude and
behaviour. The potential for attitude to serve as a third variable in this context presents an
opportunity for future studies to enhance the theoretical framework surrounding behavioural
predictions.
Watchravesringkan and Punyapiroje (2011) show the link between retail marketing practices
and shopper behaviour. Their study involved shoppers i n a few leading hypermarkets in
Thailand. Data were collected using a shopper intercept. Shoppers displayed varying attitudes
towards retail service, advertising and fair pricing. Respondents showed the same attitudes
towards offerings and product quality.
Frymier and Nadler (2017)  support these findings , indicating a direct link between internal
assessments and actions. However, research by Fabrigar et al. (2020) challenged the viability
of attitude as an impetus for the prediction of behaviour.  Attitudes alone are not reliable
predictors of behaviour, as decisions are shaped by complex cognitive factors. Several other
studies challenged this simplicity, suggesting that people's conscious intentions often did not

Theoretical Framework and Hypothesis Development
This study draws on the Marketing Theory of Exchange, which posits that marketing acts as a
dynamic process of transferring value between parties for mutual benefit (Kotler, 1969). It
relies heavily on a strategic alignment between a retailer’s internal operations and the external
psychological ecosystem of the consumer (Kotler et al., 2022).  To comprehensively evaluate
this exchange, this study operationalizes behavior and consumer behavior theories as well.
The marketing exchange theory is operationalized through marketing managem ent. In the
marketing context, marketing exchange encompasses deliberate strategic choices and customer
service protocols, which represent the specific organizational inputs that the retailer injects into
the marketplace to initiate the exchange process (Berman et al 2018). However, the Marketing
Theory of Exchange has historically faced critiques for focusing too strictly on transactional
mechanics while ignoring nuanced social and psychological dynamics. Marketing Theory of
Exchange is the anchor theory  which underscores the essential connection between internal
management practices and the external market environment, demonstrating that a company
cannot effectively provide value to external stakeholders without ensuring coherence  and
alignment within its internal operations (Baggozzi, 1975). However, critics argue that the rigid
nature of marketing exchange models understates the chaotic, emotional social dynamics,
which limits an understanding of the shopper (Asundi, 2015).
The study 's independent variable s are operationalised as reta il marketing pra ctices through
transaction marketing and relationship marketing. In the context of marketing exchange, the
four Ps: Product, Price, Place, and Promotion serve as essential operational levers of Exchange
Theory. Kotler et al. (2022) i ndicate that the product is identified as the primary means of
delivering utility to consumers, acting as the reward in the exchange process. Price rep resents
the financial cost incurred by the consumer, while place and promotion are designed to reduce
transaction and search costs, respectively. A successful marketing exchange occurs when the
total utility derived from the Product, Place, and Promotion e xceeds the economic cost
represented by Price, thereby fulfilling the core principle of Exchange Theory, which posits
that rewards must surpass costs for a positive exchange to take place.  Modern retailers use
relationship marketing to enhance retention through: customer lock -in, personalisation, co -
production, and database  marketing, involving customers ' participation  in the creation of
products or services (Hutt et al.,2024).  Further, this collaborative approach fosters a sense of

To address this critique, this study introduce d attitudes theory as a mediator, drawing heavily
from behavioral theories. Attitudes represent the cognitive evaluations, emotional feelings, and
action tendencies that a shopper forms in response to retail inputs  (Solomon et al.  2024).
Similarly, Dockin et al. (2021) study emphasizes the importance of behavioral psychology in
understanding retail practices, suggesting that they not only influence consumer actions but
also need to change consumer attitudes to facilitate exchanges.  However, the study shows the
attitude-behavior gap has several limitations , noting that assessing a consu mer's internal
attitudes without a solid foundation can lead to weak predictions about behavior (Kollmuss &
Agyeman, 2002). Most studies focus on direct relationships, examining how changes in retail
practices affect sales or purchase intentions, while neglecting the psychological factors at play.
This oversight leads to a phenomenon known as the Black Box Syndrome, where the
consumer's thought processes remain unexamined (Kotler & Armstrong, 2020). As a result,
there is limited evidence on how retail practices influence the Attitude -Behavior Gap. This
study aims to fill this gap by collecting primary data to analyze the roles of hedonic and
utilitarian orientations, providing measurable insig hts into the cognitive processes that drive
consumer behavior.
 Behavioural theory is operationalized through shopper attitudes , the mediator  comprising
utilitarian and hedonic orientations and intention. In the context of marketing, value is divided
into two main dimensions: utilitarian and hedonic. Utilitarian value refers to the practical and
functional benefits that a rational consumer seeks to maximi se efficiency (Solomon et al.,
2024). On the other hand, hedonic value focuses on the emotional and experiential rewards that
come from the exchange process. This study emphasizes that utility should not be viewed solely
as a financial or functional measure. Instead, it presents utility as a multi-dimensional concept
where both emotional satisfaction and pr actical benefits work together to shape a consumer's
overall perception of value.
Consumer behavior theory represents the tangible culmination of the consumer decision -
making process (Berman et al ., 2018). Within the retail exchan ge paradigm, this behavior is
the outcome measured through the emotions and actions of the shopper .The study operates
consumer behavior through shopper behavior; this dependent variable comprises engagement,
reciprocity, trust, commitment, and satisfaction. The model identifies shopper behavior as the
key response, although the behavioral outcome remains unknown. The study examines this
outcome through two periods: immediate purchase intention and long -term relationships, as
outlined in the frameworks of Mc Carthy (1960) and Rowe & Barnes (1998). The study
emphasizes a clear distinction between transactional inputs, relational inputs, and internal value
perceptions. This separation is crucial for empirical design, allowing research to explore how
these factors influence shopper behavior. The ultimate goal of the research is to uncover the
final behavioral response. The  study tested the following  hypotheses:
H01a: Transaction marketing practices have no significant direct effect on shopper
behaviour.
H02a: Transaction marketing practices have no significant effect on shopper attitudes.

METHODOLOGY
This study adopts a positivist philosophical framework. The primary objective of this study
was to investigate the mediating effect of shopper attitudes in the relationship between ret ail
marketing practices and shopper behavior within the supermarket industry. To achieve this, a
descriptive cross-sectional survey approach was deployed to test the hypothesized conceptual
framework recommended by Saunders et al. (2023). The population for this study consisted of
shoppers visiting tier one supermarkets in Nairobi. A total of 400 respondents were recruited
using a probability sampling technique  after shopping in tier one superma rkets in Nairobi,
which are supermarket chains having at least 10 branches.

nature of shopper response outcomes within the exchanges, allowing for a precise evaluation
of how distinct marketing inputs influence unified consumer behavior.

RESULTS
Preliminary Findings
Data screening was conducted to eliminate incomplete or careless responses, resulting in a final
usable sample of 372 participants, with a 93% response rate. The sample profile consisted of
205 (55.1 %) females and 167 (44.9%) male participants, with the majority falling into the 29-
39 age bracket.
Data analysis involves the application of descriptive and in ferential statistics and, finally,

Discussion
The primary objective of this study was to examine the mediating influence of shoppers'
attitudes on the relationship between retail marketing practices comprising transaction,
relationship marketing and shopper behavior. It was hypothesized that shopper attitudes have
no significant mediating effect on the relationship between retail marketing practices and
shopper behavior. Two separate null hypotheses were proposed: one for transaction marketing
and one for relationship market ing. The results led to the rejection of both null hypotheses,
demonstrating that shopper attitudes partially mediate the relationship between retail marketing
practices and shopper behavior because the behavior still happens through direct or indirect
routes, and complement each other because both paths point in the same direction.
The results from both models showed that transaction and relationship marketing practices had
positive and significant effects on shopper attitudes (Path a). The effect of shopper attitudes on
shopper behavior, whilst controlling for transaction and relationship marketing practices (Path
b), was positive and significant. While transaction marketing practices were effective at
changing shopper behavior (Path a), the mediator shopper attitude did not carry the momentum
very strongly into shopper behavior. Furthermore, the introduction of the mediator accounted
for approximately one -third of the relationship, which was transmitted through shopper
attitudes. Similarly, introducing s hopper attitudes into the relationship marketing practice
model indicated that approximately one third of relationship marketing practices' influence on
shopper behavior was transmitted through the mediator.
The mechanics of how shopper attitudes transmi t their effects to shopper behavior are
explained by comparing paths a and b in transaction and relationship marketing mediation
models. On the one hand , relationship marketing had a slightly larger proportion of its total
effect operating directly 70.6% compared with 65.9% and was more enduring or sustained, as

This direct effect indicates that immediate mechanisms, rather than complex psychological
factors, directly trigger shopper behavior. This indicates that transaction and relationship
marketing variables are powerful enough to drive changes in shopper behavior entirely on their

The findings suggest that consumer behavior can be effectively analyzed by considering both
spatial and temporal dimensions. Utilitarian behavior, which is focused on practical needs and
efficiency, typically initiates outside the supermarket, where consumers plan their purchases
based on necessity. In contrast, hedonic behavior, characterized by the pursuit of pleasure and
enjoyment, is often stimulated once consumers enter the supermarket. The analysis of effec t
sizes within the models indicates that the direct effect was significantly larger than the indirect
effect and fails to align with Bulbul and Menon's (2009) study showing that hedonism overrides
inhibitions. This disparity highlights important insights into consumer behavior, suggesting that
the shoppers involved in the study predominantly exhibited ratio nal decision -making
tendencies.
The dominance of the direct route in contributing to the total effect suggests that effective
transaction marketing practices align with shoppers' logical and cost -benefit evaluations. To
assess the practical significance of pathways, effect sizes were analyzed using path coefficients
and the Proportion of Total Effect Mediated (PM) index by Cohen’s (1988). The direct effect
of transaction marketing (c' = 0.2700) nears the medium effect threshold of 0.30, highlighting
its importance as a behavioral driver, while the indirect effect (ab = 0.1372) is considered small,
not surpassing 0.25.  This indicates that shoppers prioritize a seamless mechanical and direct
experience over indirect emotional influences, mirroring Tzeng et al. (2016) and Njoka (2010).
However, this operational focus does not exist in isolation; it is intertwined with the necessity
of fostering a positive psychological attitude among consumers , mirroring Simonetti et al.
(2026).  The findings indicate that the hedonic pathway, which relates to shopper attitudes,  is
secondary to the primary focus on operational efficiency among supermarket shoppers and fails
to mirror studies by Magidis et al. (2016) and Takao et al. (2011) that hedonic orientation has
a stronger influence. This suggests that marketers in the retail industry should enhance the
shopping atmosphere and experience, without compromising the fundamental utility of the
exchange to align with Dokcen et al. (2021).
In business, transactional marketing relies heavily on rational appeals to secure immediate,
short-term sales, while relationship marketing leverages deep emotional connections to build
long-term customer loyalty gradually. Instead of a l arge indirect path driven by emotions, the
strong direct path demonstrates that relationship marketing was built on structured commitment
based on trust and operational dependency. Rather than relying on emotional sentiment to
cultivate retention, the supe rmarket environment demands a more pragmatic approach to
relationship building. When a retailer delivers flawless, frictionless operational execution, it

CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS
Conclusion
Traditional ma rketing strategies often focus on changing consumer mindsets to enhance
enjoyment or encourage deliberation about product value. However, the findings of this
research demonstrate that effective operational and structural execution is a more powerful
driver of revenue and the primary engine that drives consumer behavior far more effectively
than abstract psychological states; however, it is intertwined with the necessity of fostering a
positive psychological attitude among consumers as a secondary boost ; however, it is
intertwined with the necessity of fostering a positive psychological attitude among consumers.
This insight suggests the need for a significant change in how resources are allocated within
the industry. Companies should prioritize investments in database management workflows. By
creating a deeply integrated operational infrastructure, supermarkets can automatically capture
and predict shopper behavior.
Recommendations
To improve the accuracy and predictive capability of retail consumer st udies, researchers
should prioritize longitudinal research designs over cross-sectional approaches. This shift will
allow for better tracking of consumer behavior and attitudes over time, addressing the
limitations of single-visit feedback. Methodologies m ust also consider common method bias
and omitted variable bias by utilizing multi-wave or multi -source data collection instead of
synchronous self-reports. Future studies should analyze individual dataset items to provide
deeper insights rather than relyin g on standardized datasets. Conceptually, models should
evolve from a unidimensional view of consumer attitudes to a bidimensional framework to
capture the interplay between hedonic and utilitarian motivations. Moreover, investigating
additional mediators and external factors in supermarket settings is essential. Broadening
research to include diverse e-commerce contexts will enrich the understanding of how digital
platforms affect consumer behavior, particularly in business -to-consumer dynamics. The
relationship between emotional engagement and habitual practices, such as loyalty, plays a
crucial role in fostering consumer loyalty, as indicated by the significant paths in the RM
framework.
