Abstract
Tourism generates substantial waste and resource pressure, and the shift toward a circular economy
(CE) increasingly intersects with digitalization, a combination known as the twin transition. This
study reviews how the tourism industry has approached circular practices and the twin transition.
A systematic literature review was conducted using Scopus, with a search string combining twin
transition or circular economy with tourism and covering all years up to October 2025. From 518
peer reviewed articles id entified, 32 were retained after screening for language, full text
availability, duplicates, and relevance, and these were analyzed by two authors using content
analysis. Publication output has grown since 2015, with Italy, Spain, and China among the most
active contributing countries. The reviewed literature focuses mainly on waste management,
particularly food waste, and on consumer behavior, while digital technologies are comparatively
underrepresented and studies that link digital and circular strategies directly are scarce. The paper
argues that managing the twin transition in tourism requires coordinated policy, targeted financial
support, training for tourism professionals, and collaboration among destinations, businesses, and
governments. It proposes  future research on skills and competencies for circular and digital
tourism, regional differences, tourist behavior, and the employment effects of the twin transition.
The findings are limited by reliance on a single database and English language publications.

1. Introduction
The tourism industry is a major contributor to pollution, animal welfare issues, and biodiversity
loss (Azam et al., 2018; Saenz -de-Miera & Rosselló, 2014). In addition to generating
approximately 35 million tonnes of solid waste annually, it causes an exc essive demand for both
land and water (Mateu -Sbert et al., 2013). So far, the majority of studies examining the
environmental impacts of the tourism and hospitality sector have concentrated on the industry's
role in accelerating global warming. Water consu mption and food waste are two other critical
factors related to environmental sustainability (Kibler et al., 2018). Consequently, these
environmental impacts are likely to worsen in the coming months and years. Finding solutions to
reduce the environmental impacts of the tourism and hospitality sectors is crucial if these sectors
are to maintain their positive impact on human health and well -being. Protecting the natural
environment is one of the four fundamental principles of sustainable tourism (Wight, 20 02). The
production of waste and its appropriate management are critical markers of environmental health
for the long-term viability of tourism. Tourism businesses are strongly urged to minimise waste
and enhance their waste disposal methods wherever neces sary (Obersteiner et al., 2021; Kasim,
2006). To advance in this domain, it is essential to own industry standards for waste volume and
disposal patterns. These goals must be attained to facilitate the development of waste management
systems that are both effective and efficient. By integrating previous research on how the twin
transition is altering their operational dynamics with current research, this study advances our
understanding of sustainable practices related to tourism. We analyzed data from prio r studies as
operators in the tourism sector must disclose the quantity and type of waste they produce. This
systematic literature review (SLR) aimed at providing tourism businesses, policymakers, and
researchers with insights on effectively targeting reform initiatives.
In the tourism sector, there is a lack of study on the potential uses of the policies, which limits the
industry's ability to apply sustainable practices (Elkhwesky et al., 2024; Prakash et al., 2023;
Streimikiene et al., 2021). It is vital to have a full awareness of the potential applications of the
policies in order to incorporate them into circular activities that are helpful to the environment in
a more effective manner. There is currently no comprehensive assessment of tourism -related
studies that is available to apply circular strategies that are relevant to this area (Gusmerotti et al.,
2024). Consequently, the purpose of this article is to present a comprehensive analysis of the
current condition of the tourism industry in order to illuminate the transition to a digital and circular
strategies that may be applied in accordance with the specific tourists needs.

2. Methodology
An extensive literature study using the method of SLR was conducted. Twin transitions have been
the topic of fragmented research throughout a number of academic fields (Damioli et al., 2025;
Gao, 2025; Collini & Hausemer, 2024). Keyword search string used in the Scopus database was (
"Twin Transition" OR "circular economy" ) AND "tourism".
The refined search, limited to peer -reviewed "Articles," yielded 518 publications. Materials
excluded from the analysis were those not published in English, exclusively technical publications,
or without full text availability. Additionally, we eliminated duplicate documents identified
throughout our search. Each author meticulously reviewed all remaining papers prior to choose
which to include. Upon thorough examination of each document, the sample size was reduced to
32 papers. The documents were subsequently analyzed to provide the requisite data for the study.
2.1 Inclusion/Exclusion Criteria
Giving a thorough description of the criteria used to determine which papers were included and
which were excluded is essential. Both inclusion criteria and exclusion criteria are part of the set
of criteria used to choose primary papers. During the data screening and extraction procedure, the
following criteria are used: only empirical literature from peer -reviewed journals were chosen.
This was carried out in an effort to guarantee the accuracy of the data through a thorough peer
review procedure. This study's scope is restricted only to the tourism industry; research from other
disciplines or sectors is not included. The initial search was not limited to a specific number of
years in order to guarantee that the present review is thorough and considers all of the material that
is currently available. For this effort, only full-length, English-language academic papers that had
undergone peer review were considered. The titles, abstracts, and keywords of every publication
were carefully examined throughout th is screening process to determine their relevance to the
subject area under investigation in this study. Following the investigation, it was discovered that
518 papers might be taken into consideration for the first phase of the literature review. The data
collection analyzed the literature available till October 31, 2025.

3.2 Twin Transition
The digital and green transition, both are termed together as the “twin transition” (Horn & Felt,
2025), has been rapidly embraced across industries. The predominant strategies devised to address
prominent tourism issues, the ramifications of climate change currently rely on technological
innovations (Scott et al., 2012). Technology is w idely recognized as a primary component of in
tourism business (Buhalis, 1998), is influencing the environmental impact of human activity (Li
et al., 2014). Green technological expertise, offering numerous solutions, focuses to aid the
transition from a lo w-carbon economy to zero -carbon emission technology. Researchers concur
that the ongoing digital revolution is altering the environment (Rasul, 2024); nevertheless, much
debate exists over the nature of these changes' evolution. The existing policies signi ficantly
influence the establishment of routes and conditions conducive to the growth of an intelligent and
sustainable tourism industry (Kong et al., 2025).
Within the tourism business, there are a number of ideas that encourage the twin transition in
tourism sectors (Aulisio & Pereno, 2024), it ought to be more thoroughly integrated with
environmental goals, and the actions ought to be put into place in order  to encourage its adoption
in tourism-related sectors as well (Myshko et al., 2024). It is essential to set specific goals for the
adoption of circularity and digitization in the tourism industry. These goals should cover a wide
range of areas, such as was te management, infrastructure, and the manufacturing of transport
vehicles. In recent years, the concept of a CE has garnered significant attention (Massaro et al.,
2021), nevertheless, it existed for a considerable period prior to this new focus. The Ellen
MacArthur Foundation, founded in 2010 to promote innovative concepts and expedite the shift
towards a regenerative and restorative CE (Truong et al., 2024), has signific antly enhanced the
prominence of this term in recent years. The increase in related studies and the research output has
resulted from the foundation's initiatives. Promoting a place's environmental, social, cultural, and
economic qualities in a way that ma kes people want to go back is one of sustainability's main
goals.
It is essential to develop new strategic plans and policies, together with focused financial programs
for the implementation of these initiatives, to enable twin transitions in the tourism business.
Literature suggested that, to include digital and circula r innovations in tourism industry, a
paradigm shift is required (De Martino et al., 2025). It can only happen if practitioners can
successfully turn the industry's mounting problems into monetary, social, and environmental
benefits. By establishing Centers offering training programs specially for twin transition's research
and development, can encourage collaboration amongst the various stakeholders to boost their
effectiveness and sustainability in tourism and its related industry (Buhalis et al, 2023).

Thatcher, 2023), however, numerous opportunities and challenges have emerged due to such

4. Theoretical and Practical Implications
The present linear economy does not promote the recycling, reusing, or recovery of resources, nor
does it effectively optimize materials (Sariatli, 2017). This is why policymakers and other
stakeholders throughout the globe are more interested in the idea of a CE. The industrial sector
has, nevertheless, constituted the bulk of CE studies. Despite being a major source of water and
energy loss, food waste, traffic issues, and carbon dioxide emissions, tourism business has received
surprisingly little attenti on among researchers regarding implementing twin transition in this
industry.
Solid wastes pose a significant threat to the environment (Ma et al., 2022). The term “waste” means
nothing more than the materials that were used to make the things that are thrown away
(Amasuomo & Baird, 2016). Landfills are a major source of greenhouse gas emissions, which in
turn contribute to global warming. Tourism sector is highly concerned  with reducing waste to
make sure its operations are environmentally sustainable. Nothing less than immediate action is
required to ensure the achievement of the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). To make the
tourism business more eco -friendly, governme nts should promote the development and use of
renewable energy sources. The administrative costs caused by bureaucracy should be reduced as
a secondary goal of policy initiatives. In order to make the shift to CE model work, the tourism
industry needs to become more socially conscious, and awareness campaigns could help get them
there. Participation is necessary from every level of society, including governments, travel
agencies, and individuals.

5. Conclusion
All parties involved, including consumers, employees, the government, the general public, other
nations, and future generations have a stake in the tourism industry's success. Travelers may shed
light on various aspects of contemporary culture, such as how  individuals organize their leisure

A robust start-up ecosystem for circular tourism is much needed to address various circular goals

5.1 Limitations of the Study
The current study contains a few limitations. A qualitative study of this nature may reveal the
researchers' biases and preconceived notions on the subject matter. Researchers may exhibit biases
at various phases of the data analysis process due to the int rinsic characteristics of this
methodology.
This study is limited by extracting data only from the Scopus database. Although it is a highly
regarded database with extensive coverage recognized by the academic community, future
research should replicate the study using other databases, such as Web of Science or PubMed, and
should include publications in languages other than English to improve the comprehensiveness of
this research domain.
The content analysis revealed topics which need more in-depth analyses in future research, as well
as studies that address the shortcomings of the current study by incorporating a wider range of
journals over a longer period of time, looking at scholarly c ontributions through citations, and
providing different representations of the literature. We suggest starting a research initiative to
help tourism businesses undergo digital transformation. This agenda's primary objectives should
be to develop a theoreti cal framework and gather empirical inputs, with an emphasis on the
characteristics that set tourism and hospitality businesses apart and impede the use of emerging
twin transition in this industry.

The current study aims to give future scholars theoretical guidance to help them better direct their

research avenue's focus is on understanding the abilities and credentials needed to achieve the CE.
