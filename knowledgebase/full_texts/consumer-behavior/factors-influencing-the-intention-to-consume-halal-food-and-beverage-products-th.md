ABSTRACT
Purpose – This study aims to examine the factors influencing awareness, attitudes,
and intention to consume halal food and beverage products through online ordering
applications, particularly among Muslim millennials in Kendari City.
Design – This research employs a quantitative approach using a survey method.
Data were collected through questionnaires distributed to 96 Muslim millennial
respondents in Kendari City, selected using purposive sampling. The data were
analyzed using the Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM)
technique to assess the relationships among religiosity, product knowledge,
awareness, attitude, and intention.
Findings – The results indicate that awareness and attitude have a significant
positive effect on the intention to consume halal products online. Religiosity and
product knowledge do not directly influence intention. However, both religiosity and
product knowledge significantly affect awareness. Furthermore, religiosity and
awareness significantly influence attitudes, while product knowledge does not show a
significant effect on attitudes.
Research Implications – The findings suggest that halal certification authorities,
online food ordering platforms, and halal food vendors should strengthen consumer
awareness by improving the visibility of halal certification, providing transparent
product information, and promoting halal literacy through digital channels.
Enhancing consumers’ awareness and attitudes toward halal products may increase
their intention to purchase halal food and beverages online. However, the study is
limited by its relatively small sample size of 96 respondents and its focus on Muslim
millennials in Kendari City, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to
other populations and geographical contexts.
Originality – This study provides empirical insights into the behavioral
determinants of halal consumption in the context of digital platforms, highlighting
the mediating roles of awareness and attitude. It contributes to the growing literature
on halal consumer behavior by focusing on Muslim millennials and the use of online
food ordering applications, particularly in a regional Indonesian context.

INTRODUCTION
Halal and haram are legal rulings in Islamic law established by Allah SWT in the Qur'an
and the Sunnah of His Messenger. Halal refers to anything that is permitted by Allah SWT to be
done, used, or consumed. Halal food is food that complies with Islamic teachings, namely halalan
tayyiban (lawful and wholesome). Every halal food is inherently good, but not all good food is
necessarily halal. Therefore, in order for Muslims to avoid consuming what is haram, they should
always ensure that the food they consume is halal. Halal is a term derived from Arabic which
means permissible and in accordance with Islamic law. In the Qur'an, specifically in Surah Al-
Baqarah verse 168, Allah commands humankind to consume halal food:

“O mankind! Eat from whatever is on earth that is lawful and good, and do not follow the footsteps of
Satan. Indeed, he is a clear enemy to you.” (Q.S. Al-Baqarah: 168).

From this verse, the Qur’an calls upon all humankind to eat and drink from what Allah
SWT has provided on earth that is lawful. Halal food refers to food that is not prohibited,
meaning that Islamic law does not forbid its consumption. Haram food is divided into two types:
intrinsically forbidden substances, such as pork, carrion, blood, and intoxicants; and food that is
essentially lawful in substance but becomes unlawful due to the way it is obtained or processed.
Therefore, the verse not only commands the consumption of halal food but also emphasizes that
it must be tayyib (good and wholesome), because not all halal food is necessarily beneficial for
human beings.
The halal product market is one of the fastest-growing markets in the world. This is
highlighted in the State of the Global Islamic Economy 2021/2022 , which reports that the global
Muslim population has reached 2.08 billion, with the global halal market demand amounting to
approximately USD 150 billion annually.
According to data from the Ministry of Home Affairs of Indonesia, the Muslim
population in Indonesia reached 237.53 million people as of December 31, 2021, representing
86.9% of the total population of 273.23 million. Meanwhile, the population of Kendari City is
approximately 359 thousand people, of which around 34% belong to the millennial generation.
The millennial generation refers to individuals born between 1981 and 1994, with a population
share of about 25.87%, or roughly 70 million people. This makes millennials a key target market
in the marketing world, as they tend to have a more consumptive lifestyle and already possess
their own income. A survey conducted by the global research institution Measurable AI reported
that 57% of Gen Z and millennials frequently purchase food from outside rather than cooking at
home or consuming available meals. They also tend to make purchases through online food
ordering applications.
Since the implementation of social restrictions during the COVID-19 pandemic,
consumer behavior has shifted from on-the-spot purchases to online transactions, particularly in
the purchase of food and beverages. According to Nova (2022), sales in the food and beverage
category at the beginning of 2021 reached 247,538 transactions in marketplaces. In 2020, the
transaction value for food and beverage purchases through applications reached USD 3.7 billion,
dominated by Grab Food at 53% and Gojek’s GoFood at 47%. In the same year, there were 16.4
million digitally connected culinary MSMEs in Indonesia. Over the past few decades,
technological development has become unavoidable, as it has penetrated nearly all aspects of
human life, including the MSME sector in food and beverages. The emergence of technology has
reduced the need for conventional stores or kiosks, giving rise instead to online shops that make
it easier for consumers to purchase any halal food and beverages they desire through online
ordering applications.
According to the consultancy Dinar Standard, Indonesia’s consumption of halal products
is projected to reach USD 281.6 billion (equivalent to IDR 4,033 trillion) by 2025. Furthermore,
halal product consumption is expected to grow at an average rate of 14.96% annually. To achieve
this target, it is necessary to accelerate efforts by identifying the factors that influence consumers’
interest in consuming halal products. By understanding consumer behavior, policymakers and

Icek Ajzen and Martin Fishbein (1975) explain human behavior through the Theory of
Planned Behavior (TPB), which suggests that an individual’s behavior can be predicted by
behavioral intention, jointly influenced by attitude, subjective norms, and perceived behavioral
control. In efforts to sustain the target of halal consumption in Indonesia, public awareness of
the importance of consuming halal food and beverages is a key indicator in the adoption of halal
practices. Therefore, the government and Badan Penyelenggara Jaminan Produk Halal need to
pay close attention to consumer behavior in order to understand how public awareness
influences the intention to consume halal food and beverage products.
Religiosity describes the extent to which an individual adheres to religious teachings in
daily life (Mansour & Diab, 2016). In this study, religiosity refers to how a person remains
obedient and committed to the rules and teachings of Islamic law and implements them in
everyday life, particularly in consuming food and beverage products. Religiosity has attracted
both academic and practical attention. From an academic perspective, it has been widely studied
and developed, while in practice, especially in the consumption of halal food and beverages,
religiosity has become an important consideration in marketing strategies. The relationship
between religiosity and intention has been discussed in several previous studies. Louis Delener
(1990) found that culture influenced by religious values significantly affects consumer behavior,
which in turn shapes shopping behavior or consumers’ intention to make purchases.
Furthermore, Janah et al. (2020) found that religion is the most significant factor influencing the
intention of religious leaders in their decision to use Islamic banking products and services. In
addition to religiosity, intention can also be influenced by halal product knowledge.
Product knowledge refers to the collection of various types of information that
consumers possess about a product, such as product categories, brands, product terms, attributes
or features, prices, and trust in the product (Nurhayati & Hendar, 2020). In the context of halal
products, halal product knowledge is the ability of individuals to understand the principles of
halal and haram in determining raw materials, processing, and distribution of products. The
relationship between halal product knowledge and the intention to consume halal products has
not been widely explored in previous studies. Azlan et al. (2015) found that halal product
knowledge is related to attitudes toward halal products. Good halal product knowledge can
positively influence attitudes and provide individuals with greater confidence and comfort in
making decisions. Shim et al. (2010) argued that halal product knowledge plays an important role
in predicting attitudes toward halal products, which ultimately leads to healthier financial
behavior. Furthermore, Albaity and Rahman (2019) found that halal product knowledge has a
significant relationship with behavior toward Islamic banking. This means that the higher an
individual’s halal product knowledge, the stronger their behavioral intention toward halal
products. In addition to halal product knowledge, another factor that influences intention to use
Islamic banking products is halal awareness.
Awareness refers to the level at which an individual recognizes the existence of a brand
within the category of halal products (Hasan, 2015). It also represents a condition in which
consumers are familiar with the characteristics, design, and services of a product, as explained by
Kevin Lane Keller (1993). Awareness is the ability to understand, perceive, and recognize an
event or object. In the context of marketing, awareness is hypothesized to play an important role
in determining consumers’ intention to choose a particular product, as noted by Aziz and Chok
(2013). Awareness of food and beverage products plays a very important role and should be a key
consideration for Muslim entrepreneurs in penetrating the Muslim market. The relationship
between awareness and intention toward halal products has received attention from previous
researchers. Maria et al. (2020) stated that consumers tend to purchase products they are already
familiar with, as they are generally reluctant to try new products and prefer those with well-
known brands and proven quality. A lack of public awareness of halal products may hinder the

From the existing phenomena and issues, it can be observed that only a limited number
of food and beverage businesses have obtained halal certification, and online food ordering

that producers and online application providers are not yet able to fully guarantee the halal status

Muslim and are increasingly aware of the need for halal assurance in the products they consume.
Therefore, it is necessary to conduct a more in-depth analysis of the factors that influence the
intention of Muslim millennial consumers to purchase food and beverages through online
ordering applications, so that the findings can serve as a reference for food and beverage
entrepreneurs.
Previous studies on the intention to consume halal products have been widely conducted;
however, most of them have focused on halal product consumption in general and the use of
Islamic banking products, leaving limited research that specifically examines the intention of
Muslim millennials to consume halal food and beverage products through online ordering
applications. Several earlier studies highlighted the influence of religiosity on consumer behavior
and the intention to use Islamic products and services, such as the studies conducted by Louis
Delener (1990) and Janah et al. (2020), but they did not specifically relate religiosity to digital-
based halal consumption behavior. In addition, studies on halal product knowledge have mainly
discussed its influence on attitudes and behavior toward halal products and Islamic banking, as
found by Azlan et al. (2015) and Albaity and Rahman (2019), yet only a few studies have
examined its effect on the intention to purchase halal food and beverage products through online
platforms.
Research related to halal awareness has generally focused on brand recognition and
purchasing behavior toward conventional halal products, while studies examining halal awareness
in the context of online food ordering services remain limited. Furthermore, the rapid
development of digital food ordering services has not been fully supported by the consistent
provision of halal labels on online platforms, creating uncertainty among Muslim consumers.
Therefore, this study aims to fill the existing research gap by analyzing the influence of religiosity,
halal product knowledge, and halal awareness on the intention of Muslim millennial consumers in
Kendari City to consume halal food and beverage products through online ordering services.
This study employs an extended Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) approach by
positioning attitude as the main construct in shaping consumption intention, while religiosity,
halal product knowledge, and halal awareness are positioned as antecedent variables that
indirectly influence intention through the formation of consumers’ attitudes and perceptions
toward online halal food consumption. The development of this TPB model is based on the
assumption that halal consumption behavior, particularly among Muslim millennials in the digital
era, cannot be fully explained solely through the original TPB constructs such as attitude,
subjective norm, and perceived behavioral control. In the context of halal consumption through
online platforms, religious values, the level of understanding of halal products, and halal
awareness are contextual factors that play an important role in influencing consumers’ decision-
making processes. Therefore, this study extends the TPB framework by integrating religiosity and
halal product knowledge as relevant contextual predictors to explain halal consumption intention
more comprehensively, so that the proposed model is expected to provide a stronger theoretical
explanation of halal consumer behavior in the digital era.

research is the individual, as it reflects responses from each research subject on an individual

SmartPLS is utilized to assess convergent validity, discriminant validity, and the reliability of the
research instruments. Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) in this study is conducted using the
PLS approach. PLS is a powerful multivariate analysis method aimed at examining the
relationships among the variables in this study. It is also combined with descriptive analysis to
explain the loading values of each indicator (manifest variable) within the SEM-PLS model. PLS
is a Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) technique based on multivariate analysis that is robust
for theory confirmation, offering analytical flexibility and broad applicability, as noted by Jörg
Henseler et al. (2016). In addition, PLS has advantages in integrating various statistical procedures
for parameter estimation and hypothesis testing, as explained by Joseph F. Hair et al. (2017).

Discussion
The results of the inferential statistical analysis provide a comprehensive understanding of
the relationships among the variables examined in this study. Religiosity was found to have no
direct positive and significant effect on intention, as indicated by a path coefficient of 0.000, a t-
statistic below the critical value (0.005 < 1.960), and a p-value of 0.996. This suggests that the
level of adherence of Muslim millennials in Kendari City to Islamic teachings does not directly
influence their intention to consume halal food and beverage products through online ordering
applications. Similarly, product knowledge does not significantly affect intention, as reflected by a
path coefficient of 0.054, a t-statistic of 0.551 (< 1.960), and a p-value of 0.582, indicating that
knowledge of halal food and beverage criteria alone is insufficient to drive consumption
intention.
In contrast, halal awareness and attitude were found to have positive and significant
effects on intention. Halal awareness demonstrates a path coefficient of 0.447, a t-statistic of
3.028 (> 1.960), and a p-value of 0.002, indicating that increased awareness significantly enhances
the intention to consume halal products. Likewise, attitude shows a path coefficient of 0.434, a t-
statistic of 3.474 (> 1.960), and a p-value of 0.001, suggesting that a more favorable evaluation of
halal products leads to stronger consumption intention. Furthermore, religiosity significantly
influences both awareness and attitude, with path coefficients of 0.598 (t = 4.449; p = 0.000) and
0.383 (t = 2.497; p = 0.013), respectively. These findings imply that higher levels of religiosity

Regarding indirect effects, the findings reveal important mediating roles of awareness and
attitude. Halal awareness significantly mediates the relationship between religiosity and intention
(β = 0.267; t = 2.324; p = 0.020), indicating that religiosity influences intention indirectly through
increased awareness. Similarly, awareness also mediates the relationship between product
knowledge and intention (β = 0.155; t = 2.383; p = 0.017), acting as a partial mediator. In
addition, attitude significantly mediates the effect of religiosity on intention (β = 0.166; t = 2.162;
p = 0.031), suggesting that religiosity can enhance intention through the formation of positive
attitudes. However, attitude does not mediate the relationship between product knowledge and
intention (β = 0.029; t = 0.673; p = 0.501), indicating that knowledge alone is insufficient to
shape intention through attitudinal pathways. Overall, these findings highlight that awareness and
attitude are key determinants of intention, while religiosity and product knowledge exert their
influence primarily through indirect mechanisms.
The finding that religiosity does not directly influence intention is particularly noteworthy.
From the perspective of the Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB), intention is more proximally
determined by cognitive evaluations and attitudes toward a specific behavior than by broader
personal values or beliefs. Although religiosity reflects an individual's commitment to Islamic
teachings, such commitment does not automatically translate into a stronger intention to
purchase halal food through online platforms. In the context of digital food ordering, consumers
may face additional considerations such as convenience, price, product availability, platform
usability, and trust in vendors. Consequently, religiosity alone may not be sufficient to directly
shape purchase intention. This finding is consistent with several studies on halal consumer
behavior which suggest that religiosity often exerts its influence indirectly through psychological
mechanisms such as halal awareness, attitudes, and perceptions rather than through a direct
pathway to behavioral intention. Similarly, product knowledge was found to have no significant
direct effect on intention.
This result suggests that possessing knowledge about halal food principles, ingredients,
and certification procedures does not necessarily motivate consumers to purchase halal products
through online applications. According to TPB, knowledge serves as a background factor that

purchasing decisions. Therefore, knowledge becomes meaningful only when it increases
consumers’ awareness of the importance of halal consumption and shapes favorable attitudes
toward halal products. This finding supports previous studies indicating that knowledge alone is
insufficient to drive behavioral intention unless it is accompanied by stronger cognitive and
affective evaluations of the behavior.
The significant effects of halal awareness and attitude on intention are also consistent
with TPB. Halal awareness represents consumers’ cognitive recognition of the importance of
complying with halal principles, while attitude reflects their overall evaluation of halal products.
As proposed by TPB, attitude is one of the most immediate predictors of intention because it
captures individuals’ positive or negative assessments of performing a behavior. Likewise, greater
halal awareness increases consumers’ sensitivity to halal-related issues and strengthens their
motivation to choose halal products. These findings suggest that awareness and attitude are more
powerful predictors of intention because they are closer to the actual decision-making process
than more distal factors such as religiosity and product knowledge.

indirectly by increasing halal awareness and fostering more positive attitudes toward halal
products. Likewise, product knowledge contributes to intention primarily through enhanced

CONCLUSION
Based on the findings of this study, it can be concluded that religiosity and product
knowledge do not have a direct significant effect on the intention to consume halal food and
beverage products through online applications. In contrast, halal awareness and attitude have
been proven to exert a positive and significant influence on intention, making them the main
determinants in shaping halal consumption interest. Furthermore, religiosity and product
knowledge significantly influence awareness, while religiosity also has a significant effect on
attitude; however, product knowledge does not significantly affect attitude. Awareness is also
found to have a significant influence on attitude, thereby reinforcing its role as a key variable
within the research model.
Moreover, the mediation analysis reveals that halal awareness significantly mediates the
relationship between religiosity and product knowledge on intention. Likewise, attitude is shown
to play a significant mediating role in the relationship between religiosity and intention. These
findings indicate that although religiosity and knowledge do not directly affect intention, both
variables still make an important indirect contribution, particularly through enhancing halal
awareness and fostering a positive attitude toward halal products. Therefore, awareness and
attitude function as intervening variables that strengthen the relationships among the constructs
examined in this study.
Based on these findings, it is recommended that the government and related institutions
in Kendari City strengthen education and socialization programs regarding halal products and the
halal certification process for both the public and business actors, as well as accelerate the equal
distribution of halal certification. Online ordering service providers should also ensure that their
business partners possess halal certification and clearly display halal labels on their platforms. In
addition, future researchers are encouraged to expand this study by incorporating other relevant
variables in order to enrich the understanding of halal consumption behavior, particularly within
the digital context.
