Abstract
The food technology industry is increasingly adopting digital innovations, where the
strategic use of big data is becoming essential for optimizing market strategies. This study
examined the implementation of big data in marketing strategies and its impact on the
marketing performance of food technology products, as well as the moderating role played
by consumer behavior. Big data has the potential to provide deeper insights into consumer
behavior and preferences, allowing companies to design more personalized and effective
marketing strategies. The method used in this study is a quantitative cross-sectional design
approach, involving 100 respondents of food technology product consumers selected
using convenience sampling. Data were gathered using a Google Form survey shared
through WhatsApp. Data analysis used structural equation modeling (SEM) with Warp -
PLS. The results showed that big data had a significant effect on the marketing strategy of
food technology products. However, the effect of big data on marketing performance was
not significant. In addition, the marketing strategy was found to have a substantial impact
on marketing performance. This study also found that consumer behavior functions as a
moderating variable that affects the effect of big data on marketing strategy and marketing
performance (moderation coefficient 0.271, p-value < 0.05). These findings indicated that
the success of implementing big data in marketing food technology products is greatly
influenced by consumer response to the strategy implemented. Overall, this investigation
gives important insights for companies to utilize big data in formulating more effective
marketing strategies, as well as adjusting marketing approaches to the dynamics of
consumer behavior that continues to change.
1. Introduction
The development of information and communication
technology (ICT) has brought significant changes to
various sectors, including the food sector. Food
technology products, which include innovations in the
production, distribution, and consumption processes of
food, are increasingly in demand by consumers who are
increasingly aware of the importance of food quality and
sustainability (Ratu et al., 2023; Galanakis, 2024; Lisboa
et al., 2024). In response to this demand, food companies
are trying to increase their competitiveness through
product innovations based on the latest technology (Bua-
in, 2024). One factor that plays a major role in changing
the marketing landscape of food products is big data. Big
data refers to a very large and diverse volume of data
that can be analyzed to gain deeper insights and assist
decision-making (Theodorakopoulos et al., 2024). In the
context of marketing, big data provides an opportunity to
better understand consumer behavior, market
preferences, and emerging trends, all of which can be
used to design more targeted and effective marketing
strategies (Cavlak and Cop, 2021; Mukhopadhyay et al.,
2024).
The application of big data in marketing is not only
limited to data collection and analysis (Brewis  et al .,
2023) but also to the ability to integrate information from
various sources, such as transaction data, social media
data, consumer behavior data, and sensor data, to create a
more personalized and relevant consumer experience

2. Materials and methods
2.1 Research design
This study employed a cross -sectional research
design (Magano et al ., 2024; Pérez -Guerrero et al .,
2024), which allowed for the collection of data at a
single point in time. This approach provides a clear
snapshot of the relationship between Big Data
implementation in marketing and its impact on
marketing performance through consumer perceptions of
food technology products.
2.2 Population and sample
The population in this study consisted of consumers
who had purchased or used food technology products.
The sample size was 100 respondents, selected using a
convenience sampling technique (Golzar et al ., 2022;
Giri, 2023). This sampling method was employed to
facilitate efficient data collection from readily available
consumers, considering limitations of time and
resources. The selected respondents provided
information about their experiences with food
technology products and their views on the application of
big data in marketing.
2.3 Research variables
2.3.1 Independent variable
Implementation of Big Data (X1) refers to the
strategic utilization of analytical technologies (including
big data tools and related infrastructures). In the
marketing of food technology products. This utilization
is performed specifically to derive insights for
understanding consumer behavior, enabling
personalization, and executing market segmentation.
2.3.2 Dependent variable
The principal variables of this investigation are
defined based on the study ’s theoretical framework and
measurement scales. Marketing Strategy (Y1) is the
strategic Implementation of marketing actions generated
based on insights from big data, specifically
encompassing market segmentation, customized
offerings, and product positioning. Marketing
Performance (Y2) is a measure of marketing
effectiveness quantified through key outcome variables
such as sales volume, customer loyalty, and brand
awareness.

2.3.3 Moderating variable
Consumer Behavior (Z) is defined as the consumer’s
view and responses to big data -based marketing efforts,
this variable acts as a mediator by influencing the
relationship between big data implementation and
marketing results.
2.4 Data collection
The research instrument was a structured
questionnaire distributed to respondents electronically
via Google Forms and WhatsApp. The questionnaire was
designed to measure each research variable using a five -
point Likert scale ranging from strongly disagree to
strongly agree. The questionnaire collected data focusing
on key study variables, including consumer perceptions
of the implementation of big data in marketing food
technology products, subsequent consumer responses to
marketing strategies implemented based on big data
analysis and the resultant marketing performance of food
technology products as perceived by consumers (e.g.,
brand awareness, loyalty, and purchase levels).
2.5 Data evaluation
2.5.1 Data analysis technique
Data analysis was carried out using Structural
Equation Modeling (SEM) with the assistance of the
Warp-PLS software. This approach allowed for the
simultaneous testing of the complex relationships
between the latent and manifest variables hypothesized
in the study.
2.5.2 Measurement model assessment
Before testing the structural model, the instrument ’s
quality was assessed using validity and reliability tests.
Validity testing was conducted by examining the loading
factor value for each indicator. Reliability testing was
conducted using the composite reliability (CR) and
average variance extracted (AVE) values to ensure the
indicators validly represented the measured constructs
2.5.3 Structural equation model assessment
SEM analysis was applied to test the hypothesized
relationships between the variables in the structural
model. The relationship between variables was analyzed
to identify direct, indirect, and total influences. The
bootstrapping technique was used to measure the
significance of the relationships by examining the p -
value, with a threshold of p < 0.05 indicating
significance. The final results were interpreted to
determine the overall effect of Big Data implementation
on marketing performance.

4. Discussion
4.1 The influence of big data on marketing strategy
The results of this study show that the influence of
big data on marketing strategy has a coefficient of 0.82
with a probability value of 0.001 < 0.05. This shows that
there is a significant influence of big data
implementation on food technology product marketing
strategies. The coefficient of 0.82 shows a fairly strong
and positive relationship between the implementation of
big data and food technology product marketing
strategies. This means that the greater the application of
big data in product marketing, the greater the impact on
the marketing strategy implemented by the company. In
this context, a coefficient of 0.82 indicates that big data
contributes significantly to increasing the effectiveness
and sophistication of marketing strategies used by food
technology product companies. This figure shows that
the implementation of big data can enhance the
company's capacity to adjust marketing strategies to
consumer needs and preferences that are more precise
and more measurable. The probability value (p -value) of
0.001 indicates that this result is very statistically
significant (Bonovas and Piovani, 2023). In general, if
the p -value is less than 0.05, this indicates that the
relationship or influence between two variables (in this
case, between big data and marketing strategy) is
significant.
With the application of big data, companies can
utilize more in -depth information about consumer
behavior, preferences, and market trends to design more
personalized, targeted, and effective marketing
strategies. These results also indicate that companies that
implement big data technology in their marketing
strategies have a competitive advantage, because they
can make smarter marketing decisions based on accurate
data.
4.2 The influence of big data on marketing performance
  The results of this study show that the influence of
big data on marketing performance has a coefficient of
0.08 with a probability value of 0.24> 0.05. This shows
that there is no significant effect of big data
implementation on the marketing performance of food
technology products. The coefficient of 0.08 indicates a
very weak positive relationship between big data
implementation and food technology product marketing
performance. This means that although there is an
influence between these two variables, the magnitude of
the influence is very small. This low coefficient indicates
that the implementation of big data does not have a
significant impact on improving marketing performance
(such as sales, customer loyalty, or brand awareness) on
the food technology products studied. The p -value of
0.24 indicates that this result is not statistically
significant because the p -value is greater than 0.05. In
general, if the p -value is greater than 0.05, it cannot
reject the null hypothesis, which states that there is no
significant influence between these variables (Kwak,
2023; Nakas and Verykouki, 2023). With a p -value of
0.24, it can be concluded that there is insufficient
evidence to support the claim that the implementation of
big data has a significant influence on the marketing
performance of food technology products. This means
that, based on the existing data, big data does not play a
significant role in improving the marketing performance
of food technology products in the context of this study.
Based on the coefficient and p -value, there is no
meaningful connection between the application of big
data and the marketing performance of food technology
products in this study. Although the application of big
data can give more in-depth information about consumer
behavior or market trends, in this case, the results of the
study indicate that the data does not contribute enough to
improving marketing performance directly.
Companies may not have utilized big data in an
effective way in their marketing strategy. The data
collected may not be managed or analyzed in a way that
provides useful insights to change or improve marketing
performance. Marketing performance may be more
influenced by other factors that are not directly related to
big data, such as product quality, traditional promotions,
or emotional aspects of consumer relationships that
cannot be fully explained by big data. Perhaps the
companies surveyed are still in the early stages of
adopting big data and have not fully integrated the
findings from big data into their marketing practices.
More mature adoption may be needed to see a more
significant impact.

4.3 The impact of marketing strategy on marketing
performance
The outcomes of this investigation show that the
impact of marketing strategy on marketing performance
has a coefficient of 0.78 with a probability value of 0.001
< 0.05. This indicates that there is a significant influence
of marketing strategy on the marketing performance of
food technology products. The coefficient of 0.78
indicates that there is a strong and significant positive
relationship between marketing strategy and marketing
performance of food technology products. This high
coefficient value (0.78) indicates that changes in the
implementation of marketing strategy will have a major
impact on marketing outcomes or performance, such as
increased sales, customer loyalty, or brand awareness. In
other words, the more effective and targeted the
marketing strategy implemented, the greater its influence
on achieving better marketing performance. In this
context, a positive influence of 0.78 indicates that a well-
designed and properly implemented marketing strategy
can significantly improve the marketing performance of
food technology products. The p-value of 0.001 indicates
that the relationship between marketing strategy and
marketing performance is statistically significant. A p -
value of less than 0.05 means that there is very strong
evidence to reject the null hypothesis (which states that
there is no influence between the variables) (Kwak,
2023), and conversely, the alternative hypothesis stating
that there is a significant influence of marketing strategy
on marketing performance can be accepted. With a p -
value of 0.001, which is much smaller than 0.05, we can
conclude that the influence of marketing strategy on the
marketing performance of food technology products is
significant. This means that the results found did not
occur by chance or randomly, but there is indeed a real
relationship between the implementation of marketing
strategies and increased marketing performance.
This strong relationship between marketing strategy
and marketing performance emphasizes the importance
of designing and implementing marketing strategies that
are in accordance with consumer needs and preferences
as well as dynamic market conditions. Data -driven and
adaptive marketing strategies (e.g., big data analytics,
digital marketing, or product personalization) can help
companies focus more on meeting consumer needs,
which in turn will have a positive effect on marketing
performance.
For companies, these results indicate that investing
in designing the right marketing strategy plays a crucial
role in company performance. In food technology
products, implementing a smarter, data-driven marketing
strategy that is tailored to market preferences can be the
key to achieving better results, both in terms of sales and
in building long -term relationships with consumers. For
decision makers in companies, it is important to continue
to develop and evaluate marketing strategies to remain
relevant and effective in the face of changing consumer
preferences and competition in the market. Through data
-based evaluation and adjustment, companies can
continue to improve marketing performance sustainably.

5. Conclusion
This study confirms the significant positive influence
of Big Data on marketing strategy, which in turn leads to
improved marketing performance in the food technology
sector. Crucially, the research identified consumer
behavior as a significant moderating barrier,
demonstrating that the expected benefits of Big Data for
personalization can be limited by irrational psychological
and social factors. To maximize Big Data ’s potential,
companies must move beyond mere data collection and
focus on strategically integrating insights across all
operational elements (product quality, service, and
distribution), thereby creating campaigns that are both
highly personalized and adaptive to consumer nuances.
Based on these findings, successful Big Data
implementation requires a holistic approach focused on
flexibility and trust. Companies should supplement
quantitative methods to better understand consumer
motivations. Furthermore, establishing transparent data
governance policies to build consumer trust, coupled
with continuous performance monitoring via real -time
dashboards, is essential. Ultimately, organizations must
cultivate a proactive data -driven culture by investing in
continuous training, ensuring that marketing teams can
translate robust analytics into agile, effective, and ethical
decision-making.
