ABSTRACT
This study examines how research on green purchasing and
consumption is positioned within broader sustainable
economic discourse through a bibliometric systematic review.
Using the PRISMA 2020 framework, 86 peer -reviewed open -
access journal articles indexed in Scopus (2016 –2026) were
systematically identified and analyzed. Bibliometric mapping
with VOSviewer  was employed to explore keyword co -
occurrence, thematic clustering, density structures, and
temporal evolution. The findings show a marked increase in
publications after 2020, indicating growing scholarly attention
to linking consumer behavior with sustainability -oriented
economic discussions. Network and density visualizations
identify green economy and consumption behavior as
dominant thematic anchors, while green purchase intention
and consumer attitude remain central explanatory constructs.
Overlay ana lysis reveals a progression from macro -level
sustainability concerns toward behaviorally oriented and
empirically refined investigations. Despite these connections,
the literature exhibits a structural imbalance. Micro -level
behavioral models are more developed than macro -level
economic transformation frameworks. The study highlights the
need to integrate institutional mechanisms, governance
structures, and production –consumption linkages to better
align consumer behavior research with broader sustainable
economic transitions.

INTRODUCTION
Green purchase behavior refers to consumers’ willingness to select products that are
environmentally friendly or less harmful to the natural environment. Over the past decade,
research on green purchasing has expanded  substantially as scholars seek to understand how
individual consumption decisions contribute to environmental sustainability (Joshi & Rahman,
2015). Prior reviews identify several consistent determinants, including environmental concern,

attitudes toward green products, subjective norms, perceived behavioral control, and product -
related characteristics (Joshi & Rahman, 2015; Biswas & Roy, 2015). Empirical studies across
different contexts confirm that attitudinal factors and perceived behavioral control si gnificantly
predict purchase intentions, although their influence varies across cultural and economic
settings (Liobikienė et al., 2016). More recent research extends this framework by incorporating
environmental knowledge, prosocial values, and trust, further strengthening pro -environmental
purchasing outcomes (do Paço et al., 2019; Naz et al., 2020). In this study, green purchasing and
green consumption are treated as closely related constructs within the broader domain of pro -
environmental consumer behav ior, while green purchase intention is considered a key
explanatory construct frequently used to predict actual purchasing outcomes.
Beyond individual -level determinants, recent empirical evidence highlights the role of
moral norms, perceived consumer effectiveness, and value orientations in reinforcing
environmentally responsible purchasing. Han (2020) demonstrates that moral obligation and
environmental concern significantly enhance intention formation within an extended Theory of
Planned Behavior framewo rk. Similarly, Wang et al. (2018) find that perceived consumer
effectiveness mediates the relationship between environmental knowledge and actual
consumption practices. These findings indicate that green purchasing is not merely an attitudinal
outcome but is embedded in broader cognitive and normative structures shaping pro -
environmental decision-making
Despite this growing body of literature, scholars continue to document an intention –
behavior gap in green purchasing. Consumers may express strong pro -environmental intentions
but fail to translate them into actual buying behavior due to perceived risk, higher prices, limited
product availability, or skepticism toward environmental claims (Gleim et al., 2013). Nguyen et al.
(2017) further demonstrate that situational constraints and price sensitivity significantly weaken
the conversion of green purchase intention into real behavior. This persistent gap indicates that
psychological determinants alone are insufficient to explain green purchasing outcomes.
Structural conditions, market transparency, regulatory frameworks, and supply -side dynamics
must also be considered.
At a broader level, discussions on sustainable economic development emphasize the
transformation of production and consumption systems toward environmentally responsible
models (Loiseau et al., 2016). The concept of sustainable consumption and production (SCP)
underscores that consumer demand plays a central role in redirecting economic systems toward
sustainability (Mont & Plepys, 2008). From this  perspective, green purchase behavior represents
a micro -level mechanism that supports macro -level economic transformation. Sustainable
economic discourse highlights systemic change involving institutional reform, technological
innovation, circular economy principles, and shifts in market structures (Kirchherr et al., 2017).
The transition toward sustainable economic systems therefore requires alignment between
consumer preferences, corporate strategies, and policy interventions.
Sustainability transition t heory further explains that changes in consumer practices are
embedded within wider socio -technical systems that shape economic activities and market
structures (Geels, 2011). Complementing this perspective, Evans et al. (2017) argue that
sustainable consumption must be understood not only as individual choice but also as socially
organized practice influenced by infrastructures, cultural norms, and policy environments. In this
context, green purchasing behavior becomes part of a broader socio -economic tran sition
process rather than an isolated consumer act.
Although green purchase behavior has been widely examined at the micro level and
sustainable economic transformation has been extensively discussed at the macro level, these
two strands of research are often treated separately. Consumer behavior studies typically focus
on individual motivations and decision -making processes, whereas sustainable economic
discourse centers on structural reforms and systemic change. Despite the growing volume of
studies, lim ited synthesis systematically examines how research on green purchasing and

Recent research reinforces the importance of bridging this divide. For instance, Sun et al.
(2019) show that institutional trust and regulatory support significantly shape consumers’ green

determinants. Similarly, Awan et al.  (2019) demonstrate that supply chain integration and

consumer purchasing patterns. These findings suggest that green purchase behavior operates
within interconnected economic, institutional, and production systems.
Given this fragmentation, a structured and integrative review is necessary to clarify how
existing scholarship positions green purchasing and consumption within broader sustainable
economic discourse. This study adopts a bibliometric systematic review approach, combining
PRISMA-based study selection with bibliometric mapping techniques using VOSviewer. By
systematically constructing the dataset and analyzing keyword co -occurrence, thematic
clustering, and temporal evolution, the study maps the intellectual structure of the field and
highlights the micro–macro sustainability gap that characterizes current research.

METHODS
This study employs a bibliometric systematic literature review approach, integrating
systematic review procedures with bibliometric mapping techniques. A systematic literature
review (SLR) is designed to systematically identify, assess, and synthesize relevant scholarly
evidence in order to address the research question and provide an in -depth analysis of the
findings (Snyder, 2019; Xiao & Watson, 2019). In addition to qualitative synthesis, this study
incorporates bibliometric analysis to examine structural relationships and thematic patterns
within the literature. The PRISMA 2020 (Preferre d Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and
Meta-Analyses) guidelines were adopted to ensure a transparent and systematic review process.
PRISMA 2020 provides a structured framework for identifying, screening, evaluating, and
reporting relevant studies in systematic literature reviews, thereby enhancing methodological
transparency and reproducibility.

The study selection followed four stages in accordance with the PRISMA frame work:
identification, screening, eligibility, and inclusion. After retrieving the initial records from the
databases, duplicate entries were removed. Titles and abstracts were subsequently screened
based on predefined eligibility criteria. Full -text articles were then assessed to determine their
conceptual alignment, geographical relevance, and methodological clarity. Studies that did not
meet the inclusion criteria were excluded, and reasons for exclusion were documented.

RESULTS
The study selection process followed the PRISMA 2020 framework (Haddaway et al., 2022),
ensuring transparency and reproducibility in article identification, screening, eligibility
assessment, and final inclusion as shown in Figure 1. The initial search results yielded 215
documents. In the initial screening phase, 41 documents were eliminated because they were not
journal articles and not final publications. Subsequently, from the 174 remaining articles, 88
were excluded after manual verification of accessibility and language criter ia to ensure full
compliance with the predefined eligibility requirements. Finally, 86 articles met the inclusion
criteria and were used in this literature study.

DISCUSSION
This bibliometric systematic review included 86 peer-reviewed open-access journal articles
examining green purchasing and consumption within sustainable economic discourse. The
distribution of publications indicates strong concentration in sustainability -oriented journals,
particularly Sustainability, Journal of Cleaner Production, Business Strategy and the Environment,

and Ecological Economics. This pattern suggests that the topic is predominantly embedded
within environmental management and sustainability scholarship rather t han mainstream
macroeconomic analysis. Such disciplinary concentration is consistent with prior assessments
that green consumption research has largely developed within sustainability and marketing
domains rather than structural economic theory (Dangelico & Vocalelli, 2017).
Geographically, the reviewed studies are largely concentrated in emerging economies, with
China appearing as the most frequently examined context, followed by Indonesia, India, and
Malaysia. European countries are represented to a lesse r extent on an individual basis. This
regional concentration reflects the growing relevance of sustainable consumption issues in
rapidly developing markets, where economic growth and environmental pressures coexist. From
a disciplinary perspective, most studies are classified within environmental science, business and
management, and social sciences. Empirical survey -based approaches dominate the literature,
frequently employing structural equation modeling techniques. The prominence of intention -
based models aligns with earlier observations that green marketing and consumer sustainability
research is strongly behaviorally oriented (Peattie & Peattie, 2009).
The bibliometric mapping further reinforces this behavioral orientation. Keywords such as
green purchase intention, consumer attitude, marketing, and perception occupy central
positions within the network, demonstrating the dominance of consumer -focused explanatory
models. This aligns with broader behavioral sustainability scholarship emphasizing cognitiv e
drivers and motivational mechanisms of pro -environmental consumption (White et al., 2019;
Trudel, 2019). Although macro-level terms such as sustainable development and green economy
are also highly connected, they often function as contextual references rather than as
operationalized economic variables within empirical models. Institutional and structural terms,
including supply chain management and regulation, appear in secondary clusters, suggesting
partial but not comprehensive integration of broader economic dimensions.
Taken together, these findings reveal a structural imbalance in the literature. Research on
green purchasing and consumption has achieved considerable maturity in explaining individual
intentions and attitudes; however, its integration with systemic sustainable economic
transformation remains conceptually limited. Broader sustainable consumption scholarship
emphasizes that meaningful economic transition requires changes in production systems,
governance structures, and market institution s (Jackson, 2005; Dangelico & Vocalelli, 2017), yet
empirical green purchasing studies rarely embed consumer behavior within such systemic
economic frameworks. The micro -level behavioral focus coexists with macro -level sustainability
discourse, yet the two domains are not fully synthesized. This indicates that bridging the micro –
macro sustainability gap remains a central challenge

CONCLUSION
This bibliometric systematic review maps the intellectual structure of research on green
purchasing and consumption within sustainable economic discourse over the period 2016 –2026.
The analysis demonstrates a clear acceleration of scholarly output after 2020, indicating that the
integration of consumer behavior and sustainability -oriented economic discussions has become
an increasingly prominent research agenda. Bibliometric mapping reveals that green economy
and consumption behavior serve as dominant thematic anchors, while intention -based and
attitude-based models remain central explanatory frameworks.
Despite the appa rent conceptual linkage between green purchasing and sustainable
economic transformation, the literature remains structurally imbalanced. Empirical studies
predominantly emphasize individual cognitive and attitudinal determinants, often employing
survey-based methodologies and behavioral theories. In contrast, macro -level economic
variables, institutional arrangements, and systemic production –consumption dynamics are less

SUGGESTION
This study has several limitations. It relies exclusively on Scopus -indexed, open -access
English journal articles published between 2016 and 2026, which may exclude relevant studies
from other databases or subscription -based sources. The bibliometric approach using
VOSviewer focuses on keyword co -occurrence and thematic structures, capturing structural
patterns rather than providing in -depth qual itative evaluation of theoretical frameworks or
methodological rigor within individual studies. In addition, the search string required explicit
linkage between green purchasing/consumption and sustainable economy terms, potentially
omitting studies that address these constructs implicitly. Future research could expand database
coverage and combine bibliometric mapping with qualitative content analysis to produce a more
comprehensive synthesis
