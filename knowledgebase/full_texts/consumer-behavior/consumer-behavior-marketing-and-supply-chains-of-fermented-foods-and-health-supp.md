Abstract

Fermented foods and health supplements are deeply embedded in the dietary traditions
of Southeast Asia and India and are increasingly positioned within expanding functional food
and preventive health markets. This narrative review aimed to synthesize evide nce on four
interrelated domains: consumer behavior, marketing dynamics, supply -chain systems, and
regulatory and certification issues associated with fermented foods and health supplements in
these regions. Peer -reviewed literature was searched primarily through Scopus and Web of
Science, with supplementary searches in ScienceDirect, SpringerLink, Emerald Insight, and
Taylor & Francis Online, with no year restriction. Studies were screened for relevance to
fermented foods and/or health supplements in South east Asia and India and were synthesized
using thematic and interpretative analysis. The review found that health consciousness,
perceived efficacy and safety, cultural familiarity, affordability, physician recommendations,
and product credibility are major determinants of consumer acceptance and purchase intention.
Digital marketing, e -commerce, social media engagement, influencer communication, and
branding increasingly shape market access and consumer trust, particularly among younger and
urban populatio ns. Supply -chain development is constrained by limited cold -chain
infrastructure, inconsistent standardization, dispersed sourcing, and variable quality control,
whereas transparency, traceability, digital logistics, and omnichannel coordination can enhance
efficiency and market responsiveness. Regulatory inconsistencies and differences in
certification and labeling requirements remain important barriers to consumer confidence and
cross-border market expansion. Conceptually, the review proposes a reciprocal  ecosystem
model in which consumer trust functions as a cross-cutting mechanism linking perceived health
value, marketing credibility, certification, product quality, and supply -chain transparency,
while regulatory, cultural, and technological factors cond ition these relationships. Greater
regulatory harmonization, scientific validation of health claims, improved supply -chain

 Introduction

Fermentation is an ancient method that helps improve the shelf life and nutritional value
of ingredients (Sivamaruthi et al., 2024). Analysis shows that fermented foods made from cereals,
vegetables, and fruits in Southeast Asia have higher microbial diversity and nutritional value than
non-fermented foods (Surya, 2024). Asian foods are also rich in bioactive compounds that help
prevent disease. Probiotics present in these foods significantly contribute to daily nutrition
requirements (Doo et al, 2024). The microorganisms in fermented foods were shaped by regional
geography, practices, and traditional fermentation knowledge (Tamang et al., 2016; Sivamaruthi
et al., 2018a). Cultural identity in local diets is reflected in Nepal's fermented foods, which are
processed with rich microbial ecosystems shaped by geography and tradition (Gautam et al.,
2025). Most people in Southeast Asian countries have fermented foods in their diet. People of
countries like Vietnam have fermented fish products as a primary ingredient of their daily diet.
These products reflect their cultural heritage and the unique fermentation techniques of the region
(Surya, 2024). Fermented fish products are high in micronutrients and protein, and attention to
their safety is needed (Atun et al., 2024). Fermented foods of Asia offer numerous health benefits.
The scientific documentation of the fermentation process and technological innovation will
enable the products to reach and be accepted globally. Parallel to fermented foods, the health
supplements sector has expanded rapidly, supported by growing preventive health awareness
(Sivamaruthi et al., 2018a; Chaiyasut et al., 2018).
Combining traditional knowledge with modern food science improves the nutritional
benefits of fermented foods and enhances their adaptability (Anal et al, 2019). The fermentation
process enhances nutrition and increases the therapeutic value of foods, whic h positions
fermented foods as an important component of preventive nutrition (Farnworth, 2008).
Peptides derived from fermented foods have been shown to reduce hypertension, while the
antioxidant properties of these foods have immunomodulatory potential. This suggests that
fermented foods are a natural therapeutic source (Kleekayai et al., 2015). Regular consumption
of fermented foods reduces inflammation, lowers risk factors for metabolic disorders, and aids
in managing lifestyle-related diseases (Paul et al., 2023). Clinical trials have provided strong
evidence for the role of fermented foods in managing gastrointestinal disorders (Mukherjee et
al., 2025). Fermented foods influence host physiology through microbial activity and extend
their impact beyond gut health to disease prevention (Sivamaruthi et al., 2018b; Sivamaruthi,
2018; Chaiyasut et a l., 2021). The global fermented foods market is anticipated to increase
from USD 259.27 billion in 2025 to USD 398.78 billion by 2034, with a compound annual
growth rate (CAGR) of 4.9% during the forecast period from 2025 to 2034 ( Coherent Market
Insights, 2026). This expansion is driven by the escalating consumer inclination towards gut
health and probiotic-enriched diets, which has heightened demand for natural and functional
foods, and by the growing integration of fermented goods into beverages, dairy, bakery, and
plant-based alternatives (Borah et al., 2025).
Southeast Asia is a culturally rich and commercially vibrant region for fermented foods,
with traditional items such as tempeh, fish sauce, and fermented rice products constituting a
substantial portion of daily diets. The Asia -Pacific region accounts for approximately 40% of
the worldwide fermented food market. The region is undergoing heightened urbanization and
dietary shifts, rising middle -class incomes and health consciousness, and a proliferation of

The fermented food sector significantly impacts both macroeconomic and microeconomic
systems. The sector encompasses extensive international production of dairy products,
beverages, and supplements, as well as high -value exports including yoghurt and kombucha.
Fermented foods bolster small -scale and cottage businesses at regional and local levels,
improve rural lives and indigenous food systems, and create employment throughout value
chains (Rawat et al., 2018; Sathe and Mandal, 2016). In India and Southeast Asia, fermented
foods connect traditional knowledge systems with contemporary market economies, generating
prospects for value enhancement and commercialization.
The supply chain of fermented foods encompasses both formal and informal industries.
Key phases include raw material procurement, conventional or industrial fermentation,
processing and packaging, and distribution through local markets, supermarkets, and d igital
platforms. The sector encounters obstacles, including restricted shelf life, absence of
standardization in conventional products, cold -chain constraints, and regulatory and quality
assurance complications. Technological innovations and industrial pr ogress are progressively
enhancing supply chain efficiency ( Niyigaba et al., 2025) . Consumer perceptions are swiftly
transforming due to shifting lifestyles and heightened health consciousness. Significant trends
encompass an intensified emphasis on gut health and microbiome consciousness, a desire for
natural, minimally processed, clean -label meals, a rising demand for plant -based fermented
goods, and a revitalized interest in classic cuisine rebranded for contemporary appeal. Urban
consumers, especially millennials and Generation Z, are driving demand for fermented foods
worldwide, including kombucha and kefir, demonstrating a willingness to pay premium prices
for functional health benefits (Hossain et al., 2025).  Marketing analysis is essential for
fermented foods because of market fragmentation between traditional producers and industrial
manufacturers, varied regional preferences, and supply chain inefficiencies. It assists in
identifying target consumer segments , optimizing product positioning, formulating pricing
strategies, enhancing branding, and supporting policy and export promotion efforts, so
strengthening global competitiveness (Zhao et al., 2025). Digital transformation is essential for
the growth of the fermented food business. Digital marketing enhances consumer awareness of
the benefits of probiotics and strengthens brand identity. E -commerce platforms broaden
market access, facilitate direct -to-consumer methods, and promote niche and artisanal
products. Social media influencers advocate for fermented foods within healthy lifestyles,
enhance consumer trust via reviews and demonstrations, and popularize traditional meals
among younger demographics.  These channels are crucial for startups and small -scale
producers, facilitating the scaling of indigenous products and enhancing awareness of obscure
fermented foods.

Previous reviews have substantially advanced knowledge of the nutritional properties,
microbial diversity, health effects, traditional processing, and safety of fermented foods, while
other studies have examined consumer attitudes, functional food acceptance, digital marketing,
or supply chain issues in more specific contexts. However, these strands of evidence remain
largely fragmented. In particular, limited research has examined how consumer perceptions
and trust are simultaneously shaped by marketing practices, product availability, supply chain
transparency, certification, regulatory assurance, and cultural familiarity. Similarly, the
relationships between commercialization strategies and operational constraints, such as
standardization, cold-chain infrastructure, traceability, and cross-border regulatory differences,
remain insufficiently integrated in the existing literature. This fragmentation limits
understanding of how market growth actually occurs across the full fermented food and health
supplement ecosystem.
An integrated review is therefore needed because consumer behavior, marketing,
supply chains, and regulation are mutually dependent rather than isolated domains. Consumer
demand influences product positioning and distribution strategies, while marketing
communication shapes awareness, perceived value, and trust. At the same time, supply -chain
performance determines product availability, consistency, safety, and traceability, whereas
regulatory frameworks and certifications influence both market access and c onsumer
confidence. These interactions are particularly important in Southeast Asia and India, where
traditional food cultures coexist with rapidly expanding functional food, supplement, e -
commerce, and modern retail markets. The present review addresses t his gap by synthesizing
these interconnected dimensions within a unified conceptual framework.
The overall objective of this review was to synthesize existing evidence on the
consumer interface, marketing dynamics, supply -chain systems, and regulatory environment
of fermented foods and health supplements in Southeast Asia and India. Specifically, th e
review aimed to: 1) identify the major factors influencing consumer perceptions, trust, and
purchase decisions; 2) examine the roles of digital marketing, e -commerce, social media,
branding, and cultural positioning in market development; 3) evaluate key  supply-chain
characteristics and challenges, including production, logistics, cold -chain management,
distribution, transparency, and traceability; and 4) assess how regulatory frameworks,
certifications, labeling, quality assurance, and scientific validat ion influence consumer
confidence and market expansion.
The proposed framework conceptualizes the fermented foods and health supplements
market as an interconnected ecosystem in which consumer interface, marketing dynamics, and
supply chain systems operate through reciprocal rather than linear relationships. Co nsumer
factors, including health awareness, cultural familiarity, perceived value, and product
positioning, shape product demand and responses to marketing activities. In turn, digital
marketing, e -commerce, social media engagement, branding, and trust inf luence consumer
awareness, perceived value, and purchase intention. These market –consumer interactions

 Methodology

This study was conducted as a narrative review with a structured literature-search approach,
rather than as a formal systematic review or meta-analysis. Peer-reviewed literature was searched
primarily in Scopus and Web of Science, with supplementary search es conducted through
ScienceDirect, SpringerLink, Emerald Insight, and Taylor & Francis Online. No restriction was
imposed on the year of publication; therefore, publications available from database inception up to
the final search date were eligible for consideration. Searches were restricted to English-language
publications. The search strategy combined terms relating to the two principal product categories and
the major domains of interest, including “fermented food,” “fermented product*,” “health
supplement*,” “dietary supplement*,” “consumer behavior,” “consumer perception,” “purchase
intention,” “marketing,” “digital marketing,” “e -commerce,” “supply chain,” “logistics,”
“traceability,” “regulation,” “certification,” “Southeast Asia,”* and “India.” Boolean operators
(AND/OR) were adapted according to the search functions of each database.
Studies were considered eligible when they: 1) addressed fermented foods and/or health
or dietary supplements; 2) examined at least one relevant dimension of consumer behavior,
consumer perception, marketing, commercialization, supply -chain management, log istics,
regulation, certification, labeling, quality assurance, or related market factors; 3) contained
evidence relevant to Southeast Asia and/or India; 4) were published in English; and 5) provided
sufficient information to contribute to the thematic synthesis. Publications were excluded when
they were unrelated to the geographical scope or review domains; focused exclusively on
laboratory, microbiological, clinical, or technological characteristics without relevance to
consumer, market, supply -chain, or regulatory aspects; were duplicates; or did not provide
sufficient information for thematic interpretation.
Studies were included when they met the following criteria:
• Peer-reviewed journal articles.
• Articles published in English.
• Studies focused on Southeast Asia and/or India.
• Studies related to fermented foods and/or health supplements.
• Studies addressing at least one of the principal review domains, including
marketing, consumer behavior, supply-chain systems, or regulatory issues.
• Empirical studies or high-quality conceptual studies that provided sufficient
information relevant to the objectives of the review.
Studies were excluded when they met one or more of the following criteria:
• Non-peer-reviewed materials such as blogs, news articles, and magazine articles.
• Conference abstracts for which a full paper was unavailable.
• Studies outside the selected geographical scope of Southeast Asia and India.
• Studies focusing exclusively on biomedical, microbiological, biochemical, clinical,
or therapeutic aspects of fermented foods or health supplements without relevance
to marketing, consumer behavior, supply-chain systems, or regulatory issues.
• Duplicate publications.

The database searches identified 132 records, and an additional 27 were found through
industry reports and reference searches, yielding 159 records in total. After removal of 31
duplicate records, 128 records remained for title and abstract screening. Of t hese, 44 records
were excluded because they did not meet the predefined scope and eligibility criteria.
Consequently, 84 full -text publications were assessed for eligibility; all met the criteria and
were included in the final thematic analysis.
Because this work was designed as a narrative and interpretative review rather than a
systematic review of intervention effectiveness, a formal risk -of-bias or quantitative quality -
scoring instrument was not applied. Instead, priority was given to peer -reviewed scholarly
literature indexed in established academic databases, and studies were evaluated for relevance
to the research objectives, methodological clarity, contextual relevance, and usefulness for
thematic synthesis. This limitation and the narrative nature of the evidence appraisal have now
been explicitly acknowledged in the revised manuscript.
The synthesized themes were interpreted across evidence originating from Southeast
Asia and India to identify recurrent patterns and context-specific observations. The review was
not designed as a formal comparative analysis between the two regions because  of
heterogeneity in study populations, product categories, study designs, and country-level market
and regulatory environments.

Results

1. Consumer Perception of Fermented Foods and Health Supplements
Across the reviewed studies, three recurring groups of determinants emerged as central
to consumer acceptance and purchase of fermented foods and health supplements: 1) health -
related motivations and perceived efficacy, 2) cultural and social familiarity, and 3) perceived
value, credibility, and trust. Health consciousness, preventive -health orientation, perceived
effectiveness, and physician recommendations consistently encouraged product adoption,
whereas unfamiliarity, sensory concerns, unclear labeling, and perceived risk acted as barriers.
Cultural familiarity with traditional fermented foods and herbal products further facilitated
acceptance, while affordability, credible brands, scientific validation, and transparent product
information strengthened p erceived value and purchase confidence (Sulaiman et al., 2025;
Huang et al., 2020; Soon et al., 2024; Shrestha et al., 2021; Jadhav et al., 2023; Yeo et al., 2023;
Ang et al., 2023; Kim et al., 2021; Ariffin et al., 2021).
Health awareness positively increases acceptance of functional foods, and there is a
need for consumer -targeted communication. Unfamiliarity and sensory concerns of the
products act as barriers (Sulaiman et al, 2025). The combination of cultural heritage a nd
scientific proof supporting the health benefits of traditional fermented foods improves
consumer engagement and market expansion (Lee et al, 2024). The perception of health
benefits and flavour may influence purchase intention, and there is a need for balanced product
design (Huang et al., 2020).
Consumers usually associate fermented foods with digestion and immunity benefits.
Poor and unclear labels, as well as misinformation, affect consumer decision-making (Soon et
al., 2024). The consumption of traditional fermented foods has declined with urba n lifestyles,
but it is now being revived by growing health awareness and modern retail formats (Surya,

2. Marketing of Fermented Foods and Health Supplements
Across the marketing literature, three interrelated mechanisms consistently emerged:
digital accessibility, trust-building communication, and culturally relevant product positioning.
E-commerce and social media expand market reach and facilitate engagement , particularly
among younger consumers, whereas platform authenticity, credible branding, transparent
influencer communication, and evidence-based health claims determine whether such exposure
translates into consumer trust and purchase intention. At the s ame time, culturally congruent
positioning enables traditional fermented foods and health supplements to retain their cultural
identity while adapting to modern functional -food markets (Falahat et al., 2019; Ong et al.,
2023; Wan Mohd Khair, 2023; Chuah et  al., 2023; Hussin et al., 2024; Desembrianita et al.,
2024; Dharma et al., 2024; Rodzi & Lee, 2021; Sudaryanto et al., 2021). In detail, supplements
have expanded through e-commerce among young consumers, and authenticity and regulation
support this (Seow  et al., 2026). The purchase intention of Malaysian consumers is strongly
influenced by perceived product authenticity on online platforms, underscoring the importance
of secure digital commerce ( Falahat et al., 2019). Active digital engagement strengthens the
brand-consumer relationship and positively influences consumers' purchase intention in
competitive online markets (Ong et al., 2023). Even though regulations and quality assurance
remain important f or consumer trust and market expansion, commerce accelerates market
expansion better in Asia (Choi & Mai, 2018). The rise of health awareness and cultural revival
is supporting the expansion of the market for fermented foods in East and Southeast Asia
(Marco et al., 2017). Social media influencers enhance consumer engagement through brand
recall, but transparency is necessary to maintain trust in the health products (Wan Mohd Khair,
2023).

3. Supply chain of Fermented foods and Health supplements in Southeast Asia and India
Localized supply chains for fermented foods improve sustainability. Scalability and
standardization challenges should be addressed to support wider distribution of the products
(Yee et al, 2025). Supply chain systems for fermentation improve value addition and resilience.
They also support rural livelihoods and sustainable food production (Wache et al., 2018). The
optimization of e -commerce logistic strategies improves customer satisfaction and increases
the competitive advantage of products online (Kawa & Zdrenka, 2023). High supply chain
integration improves efficiency and product quality in nutraceutical product markets (Datta et
al., 2023). Digital transport tools improve transparency and efficiency. It also supports supply
chain resilience (KPMG, 2023).  Offline and online channel integration strategies increase
consumer reach, and channel coordination improves consistency in brand and customer
experience (Luin & Bahri, 2025). Supply chain resilience depends on effective risk
management that integrates technology, reflecting the need for adaptive logistics techniques in
distribution (Annamala et al., 2025). A transparent supply chain enhances consumer trust and
reduces safety challenges (Nguyen & Nguyen, 2025). Supply chain management using big data
analytics improves efficiency and market responsiveness (Virgineta et al, 2025).  Complex
product categories need strong coordination and trust between the retailers and suppliers.

4. Regulations and Certifications
There is a need to balance efficiency with preservation of fermented foods, even though
industrialization improves safety (Anal et al, 2020). Though scientific validation and
standardization are needed to increase the global market, Southeast Asian fermented foods are
culturally accepted (Chaiyasut et al., 2018). Fermented meat products provide numerous
bioactive compounds, but they require strict quality control to mitigate safety risks and balance
health benefits. Fragmented regulations and multiple inter mediaries increase operational
complexity. There is a need for integrated governance and policy alignment (Kassahun et al.,
2023). Even if regulatory inconsistencies create compliance challenges for companies, robust
regulation improves consumer protection and market credibility (Waché et al., 2018). Among

transparent labelling and regulatory assurance since perceived risk negatively affects consumer

 Discussions

The review reveals that there are only a few longitudinal studies which analyze the
consumer behavior towards fermented foods and health supplements, and there is a lack of
interdisciplinary frameworks. The impact of regulations is under -researched, and th ere is
insufficient empirical data regarding it. Quantitative modelling of supply chain efficiency
remains limited, and cross -country comparative studies within Southeast Asia remain
unexplored.
The reviewed evidence indicates that health consciousness, perceived efficacy, cultural
familiarity, and consumer trust are the most consistently supported primary determinants of
consumer acceptance. Affordability, physician recommendation, branding, certification, digital
engagement, and product accessibility appear to function more as secondary or moderating
influences. Evidence regarding health motivation and trust was relatively consistent across
contexts, whereas findings on influencer marketing, cert ification effects, and regulatory
influences were more context -dependent and less uniformly supported. These distinctions
suggest that market development is driven primarily by perceived health value and trust, while
marketing, certification, and supply -chain conditions determine whether these motivations
translate into actual purchase and repeated use.
The findings of this literature review denote that traditional foods are deeply rooted in
the regional diets of Southeast Asia, and consumption remains strong. Indian consumers exhibit
increasing interest in preventive nutrition. Health motivation is the m ajor determinant of
consumer behavior. Consumers prefer gut health, comfortable digestion, improved
metabolism, and immunity. Health supplements are largely adopted for disease prevention
rather than for treatment. In alignment with the consumer value theory, the analyzed literature
indicates that physician recommendation, awareness towards preventive health and product
efficacy influence the purchase intention of the consumer. Digital platforms enable market
expansion and improved product accessibility. Young and urban consumers are influenced by
digital and influencer marketing. E -commerce has improved the market level in both regions
of the study. The authenticity of digital platforms, social media influencers, and interactive
content increases consumer e ngagement. Supply chain faces challenges in scaling the
fermented food market. Even though local production improves rural livelihoods, scalability is
hindered by limited cold -chain infrastructure, dispersed sourcing, diverse regulations, and
inconsistent product quality control.
In contrast, health supplements exhibit strong vertical integration and a transparent
supply chain. A transparent supply chain improves consumer trust, and the review highlights
the importance of digital logistics management and omnichannel coordination. R egulatory
frameworks are inconsistent across Southeast Asia and India. Robust regulations will enhance
consumer protection and market credibility. Halal certifications and recognized quality
standards increase consumer confidence towards the purchase. Dive rse regulations limit
regional harmonization and primarily affect the cross-border expansion of the market. There is
a need for standard labeling systems and scientific validation of health claims. Proper cross -
country coordination of regulations and enforcement of strict digital marketing guidelines will
increase consumer trust in the products.

    Conclusion and suggestions

After examining the marketing dynamics, consumer interface, and supply chain of
fermented foods and health supplements in Southeast Asia and India, this review highlights
increasing health consciousness, the cultural significance of fermented foods, and di gital
marketing as key drivers of market growth. Consumer trust is strongly influenced by perceived
health benefits, product safety, regulatory measures, and credible certification, while supply
chains continue to face persistent challenges, particularly l imited cold -chain infrastructure,
inconsistent quality control, and regulatory differences across countries. By synthesizing
evidence from diverse studies, this review provides a holistic understanding of the sector and
demonstrates the interconnected roles of consumers, marketing, supply chains, and regulatory
systems. Nevertheless, the review is limited by its inclusion of only English -language
publications, which may have excluded relevant regional-language studies, and by differences
in regulatory and m arket environments across Southeast Asian countries, which also
constrained direct cross-country comparisons.
To address these challenges, governments, industry stakeholders, and community
producers should collaborate to strengthen product standardization, transparent labeling,
scientific validation of health claims, certification systems, and regulatory harmoniza tion
across countries. Greater investment in cold -chain infrastructure, digital logistics, traceability
systems, and omnichannel distribution is also recommended to improve supply chain
efficiency, market competitiveness, and consumer confidence. Future re search should
emphasize longitudinal and comparative cross -country studies within Southeast Asia and
between Southeast Asia and India, together with empirical assessments of regulations,
certifications, consumer trust, and quantitative supply chain perform ance. Such evidence
would improve the generalizability of the findings and support the development of more
effective policies and market strategies to promote the sustainable growth of fermented foods
and health supplements.

 New knowledge and the effects on society and communities

The new knowledge generated by this review is the integrated understanding that
consumer behavior, marketing dynamics, supply chain systems, regulation, culture, and
technology should not be viewed separately but as interconnected components of a single
fermented food and health supplement ecosystem. The framework presented in the study shows
that consumer trust acts as an important link among perceived health benefits, product quality,
credible marketing, certification, and supply chain transparency. It al so highlights how
traditional fermented foods can be transformed from locally consumed cultural products into
higher-value functional foods when traditional knowledge is combined with scientific
evidence, modern processing, branding, and digital commercialization.
For society and communities, these findings may contribute to greater consumer
awareness, safer product choices, stronger preventive health practices and more responsible
communication of health benefits. At the community level, improved market access and value
chain development can support farmers, traditional producers, small enterprises, and rural
communities by creating income, employment, and value-added opportunities while helping to
preserve indigenous food knowledge and cultural identity. Digital pl atforms and e-commerce
may further enable community -based producers to reach wider markets, provided that they
receive adequate support in quality assurance, certification, logistics, and regulatory compliance.
The principal conceptual contribution of this review is the identification of consumer
trust as a cross-cutting mechanism linking health-value perceptions with marketing credibility,
certification, product quality, and supply-chain transparency.
