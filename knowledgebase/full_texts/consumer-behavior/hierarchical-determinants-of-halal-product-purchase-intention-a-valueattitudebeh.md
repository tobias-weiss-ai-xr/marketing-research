Abstract: The primary aim of this study is to identify the
determinants of purchase intention for halal products. The
theoretical foundation of the research is based on the VAB
Hierarchy Model. Within this framework, the study examines
the relationships among relig ious belief, attitude, and purchase
intention. The research data were collected from consumers aged
18 and above through an online survey using a convenience
sampling technique. To test the research hypotheses, CB -SEM
was employed, and data were analyzed u sing the AMOS
program. The findings obtained from the data of 451 participants
indicate that religious belief and attitude are the factors that
directly influence purchase intention toward halal products. The
study reveals that religious belief has an effect on  purchase
intention both directly and indirectly through attitude.
Furthermore, the positive attitude developed toward halal
products has been statistically confirmed to have a significant
impact on consumers’ purchase intentions. In addition, attitude
plays a mediating role in the relationship between religious belief
and purchase intention toward halal products. By empirically
examining the primary determinants of halal product purchase

Introduction
The belief-based structure of human behavior renders the influence of religious belief on
consumer preferences inevitable (Shaari & Arifin, 2010). The impact of religious beliefs on
individuals’ consumption behaviors constitutes a dynamic process shaped by  the
interaction between the theological framework of the belief system and the individual’s
level of religiosity (Lada et al., 2009). Within the framework of cultural, social, personal,

and psychological factors that determine consumer behavior, particularly in predominantly
Muslim societies, the concept of halal is regarded as a significant phenomenon guiding
consumption preferences (Saygılı, 2019). The concept of halal products may be d efined as
“goods or services prepared entirely in accordance with Islamic principles from the
production stage to their presentation to consumers, and containing no elements prohibited
under Islamic law” (Topçuoğlu et al., 2017, p. 22).
In academic studies examining halal product consumption and purchasing behavior, the
most commonly employed foundational theories to explain consumers’ decision -making
processes include the Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) (Alam & Sayuti, 2011;  Khalek &
Ismail, 2015; Memon et al., 2020), the Theory of Reasoned Action (TRA) (Lada et al., 2009;
Mukhtar & Butt, 2012; Hussain et al., 2016), and the Stimulus -Organism-Response Model
(S-O-R) (Begum et al., 2025; Suparno, 2021; Ossman et al., 2025). In addition to these widely
utilized theories and foundational models explaining consumer behavior, another
significant theory examining the influence of individuals’ internal value judgments on
attitudes and subsequent  purchasing behavior is the Value -Attitude-Behavior Hierarchy
Model (VAB), developed by Homer and Kahle (1988).
Within the social psychology literature, investigations into the interactions among values,
attitudes, and behaviors constitute one of the core areas of inquiry in the discipline. Homer
and Kahle (1988) proposed a causal model incorporating “value –attitude–behavior” to
explain the relationships among these variables. According to the Value-Attitude-Behavior
(VAB) hierarchy conceptualized by Homer and Kahle (1988), values function as the direct
antecedents and predictors of attitudes, while attitudes serve as predictors that  transmit
this influence on behavior and directly determine behavioral outcomes (Marso & Hasan,
2017). From a theoretical perspective, attitude is positioned as an outcome of values,
whereas behavioral intention is conceptualized as a primary antecedent of actual behavior
(Fishbein & Ajzen, 1975; Ajzen, 1985; Homer & Kahle, 1988).
According to Edgar et al. (2026), within VAB theory, attitudes derived from personal values
emerge as a central variable. However, unlike values, attitudes are more specific and may
vary depending on contextual influences. The literature includes studies e mploying the
VAB Hierarchy Model to explain relationships among variables in significant and
contemporary contexts such as food waste in restaurants (Long et al., 2024), employees’
green behaviors (Tran & Nguyen, 2026), purchase intention toward electric vehicles (Dau
et al., 2026), consumers’ homeownership intentions (Chua et al., 2026), and sustainable food
consumption (de Matos et al., 2026). In a study conducted by Demirağ  et al. (2020), which
examined Muslim consumers’ perceptions of halal food -labeled products in Germany
through the VAB Model, consumer religiosity was identified as a significant determinant
of attitudes toward purchasing halal food -labeled products, and r eligiosity was found to
positively influence both attitudes and behavioral intentions. Similarly, Marso and Hasan
(2017), employing the VAB Hierarchy Model to investigate purchase behavior toward halal
food, demonstrated that a model incorporating religios ity, attitude, and behavior
components significantly explains behavioral intention toward halal food.
Within the framework of the VAB (Value, Attitude, Behavior) hierarchy, the most
fundamental and determinative layer of the model, namely the Value variable, has been
conceptualized in this study as Religious Belief. According to Rokeach (1973, p. 5), a value
is defined as “an enduring belief that a specific mode of conduct or end state of existence is

existence”. From this perspective, religious belief can be considered a significant element

The primary aim of this study is to examine religious belief in halal product consumption
within the context of the VAB hierarchy; specifically, to investigate the effects of religious
belief on attitude and purchase intention, and the effect of attitude o n purchase intention.
In this regard, the study seeks to address the following research questions:
RQ1: In the context of halal consumption, does religious belief function as a
determining value element in the formation of consumers’ attitudes toward halal
products within the framework of the VAB hierarchy model?
RQ2: In the context of halal products, does religious belief have a direct effect on
purchase intention, and to what extent does the manifestation of this effect through
attitude align with the hierarchical structure proposed by the VAB model?
RQ3: Does attitude toward halal products assume a mediating role in the relationship
between religious belief and purchase intention, thereby empirically validating the
hierarchical structure of the VAB model?
1. Hypotheses and Conceptual Framework
1.1. Religious Belief-Attitude
Religion is among the foremost factors influencing individuals’ attitudes, behaviors, and
value judgments at both personal and societal levels (Saygılı, 2019:12). Religiosity, in turn,
refers to the degree to which an individual adopts and practices specific religious beliefs
and rituals (Ko c et al., 202 5). An individual’s level of belief constitutes a significant
determinant of consumption behavior (Handriana et al., 2021). Particularly among
Muslims, religion represents one of the potential factors shaping consumption decisions.
For instance, the concept s of halal (permissible) and haram (prohibited) in Islam directly
influence consumption choices (Quantaniah et al., 2013). In other words, Islamic marketing
integrates specific beliefs and ideologies that differ fundamentally from conventional
marketing practices, guiding Muslim consumers to align their consumption behaviors with
Islamic beliefs and principles (Alotaibi & Abbas, 2023).
In order to better understand halal products within the scope of this research, it is essential
to comprehend the concept of halal, which influences Muslim consumers’ ultimate
decisions. According to Wilson (2018 , p. 48), halal refers to “a good or action that is
permissible according to the rules and teachings specified in Islam.” Moreover, the
literature includes numerous studies examining halal across various categories, such as
halal food (Ab Talib et al., 2016; Se ptiani & Ridlwan, 2020; Quantaniah, 2013), halal
cosmetics ( Genoveva & Utami, 2020; Handriana et al., 2021; Suparno, 2021), and halal
tourism (Battour & Ismail, 2016; Boğan & Sarıışık, 2019; Prayag, 2020).
In a study conducted by Koc et al. (202 5) on Turkish consumers examining the effects of
trust in halal products and religiosity on purchase intention and attitudes, it was concluded
that individuals’ level of belief influences their intention to purchase halal products, while
the level of religio sity-practice affects attitudes toward halal products. Additionally,
numerous studies in the literature have investigated the effect of religious belief on attitude

H1: Religious belief has a positive effect on attitudes toward halal product.
1.2. Religious Belief – Purchase Intention
Belief, which significantly influences consumption behavior, also affects consumers’
intention to purchase a product or service in the final decision-making stage. In the context
of halal product, the impact of religious belief on purchase decisions plays an important
role within consumer behavior research. In a study conducted by Ashraf et al. (2017)
examining whether religion influences potential customers’ purchase decisions, a positive
relationship was identified between belief and the intention to purchase luxury fashion
products.
Similarly, Mukhtar and Butt (2012), investigating the role of Muslims’ attitudes toward
halal products, subjective norms, and religiosity in predicting their intention to choose halal
products, found that the Theory of Reasoned Action (TRA) is a valid mode l for predicting
the intention to select halal products and that personal religiosity positively affects attitudes
toward halal products. Within this context, numerous studies in the literature suggest that
religious belief influences purchase intention to ward halal products (Alotaibi & Abbas,
2023; Awan et al., 2015; Widad & Asih, 2023). Accordingly, the following hypothesis is
developed:
H2: Religious belief has a positive effect on halal product purchase intention.
1.3. Attitude – Purchase Intention
Attitude refers to an individual’s favorable and/or unfavorable evaluations or tendencies
toward a particular person, object, or event (Ajzen, 2005). Understanding consumers
fundamentally requires knowing their level of knowledge about products  This is because
the level of knowledge regarding products shapes consumers' attitudes. Based on this,
attitudes significantly influence consumers' purchase intentions (Saygılı & Sütütemiz,
2023). Within the context of halal products, it may be argued that consumers who
demonstrate sensitivity to religious values are likely to develop positive attitudes toward
products and services that align with these values.
In a study conducted by Souiden  and Rani (2015) examining the effect of religiosity on
consumers’ attitudes toward Islamic banking, the hypothesis that the more positive an
individual’s attitude toward Islamic banks, the greater their intention to purchase such
services was empirically supported. Similarly, Alam and Sayuti (2011), in their research
investigating halal food purchasing behavior in Malaysia within the framework of the
Theory of Planned Behavior, found through multiple regression analysis that all factors,
including attitude, exerted a positive and significant effect on halal food purchase intention.
Furthermore, numerous studies have reported that attitude influences halal product
purchase intention (Hussain et al., 2024; Letsoin & Riorini, 2023; Widad & Asih, 2023). Thus,
the following hypothesis is proposed:
H3: Attitude has a positive effect on halal product purchase intention.

1.4. Religious Belief – Attitude – Purchase Intention
Many studies on halal products in the literature are grounded in the Theory of Planned
Behavior (Alam & Sayuti, 2011; Lada et al., 2009; Afendi et al., 2014; Akmaz, 2026) and the
Value Attitude Behavior Hierarchy Model (Hasan, 2019; Manan, 2016; Demirağ et al., 2020).
Additionally, some studies incorporating the variables of Religious Belief, Attitude, and
Purchase Intention have included moderator and mediator variables. For example, Basri
and Kurniawati  (2019), in their study examining factors influencing purchase intention
toward halal products, employed halal certification as a moderator variable. Their findings
indicated that religiosity and halal awareness significantly influenced purchase intention
prior to the introduction of the moderation effect; however, after the moderation effect was
introduced, religiosity and halal awareness no longer exerted a significant influence.
Similarly, Letsoin and Riorini  (2023), in their study identifying factors that may enhance
purchase intention toward local Indonesian halal cosmetic brands through attitude,
demonstrated that attitude plays a mediating role between brand image, perceived value,
religious belief, halal certification, and purchase intention.
Another distinguishing feature of the present study lies in its focus on the mediating role
of attitude, as the literature contains a limited number of studies examining this specific
mediating effect. Accordingly, the study advances the following original hypothesis:
H4: Attitude has a mediating effect on the relationship between religious belief and halal product
purchase intention.
2. Methodology
The primary aim of this study is to explain consumers’ halal product purchase intentions
from the perspective of the Value-Attitude-Behavior Hierarchy Model. The VAB approach
proposes a hierarchical and causal structure in which individual values shape attitudes,
and attitudes, in turn, shape behavioral intentions (Homer & Kahle, 1988). Withi n this
framework, religious belief represents the “value” dimension, attitude constitutes the
central cognitive mechanism, and halal product purchase intention reflects behavioral
intention.
Within the proposed model, the direct effects of religious belief on attitude and purchase
intention, the effect of attitude on purchase intention, and the mediating role of attitude in
the relationship between religious belief and purchase intention were tested. The research
model guiding the study is presented in Figure 1. The model was developed by considering
the relationships among constructs discussed within the conceptual framework of the
literature.

To test the proposed theoretical model and its hierarchical structure, Structural Equation
Modeling (SEM) was employed. In the study, the covariance -based approach (CB -SEM)
was preferred, as it enables the confirmation of theoretically predefined causal relationships
and allows for a comprehensive evaluation of overall model–data fit (Hair et al., 2014). The
analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS AMOS, following the two -step approach
recommended in the literature (Anderson & Gerbing, 1988).
In the first stage, the measurement model was assessed through Confirmatory Factor
Analysis (CFA), and the construct validity of the scales, including convergent and
discriminant validity, was evaluated. After establishing that the measurement model
demonstrated adequate psychometric properties, the structural model was specified in the
second stage, and the hypotheses were tested (Byrne, 2016). For parameter estimation, the
Maximum Likelihood (ML) estimation method, known for producing efficient results when
the assumption of multivariate normality is satisfied, was utilized (Kline, 2016). Model fit
was comprehensively evaluated by considering absolute fit indices, incremental
(comparative) fit indices, and error-based fit indices together (Hu & Bentler, 1999).
Finally, to determine the mediating role of the attitude variable, which occupies a central
position within the Value -Attitude-Behavior Hierarchy Model, indirect effects were
analyzed using the bootstrap method. Confidence intervals at the 95% level were calculated
based on 5,000 resamples, and the significance of indirect effects was assessed according to
whether the confidence interval excluded zero (0) (Preacher & Hayes, 2008; Hayes, 2018).
This approach was preferred because it reduces reliance on param etric assumptions
regarding the sampling distribution of indirect effects and provides more robust and
reliable results.

2.2. Measurement
A structured questionnaire was employed as the data collection instrument. The
questionnaire consisted of two main sections. The first section included items designed to
measure the latent constructs within the Value–Attitude–Behavior (VAB) hierarchy model,
while the second section contained questions aimed at identifying participants’
demographic characteristics (gender, marital status, age, education level, occupation, and
monthly income).
In measuring the variables constituting the VAB hierarchy model, scales with established
validity and reliability in the international literature were utilized. The religious belief
construct was measured using 11 items adapted from Achour et al. (201 5); the attitude
construct was measured with 5 items adopted from Khalek and Ismail (2015); and halal
product purchase intention was measured using 7 items from Khalek and Ismail (2015). All
constructs in the theoretical model were assessed using a five -point Likert-type scale (1 =
Strongly Disagree, 3 = Neither Agree nor Disagree, 5 = Strongly Agree), due to its sensitivity
in capturing respondents’ evaluations and its suitability for statistical analysis.
To ensure linguistic equivalence in adapting the scales into Turkish, the translation –back
translation method was applied (Brislin, 1970). The items were first translated into Turkish
by three independent experts who hold Ph.D. degrees in Business Administ ration and
Marketing, and  possess advanced bilingual proficiency in English and Turkish.
Subsequently, a different expert, a professional academic translator specializing in Business
Administration and Marketing research who had not seen the original versions, translated
the items  back into English solely to verify semantic consistency with the original
instrument. The draft questionnaire was then evaluated by a panel of three subject -matter
experts (all senior faculty members in the field of Business Administration and Marketing
Methodology) in terms of content and face validity, and necessary revisions were made.
Prior to finalizing the instrument, a pilot study was conducted to assess clarity and
linguistic appropriateness. Based on participant feedback, minor linguistic adjustme nts
were implemented to enhance the applicability and comprehensibility of the measurement
tool. Following this linguistic adaptation process, the survey was administered in Turkish
to the target population.
The implementation process of the study was conducted in accordance with ethical
principles. Ethical approval was obtained from the Ethics Committee of Sakarya University
of Applied Sciences (Date: 27.12.2021, Document No: E -26428519-044-33630). Participants
were informed about the purpose of the research and the confidentiality of their data, and
informed consent was obtained on a voluntary participation basis. Anonymity and
confidentiality principles were strictly maintained throughout the data collection process.

Conclusion
This study tested a research model developed within the context of the Value –Attitude–
Behavior (VAB) hierarchy model, focusing on the determinants of purchase intention
toward halal products. The proposed model demonstrates that religious belief and attitude
are the primary factors directly influencing purchase intention.
The findings statistically confirm that religious belief has a positive effect on both attitude
toward halal products and purchase intention. Moreover, attitude toward halal products
was found to have a positive and statistically significant effect on purc hase intention.
Another important finding of the study is the mediating role of attitude in the relationship
between religious belief and purchase intention. Overall, the results support the theoretical
assumptions of the VAB hierarchy model. Specifically,  the findings reveal that religious
belief constitutes a fundamental driving force in halal product consumption. Religious
belief is not merely an abstract construct; rather, it serves as an important determinant in
shaping attitudes toward halal products and in forming consumers’ purchase intentions.
Furthermore, the results indicate that the influence of religious belief on purchase intention
operates both directly and indirectly through attitude, highlighting the critical mediating
role of attitude.
At this point, religious belief can be considered a crucial starting point in influencing
purchase intention. However, attitude, which is formed not only through belief but also

According to Aglozo  and Cohen (2025), religion, despite its profound and multifaceted
influence on individuals’ lives, has not been sufficiently examined within the field of
consumer behavior. Research on the role of religious values in consumer behavior is still
evolving. T he hierarchical structure that links religious belief, attitude, and purchase
intention is also supported by the Theory of Planned Behavior developed by Ajzen (1991).
However, whereas the Theory of Planned Behavior generally treats religion as an external
variable in the process through which belief is translated into intention, the Value Attitude
Behavior hierarchy model conceptualizes religion as an internal value.
The present study demonstrates that both religious belief and attitude positively influence
halal product purchase intention. In other words, individuals’ levels of religious belief play
a significant role in shaping favorable attitudes toward halal produc ts and in forming
purchase intentions. The findings are consistent with prior research. For example,
Ustaahmetoğlu (2020) reported that participants with higher levels of religiosity exhibited
different attitudes and purchase intentions toward advertisements containing religious
messages compared to those with lower levels of religiosity. Similarly, Alam and Sayuti
(2011) found that consumers with more positive attitudes demonstrated stronger intentions
to purchase halal food products. Widad and Asih (2023) also concluded that religiosity
positively and significantly affects both attitude and purchase intention regardi ng halal
foods. Likewise, Efendi et al. (2019) found that religiosity had a positive and significant
impact on purchase intention among Muslim students in the context of halal -labeled
snacks. In another study, Lim et al. (2022) showed that attitude, subjective norms, and
perceived behavioral control p ositively influenced halal food purchase decisions among
non-Muslim consumers. These findings suggest that the perception of halal products may
signify a religious obligation for Muslim consumers, while reflecting a cultural or rational
preference for non -Muslim consumers. Moreover, the results indicate that halal product
perceptions are not limited solely to Muslim consumers and that attitude serves as a key
mechanism transforming perception into purchase intention.
The theoretical foundation of this study is grounded in the VAB hierarchy model proposed
by Homer and Kahle (1988). Their findings demonstrated that values directly influence
attitudes, attitudes directly influence behavior, and attitudes mediate the relat ionship
between values and behavior. Consistent with this framework, the present study confirms
that attitude mediates the effect of religious belief on purchase intention. Letsoin and
Riorini (2023) similarly reported the mediating role of product attitude in the relationship
between religious belief and purchase intention. Numerous other studies in the literature
support findings aligned with the results of this study (Abou-Youssef et al., 2015; Koc et al.,
2025; Salim et al., 2022). Furthermore, many halal studies in the literature are based on the
Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB). The few studies that use the VAB model generally rely
on abstract "general human values" (Homer & Kahle, 1988). In this study, however, the
concept of "Value" is positioned as "Religious Belief," thus carrying out a theoretical
expansion suitable to the nature of halal consumption.
In general, the results of this study confirm, in a manner consistent with the VAB hierarchy,
that religious belief, as a core value, plays a fundamental role in building consumer trust.
In this context, factors such as product ingredients, production proc esses, and compliance

Although the empirical findings of this study offer significant contributions to the halal
product market, they also have certain methodological and theoretical limitations. First, the
fact that the research data were obtained using convenience sampling a non-probability
sampling method and that the sample was limited to 451 participants restricts the sample’s
representativeness of the population and makes it difficult to directly generalize the
findings to the entire consumer population. Although the sample size achieved in this study
is considered statistically sufficient and robust for the analyses conducted in AMOS based
on Covariance-Based Structural Equation Modeling (CB-SEM), future research will need to
examine larger and more heterogeneous groups. From a methodological perspective, while
the use of a cross -sectional data structure in this study allows for the empirical validation
of causal relationships among variables, it limits the ability to track the dynamic course and
changes in these relationships over time. From a theoretical perspective, the study is limited
to the relationships between religious beliefs, attitudes, and purchase intention within the
framework of the Values-Attitudes-Behavior (VAB) Hierarchy Model; other potential key
variables that could play a role in the purchase decision —such as the perception of halal
certification, product quality, brand trust, price sensitivity, and social influence—have not
been included in the current model.
In light of  these limitations, various directions are suggested for future research. First, to
enhance the representativeness of the sample and the generalizability of the findings, it
would be beneficial for subsequent studies to employ probability-based sampling methods
and to reach broader groups of participants with diverse sociodemographic profiles.
Second, to overcome the static nature of cross -sectional studies—which rely on one -time
data collection—longitudinal research designs can be employed to examine the t rajectory
and sustainability of causal relationships between religious beliefs, attitudes, and purchase
intent over time. Finally, to enhance the explanatory power of the VAB hierarchical model,
incorporating variables such as perceptions of halal certific ation, brand trust, product
quality, or social impact into the model—as well as conducting comparative or moderator
effect analyses across different cultures or across product categories such as halal cosmetics,
finance, and tourism—will provide valuable contributions to the literature.
The findings from this study offer important insights for practitioners and marketing
managers in the halal products sector. The fact that religious belief influences purchase
intention both directly and indirectly through attitudes indicates that business es must
prioritize alignment with religious and ethical values in their retention strategies. Given the
mediating role of attitude in the relationship between religious belief and purchase
intention, marketing managers must carefully evaluate this situatio n. In this context,
marketing professionals can facilitate the translation of religious values into purchasing
behavior by establishing transparent supply chains to foster positive attitudes among
consumers, clearly communicating accredited halal certifica tion processes through digital
channels such as QR codes and on packaging, and positioning the concept of halal within
the framework of hygiene, quality, and food safety.
