Abstract

Background:

Augmented reality (AR) has emerged as a transformative technology in e
-
commerce, enabling immersive,
interactive, and personalized shopping experiences that help bridge the gap between online and offline retail environments.
Although the adoption of AR ha
s gained increasing attention in digital commerce, empirical evidence regarding the determinants
of AR
-
enabled purchasing behavior remains limited, particularly among young consumers in emerging digital economies.

Objective:

This study aims to examine the factors that influence AR
-
enabled online purchasing decisions and identify the
technological, psychological, social, and behavioral drivers that encourage consumers to adopt AR
-
supported shopping
experiences. The proposed fr
amework is illustrated through a survey conducted among young consumers in Hanoi, Vietnam, to
provide empirical evidence.

Methods:

An integrated research framework was developed based on the TRA, TPB, UTAUT2, and social impact models. We
employed a quantitative approach using data collected from 400 respondents who had prior experience using AR features in
online shopping.
SEM

was applied to test the proposed hypotheses and evaluate the relationships among the constructs.

Results:

The findings indicate that Habit, Belief, Satisfaction, Social Influence, Facilitating Conditions,
Perceived Number of
Users
, Subjective Norms, Effort Expectancy, and Attitude significantly influence consumers’ behavioral intention and purchase
decision. In addition, BI serves as a significant mediating mechanism through which AR adoption factors are translated into
actual purc
hasing behavior.

Conclusion:

By providing a comprehensive framework for understanding consumer adoption of AR technologies
in online shopping environments, this study contributes to the growing literature on AR
-
enabled commerce. The
findings offer practical implications for e
-
commerc
e platforms, digital retailers, and technology providers seeking
to enhance customer engagement, trust, and purchase conversion through AR
-
enabled shopping experiences. The
empirical evidence from Vietnam further enriches the understanding of AR adoption i
n emerging digital markets
while supporting the proposed framework’s broader applicability across e
-
commerce contexts.

Augmented Reality, online purchasing decisions, young consumers, behavioral intention, digital commerce

A.

Theoretical Foundations

This study draws upon four well
-
established theoretical perspectives to explain consumers’ adoption of AR

technologies and their online purchase decisions. First, the TAM

[
26]

proposes that the primary determinants of
technology acceptance are perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use. According to the TAM, users are more
likely to adopt a technology when they believe it can improve their performance and is easy to use. Gi
ven that AR
applications are designed to enhance product visualization and facilitate information processing, TAM provides a
useful foundation for understanding the acceptance of AR
-
enabled shopping tools by consumers.

Second, UTAUT and its extension UTAUT2 broaden the technology adoption perspective by incorporating
additional determinants, such as effort expectancy, social influence, facilitating conditions, and habit

[20], [27]
. These
factors are particularly relevant in digital commerce environments where consumers’ technology usage is influenced
not only by functional considerations but also by social interactions, facilitating resources, and accumulated
experiences. The inclu
sion of habit is especially important in explaining the continued engagement of consumers with
AR
-
enabled shopping applications.

Third, the TPB

[
18]

explains how attitudes, subjective norms, and PBC shape behavioral intentions and subsequent
actions. In the context of AR
-
enabled commerce, TPB suggests that consumers’ evaluations of AR technology, social
influences, and perceived ability to use AR appl
ications effectively may significantly affect their purchasing behavior.
As AR shopping often involves interactive and experiential elements, consumers’ attitudes toward AR and the
influence of peers or social networks are expected to play an important rol
e in influencing purchase decisions.

Finally, the ECM

[19]

explains post
-
adoption behavior by emphasizing the importance of satisfaction and
confirmation of expectations. ECM posits that when users’ initial expectations are confirmed through actual
experience and when they perceive satisfactory outcomes, they are

more likely to continue using a technology.
Previous studies have demonstrated that satisfaction is a critical determinant of the intention to continue using mobile
applications and online shopping platforms

[28].
Because AR applications are designed to provide engaging,
informative, and enjoyable shopping experiences, consumer satisfaction is expected to serve as an important
mechanism linking AR usage to purchasing outcomes.

Collectively, these theories provide a comprehensive foundation for examining the technological, psychological,
social, and behavioral determinants of AR adoption and OP behavior. By integrating TAM, UTAUT2, TPB, and ECM,
the present study develops a holis
tic framework that explains how consumers evaluate AR technologies, form
behavioral intentions, develop satisfaction, and ultimately make online purchase decisions within immersive digital
shopping environments.

V.

D
ISCUSSION

This study examined the determinants of AR
-
enabled online purchase decisions among young consumers by
integrating TRA, TPB, UTAUT2, ECM, and SIT. The findings provide strong empirical support for the proposed
framework, as all 10 hypotheses were accepted.
More importantly, the results demonstrate that AR
-
enabled purchasing
behavior cannot be fully explained by technological factors alone; rather, it emerges from the interaction of
technological readiness, cognitive evaluation, social influence, and accumula
ted user experience.

From a technological perspective, EE was identified as the strongest determinant of Purchase Decision (β = 0.269,
p < 0.001). This finding is consistent with UTAUT2
[20]

and prior AR
-
commerce studies, which emphasize that
consumers are more likely to adopt intuitive, easy
-
to
-
navigate, and capable of simplifying decision
-
making processes
[2], [16], [23]
. AR applications reduce the cognitive burden associated with online shopping by allowing consumers
to visualize products in realistic contexts before purchase. In contrast to traditional e
-
commerce interfaces that rely
heavily on static images and textual

descriptions, AR enables consumers to interact with products dynamically, thereby
reducing information asymmetry and increasing purchase confidence
[8], [15]
. This finding extends the literature on
AR by demonstrating that ease of use remains a critical adoption factor even among Generation Z consumers who are
already familiar with digital technologies.

The significant effect of Facilitating Conditions (FC) on Behavioral Intention (BI) (β = 0.118, p < 0.05) further
confirms the importance of technological infrastructure in AR adoption. This result supports the arguments of
[20]
,
who emphasized that access to appropriate resources and technical support significantly influences users’ willingness
to engage with new technologies. In emerging digital economies, such as Vietnam, consumers may still experience
variations in smartphone

capability, internet connectivity, and platform performance. Consequently, the effectiveness
of AR applications depends not only on innovative features but also on the availability of reliable technological
ecosystems that enable smooth and uninterrupted
user experiences.

The psychological dimension of AR adoption also demonstrated substantial explanatory power. Attitude (AT),
Belief (BL), and Satisfaction (SF) significantly influenced consumers’ behavioral intentions and purchasing decisions.
Among these variables, BL exer
ted one of the strongest effects on Behavioral Intention (β = 0.226, p < 0.01), indicating
that consumers are more willing to adopt AR technology when they perceive it as useful, trustworthy, and capable of
improving product evaluation. This finding aligns

with TAM
[26]

and previous studies, which suggest that perceived
usefulness remains a critical driver of technology acceptance
[13], [14]
. Consumers are likely to develop stronger
beliefs regarding the value of AR in the context of AR
-
enabled commerce when the technology provides realistic
product visualization, accurate size estimation, and enhanced product understanding. Such capabilities

reduce
uncertainty and increase confidence in purchasing decisions, particularly for experience
-
oriented products such as
fashion, cosmetics, and home decoration.

Another noteworthy finding concerns the role of Perceived Number of Users (NB) (β = 0.156, p < 0.05). Based on
the social impact theory, this result suggests that consumers are more likely to adopt AR technology when they
perceive it to be widely accepted
and frequently used by others
[25]
. The effect of NB extends the literature on AR
adoption by highlighting the importance of social proof mechanisms. Indicators, such as AR usage statistics, virtual
try
-

ty and enhance
confidence. In collectivist cultures such as Vietnam, where social validation and group norms strongly influence
consumer behavior, the perceived legitimacy of new technologies can be substantially increased by visible evidence
of widespread

The behavioral dimension represented by HB also significantly influenced Behavioral Intention (β = 0.191, p <

transforms adoption behavior into ro
utine practice
[20], [32]

functionalities, the effort required to use the technology decreases while perceived convenience increases.
Consequently, AR usage becomes integrated into the regular shopping processes of consumers rather than r
emaining
a novelty
-
driven activity.

Finally, Behavioral Intention (BI) significantly influenced Purchase Decision (β = 0.203, p < 0.01), confirming its
mediating role within the proposed framework. This finding reinforces the central propositions of TRA, TPB, and
UTAUT2, all of which identif
y intention as the most immediate predictor of actual behavior
[18], [20], [24]
. The
results indicate that technological characteristics, psychological evaluations, social influences, and habitual behaviors
primarily affect purchasing outcomes through their ability to strengthen consumers’ intentions to engage with AR
-
enabled shoppin
g experiences.

Several implications emerge from a managerial perspective. E
-
commerce platforms should prioritize intuitive AR
interfaces, realistic visualization quality, and seamless mobile integration to enhance perceived ease of use. To
strengthen belief and satisfact
ion, retailers should invest in advanced AR content, such as virtual fitting rooms,
interactive product demonstrations, and accurate 3D product models. Given the strong effects of social influence and
subjective norms, AR implementation strategies should b
e closely integrated with IM, livestream commerce, and UGC
campaigns. Furthermore, platforms should prominently display social proof indicators, such as virtual try
-
on counts,
customer reviews, and AR engagement statistics, to increase technology acceptanc
e. Finally, loyalty programs,
gamification mechanisms, and personalized AR experiences may encourage repeated usage and foster long
-
term habit
formation, thereby increasing AR adoption and purchase conversion rates.
