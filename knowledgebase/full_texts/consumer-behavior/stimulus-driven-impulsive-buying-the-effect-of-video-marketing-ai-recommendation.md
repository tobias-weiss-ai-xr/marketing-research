ABSTRACT
TikTok has increasingly become an important digital commerce platform where short -form
content, personalized recommendations, and consumer engagement influence purchasing behavior,
particularly in the women’s fashion market. This study examines the relationships among video
marketing, AI-based recommendation system, perceived value, and impulsive buying in the TikTok
women’s fashion context in Indonesia. Using a quantitative research approach, this study employed
purposive sampling to obtain 207 valid respondents and analyzed the data using Partial Least
Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) with SmartPLS 4. The results show that video
marketing and AI-based recommendation system are positively related to impulsive buying and
perceived value, while perceived value is positively related to impulsive buying. Perceived value also
partially mediates the relationships between both digital stimuli and impulsive buying. The study
contributes to the application of the Stimulus-Organism-Response (S-O-R) framework by examining
digital stimuli, internal value evaluation, and consumer behavioral responses in TikTok commerce.
The findings suggest that fashion sellers and digital commerce practitioners should focus on engaging
video content, relevant product recommendations, and effective communication of product value.

Keywords: AI-Based Recommendation System, Impulsive Buying, Perceived Value, TikTok, Video
Marketing.

INTRODUCTION
TikTok has developed from a short-video platform into a digital environment where
content consumption, consumer engagement, and commercial activities increasingly
intersect (Wahid et al., 2023). In Indonesia, TikTok recorded 157.6 million active users
as of July 2024, while TikTok Shop Indonesia generated a Gross Merchandise Value
(GMV) of USD 6.2 billion in 2024, making Indonesia a major TikTok Shop market
globally (Statista, 2024; Momentum Works, 2025). This rapid development makes
TikTok a relevant context for examining how consumers respond to digital marketing
stimuli throughout the purchasing process. Women’s fashion provides an appropriate
context because impulsive buying behavior has been examined in Indonesian fashion
settings, while recent research on TikTok Live has also identified impulse buying behavior
involving fashion products (Arianty & Purba, 2023; Kaniati et al., 2024).
Impulsive buying refers to spontaneous and relatively unplanned purchasing behavior
triggered by situational and marketing cues (Rook & Fisher, 1995; Beatty & Ferrell, 1998).
On TikTok, consumers are continuously exposed to short-form audiovisual content and
algorithmically selected recommendations (Wang, 2022; Wahid et al., 2023). Teo et al.
(2023) found that TikTok influences impulsive purchase behavior, while Kaniati et al.
(2024) identified impulse buying in fashion products on TikTok Live. Similarly, Fajar and
Otok (2024) showed that stimulus-related attributes are associated with online impulsive

buying. These findings highlight the importance of examining how specific digital stimuli

The first research gap concerns video marketing. Previous studies by Gao et al. (2022),
Shen and Wang (2024), and Luo et al. (2025) have established the relevance of short-
video environments to consumer responses. However, many have focused primarily on
purchase intention rather than the mechanisms underlying impulsive buying. In addition,
Fan et al. (2024) showed that short-video content attributes can influence perceived value,
suggesting that the characteristics of video -based content may shape consumers’
evaluations of the benefits they receive from products or shopping experiences. However,
the role of video marketing in generating impulsive buying through perceived value,
particularly in TikTok women’s fashion commerce, remains insufficiently examined.
The second research gap relates to perceived value as an internal consumer evaluation.
Perceived value represents a consumer’s judgment of what is received relative to what is
given up in a transaction (Sweeney & Soutar, 2001). Yang et al. (2021) established the
relationship between perceived value and impulse buying in mobile commerce, while
Rani et al. (2023) examined value dimensions in TikTok -related impulsive buying.
Meanwhile, Zhang et al. (2024) explored the role of perceived value in live-streaming
commerce. Nevertheless, limited evidence explains how perceived value functions as an
internal mechanism linking multiple TikTok-based marketing and technological stimuli
to impulsive buying behavior. This limitation is particularly relevant in women’s fashion
commerce, where visual content, product presentation, and personalized
recommendations may jointly shape consumers’ evaluations before purchase.
The third research gap concerns AI-based recommendation system. Recommendation
systems influence the relevance and personalization of the products and content
consumers encounter. Previous studies have examined personalized recommendations
from a Stimulus-Organism-Response (S-O-R) perspective and investigated consumer
responses to algorithmic recommendations (Jeong et al., 2022; Wang, 2022; Gallin &
Portes, 2024). TikTok-related studies have likewise examined recommendation
algorithms and their influence on consumer responses (Febriani, 2025; Indrianto et al.,
2025). However, evidence remains limited regarding how AI-based recommendation
system shape perceived value and subsequently influence impulsive buying behavior,
particularly within TikTok’s women’s fashion context.
This study applies the S-O-R framework developed by Mehrabian and Russell (1974)
to explain how digital stimuli influence consumer behavior through internal evaluations.
In this framework, video marketing and the AI-based recommendation system represent
environmental stimuli, perceived value represents the organismic evaluation, and
impulsive buying represents the behavioral response. Although previous studies have
examined these variables individually, their integrated relationship through perceived
value remains limited, particularly in the context of women’s fashion commerce on
TikTok in Indonesia.
Based on these research gaps, this study examines the influence of video marketing
and AI-based recommendation system on impulsive buying of women’s fashion products
on TikTok, with perceived value as a mediating variable. The study examines the direct
relationships among video marketing, AI-based recommendation system, perceived
value, and impulsive buying, as well as the mediating role of perceived value in these
relationships. Through this model, the study contributes to the application of the S-O-R
framework by examining parallel digital stimuli and the internal value evaluation process
underlying impulsive buying in Indonesian women’s fashion social commerce.

Video marketing refers to the use of short-form audiovisual content to communicate

The Effect of Perceived Value
A consumer’s judgment of what is received relative to what is sacrificed in an exchange
is defined as perceived value (Sweeney & Soutar, 2001). Within the S-O-R framework,
digital content functions as a stimulus that shapes consumers’ internal evaluations before
behavioral responses occur. Fan et al. (2024) found that short-video content attributes
influence perceived value, while Shen and Wang (2024) examined the role of short-video
marketing in shaping consumer responses. In the context of TikTok women’s fashion,
engaging video marketing may therefore influence how consumers evaluate product
benefits, relevance, and attractiveness. Recent evidence confirms that short-video content
quality, informativeness, interactivity, and entertainment can strengthen consumers’
perceived value by improving their functional, emotional, and experiential evaluations
(Wu & Zhang, 2024; Tong et al., 2025). Video marketing is expected to contribute to
consumers’ perceived value formation, leading to the following hypothesis:
AI-based recommendation system may also shape consumers’ internal evaluations by
increasing the relevance and personalization of product information. Jeong et al. (2022)
highlighted the role of personalized recommendations in consumer responses, while
Wang (2022) discussed recommendation algorithms within the TikTok environment.
Febriani (2025) further examined TikTok recommendation algorithms, perceived
personalization, and purchase decisions. These findings suggest that personalized
recommendations can influence how consumers perceive the benefits and relevance of
recommended products. Recent research further indicates that the quality of personalized
recommendation systems can enhance perceived value, particularly when
recommendations provide relevant information and improve the shopping experience

The Effect of Perceived Value on Impulsive Buying
Perceived Value represents consumers’ internal assessment of the benefits obtained
from a product relative to what they give up in the exchange. Within online commerce,
favorable value evaluations can strengthen consumers’ responses to purchasing stimuli by
increasing the perceived benefits and attractiveness of products. Yang et al. (2021) found
that perceived value is associated with impulse buying in mobile commerce, indicating
that consumers who perceive greater value may be more likely to engage in spontaneous
purchasing. In TikTok-related consumption contexts, Rani et al. (2023) examined value
dimensions and their relationship with impulsive buying, demonstrating the relevance of
value perceptions in explaining consumers’ spontaneous purchase behavior.  Recent
studies further demonstrate that perceived value can translate favorable product
evaluations into impulsive purchasing responses in digital retail environments. This
relationship is particularly relevant in fashion and social -commerce contexts, where
hedonic and utilitarian value perceptions can encourage spontaneous purchase decisions
(Sun et al., 2023; Buford & Cho, 2025). These findings support the view that perceived
value can function as an important internal evaluation in translating consumers’
perceptions of products into impulsive buying responses.
Further evidence indicates that value -related factors are relevant to impulsive
consumption across digital commerce contexts. Dang et al. (2025) and Silalahi et al.
(2025) provided broader evidence regarding the association between value-related factors
and impulsive consumption. In TikTok women’s fashion commerce, consumers may
perceive greater value when products are considered beneficial, attractive, relevant, and
worthwhile. Such positive evaluations may increase consumers’ willingness to make
spontaneous purchase decisions, particularly when they encounter products through
engaging digital content and personalized recommendations. Evidence from
omnichannel and online retail settings also indicates that value-related evaluations are
closely connected with consumers’ impulse buying responses (Pereira et al., 2023; Wu &
Tseng, 2022). Therefore, higher perceived value is expected to increase consumers’
tendency to engage in impulsive buying
H5: Perceived value has a positive effect on impulsive buying.
The Mediating Effect of Perceived Value
According to the S-O-R framework, external stimuli may influence behavioral
responses through internal consumer evaluations. Video marketing and the AI-Based
recommendation system may shape consumers’ perceived value by providing attractive,
relevant, and personalized product information, which can subsequently influence
impulsive buying. Fan et al. (2024) found that short-video content attributes influence
perceived value, while Yang et al. (2021) showed that perceived value is associated with
impulse buying. Nurmalasari et al. (2025) further found that perceived value positively
influences impulsive buying among TikTok Shop users, reinforcing the role of value
evaluation in translating digital commerce stimuli into spontaneous purchasing responses.
These findings indicate that digital marketing stimuli may shape consumers’ internal
evaluations before influencing their purchasing responses.
Perceived value may therefore mediate the relationship between digital marketing
stimuli and impulsive buying. Xue et al. (2024) found that perceived value mediates the
influence of social-commerce cues on impulse buying, demonstrating that consumers’
value evaluations can translate external stimuli into spontaneous purchasing responses.
Similarly, Ying (2025) showed that perceived value mediates the effect of short-video

RESEARCH METHODS
A quantitative methodology, grounded in positivism and structured as an explanatory
design, was adopted for this investigation. The research examined the relationships
among video marketing, AI-based recommendation system, perceived value, and
impulsive buying in the context of women’s fashion products on TikTok. Data analysis
was conducted using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM)
with SmartPLS 4. The population consisted of Indonesian female TikTok users who were
at least 18 years old and had purchased women’s fashion products through TikTok. The
study applied purposive sampling to select respondents who met these criteria. The final
analytical sample comprised 207 participants, derived from an initial pool of 208
responses after discarding one invalid entry. This dataset was acquired via a web-based
questionnaire administered from February to May 2026.
The survey instrument was divided into two distinct parts. While the initial segment
gathered demographic profiles, the subsequent section presented 32 items evaluating four
key constructs: video marketing, AI-based recommendation system, perceived value, and
impulsive buying. Each item was assessed on a five-point Likert scale, anchored by
‘strongly disagree’ and ‘strongly agree’. The measurement model consists of four
variables: video marketing, AI-based recommendation system, perceived value, and
impulsive buying. Video marketing is measured through indicators covering the creativity
and engagement of fashion videos, aesthetic visual presentation, clarity of product

information, adequacy of information for purchase decisions, entertainment value,
viewing enjoyment, immediate purchase motivation, and the ability of videos to convince
consumers of product value, adapted from Gao et al. (2022) and Shen and Wang (2024).
The AI-based recommendation system is measured through indicators reflecting
recommendation fit with consumers’ tastes and styles, relevance to current needs,
discovery of previously unseen products, discovery of interesting fashion items,
personalization, perceived algorithmic understanding of fashion preferences, spontaneous
purchase interest, and unexpected product discovery, based on Jeong et al. (2022) and
Wang (2022). Perceived value is measured through indicators covering value for price,
consistency between product descriptions and actual products, price affordability,
promotional benefits, satisfaction, purchase decision evaluation, social -image
enhancement, and keeping up with fashion trends, drawing on Sweeney and Soutar
(2001) and Rani et al. (20 23). Impulsive buying is measured through unplanned
purchases, immediate purchasing, strong purchase urges, inability to resist attractive
products, emotion-based purchasing, post-purchase satisfaction, limited consideration of
financial consequences, and purchasing without prior product comparison, based on
Rook and Fisher (1995) and Beatty and Ferrell (1998).
The measurement model was evaluated through outer-model assessment, including
factor loadings, Cronbach’s alpha, rho_A, Composite Reliability (CR), Average Variance
Extracted (AVE), and discriminant validity using the Heterotrait–Monotrait Ratio
(HTMT) criterion. The structural model was evaluated through inner-model assessment,
including VIF, R-square, f-square, path coefficients, t-statistics, and p-values. Hypothesis
testing was conducted using bootstrapping with 5,000 subsamples. Mediation analysis for
H6 and H7 was assessed using the Variance Accounted For (VAF) procedure.

generating direct behavioral responses. In the context of TikTok women’s -fashion

digital stimuli that can shape consumers’ perceptions of product value and their tendency
to make unplanned purchases.
The findings confirm a significant positive relationship between video marketing and
both perceived value and impulsive buying. This suggests that video -based product
presentation can influence how consumers evaluate fashion products while
simultaneously encouraging immediate purchasing responses. This finding is consistent
with Gao et al. (2022) and Teo et al. (2023), who highlighted the capacity of short-video
environments to stimulate unplanned purchasing behavior. It also supports Fan et al.
(2024), who emphasized the role of video features in shaping consumer value judgments.
The alignment with these studies suggests that visually engaging and informative product
presentations can enhance consumers’ perceptions of product benefits while creating
conditions that encourage spontaneous purchasing. Nevertheless, the relationship
between video marketing and impulsive buying remains context-dependent and should
not be interpreted as an absolute outcome across all video marketing activities.
The AI-based recommendation system also demonstrates positive relationships with
perceived value and impulsive buying. The findings suggest that relevant and personalized
product exposure can contribute to consumers’ behavioral responses and evaluations of
product benefits. This result is consistent with Jeong et al. (2022) and Gallin and Portes
(2024), who discussed the role of recommendation systems, personalization, and
algorithm-related experiences in shaping consumer behavior. Compared with video
marketing, however, direct empirical evidence concerning the relationship between AI-
based recommendation system and perceived value remains relatively limited. The
present finding therefore provides contextual support that personalized recommendations
can strengthen the perceived relevance and usefulness of products within TikTok
women’s-fashion commerce.
Perceived value is also positively related to impulsive buying, indicating that
consumers’ evaluations of product benefits relative to sacrifices are associated with their
spontaneous purchasing responses. This finding supports Yang et al. (2021), who
examined perceived value and impulse buying in mobile commerce. It is further consistent
with Rani et al. (2023), Dang et al. (2025), and Silalahi et al. (2025), which indicate that
value-related factors can contribute to impulsive consumption in digital comme rce
environments. Thus, perceived value represents an important internal evaluation through
which consumers assess the attractiveness and benefits of products encountered in digital
shopping environments.
The mediation findings further indicate that perceived value partially mediates the
relationships between video marketing and impulsive buying and between AI-based
recommendation system and impulsive buying. This result reinforces the S -O-R
perspective because digital stimuli can influence behavioral responses through internal
evaluations, while the significant direct relationships indicate that perceived value does
not fully account for these effects. This finding is consistent with Fan et al. (2024) on video
content and value evaluation, Yang et al. (2021) on perceived value and impulse buying,
and Li et al. (2022) on organism-mediated responses in digital commerce. This study
therefore extends the application of S-O-R by positioning video marketing and AI-based
recommendation system as parallel digital stimuli, perceived value as the organismic
evaluation, and impulsive buying as the behavioral response.

engaging video content with relevant and personalized recommendation strategies. Video
marketing should provide clear product information, attractive visual presentation, and
convincing communication of product benefits, while recommendation systems should
emphasize product relevance and personalization. Communicating functional, economic,

CONCLUSION
This study examined the relationships among video marketing, AI-based
recommendation system, perceived value, and impulsive buying in the TikTok women’s
fashion context. The findings demonstrate that digital stimuli within TikTok commerce
are associated with impulsive buying through both direct pathways and internal value
evaluation mechanisms. In addition, perceived value partially mediates the relationships
between the two digital stimuli and impulsive buying. These findings indicate that
consumer responses in TikTok commerce are shaped through both direct digital stimulus
pathways and pathways involving internal value evaluation. The study contributes to the
application of the S-O-R framework by examining video marketing and AI-based
recommendation system as digital stimuli, perceived value as the organismic evaluation,
and impulsive buying as the behavioral response. Within the TikTok women’s-fashion
context, the findings provide empirical support for the relevance of S-O-R in explaining
how digital stimuli may relate to consumer behavioral responses through internal
evaluations.
The findings suggest that fashion sellers and digital commerce practitioners should
focus on engaging video content, relevant product recommendations, and effective
communication of product value. Video presentations that clearly communicate product
benefits and recommendation-based exposure that aligns with consumer preferences may
support value evaluation and purchasing responses. However, recommendation
mechanisms should be understood as platform-based processes rather than factors directly
controlled by sellers. Acknowledging its boundaries, this research is limited by potential
self-reporting biases and constrained generalizability due to purposive sampling.
Additionally, the cross-sectional nature and exclusive focus on female fashion limit the
temporal and contextual scope. Future investigations should diversify product categories,
expand sample frames, and evaluate alternative social commerce settings. Future studies
may also consider longitudinal approaches and examine the potential roles of digital
literacy and TikTok usage intensity, as these factors were not included in the present study
and require separate empirical testing.
