Abstract
This study maps research at the intersection of environmental, social, and governance (ESG) issues and
consumer behavior. It identifies documents, sources, and authors with citation influence and examines the
themes that shaped the corpus from 2011 to 2023 . The review followed the PRISMA and SPAR -4-SLR
guidelines. The final corpus contained 1,186 SSCI-indexed articles from the Web of Science Core Collection.
VOSviewer was used for citation analysis, bibliographic coupling, and keyword co-occurrence. KH Coder was
used to compare theme prevalence across six periods. Firm, finance, and disclosure studies accounted for much
of the selected corpus. Organisational ESG Dynamics appeared across all periods, but no theme differed
significantly after the Holm correction. Trust, perceived value, purchase intention, loyalty, and willingness to
pay were outside the high-frequency keyword core. The study combines network mapping with period-based
content analysis and identifies the limited visibility of consumer -response c onstructs within the selected
corpus.

1. Introduction
Sustainability is fundamentally about ensuring the enduring health of economic, environmental, and social
systems to meet present needs without compromising the future (Baratta et al., 2023; Di Simone et al., 2022;
Hallinger, 2021; Li et al., 2023; Shalhoob & Hussainey, 2023) . It incorporates practices aimed at reducing
carbon emissions and achieving the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), focusing on a comprehensive
approach that balances economic growth, environmental protection , and social progress. Highlighted by
international initiatives such as the United Nations SDGs, it promotes protecting and preserving the Earth’s
resources for future generation s. Sustainability is a multifaceted strategy that includes conserving natural
resources, ensuring social fairness, and fostering economic growth to support the Earth’s long-term well-being
(Baratta et al., 2023; Di Simone et al., 2022; Gjergji et al., 2020; Shalhoob & Hussainey, 2023).
Environmental, social, and governance (ESG) criteria describe how firms manage environmental effects,
social responsibilities, and governance processes. Firms use ESG measures to report on emissions, labor
practices, board oversight, and risk controls. These measures also shape stakeholders’ judgments of a firm’s
conduct. Previous studies have related ESG practices to sustainability, innovation, disclosure , and financial
outcomes (Di Simone et al., 2022; Kassem & Trenz, 2020; Li et al., 2023; Shalhoob & Hussainey, 2023). This
area of work integrates ESG into firm strategy and stakeholder communication.

Consumer behavior is one aspect of this stakeholder response. In this review, consumer behavior includes
trust, perceived value, purchase intention, loyalty, and willingness to pay. Consumers may use ESG
information when comparing firms, brands, products, or services. The value of this information depends on its
content, source, and clarity (Kassem & Trenz, 2020; Sardanelli et al., 2022). Therefore, ESG research should
explain how corporate actions and disclosures are connected to consumer responses (de Souza Barbosa et al.,
2023; Shalhoob & Hussainey, 2023).
Previous studies have mapped ESG research across industries, sustainability performance, and risk
management. For example, Baratta et al. (2023) studied ESG practices and carbon emissions in the industry.
de Souza Barbosa et al. (2023) reviewed ESG integration and corporate sustainability. Gomez‐Valencia et al.
(2021) examine the relationship  between sustainability and risk management. These reviews expand the
broader ESG knowledge base, but consumer outcomes are not the main unit of analysis.
Two areas require further examination. First, ESG reviews have focused on firms, finance, disclosure, and
sustainability outcomes. The position of consumer outcomes within ESG and consumer behavior research
remains unclear. This review examines the visibil ity of consumer response constructs within the selected
corpus and the thresholds applied. Second, few reviews have combined citation-network analysis with period-
based content analysis. This study addresses these needs through keyword mapping and period-based content
analysis.
To meet these gaps, this study maps and synthesises research on ESG and consumer behavior. It uses
systematic literature network analysis and content analysis (Colicchia et al., 2012; Wong et al., 2022; Wong et
al., 2023). The study uses a fixed dataset from the Web of Science (WOS) to support replication  (Sauer &
Seuring, 2023; Vergura et al., 2023) . The analysis does not test causal relationships. It examines citation
influence, corpus structure, and differences across periods. Three research questions guide the review.
• RQ1. Which documents, sources, and authors have the highest citation influence in research on ESG and
consumer behavior?
• RQ2. Which themes structure the corpus, and how does their prevalence vary across the selected periods
from 2011 to 2023?
• RQ3. Which underexamined areas and future research directions emerge from the network and temporal
results?
2. Materials and Methods
2.1. Review Design
This study follows the PRISMA and SPAR-4-SLR guidelines to define, screen, and report the review process
(Palmatier et al., 2017; Paul et al., 2023; Paul et al., 2021). The study adopts the SLNA workflow and content
analysis, as shown in Figure 1. SLNA combines a systematic  literature review and bibliometric analysis
(Colicchia et al., 2012) . Content analysis extends network results by examining variation in terms across
periods (Ahuvia, 2001; Higuchi, 2016, 2017). Figure 1 presents the sequence from record selection to network
mapping and period comparisons. Each method serves a part of the research objective.
Citation analysis identifies influential documents, sources, and authors based on citation rates.
Bibliographic coupling clusters documents sharing common references. Keyword co-occurrence maps terms
that appear in the same records. KH Coder groups terms in abstracts and compares them across different
periods from 2010 to 2023. This combines the field structure with theme change (Cobo et al., 2011; Donthu et
al., 2021; van Eck & Waltman, 2010).

2.2. Data Extraction
The search was conducted in the WOS Core Collection on March 3, 2024. The literature sample included
journal articles indexed in the Social Sciences Citation Index (SSCI). The SSCI was selected for two reasons
(Sauer & Seuring, 2023; Vergura et al., 2023) . First, it covers journals on management, marketing, and
consumer behavior. The journal selection process also supports the inclusion of research that meets publication
standards. Second, WOS provides citation records in a single data format. This format supports bibliometric
analysis, reduces data cleaning, and enables repeated analyses. However, WOS and Scopus differ in terms of
journal coverage (Archambault et al., 2009; Asubiaro et al., 2024; Mongeon & Paul -Hus, 2015). Therefore,
the choice of database defines the literature sample and does not indicate database superiority.

2.3. Search and Screening
The search was conducted in the Web of Science Core Collection on March 3, 2024. The Topic field, which
covers titles, abstracts, author keywords, and Keywords Plus, was searched using TS=((“environmental, social,
and governance” OR ESG) AND “consumer behavio*”). The search covered publications from 2010 to 2023.
Only English-language articles, including early-access articles indexed in the SSCI, were retained. The initial
search identified 7,642 records. The publication-year filter reduced this number to 2,277, while the document-
type filter reduced it to 2,046. The SSCI and language filters further reduced the sample to 1,203 and 1,202
records, respectively. Title and abstract screening excluded 16 records that did not address ESG in relation to
consumer behavior. The final corpus comprised 1,186 articles published between 2011 and 2023. The search

2.4. Analysis Procedures
Excel was used to summarise corpus characteristics. VOSviewer version 1.6.20 was used for citation analysis,
bibliographic coupling, and keyword co-occurrence (Cobo et al., 2011; van Eck & Waltman, 2010). KH Coder
version 3b07e was used for frequency analysis, correspondence analysis, hierarchical clustering, cross -
tabulation, and co -occurrence analysis (Higuchi, 2016, 2017) . Abstracts were the unit of text analysis.
Normalised citations were used for RQ1. Jaccard coefficients were used to examine term and period co -
occurrence.
The limits were established before the maps were analyzed. Document citation analysis required at least 40
citations. Source and author analyses required at least five documents and one citation. Bibliographic coupling

required at least 180 citations, and keyword analysis required at least 25 occurrences of a keyword. Each results
subsection reports the number of records that met the threshold.
In VOSviewer, the node size indicates the selected weight and correlates with the number of network
connections. The cluster colors identify the groups created by the software. The total link strength reflects the
combined strength of a node connection. Normalised citations enable comparisons across publication years
and fields. They show citation influence within the corpus but do not assess the study quality.
2.5. Temporal Comparison and Coverage Boundary
The main analysis used six periods: 2011–2017, 2018–2019, 2020, 2021, 2022, and 2023. The first two periods
combined years with few records. Annual periods began in 2020 because the publication count reached 120.
The unequal period lengths should be considered when interpreting the results. The comparisons describe
variation within the selected periods and should not be interpreted as annual trends.
The search was conducted in March 2024, but the coverage period ended in 2023 to avoid including an
incomplete publication year. Later database updates may change the number of records indexed for 2023.
Therefore, the 2023 count reflects the records availa ble on the search date and should not be treated as
complete.
The analysis answers the research questions  posed in this study . RQ1 was answered through citation
analysis. RQ2 was analysed through keyword co -occurrence and period analysis using KH Coder. RQ3 is
supported by bibliographic coupling and integrated theme results. Descriptive statistics provide context, but
they do not answer any of the research questions.

3.2. Bibliometric Network Analysis
VOSviewer was used for citation analysis, bibliographic coupling, and keyword co -occurrence (van Eck &
Waltman, 2010). Citation analysis identifies the influence within the corpus. Bibliographic coupling groups
documents through shared references. Keyword co-occurrence maps the conceptual structure.
Citation analysis answers RQ1. Bibliographic coupling and keyword co -occurrence identify the thematic
structure examined in RQ2. The integration of the network and temporal findings supports the future research
directions developed for RQ3.
Citation analyses were conducted for documents, sources, and authors. The rankings use normali sed
citations to minimise discrepancies related to the publication year and field. The tables include raw citations
and total link strength. The results revealed the influence present in the selected dataset. They do not establish
the quality of a study or journal.
Bibliographic coupling measures the shared references of citing documents. Documents with more shared
references appear closer together on the map. This method suits the current topic structure because recent
documents can be coupled before they gain many co -citations. Cluster labels were assigned after reviewing
the document titles. The labels describe the number of documents and do not examine relationships.
Keyword co-occurrence measures how terms appear together across records. The map identifies groups of
connected concepts. Four search domain terms were removed to reduce the field-definition effect. VOSviewer
then grouped the remaining terms. KH Coder was then used to test how related themes appeared across periods.

• Cluster 1: ESG Investment Strategies and Performance Measures
Cluster 1 includes ESG ratings, green bonds, crisis periods , and responsible investing. The documents
explore how ESG factors influence valuations, portfolio selection, and shareholder results. This cluster centres
on investors and behavioural questions. It pays less attention to consumer trust or purchase choices. Consider
this difference when interpreting the results.
• Cluster 2: Corporate ESG Activity and Financial Outcomes
Cluster 2 covers ESG activity, firm value, controversies, and financial performance. The documents assess
how firms gain or lose value from ESG activities. This cluster relates ethical responsibilities to financial
outcomes. However, it  does not reveal how consumers assess these actions. This pattern indicates that
consumer evaluation receives limited attention within this cluster.
• Cluster 3: Governance, Disclosure, and Reporting
Cluster 3 focuses on board structure, CEO authority, and the quality of reporting and disclosure . These
studies explain how companies generate and manage ESG data. Clusters provide a foundation for consumer
research because consumers need information before making decisions. The current collection of studies does
not trace the entire process from disclosure to consumer choices. Future studies should examine this pathway.

4. Discussion
The results show that firm, finance, and disclosure studies account for much of the selected corpus. This pattern
is consistent with reviews that connect ESG with corporate sustainability and performance (Baratta et al., 2023;
de Souza Barbosa et al., 2023) . Investment, risk, and financial performance also appear across the network.
Consumer-response constructs have limited visibility in the high-frequency keyword map.
Organisational ESG Dynamics appeared across all periods. This result is consistent with studies that treat
ESG as part of firm strategy, innovation, and governance (Di Simone et al., 2022; Li et al., 2023; Yang et al.,
2024). Innovation and Sustainability and ESG Financials had different shares across the six periods. However,
the corrected tests did not confirm a systematic temporal trend.
Corporate Transparency remained a central theme. This result supports the calls for ESG metrics, reporting
systems, and data controls  (Baratta et al., 2023; Gholami et al., 2022; Kassem & Trenz, 2020) . The lower
percentage in 2023 should not be interpreted as a loss of importance. Disclosure terms may have become linked
to other themes as the field expanded. The network connection between transparency and organisational ESG
activity supports this interpretation.

The absence of these constructs from the high-frequency keyword set suggests that firm actions and investor
responses are more visible than consumer -response processes within the selected network. Future models
should connect ESG signals with consumer evaluation and behavioral outcomes. They should also distinguish
consumer decisions from investor decisions.
5. Contributions and Implications
5.1. Theoretical Contributions
This study proposes a three -stage framework of signal, evaluation, and response. Signaling theory explains
how firms communicate ESG actions to reduce information gaps between firms and consumers (Lee et al.,
2022). Organisational ESG Dynamics represents the source of the signal. Corporate Transparency represents
its channel and clarity. The stimulus –organism–response model explains how ESG information can shape
consumer evaluation and behavior. ESG information serv es as the stimulus. Trust and perceived value
represent consumer evaluation. Purchase intention, loyalty, and willingness to pay represent consumer
responses (V afaei-Zadeh et al., 2024; Wong, 2023) . Investment Analysis remains a separate path because
investors use ESG information for financial decisions. The framework is proposed for future testing. This
review does not test causal relationships.
5.2. Practical and Managerial Implications
Managers should connect ESG operations with information that consumers can evaluate. The Organisational
ESG Dynamics theme shows that disclosure must begin with internal data and governance  issues. The
Corporate Transparency theme shows a need for metrics, definitions, and evidence. Firms should avoid making
claims that cannot be linked to actions or outcomes. Product and brand messages should explain how an ESG
action changes an offer or its impact. This approach can support trust without treating disclosu re as a
promotion.
Policymakers can use these findings to develop reporting and claim standards. Standard terms may reduce
the gap between firm reports and consumer understanding. Reporting rules can require firms to state the scope,
unit, period, and evidence supporting eac h ESG claim. Consumer agencies can also examine product labels
and digital claims for consistency. Separate presentation formats can provide investors and consumers with
information suited to their decisions.
5.3. Research Limitations
This study has several limitations. First, the dataset was drawn only from the Web of Science Core Collection
and the SSCI index; therefore, it may not represent the full scope of ESG research. Second, the search terms
may have omitted studies that used re lated terms, such as consumer response, customer behavior, purchase
intention, or sustainable consumption. Third, the review included only English -language articles, including
early-access articles, and excluded other document types and languages. Finally,  citation counts may be
affected by database coverage, publication age, and the accuracy of author names.
The content analysis relied on abstracts rather than full texts. It was also exploratory and did not apply an
a priori dictionary to identify consumer -response constructs. In addition, the six periods differed in length
because publication volume was low b efore 2020, and the March 2024 search may not have captured every
article published in 2023. Future reviews should combine Web of Science with another database, such as
Scopus; expand the search terms; compare different threshold settings; apply a theory -led coding dictionary;
and analyse full texts where access permits. Updated searches should also assess whether consumer-response
constructs remain underrepresented after 2023.

5.4. Future Studies Recommendations
There are four recommendations for future studies: (a) organi sational ESG dynamics, (b) corporate
transparency and reporting quality, (c) investment analysis, and (d) cross-country studies. First, future studies
should examine how organisational ESG dynamics affect consumer behavior. The model should connect firm
actions to consumer awareness, trust, and perceived value. The analysis should differentiate between actual
ESG performance and ESG claims. It should also compare the ESG signals.
Second, f uture studie s should examine the relationship between corporate transparency and reporting
quality. For example, Baratta et al. (2023)  indicated limits in ESG metrics, while Kassem and Trenz (2020)
discussed reporting systems. Future studies should investigate whether standard metrics decrease confusion
and build trust. They could compare firm-level reports with product -level labels. Future studies could also
examine how disclosure quality influences purchase intention and customer loyalty.
Third, investment analysis should be a separate stakeholder stream. Investors and consumers may use the
same ESG information but have different objectives. Future reviews should compare these groups rather than
merging them. Studies have explored whether market signals reach consumers through price, reputation, or
branding. This clarifies the boundaries of ESG consumer behavior.
Fourth, cross-country studies should examine whether standards, culture, and market structures influence
these patterns. Full-text reviews should compare theories, methods, samples, and consumer outcomes. Updated
searches should assess whether consumer terms receive more attention after 2023. Database comparisons could
also examine whether the field structure varies across sources.
6. Conclusion
This study analy ses 1,186 articles from SSCI -indexed journals in the WOS database to examine  the
relationship between ESG and consumer behavior. Citation analysis identifies the top three most influential
documents based on their normalised citation rates. Berg et al. (2022), Broadstock et al. (2021), and Pedersen
et al. (2021) are the top three influential documents. The top three influential sources are (a) Business Strategy
and the Environment, (b) Sustainability, and (c) Corporate Social Responsibility and Environmental
Management, respectively. The top three influential authors are Serafeim, Chang, and Feng.
The network and content results provide the main contributions. VOSviewer identifies disclosure,
investment, governance, and market themes. KH Coder shows that organisational ESG dynamics persisted
across six periods. (a) Innovation and Sustainability and (b) ESG Financials gained share, whereas (c)
Corporate Transparency and (e) Investment Analysis remained central. Consumer constructs were not part of
the high-frequency keyword core.
Future studies should trace how firm actions and disclosures influence trust, perceived value, purchase
intention, loyalty, and willingness to pay. They should also separate consumer and investor outcomes. This
review provides a field map and a time-based account of theme changes.
