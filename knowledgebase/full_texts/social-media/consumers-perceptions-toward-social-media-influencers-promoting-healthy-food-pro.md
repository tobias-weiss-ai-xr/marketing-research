Abstract
The increasing awareness of health and wellness has significantly influenced consumers' food consumption patterns, while soci al media
influencers have emerged as important sources of information and recommendations regarding healthy food products. The pres ent study was
undertaken to examine the impact of social media influencers on consumers' purchase intention toward healthy food products. S pecifically,
the study assessed consumers' socio -economic characteristics, social media usage patterns, perceptions t oward social media influencers, and
their influence on purchase intention. A descriptive and analytical research design was employed, and primary data were colle cted from 400
social media users through a structured online questionnaire. Descriptive statistics such as frequency, percentage, and mean scores were used
to analyze the data. The study was guided by Uses and Gratifications Theory (UGT), Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB), Health Belief Model
(HBM), Stimulus–Organism–Response (S-O-R) Theory, and Social Cognitive Theory (SCT) to explain the relationship between social media
influencer exposure and consumer purchase intention. The findings revealed that the majority of respondents were young and ed ucated
consumers who actively engaged with social media platforms. Instagram emerged as the most preferred platform for obtaining information
related to healthy food products, followed by YouTube. Respondents generally exhibited positive perceptions toward social media influencers,
particularly regarding their ability to increase awareness, provide useful information, and facilitate informed purchasing decisions. Influencer
credibility, trustworthiness, and health -related recommendations were found to positively shape consumers' attitudes toward healthy food
products and enhance their purchase intentions. The study concludes that social media influencers play a significant role in promoting healthy
food consumption by influencing consumers' knowledge, perceptions, and behavioral intentions. The findings provide v aluable insights for
food marketers, health promoters, and policymakers seeking to utilize influencer marketing strategies to encourage healthier food choices
among consumers.

Introduction
The rapid growth of social media platforms has transformed
the way consumers access information and make purchasing
decisions. Social media influencers  have emerged as
important opinion leaders who shape consumer attitudes and
behaviors through product recommendations, reviews, and
personal experiences. In the healthy food sector, influencers
play a significant role in promoting nutritional awareness,
healthy lifestyles, and food choices among consumers. Their
ability to communicate information in an engaging and
relatable manner has increased consumers' reliance on
influencer-generated content when evaluating healthy food
products.
Consumer perception toward influencers is a crucial
determinant of the effectiveness of influencer marketing.
Factors such as credibility, trustworthiness, expertise,
authenticity, and the quality of information provided by
influencers can significantly in fluence consumers’ attitudes
toward promoted products. Positive perceptions of
influencers may enhance consumers' confidence in healthy
food products and increase their willingness to purchase
them. Therefore, understanding consumers' perceptions
toward social media influencers is essential for marketers and
healthy food brands seeking to develop effective digital
marketing strategies. In view of this, an attempt was made to
assess consumers' perceptions toward social media
influencers promoting healthy food products.

Methodology
The present study was conducted to examine the impact of
social media influencers on consumers' purchase intention
toward healthy food products. A descriptive and analytical
research design was adopted to understand consumers'
perceptions, attitudes, and behavioral intentions regarding
healthy food products promoted through social media
platforms. The study was carried out among social media

developed and administered using a survey platform. The
questionnaire consisted of sections covering socio-economic

perception levels of respondents. Reliability of the
measurement scales was assessed using Cronbach's alpha
coefficient. To examine the relationships among variables,
correlation analysis and multiple regression analysis were
performed. The influence of social media influencers on
consumers' purchase intention toward healthy food products
was determined through regression coeff icients and
significance testing. The findings were interpreted at 5
percent and 1 percent levels of significance. The study
framework was grounded in Uses and Gratifications Theory
(UGT), Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB), Health Belief
Model (HBM), Stimul us–Organism–Response (S -O-R)

a comprehensive understanding of how social media
influencer content affects consumers' perceptions, attitudes,
and purchase intentions toward healthy food products.

Theoretical Framework
To comprehensively examine the impact of social media
influencers on consumers' purchase intention toward healthy
food products, this study integrates five complementary
theories: Uses and Gratifications Theory (UGT), Theory of
Planned Behavior (TPB), Heal th Belief Model (HBM),
Stimulus–Organism–Response (S -O-R) Theory, and Social
Cognitive Theory (SCT). Together, these theories provide a
robust framework for understanding how social media
content influences consumers’ attitudes, perceptions, beliefs,
and behavioral intentions regarding healthy food products.

Uses and Gratifications Theory (UGT)
Uses and Gratifications Theory, developed by Katz, Blumler,
and Gurevitch (1973), posits that individuals are active media
users who selectively engage with media c ontent to satisfy
specific psychological and social needs. These needs include
information seeking, entertainment, social interaction, and
personal identity enhancement. In the context of social
media, UGT has gained increasing relevance because digital
platforms offer users greater autonomy, interactivity, and
engagement opportunities. Consumers often follow health -
related influencers to obtain nutritional information, healthy
lifestyle guidance, product recommendations, and social
support. Consequently, s ocial media serves as an important
source of gratification that shapes consumers' perceptions
and influences their purchase intentions toward healthy food
products.

Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB)
The Theory of Planned Behavior, proposed by Ajzen (1991),
is one of the most widely applied theories for predicting
behavioral intentions. According to TPB, an individual's
intention to perform a behavior is determined by three key
factors: attitude toward the behavior, subjective norms, and
perceived be havioral control. Attitude refers to an
individual's positive or negative evaluation of a particular
behavior, subjective norms represent perceived social
pressure from significant others, and perceived behavioral
control reflects the perceived ease or difficulty of performing
the behavior. In the present study, positive attitudes toward
healthy food products, social influence exerted by influencers
and peers, and consumers’ confidence in purchasing healthy
foods are expected to enhance their purchase intentions.

Health Belief Model (HBM)
The Health Belief Model, introduced by Rosenstock (1974)
[3], explains health -related behaviors based on individuals'
perceptions of health risks, benefits, and barriers. The model
suggests that people are more likely to adopt healthy
behaviors when they perceive themselves as vulnerable to
health problems and beli eve that a particular action can
reduce those risks. In the context of healthy food
consumption, consumers who recognize the health benefits
associated with nutritious foods and perceive fewer obstacles
to their purchase and consumption are more likely to develop
favorable purchase intentions. Social media influencers often
communicate health-related information that can strengthen
consumers’ perceptions of benefits and motivate healthier
purchasing decisions.

Stimulus–Organism–Response (S-O-R) Theory
The Stimulus–Organism–Response (S -O-R) Theory,
developed by Mehrabian and Russell (1974) [5], explains how
environmental stimuli influence behavioral responses
through internal cognitive and emotional processes. The
framework consists of three components: Stim ulus (S),
Organism (O), and Response (R).
In this study, social media influencer content acts as the
stimulus by providing health -related information, product
reviews, recommendations, endorsements, and promotional
messages concerning healthy food products . Exposure to
such content affects consumers’ internal psychological states,
including awareness, trust, attitudes, perceptions, and health
beliefs, which collectively constitute the organism
component. These cognitive and emotional evaluations

Social Cognitive Theory (SCT)
Social Cognitive Theory, proposed by Bandura (2001)  [6],
emphasizes that human behavior  is shaped through the
reciprocal interaction of personal factors, environmental
influences, and behavioral outcomes. A central concept of
SCT is observational learning, whereby individuals acquire
knowledge, attitudes, and behaviors by observing others
within their social environment. Within the context of this
study, social media serves as a significant environmental
influence by exposing consumers to health information,
expert advice, influencer endorsements, testimonials, and
experiences shared by other  users regarding healthy food
products. Such exposure enhances consumers’ knowledge,
awareness, and perceptions of healthy foods, thereby shaping
their attitudes and beliefs. Through observational learning,
consumers may emulate the behaviors and recommendations
of trusted influencers, ultimately strengthening their intention
to purchase healthy food products. Integration of Theories:
The integration of UGT, TPB, HBM , S -O-R Theory, and
SCT provides a comprehensive explanation of how social
media influencers  affect consumers’ purchase intentions
toward healthy food products. UGT explains consumers’
motivations for engaging with influencer content; TPB
clarifies how attitudes, social norms, and perceived control
shape behavioral intentions; HBM highlights the role of
health perceptions and benefits; S-O-R Theory illustrates the
mechanism through which social media stimuli influence
consumer responses via internal psychological processes; and
SCT explains how observational learning and social
influence contribut e to behavioral outcomes. Collectively,
these theories offer a strong conceptual foundation for
understanding the relationship between social media
influencer exposure and consumers’ intentions to purchase
healthy food products

Conclusion
The present study highlights the growing influenc e of social
media influencers on consumers' purchase intentions toward
healthy food products. The findings revealed that consumers,
particularly young and educated social media users, actively
engage with influencer -generated content to obtain
information, recommendations, and guidance regarding
healthy food choices. Instagram and YouTube emerged as the
most preferred platforms for accessing health and nutrition -
related content, indicating the increasing importance of visual
and interactive media in shaping consumer behavior.
The study further demonstrated that consumers generally
hold positive perceptions toward social media influencers,
recognizing their role in enhancing awareness, providing
useful product information, and facilitating informed
purchasing decisions. Influencer credibility, trustworthiness,
expertise, and the quality of health -related content were
found to contribute significantly to favorable consumer
attitudes toward healthy food products. Through exposure to
influencer endorsements, prod uct reviews, and personal
experiences, consumers developed greater confidence in
healthy food products, which positively influenced their
purchase intentions.
The theoretical integration of Uses and Gratifications Theory
(UGT), Theory of Planned Behavior ( TPB), Health Belief
Model (HBM), Stimulus –Organism–Response (S -O-R)
Theory, and Social Cognitive Theory (SCT) provided a
comprehensive explanation of how social media influencer
content affects consumers' internal perceptions, attitudes, and
behavioral intentions. The results suggest that social media
influencers act as important intermediaries between healthy
food brands and consumers by shaping awareness, health
beliefs, and purchase-related decisions.
Overall, the study concludes that social media influe ncer
marketing is an effective strategy for promoting healthy food
products and encouraging healthier consumption behavior
among consumers. Food marketers and health promotion
agencies should collaborate with credible and knowledgeable
influencers to disse minate accurate health information and
strengthen consumer trust. Future research may explore the
moderating effects of demographic characteristics, influencer
type, and platform -specific features on consumer purchase
behavior toward healthy food products.
