Introduction: This study examines how customer engagement (CE) and
customer involvement (CI) shape value co-creation (VCC), relationship quality
(RQ), and social media usage intention (SMUI) among Instagram users in
Greater Jakarta. Drawing on Service-Dominant Logic and relationship
marketing theory, the study proposes an integrated pathway in which
participatory interaction enhances co-created value and strengthens relational
evaluations that support continued platform use.
Methods: Data were collected through a cross-sectional online survey of 223
active Instagram users and analyzed using partial least squares structural
equation modeling (PLS-SEM). The measurement model was re-estimated
after removing two indicators with excessive collinearity, and indirect-effect
analysis, Gaussian copula analysis, PLSpredict, and CVPAT were used as
robustness and predictive checks.
Results: The revised model shows that CE strongly predicts VCC, whereas CI
does not signiﬁcantly predict VCC. CI strongly predicts RQ, RQ strongly
predicts SMUI, and VCC contributes to SMUI indirectly through RQ in the
main model. However, the VCC -> RQ pathway is sensitive to Gaussian
copula correction and should therefore be interpreted cautiously. The main
model explained 70.8% of the variance in VCC, 82.2% of RQ, and 50.7% of
SMUI. The Gaussian copula robustness check conﬁrmed the stability of the
CE -> VCC, CI -> RQ, and RQ -> SMUI paths.
Discussion: Continued social media use is sustained less by co-creation alone
than by the extent to which participatory experiences are translated into
relationship quality. The study contributes by clarifying the distinct roles of
engagement, involvement, co-creation, and relationship quality in an
emerging-market social media context.

1 Introduction
Digital transformation has reshaped how individuals, organizations, and communities
interact, communicate, and create value in networked environments. Within
contemporary social media ecosystems, users no longer function only as passive
audiences; they increasingly act as contributors, commenters, advocates, and co-
creators whose interactions help sustain the platform itself. This shift is especially

salient in Indonesia, where social media is deeply embedded in
everyday life. According to the Digital 2024 Indonesia report,
the country had 139.0 million active social media user identities
in January 2024 and internet users aged 16 –64 spent an average
of 3 hours and 11 minutes per day using social media ( We Are
Social and Meltwater , 2024).
In this context, value co-creation (VCC), customer
engagement (CE), customer involvement (CI), relationship
quality (RQ), and social media usage intention (SMUI) are
central constructs for understanding the dynamics of platform
participation and continued use. Service-Dominant Logic (SDL)
views value as co-created through interaction rather than
delivered unilaterally by ﬁrms ( Vargo and Lusch , 2004, 2008).
Relationship marketing, meanwhile, emphasizes that enduring
exchange relationships are sustained by trust, satisfaction, and
commitment ( Morgan and Hunt , 1994). Together, these
perspectives suggest that continued social media use is shaped
not only by content exposure or functional convenience, but
also by users’ participatory experiences and their evaluations of
the relationship they develop with the platform over time.
Although research on social media behavior in Indonesia is
expanding, the literature remains theoretically fragmented.
Existing studies have often examined engagement, involvement,
co-creation, relationship quality, or usage intention separately,
or have focused on pairwise links rather than on a uniﬁed
explanatory pathway. In addition, much local work has
concentrated on adoption, MSME promotion, digital marketing
tactics, or political communication, whereas fewer studies
explain how participatory interaction becomes co-created value
and, subsequently, a relational basis for continued platform use.
This is an important gap because social media platforms are
sustained not merely by users’ presence, but by whether users
perceive their interactions as meaningful, inVf_luential, and
worth continuing.
This study addresses that gap by developing and testing an
integrated model linking CE, CI, VCC, RQ, and SMUI among
Instagram users in Greater Jakarta. The study makes three
contributions. First, it integrates psychological participation
variables (engagement and involvement), a collaborative
mechanism (co-creation), a relational mechanism (relationship
quality), and a behavioral outcome (usage intention) in a single
framework. Second, it extends the application of SDL and
relationship marketing to an emerging-market social media
context characterized by dense digital participation and strong
community visibility. Third, it clariﬁes that participation does
not operate uniformly: engagement is expected to energize co-
creation, whereas involvement may be more closely tied to
relational evaluation and continued use through
relationship quality.
2 Literature review and hypotheses
development
2.1 Theoretical foundations
This study is grounded primarily in Service-Dominant Logic
and relationship marketing theory. SDL proposes that value is
not embedded in a product or service in advance, but emerges
through interaction, resource integration, and collaborative
exchange among actors ( Vargo and Lusch , 2004, 2008). In social
media environments, this logic is highly relevant because users
actively shape value through commenting, sharing, feedback,
peer assistance, and user-generated content. Value is therefore
not simply provided by the platform; it is continually
reproduced through user participation.
Relationship marketing provides a complementary lens by
explaining how repeated exchange can develop into durable
relational outcomes. Morgan and Hunt (1994) argue that trust
and commitment are central to maintaining long-term
relationships. In digital platform and social commerce settings,
these relational evaluations are commonly reVf_lected in
relationship quality, which captures users’ assessments of trust,
satisfaction, and commitment toward the focal platform and has
been linked to continuance, intention, and co-creation-related
outcomes ( Hajli, 2015; Hossain et al. , 2020; Liang et al. , 2011;
Nadeem et al. , 2020; Tajvidi et al. , 2020). When combined, SDL
and relationship marketing suggest that participatory interaction
on social media may ﬁrst generate co-created value and then
strengthen relational quality, which in turn supports continued
usage intention.
2.2 Customer engagement and value co-
creation
Customer engagement refers to the degree to which users are
cognitively attentive, emotionally connected, and behaviorally
active in relation to a focal platform ( Brodie et al. , 2011;
Hollebeek et al. , 2014; Pansari and Kumar , 2017). Engagement
may also involve differently valenced states, reinforcing the need
to distinguish engagement intensity from relational evaluation
(Hollebeek and Chen , 2014). In social media settings, engaged
users do more than consume content. They explore features,
comment, share experiences, and interact with other users in
ways that contribute to the vibrancy of the platform.
Engagement therefore provides the psychological energy that
transforms exposure into contribution. This relationship is
theoretically consistent with SDL because engaged users are
more likely to integrate their own resources, such as time,
attention, knowledge, and interaction, into the value-creation
process. Prior studies have shown that engagement stimulates
collaborative participation in online communities and interactive
commerce environments ( Dessart et al. , 2015; Ye et al. , 2023).
In the context of Instagram, stronger engagement should
therefore increase users’ propensity to take part in co-creative
activities on the platform.
H1: Customer engagement positively inVf_luences value co-
creation.
2.3 Customer involvement and value co-
creation
Customer involvement captures the extent to which users
perceive a focal platform or interaction as personally relevant
and worthy of attention and participation. In digital settings,
involved users are more likely to monitor content, invest effort

in interaction, provide feedback, and participate in improvement-
oriented behaviors. Whereas engagement emphasizes the energy
users invest in interaction, involvement emphasizes the
perceived relevance of that interaction to the user. From an SDL
perspective, involvement should facilitate co-creation because
users who perceive the platform as important are more likely to
act as legitimate contributors rather than passive recipients.
Research in service and online contexts suggests that
involvement increases collaborative participation and enhances
co-created outcomes ( Cheung and To , 2021; Dewarani and
Alversia, 2023). In Instagram-based interaction, higher
involvement should therefore strengthen users’ participation in
activities that generate co-created value.
H2: Customer involvement positively inVf_luences value co-

2.4 Value co-creation and relationship
quality
SDL proposes that value emerges through reciprocal interaction
and shared experience (Vargo and Lusch, 2004, 2008). When users
perceive that they can contribute ideas, experiences, or assistance to
others, they are likely to experience reciprocity a nd recognition
from the platform environment. This user-contributi on logic is
also consistent with lead-user theory, which views users as
potential sources of novel product or service ideas  ( von Hippel ,
1986). These experiences can support stronger trust, sa tisfaction,
and commitment, which are core elements of relation ship quality.
In digital platform settings, co-creation is not me rely functional;
it can also shape how users evaluate the relationsh ip itself. Users
who perceive their participation as meaningful may feel more
attached to the platform and more satisﬁed with the ir ongoing
interaction. Prior research has shown that co-creat ion is closely
connected to relationship quality, brand co-creatio n, and
relational attachment in social commerce and platfo rm settings
(Mustafa et al. , 2022; Nadeem et al. , 2020; Tajvidi et al. , 2020;
Widyastuti, 2024). Therefore, higher value co-creation is expected
to improve relationship quality.
H3: Value co-creation positively inVf_luences relationship quality.
2.5 Customer involvement and relationship
quality
Relationship marketing emphasizes that durable exchange
relationships are sustained by positive relational evaluations
rather than by isolated transactions ( Morgan and Hunt , 1994).
In social media settings, involvement may strengthen
relationship quality because users who feel that their
participation matters are more likely to perceive the relationship
with the platform as worthwhile and satisfying. This mechanism
is especially relevant in participatory digital environments,
where being heard, recognized, and able to contribute may itself
become part of the relational experience. Prior studies indicate
that involvement is associated with stronger satisfaction, loyalty,
and service evaluations ( Cheung and To , 2021; Dewarani and
Alversia, 2023). In a social media platform context, users who
feel personally invested in platform activities should therefore
report stronger relationship quality. In this study, relationship
quality is not treated as an exogenous predictor, but as a
downstream relational mechanism shaped by participatory
experience.
H4: Customer involvement positively inVf_luences relationship
quality.
2.6 Value co-creation and social media
usage intention
Social media usage intention reVf_lects users’ willing ness to
continue using a platform in the future. Continued use is shaped
not only by entertainment or convenience, but also by whether
users perceive the platform as a valuable space for participation and
exchange. When users co-create value, they invest e ffort, interact
with others, and derive social and experiential ben eﬁts from the
platform. These contributions can strengthen the pe rceived worth
of continued use. Prior studies in social commerce and platform
research suggest that co-creation experiences posit ively affect
intention to continue participating in or using a service (Raﬁ et al.,
2025; Yang et al., 2024). In Instagram-based interaction, users who
perceive greater co-created value should therefore be more willing
to continue using the platform.
H5: Value co-creation positively inVf_luences social media
usage intention.
2.7 Relationship quality and social media
usage intention
Relationship quality is expected to be the most proximal relational
driver of continued use. Users who trust the platfo rm, feel satisﬁed
with their experiences, and remain committed to ong oing
interaction should be more likely to continue using it. This logic is
consistent with relationship marketing and with pri or research
showing that trust, satisfaction, and commitment su pport
continuance intention in digital and social commerce environments
(Hossain et al., 2020; Liang et al., 2011; Rahayu et al., 2015). In the
present model, relationship quality captures how pa rticipatory
experiences become durable platform use. Co-creation may provide
collaborative value, but continued usage is more li kely when that
value is translated into favorable relational evalu ations. Thus,
relationship quality should positively inVf_luence soc ial media
usage intention.
H6: Relationship quality positively inVf_luences social media
usage intention.
3 Materials and methods
3.1 Research design
This study adopts a positivist, explanatory research design and
uses a quantitative cross-sectional survey to test the proposed
relationships among CE, CI, VCC, RQ, and SMUI ( Creswell, 2013;
Malhotra et al. , 2013). The conceptual model was evaluated using
partial least squares structural equation modeling (PLS-SEM),
which is suitable for estimating complex relationships among latent

constructs and for simultaneously assessing the mea surement and
structural models (Hair et al., 2018; Hair et al., 2019).
3.2 Research setting, respondents, and
sampling
The unit of analysis is the individual social media user. The
empirical setting is Greater Jakarta (Jabodetabek), an urban area
with intense digital participation and widespread use of social
networking platforms. Instagram was selected as the focal
platform because of its high penetration and central role in
everyday digital interaction in Indonesia ( Kemp, 2024). The
study employed purposive sampling to reach respondents who
matched the research context. The online questionnaire was
circulated through social media and messaging channels to
potential respondents who met the inclusion criteria, and the
screening section at the beginning of the survey conﬁrmed that
participants were active Instagram users residing in the Greater
Jakarta area. Only complete responses that passed the screening
stage were retained, resulting in 223 usable questionnaires.
Because the survey relied on purposive online circulation rather
than a closed invitation list, a conventional response rate could
not be estimated reliably; accordingly, the study reports the
number of usable screened cases retained for analysis.
3.3 Instrument development and
measurement
All constructs were operationalized using measurement items
adapted from prior peer-reviewed studies: value co-creation from
Widyastuti (2024) , customer engagement from Vinerean and
Opreana (2021) , customer involvement from Cheung and To
(2021), relationship quality from Chonody et al. (2018) , and
social media usage intention from Yuan et al. (2021) . Table 1
summarizes the construct operationalization and retained
indicators. The relationship quality items were adapted to reVf_lect
users’ platform-oriented trust, satisfaction, and commitment
toward Instagram, rather than interpersonal relationship quality
in a narrow sense. Although the original relationship quality
scale was developed outside the speciﬁc Instagram context, this
adaptation is consistent with relationship quality research in
social commerce and digital interaction settings, where relational
evaluations are commonly used to explain continued digital
interaction, commerce-related intention, and co-creation-related
outcomes ( Hajli, 2015; Hossain et al. , 2020; Liang et al. , 2011;
Nadeem et al. , 2020; Tajvidi et al. , 2020).
All items were measured on a 7-point Likert scale ranging
from 1 (strongly disagree) to 7 (strongly agree). The ﬁnal
retained indicators are summarized in Table 1.
3.4 Pretest, reliability, and validity
A pretest with 30 participants was conducted before  the main
survey to assess item clarity and measurement quali ty. Instrument
quality was examined through exploratory factor ana lysis (EFA)
and reliability testing in IBM SPSS 25. Sampling ad equacy was
assessed using the Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin statistic and  Measure of
Sampling Adequacy, while reliability was examined u sing
Cronbach’s alpha. Six items were removed during the  pretest
stage because they did not meet the predeﬁned measu rement
criteria: VCC1, VCC2, VCC3, CE2.4, RQ3, and RQ7. In  the
revised PLS-SEM assessment, RQ8 and VCC6 were furth er
removed because their indicator-level VIF values ex ceeded the
recommended threshold, indicating potential redunda ncy and
collinearity. The ﬁnal measurement model therefore retained the
items that satisﬁed the pretest, indicator reliabil ity, convergent
validity, and collinearity criteria. In particular,  customer
involvement retained four indicators (CI1-CI4), cus tomer
engagement retained ten indicators after CE2.4 was removed, and
all constructs retained at least three indicators i n the ﬁnal model.

Potential endogeneity and robustness of the structural estimates
were assessed through Gaussian copula analysis. The copula
terms were added to the main structural paths and evaluated
through bootstrapping, following the endogeneity-checking logic
for PLS-SEM applications ( Hult et al. , 2018). Prior to the
Gaussian copula assessment, construct-score distributions were
inspected, and Shapiro –Wilk diagnostics indicated departures
from normality for the relevant predictor constructs, supporting
the use of the copula-based diagnostic. Because copula-based
endogeneity checks require careful interpretation, the copula-
adjusted results are reported as robustness evidence rather than
as a replacement for the main theoretical model. Out-of-sample
predictive assessment was also examined using PLSpredict and
CVPAT as complementary predictive checks ( Sarstedt et al. ,
2022; Ringle et al. , 2024). The corresponding predictive
assessment summary is reported in the Results section.
Because all focal constructs were measured using self-reported
responses collected in a single survey wave, common method bias
was considered at both the procedural and statistical levels.
Procedurally, the survey was anonymous, participation was
voluntary, and respondents were informed that there were no
right or wrong answers. Statistically, the revised main model
showed acceptable collinearity diagnostics, with inner VIF values
ranging from 1.906 to 3.101 and all retained indicator VIF
values below 5. These results indicate that collinearity was not a
critical concern in the revised model, although the cross-
sectional and self-report design remains a limitation.
3.6 Ethical considerations
Participation was voluntary and based on informed consent.
Respondents were informed about the purpose of the study and
assured that their responses would be treated conﬁdentially and
analyzed only in aggregate form. No personally identifying or
sensitive data were collected. The study complied with
institutional research guidelines and the ethical principles of the
Declaration of Helsinki. Ethical approval was obtained from
Universitas Esa Unggul, Jakarta, under approval number 1025/
PUSDI/FEBUEU.
4 Results
4.1 Descriptive analysis
A total of 223 valid responses were collected from active
Instagram users residing in the Greater Jakarta area. The sample
comprised 61% females and 39% males, with most respondents
aged 21 –25 years (45%) and 26 –30 years (17%), indicating the
prominence of young, digitally active users. Respondents mainly
resided in Jakarta (41%), followed by Tangerang (29%), Bekasi
(17%), Depok (9%), and Bogor (4%). Employment distribution
consisted of private employees (38%), students (37%),
entrepreneurs (18%), and civil servants (8%).
In terms of platform experience, 45.7% had used Instagram for
more than 10 years, 33.2% for 6–10 years, and 20.2% for 1–5 years.
Most respondents accessed Instagram three to ﬁve times per day
(28%) or one to two times per day (25%), while daily use was
commonly 30 –60 min (38%) or less than 30 min (37%). Across
the focal constructs, mean values were above the midpoint of
the 7-point scale, indicating generally favorable perceptions.
Social media usage intention showed the highest mean
(M = 5.60, SD = 1.14), followed by value co-creation (M = 5.43,
SD = 1.37) and customer engagement (M = 5.30, SD = 1.20).
Customer involvement (M = 4.79, SD = 1.55) and relationship
quality (M = 4.84, SD = 1.41) were also positive, though
somewhat more variable.
4.2 Group differences
ANOVA results indicated no statistically signiﬁcant
differences across gender, location, employment status, duration
of use, usage frequency, or daily duration (all p > .05). Age,
however, showed signiﬁcant differences for value co-creation
(p = .009), customer engagement ( p = .037), and marginally for
customer involvement ( p = .050). This pattern suggests that
younger respondents were more active in participatory and co-
creative aspects of Instagram use, while relationship quality and
usage intention were comparatively stable across
demographic categories.
4.3 Measurement model
The revised measurement model demonstrated strong internal
consistency and convergent validity after removing RQ8 and
VCC6 due to indicator-level collinearity concerns. All retained
outer loadings were above 0.70, ranging from 0.719 to 0.936.
Average variance extracted values ranged from 0.700 to 0.851,
composite reliability (rho_c) from 0.923 to 0.959, Cronbach’s
alpha from 0.875 to 0.952, and rho_A from 0.876 to 0.954,
indicating satisfactory reliability and convergent validity. Table 2
reports the measurement model quality. The ﬁnal indicator
set also ensured adequate construct coverage: CE retained ten
indicators, CI retained four indicators, VCC retained three
indicators, RQ retained ﬁve indicators, and SMUI retained four
indicators. Discriminant validity showed a mixed but
interpretable pattern. The Fornell-Larcker criterion and cross-
loadings supported indicator-level separation. However, the
HTMT value between customer involvement and relationship
quality remained high [HTMT = 0.953; 95% bootstrap CI (0.919,
0.979)], and the HTMT value between customer engagement
and value co-creation was also above the conservative threshold
[HTMT = 0.919; 95% bootstrap CI (0.870, 0.960)]. Because the
bootstrapped conﬁdence intervals did not include 1.000, the

Speciﬁc indirect effects were estimated to assess whether

5 Discussion
5.1 Interpretation of the integrated pathway
The revised ﬁndings support an integrated but diffe rentiated
explanation of social media usage intention. Engage ment and
involvement do not operate in the same way. Engagem ent
functions mainly as the psychological energy that s upports value
co-creation, whereas involvement functions primaril y as a
relational driver that strengthens relationship qua lity. Relationship
quality then becomes the strongest proximal predict or of
continued use. This pattern shows that sustained pl atform use is
not simply an outcome of attention, exposure, or co -created
activity; it is relationally constructed when parti cipatory
experiences are translated into trust, satisfaction, and commitment.
5.2 Customer engagement and value co-
creation
The positive CE ->VCC relationship indicates that engagement
functions as a psychological engine for collaborative behavior. This
result is consistent with engagement theory, which argues that
emotionally and cognitively invested users are more  likely to
participate in platform-related activities ( Brodie et al. , 2011;
Hollebeek et al. , 2014). It also aligns with work on online
communities and interactive commerce, showing that engagement
stimulates content contribution, advocacy, and coll aborative
exchange (Dessart et al. , 2015; Ye et al. , 2023). In the Instagram
context, engagement appears to convert attention into action: users
who feel connected to the platform are more likely to comment,
share, and participate in experiences that co-creat e value.
Importantly, this relationship remained signiﬁcant in the Gaussian
copula robustness check, strengthening conﬁdence in  engagement
as the primary antecedent of value co-creation in this model.
5.3 Customer involvement, co-creation,
and relationship quality
Customer involvement showed a more selective pattern than
originally expected. It did not signiﬁcantly inVf_luence value co-
creation, but it strongly inVf_luenced relationship quality. This
ﬁnding suggests that perceiving Instagram as personally relevant
does not automatically lead users to co-create value through
contributions, feedback, or collaborative interaction. Instead,
involvement appears to shape how users evaluate their
relationship with the platform. When users feel that their
presence, attention, and interaction matter, they are more likely
to experience the platform relationship as satisfactory,
trustworthy, and worth maintaining. This result extends prior
service research by showing that involvement can operate more
strongly as a relationship-building mechanism than as a co-
creation driver in social media contexts ( Cheung and To , 2021;
Dewarani and Alversia , 2023). At the same time, the high
empirical closeness between CI and RQ suggests that future
studies should further reﬁne item wording to separate personal
relevance from relational evaluation more sharply.

quality

is consistent with Service-Dominant Logic and the idea that value

5.5 Usage intention as a relational outcome
The results conﬁrm relationship quality as the key relational
driver of social media usage intention. RQ signiﬁcantly
predicted SMUI in both the main model and the Gaussian
copula robustness check, indicating that users are more willing
to continue using Instagram when they trust the platform, feel
satisﬁed with their interaction, and perceive the relationship as
worth maintaining. By contrast, the direct effect of value co-
creation on SMUI was not signiﬁcant at the 5% level. The
speciﬁc indirect effect from VCC to SMUI through RQ was
signiﬁcant in the main model, and the total effect of VCC on
SMUI was also signiﬁcant. However, this VCC-related pathway
should be interpreted as main-model evidence rather than as a
fully robustness-conﬁrmed mechanism. Substantively, co-
creation may generate interactional value, but continued usage
intention requires users to convert that value into stable
relational evaluations. Without trust, satisfaction, or
commitment, co-created experiences may remain episodic and
insufﬁcient to produce continued usage intention.
5.6 Why the Indonesian urban context
matters
The Greater Jakarta setting helps explain why participatory and
relational variables were inVf_luential. Urban Indonesian social media
users operate in a dense environment of peer intera ction,
community visibility, and rapid content circulation . In such a
setting, participation is often socially meaningful : commenting,
responding, sharing, or joining interactive campaig ns may
reinforce belonging, recognition, and public identi ty. This makes
relationship quality especially salient as a mechan ism that
converts platform involvement into continued usage.  The age
differences found in the analysis also support this  interpretation,
because younger respondents showed stronger engagem ent and
co-creation tendencies than older groups, even thou gh
relationship quality and usage intention were compa ratively stable
once the platform relationship was established.
5.7 Theoretical implications
This study contributes theoretically in three ways. First, it
moves beyond fragmented pairwise explanations by integrating
engagement, involvement, co-creation, relationship quality, and
usage intention in one framework while showing that not all
participatory variables operate through the same mechanism.
Second, it extends Service-Dominant Logic and relationship
marketing to an emerging-market social media context by
demonstrating that engagement is more directly tied to co-
creation, whereas involvement is more directly tied to
relationship quality. Third, it adds nuance to the role of
relationship quality by formally testing indirect effects. The
signiﬁcant indirect effect of CI on SMUI through RQ shows that
relationship quality is not merely an additional predictor but a
key mechanism through which involvement becomes continued
usage intention. The ﬁndings also caution against overstating
the role of value co-creation because its direct effect on SMUI
was not supported and its relationship with RQ was sensitive to
the endogeneity robustness check.
5.8 Practical and managerial implications
For social media managers and digital marketers, the ﬁndings
suggest that participatory architecture should be designed not
merely to generate user activity, but to cultivate stronger
relationship quality. Platform managers and digital content
teams should therefore avoid relying only on visibility metrics,
short-term engagement spikes, or surface-level interaction
counts. Instead, they need to design interaction formats that
make users feel heard, recognized, and relationally connected to
the platform or brand community. User-generated campaigns,
collaborative content prompts, interactive storytelling, and
responsive feedback channels may support value co-creation by
encouraging users to contribute ideas, share experiences, and
participate in brand-related conversations. However, the
managerial interpretation of the VCC → RQ pathway should be
treated cautiously. Because this relationship became sensitive
after endogeneity correction, managers should not assume that
value co-creation automatically or deﬁnitively improves
relationship quality. Rather, value co-creation initiatives should
be implemented with continuous evaluation to ensure that user
participation genuinely leads to satisfaction, trust,
and commitment.
The non-signiﬁcant CI → VCC path also provides an
important practical lesson. Personal relevance or psychological
involvement alone may not be sufﬁcient to stimulate active co-
creative behavior. Users who feel interested in a brand, platform,
or social media content may still remain passive if the platform
does not provide clear participation mechanisms. Therefore,
managers should provide explicit prompts, simple contribution
formats, feedback loops, community recognition, and visible
appreciation for user input. These mechanisms can help
transform involvement into more active forms of participation.
Overall, the results imply that social media strategy should move
beyond attracting attention toward managing the quality of
interaction. Digital marketers should design engagement systems
that not only invite participation, but also monitor whether
participation produces meaningful relational outcomes. In this
sense, co-creation should be managed as a structured relational
process rather than as a spontaneous consequence of
user involvement.

5.9 Limitations and future research
Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this study
uses a cross-sectional design, which limits the ability to establish
temporal causality or explain how customer engagement, value
co-creation, relationship quality, and social media usage
intention evolve over time. Although the proposed model is
theoretically grounded, the empirical results should be
interpreted as evidence of associations among constructs rather
than deﬁnitive causal relationships. Second, the sample is
limited to active Instagram users in an urban Indonesian setting.
This context provides relevant insight into social media-based
engagement behavior, but it also restricts the generalizability of
the ﬁndings to other platforms, demographic groups, cultural
settings, or non-urban populations. Future studies could
compare different social media environments, such as TikTok,
YouTube, Facebook, or emerging social commerce platforms, to
examine whether the same relational mechanisms operate across
platform types.
Third, although the HTMT value between customer
involvement and relationship quality was technically acceptable
because the bootstrapped conﬁdence interval did not include
1.000, the value remained relatively high at 0.953. This indicates
empirical closeness between the two constructs and may limit
the precision of conclusions drawn from pathways involving
customer involvement and relationship quality. Future research
should therefore reﬁne the wording of measurement items, test
alternative measurement speciﬁcations, or examine whether
these constructs are more clearly distinguishable across different
contexts or respondent groups. Fourth, although Gaussian
copula analysis was conducted to assess potential endogeneity,
signiﬁcant copula terms were observed for several key paths, and
the VCC → RQ path became non-signiﬁcant after correction.
This suggests that the robustness analysis should be understood
as an important diagnostic procedure rather than as conclusive
evidence of causal ordering. Consequently, interpretations and
practical recommendations related to the value co-creation to
relationship quality pathway should remain tentative.
Future research should use longitudinal, experimental, or
multi-source designs to provide stronger evidence regarding
causal direction. Longitudinal studies could examine whether
engagement and co-creation gradually strengthen relationship
quality, while experimental designs could test whether speciﬁc
platform features, feedback mechanisms, or co-creation prompts
directly inVf_luence relational outcomes. Additional studies may
also extend the model by incorporating variables such as digital
well-being, privacy concerns, perceived authenticity, social
norms, cultural orientation, or algorithmic transparency.
6 Conclusions and future research
directions
This study shows that customer engagement is a strong
antecedent of value co-creation, whereas customer involvement
is more strongly associated with relationship quality than with
value co-creation. Relationship quality emerged as the most
important proximal predictor of social media usage intention
and also mediated the inVf_luence of customer involvement on
continued use. Value co-creation contributed to continued use
mainly through relational evaluation in the main model rather
than through a robust direct path to usage intention, and this
indirect contribution should be interpreted cautiously because
the VCC ->RQ path was sensitive to Gaussian copula
correction. The Gaussian copula robustness check further
indicated that the CE ->VCC, CI ->RQ, and RQ ->SMUI
relationships remained substantively stable, while the VCC ->RQ
path should not be overstated. By integrating engagement,
involvement, co-creation, relationship quality, and usage
intention in a single model, the study provides a more nuanced
explanation of continued Instagram use in an urban Indonesian
setting. Future research should broaden the demographic and
platform scope of the model, improve the discriminant validity
of involvement and relationship quality measures, and test the
proposed pathway using longitudinal or experimental designs.
