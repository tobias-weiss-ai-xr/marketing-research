This study takes Chengdu cultural tourism as the research object, and builds
an integrated theoretical model on the impact of social media participation
on cultural tourism intention based on social cognitive theory and
technology acceptance model. This study adopted a quantitative research
approach, utilizing 600 valid questionnaires and employing structural
equation modeling (SEM) and other statistical analysis methods to
systematically explore the mediating role of digital engagement and the
moderating e ffect of demographic characteristics.Empirical analysis found
that: (1) Social media participation has a significant positive impact on
cultural tourism intention (β=0.435, p<0.001), and this impact is more
significant among high -frequency social media user groups ( β= 0.512,
p<0.001); (2) Digital participation plays a significant partial mediating role
between social media participation and cultural tourism intention. The
mediating effect accounts for 30.1% of the total effect, among which the
cognitive pa rticipation path (13.2 %) has the largest contribution; (3)
Educational level has a significant moderating effect on the relationship
between social media participation and cultural tourism intention, and the
highly educated group shows a stronger influence (β=0.489 vs. β=0.376).
The research results reveal the key role of digital marketing strategies in
improving the attractiveness of Chengdu's cultural tourism, which not only
enriches the theoretical framework of cultural tourism marketing, but also
provides practical guidance for developing precise marketing strategies for
cultural tourism destinations.

Introduction
The rise of digitalization has transformed cultural tourism, with social media reshaping tourists’
decision-making and destination marketing. In the Web3.0 era, platforms have evolved from
information channels to ecosystems that shape destination images and guide consumer behavior,
particularly in markets like China, where destinations like Chengdu leverage social media for
global competitiveness. Despite evidence from Xiang and Gretzel (2010) highlighting social
media’s role in travel information search, t he mechanisms driving cultural tourism intentions in
the Chinese context remain underexplored. This study aims to develop a theoretical model to
examine how social media participation influences cultural tourism intentions through direct and
indirect pathways, focusing on digital engagement as a mediator and demographic factors as

moderators. By testing three hypotheses ---(H1) social media participation positively impacts
cultural tourism intention, (H2) digital engagement mediates this relationship, and (H3)
demographics moderate the effect ---this research seeks to provide empirical insights and
practical guidance for precision marketing in cultural tourism through rigorous quantitative
analysis.
Recent studies underscore social media’s transformative role in cultural tourism by altering
information access and decision -making. Kim, Lee, and Jung (2021) demonstrate that short -form
videos and live streams on platforms like Douyin enhance tourists’ cognitive and emotional
perceptions, accelerating travel decisions. Similarly, Zhang and Chen (2022) highlight social
media’s role in fostering cultural identity in Asian tourism markets, though they note gaps in
understanding engagement’s mediating effects. Leung et al. (2013) emphasize that user -
generated content (UGC) reshapes information ecosystems, enhancing trust and decision-making,
as supported by Ayeh, Au, and Law (2013). Additionally, Wang, Xu, and Li (2022) show that high
digital engagement correlates with efficient decision -making, but cross -cultural validation
remains limited, particularly for Eastern contexts, necessitating further empirical exploration.
The paradigm of cultural tourism marketing is shifting due to digitalization, with social media
enabling omni -channel strategies and cultural narratives. Li and Wang (2023) argue that
integrating visual and interactive content, such as virtual heritage tours, boosts destination
appeal in China. Chen, Huang, and Zhang (2024) explore AI -driven personalization, noting its
potential to enhance engagement while highlighting authenticity challenges. Mariani, Di Felice,
and Mura (2016) demonstrate that social media strengthens destination brand assets through
emotional interactions, particularly among younger tourists. Zhao and Liu (2023) validate digital
engagement’s mediating role in building tourist loyalty, while Hays, Page, and Buhalis (2013)
emphasize interactive participation’s role in cultural value transmission. These findings highlight
the need for a comprehensive model to integrate digital engagement and cross -cultural
perspectives in cultural tourism research.
Materials and Methods
This study adopts a quantitative research design using cross -sectional survey methodology. The
research framework is grounded in established statistical analysis procedures, employing
structural equation modeling (SEM) to test theoretical hypotheses. The study utilizes a single -
stage data collection approach through a large -scale questionnaire survey to examine the
relationships between social media participation, digital engagement, and cultural tourism
intention. Following Hair et al.'s (2016) quantitative research guidelines, the framework positions
social media participation as the core independent variable, cultural tourism intention as the
dependent variable, and digital participation as a key mediating variable, examining direct,
mediating, and moderating effects through statistical modeling. This quantitative design ensures
statistical rigor and provides a robust methodological foundation for examining the relationships
between study variables.  This study employed a rigorous stratified sampling method to ensure
sample representativeness and balance, yielding 600 valid questionnaires with a well -distributed

demographic profile. The age structure forms a pyramid, with the 26 -35 age group comprising
35.8%, aligning with the primary consumer segment in the cultural tourism market. The gender
ratio is nearly 1:1, and 68.5% of respondents hold a bachelor’s degree or higher, reflecting the
educated profile of cultural tourism consumers. Income distribution follows a normal curve,
dominated by middle -income groups, consistent with cultural tourism consumption patterns.
Variable measurement utilized validated scales adapted for the Chinese context. Social media
participation was measured using Harrigan et al.’s (2017) scale (Cronbach’s α = 0.892), cultural
tourism intention was assessed with Li et al.’s (2009) framework covering cognitive, emotional,
and behavioral dimensions (Cronbach’s α = 0.875), and digital engagement was measured with a
novel 12 -item scale based on Martinez and Lee (2023) (Cronbach’s α = 0.903), ensuring high
reliability for robust data analysis.  This study constructed a systematic quantitative data analysis
framework, integrating descriptive statistics, confirmatory factor analysis, and structural equation
modeling. At the statistical analysis level, two major statistical software platforms, SPSS 26.0 and
AMOS 24.0, were used to test theoretical hypotheses using structural equation models (SEM).
The model was estimated using the maximum likelihood method, and the mediation effect test
was performed using the Bootstrap method. The sampling number was set to 5000 times, and the
confidence interval was 95%. This setting ensured the robustness of statistical inference. The
technical route of data analysis followed the strict process of "data cleaning -descriptive statistics-
exploratory factor analysis -confirmatory factor analysis -hypothesis testing -robustness testing",
and corresponding quality control standards were set for each link. In particular, when dealing
with missing values and outliers, multiple interpolation methods were used, which has better
data fidelity than the traditional list deletion method.
Results and Discussion
The analysis results based on 600 valid sample data show that the core variables show significant
statistical characteristics. As shown in Table 1, the average score of the social media participation
scale is 5.32 (standard deviation = 0.87), which is higher than the theoretical median value of 4.0,
indicating that the respondent group as a whole shows a higher tendency to use social media.
Among the specific dimensions of social media participation, the mean value of content browsing
behavior is the highest (M=5.76, SD=0.92), followed by interactive comments (M=5.24, SD=0.88),
while the mean value of content creation behavior is relatively low (M =4.96, SD=1.03). This
"emphasis on consumption, light on production" participation characteristics reflects the current
users' social media usage patterns.

This study used a rigorous methodological process to comprehensively test the measurement
model. First, the overall fit of the measurement model was assessed through confirmatory factor
analysis (CFA). The results show that all fitting indicators have reached the ideal standard: the
χ²/df value is 2.437, which is lower than the critical value 3, indicating that the model has good
parsimony; the RMSEA is 0.058, which is within the ideal interval (< 0.08), reflecting the model's
simplicity. The approximate fitting degree is good; both CFI (0.942) and TLI (0.936) exceed the
standard value of 0.90, indicating that the model has significantly improved compared to the zero
model; SRMR is 0.045, which is lower than the standard of 0.05, indicating that the model
residual is relatively small. Small. Together, these indicators confirm the good overall fit of the
measurement model.
When verifying the measurement equivalence, the study adopted a stepwise constraint method.
First, the configuration invariance was tested to ensure that the measurement models of
different groups had the same factor structure; secondly, the measurement invariance was tested
to verify whether the factor loadings of different groups were equivalent; finally, the structural
invariance was tested to examine the stability of the relationship between latent variables. The
ΔCFI values in each level of test were all less than the standard of 0.01, indicating that the
measurement model had good equivalence between different groups, laying the foundation for
subsequent comparisons between groups.

Conclusion
This study’s empirical analysis of 600 valid samples reveals significant insights into the interplay of
social media participation, digital engagement, and cultural tourism intention. Social media
participation directly enhances cultural tourism intention (β=0.435, p<0.001), with a stronger
effect among high -frequency users (β=0.512, p<0.001), reinforcing Xiang and Gretzel’s (2010)
findings on its role in travel decision -making. Digital engagement mediates this relationship,
accounting for 30.1% of the total effect (95% CI=[0.142, 0.235]), enriching Kim and Hall’s (2019)
virtual tourism model, with notable heterogeneity across demographics. Education level
significantly moderates the effect, with stronger impacts among highly educated groups (β=0.489
vs. β=0.376), supporting Buhalis and Law’s (2008) framework. Theoretically, the study expands
cultural tourism research by validating an integrated model incorporating digital engagement as a
mediator, confirming Western theories’ applicability in Eastern contexts, and using multi -group
analysis to highlight demographic influences, thus advancing Leung et al.’s (2013) framework.
Practically, the findings suggest tailored social media marketing strategies for cultural tourism
destinations like Chengdu, emphasizing culturally rich, interactive content for highly educated
audiences and immersive digital experiences via AR/VR to boost engagement. High -quality,
authentic content is critical for enhancing destination appeal and brand building. However,
limitations include reliance on cross -sectional data, limiting causal inferences, a China -centric
sample which may restrict generalizability, potential social desirability bias inherent in self -report
measures, and the absence of actual behavioral data to validate intention measures. Future
research should adopt longitudinal designs to establish causality, expand to cross -cultural
samples to enhance generalizability, incorporate behavioral measures alongside intention data,
and explore emerging technologies like the metaverse to further understand their quantifiable
impact on cultural tourism marketing.
