The impact of social media on e-commerce purchase behavior in Türkiye
Abstract
Due to the increasing involvement of social media in e -commerce, there is also a remarkable change in the
consumer purchasing behavior. The aim of this paper is to examine the influential factors of e-commerce purchase
behavior and also compare differences among demographics in the context of social commerce. The research was
performed using an online questionnaire survey with 407 social media users from Türkiye. One-way analysis of
variance, correlation analysis and independent samples t-test were used for data analysis.
According to the study, influencer impact, effectiveness of campaigns/discounts, trust in user -generated reviews,
and exposure to e-commerce content had a significant positive relationship with e -commerce shopping behavior.
However, the study found no significant relationship between purchasing behavior and social media advertising
trust. In addition, there were significant differences in shopping behavior among genders and generations.
The study contributes to the social commerce literature because of its practical approach to the issue and analysis
of factors affecting consumer online purchasing behavior. The research shows that the traditional social media
trust is not enough to change consumer buying behavior; hence, companies need to create more complex strategies
for promoting products/services in social networks.
Keywords: Social commerce, Social media usage, Influencer impact, Trust, Exposure to e -commerce content,
E-commerce purchase behavior

EXTENDED ABSTRACT
Introduction
Increased use of social platforms in online shopping has attracted attention from academic and management circles
in recent years. Despite numerous studies on social commerce, relatively few studies examine individual factors
such as influencer impact, advertising, or online reviews within a single framework. However, the relative impact
of these factors may vary across markets and consumer age groups.
 More empirical research, especially in markets such as Türkiye, is needed to better understand how common
factors associated with social media affect e-commerce purchasing behavior.
From this perspective, this research attempts to investigate and interpret the relationship between social media
usage intensity, influencer impact, trust in social media advertisements, campaign and discount effectiveness, trust
in user -generated reviews,  exposure to e -commerce content, and e -commerce purchase behavior. Besides the
above behavioral determinants,  the study examines whether purchasing behavior differs significantly across
gender and age groups.
Accordingly, the study addresses the following research questions:
1. Do social media usage intensity and exposure to e-commerce content influence e-commerce purchase behavior?
2. How do influencer impact, trust in social media advertisements, trust in user -generated reviews, and campaign
effectiveness relate to purchasing behavior?
3. Does e-commerce purchase behavior differ significantly across gender and age groups?

across gender and age groups.
Method
The present research explored the association between social media factors related to e -commerce purchasing
behavior. A questionnaire was collected using convenience sampling, including 407 social media users residing
in Türkiye through voluntary participation between October 12, 2025 and November 2, 2025 using Google Forms.
The questionnaire included items related to influencer impact, demographic characteristics, exposure to e -
commerce content,   social media usage intensity, trust in social media advertisements, trust in user -generated
reviews, campaign and discount effectiveness and e -commerce purchase behavior. All variables were measured
using a 5-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (strongly dis agree) to 5 (strongly agree). The hypotheses were tested
using correlation analysis, ANOVA and independent samples t-test using SPSS software.
Findings
Here is where the findings from the tests of hypotheses conducted to establish the connection between social media
and e -commerce purchase behaviors are shown. The findings showed that influencer impact, campaign and
discount effectiveness, trust in user -generated reviews, and exposure to e -commerce content were significantly
and positively associated with e -commerce shopping behavior. In contrast, trust in social media advertising did
not have a significant relationship with shopping behavior. The results also revealed significant differences in
shopping behavior between gender and age groups.
Conclusion, Discussion, and Recommendations
This study supports the role of influencer impact, campaign and discount effectiveness and user-generated reviews
in shaping online purchasing behavior, similar to previous social commerce research, in digital shopping
environments. The results show a significant and positive effect of social media usage  intensity on e -commerce

information seeking, and participation in social interactions.
Another factor influencing online purchasing decisions is influencer impact (H2). This finding supports previous
research that emphasizes the expertise and relevance of influencers and the importance of perceived credibility in
shaping purchasing behavior (Lou and Yuan, 2019; De Veirman et al., 2017; Çakır, 2023). The findings suggest

INTRODUCTION
Information and communication technologies has resulted in drastic changes in the private life
of people in modern society. The development of online devices has made social media
important not only in business operations but also during purchasing actions  by consumers
(Kaplan & Haenlein, 2010; Kietzmann et al., 2011; Carr & Hayes, 2015). The growing
integration of social media with e -commerce because of user -generated content,
recommendation of influencers, reviews, and targeted advertisement has been an i mportant
factor determining purchasing behavior of consumers (Hajli, 2015; Liang & Turban, 2011). The
rising role of social networking in online purchasing has been noticed by researchers and
management circles recently.
Past research indicated that purchasing behavior could be affected by various factors such as
user-generated content, influence marketing, trust, and campaigns, as well as discounts
(Dwivedi et al., 2021; Lou & Yuan, 2019; Hajli, 2015; Liang & Turban, 2011; Molinillo et al.,
2020). While many researchers have studied the topic of social commerce, only a limited
number of studies explore particular aspects such as the influencer impact, ads, and reviews
within a common setting. No netheless, their relative effects may vary from one market and
generation to another.
According to the Digital 2026 Turkey Report, Turkey’s internet penetration has reached 88.3%,
hosting over 62 million active social media user identities corresponding to approximately
70.2% of the total population. Users spend an average of approximately 2 hours and 30 minutes

Social Media and Consumer Purchasing Behavior
One of the key factors that influence consumers' behavior in online environments is trust
(Mayer et al., 1995; McKnight et al., 2002). It is the tendency of consumers to use digital spaces,
sources of information, or other market actors even in the face of uncertainty and perceived risk
(Gefen et al., 2003; Kim & Peterson, 2017). Trust is also important in social commerce
environments to minimize uncertainty, enhance consumer perceived credibility and promote
consumer willingness to participate in online bu ying activities ( Hajli, 2015). Because
consumers are exposed to multiple information sources on social media, trust should be viewed
as a multidimensional construct encompassing trust in digital content, social media
advertisements, influencer recommendations, and user-generated reviews.
Among these dimensions, trust in social media advertisements reflects consumers' beliefs that
promotional messages presented on social networking platforms are truthful, reliable, and
credible. When consumers perceive advertisements as trustworthy, they ar e more likely to
develop favorable attitudes toward promoted products and exhibit stronger purchase intentions
(Lou & Yuan, 2019; Kim & Peterson, 2017). However, increasing awareness of persuasive
commercial practices may also reduce consumers' confidence in sponsored advertising, making
the relationship between advertising trust and purchasing behavior worthy of empirical
investigation.
Purchasing behavior refers to the process through which consumers recognize a need, search
for information, evaluate alternatives, make a purchase decision, and assess their post-purchase
experience (Kotler & Keller, 2016). In e -commerce environments, purchasin g behavior
encompasses consumers' intentions, decisions, and actual buying activities conducted through
digital platforms (Laudon & Traver, 2024). Social commerce extends this process by
integrating social interaction, user -generated content, online review s, and influencer
recommendations into consumers' decision-making journeys (Hajli, 2015; Zhang et al., 2022).
Consequently, e-commerce purchase behavior in this study is conceptualized as consumers'
tendency to engage in online purchasing activities, including purchasing frequency, the

influence of social media on purchase decisions, and recommendation intentions, which
together reflect consumers' overall engagement with social commerce platforms.
From the consumer behavior perspective, social media facilitates information exchange, social
interaction, and access to electronic word -of-mouth, all of which contribute to shaping
consumers' confidence in online purchasing decisions. Previous studies have consistently
demonstrated that the level of trust in digital content, in ads and recommendations from
influencers, in user-generated information positively affects the e-commerce purchase behavior
by decreasing the perceived risk and increasing the purchase confidence (Hajli, 2014; Arslan &
Beğendik, 2023). These arguments further suggest that social media usage intensity and
exposure to e -commerce content may reinforce purchasing frequency and decision -making
behavior, thereby providing the theoretical basis for the proposed hypotheses.
Moreover, the Social Proof Theory heavily influences consumers' interactions within these
platforms. Social proof is a psychological concept in which people mimic the actions, decisions
and thinking of others when they are unsure of what to do in unfamiliar or uncertain situations.
That is a henomenon that works via basic sociopsychological mechanisms: economy of thought
(saving mental energy by trusting in past events), economy of action (lessening the effort
needed for the physical action), and economy of communication (avoiding redundant words to
save mental effort). The three needs are affiliation (conforming to the norms of the group for
social alignment), need for trust in authority or expert peers (Cialdini, 2001).
Social media usage ıntensity
The intensity of social media use describes the frequency, amount, and depth of consumers'
social media usage. Those who spend more time on social media are likely to come across more
commercial content, influencers' endorsements, and electronic word of mo uth,  thereby
increasing opportunities for product discovery and purchase consideration (Kaplan & Haenlein,
2010; Kapoor et al., 2018;  Dwivedi et al., 2021). Past studies have repeatedly found that the
use of social media has a positive effect on online buying behavior, as it makes information
more accessible, encourages consumer engagement, and raises brand awareness (Hajli, 2015;
Appel et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2022; Elkatmış , 2024). With increased frequency of contact
with digital platforms, social media becomes part of the purchase process, and as the intensity
of contact increases, so does the expectation that it should positively influence the e-commerce
purchase process.

Influencer impact
Influencer impact refers to the perceived influence of consumers' attitudes and purchasing
decisions by the social media content creators due to their perceived expertise, authenticity, and
parasocial relationships (Lou & Yuan, 2019). Unlike conventional c elebrity endorsements,
influencers create sustained interactions with followers which fosters a sense of personal
connection and trustworthiness, thus increasing the persuasiveness of the recommendations
they make (De Veirman et al., 2017). The findings of the meta-analysis show that the credibility
of influencers has a significant impact on consumer trust, brand attitudes and purchase intent
across platforms (Ao et al., 2023; Pan et al., 2024). Among the younger segment of consumers,
their perception of influencers is more relatable and authentic than traditional advertising
channels, making influencer marketing more effective in this audience (Çakır, 2023).
Trust in social media advertisements
Trust in social media ads involves consumers' trust that the promoted social media content is
accurate, dependable, and not designed to deceive the consumer (Kim & Peterson, 2017; Lou
& Yuan, 2019). This trust factor is essential because social media ads c an become part of the
natural flow of content, making it difficult for consumers to distinguish between commercial
messages and user -generated information (Tuten & Solomon, 2017). Research suggests that,
advertising trust has been found to rely on several factors including message clarity, credibility
of the source (Hanaysha & Alhyasat, 2025) and perceived relevance. With the rise of consumer
awareness of targeted advertising, however, there has been a rise in individuals' skepticism,
which could reduce the direct impact of advertising trust on purchasing behavior (Campbell &
Grimm, 2019; Küçükelçi et al., 2024).
Exposure to e-commerce content
Exposure to e -commerce content refers to the frequency with which consumers encounter
product-related information, advertisements, and purchase links while using social media
platforms (Appel et al., 2020).Content recommendation systems based on algorithms are used
to deliver relevant and effective product exposure based on user browsing and interaction
history (Güven & Güven, 2023). The Mere Exposure Effect (Zajonc, 1968) proposes that
exposure to e-commerce content to stimuli increases familiarity and positive evaluation, leading
to increased consideration of purchase (Duffett, 2017). Additionally, exposure to e -commerce
content may activate consumers' purchase intentions through social influence mechanisms, as

Trust in user-generated reviews
Consumers' trust in user -generated reviews is based on confidence that reviews submitted by
other consumers are authentic, accurate and useful (Filieri et al., 2018; Erkan & Evans, 2016).
The reviews generated by users are one of the main types of electronic word-of-mouth, as they
make online shopping less risky and certain by giving social proof (Papageorgiou & Samanta,
2022). Most of the reviews come from peers, not firms, so they are thought to be more objective
and credible, and thus more influential in the decision making process (Hajli , 2015). Research
suggests that positive reviews boost consumer confidence in purchasing and improve
conversion rates, whereas negative reviews can greatly affect consumer engagement
(Palaniswamy & Duraiswamy, 2023).
Campaign and discount effectiveness
Campaign and discount effectiveness reflects what consumers think of the attractiveness,
timeliness and economic value of a campaign they have seen in social media ( Zhang  &
Benyoucef, 2022). Earlier research suggests that promotional campaigns positively affect
consumers' intent to purchase by increasing perceived savings and increasing consumers to
purchase now (Campbell & Grimm, 2019).  Promotional campaigns are quickly transmitted in
social commerce contexts via social sharing and tags, which increase their spread and
effectiveness (Molinillo et al., 2020). Research has shown that successful promotional
strategies have a positive effect on purchase intentions, especially  when supported by social
endorsement and peer recommendation (Ismat et al., 2023; Erkasap, 2024). In addition to the
economic advantages, promotional campaigns make customers feel the need to act now, as they
may not be able to find the same deal later. Thus, campaign and discount efficiency must not
only be considered a push technique but as a psychological push as well that enhances
consumers' purchase motivation in social commerce settings (Hanaysha & Alhyasat, 2025;
Campbell & Grimm, 2019).

The multidimensional effects of social media on e -commerce have been discussed in many
studies. Various studies such as Isha, Malviya, & Vidani (2024) and Mapanje (2024), Dwivedi

METHODS
This study employed a cross -sectional survey design using convenience sampling including
social media users residing in Türkiye through voluntary participation to examine the
relationships between social media use and factors related to e -commerce purchase behavior.
Due to time and access constraints, probability sampling was not possible; therefore, the
generalizability of the findings is limited.
Data were collected between October 12, 2025, and November 2, 2025, via Google Forms and
disseminated through social media platforms such as Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, and
Telegram. A total of 407 valid responses were included in the final analysis.   This study was
conducted with the approval of the Ethics Committee of Istanbul Gelisim University (Protocol
No: 2025-18-03).
The questionnaire included items related to influencer impact, social media usage intensity,
exposure to e-commerce content, trust in social media advertisements, trust in user -generated
reviews, campaign and discount effectiveness , demographic characteristics,   and e-commerce
purchase behavior. All variables were measured using a 5 -point Likert scale ranging from 1
(strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). The hypotheses were tested using correlation analysis,
ANOVA and independent samples t-test.
E-commerce purchase behavior was measured by three items assessing measuring shopping
frequency, perceived influence of social media on purchase decisions, and recommendation
intention, adapted from Hajli (2015). A composite index was created by averaging the three
items.

Influencer impact was measured by several items that evaluated perceived credibility,

Trust in social media advertisements, exposure to e-commerce content, campaign and discount
effectiveness, and trust in user-generated reviews were measured using single-item indicators,

Hypothesis Testing Results
This section displays the results of hypothesis testing performed to investigate the relationship
between factors associated with social media and e -commerce purchase behaviors. Before
hypothesis testing, the normality of data was checked through the calcu lation of values of
skewness and kurtosis. Correlation analyses, one-way ANOVA and independent samples t-tests
were conducted to test the proposed hypotheses. A significance level of 0.05 was adopted for
all statistical analyses.
H1: Social Media Usage Intensity → Purchase Behavior
A statistically significant and positive relationship was established between the frequency of
social media use and e-commerce purchase behavior (r = 0.337, p < .001, N = 407). This
suggests that users who are more active on social media are likely to display stronger e-
commerce purchase behaviors. Hence, H1 is supported.
H2: Influencer Impact → Purchase Behavior
The findings outlined a positive correlation between influencers impact and purchase behavior
on e-commerce (r = 0.709; p < .001). Based on the findings revealed above, H2 is supported.
H3: Trust in Social Media Advertisements → Purchase Behavior
Since the normality assumption for trust in social media advertisements was not met, a
Spearman’s rho correlation test was performed. The results showed there is no significant
relationship between trust in social media advertisements and e -commerce purchase behavior
(ρ = -0.012, p = .807).  Therefore, H3 is not supported.
H4: Campaign and Discount Effectiveness → Purchase Behavior
Analysis of the Pearson correlation showed a significant positive relationship between the
perceived campaign and discount effectiveness and e-commerce purchase behavior (r = 0.635,
p < 0.001). Hence, H4 was supported.
H5: Trust in User-Generated Reviews → Purchase Behavior
Since the distribution in the user -generated reviews variable is not normally distributed,
Spearman’s rho correlation test was used. The findings indicated a weak significant positive
correlation between trust in user-generated reviews and e-commerce purchasing behavior (ρ =
0.197, p < .001). This supports H5.

DISCUSSION and CONCLUSION
This paper focused on identifying determinants of purchasing behavior in e-commerce using a
framework consisting of behavioral exposure, trust constructs, and demographics. This study
contributes towards understanding the effects of social environment s created by social media
platforms on purchasing behaviors of consumer’s e-commerce, especially in a developing
country like Türkiye.
Behavioral Drivers of E-commerce Purchase Behavior
The results indicate that social media usage intensity positively and significantly influences e -
commerce purchasing behavior (H1). This is in line with the Uses and Gratifications Theory
(Katz, Blumler, & Gurevitch, 1973), which indicates that people utilize various media platforms
to fulfill their needs, such as seeking information, seeking entertainment, and engaging in social
interactions. Greater engagement with social media platforms may increase e xposure to
commercial content and purchasing opportunities. Similar observations were found in past
studies that stressed the importance of engagement intensity in e-commerce (Hajli, 2015; Appel
et al., 2020).
Another factor that emerged as a significant predictor of purchasing behavior is influencer
impact (H2). This finding supports previous research emphasizing the importance of perceived
credibility, expertise, and relatability of influencers in shaping consumer attitudes and online
purchasing decisions (Lou and Yuan, 2019; De Veirman et al., 2017; Çakır, 2023).
Campaigns and discounts were found to be positively associated with e -commerce purchase
behavior. This result suggests that promotional strategies continue to play an important role in
encouraging online purchasing decisions within social commerce environm ents. Previous
studies similarly emphasize the influence of price advantages and promotional offers on online
consumer behavior (Zhang & Benyoucef, 2022).
Furthermore, being engaged in viewing e-commerce content can have significant influence on
purchasing behaviors (H6). The results are consistent with the Mere Exposure Effect hypothesis
(Zajonc, 1968), suggesting repeated exposure to a stimulus may increase familiarity and
positive evaluation. repeated exposure to product -related content, advertisements, and
influencer posts in the field of social commerce can stimulate the engagement of consumers in
the process of online purchasing activities (Duffett, 2017; Appel et al., 2020).

Trust-Based Mechanisms and Social Proof
Despite the researchers' hypothesis, it was found that there is no statistically significant
connection between the level of trust in social media advertisement and purchasing behavior
(H3). The reason for such results could be that consumers are becoming more and more
skeptical towards social media advertisements because of their knowledge about business
objectives. Similar findings have been reported in studies emphasizing declining trust in digital
advertising environments (Campbell & Grimm, 2019).
Nevertheless, the relationship between trust in user -generated reviews and social commerce
purchase behavior has proven to be significantly positive (H5). The established relationship is
consistent with the Social Proof Theory posited by Cialdini (2001). Social Proof Theory refers
to the psychological tendency of individuals to rely on the opinions and behaviors of others
when making decisions under conditions of uncertainty. According to Cialdini (2001), people
frequently interpret the behavior of others a s evidence of the correct course of action. This
phenomenon operates through three interrelated mechanisms: cognitive efficiency (using
others' judgments to reduce decision -making effort), the need for social affiliation (seeking
alignment with group norms  for social acceptance), and trust in perceived  experts or
knowledgeable peers . Also, trusting others' opinions might minimize the risk and increase
confidence when making online purchases. Previous research into social commerce has also
produced findings that support the current finding (Erkan & Evans, 2016; Filieri et al., 2018).
The insignificant relationship between trust in social media advertisements and purchase
behavior is also consistent with recent empirical studies suggesting that advertising perceptions
alone may not directly influence online purchasing decisions. Instead, purchasing behavior may
depend on broader contextual factors such as digital literacy, prior online shopping experience,
and perceptions of authenticity (Küçükelçi et al., 2023)
Demographic Differences in Social Commerce Behavior
The results identified significant gender -based differences in e -commerce purchase behavior,
with female users showing higher purchase engagement than male users. This finding is
consistent with previous studies suggesting that women tend to engage more ac tively with
social media interactions and online shopping activities (Dittmar, Long, & Meek, 2004; Hasan,
2010).
Additionally, there were important differences between the various age groups (H8): Younger
users were more engaged in social commerce than the older ones. This finding may be

Key Findings
The findings demonstrate that several social media -related factors are associated with e -
commerce purchasing behavior. The findings of this study show that social media usage
intensity, influencer impact, exposure to e -commerce content, campaign and discou nt
effectiveness, and trust in user -generated reviews are significant positive determinants of
purchasing behavior.
In contrast, there was no significant relationship found between trust in social media
advertisements and purchasing decisions, and this highlights that, in social commerce
platforms, trust in advertisements alone could not be a guarantee to influence purc hasing
decisions. In addition, there were significant differences found in purchasing decisions among
different genders and ages, and this shows that demographic factors remain important in
understanding social commerce behavior.
Theoretical Contributions
This study contributes to the social commerce literature by examining multiple social media –
related factors associated with e -commerce purchase behavior within the context of Türkiye.
The findings were interpreted through theoretical perspectives such as Uses and Gratifications
Theory, the Mere Exposure Effect, and Social Proof Theory, which helped explain the
behavioral and trust-based dimensions of social commerce engagement.
The study also highlights the continued importance of influencer impact, promotional
strategies, and user-generated reviews in shaping online purchasing behavior. In this respect,
the findings support previous social commerce research emphasizing the combi ned role of
social influence, repeated exposure, and peer -generated content in digital purchasing
environments.

The findings suggest that businesses may benefit from social commerce strategies that combine
influencer collaborations, product visibility, promotional campaigns, and user -generated
reviews. Rather than relying solely on traditional social media advertisi ng, firms may achieve

Limitations and Future Research
Despite its contributions, this study has several limitations. First, the use of convenience
sampling limits the generalizability of the findings. Second, the cross -sectional nature of the
study does not allow causal interpretations of the observed relatio nships. Future studies may
employ longitudinal or experimental research designs to examine changes in consumer
behavior over time. Third, while this study utilized single -item indicators for constructs such
as trust in advertisements, content exposure, campaign effectiveness, and user reviews based
on their concrete and unidimensional nature, this approach may limit the comprehensive capture
of multi -faceted psychological constructs. Future research should deploy multi -item
psychometric scales to validate and expand upon the internal consistency and variance
explained by these specific behavioral determinants.
In addition, future research may investigate platform -specific differences across social media
environments such as Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube. Comparative cross -cultural studies
and the inclusion of additional socioeconomic variables may also contribu te to a deeper
understanding of social commerce behavior.
Another limitation concerns the unequal distribution of participants across age groups.
Although one-way ANOVA is considered a robust statistical method for comparing groups with
different sample sizes, the relatively small number of participants in the 55  years and above
category compared with younger age groups may have slightly reduced the statistical power of
age-related comparisons. Therefore, age-related findings should be interpreted with caution and
validated using more balanced samples in future studies.
