/one.tnum Introduction
The rapid advancement of digital technologies has
fundamentally transformed the tourism industry, reshaping
how destinations communicate with potential visitors, build
destination brands, and inﬂuence travel-related decision-making.
Within broader discussions of sustainable tourism management,
digital transformation is relevant because it can inﬂuence how
destinations communicate with prospective visitors, manage
destination visibility, and shape visitor interest. However, digital
promotion alone does not establish sustainable tourism outcomes,
which depend on how visitor demand is managed in relation
to environmental capacity, cultural resources, community
participation, beneﬁt distribution, and long-term destination
resilience. In this study, sustainable tourism development is treated
as a broader destination-management context rather than as an
empirically measured outcome. The model examines Destination
Behavioral Intention (DBI), which captures prospective behavioral
responses and destination preferences. Such behavioral intention
may be relevant to tourism demand and destination development,
but stronger behavioral intention is not inherently equivalent
to sustainable tourism development. Whether visitor demand
contributes to sustainable outcomes depends on how destinations
manage environmental pressures, carrying capacity, cultural
resources, community participation, beneﬁt distribution, and
long-term economic resilience. Accordingly, the present study
does not interpret DBI as a proxy measure of sustainability.
Instead, its sustainability relevance lies in identifying digital and
experiential factors associated with destination evaluation and
visitor interest that destination managers may incorporate into
broader sustainability-oriented tourism strategies. This distinction
is consistent with recent evidence showing that sustainability-
oriented behavioral intention and loyalty-related intentions such
as revisiting and recommending a destination can represent
theoretically distinct behavioral outcomes associated with diﬀerent
experiential pathways (Fan et al., 2026).
The proliferation of social media platforms, online review
systems, mobile applications, and immersive digital experiences
now lets destinations engage global audiences in real time
(Sisli et al., 2026 ). Unlike traditional marketing channels, digital
platforms facilitate interactive communication, personalized
content delivery, and user-generated information exchange,
enabling destinations to establish stronger relationships with
prospective tourists and inﬂuence perceptions of destinations more
eﬀectively. As travelers increasingly rely on digital information
sources to search for travel opportunities, evaluate destination
alternatives, and share post-visit experiences, digital marketing
has emerged as a strategic tool for enhancing destination
competitiveness and attracting visitors in highly competitive
tourism markets ( Jasrotia et al., 2026 ; Leung et al., 2013 ;
Pandey and Kumar, 2025 ; Tran and Rudolf, 2022 ). The growing
importance of digital marketing is particularly evident in emerging
tourism destinations, where limited international visibility and
resource constraints often hinder destination development. In such
contexts, digital technologies provide cost-eﬀective mechanisms
to increase destination awareness, reduce information asymmetry,
and strengthen destination branding among domestic and
international tourists. Social media platforms, online communities,
and electronic word-of-mouth have become inﬂuential sources
of information that shape tourists’ cognitive evaluations and
behavioral intentions before actual travel occurs.
Consequently, tourism organizations and destination
management authorities increasingly invest in digital marketing
initiatives designed to enhance destination image, improve online
reputation, and communicate authentic tourism experiences that
resonate with contemporary travelers ( Lina and Aisulu, 2025 ).
Within this evolving landscape, the China-Pakistan Economic
Corridor (CPEC) presents a unique and strategically signiﬁcant
context for examining the role of digital marketing in tourism
development. As a ﬂagship initiative of the Belt and Road Initiative,
CPEC connects western China with Pakistan through extensive
investments in transportation infrastructure, communication
networks, logistics systems, and regional development projects.
Beyond its economic and geopolitical signiﬁcance, the corridor
encompasses destinations characterized by rich cultural heritage,
ethnic diversity, historical signiﬁcance, and distinctive natural
landscapes. Destinations such as Kashgar and the surrounding
regions possess substantial tourism potential due to their unique
cultural identities, Silk Road heritage, and ecological attractions
(Yu et al., 2023 ). Tourism development within the corridor
has therefore been recognized as an important mechanism
for promoting regional economic diversiﬁcation, employment
generation, cultural preservation, and sustainable development
(Ahmed et al., 2025 ; Hameed et al., 2022 ; Mahmood et al., 2020 ).
Recent CPEC-speciﬁc evidence further indicates that infrastructure
development, cultural integration, and spatial connectivity are
closely associated with cultural tourism development and that
tourism-related development may generate spatial spillover eﬀects
across connected regions ( Zuhaib et al., 2025 ). The expansion
of digital infrastructure accompanying CPEC development has
further accelerated opportunities for destination promotion and
tourism market expansion.
Improvements in internet connectivity, mobile technologies,
digital payment systems, and online communication networks
have increased the accessibility of tourism information and
enhanced interactions between destinations and prospective
visitors ( Mandi´c and Garbin Praniˇcevi´c, 2019 ). Social media
platforms such as Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, and WeChat
enable destination marketers to disseminate promotional
content, encourage user-generated storytelling, and foster
online communities that inﬂuence travel perceptions and
destination evaluations ( Baber and Baber, 2023 ; Preisler,
2025). Similarly, online review platforms such as Tripadvisor,
https://www.booking.com, and Google Reviews have become
inﬂuential sources of information through which tourists assess
destination quality, credibility, and trustworthiness ( Zelenka et al.,
2021). Furthermore, digital storytelling and immersive online
experiences enable destinations to communicate authentic
cultural narratives and emotional value propositions that
appeal to increasingly experience-oriented travelers. Despite
these opportunities, destinations located within the CPEC
region continue to face substantial marketing challenges. Many
destinations remain relatively unknown to international audiences
and compete against established tourism destinations possessing

social media marketing activities enhance destination awareness
and engagement by providing information-rich and interactive
communication channels ( Huang et al., 2024 ; Hudson and Thal,
2013). Similarly, online reputation management has been shown
to inﬂuence tourists’ trust, perceived destination quality, and
destination evaluations through electronic reviews and ratings
(Filieri, 2015 ; Li et al., 2018 ). Research has also emphasized
the signiﬁcance of authentic tourism experiences in creating
meaningful emotional connections between tourists and
destinations, thereby strengthening destination attractiveness
and visitor satisfaction ( Jiang et al., 2017 ). Although these studies
have generated valuable insights, the existing literature remains
fragmented in several important respects. First, previous research
has predominantly examined social media marketing, online
reputation, and experiential authenticity as separate predictors of
tourist perceptions and behavioral outcomes of tourist behavior,
providing a limited understanding of how these digital marketing
stimuli collectively inﬂuence destination evaluations within a
uniﬁed theoretical framework.
Second, while destination image has long been recognized as
an important correlate of tourist decision-making, comparatively
limited attention has been devoted to examining whether indirect
relationships between digital and experiential destination-
related stimuli and destination behavioral intention operate
through destination image within an integrated framework.
Third, empirical evidence from emerging tourism destinations
remains relatively limited, particularly in strategically important
regions experiencing simultaneous digital transformation and
infrastructure development. Consequently, current knowledge
regarding the relationships between digital destination-marketing
factors and tourists’ behavioral intentions in emerging tourism
markets remains incomplete. To address these gaps, the present
study integrates the Stimulus–Organism–Response (S-O-R)
framework with Destination Image Theory to explain how
destination-related marketing and experiential perceptions
are associated with tourists’ destination evaluations and
behavioral intentions. Within the S-O-R framework, social
media marketing eﬀorts, e-reputation, and perceived authentic
destination experience represent salient destination-related stimuli
that may shape tourists’ cognitive and aﬀective evaluations. In
this study, perceived authentic destination experience refers to
tourists’ perceptions or expectations that a destination oﬀers
culturally genuine, distinctive, locally embedded, and heritage-
related experiences. Importantly, the construct concerns the
perceived authenticity of the destination experience itself,
rather than the perceived authenticity of digital marketing
communication. These destination-related evaluations are
reﬂected in destination image, which is theoretically associated
with subsequent behavioral intentions.
Destination Image Theory complements this perspective by
explaining how information and experiential cues contribute
to tourists’ overall cognitive and aﬀective representations of
destinations. Accordingly, this study develops and empirically
evaluates a conceptual model examining the relationships among
social media marketing eﬀorts, e-reputation, perceived authentic
destination experience, destination image, and destination
behavioral intention in CPEC destinations. Speciﬁcally, the study
pursues three objectives: (1) to examine the associations of key
digital and experiential destination-related stimuli with destination
image; (2) to assess the association between destination image
and destination behavioral intention; and (3) to evaluate whether
the observed indirect relationships through destination image are
consistent with the mediating pathways proposed by the S-O-R
framework. While these relationships may provide useful insights
for tourism development and destination management, the study
does not directly operationalize environmental, social, community,
or long-term economic sustainability outcomes. This study makes
several contributions to tourism and destination-marketing
research. First, it integrates Social Media Marketing Eﬀorts,
E-Reputation, and Perceived Authentic Destination Experience
within a common theoretical framework, allowing digital, socially
generated reputational, and experiential destination-related cues
to be examined simultaneously rather than as isolated predictors.
Second, the integration of the Stimulus–Organism–Response
(S-O-R) framework with Destination Image Theory assigns
complementary theoretical roles to the two perspectives.
S-O-R provides the general process architecture by
distinguishing destination-related stimuli, an internal organism
state, and a behavioral response, whereas Destination Image
Theory gives tourism-speciﬁc substantive meaning to the organism
component by specifying Destination Image as tourists’ cognitive–
aﬀective representation of the destination. The theoretical
contribution therefore lies not in claiming that the individual
associations examined are entirely new, but in organizing
heterogeneous digital, reputational, and experiential cues within
a common evaluative structure in which Destination Image
represents the theoretically speciﬁed internal state associated
with Destination Behavioral Intention. Third, the signiﬁcant
indirect associations through Destination Image provide empirical
evidence consistent with this theoretically ordered pathway,
although the cross-sectional research design does not establish
temporal precedence or causal mediation. Fourth, the study
extends the application of this integrated framework to CPEC
destinations, an emerging tourism context characterized by
simultaneous infrastructure development, expanding digital
connectivity, cultural heritage, and increasing destination visibility.

/two.tnum./one.tnum Destination image and destination
behavioral intention
Destination image is one of the most inﬂuential concepts
in tourism marketing because it shapes tourists’ perceptions,
evaluations, and behavioral intentions toward a destination
(Yuan and Vui, 2023 ). Destination Image Theory suggests
that individuals form mental representations of destinations
based on cognitive beliefs, aﬀective evaluations, and conative
intentions, which subsequently inﬂuence travel-related decisions
(Michael et al., 2018 ; Park and Lee, 2022 ). The cognitive
dimension reﬂects tourists’ knowledge and beliefs regarding
destination attributes such as attractions, accessibility, safety, and
infrastructure, whereas the aﬀective dimension captures emotional
responses, including excitement, pleasure, and relaxation.
Together, these dimensions contribute to the overall image of
a destination and inﬂuence subsequent behavioral outcomes
(Todorovi´c et al., 2018 ). The growing importance of digital media
has transformed the processes of destination image formation.
Unlike traditional destination marketing channels, digital
platforms enable tourists to access large volumes of user-generated
content, online reviews, photographs, videos, and travel narratives
before making travel decisions. Social networking platforms and
online review systems provide rich information environments that
inﬂuence both cognitive assessments and emotional responses
toward destinations. Consequently, the destination image is
increasingly shaped by digital interactions rather than solely by
oﬃcial promotional materials.
Within the S-O-R framework, destination image is
conceptualized as the organism component representing
tourists’ internal cognitive and aﬀective evaluations of
destination-related stimuli. The framework theoretically
proposes that such evaluations are associated with subsequent
behavioral responses. Previous tourism research has reported
positive associations between favorable destination image
and behavioral intentions such as visiting, recommending,
and revisiting destinations ( Afshardoost and Eshaghi, 2020 ;
Taecharungroj and Mathayomchan, 2021 ). Accordingly,
destination image is treated in the present study as an important
evaluative construct that is theoretically positioned between
destination-related stimuli and destination behavioral intention.
H1: Destination image is positively associated with destination
behavioral intention.
/two.tnum./two.tnum Social media marketing eﬀorts and
destination image
Social media marketing has become an essential component of
destination marketing strategies, enabling tourism organizations
to communicate directly with potential visitors through
interactive and user-centered digital platforms ( Ergasheva,
2026). Social media platforms facilitate information sharing, user
engagement, and electronic word-of-mouth, allowing destinations
to promote attractions, communicate brand values, and strengthen
relationships with tourists ( Sotiriadis and V an Zyl, 2013). Through
visual content, destination stories, online reviews, and user-
generated experiences, social media provides rich informational
and emotional cues that inﬂuence tourists’ perceptions of
destinations ( Hudson and Thal, 2013 ; Abubakar and Ilkan,
2016). According to Digital Marketing Theory, interactive
communication and personalized content enhance consumer
engagement and positively inﬂuence brand perceptions. In
tourism settings, social media platforms allow tourists to access
authentic travel experiences, destination images, and peer
recommendations, thereby reducing information uncertainty
and improving destination evaluations. Moreover, destination
marketers can utilize social media to showcase cultural attractions,
natural landscapes, and unique tourism experiences, enhancing
destination visibility and attractiveness among potential visitors
(Leong et al., 2024 ). Within the S-O-R framework, social media
marketing eﬀorts represent external stimuli that inﬂuence
tourists’ internal evaluations of destinations. Frequent exposure
to engaging and informative social media content can strengthen
positive cognitive and aﬀective perceptions, thereby enhancing
destination image. Empirical evidence indicates that social
media marketing signiﬁcantly improves destination evaluations,
destination branding eﬀectiveness, and tourists’ travel intentions
(Li et al., 2018 ; Siegel et al., 2024 ). Therefore, eﬀective social media
marketing eﬀorts are expected to contribute positively to the
formation of destination image.
H2: Social media marketing eﬀorts are positively associated
with destination image.
/two.tnum./three.tnum E-reputation and destination image
E-reputation refers to the collective online perception
of a destination formed through tourists’ reviews, ratings,
recommendations, and electronic word-of-mouth (eWOM)
shared across digital platforms. In contemporary tourism
markets, travelers increasingly rely on online information
when evaluating destinations because peer-generated content
is often perceived as more credible and trustworthy than
traditional promotional communications ( Baber et al., 2022 ;
Filieri, 2015 ). Consequently, e-reputation has become a critical
factor inﬂuencing tourists’ perceptions and destination evaluation
processes. According to signaling theory, online reviews and
ratings serve as informational signals that reduce uncertainty
and perceived risk associated with travel decisions. Positive
reviews, favorable ratings, and supportive recommendations
enhance perceptions of destination quality, reliability, and
trustworthiness, whereas negative online feedback can adversely

/two.tnum./four.tnum Perceived authentic destination
experience and destination image
Authenticity is an important component of tourists’ evaluation
of cultural and experiential destinations because travelers may
value encounters that are perceived as culturally genuine,
distinctive, and representative of local heritage, traditions, and
ways of life ( Tian et al., 2020 ; Zhou et al., 2023 ; Çiftçi and Çizel,
2024). In the present study, Perceived Authentic Destination
Experience (AE) refers to the extent to which individuals
perceive or anticipate that a destination can provide culturally
genuine experiences, meaningful interaction with local people
and traditions, distinctive cultural encounters, and a sense of
local heritage and lifestyle. This conceptualization concerns the
perceived authenticity of the destination experience, rather than
the perceived authenticity or credibility of digital marketing
communication. This distinction is particularly relevant in
destination decision-making because perceptions of experiential
authenticity may be formed both through prior visitation and
through destination-related knowledge acquired before travel.
Prospective tourists can develop expectations concerning local
culture, heritage, traditions, and experiential distinctiveness from
multiple information sources, whereas previous visitors can draw
additionally on their own destination experiences. Accordingly,
throughout this study, AE denotes the perceived or anticipated
experiential authenticity of the destination itself; it does not refer
to an “authentic digital experience” or to the perceived authenticity
or credibility of destination-marketing content.
Recent evidence from rural tourism destinations further
suggests that perceived authenticity can be associated with
tourists’ aﬀective responses and sustainability-oriented behavioral
intentions. Speciﬁcally, Fan et al. (2026) found that perceived
authenticity was positively associated with place attachment, which
in turn was associated with sustainable behavioral intention. This
evidence reinforces the broader theoretical relevance of experiential
authenticity while also indicating that authenticity-related
perceptions may be associated with behavioral outcomes through
diﬀerent evaluative pathways. Authentic destination experiences
may contribute to favorable destination image formation
because perceived cultural genuineness, local distinctiveness,
and opportunities for meaningful cultural engagement provide
cognitive and aﬀective cues about what a destination represents
(Kolar and Zabkar, 2010 ; Yeh et al., 2025 ; Zhou et al., 2023 ).
Within the S-O-R framework, perceived authentic destination
experience therefore represents an experiential destination-related
stimulus associated with tourists’ internal evaluations of the
destination. Destinations perceived as oﬀering more genuine and
culturally distinctive experiences would consequently be expected
to receive more favorable cognitive and aﬀective evaluations.
H4: Perceived authentic destination experience is positively
associated with destination image.
/two.tnum./five.tnum Destination image as a proposed
mediating pathway
The S-O-R framework theoretically proposes that
environmental stimuli are associated with behavioral responses
through individuals’ internal cognitive and aﬀective states
(Mehrabian and Russell, 1974 ). Applied to the present tourism
context, social media marketing eﬀorts, e-reputation, and perceived
authentic destination experience represent destination-related
stimuli, whereas destination image represents tourists’ cognitive
and aﬀective evaluation of the destination. Destination behavioral
intention constitutes the behavioral-response component of
the framework. From this theoretical perspective, destination
image is proposed as an intervening evaluative pathway linking
destination-related stimuli with destination behavioral intention.
Previous research has reported associations between social
media engagement and destination perceptions, between online
reputation and destination evaluation, and between perceived
experiential authenticity and favorable destination perceptions.
Destination image, in turn, has been associated with behavioral
intentions such as willingness to visit, recommend, and prefer a
destination (Zhang et al., 2018 ; Chi and Qu, 2008 ; Lee and Gretzel,
2012; Pop et al., 2021 ; San Martín and Del Bosque, 2008 ;
Ha and Nguyen, 2023 ; Stylidis et al., 2017 ). These relationships
provide a theoretical basis for expecting signiﬁcant indirect
associations between the antecedent constructs and destination
behavioral intention through destination image. Importantly, the
proposed mediation relationships represent theoretically speciﬁed
pathways evaluated using cross-sectional survey data. Accordingly,
signiﬁcant indirect eﬀects would provide evidence consistent with
the S-O-R-based mediating pathways but would not establish
temporal precedence or causal mediation among the constructs.
Therefore, the following mediation hypotheses are proposed:
H5: Destination image mediates the association between social
media marketing eﬀorts and destination behavioral intention.
H6: Destination image mediates the association between e-
reputation and destination behavioral intention.
H7: Destination image mediates the association between
perceived authentic destination experience and destination
behavioral intention.

/two.tnum./six.tnum Conceptual framework
As illustrated in Figure 1, this study adopts the Stimulus–
Organism–Response (S-O-R) framework to examine how
destination-related digital and experiential stimuli are associated
with destination behavioral intention in destinations along China’s
CPEC corridor. Within this framework, social media marketing
eﬀorts (SMME), e-reputation (ER), and perceived authentic
destination experience (AE) represent the stimulus (S) component.
SMME and e-reputation capture digital marketing and online
informational cues, whereas AE captures respondents’ perceptions
or expectations regarding the cultural genuineness, distinctiveness,
local interaction, and heritage-related character of the destination
experience. Thus, AE refers to the perceived authenticity of
the destination experience itself and not to the authenticity of
digital promotional content. In the present study, Social Media
Marketing Eﬀorts (SMME) is operationalized as a ﬁrst-order
reﬂective construct capturing respondents’ overall perceptions of
destination-related social media marketing activities, including
engaging content, online promotional exposure, inﬂuencer or
travel-blogger collaboration, informational value, and perceived
relevance to travel decision-making. These stimuli provide
informational, social, and emotional cues that inﬂuence tourists’
evaluations during the destination decision-making process.
The organism (O) component is represented by the destination
image (DI), which reﬂects tourists’ overall cognitive and aﬀective
assessments of a destination.
The response (R) component is represented by Destination
Behavioral Intention (DBI), which captures respondents’ intentions
and destination preferences, including willingness to visit,
recommend, seek further information about, and consider the
destination among preferred future travel choices. The integration
of these two theoretical perspectives is complementary rather
than redundant. The S-O-R framework provides the general
process architecture by distinguishing destination-related stimuli,
an internal organism state, and a behavioral response, whereas
Destination Image Theory provides tourism-speciﬁc substantive
meaning to the organism component by conceptualizing
Destination Image as tourists’ cognitive–aﬀective representation
of the destination. Accordingly, the theoretical value of their
integration does not rest on proposing that each individual
association is unprecedented; rather, it lies in positioning
heterogeneous digital, reputational, and experiential destination-
related cues within a common evaluative process in which
Destination Image serves as the theoretically speciﬁed internal state
associated with Destination Behavioral Intention. The framework
theoretically proposes that social media marketing eﬀorts, e-
reputation, and perceived authentic destination experience are
associated with destination image, while destination image is
associated with destination behavioral intention. Furthermore,
destination image is proposed to mediate the associations
between the three destination-related antecedents and destination
behavioral intention. Accordingly, the conceptual model speciﬁes
indirect pathways through destination image that are consistent
with the S-O-R framework. These pathways represent theoretically
ordered relationships and should not be interpreted as establishing
temporal or causal sequencing in the present cross-sectional
research design.

/three.tnum./one.tnum Research context
As shown in Figure 2, this study was conducted in tourism
destinations located within the Chinese section of the China-
Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC), a ﬂagship initiative of
China’s Belt and Road Initiative (BRI). The study area primarily
encompasses Kashgar Prefecture and surrounding tourism
destinations in western China, a region characterized by rich
cultural heritage, historical signiﬁcance, and unique natural
landscapes. Recent investments in transportation infrastructure,
digital connectivity, and tourism facilities have substantially
enhanced destination accessibility and tourism development
opportunities. Simultaneously, the increasing adoption of digital
technologies has transformed how destinations communicate with
potential visitors, manage their online reputation, and promote
authentic tourism experiences. Consequently, the CPEC region
provides an appropriate context for examining the inﬂuence
of digital marketing stimuli on destination image and tourist
attraction within an emerging tourism market.
/three.tnum./two.tnum Research design and data collection
This study employed a quantitative research design to examine
the relationships among social media marketing eﬀorts, e-

conceptual model also concerns prospective tourists’ destination

/three.tnum./three.tnum Measurement of constructs
All measurement items were adapted from established
scales that have demonstrated satisfactory reliability and validity
in previous tourism and marketing research. Minor wording
modiﬁcations were introduced to ensure contextual relevance to
destinations located along China’s CPEC corridor while preserving
the original conceptual meaning of each construct. All items
were measured using a ﬁve-point Likert scale ranging from 1
(strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree) ( Armstrong, 1987 ). The
complete list of measurement items is presented in Appendix A.
Social Media Marketing Eﬀorts (SMME) were operationalized
as a ﬁrst-order reﬂective construct using ﬁve items adapted
from Hays et al. (2013) and Hudson and Thal (2013) . The items
assess respondents’ overall perceptions of destination-related social
media marketing activities, including the provision of engaging
content, frequency of exposure to destination-related social
media posts, collaboration with inﬂuencers or travel bloggers,
provision of useful and informative content, and the perceived
relevance of social media marketing to travel decision-making.
The ﬁve items are presented in Appendix A1. Accordingly, the
ﬁve observed indicators were speciﬁed as reﬂective manifestations
of respondents’ overall perception of destination-related social
media marketing eﬀorts; no separate ﬁrst-order dimensions of e-
WOM, trendiness, interaction, customization, or entertainment
were estimated in the ﬁnal measurement model.
E-Reputation (ER) was measured using scales adapted
from Filieri (2015) , Leung et al. (2013) , and Litvin et al. (2008) ,
capturing tourists’ perceptions of the credibility, trustworthiness,
and usefulness of online reviews, ratings, and electronic word-
of-mouth (eWOM). These measures have been extensively
validated in tourism and hospitality contexts, where online
information signiﬁcantly inﬂuences travel decision-making.
Perceived Authentic Destination Experience (AE) was measured
using ﬁve items adapted from Kolar and Zabkar (2010) and
Yeh et al. (2025) . The items capture respondents’ perceptions
or expectations that the destination provides culturally genuine
experiences, distinctive tourism activities, interaction with
local people and traditions, unique cultural encounters, and a
meaningful sense of local heritage and lifestyle. Accordingly,
AE is operationalized as perceived experiential authenticity of
the destination rather than as perceived authenticity of digital
marketing content or communication. Because the sample
included both previous visitors and individuals familiar with the
destination who had not yet visited, the construct should not be
interpreted exclusively as a post-visit evaluation. Accordingly, the
ﬁve AE indicators were interpreted as destination-level perceptions
or expectations that could be evaluated on the basis of either
prior visitation or existing destination knowledge, rather than as a
retrospective post-visit scale requiring direct personal experience.
Destination Image (DI) was measured using cognitive and
aﬀective dimensions adapted from previous destination image
research. The cognitive dimension captures tourists’ evaluations
of destination attributes, whereas the aﬀective dimension reﬂects
emotional responses toward the destination. These dimensions
have been extensively validated and widely applied in tourism
studies examining destination perceptions and behavioral
intentions ( San Martín and Del Bosque, 2008 ; Stylidis et al.,
2017). Finally, Destination Behavioral Intention (DBI) was
measured using items adapted from previous tourism studies
on destination choice and behavioral intentions ( Chi and Qu,
2008; Lee and Gretzel, 2012 ). The construct captures respondents’
prospective behavioral intentions and destination preferences,
including intention to visit, willingness to recommend the
destination, motivation to learn more about it, and willingness to
consider it among preferred future travel choices. Accordingly,
DBI represents a behavioral-intention construct rather than the
attractiveness or attributes of tourist attractions themselves. Higher
scores indicate stronger favorable behavioral intentions toward
the destination.

/five.tnum Discussion
This study examined the associations among social media
marketing eﬀorts, e-reputation, perceived authentic destination
experience, destination image, and destination behavioral intention
in emerging tourism destinations along the China–Pakistan
Economic Corridor (CPEC). Grounded in the Stimulus–
Organism–Response (S-O-R) framework, the results indicate
that social media marketing eﬀorts, e-reputation, and perceived
authentic destination experience are positively associated with
destination image, while destination image is positively associated
with destination behavioral intention. The signiﬁcant indirect
eﬀects through destination image are consistent with the mediating
pathways theoretically proposed by the S-O-R framework.
These ﬁndings highlight the relevance of digital engagement,
online reputation, experiential authenticity, and destination
evaluation within the proposed model, while the cross-sectional
design does not establish causal or temporal relationships
among these constructs. Social Media Marketing Eﬀorts were
positively associated with Destination Image, supporting H2.
This result is consistent with previous studies highlighting
the role of social media platforms in enhancing destination
visibility, engagement, and brand perceptions ( Hays et al., 2013 ;
Li et al., 2018 ). Through user-generated content, inﬂuencer
communication, and destination storytelling, social media can
provide informational and emotional cues relevant to tourists’
pre-visit evaluations.
In the present study, the positive association between SMME
and Destination Image is consistent with the view that social-media
communication may be related to destination awareness and
favorable destination evaluations; however, the cross-sectional
design does not establish that greater social-media marketing
causally produces these outcomes. Similarly, E-Reputation was
positively associated with Destination Image, supporting H3. This
ﬁnding aligns with prior research emphasizing the importance of
online reviews, ratings, and electronic word-of-mouth in shaping
destination credibility and reducing perceived risk ( Baber et al.,
2022; Baber and Baber, 2023 ). Positive online reputation has
been associated in prior tourism research with greater trust
and conﬁdence in destination quality. In the present study,
E-Reputation was positively associated with Destination Image,
but this association should not be interpreted as demonstrating
that improvements in online reputation would necessarily
cause corresponding changes in destination evaluations. Among
the examined antecedents, Perceived Authentic Destination
Experience showed the strongest positive association with
Destination Image among the three antecedents, supporting
H4. This ﬁnding is consistent with research emphasizing the
importance of perceived cultural authenticity, experiential
distinctiveness, and meaningful engagement with local heritage in
destination evaluation (Jiang et al., 2017; Kolar and Zabkar, 2010).
The ﬁnding should be interpreted as indicating that
respondents who perceived or anticipated more culturally
genuine and distinctive destination experiences also reported
a more favorable destination image. Importantly, the result
concerns perceptions of the authenticity of the destination
experience rather than perceptions of whether digital marketing
content itself is authentic. In the CPEC region, rich cultural
heritage and local traditions provide strong opportunities for
developing diﬀerentiated and competitive tourism oﬀerings.
Destination Image was positively associated with Destination
Behavioral Intention, supporting H1. Consistent with Destination
Image Theory and previous tourism research, respondents
reporting a more favorable cognitive and aﬀective evaluation
of the destination also reported stronger behavioral intentions,
including willingness to visit and recommend the destination.
The explained variance in Destination Behavioral Intention ( R2
= 0.58) further indicates the substantial explanatory relevance
of destination image within the estimated model. The mediation
analysis identiﬁed signiﬁcant indirect associations between Social
Media Marketing Eﬀorts, E-Reputation, and Perceived Authentic
Destination Experience and Destination Behavioral Intention
through Destination Image. This pattern is consistent with the
S-O-R framework, which theoretically positions tourists’ cognitive
and aﬀective evaluations between destination-related stimuli
and behavioral responses. Accordingly, the ﬁndings support
Destination Image as a plausible mediating pathway within the
proposed conceptual model.
Nevertheless, the cross-sectional design does not establish
that changes in the antecedent constructs temporally precede
changes in Destination Image or that changes in Destination Image
subsequently cause stronger Destination Behavioral Intention. The
mediation results should therefore be interpreted as evidence of
statistically signiﬁcant indirect associations that are consistent
with, rather than causal proof of, the theoretically proposed
S-O-R sequence. Furthermore, the Importance–Performance
Map Analysis (IPMA) provides a complementary model-based
perspective on the relative importance and performance of the
examined antecedents.

/five.tnum./one.tnum Theoretical implications
This study oﬀers several theoretical contributions to tourism
and destination-marketing research. First, its contribution
lies in theoretically organizing multiple destination-related
antecedents within a common evaluative structure rather than
in claiming that the individual relationships among social media
marketing, online reputation, experiential authenticity, destination
image, and behavioral intention are themselves entirely novel.
Social Media Marketing Eﬀorts, E-Reputation, and Perceived
Authentic Destination Experience represent conceptually distinct
sources of destination-related cues, such as digital marketing
communication, socially generated reputational information, and
perceived or anticipated experiential authenticity, respectively,
and their simultaneous examination permits these heterogeneous
stimuli to be considered within the same destination-evaluation
process. Second, the study clariﬁes the complementary theoretical
roles of the S-O-R framework and Destination Image Theory.
S-O-R supplies the general Stimulus–Organism–Response
architecture, whereas Destination Image Theory speciﬁes the
tourism-speciﬁc substantive content of the organism component
by conceptualizing Destination Image as tourists’ cognitive and
aﬀective representation of destination-related information and
experiential cues. Destination Image is therefore positioned
not simply as another predictor in the structural model, but
as the theoretically speciﬁed evaluative state situated between
heterogeneous destination-related stimuli and Destination
Behavioral Intention. This integration provides greater theoretical
speciﬁcity to the organism component of S-O-R while situating
Destination Image within a broader process-oriented framework.
Third, the statistically signiﬁcant indirect associations
through Destination Image are consistent with this integrated
theoretical positioning. They indicate that the observed pattern
of relationships is compatible with a framework in which digital,
reputational, and experiential destination-related perceptions
are associated with Destination Behavioral Intention through a
common destination-evaluation state. However, because the study
is based on cross-sectional self-report data, these ﬁndings should
be interpreted as support for a theoretically ordered explanatory
framework rather than as evidence that the antecedent perceptions
temporally cause changes in Destination Image or that Destination
Image subsequently causes behavioral intention. Longitudinal or
experimental research would be required to establish the temporal
sequence implied by the theoretical framework.

/five.tnum./three.tnum Limitations and future research
directions
Despite its contributions, this study has several limitations
that provide avenues for future research. First, the study employed
a cross-sectional research design in which the antecedents,
destination image, and destination behavioral intention were
measured within the same survey framework. Consequently,
although the estimated indirect eﬀects are statistically signiﬁcant
and consistent with the theoretically proposed S-O-R pathways, the
design cannot establish temporal precedence or causal mediation
among the constructs. Alternative directional relationships cannot
be ruled out. Future research could employ longitudinal, panel,
experimental, or temporally separated designs to evaluate the
sequencing of destination-related stimuli, destination-image
formation, and behavioral intentions more directly. Second, the
study relied on self-reported survey data, with the predictor,
mediator, and outcome variables collected from the same
respondents using a single questionnaire. This design may be
susceptible to common method bias and respondent subjectivity.
Although procedural remedies and statistical diagnostics were
employed and did not indicate severe common method variance
under the applied criteria, these procedures cannot eliminate or
deﬁnitively rule out common method bias. Future research could
reduce this concern by using temporally separated measurements
and incorporating multi-source data such as social media
analytics, online reviews, behavioral tracking data, and destination
performance indicators.
Third, the study employed a non-probability snowball
sampling strategy and recruited respondents primarily through
digitally mediated tourism communities, including social media
groups, online networks, and travel-related platforms. This
recruitment approach may have disproportionately reached
individuals who are relatively familiar with social media, online
travel information, and digital destination content. This issue is
particularly relevant because Social Media Marketing Eﬀorts and
E-Reputation are central antecedent constructs in the proposed
model. Consequently, the observed relationships may be more
representative of digitally connected individuals familiar with
CPEC destinations and should not be generalized uncritically
to tourists with limited social-media use, lower levels of digital
engagement, or diﬀerent travel-information search behaviors.
Future research could employ probability-based, stratiﬁed,
destination-intercept, or mixed-mode sampling approaches and
recruit participants with varying levels of digital engagement to
assess the robustness and broader generalizability of the ﬁndings.
Fourth, the study focused exclusively on destinations within
China’s CPEC region. While this provides valuable insights into
an emerging tourism context, the generalizability of the ﬁndings
to other geographical and cultural settings may be limited. Future
studies are encouraged to replicate the proposed model in other
Belt and Road Initiative destinations and emerging tourism
markets to validate and extend its applicability.
Fifth, the use of a quantitative PLS-SEM approach may not fully
capture the depth and richness of tourists’ experiential perceptions.
Future research could adopt mixed-method approaches, including
interviews, focus groups, or ethnographic studies, to gain deeper
insights into how tourists interpret authenticity, destination image,
and digital marketing communication. An additional limitation
concerns the sustainability framing of the study. Although the
CPEC tourism context is relevant to broader discussions of
sustainable destination development, the empirical model did
not directly measure environmental sustainability, community
wellbeing, cultural impacts, resident outcomes, carrying capacity,
resource use, or long-term economic resilience. Destination
Behavioral Intention should therefore not be interpreted as
a proxy for sustainable tourism development. Future research
could extend the present framework by incorporating explicit
multidimensional sustainability outcomes and examining whether
digitally stimulated visitor demand contributes to, conﬂicts with,
or is contingent upon environmental, social, cultural, community,
and economic sustainability conditions. Such research would
permit a more direct assessment of when destination marketing

/six.tnum Conclusion
This study examined the relationships among social media
marketing eﬀorts, e-reputation, perceived authentic destination
experience, destination image, and destination behavioral
intention in CPEC destinations by integrating the Stimulus–
Organism–Response framework with Destination Image Theory.
The results show positive associations between each of the
three destination-related antecedents and destination image,
with perceived authentic destination experience exhibiting
the strongest association among the examined antecedents.
Destination image was also positively associated with destination
behavioral intention, and the statistically signiﬁcant indirect
associations were consistent with the theoretically proposed
mediating pathways. However, because the study employed a
cross-sectional research design, these relationships should not
be interpreted as establishing temporal precedence or causality.
Moreover, Destination Behavioral Intention is a self-reported
prospective-intention construct; therefore, the ﬁndings do not
establish actual visitation, recommendation behavior, realized
tourism demand, or subsequent behavioral change. Given the
online non-probability snowball sampling strategy, the ﬁndings
are most appropriately interpreted in relation to digitally
connected individuals familiar with CPEC destinations, and
broader generalization to less digitally engaged tourist populations
should be made cautiously. The study contributes to digital
destination-marketing research by showing how digital marketing
communication, socially generated online reputation, perceived
experiential authenticity, destination image, and behavioral
intention can be theoretically organized and examined within an
integrated S-O-R and Destination Image framework in an emerging
tourism context. In particular, the framework positions destination
image as the cognitive–aﬀective evaluative state associated with
heterogeneous digital, reputational, and experiential destination-
related cues and subsequent behavioral intention. At the same
time, the ﬁndings should not be interpreted as direct evidence of
sustainable tourism development. Destination Behavioral Intention
captures respondents’ prospective destination-related intentions
and preferences rather than environmental, social, cultural,
community, or long-term economic sustainability outcomes. The
results may therefore inform sustainability-oriented destination
planning by identifying factors associated with visitor perceptions
and interest, but whether such visitor demand contributes to
sustainable tourism ultimately depends on how it is governed
and balanced with destination carrying capacity, environmental
protection, cultural preservation, community participation and
beneﬁt distribution, and long-term development objectives.
