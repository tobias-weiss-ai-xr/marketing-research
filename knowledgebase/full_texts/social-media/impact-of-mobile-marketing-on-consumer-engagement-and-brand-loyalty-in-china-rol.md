brand loyalty in China's post

Consumer (KOC) influence over Key Opinion Leader (KOL) channels, and the maturation

2. Literature Review
2.1 Mobile Marketing
Mobile marketing is defined as a set of practices that enables organisations
to communicate and engage with their audience in
an interactive and relevant manner through and with any mobile device or network (MMA Global, 2009, as cited in Shankar &
Balasubramanian, 2009). Since this foundational definition was established, the mobi

for SMS

social commerce integrations, live
 -

 -
on features,
and AI
 -
driven
 personalisation
engines (Kannan & Li, 2017; Lamberton & Stephen, 2016). Global smartphone penetration reached approximately 85 percent
of the world's internet users in 2023 (GSMA Intelligence, 2023). This makes the mobile channel not merely one among many
touchpoints but the primary interface through which consumers interact with brands in most major markets.
China's mobile marketing environment is distinguished from global norms by several structural features. First, the super
 -
app
architecture of WeChat, Alipay, and Douyin means that consumers can move from brand content discovery to product research
to purcha
 se completion to post

friction that characterises

commerce (real

mainstream channel accounting for hundred

Third, the PIPL regulatory environment has constrained the third

2.2 Consumer Engagement
Consumer engagement is defined as a psychological state that occurs by virtue of interactive, co
 -
creative customer experiences
with a focal agent or object in service relationships (Brodie et al., 2011, p. 260). It is characterised by cognitive, emotio
 nal,
and
behavioural

Consumer engagement theory posits that engagement is distinct from satisfaction and loyalty in being interactive and relation
 al
in character:

behaviour or attitudinal evaluation (Hollebeek et al., 2021). The mobile channel enables a distinctive set of engagement
behaviours

describes (Brod

2.4 Customer Experience
Customer experience is defined as the internal and subjective response customers have to any direct or indirect contact with
 a
company across the full customer journey (Meyer & Schwager, 2007, as cited in Lemon & Verhoef, 2016). Lemon and Verhoef
(2016) di

use, and hedonic customer experience characterised
 by pleasure, excitement, and aesthetic satisfaction. Both dimensions
mediate the path from brand touchpoints to loyalty, but research suggests that hedonic customer experience produces stronger
and more durable affective brand bonds (Voorhees et al., 2022
 ; Pine & Gilmore, 1998). Mobile applications enable the delivery
of both dimensions: functional experience through seamless checkout flows, personalised recommendations, and loyalty
programme
 management; hedonic experience through gamified rewards, exclusive digital content, and curated aesthetic
environments that align with brand identity (Lemon & Verhoef, 2016; Kim et al., 2019).
2.5 Social Media Influence
Social media influence on consumer behaviour is theorised through Kelman's (1958) framework of social influence processes,
which identifies three mechanisms: compliance, meaning attitude or behaviour
change driven by the social rewards associated
with agreeing with a source; identification, meaning change driven by the desire to maintain a satisfying relationship with a
 n
admired source; and internalization, meaning change driven by congruence between
 the source's message and the consumer's
own values. In the context of digital social media, these three mechanisms manifest differently. Compliance corresponds to
behaviour
change driven by viral peer recommendation and trending social signals, where the motivation is social belonging
rather than product evaluation. Identification corresponds to change driven by aspiration to align with the identity of an
admired influencer
 or brand community. Internalization corresponds to change driven by genuine value alignment, where the
consumer adopts the brand as an expression of personal or cultural identity (Kelman, 1958; De Veirman et al., 2017; Lou &
Yuan, 2019).
2.6 Research Gap
Three significant gaps remain in the literature. First, the simultaneous examination of customer experience as a mediator and
social media influence as a moderator within a single integrated framework is underrepresented, particularly in non

Western
contex

 -
to
-

and the maturation of live commerce) have not been adequately integrated into existing theoretical frameworks, the majority
of which were
developed in pre
-
2020 conditions. Third, which mechanisms of social media influence produce the most durable
loyalty outcomes, and whether this varies by brand archetype, has not been addressed through comparative case analysis
grounded in publicly availab

3. Methodology
3.1 Theoretical/Conceptual Framework
The study is grounded in four theoretical frameworks that together constitute its conceptual architecture. Relationship
Marketing Theory, as developed by Morgan and Hunt (1994), provides the foundational premise that sustained consumer
engagement and brand
 loyalty are outcomes of trust
 -
based, commitment
 -
oriented relationships between brands and
consumers, rather than merely transactional exchanges. This framework grounds the expectation that mobile marketing
effectiveness depends not only on reach or freque
 ncy of exposure but on the quality of relational connection it facilitates. The
proposition derived from this framework is P1: Mobile marketing strategies that facilitate trust and commitment will produce
higher levels of consumer engagement and brand loya
 lty than strategies that
 prioritise reach or frequency alone.
Customer Experience Theory (Lemon & Verhoef, 2016; Voorhees et al., 2022) provides the mediating construct, distinguishing
between functional experience (defined by efficiency, reliability, and ease) and hedonic experience (defined by pleasure,
excitement,
and aesthetic satisfaction. The proposition derived is P2: Customer experience mediates the relationship between
mobile marketing and consumer engagement and brand loyalty outcomes. Social Influence Theory (Kelman, 1958; De Veirman
et al., 2017; Lou & Yuan, 2019) provides the moderating framework. The proposition derived is P3: Social media influence
moderates the relationship between mobile marketing and consumer outcomes, with strength and direction varying by the
mechanism of inf
 luence, whether compliance, identification, or internalization. Consumer Engagement Theory (Brodie et al.,
2011; Hollebeek et al., 2021) provides the construct through which the interactive and co
-
creative character of mobile marketing
relationships is
 theorised.

3.2 Research Design
The study adopts a constructivist ontology and interpretivist epistemology. A constructivist ontology holds that reality is s
 ocially
constructed and that phenomena such as consumer engagement and brand loyalty are not fixed entities measurable through
surv
ey instruments but rather processes that are shaped by context, practice, and the interpretive frameworks of consumers
and brands. An interpretivist epistemology holds that understanding these phenomena requires interpretation of meaning from
the perspecti
ve of those involved, rather than objective measurement of variables from an external, value
 -
neutral standpoint.
From these philosophical commitments, an inductive research logic follows: the study moves from empirical observations
(documented brand strate
 gies and consumer response patterns) toward theoretical refinement, rather than from hypotheses
toward statistical confirmation.
Within this philosophical framework, a qualitative multiple case study design is employed. This follows the methodology
established by Yin (2018). The multiple case study design is selected for its capacity to generate theory through replication
logic: whe
 n a theoretical proposition is confirmed across multiple cases that are diverse in brand archetype and strategic
approach, the confidence in the proposition's generalisability
increases beyond what any single case could support (Yin, 2018).
The replication logic employed here is literal replication (the expectation that similar results will be found across cases w
 ith
respect to core propositions) combined with theoretical repli
 cation (the expectation that moderating variables will produce
different results across cases in theoretically predictable ways).
3.3 Case Selection
Three cases are selected through a combination of purposive and theoretical sampling. The selection criteria require each cas

to be a brand that: (1) operates at scale in China's consumer market with publicly verifiable performance data; (2) has
implement

3.5 Data Analysis Procedure
Data analysis follows Braun and Clarke's (2006) reflexive thematic analysis approach, applied in two sequential phases. In th
 e
first phase, deductive coding is applied using the four theoretical frameworks as an a priori coding schema. Passages in the
 data
are coded against the five focal constructs (mobile marketing format types, consumer engagement manifestations, brand loyalty
indicators, customer experience dimensions, and social media influence mechanisms) and against the three propositions
developed f
rom the theoretical frameworks. In the second phase, inductive coding is applied within each deductive code
category to surface emergent themes not anticipated by the a priori schema. The trustworthiness of the analysis is establishe
 d
through four criteria
specified by Lincoln and Guba (1985): credibility, through sustained engagement with source documents
and multiple data source types per case; transferability, through thick description that provides sufficient contextual detai
 l for
readers to assess appl

confirmability, through reliance on publicly verifiable secondary data sources rather than researcher interpretations of
interview data.

The study's findings advance scholarly understanding in four specific respects. The first contribution is the replication and
contextualisation
