1. Int
roduction
The
 rapid advancement of digital technology has
significantly transformed consumer behavior and
business models worldwide. In Indonesia, this
transformation is reflected in internet penetration
reaching 80.66% in 2025 [ 1], supporting the growth
of the digital economy and strengthening e -
commerce as a national priority sector. Indonesia
also accounted for approximately 44% of Southeast
Asia’s e-commerce Gross Merchandise Value
(GMV) in 2024, making it the region’s largest e -
commerce market.
Amid this development, social commerce has
emerged as an interactive form of e-commerce that
integrates social interaction with transactional
activities through features such as live streaming,
interactive communication, and user -generated
content. This model creates a more engaging
shopping experience and is projected to grow
significantly faster than conventional e -commerce
[2].
Despite its rapid growth, social commerce
performance remains uneven across product
categories.

One example is KreasiOne, one of Indonesia’s
leading social commerce platforms, which remains
highly dependent on specific product cate gories. The
household products category, comprising furniture,
kitchenware, tools and hardware, home supplies,
home improvement, and textiles and soft furnishings,
records a relatively high Average Order Value
(AOV) but contributes only approximately 4.10%  of
the platform’s GMV [ 3]. This disparity suggests that
its relatively high product value has not translated
into comparable sales performance. One possible
explanation is consumers’ continued preference for
purchasing household products from physical stores,
where product attributes can be directly inspected
before purchase [ 4]. Furthermore, trust plays an
important role in reducing uncertainty and facilitating
purchase decisions in social commerce [5].
Although social commerce offers interactive
features that may reduce uncertainty and strengthen
consumer trust, the factors influencing purchase
intention for household products remain
underexplored, particularly in the Indonesian context.
Previous studies have also provided limited
integration of these factors within a comprehensive
behavioral framework and have rarely translated their
findings into actionable strategic insights [6].
Therefore, this study aims to identify the factors
influencing consumers’ purchase intention toward
household products on Kre asiOne using the
Stimulus–Organism–Response (S -O-R) framework.
Furthermore, Importance–Performance Map Analysis
(IPMA) is employed to identify strategic
improvement priorities and formulate
recommendations to enhance purchase intention,
platform competitiveness, and the performance of the
household products category.

2. Literature Review

This section reviews the theoretical concepts and
relevant literature underlying this study. The
discussion focuses on social commerce as the
research context and the Stimulus –Organism–
Response (S -O-R) framework as the theoretical
foundation for examining the factors influencing
consumers’ purchase intention , especially  toward
household products.

2.1. Social Commerce

Social commerce represents an evolution of
traditional e -commerce by integrating social media
features into online shopping environments [ 7]. It
enables consumers to actively participate in content
creation, product evaluation, and interaction with
other users, thereby enhancing engagement and trust.
Unlike conventional e -commerce, which
primarily focuses on transactional efficiency, social
commerce emphasizes multidirectional
communication and user experience through features
such as live streaming, reviews, and user -generated
content [5].
Previous studies have shown that social
commerce significantly improves consumer
engagement and facilitates more informed decision -
making processes. The presence of interactive
features and peer communication allows consumers
to reduce uncertainty and increase co nfidence in
online transactions. As a result, social commerce has
become a rapidly growing digital business model,
particularly in emerging markets.

2.2. The Stimulus-Organism-Response Framework

The Stimulus –Organism–Response (S -O-R)
framework, originally proposed by Mehrabian and
Russell, provides a theoretical foundation for
understanding consumer behavior in response to
environmental stimuli. According to this model,
external stimuli influence internal psychological
states, which subsequently drive behavioral
responses [8].
In digital commerce contexts, stimuli are often
represented by platform features and external
influences, while organism variables include
emotional and cognitive evaluations such as
perceived value and trust. The response i s typically
reflected in behavioral outcomes such as purchase
intention. The S-O -R framework has been widely
applied in online and social commerce research due
to its ability to explain the interaction between
technological features and consumer psychology.

3. Methodology Section

This study adopts a quantitative research
approach to examine the relationships among the
proposed variables within the S -O-R framework.
Data were collected through an online survey
distributed to individuals with experience using
social commerce platforms, resulting in a total of 413
valid responses, which exceeds the minimum sample
size requirement and provides sufficient statistical
power for the analysis.
The measurement instrument was developed
based on established scales from prior literature and
initially consisted of 23 indicators. Following expert
validation, two indicators were removed, resulting in
21 valid indicators used in the final analysis. All
constructs were measured using a five -point Likert
scale to capture respondents' perceptions.

Reliability and validity were confirmed through
pilot testing, with all constructs demonstrating
Cronbach's alpha values above the acceptable
threshold of 0.70 [9].
Data analysis was conducted using Partial Least
Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM ),
which is suitable for analyzing complex models and
exploratory research contexts [ 9]. The analysis was
carried out in two stages: evaluation of the
measurement model (outer model) and evaluation of
the structural model (inner model). The measurement
model was assessed based on indicator reliability,
internal consistency, and convergent validity, while
the structural model was evaluated using path
coefficients, significance levels, and explanatory
power.
In addition to PLS-SEM, this study employs
Importance–Performance Map Analysis (IPMA) [ 10]
to provide deeper managerial insights. While PLS -
SEM identifies the significance of relationships
between variables, IPMA helps determine which
factors are most important yet underperforming,
thereby enabling the for mulation of more targeted
and actionable strategic recommendations.

4. Results

This section presents the empirical findings of the
study based on the data collected from 413
respondents.
The analysis is conducted using Partial Least
Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM)
and Importance–Performance Map Analysis (IPMA)
to evaluate the proposed relationships and identify
priority areas for improvement.

4.1. PLS-SEM Analysis

The analysis using Partial Least Squares
Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM) was
conducted in two stages, namely the evaluation of the
measurement model (outer model) and the structural
model (inner model). The conceptual model to be
tested is illustrated in Figure 1, which is developed
based on the Stimulus –Organism–Response (S-O-R)
framework and comprises three main components.
The stim ulus variables include interactivity,
information quality, and subjective norm,
representing external factors derived from platform
features and social influence [ 11]. These stimuli
affect the organism variables, namely hedonic value,
utilitarian value, an d trust, which reflect consumers'
internal psychological and evaluative responses.
Ultimately, these internal states influence the
response variable, which is purchase intention,
indicating the consumer's likelihood of purchasing
household products through the social commerce
platform.

The results of the measurement model evaluation
show that all indicators meet the required thresholds

loading values exceed 0.708, confirming indicator
reliability. In addition, the Average Variance
Extracted (AVE) values for all constructs are above
0.50, indicating adequate convergent validity.
Furthermore, all constructs have  Cronbach's
Alpha and Composite Reliability values above 0.70,
demonstrating strong internal consistency. The

4.1. Importance-Performance Map Analysis (IPMA)

To complement the PLS-SEM results, this study
employs Importance–Performance Map Analysis
(IPMA) to identify indicators that warrant managerial
attention based on their relative importance and
performance. While PLS -SEM assesses the
relationships between constructs, IPMA provides
additional insight into the priority areas for
improvement, as presented in Figure 4.
The IPMA results identify seven indicators in
Quadrant I (Concentrate Here), indicating high
importance but below -average performance and
therefore representing the primary priorities for
improvement. These indicators are HV3 (β = 0.159),
IN3 (β = 0.152), IN2 (β = 0.151), TR1 (β = 0.133),
TR2 (β = 0.127), IQ3 (β = 0.114), and IQ1 (β =
0.113). The findings indicate that improvements
should focus on shopping experience, interactivity,
trust, and information quality.

5. Discussion

The findings of this study provide empirical
support for the application of the Stimulus –
Organism–Response (S -O-R) framework in the
context of social commerce. Nine of the ten proposed
hypotheses were supported, demonstrating that
information quality and inter activity, as external
stimuli, significantly shape consumers’ internal
evaluations in terms of hedonic value, utilitarian
value, and trust, which subsequently influence
purchase intention. In contrast, subjective norm did
not significantly affect trust or purchase intention,
suggesting that household product purchases are
more strongly driven by consumers’ personal
evaluations than by social influence.
Hedonic value emerged as the strongest predictor
of purchase intention, indicating that an enjoyable
and i mmersive shopping experience can be more
influential than functional considerations alone. This
finding suggests that despite the functional nature of
household products, consumers remain responsive to
experiential elements such as engaging content, live
streaming, and interactive shopping experiences.
Trust also demonstrated a strong positive effect on
purchase intention, highlighting the importance of
reducing uncertainty and establishing perceptions of
platform reliability and security. Utilitarian value ,
although comparatively weaker, remained a
significant predictor, indicating that consumers
continue to consider functional benefits when
evaluating household products.
Information quality was the strongest determinant
of trust, emphasizing the importance  of complete,
accurate, and clear product information in reducing
uncertainty during online purchases.
Interactivity also significantly contributed to trust
and had a stronger influence on hedonic value,
indicating that responsive and engaging interactions
are particularly effective in enhancing consumers’
emotional shopping experience. These findings
reinforce the complementary roles of informational
and interactive stimuli in shaping consumers’
cognitive and affective evaluations.
The IPMA results further complement the
structural model by identifying seven indicators in
the “Concentrate Here” quadrant, namely HV3, IN3,
IN2, TR1, TR2, IQ3, and IQ1. These indicators
represent high -importance attributes with below -
average performance and therefore constitut e the
primary areas for managerial improvement. The
findings provide the basis for eleven improvement
strategies focusing on personalization and shopping
experience, communication responsiveness, trust
enhancement, and information quality. Thus, the
integration of PLS-SEM and IPMA enables the study
to move beyond identifying significant relationships
toward determining specific areas that should receive
immediate managerial attention.

6. Conclusion

This study aimed to identify the factors
influencing consumers’ purchase intention toward
household products on the KreasiOne social
commerce platform and to formulate strategic
improvement priorities using the Stimulus –
Organism–Response (S -O-R) framework and
Importance–Performance Map Analysis ( IPMA).
Based on data from 413 respondents, the findings
demonstrate that hedonic value is the strongest
predictor of purchase intention, followed by trust and
utilitarian value. Information quality and interactivity
also play important roles by shaping consumers’
internal evaluations, with information quality being
the strongest determinant of trust and interactivity
having a particularly strong influence on hedonic
value. In contrast, subjective norm does not
significantly influence trust or purchase intention,
indicating that household product purchasing
decisions are primarily driven by individual
evaluation rather than social influence.
The IPMA results further identify seven
indicators as priority areas for improvement,
covering shopping experience, int eractivity, trust,
and product and shipping information. Based on
these priorities, eleven improvement strategies were
formulated to address performance gaps in the most
important indicators.

Practically, this study suggests that social
commerce platforms should focus on: (1) enhancing
personalization and interactive features, including
recommendation algorithms and Augmented Reality
(AR), to create a more engaging shopping
experience; (2) impr oving communication
responsiveness through more effective message
management, notifications, and real -time interaction;
(3) strengthening consumer trust through greater
transparency, social proof, help resources, and store
performance information; and (4) enhancing
information quality through standardized product
descriptions, verified reviews, and comprehensive
shipment tracking. By aligning these priorities with
the attributes that have the strongest influence on
purchase intention, social commerce platfo rms can
improve user experience, strengthen consumer trust,
and unlock the growth potential of household product
categories.
Despite these contributions, this study is limited
to the KreasiOne platform and the Indonesian
market. Future research should exam ine additional
variables, other social commerce platforms, broader
geographic contexts, and specific consumer segments
to improve the generalizability of the findings.
