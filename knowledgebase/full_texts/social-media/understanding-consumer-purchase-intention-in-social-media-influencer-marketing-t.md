Introduction: The rapid growth of social media has transformed marketing
communica tion, particularly through the rise of influencer marketing. As
social media influencers increasingly shape consumer attitudes and
purchasing decisions, understanding the mechanisms underlying their
effectiv eness has become an important research concern. While prior
research has emphasized trust as a key driver of consumer responses, the
underlying relational mechanisms through which influencer credibility
translates into purchase intention remain insufficiently explored. This study
examines the impact of influencer credibility on consumer purchase
intention in the Moroccan context, focusing on the mediating roles of
parasocial relationships and trust.
Methods: Drawing on Source Credibility Theory and Parasocial Interaction
Theory, a conceptual model was developed and empirically tested using
Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM). Data were
collected through a structured survey administered to 304 active social media
users in Morocco. The proposed model assessed the direct relationships
among influencer credibility, parasocial relationships, trust, and purchase
intention, as well as the indirect and sequential mediation effects linking
these constructs.
Results: The results indicate that influencer credibility significantly enhances
both parasocial relationships and trust. In addition, parasocial relationships
positively influence trust, confirming the importance of emotional and
relational bonds between influencers and their followers. Furthermore, a
sequential mediation effect is identified, whereby parasocial relationships
strengthen trust, ultimately leading to increased purchase intention.

1 Introduction
Over the past decade, the rapid expansion of social media has
profoundly reshaped the way brands communicate with
consumers, giving rise to influencer marketing as a dominant
digital strategy. Social media influencers, active on platforms
such as Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube, have become central
actors in marketing communication due to their ability to
produce content perceived as authentic, interactive, and
engaging (Pradhan et al., 2023; van Driel and Dumitrica, 2021).
As a result, they are strongly associated with consumers’
attitudes, perceptions, and purchase intentions (Kostygina et al.,
2026; Lou and Yuan, 2019).
Despite this growing prominence, the mechanisms through
which influencers shape consumer behavior remain insufficiently
theorized. Existing research has largely emphasized the
persuasive power of influencer credibility, drawing on source
credibility theory (Hovland and Weiss, 1951), which posits that
message effectiveness depends on the perceived expertise,
trustworthiness, and attractiveness of the source. In the context
of social media, prior studies confirm that influencer credibility
plays a key role in shaping consumer attitudes and behavioral
intentions (Hwang and Zhang, 2018; Khan et al., 2020; Sokolova
and Kefi, 2020).
However, focusing solely on credibility provides an incomplete
understanding of influencer effectiveness. Influencer marketing is
inherently embedded in social and relational dynamics that extend
beyond cognitive evaluations (Hartmann and Goldhoorn, 2011).
Parasocial relationships defined as one-sided psychological
bonds between audiences and media figures, have emerged as a
critical mechanism in digital environments (Breves and Liebers,
2022; Yi, 2023). On social media platforms, these relationships
are intensified by continuous interaction, personal content
sharing, and perceived intimacy, leading consumers to develop
emotional connections with influencers (Bi and Zhang, 2022;
Farivar et al., 2021; Hwang and Zhang, 2018; Reinikainen et al.,
2020; Tukachinsky, 2010).
Recent work demonstrates that these enduring, friendship-like
bonds predict followers’ purchase intentions and constitute a
foundational antecedent of brand trust (Bi and Zhang, 2022;
Koay et al., 2024), underscoring the centrality of parasocial
dynamics in digital consumer behavior. Trust, reflecting the
extent to which individuals believe influencers act honestly and
in their best interest (Gefen et al., 2003); is widely recognized as
a key mechanism reducing perceived risk and facilitating
purchase decisions in online environments (Lou and Yuan,
2019; Song et al., 2017).
A further gap concerns the sequential process linking these
constructs. While some studies examine credibility and
parasocial relationships independently, few integrate both within
a unified sequential mediation framework. Sharkasi and
Rezakhah (2023) and Tripopsakul and Hoonsopon (2025)
represent notable exceptions, empirically validating models in
which credibility predicts parasocial relationships, which then
reinforce trust and ultimately purchase intention.
This gap is particularly pronounced in emerging markets such
as Morocco. Morocco represents a relevant and underexplored
context for influencer marketing research for several reasons.
According to the Digital, 2024 report (DataReportal, 2024),
Morocco had approximately 22.6 million social media users as
of January 2024, representing a social media penetration rate of
59.4% of the total population. Instagram ranks among the most
widely used platforms, particularly among users aged 18–34,
who constitute the primary target demographic for influencer
marketing campaigns (ANRT, 2023). The Moroccan influencer
marketing ecosystem has grown substantially in recent years,
with estimates suggesting that brand partnerships with local
influencers increased by over 40% between 2021 and 2023
(Statista, 2023). At the same time, Morocco’s e-commerce
market, while expanding rapidly, remains at an earlier stage of
development than comparable Western markets, with many
consumers still exercising caution regarding online purchases
(ANRT, 2023). In this context, interpersonal trust and relational
cues may play a particularly important role in consumer
decision-making, consistent with Morocco’s moderately
collectivist cultural orientation.
Although recent Moroccan studies have examined the direct
effects of influencer characteristics on purchase intention
(Azzouzi and Naoui, 2025; Bouaddi et al., 2025; Ghandour and
Touhami, 2023; Slimani and el Idrissi Tissafi, 2023), they tend
to overlook the relational mechanisms underlying these effects.
Moreover, most research on parasocial relationships has been
conducted in Western or East Asian contexts, leaving emerging
markets underexplored (Hwang and Zhang, 2018; Tripopsakul
and Hoonsopon, 2025).
In response to these limitations, this study examines the
impact of influencer credibility on consumer purchase intention
by integrating the mediating roles of parasocial relationships and
trust within a sequential framework. It makes three
contributions: (1) highlighting parasocial relationships as a

2 Literature review and hypothesis
development
The rapid expansion of social media has significantly
transformed communication between brands and consumers,
leading to the emergence of influencer marketing as a dominant
digital strategy (Kumar et al., 2025; Vrontis et al., 2021). Social
media influencers are now recognized as key actors capable of
shaping consumer attitudes, perceptions, and purchase behaviors
(Lou and Yuan, 2019; Martiningsih and Setyawan, 2022). Unlike
traditional advertising, influencer marketing relies on social
interactions, emotional proximity, and perceived authenticity,
which enhance its persuasive effectiveness (Hwang and Zhang,
2018; Sokolova and Kefi, 2020).
To understand the mechanisms underlying influencer
marketing, this study draws on two complementary theoretical
frameworks: source credibility theory and parasocial
interaction theory.
Source credibility theory posits that the effectiveness of a
persuasive message depends on the perceived credibility of its
source (Hovland and Weiss, 1951). Credibility is conceptualized
through three dimensions: expertise, trustworthiness, and
attractiveness (Ohanian, 1990). In influencer marketing,
consumers are more likely to rely on recommendations from
influencers they perceive as credible. Importantly,
trustworthiness as conceptualized within this framework refers
to the perceived honesty and integrity of the source as a
message attribute, evaluated at the point of exposure, whereas
trust represents a broader relational belief developed through
repeated interactions (Gefen et al., 2003; Lou and Yuan, 2019).
Thus, influencer credibility represents an overall source
evaluation based on characteristics such as expertise,
trustworthiness, and attractiveness, whereas trust reflects the
follower’s relational belief that the influencer is reliable, honest,
and acts in the follower’s interests. Although these constructs
are conceptually related, they represent different levels of
evaluation: credibility concerns the perceived quality of the
source, while trust reflects the follower’s confidence in the
relationship with that source.
In addition, this study mobilizes parasocial interaction theory,
which explains the formation of one-sided relationships between
audiences and media figures (Hartmann and Goldhoorn, 2011).
In social media environments, these relationships are
strengthened through continuous interaction, self disclosure, and
perceived intimacy between influencers and their followers
(Hwang and Zhang, 2018; Tukachinsky, 2010). As a result,
consumers may develop a sense of familiarity and emotional
attachment, which enhances engagement and receptivity to
influencer content (Yi, 2023).
Recent empirical research has examined how these
mechanisms translate into consumer responses to online
promotional signals. Bi and Zhang (2022) demonstrate that
parasocial relationships mediate the effect of credibility on
purchase intentions, while Reinikainen et al. (2020) show that
audience comments moderate this process by reinforcing
parasocial bonds. Koay et al. (2024) validate a sequential
mediation model in which self-influencer congruence predicts
parasocial relationships, which then predict credibility and
purchase intentions. Tripopsakul and Hoonsopon (2025)
confirm that credibility and parasocial relationships jointly
predict purchase intentions through both emotional and
cognitive pathways, while Masuda et al. (2022) identify
characterizations as key mediating mechanisms. More recent
evidence also shows that online promotional cues can influence
purchase intentions through intermediate psychological
mechanisms such as mental simulation and perceived value
(Laghari et al., 2026). These studies collectively suggest that the
pathway from credibility to purchase intention is mediated by
relational and psychological processes, rather than being
purely direct.
Trust plays a central role in this framework. As a relational
construct, trust reflects the consumer’s accumulated belief that
an influencer acts consistently, honestly, and in their interest
across repeated interactions (Gefen et al., 2003). This is
conceptually distinct from source trustworthiness, which is an
evaluative attribute of a single message or source. In digital
environments characterized by uncertainty, trust reduces
perceived risk and facilitates decision-making (Yasin et al.,
2019). Prior research consistently shows that influencer
credibility predicts trust, which in turn predicts purchase
intention (Lou and Yuan, 2019; Sokolova and Kefi, 2020).
Parasocial relationships further reinforce trust by creating
perceived proximity and authenticity (Yi, 2023), suggesting that
consumer responses to influencer marketing are driven by both
cognitive and emotional mechanisms operating sequentially.
2.1 Influencer credibility
Influencer credibility refers to consumers’ perceptions of an
influencer’s reliability, competence, and honesty as a source of
information and recommendations (Kalajdzini, 2024). Grounded
in source credibility theory (Hovland and Weiss, 1951), it is
operationalized through three dimensions: expertise,
trustworthiness as a source attribute, and attractiveness
(Ohanian, 1990). These dimensions determine how consumers
evaluate influencer-generated content (Julianti et al., 2025;
Nguyen et al., 2022).
Recent empirical evidence further supports the relevance of
influencer credibility to consumer responses. Sun et al. (2025)
found that social media celebrity credibility, conceptualized
through attractiveness, trustworthiness, and expertise, positively
influences followers’ online purchase intentions, with follower
loyalty serving as a mediator. While their study highlights
follower loyalty as a relational mechanism, the present research
focuses on parasocial relationships and trust and examines their
sequential role in linking influencer credibility to purchase
intention.
Empirical studies consistently show that influencer credibility
is positively associated with purchase intention (Martiningsih and
Setyawan, 2022; Sokolova and Kefi, 2020). However, this
relationship is not purely direct (Domguia and Evina, 2025;
Ghandour and Touhami, 2023). Credible influencers are more

2.2 Parasocial relationship
Parasocial relationships refer to one-sided emotional bonds that
consumers develop with media figures, including social media
influencers (Hartmann and Goldhoorn, 2011). These relationships
are particularly relevant in digital environments, where influencers
share personal content and interact frequently with their followers.
This continuous interaction creates a sense of intimacy and
familiarity, reinforcing the perceived closeness between influencers
and their audience (Hwang and Zhang, 2018; Tukachinsky, 2010).
As a result, consumers may feel emotionally connected to
influencers, even in the absence of real interaction.
Previous studies consistently demonstrate that parasocial
relationships constitute a key relational mechanism shaping
consumer responses to influencer marketing (Damaj and Nofal,
2026; Gong et al., 2024; Huang and Gong, 2024). According to
Parasocial Interaction Theory, repeated exposure to influencer-
generated content fosters emotional attachment, familiarity, and
perceived intimacy, making consumers more receptive to
influencers’ opinions and recommendations. Consequently,
individuals who develop strong parasocial relationships are more
likely to engage with influencer content, adopt product
recommendations, and form favorable purchase intentions
(Sokolova and Kefi, 2020). Beyond their direct effect on
purchase intention, parasocial relationships also facilitate the
development of trust. Consumers who perceive influencers as
psychologically close tend to evaluate their recommendations as
more authentic and sincere, thereby strengthening interpersonal
trust and increasing purchase likelihood (Masuda et al., 2022;
Yi, 2023). Accordingly, the following hypotheses are proposed:
- H4. Parasocial relationships are positively associated with
purchase intention.
- H5. Parasocial relationships are positively associated
with trust.
2.3 Trust in influencers
Trust represents a fundamental mechanism explaining
consumer responses in influencer marketing. It refers to
consumers’ willingness to rely on an influencer’s
recommendations based on perceived honesty, benevolence, and
competence (Gefen et al., 2003). Unlike source trustworthiness,
which reflects consumers’ evaluation of an influencer’s reliability
and integrity, trust represents a broader relational belief that
develops through repeated exposure, interactions, and
accumulated experiences with the influencer.
In influencer marketing contexts, trust is closely associated with
influencer credibility and parasocial relationships. Influencers
perceived as authentic, transparent, and competent are more likely
to establish trusting relationships with their followers (Lou and
Yuan, 2019). Moreover, emotional bonds developed through
parasocial interactions can reinforce consumers’ confidence in
influencers’ recommendations. Previous empirical studies confirm
that trust significantly influences purchase intention by reducing
perceived risk and facilitating consumers’ transition from
evaluation to behavioral intention (Li et al., 2025; Yasin et al.,
2019). Therefore, trust acts as an essential mechanism through
which influencer-related characteristics translate into consumer
purchase responses. Thus, the following hypothesis is proposed:
- H6. Trust is positively associated with purchase intention.
2.4 Mediating role of parasocial relationship
and trust
Understanding influencer effectiveness requires examining the
mechanisms through which influence occurs, as the relationship
between influencer credibility and consumer behavior cannot be
reduced to direct effects alone (Azzouzi and Naoui, 2025).
Among the mediating variables that explain how and why these
effects occur, parasocial relationships and trust occupy a central
place in explanatory models of consumer behavior (Hwang and
Zhang, 2018; Sokolova and Kefi, 2020).
Parasocial relationships constitute an intermediate
psychological mechanism through which consumers develop
emotional bonds and perceived closeness with influencers
(Hwang and Zhang, 2018). Influencers perceived as credible are
more likely to foster stronger parasocial relationships, which
increase consumers’ receptiveness to influencer recommendations
and consequently strengthen purchase intention (Bi and Zhang,
2022; Sokolova and Kefi, 2020; Yi, 2023). Accordingly, parasocial
relationships represent an important affective pathway linking
influencer credibility to consumers’ behavioral intentions.
Trust represents a complementary relational mechanism that
reflects consumers’ willingness to rely on an influencer’s
recommendations by reducing uncertainty and perceived risk
(Azzouzi and Naoui, 2025; Lou and Yuan, 2019). While
influencer credibility directly strengthens consumers’ trust by
enhancing perceptions of expertise, honesty, and reliability,
parasocial relationships further reinforce trust through the
development of emotional closeness, familiarity, and perceived
authenticity (Masuda et al., 2022; Yi, 2023). Consequently, trust
may mediate the relationship between influencer credibility and
purchase intention both directly and through the relational
influence of parasocial relationships.
Based on the above, the following hypotheses are proposed:
- H7. Parasocial relationships mediate the positive relationship
between influencer credibility and purchase intention.

- H8. Trust mediates the positive relationship between

Beyond these individual mediation effects, the present study
further examines whether parasocial relationships and trust
operate as sequential mediators. Drawing on Sharkasi and
Rezakhah (2023) and Tripopsakul and Hoonsopon (2025),
influencer credibility is expected to initiate a sequential
psychological process in which credibility strengthens parasocial
relationships, these relationships further reinforce trust, and
trust ultimately enhances purchase intention. This sequence
reflects the progression from an initial cognitive evaluation of
the influencer’s credibility to emotional attachment, relational
confidence, and finally behavioral intention. Accordingly:
- H9. Parasocial relationships and trust sequentially mediate
the positive relationship between influencer credibility and
purchase intention (IC → PSR → TR → PI).
Building on the theoretical foundations and hypotheses developed
above, this study proposes a conceptual model explaining how
influencer credibility translates into consumers’ purchase
intention. Grounded in source credibility theory and parasocial
interaction theory, the model integrates both direct and indirect
relationships among the study constructs. Specifically, influencer
credibility is expected to directly influence purchase intention
while also fostering parasocial relationships and trust. In
addition, parasocial relationships are expected to strengthen
trust, giving rise to both simple and sequential mediation
mechanisms. Consequently, the proposed framework captures
the cognitive, emotional, and relational processes through which
influencer credibility shapes consumers’ purchase intentions.

3 Materials and methods
This study adopts a quantitative cross-sectional research
design to examine the relationships among influencer credibility,
parasocial relationships, trust, and purchase intention. The
proposed conceptual model was tested using Partial Least
Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) with
SmartPLS 4.0.
3.1 Measurement of constructs and
questionnaire development
All constructs were measured using validated scales adapted
from prior literature to fit the context of social media influencer
marketing in Morocco. Following previous influencer marketing
studies, representative items were selected from the original
scales while preserving their conceptual meaning and ensuring
an appropriate questionnaire length. All items were measured
using a five-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (strongly disagree)
to 5 (strongly agree).
Influencer credibility (IC) was measured using four items
adapted from Ohanian (1990). The selected items represent the
three dimensions of source credibility; expertise, trustworthiness
as a source attribute, and attractiveness; while being adapted to
the context of social media influencer marketing. This
operationalization has been widely adopted in previous
influencer marketing studies (Lou and Yuan, 2019; Martiningsih
and Setyawan, 2022; Sokolova and Kefi, 2020). Parasocial
relationships (PSR) were measured using items adapted from
Tukachinsky (2010), which assess perceived closeness and
emotional attachment between audiences and media figures.
This scale has been widely applied in research on social media
influencers (Hwang and Zhang, 2018). Trust (TR) was initially
measured using five items adapted from Gefen et al. (2003),
capturing consumers’ confidence in and willingness to rely on
influencers’ recommendations. During the assessment of the
measurement model, item TR3 “I believe that the influencers
I follow act in the best interests of their followers” was removed
because its standardized loading (0.594) was below the
recommended threshold of 0.70 (Hair et al., 2019). Following
the recommendations of Hair et al. (2019), indicators with low

3.2 Sample and data collection
The target population consisted of Moroccan social media
users who follow at least one social media influencer. A non-
probability convenience sampling technique was employed, as
no comprehensive sampling frame for this population was
available, a procedure commonly adopted in digital marketing
research (Hair et al., 2019).
Data were collected through an online questionnaire
distributed via Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp, and a QR code
that enabled direct smartphone access. A total of 385
questionnaire responses were obtained. After excluding 44
incomplete responses and 37 responses from individuals who
indicated that they did not follow any social media influencer,
304 valid responses were retained for analysis. This corresponds
to 79.0% of the initial responses obtained (304/385). No missing
data were identified among the retained responses.
To ensure respondents’ eligibility, the questionnaire began
with a screening question asking whether participants followed
at least one social media influencer and were able to complete
the questionnaire in French. Only respondents answering
positively were allowed to complete the survey. Participation was
voluntary, anonymous, and without financial compensation.
Data collection was conducted over a six-week period, from 15
February to 2 April 2026.
Approximately 25.3% of respondents reported using social
media “rarely”. These participants were nevertheless retained
because they indicated following at least one influencer and
being exposed to influencer-generated promotional content.
However, this characteristic is acknowledged as a limitation, as
lower exposure frequency may influence the intensity of
influencer effects.
3.3 Data analysis
Data were analyzed using PLS-SEM with SmartPLS 4.0
(Ringle et al., 2022). PLS-SEM was selected based primarily
on the study’s explana tory-predictiv e objective rather than on
the presence of mediation alone. The proposed model
specifies several latent constructs and seeks to assess the
variance explained in parasocial relationships, trust, and
purchase intention while simultaneously evaluati ng the
measurement and structur al models. PLS-SEM is appropriate
in this context because it estima tes the relationships among
latent variables while emphasizing explained variance and
predictive relevance. The approa ch also permits the
simultaneous assessment of direct, specific indirect, and
sequential indirect effects within the proposed nomological
network. Accordingly, the choice of PLS-SEM was driven by
the analytical objective and model structure rather than by
mediat ion analysis per se.
We acknowledge that CB-SEM could have been considered for
confirmatory theory-testing purposes given the theoretical
grounding of the model and the adequate sample size (n = 304).
However, given the study’s primary focus on variance
explanation and predictive relevance across multiple endogenous
constructs, PLS-SEM was retained as the most appropriate
analytical approach (Sarstedt et al., 2021).
The analysis proceeded in two stages: (1) assessment of the
measurement model (outer loadings, Cronbach’s alpha,
composite reliability, AVE, HTMT, Fornell-Larcker criterion);
and (2) assessment of the structural model (path coefficients, R2,
f2, Q2, bootstrapping with 5,000 resamples for significance
testing and confidence intervals).
Common method bias (CMB) was addressed through both
procedural and statistical measures. Procedurally, questions were
presented in randomized blocks, respondent anonymity was
guaranteed, and neutral wording was used throughout the
instrument to minimize social desirability bias (Podsakoff et al.,
2003). Statistically, Harman’s single-factor test was conducted by
entering all construct items into a single unrotated exploratory
factor solution; the first factor explained 31.2% of total variance,
well below the 50% threshold (Podsakoff et al., 2003), suggesting
that no single factor dominates the variance structure and that
CMB is unlikely to represent a major concern. As a
supplementary diagnostic, the full collinearity assessment
recommended by Kock (2015) was performed by treating all
constructs simultaneously as both predictors and criteria; all VIF
values obtained were below the threshold of 3.3 (IC = 1.82;
PSR = 1.47; TR = 2.31; PI = 1.36), confirming the absence of
pathological collinearity that could indicate the presence of
common method bias (Kock, 2015). Despite these measures,
CMB cannot be entirely excluded in cross-sectional single-
source designs, and this is acknowledged as a limitation of
the study.

Predictive relevance (Q2) was calculated using the blindfolding

the predictor is included. Mediation analysis followed the
procedure recommended by Sarstedt et al. (2021), based on
bias-corrected and accelerated bootstrapped confidence intervals
(BCa, 95%) with 5,000 resamples. Mediation is confirmed when
the bootstrapped confidence interval for the indirect effect does
not include zero.
Given the cross-sectional design of this study, all estimated
path coefficients should be interpreted as predictive associations
rather than causal relationships. Establishing causal
directionality would require longitudinal or experimental designs
(Rindfleisch et al., 2008).

Indirect effects were examined to assess the mediating roles of
parasocial relationships and trust, as well as the sequential

The indirect effect of IC on TR through PSR is significant
[β = 0.084; t = 3.163; p = .002; 95% CI (0.032; 0.137)], confirming
that parasocial relationships mediate the credibility–trust
relationship. The indirect effect of IC on PI through TR is
significant [β = 0.195; t = 3.215; p = .001; 95% CI (0.076; 0.316)],

existence of a sequential psychological process in which
influencer credibility strengthens parasocial relationships, which
in turn reinforce trust, ultimately enhancing purchase intention.
Additionally, PSR exerts a significant indirect effect on PI
through TR [β = 0.045; t = 2.341; p = .019; 95% CI (0.008; 0.083)].
Since the direct effect of IC on PI remains significant after
introducing the mediators (β = 0.183; p = .027), the model
exhibits partial mediation: influencer credibility predicts
purchase intention both directly and indirectly through
parasocial relationships and trust. Following Zhao et al. (2010),
this pattern is consistent with complementary partial mediation,
as both the direct and indirect effects are positive and significant.
4.3.3 Coefficient of determination (R2)
As shown in Figure 2, the explanatory power of the structural
model was assessed using the coefficient of determination (R2),
following the recommendations of Hair et al. (2019). The R2
value represents the proportion of variance in endogenous
constructs explained by their predictor variables. The results
indicate that influencer credibility explains 27.2% of the variance
in parasocial relationships (R2 = 0.272), reflecting moderate
explanatory power. Furthermore, influencer credibility and
parasocial relationships explain 63.3% of the variance in trust
(R2 = 0.633), indicating substantial explanatory power. However,
this result should be interpreted with caution given the limited
discriminant validity observed between influencer credibility and
trust in the HTMT analysis. Finally, influencer credibility,
parasocial relationships, and trust explain 37.1% of the variance
in purchase intention (R2 = 0.371), reflecting moderate
explanatory power. Overall, these findings suggest that the
proposed model provides satisfactory explanatory power while
supporting the relevance of the hypothesized relationships.

5 Discussion
This study examined the associations among influencer
credibility, parasocial relationships, trust, and purchase intention
among Moroccan social media users. The results largely support
the proposed hypotheses and provide several theoretical and
empirical contributions to the influencer marketing literature.
Given the cross-sectional design, all findings are interpreted as
associations and predictive relationships rather than causal
claims (Rindfleisch et al., 2008).
Influencer credibility emerges as the foundational driver of the
influence process, operating simultaneously through direct and
relational pathways. The direct association between credibility
and purchase intention, while significant (β = 0.183), is relatively
modest compared to the total indirect effect (β = 0.347,
comprising IC→PSR→PI: 0.129; IC→TR→PI: 0.195; and
IC→PSR→TR→PI: 0.023). This pattern suggests that credibility
signals alone may be insufficient to predict behavioral
intentions, and that their effectiveness is predominantly
channeled through relational and psychological mechanisms.
This finding is consistent with source credibility theory
(Hovland and Weiss, 1951), which posits that the persuasive
power of a source depends not only on its perceived attributes
but also on the relational context in which communication
occurs. It also aligns with prior Moroccan studies (Azzouzi and
Naoui, 2025; Ghandour and Touhami, 2023) and with the
broader international literature (Martiningsih and Setyawan,
2022; Sokolova and Kefi, 2020) while extending them by
demonstrating that the credibility–intention link is substantially
mediated by relational constructs.
The strong association between credibility and parasocial
relationships (β = 0.521) confirms that perceived authenticity,
competence, and honesty enhance the formation of emotional
bonds with influencers (Hwang and Zhang, 2018; Yi, 2023).
When consumers perceive an influencer as credible, they are
more likely to interpret personal disclosures and engagement
behaviors as genuine, thereby fostering a sense of closeness and
emotional attachment consistent with parasocial interaction
theory (Hartmann and Goldhoorn, 2011). In the Moroccan

The strong association between influencer credibility and trust
(β = 0.700) should therefore be interpreted cautiously. The large
effect size (f2 = 0.971) indicates substantial explanatory power,
but it may also partly reflect the conceptual proximity between
the two constructs, as suggested by the HTMT value of 0.933.
Thus, the strength of the IC → TR relationship should not be
interpreted solely as evidence of a strong substantive effect.
Rather, it may indicate that respondents in the present context
perceive credibility and trust as closely related evaluations of
the influencer.
As discussed in the discriminant validity assessment above,
source trustworthiness; operationalized within Ohanian’s (1990)
credibility scale; and trust as a relational disposition (Gefen
et al., 2003) are theoretically distinct but empirically proximate
in the current sample. The large coefficient likely captures both
genuine predictive power and partial construct overlap. Despite
this caveat, the finding confirms that consumers who perceive
influencers as credible develop stronger relational trust
dispositions, consistent with the theoretical logic that credibility
evaluations update and reinforce trust beliefs over repeated
interactions (Gefen et al., 2003; Lou and Yuan, 2019). Future
research should employ more clearly differentiated
operationalizations of these constructs; for instance, measuring
source trustworthiness through message-level evaluations and
trust through behavioral intention-based items reflecting reliance
and vulnerability; to more cleanly separate their
empirical boundaries.
Beyond credibility, parasocial relationships demonstrate
independent predictive value, being positively associated with
both purchase intention (β = 0.247) and trust (β = 0.161). The
association between parasocial relationships and purchase
intention aligns with prior research demonstrating that
emotional bonds with influencers enhance receptivity to their
recommendations (Gong et al., 2024; Sokolova and Kefi, 2020).
When consumers develop a sense of closeness with an
influencer; perceiving them as a trusted friend rather than a
distant celebrity; they are more likely to interpret
recommendations as personally relevant and sincere. This result
confirms that purchase intention in influencer marketing is not
solely driven by cognitive evaluations of credibility, but also by
affective and relational mechanisms operating independently,
underscoring the importance of integrating both cognitive and
affective pathways in models of influencer effectiveness. The
association between parasocial relationships and trust is
particularly theoretically significant: it confirms that emotional
closeness contributes to building trust beyond what credibility
alone can explain (Masuda et al., 2022; Yi, 2023). When
consumers feel emotionally connected to an influencer, they
tend to attribute benevolent intentions to that influencer and are
less likely to perceive their recommendations as purely
commercial, thereby reinforcing their willingness to rely on
influencer recommendations. This finding enriches the existing
literature by identifying parasocial relationships as a relational
antecedent of trust; a pathway that had been undertheorized in
prior work; and positions emotional attachment as a bridge
between cognitive credibility evaluations and dispositional
trust formation.
Trust, in turn, is positively associated with purchase intention
(β = 0.278), confirming its role as a key mechanism reducing
perceived risk in online consumer decision-making (Li et al.,
2025; Yasin et al., 2019). The fact that trust predicts purchase
intention even after controlling for the direct effects of
credibility and parasocial relationships demonstrates its
incremental explanatory value as a distinct relational construct,
and supports the theoretical decision to include it as a mediator
rather than treating it as redundant with credibility. The specific
implications of this finding for the Moroccan context are
discussed below.
The mediation analysis provides the most theoretically
significant findings of this study. Parasocial relationships
mediate the credibility–purchase intention relationship
[β = 0.129; CI (0.065; 0.193)], and trust mediates the same
relationship [β = 0.195; CI (0.076; 0.316)]. Most importantly, the
sequential mediation pathway IC → PSR → TR → PI is
significant [β = 0.023; CI (0.003; 0.046)], confirming that the
pathway from credibility to purchase intention follows a logical
multi-stage progression: credibility evaluations first foster
emotional bonds, which then reinforce relational trust, which
ultimately predicts behavioral intentions. This sequence reflects
a progression from cognitive appraisal to affective engagement
to dispositional trust to behavioral intent, consistent with recent
empirical findings from Sharkasi and Rezakhah (2023) and
Tripopsakul and Hoonsopon (2025). The partial nature of
mediation; with the direct IC → PI path remaining significant;
further suggests that credibility operates through both direct and
indirect channels. Following Zhao et al. (2010), this pattern is
consistent with complementary partial mediation: consumers
with high credibility evaluations may directly translate these
assessments into purchase intentions through a peripheral
processing route, while simultaneously developing parasocial
bonds and trust through a more elaborated relational route. This
dual-process interpretation adds nuance to the understanding of
influencer persuasion mechanisms and represents a novel
theoretical contribution to the sequential mediation literature in
the influencer marketing domain.
These findings carry specific implications for the Moroccan
context. Morocco’s moderately collectivist cultural orientation
suggests that interpersonal bonds, perceived relational closeness,
and community-based social influence play a more prominent
role in consumer decision-making than in more individualist
contexts (De Mooij and Hofstede, 2011). In such cultural
environments, consumers may be particularly attentive to the
relational quality of influencer interactions, perceiving
influencers who share personal content and engage authentically
with their audiences as embedded within a quasi-social network
rather than as commercial intermediaries. This cultural dynamic

may amplify the role of parasocial relationships as a mediating
mechanism, as credible influencers who successfully cultivate
parasocial bonds may be perceived as trusted community

The finding that trust predicts purchase intention (β = 0.278)
is also particularly relevant in the Moroccan digital commerce
context. According to the ANRT (2023), a significant proportion

consumers with the relational confidence necessary to transition
from information search to purchase intention. This
interpretation is consistent with trust transfer theory (Stewart,
2003), which posits that trust can migrate from a trusted person
to a less familiar commercial context.
6 Theoretical implications
This study makes several theoretical contributions to the
influencer marketing and consumer behavior literature.
First, by integrating source credibility theory and parasocial
interaction theory within a unified sequential framework, the
study provides a more comprehensive account of how
influencers shape consumer purchase intentions than models
focusing on a single mechanism. The demonstration that
credibility, parasocial relationships, and trust operate as
interconnected predictors; rather than independent drivers;
advances the theoretical understanding of influence processes in
digital environments and responds to calls for more integrative
models of influencer effectiveness (Lou and Yuan, 2019;
Sokolova and Kefi, 2020).
Second, the identification of parasocial relationships as a
relational antecedent of trust constitutes an original contribution
to the understanding of relational mechanisms underlying
influencer effectiveness. While prior research has established
both credibility trust and parasocial relationship purchase
intention linkages, the mechanism through which emotional
bonding reinforces relational trust dispositions had been
insufficiently theorized. This study provides empirical support
for a relational pathway in which emotional proximity enhances
trust formation beyond what credibility alone can explain,
positioning parasocial relationships as a bridge between
cognitive source evaluations and dispositional trust.
Third, the sequential mediation model (IC → PSR → TR →
PI) extends prior mediation models that examined credibility
and parasocial relationships as independent mediators (Bi and
Zhang, 2022; Sharkasi and Rezakhah, 2023) by proposing and
empirically testing their theorized sequential ordering. This
contributes to theory-building by clarifying that the influence
process involves a theorized progression from cognitive
evaluation to emotional engagement to dispositional trust to
behavioral intent, a specification that adds precision to existing
accounts of influencer persuasion.
Fourth, the study raises important theoretical questions about
the boundary between source credibility and trust as constructs.
The HTMT value of 0.933 and the large effect size for the IC →
TR path (f2 = 0.971) suggest that these constructs, as currently
operationalized, share substantial empirical variance. Future
theoretical work should more precisely delineate the conceptual
and operational boundaries between source trustworthiness as a
credibility dimension reflecting message-level evaluations and
relational trust as a behavioral disposition developed through
repeated interactions and develop measurement instruments that
better capture this distinction. This limitation simultaneously
points to a theoretical gap that future research should address.
Fifth, by providing empirical evidence from Morocco, the
study contributes to the growing body of work extending
influencer marketing research to emerging market contexts. The
findings suggest that the sequential credibility → parasocial
relationship → trust → purchase intention process is operative
in a culturally distinct, emerging digital market, with contextual
specificities including the role of collectivist cultural values in
amplifying parasocial bonds and trust-based decision-making
that distinguish it from Western and East Asian contexts and
warrant further cross-cultural investigation.
7 Managerial implications
The findings of this study offer several actionable implications
for brands and marketing practitioners operating in Morocco and
comparable emerging markets. It should be noted that, since this
study measures purchase intention rather than actual purchase
behavior, the following recommendations should be understood
in terms of their expected impact on consumers’ purchase
intentions rather than on confirmed purchasing outcomes.
Nevertheless, purchase intention is widely recognized in the
consumer behavior literature as a strong proximal predictor of
actual purchasing behavior, lending practical relevance to
these recommendations.
First, companies should prioritize influencer credibility as a
foundational selection criterion. The results confirm that
credibility encompassing expertise, perceived honesty, and
attractiveness is positively associated with purchase intention
both directly and indirectly through relational mechanisms.
Brands should therefore conduct rigorous credibility assessments
of potential influencer partners, beyond merely considering
follower counts or engagement metrics. Evaluation criteria
should include the influencer’s domain knowledge, consistency
of messaging, transparency in brand partnerships, and audience
perceptions of authenticity. Selecting credible influencers is not
only a prerequisite for direct persuasive impact but also a
necessary condition for the relational processes parasocial
bonding and trust that amplify this impact.
Second, brands should encourage and facilitate the
development of strong parasocial relationships between
influencers and their audiences. This requires moving beyond
transactional, campaign-specific collaborations and supporting
influencers in producing personal, culturally resonant, and
consistent content over time. Influencers who share authentic
aspects of their daily lives, engage genuinely with their followers,
and maintain consistent communication styles are more likely to
develop the emotional bonds associated with positive purchase
intentions and higher trust levels. Brands should evaluate
Nassim et al.                                                                                                                                                         10.3389/fcomm.2026.1911649
Frontiers in Communication 12 frontiersin.org

influencers not only on their reach but also on the quality and
depth of their audience relationships.
Third, trust should be treated as a strategic asset rather than an
assumed byproduct of credibility. The results show that trust is
associated with purchase intention and plays a mediating role
between both credibility and parasocial relationships on the one
hand, and purchase intentions on the other. Brands should
therefore encourage influencers to adopt transparent
communication practices clearly disclosing partnerships,
avoiding excessive promotional content, and providing honest
product assessments. These practices reinforce the perception of
sincerity that underpins relational trust and, ultimately, positive
purchase intentions. Overly scripted or commercially saturated
content may undermine both parasocial bonds and trust,
reducing the overall effectiveness of influencer campaigns.
Fourth, the sequential mediation finding carries a specific
managerial implication: the full impact of influencer credibility
on purchase intentions is realized through the sequential
development of parasocial bonds and trust. This suggests that
the influence of influencer marketing campaigns on consumer
purchase intentions may not be fully realized in the short term,
as parasocial bonds and trust develop progressively through
repeated exposure and interaction. Brands should therefore
prioritize long-term, sustained collaborations over one-off
sponsored posts, as these allow the relational mechanisms
identified in this study to unfold and consolidate over time.
Metrics evaluating influencer campaign performance should
accordingly incorporate relational indicators such as audience
sentiment, comment quality, and engagement consistency
alongside traditional reach and conversion metrics.
Fifth, in the Moroccan cultural context specifically, brands
should select influencers who demonstrate cultural proximity
and linguistic adaptability. Influencers who communicate in
Moroccan Arabic (Darija) or French, reference local cultural
norms and events, and engage with Moroccan consumer
concerns are likely to be more effective at generating positive
purchase intentions by fostering the parasocial bonds and trust
that are particularly salient in this collectivist cultural
environment. In a market where interpersonal trust and
relational closeness play a central role in purchase decision-
making, culturally embedded influencers may offer a distinct
advantage over globally recognized but culturally
distant personalities.
8 Limitations and future research
directions
Despite its contributions, this study has several limitations that
should inform the interpretation of findings and guide
future research.
First, the cross-sectional design does not allow for causal
inference. All relationships are interpreted as associations and
predictive effects rather than causal links (Rindfleisch et al.,
2008). Future research should adopt longitudinal designs to
examine how parasocial relationships and trust develop over
time through repeated exposure to influencer content, and
whether their progressive consolidation predicts sustained
changes in purchase intention. Experimental designs could also
be used to manipulate credibility cues and parasocial cues
independently, allowing for stronger causal conclusions about
their relative contributions.
Second, the empirical proximity between influencer credibility
and trust (HTMT = 0.933) represents an important measurement
limitation. Although the bootstrapped 95% confidence interval
[0.878; 0.988] does not include unity, suggesting that the
constructs remain empirically distinct, the magnitude of the
HTMT ratio indicates borderline discriminant validity and
warrants caution in interpreting the IC → TR path coefficient
and the associated effect size (f2 = 0.971). This empirical
proximity suggests that respondents may perceive influencer
credibility and trust as closely related concepts in the present
context. Future research should develop and validate
measurement instruments that more clearly distinguish source
trustworthiness as a credibility dimension reflecting message-
level evaluations from relational trust as a belief developed
through repeated interactions. Multi-method approaches
combining survey data with behavioral or experimental
measures could provide stronger evidence of discriminant
validity between these constructs and assess whether the
distinction remains robust across different samples and contexts.
Third, the convenience sampling approach and the inclusion
of respondents reporting infrequent social media use limit the
generalizability of findings to the broader Moroccan population
and to highly active influencer followers specifically. Future
research should employ stratified probability sampling to obtain
more representative samples and enable subgroup analyses by
age, gender, platform usage intensity, and influencer genre. This
would allow researchers to examine whether the sequential
mediation process operates differently across segments, for
instance whether younger, more active users exhibit stronger
parasocial bonds and higher trust levels than occasional users.
Fourth, the study was conducted in a single cultural and
national context, Morocco, which while representing an original
contribution limits the generalizability of findings to other
markets. The collectivist cultural orientation prevalent in
Morocco may amplify the role of parasocial relationships and
interpersonal trust in the influence process in ways that do not
generalize to individualist cultural contexts. Future cross-cultural
research should explicitly examine whether the sequential
credibility, parasocial relationship, trust and purchase intention
process is moderated by cultural dimensions such as
individualism-collectivism (De Mooij and Hofstede, 2011),
uncertainty avoidance, or power distance.
Fifth, all constructs were measured using self-reported survey
items collected at a single point in time from the same
respondents, which introduces the risk of common method bias.
Although both procedural and statistical measures were
implemented to mitigate this concern, including randomized
item ordering, guaranteed anonymity, Harman’s single-factor
test (31.2% of variance explained by the first factor), and a full
collinearity assessment (Kock, 2015) in which all construct VIF
values were below 3.3, the possibility of residual method
variance cannot be entirely excluded in cross-sectional single-
source designs. Future research should consider multi-source
data collection approaches, combining self-reported attitudinal
measures with platform-level behavioral data, to reduce reliance
on single-source perceptual measures.

Sixth, the study focused on influencer marketing as a general
phenomenon without distinguishing between influencer types,
platform characteristics, or product categories. Prior research
suggests that the effects of credibility and parasocial
relationships may vary across micro-influencers vs. macro-
influencers, across platforms such as Instagram, TikTok, and
YouTube, and across product categories such as beauty, fashion,
food, and technology. Future research should examine whether
the sequential mediation model holds equally across these
boundary conditions or whether specific moderating variables
such as influencer size, content format, or product involvement
attenuate or amplify the identified pathways.
