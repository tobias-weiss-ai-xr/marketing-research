Abstract. The rapid development of digital platforms has transformed new media marketing
from an auxiliary promotional channel into an increasingly integrated component of e -commerce.
Short-form video, live -streaming commerce, social commerce, influencer marketing, visual
storytelling, and data-driven personalization have expanded the ways in which firms attract, engage,
and convert customers. At the same time, content homogenization, platform dependence, trust and
authenticity risks, rising traffic and creative cost s, data-governance requirements, and shortages of
specialized digital talent constrain sustainable adoption.
 This study applies bibliometric science mapping to examine research on new media
marketing in e-commerce during 2015–2025. Records were retrieved from the Web of Science Core
Collection and Google Scholar using conceptually equivalent search logic adapted to each database's
technical capabilities. Extraction was completed in March 2025; therefore, records dated 2025 cover
January–March only, and the bibliometric analysis was carried out in May 2026. After screening, we
retained 1,116 eligible publications,  comprising 1,064 journal articles and 52 review articles from
286 journals. The dataset contains 3,075 unique authors, 13,957 cited references, and 4,768 author
keywords. VOSviewer was used for country and keyword network visualization, while CiteSpace
was used for temporal and cluster -oriented analysis. The findings reveal three interrelated strategic
dimensions: content innovation, consumer -behavior-driven marketing, and full -funnel business
strategy. The research trajectory moves from social-media advertising and electronic word of mouth
toward short -form video, live -streaming commerce, digital transformation, and AI -assisted
marketing. The study contributes an integrated knowledge map while identifying the principal
organizational and market barriers th at should be addressed in future research and managerial
practice.

streaming commerce; bibliometric analysis

1. Introduction
E-commerce has become increasingly
embedded in platform -based communication
environments in which marketing, social
interaction, content production, and
transactions are closely connected. Traditional
advertising and one -way promotion remain
relevant, but purchase intentions increasingly
form through social interaction,
recommendations, and user -generated content
on commercial platforms (Hajli, 2015).
New media marketing provides
interactive environments through which firms
can communicate product information,
stimulate electronic word of mouth, build
social presence, and facilitate purchasing
decisions (Alalwan et al., 2017).
Social commerce integrates social
influence, information exchange, and
commercial activity, shifting consumers from
passive recipients to active participants in
product discovery and evaluation (Busalim &
Hussin, 2016).
Live-streaming and r elated interactive
formats add real -time communication to this
environment and can strengthen engagement
and trust when sellers provide credible
information and responsive communication
(Wongkitrungrueng & Assarut, 2020).
Relational bonds in e -commerce liv e
streaming can further strengthen consumer
engagement by supporting affective
commitment and continuing interaction with
the marketplace or broadcaster (Hu &
Chaudhry, 2020).
The topic is timely because the research
landscape has changed substantially during the
last decade. Social -media advertising and
electronic word of mouth were prominent early
mechanisms, whereas short-form video, social
commerce, and live -streaming increasingly
connect content consumption directly with
product discovery and transacti on. More
recently, AI -assisted recommendations,
analytics, content generation, and campaign
automation have entered the research agenda.
Recent review evidence indicates that live -
streaming commerce has developed from a
technology-adoption topic toward a b roader
research area concerned with consumer
behavior, platform mechanisms, and
commercial outcomes (Luo et al., 2025).
Bibliometric analysis is appropriate for
such a rapidly expanding and fragmented field
because it combines publication -performance
indicators with science mapping, co -
occurrence analysis, collaboration analysis,
and citation -based techniques (Donthu et al.,
2021).
CiteSpace provides a framework for
detecting emerging trends, temporal research
fronts, and transient patterns in scientific
literature (Chen, 2006).
VOSviewer complements this
approach by supporting the construction and
visualization of bibliometric networks,
including co -occurrence and collaboration
maps (van Eck & Waltman, 2010).
The present study therefore examines
e-commerce new media marketing from an
integrated perspective. Instead of treating
media technology, consumer behavior, and
business strategy as isolated streams, it maps
their relationships and interprets how they
develop together. The study does not claim that
the three dimensions have never been
examined separately; rather, it contributes by
bringing them into a single bibliometric
framework and linking the resulting
knowledge structure to practical opportunities
and barriers for e-commerce enterprises.
The study a ddresses three research
questions:
RQ1. How is the literature on new
media applications in e -commerce
marketing structured?
RQ2. What major research trends
and emerging themes can be identified in
literature?
RQ3. What barriers and strategic
challenges are emphasized in research on
e-commerce new media marketing?
2. Literature Review
2.1 New media and social commerce
Literature establishes social interaction
as a central mechanism of contemporary e -
commerce. Empirical evidence shows that
social presence cre ated through platform
interaction contributes to trust, which in turn
drives purchase intention in social -commerce
settings (Lu et al., 2016).

Social-media marketing research
similarly emphasizes engagement, interaction,
electronic word of mouth, customer
relationship management, and brand
performance as interconnected research
themes (Alalwan et al., 2017). These
developments provide the conceptual
foundation for understanding new media not
simply as a set of communication technologies,
but as an environment in which commercial
and social functions converge.
2.2 Live -streaming, trust, and
consumer engagement
Live-streaming commerce adds real -
time interaction to the social -commerce
environment. Research shows that social
presence can contribute to t rust and purchase
intention in social -commerce settings (Lu et
al., 2016), and that the perceived
characteristics of a social -commerce
environment shape both consumer trust and its
behavioral consequences (Kim & Park, 2013).
Relational bonds can further st rengthen
engagement by increasing perceived
connection, affective commitment, and
responsiveness (Hu & Chaudhry, 2020). This
stream is directly relevant to RQ2 because it
marks a major shift in the field from channel -
oriented research to mechanism -oriented
explanations.
2.3 Visual storytelling and electronic
word of mouth
Visual storytelling extends new media
marketing beyond product display by
organizing commercial information into
narratives that can influence attention,
experience, brand perceptions, and  behavioral
intention. Systematic evidence shows that
visual narratives can facilitate narrative
transportation and engagement when visual
elements communicate coherent stories
(Nikulina et al., 2024).
Storytelling in online shops can
improve explicit and implicit user experience,
strengthen brand perceptions, and influence
behavioral intentions when commercial
information is organized into a coherent
narrative (Karampournioti & Wiedmann,
2022).
Electronic word of mouth provides
another consumer -generated i nformation
mechanism through which social -media
content can affect information adoption and
purchase intention, including in TikTok-based
commerce contexts (Indrawati et al., 2023).
These mechanisms connect content innovation
with consumer response and sug gest that the
value of short -form video and visual
communication depends not only on reach but
also on credibility, information quality,
narrative coherence, and the ability to support
consumer interpretation.
2.4 AI, data analytics, and the emerging
research frontier
The recent literature increasingly
connects marketing with artificial intelligence
and data -driven decision making. AI can
support recommendation, personalization,
segmentation, content production, and
performance analysis. However, the
commercial value of these applications
depends on how consumers interpret machine-
generated recommendations: experimental
evidence shows that consumers prefer
algorithmic recommendations for utilitarian
attributes but human recommendations for
hedonic ones (Longoni & Cian, 2022). It also
depends on how firms manage data and
privacy, and how human judgment is
integrated into automated processes. These
issues are treated below as part of the emerging
research frontier and are not assumed to imply
that AI is univers ally superior to human
decision making.
2.5 Research gap and analytical
contribution
Existing literature provides strong
evidence on individual mechanisms —social
commerce, live streaming, visual
communication, trust, and AI —but these
streams are often disc ussed within different
conceptual or methodological boundaries. The
present study addresses this fragmentation
analytically by mapping the field as a
connected system. Its intended contribution is
descriptive and integrative: to identify the
structure of t he knowledge base, trace major
thematic transitions, and synthesize the
opportunities and barriers that recur across
new-media formats. The study therefore
complements, rather than replaces, topic -
specific systematic reviews.

3.3 Eligibility and screening
The initial retrieval contained 21,876
records. Screening was conducted in stages,
summarized in Figure 2 and Table 2. First,
records were restricted to 2015 –2025, journal
articles or review articles, and English -

records were removed. Third, two authors
independently screened titles and abstracts for
substantive relevance to new media and e -

resolved through discussion. After screening
and deduplication, 1,116 publications
remained eligible.
The final sample size serves as a descriptive
basis for network analysis rather than a
universal minimum threshold. Rogers et al.

stability and interpretation  of bibliometric
indicators; we do not use their study to claim a
mandatory 1,000 -publication cutoff. The
present sample is instead justified by the stated
research design and by its sufficient number of
publications, authors, references, and
keywords for the reported descriptive and
network analyses.

3.4 Data cleaning and analytical procedures
After merging the two database exports, we
manually removed duplicate records.
Descriptive analysis covered publication
volume, document type, journal sources,
authorship, references, and author keywords.
VOSviewer (version 1.6.20) was used for
country col laboration and keyword co -
occurrence visualization. CiteSpace (version
6.2.R4, 64 -bit Advanced) was used for
temporal patterns and cluster -oriented
analysis, with the following settings: timespan
2015–2025, slice length 1 year, selection
criterion g-index with k = 15, LRF = 3.0, L/N
= 10, LBY = 5, e = 1.0, Pathfinder pruning, and
1.0% of nodes labelled. The resulting network
contained 199 nodes and 361 links, with
modularity Q = 0.6944 and weighted mean
silhouette S = 0.8622.
A key distinction is maintained between
retrieval terms and author keywords. Retrieval
terms determine whether a record enters the
search process, whereas author -keyword
frequencies describe only the terms explicitly
supplied as author keywords in the retained
records. Therefo re, the occurrence of the
keyword “social media” (85) does not imply
that only 85 records matched the broader
search strategy. Likewise, the country network
is reported as a subset because only 193
retained records contained usable country-field
information. These subset definitions are
stated explicitly to avoid treating them as the
full 1,116-record corpus.
3.5 Reproducibility and temporal coverage
The extraction date was March 2025 and the
bibliometric analysis was performed in May
2026. Consequently, th e 2025 observation is
incomplete and covers January –March only.
The reported annual growth indicator of 14.3%
is therefore retained solely as a descriptive
statistic and is not interpreted as a full -year
compound growth rate. Year -to-year
comparisons invol ving 2025 are treated
cautiously. The country analysis likewise
describes only the records with usable country
information and should not be generalized to
the full corpus.
Software selection is limited to the tools
documented as used in the analysis:
VOSviewer and CiteSpace. VOSviewer is
used for network visualization and co -
occurrence/collaboration mapping, while
CiteSpace is used for temporal analysis,
thematic clustering, and the betweenness -
centrality values reported for the country
network. No results  are attributed to
Bibliometrix or Gephi, because their use is not
documented in the analytical outputs supplied
for this study.
4. Results and Discussion
4.1 RQ1: Structure of literature
RQ1 asks how the literature on new media
applications in e -commerce marketing is
structured. The final dataset includes 1,116
eligible publications: 1,064 journal articles and
52 review articles from 286 journals. It
involves 3,075 unique authors, 13,957 cited
references, and 4,768 author keywords. The
average number of au thor appearances per

The strategic implication is that firms
should move from content quantity toward
content differentiation. Short -form video and
live-streaming lower the technical barrier to
content production while increasing
competition for attention. Differentiated
storytelling, credible product demonstrations,
customer evidence, and culturally appropriate
communication can therefore be more valuable
than generic exposure. Trust should be treated
as a strategic asset because trust transfer
between sellers and brands can influ ence
continuous purchase intentions in C2C social
commerce (Zhao et al., 2019).
Trust is also linked to information
seeking, social presence, familiarity, and
purchase intentions on social networking
platforms (Hajli et al., 2017).
Electronic word of mouth can influence
purchase intention through the quality,
credibility, usefulness, and adoption of
information exchanged on social media (Erkan
& Evans, 2016).
In live -streaming e -commerce,
multidimensional consumer value and
streamer popularity can also shap e purchase
intention, reinforcing the importance of
interaction and commercial context in
conversion-oriented marketing (Zhang et al.,
2024).
Firms should also adopt full -funnel
performance management. Reach and
impressions remain useful indicators of
awareness, but they should be connected to
engagement, consideration, conversion,
retention, and advocacy. A campaign that
generates traffic without sustainable customer
value may have limited strategic importance.
Conversely, smaller campaig ns that generate
stronger trust and customer lifetime value can
be strategically superior. This perspective links
new-media activity to business performance
rather than treating platform metrics as final
outcomes.

human-in-the-loop principle. Automated
systems can support repetitive and data -
intensive tasks, but human professionals
remain responsible for strategic positioning,

4.4 Integrated interpretation and
theoretical contribution
The evidence supports an integrated
interpretation in which media te chnology,
consumer behavior, and business strategy form
a circular system, represented in Figure 6.
Technological affordances influence content
production and distribution; content influences
attention, trust, and consumer behavior; and
consumer responses generate data that feed
back into strategic decisions. The resulting
ecosystem includes social platforms, creators,
live-commerce functions, recommendation
systems, payment services, customer -service
processes, and e -commerce stores. This
ecosystem perspec tive extends new -media
marketing beyond individual channels.
The study therefore contributes in three
ways. First, it provides a structured map of a
rapidly evolving research field. Second, it links
thematic evolution to managerial questions
about content differentiation, trust,
measurement, and digital capability. Third, it
highlights AI and data analytics as an emerging
frontier while emphasizing that technological
sophistication alone does not guarantee
commercial value. These contributions are
intentionally framed as integrative and
descriptive rather than as claims of exclusive
theoretical novelty.
5. Limitations and Future Research
Several limitations should be
acknowledged. First, the dataset is restricted to
English-language records retrieved from We b
of Science Core Collection and Google
Scholar, so relevant studies indexed elsewhere
or published in Chinese, Russian, Kazakh, and
other languages may be underrepresented.
Second, the two databases differ in search
syntax, coverage, and filtering capabilities. We
therefore applied conceptually equivalent
searches and merged the results, introducing
database-combination heterogeneity.
Third, the extraction date was March
2025, so the 2025 observation covers only
January–March. The reported annual growth
indicator is consequently descriptive and
should not be interpreted as a full-year growth
rate. Fourth, country-level analysis is based on
193 records with usable country -field
information, while the full bibliometric corpus
contains 1,116 publications. Coun try findings
therefore describe a subset and are not
generalized to the entire corpus.
6. Conclusion
This study examined the structure,
evolution, and strategic challenges of new
media marketing in e -commerce through
bibliometric science mapping. For RQ1, the
literature is structured around interconnected
themes including social media, e -commerce,
trust, consumer behavior, live -streaming
commerce, digital transformation, and
retailing. The reported country subset is led by
China, followed by the United Stat es and
England, while England and Scotland remain
separate network entries rather than being
combined without recalculation.
For RQ2, the research trajectory moves
from social -media advertising and electronic
word of mouth toward short-form video, visual
storytelling, live-streaming commerce, digital
transformation, and AI -assisted marketing.
The field has therefore shifted from channel
adoption toward mechanism -based
understanding of how content, interaction,
trust, consumer behavior, and data capabilities
jointly affect e-commerce processes.
For RQ3, the principal barriers are
content homogenization, platform
dependence, trust and authenticity risks, rising
traffic and creative costs, data/privacy and
governance requirements, and shortages of
specialized d igital talent. These challenges
imply that firms should not evaluate new -
media marketing solely through reach or
traffic. Sustainable capability requires
differentiated content, credible consumer
relationships, full -funnel measurement, data
governance, org anizational coordination, and
responsible human–AI collaboration.
Overall, the study provides an
integrated knowledge map of a rapidly
evolving research domain while clearly noting
incomplete 2025 coverage, subset -based
country analysis, and database heter ogeneity.
The results support the view that the future of
e-commerce new media marketing lies not in
