Abstract:  Increasing competition in the property industry has encouraged companies to
implement effective marketing strategies to enhance consumers' purchasing
decisions. This study aims to analyze the influence of social media, references,
and below-the-line marketing on purchasing decisions, with walk -in customer
serving as a mediating variable at Java Residence. This research employed a
quantitative approach using a survey method. Data were collected through
questionnaires distributed to 180 Java Residence consume rs selected using
purposive sampling. Data analysis was conducted using Structural Equation
Modeling–Partial Least Squares (SEM -PLS). The results indicate that social
media, references, and below-the-line marketing have a positive and significant
effect on walk-in customer. Furthermore, social media, references, below -the-
line marketing, and walk-in customer also have a positive and significant effect
on purchasing decisions. The mediation analysis reveals that walk -in customer
significantly mediates the effects of social media, references, and below-the-line
marketing on purchasing decisions. The findings also demonstrate that walk -in
customer is the most influential factor affecting purchasing decisions, while
among the marketing variables, references exer t the strongest direct effect on
purchasing decisions compared with social media and below-the-line marketing.
These findings suggest that enhancing the effectiveness of digital marketing
strategies, strengthening customer referral programs, optimizing below-the-line
promotional activities, and improving customers' on-site visiting experience can
serve as effective strategies for increasing purchasing decisions at Java
Residence.

1. Introduction
The increasing population density has led to a proportional rise in the need for housing
(Novianto & Widodo, 2024) . The depletion of residential land, especially in large cities and
their surrounding suburban areas, has caused property prices to become increasingly expensive.
However, owning a house remains a dream and a basic necessity for everyone, especially those
with families. The value of construction projects in 2024 totaled Rp30.1 trillion, with the
residential sector contributing a project value o f Rp 6.7 trillion (Pratama, 2024). The

Java Residence is a housing product developed to meet this specific market segment. Java
Residence consists of four clusters spread across the Sidoarjo area (Krian and Sukodono).
Clusters 1 and 2 were considered highly successful as they sold out in a re latively short time,
Cluster 1 sold 257 units in just six months, and Cluster 2 sold 200 units in eight months.
Following this success, Java Residence opened Cluster 3, which has sold 107 units with 98
units remaining, and is preparing to launch Clusters 4  and 5. With this ongoing development,
an effective marketing strategy is crucial to accelerate the sales of the remaining units in Cluster
3 and ensure the upcoming clusters achieve similar success.
To promote its housing, Java Residence utilizes several marketing strategies, primarily
focusing on social media, below -the-line (BTL) promotions, and references. Social media
platforms like TikTok, Instagram, Facebook, and YouTube are actively used due to their
accessibility, speed, interactivity, and longevit y. Among these, the Java Residence TikTok
account has the highest engagement, boasting 16.4K followers and over 319K likes, utilizing
attractive visual content and educational property videos. Below -the-line promotions are
executed through property exhibit ions and brochure distributions. Exhibitions allow the
company to present product samples, site plans, and housing designs directly to potential
buyers, providing a tangible experience. Furthermore, references or word -of-mouth
recommendations are highly ef fective, as they stem from personal experiences and are often
perceived as more trustworthy by potential buyers compared to traditional advertisements.
Despite these extensive marketing efforts, a significant business phenomenon was
observed. Internal data from February 2025 indicated that while Java Residence successfully
attracted 142 walk-in customers (visitors to the model house), only 12 people (8%) decided to
make a purchase, whereas 92% postponed or declined the purchase. This indicates that while
the marketing stimuli successfully generated initial interest and site visits, there is a gap in
converting these walk-in customers into actual buyers. The property purchasing decision is a
high-involvement process that includes problem recognition, information search, evaluation of
alternatives, and the final purchase decision (Kotler's Buyer's Black Box Theory).
Previous literature highlights the importance of these variables. Chopra & Gupta (2019)
and Angelyn & Kodrat (2021) demonstrated the significant im pact of social media marketing
on consumer behavior and purchasing decisions. Ahmad & Elahi (2023) confirmed the
effectiveness of brochure advertising (BTL) on sales, while Choi & Lee (2018) emphasized the
critical role of physical model home visits on property purchasing decisions. Building on these
studies, this research aims to comprehensively analyze the direct and indirect influences of
social media, references, and BTL marketing on purchasing decisions, with walk-in customers
serving as the mediating variable.

2. Research Method
This study employs a quantitative approach using a survey method. The population in this study
comprises all consumers who have purchased a Java Residence product and have conducted a
walk-in visit, having received information th rough social media, references, or BTL media.
Due to the exact population size being unknown, the sample size was determined using the
guideline for SEM-PLS analysis, which recommends 5 to 10 times the number of indicators.
With 18 indicators in this study, the minimum sample size is 180 respondents.
A purposive sampling technique was used to select the 180 respondents based on strict

one of the studied marketing channels (social media, references, BTL). Employees or internal
marketing staff of Java Residence were excluded from the sample.
Data was collected using an online questionnaire distributed via Google Forms, utilizing a
5-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (Strongly Disagree) to 5 (Strongly Agree). The research
variables and their operational definitions are as follows:
1. Social Media (X1):  Measured by ease of obtainin g information, completeness of
information, attractiveness of content, and interaction between managers and users.
2. References (X2):  Measured by recommendations from family, recommendations from
friends/relatives, and the level of trust in the received recommendations.
3. Below The Line (X3): Measured by the attractiveness of brochures, clarity of promotional
information, effectiveness of exhibitions, and effectiveness of personal selling.
4. Walk-in Customer (M): Measured by the interest to visit directly, seriousness in seeking
information during the visit, and desire to know more about the product post-visit.
5. Purchasing Decision (Y):  Measured by the confidence in choosing Java Residence,
product suitability with needs, the decision to buy a unit, and willingness to recommend
to others.
Data analysis was conducted using Structural Equation Modeling -Partial Least Squares
(SEM-PLS) via SmartPL S software. The evaluation included the Outer Model (convergent
validity, discriminant validity, and internal consistency reliability) and the Inner Model (R -
square, path coefficients, and hypothesis testing through bootstrapping).

3.2. Discussion
The effect of social media, references, and below the line on walk-in customers
The hypothesis testing demonstrated that Social Media (X1) has a significant and positive
effect on Walk-in Customer (M) with a high original sample coefficient of 0.431. This finding
aligns with the Integrated Marketing Communication (IMC) theory proposed by Kotler &
Keller (2016), which positions social media as a primary interface for connecting companies
with consumers. By delivering informative and visually appealing content—such as high -
resolution property photos, virtual tours, and updates on building progress —social media
successfully stimulates consumer curiosity. This curiosity acts as an initial stimulus that drives
potential buyers to verify the information by v isiting the property physically. This is
corroborated by Chopra & Gupta (2019), who state that compelling social media content
directly encourages consumers to explore products further, resulting in physical site visits.
Similarly, References (X2) showed a  significant positive impact on Walk -in Customer
(0.295). This result strongly supports the Reference Group Theory (Kotler & Keller, 2016),
highlighting the critical role of trust. Alfiansyah (2023) and Smruti Rekha Patro (2025) note
that recommendations f rom family, friends, and peers significantly lower the perceived
financial risk associated with property buying. Consumers generally consider personal
experiences from trusted individuals to be more objective than corporate advertising, thereby
strengthening their intent to visit the site.
Furthermore, Below The Line marketing (X3) significantly influences walk -in customer
rates (0.255). Belch & Belch (2021) assert that IMC strategies executed through direct events
and exhibitions enhance active consumer en gagement. Tangible promotional efforts, such as
well-designed brochures and direct explanations by sales personnel at exhibitions, provide
concrete information regarding pricing, building specifications, and facilities. This is consistent

The effect of social media, references, and below the line on purchasing decisions
The direct influences of the marketing mix on the final Purchasing Decision (Y) are all
significant. Social media holds a direct path coefficient of 0.146. Consistent with the Consumer
Decision-Making Pr ocess (Kotler & Keller, 2016), consistent digital exposure helps build
brand awareness and trust, gently nudging the consumer toward a purchase decision over time.
Below The Line marketing exhibits a stronger direct effect (0.250). In accordance with IMC
principles (Belch & Belch, 2021), detailed and physical interactions —such as comprehensive
brochures and transparent communication by sales agents—strengthen buyer conviction.
Most notably, among the external marketing stimuli, References exert the strongest direct
effect on purchasing decisions (0.263). Real estate is intrinsically a high-involvement and high-
risk product. According to Reference Group Theory (Kotler & Keller, 2016), when evaluating
such significant investments, buyers naturally place more w eight on the objective, word -of-
mouth recommendations from trusted sources, which effectively mitigates perceived risks and
solidifies their confidence to purchase.

The Crucial Role of Walk-in Customers and Mediation
The findings establish that Walk-in Customer (M) is the most dominant factor influencing
the Purchasing Decision (Y), exhibiting the highest path coefficient of 0.413. Buying a house
requires the physical validation of seeing the property, experiencing the environment, and
directly interacting with sales personnel to alleviate any remaining uncertainties. This aligns
with Lemon & Verhoef (2020), who assert that direct customer experiences significantly
impact purchasing decisions and brand loyalty. Furthermore, physical interaction at the model
homes builds trust and ownership (Rojas & Villanueva, 2018; Arnett & McCarty, 2021) and
substantially accelerates the purchasing process (Cohen et al., 2019; Wilson et al., 2020).
Moreover, the mediation analysis (H8 -H10) proves that Walk -in Customer  acts as a
significant partial mediator. The highest indirect effect is observed in the Social Media  →
Walk-in Customer → Purchasing Decision pathway (0.178). This effectively illustrates the
Customer Journey Theory (Kotler & Keller, 2016). While external marketing stimuli ( Social
Media, BTL, References) are essential for generating initial awareness and interest, the
physical visit acts as the critical conversion point. Therefore, the ultimate success of marketing
strategies relies on their ability to funn el consumers into making physical site visits, which
eventually mature into final purchasing decisions.

4. Conclusion
This comprehensive study concludes that Social Media , References, and Below The Line
marketing have positive and significant direct impacts  on generating Walk-in Customers and
enhancing Purchasing Decisions at Java Residence. The results validate that Walk -in
Customers successfully and significantly mediate the relationship between these three
independent marketing variables and the final pur chasing decision. The physical visit to the
property exerts the strongest overall influence on the decision to buy, proving that while
marketing creates the initial interest, the physical interaction solidifies the transaction.

Managerial Implications and Suggestions
Based on the findings, it is highly recommended that Java Residence shifts its primary
marketing KPI from simply generating "leads" to increasing the conversion rate of "site visits"
(Walk-in Customers). Management should implement an integrat ed digital booking system
across all social media platforms with clear Call -to-Action (CTA) buttons (e.g., "Schedule a
Site Visit") linking to automated reminders. Given the high impact of References, Java
Residence must develop a structured, incentive -based referral program rewarding past buyers
who successfully bring in new customers. Furthermore, BTL activities should utilize digital
registrations (like QR codes at exhibitions) to seamlessly bridge offline interest with scheduled
physical visits. Finally , standardizing the customer visit experience via strict Standard
Operating Procedures (SOPs) for sales agents will ensure every walk -in customer receives
exceptional service, thereby maximizing the likelihood of a successful purchase.
