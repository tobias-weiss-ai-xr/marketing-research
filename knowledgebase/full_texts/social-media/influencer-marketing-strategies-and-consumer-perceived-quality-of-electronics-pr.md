Abstract
This study examined the relationship between influencer marketing strategies and consumer
perceived quality of electronics products in Rivers State, Nigeria. Specifically, the study assessed
how audience targeting, content authenticity, and influencer prof essionalism influence consumer
perceived quality of electronics products. Anchored on the Social Influence Theory, the study
adopted a positivist philosophical paradigm and employed a cross -sectional research design.
Primary data were obtained through a st ructured questionnaire administered via Google Forms
to 273 usable respondents drawn from active social media users aged 18 –50 who are consumers
of electronics products in Rivers State. The formulated hypotheses were tested using Partial Least
Squares Stru ctural Equation Modelling (PLS -SEM) with SmartPLS 4.1 software. Descriptive
statistics were analysed using SPSS version 26. The findings revealed that audience targeting had
a negative and statistically non -significant relationship with perceived quality, while content
authenticity and influencer professionalism demonstrated positive and statistically significant
relationships with perceived quality. The model explained 34.4% of the variance in consumer
perceived quality. The study concluded that while audi ence targeting alone may not significantly
influence perceived product quality, authentic content and professional influencer conduct are
crucial in shaping consumer quality perceptions. It was recommended that electronics firms
prioritize authentic influe ncer content, partner with credible and technically knowledgeable
influencers, and develop data-driven audience targeting strategies to improve consumer perceived
quality.

Keywords: Influencer Marketing Strategies; Audience Targeting; Content Authenticity; Influencer
Professionalism; Consumer Perceived Quality

1.0 Introduction
The electronics sector occupies a strategic position in Nigeria's economy, contributing
significantly to trade, employment, and technological advancement. The sector encom passes a
wide range of products from smartphones, laptops, and televisions to power banks, generators, and
household appliances, and its contribution to the country's gross domestic product (GDP) through
direct sales, import -export revenues, and retail employment is considerable (Aliyu & Amadu,
2017; Eze et al., 2021). However, despite this economic significance, the electronics mar ket in
Nigeria continues to grapple with persistent challenges that undermine its growth potential. Issues

Consumer perceived quality is widely recognised as a central determinant of consumer purchasing
decisions, particularly in complex, high -involvement product categories such as electronics
(Aaker, 2009; Solin & Curry, 2022). Perceived quality is not merely an objective assessment of a
product's technical attributes but rather a subjective cognitive and affective evaluation through
which consumers compare their expectations with the actual performance characteristics of a
product (Schmidt & Neumann, 2013; Sty lidis et al., 2019). In electronics markets, where the
majority of buyers lack the technical expertise to independently verify product specifications,
perceived quality becomes the primary heuristic guiding purchase decisions. Consumers rely on a
range of external and internal cues including brand reputation, durability, product appearance, and
social endorsement, to form quality judgements (İlhan & Çelik, 2017; Tebulo Laia & Handini,
2022). The significance of perceived quality is further amplified in emer ging markets, where the
prevalence of counterfeit products makes quality assessment particularly challenging for ordinary
consumers. When consumers cannot physically inspect products or rely on established quality
assurance systems, they increasingly turn to trusted information sources in their digital
environments to reduce uncertainty and guide their purchasing decisions (Stylidis et al., 2020;
Yang et al., 2023).
In response to these information gaps and the growing digitization of consumer behaviour,
influencer marketing has emerged as one of the most powerful tools for shaping consumer quality
perceptions. Influencer marketing refers to a digital advertising stra tegy in which companies
collaborate with individuals who possess significant social media followings and credibility to
market goods and services through relatable, authentic content (Abidin, 2018; Djafarova & Bowes,
2021). Unlike traditional advertising, which is largely one-directional and impersonal, influencer
marketing leverages the para -social relationships that have developed between content creators
and their audiences, producing a form of social proof that strongly influences how consumers
evaluate product quality (Lee & Kim, 2024; Lou & Yuan, 2019). With global influencer marketing
investments projected to exceed $24 billion (Chantanasewi & Parnkam, 2024), the practice has
become indispensable for electronics brands seeking to differentiate their p roducts and build
consumer confidence. Influencers can simplify complex product features, demonstrate real-world
product usage, address misinformation about counterfeits, and build social proof around quality
claims in ways that conventional marketing channels cannot replicate (Campbell & Farrell, 2020;
Zhao et al., 2024).
Three strategically important dimensions of influencer marketing are particularly relevant to
understanding consumer perceived quality in the electronics se ctor. First, audience targeting
ensures that quality-related information reaches the most receptive consumer segments (Abrahams
et al., 2013; Naim & Malik, 2024). When influencer content is carefully matched to audience
characteristics, it is more likely to resonate, be perceived as credible, and ultimately shape positive
quality assessments. S econd, con tent authenticity plays a critical role in reducing consumer
skepticism about sponsored endorsements (Audrezet et al., 2020; Ryu & Han, 2021). Authentic
content, characterized by honest product reviews that include both strengths and limitations, has
been found to significantly enhance perceived quality because it minimizes the credibility gap that
often accompanies commercial messaging (Evans et al., 2017; Kumar & Rao, 2024). Th ird,

Despite the growing body of research on influencer marketing globally, several important gaps

through audience targeting, content authenticity,  and influencer professionalism, shape  the
consumer perceived quality of electronics products in Rivers State, Nigeria.

1.1 Statement of the Problem
The electronics sector in Rivers State continues to face significant challenges in sustaining
consumer perceived quality amidst rising market competition, widespread distribution of
counterfeit products, and persistent information asymmetry between sellers  and buyers. While
electronics firms have invested considerably in product development and distribution, consumer
confidence in the quality of electronics products remains fragile. Evidence from prior studies
suggests that a large proportion of consumers in emerging markets such as Nigeria remain hesitant
to purchase electronics due to weak perceptions of product quality, driven largely by the limited
availability of reliable, independent quality information (Dachyar & Banjarnahor, 2017; Yang et
al., 2023).  In Rivers State specifically, where access to authorized service centers and formal
consumer protection mechanisms is limited, the inability to adequately assess product quality prior
to purchase has been identified as a fundamental barrier to consumer sa tisfaction and repeat
patronage.
Existing studies on digital marketing in Nigeria have demonstrated that online strategies can
improve customer engagement and brand visibility (Alo, 2023; Akpiri, 2018), yet they do not
explicitly explain how influencer-specific practices such as precise audience targeting, authentic
content creation, and professional conduct, affect consumer quality perceptions for technically
complex products like electronics. International studies have similarly focused on broader digital
transformation outcomes rather t han isolating the specific pathways through which influencer
marketing dimensions affect perceived quality (Li & Xu, 2022; Macedo et al., 2022). The absence
of this specificity is particularly problematic for electronics firms, given that quality perceptio ns
in this sector are shaped by a distinctive set of information cues that differ markedly from those
relevant in fashion, beauty, or food sectors. Furthermore, prior Nigerian studies rarely adopted
advanced methodological approaches such as Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling

1.2 Objectives of the Study
The aim of this study was to examine how influencer marketing strategies shape the consumer
perceived quality of electronics products in Rivers State. Specifically, the study sought to:
i. Assess the relationship between audience targeting and perceived quality of electronics
products.
ii. Evaluate the relationship between content authenticity and perceived quality of electronics
products.
iii. Examine the relationship between influencer professionalism and perceived quality of
electronics products.

1.3 Research Hypotheses
The following null hypotheses guided the study:
H₀₁: Audience targeting has no significant relationship with perceived quality of electronics
products.
H₀₂: Content authenticity has no significant relationship with perceived quality of electronics
products.
H₀₃: Influencer professionalism has no significant relationship with perceived quality of
electronics products.

2.0 Literature Review
2.1 Conceptual Framework
2.1.1 Influencer Marketing Strategies : Influencer marketing, as conceptualized by Abidin
(2018), is a digital advertising tactic in which companies collaborate with social media
personalities to promote goods through relatable and authentic content. According to Kapoor and
Gupta (2024), this practice involves harnessing the authority and specialized knowledge of online
opinion leaders to influence customer behavior, while Djafarova and Bowes (2021) describe it as
a conduit between audiences and businesses t hat prioritizes the development of trust via
personalized endorsements rather than conventional advertising techniques. The scope and
strategy of influencer marketing has evolved significantly with the proliferation of platforms such
as Instagram, YouTube, TikTok, and Facebook, enabling influencers to produce co ntent that
profoundly affects customer purchasing decisions across a range of product categories (Evans et
al., 2021; Godey et al., 2016). Brown and Hayes (2008) and Campbell and Farrell (2020)
emphasize that influencers serve not merely as promoters but as curators and validators who
simplify complex consumer choices, particularly in industries such as technology and electronics
where product knowledge is often limited among average buyers. The effective ness of influencer
marketing is fundamentally predicated on the degree to which the influencer's values, content style,
and audience demographics align with the brand's target market, as misalignment reduces both

further identify five essential traits of effective influencers - relatability, knowledge, helpfulness,
confidence, and articulation that collectively enable them to build audience trust and effectively
convey persuasive messages. Influencer marketing encompasses diverse campaign formats,
including sponsored posts, product reviews, unboxing demonstrations, tutorials, long -term brand
ambassadorships, and interacti ve giveaways, each varying in their persuasive mechanism and

consumers acquire quality-related information from sources they perceive as credible and relatable
(Diemuodeke & Olannye, 2025; Ovie & Ugwuonah, 2023).

2.1.2 Dimensions of Influencer Marketing Strategies
2.1.2.1 Audience Targeting: Audience targeting in influencer marketing refers to the strategic
practice of identifying and aligning content delivery with the demographic, psychographic,
behavioural, and contextual profiles of the most relevant consumer segments (Abrahams et al.,
2013; Naim & Malik, 2024). According to Kotler (2012) and Kotler and Keller (2009), targeting
is the deliberate selection of a specific group of clients whose needs and preferences the business
intends to meet through tailored commu nication, ensuring that marketing resources are directed
toward the most receptive audiences. Lowe et al. (2014) describe audience targeting as a process
involving the logging of user -consumed content, classifying it into thematic categories, and
mapping these themes to users based on trade and consumption patterns, thereby enabling precise
content delivery. Weinmeister (2022) further explains that customizing a website's or platform's
experience based on individual user roles, preferences, and purchasing cycles represents a form of
audience targeting that moves beyond the one -size-fits-all approach. Mathewson et al. (2010)
emphasize that effective audience targeting requires producing highly relevant content attuned to
particular user needs, optimizing keywords for search algorithms, and using analytical tools to
ensure content aligns with user roles and purchasing cycles. In influencer marketing, audience
targeting is operationalized through key indicators including audience profiling, market
segmentation, behavioral targeting, and target audience identification, all of which collectively
ensure that influencer content reaches consumers whose characteristics and online behaviors align
with electronics product preferences (Inayatullah et al., 2014; Waller, 2020). Developing thorough
audience personas and precisely identifying the target market are identified by Naim and Malik
(2024) as essential components of effective audience targeting. By semantically and sentimentally
matching advertisement material to suitable media channels and audiences, audience targeting
maximises message relevance and the probability of influencing perceived quality assessments
among high -interest consume r segments (Abrahams et al., 2013; Dittus, 2012). In electronics
market where consumer demographics are diverse and product involvement is high, accurate
audience targeting ensures that quality -related influencer messages reach the consumers most
likely to use them as decision-making inputs (Chantanasewi & Parnkam, 2024; Ryu & Han, 2021).

2.1.2.2 Content Authenticity : Content authenticity in influencer marketing is broadly
conceptualized as the degree to which influencer -generated communications are perceived as
genuine, transparent, unscripted, and congruent with the influencer's actual lifestyle, values, and
personal experiences (Audrezet et al., 2020; Marwick, 2013). According to Djafa rova and
Trofimenko (2020), authenticity emerges when an influencer's endorsements align with their life
narrative, and when transparent sponsorship disclosures and honest product usage accounts reduce
consumer skepticism. Lee and Kim (2024) link authenticity to parasocial closeness, explaining
that audiences perceive influencers as digital peers when content feels intimate and non -
commercial, a perception that substantially enhances message credibility and persuasive impa ct.
Kumar and Rao (2024) conceptualize authenticity as narrative congruence, wherein influencers
create emotional resonance by matching their messaging to the ideals and expectations of their
audience, thereby fostering deeper psychological connections that facilitate favourable quality
judgements. Petrangeli et al. (2024) emphasize the role of content authenticity in combating
misinformation, noting that its capacity to verify the origin and integrity of digital content makes
it an essential trust -building mechanism in consumer decision -making environments. According
to Evans et al. (2017), transparency in influencer communications including honest disclosure of
sponsorship arrangements and candid discussion of both positive and negative product attributes,
strengthens the credibility of endorsements and builds long -term relational trust with audiences.
Vanienė-Janssen and Kuprienė (2018) define content authenticity as the capacity to demonstrate
that a digital object is precisely what it purports to be, requiring thorough provenance information
to build confidence in the integrity of the content. Hasan et al. (2024) and Schouten et al. (2020)
further demonstrate that authentic influencer commun ications are among the strongest drivers of
brand trust and consumer quality perceptions across product categories. In the electronics sector
in Rivers State, where the risk of counterfeit products is high and consumers rely heavily on trusted
social voices for quality guidance, authentic influencer content serves as a powerful mechanism
for elevating consumer perceived quality by reducing information asymmetry and building
product-specific credibility (Diemuodeke & Olannye, 2025; Wel et al., 2024).

2.1.2.3 Influencer Professionalism: Influencer professionalism refers to the set of behavioural,
competence-based, and presentation -related attributes that determine the standard of quality,
credibility, and effectiveness of influencer -generated communications (Araç, 2023; van Driel &
Dumitrica, 2021). Kolo et al. (2022) explain that influencer professionalism is fuelled by a
combination of brand orientation including market expertise  and brand engagement, and
entrepreneurial orientation, encompassing proactivity, risk-taking, and strategic content planning.
Araç (2023) further describes professionalism as encompassing proactive communication,
trustworthy information sourcing, and the consistent alignment of messaging with brand values,
all of which contribu te to influencer credibility and audience trust. Ki and Kim (2019) identify
high-quality influencer content characterised by clear visuals, well -structured narratives, and
accurate te chnical information, as a core determinant of audience engagement and per suasive
influence, particularly in sectors where product complexity requires clear demonstration and
explanation. Casaló et al. (2020) and Jin et al. (2019) demonstrate that influencers who deliver
reliable, consistent, and engaging content are significant ly more likely to retain followers and
attract brand partnerships, as such consistency signals professionalism, accountability, and long -
term commitment. According to Lou and Yuan (2019), the repeated and well -structured
demonstrations of product features that characterise professional influencer content are especially
important in electronics markets, where buyers rely on multiple exposures to product information

2.1.3 Consumer Perceived Quality: Consumer perceived quality is a multidimensional construct
defined as the customer's subjective assessment of a product or service's overall excellence or
superiority relative to its intended purpose and in comparison, to available alternatives (Aaker,
2009; Solin & Curry, 2022). Tebulo Laia and Handini (2022) highlight both the subjective and
objective dimensions of perceived quality, explaining that it depends on quality cues especially in
situations where direct product knowledge is restricted, thereby making it a critical psychological
construct in markets characterised by information uncertainty. Schmidt and Neumann (2013)
describe perceived quality as a cognitive and affective assessment process through which
consumers contrast their conscious and unconscious expectations with the actual features of a
product in specific usage scenarios, a process influenced by multiple factors including price,
design, brand image, and social endorsements. Stylidis et al. (2019, 2020) e mphasise that
perceived quality is a customer -centric concept shaped by sensory impressions - visual, tactile,
auditory, and olfactory, and that it integrates client -specific factors that bridge the gap between
objective product attributes and individual consumer perceptions. İlhan and Çelik (2017) define
perceived quality as a consumer's assessment of a brand's or product's capacity to me et
expectations, while Lima and Neto (2013) conceptualise it as an evaluation of service excellence
based on a compariso n between expectations and actual performance. Yang et al. (2023)
underscore that manufacturers must improve the perceived quality of their products and that social
media customer reviews have become increasingly important tools for accurately measuring an d
managing this perception. In the electronics sector, perceived quality is operationalised through
key dimensions including performance quality, durability, brand reputation, and product
reliability, all of which serve as evaluative cues through which con sumers judge a product's
excellence and functional adequacy (Schmitt & Pfeifer, 2009; Stylidis et al., 2020). The role of
perceived quality is particularly significant in high -involvement product categories such as
electronics, where purchase decisions are typically costly, reversals are difficult, and the inability
to physically examine products prior to purchase amplifies the importance of quality heuristics
drawn from credible information sources (Dodds et al., 1991; Yang et al., 2023). In the Rivers
State context, where counterfeit electronics are prevalent and formal quality assurance
mechanisms are limited, consumer perceived quality functions as both a motivator and a barrier to
purchase, making its enhancement through strategic influencer marketing a n urgent priority for
electronics firms (Diemuodeke & Olannye, 2025; Tebulo Laia & Handini, 2022).

2.2 Theoretical Framework: Social Influence Theory
The Social Influence Theory has its foundational origins in the seminal work of Deutsch and
Gerard (1955), who distinguished between two fundamental forms of social influence: normative
influence, which reflects the tendency to conform to the expectations of others in order to gain
social approval, and informational influence, which reflects the inclination to accept information
provided by others as credible evidence about reality. Building upon this foundational distinction,
Turner (1991) extended the con ceptualisation of social influence by examining how group
membership, social identity, and reference group dynamics mediate individuals' susceptibility to
influence processes. Cialdini (2009) further enriched the theoretical landscape by identifying six
principles of social influence - reciprocity, commitment, social proof, authority, likin g, and
scarcity, each of which operates as a persuasive mechanism through which individuals' attitudes
and behaviours are shaped by their social environment. The key assumptions underlying the Social
Influence Theory include the proposition that individuals are inherently social beings whose
cognitions, attitudes, and behaviours are continuously shaped by real or implied social interactions;
that information communicated through credible sources enjoys heightened persuasive power; and
that social conformity and imitation are fundamental drivers of attitudinal and behavioural change
in consumer markets (Bearden et al., 1989; Cialdini, 2009; Turner, 1991). Despite its explanatory
power, the Social Influence Theory has attracted criticisms, most notably for its tendency to
overemphasise the passive role of individuals as recipients of social pressure while
underestimating their active agency in evaluating, resisting, or selecti vely incorporating social
information (Cialdini, 2009). Critics have also noted that the theory's original formulations were
primarily developed in laboratory settings and may not fully account for the complex, multi -
directional, and highly individualised nature of social influence processes in digital environments
characterised by algorithmic mediation, parasocial relationships, and information overload
(Deutsch & Gerard, 1955; Turner, 1991).
The Social Influence Theory directly underpins the present study by providing a coherent
theoretical lens through which to explain how influencer marketing strategies shape consumer
perceived quality. In the context of this study, influencers function as credible, aspirational social
sources whose content about electronics products triggers both normative and informational
influence among their followers (Cialdini, 2009; Turner, 1991). Normatively, exposure to
influencer endorsements from respected social media personalities creates implicit social pressure
for consumers to align their quality perceptions with those communicated by influencers,
particularly when these influencers are perceived as peer -like or aspirational figures.
Informationally, influencer content especially when characterised by content authenticity  and
influencer professionalism, provides consumers with quality -relevant evidence that reduces
uncertainty and shapes favourable product evaluations (Deutsch & Gerard, 1955). Audience
targeting strengthens the relevance and persuasive force of these social influence processes by
ensuring that influencer messages reach consumers whose demographic and psychographic
profiles make them most susceptible to the quality cues being communicated.

3.0 Methodology: This study adopted a positivist philosophical paradigm and employed a cross -
sectional survey research design  (Creswell & Poth, 2018; Saunders et al., 2019). Quantitative
primary data were gathered directly from respondents through a structured questionnaire
administered via Google Forms, with the survey link distributed to potential respondents across
six ident ified social media platforms - Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Facebook, X (formerly
Twitter), and Snapchat. Distribution was carried out using a combina tion of paid targeted
advertisements, influencer collaborations, and organic sharing within Rivers -State-based
electronics and technology interest groups (Malhotra et al., 2021; Saunders et al., 2019). The target
population comprised active social media us ers aged 18 –50 who are consumers of electronics
products in Rivers State, representing a population of 986 identified individuals. The sample size
of 285 respondents was determined using the Taro Yamane (1967) formula at a 95% confidence
level. A stratified random sampling technique was applied to ensure proportional representation
across the identified social media platforms, thereby enhancing the representativeness and
generalizability of the findings (Bryman, 2016; Creswell & Creswell, 2018). The questio nnaire
comprised five demographic questions and thirty -five statement items measured on a five -point
Likert scale (Strongly Disagree = 1 to Strongly Agree = 5), divided into sections addressing the
dimensions of influencer marketing strategies (audience ta rgeting, content authenticity, and
influencer professionalism) and consumer perceived quality. Items measuring influencer
marketing strategies were adapted from Ryu and Han (2021), Chantanasewi and Parnkam (2024),
and Zhao et al. (2024), while items measur ing consumer perceived quality were adapted from
Dachyar and Banjarnahor (2017) and Nguyen and Huynh (2022).
Face and content validity of the research instrument were rigorously established prior to full-scale
data collection. Face validity was confirmed through expert review by five marketing academics,
while content validity was ascertained using the Content Va lidity Index (CVI) approach
recommended by Polit and Beck (2006) and Yusoff (2019). The  Item-level CVI (I -CVI) for all
scale items exceeded 0.83, and the Scale -level CVI/Average (S -CVI/Ave) reached 0.94,
confirming excellent content validity (Hair et al., 2019). A pilot study conducted with 40
respondents confirmed satisfactory Cronbach's alpha reliability for all constructs: Audience
Targeting (α = 0.767), Content Authenticity (α = 0.879), Influencer Professionalism (α = 0.723),
and Perceived Quality (α = 0.714), all meeting the minimum threshold of 0.70 recommended by
Nunnally and Bernstein (1994). The collected data were analysed using Partial Least Squares
Structural Equation Modelling (PLS-SEM) implemented in SmartPLS 4.1 software, an approach
particularly well-suited for predictive research models involving complex latent variable structures
and small-to-moderate sample sizes typical of consumer research in emerging economies (Hair et
al., 2022; Sarstedt et al., 2022). The two -stage PLS-SEM procedure recommended by Becker et
al. (2012) and Hair et al. (2022) was followed: the first stage evaluated the measurement model
through indicator reliability, convergent validity (Average Variance Extracted, AVE ≥ 0.50), and
discriminant validity (Fornell-Larcker criterion); the second stage assessed the structural model by
examining path coefficients, coefficient of determination (R²), predictive relevance (Q²), effect
size (f²), and model fit using the Standardized Root Mean Square Residual (SRMR). Bootstrapping
with 10,000 subsamples was used to determine the statistical significance of path coefficients
through t -statistics and p -values (Streukens & Leroi -Werelds, 2016). Descriptive statistics
including means, standard deviations, skewness, and kurtosis were computed using SPSS version
26 to provide a preliminary characterisation of responses across all study constructs (Hair et al.,
2019).

4.2 Discussion of Findings
The finding that audience targeting had no significant relationship with consumer perceived
quality (β = -0.095, t = 1.111, p = 0.267) is noteworthy and warrants critical examination. This
result is consistent with the findings of Okeniyi and Ighomereho (2 025), who similarly reported

International Journal of Marketing and Communication Studies E -ISSN 2545-5273
P-ISSN 2695-2173 Vol 10. No. 3 2026 www.iiardjournals.org online version

IIARD – International Institute of Academic Research and Development

Page 171
insignificant effects of reach and content quality dimensions on consumer purchase intention
among social media users in Nigeria, and partially aligns with Arora (2 025), who found that
engagement had only minor influence on purchase intentions. However, the finding diverges from
the expectations generated by the Social Influence Theory, which posits that precisely targeted
social messages reaching demographically aligned audiences should amplify th e normative
influence process and thereby strengthen quality  perceptions (Deutsch & Gerard, 1955; Turner,
1991). A plausible explanation for this divergence is that in the Rivers State electronics market,
where consumer trust in targeted advertising is eroded by perceptions of promotional intent and
algorithmic manipulation, audience targeting by itself may not constitute a sufficiently credible
quality signal. Consumers may be exposed to targeted influencer content regularly yet remain
sceptical about the authenticity of targeted recommendations, particularly when other dimensions
such as content authenticity, are perceived as insufficient. This interpretation is consistent with
Diemuodeke and Olannye (2025), who found that storytelling and brand advocacy were more
effective influencer marketing levers than mere reach or targeting in the Nigerian smartphone
market. The negative path coefficient, while negligible, also suggests that overly targeted
influencer content may paradoxically raise consumer suspicion about commercial intent, thereby
slightly undermining perceived quality.
The finding that content authenticity significantly and positively influenced perceived quality (β =
0.406, t = 4.882, p = 0.000) with the highest path coefficient among the three predictors strongly
supports both the Social Influence Theory and the broade r empirical literature. This result
resonates with the findings of Wel et al. (2024), who demonstrated in the Malaysian context that
the authenticity of influencer content significantly influences consumer trust and purchasing
decisions, and with Fathy et al. (2025), who confirmed that influencer marketing significantly
boosts customer trust in the restaurant sector - a finding paralleled here in the electronics context.
The result is also consistent with Lee and Kim (2020), who established that brand authenticity
mediated by consumer trust significantly predicted purchase intention, and with Evans et al.
(2017), who underscored the role of transparent sponsorship disclosure in building consumer trust
and credibility. From the perspective of the Social Influ ence Theory, authentic content operates
primarily through the informational influence mechanism: when influencer communications are
perceived as genuine, non -scripted, and congruent with the influencer's actual experience, they
serve as credible evidence o f product quality that consumers incorporate into their quality
judgements (Cialdini, 2009; Turner, 1991). In the Rivers State context, where counterfeit
electronics create pervasive quality uncertainty, authentic influencer communications that honestly
address product strengths and limitations provide a particularly valuable quality signal that
meaningfully elevates consumer perceived quality. The moderate effect size (f² = 0.128) further
confirms that content authenticity's contribution to perceived quality is substantively meaningful,
even if it falls short of a large effect.
The finding that influencer professionalism significantly and positively predicted perceived quality

argued for the role of professional communication in building influencer credibility, and Ki and

persuasion, few studies had  empirically tested the direct effect of professionalism on perceived
product quality. The present finding, supported by the Social Influence Theory's informational
influence mechanism, suggests that when influencers demonstrate technical competence,
consistent branding, and responsive engagement with consumer queries, they effectively function

5.0 Conclusion
This study examined the relationship between influencer marketing strategies and consumer
perceived quality of electronics products in Rivers State, Nigeria. The study established that
content authenticity and influencer professionalism each demonstrated significant positive effects
on consumer perceived quality, while audience targeting showed a non-significant and negligible
negative relationship with the same  outcome. The collective explanatory power of the three
influencer marketing strategy dimensions was moderate, indicating that these strategies account
for a meaningful but not exhaustive portion of the variance in consumer perceived quality. These
findings underscore the critical importance of genuine, transparent, and professionally delivered
influencer content in shaping positive quality perceptions among electronics consumers in Rivers
State.

5.1 Recommendations
Based on the findings of this study, the following practical recommendations are offered:
i. Since content authenticity emerged as the strongest predictor of consumer perceived
quality, electronics firms should make authentic communication a non-negotiable standard
in influencer marketing contracts. Brand managers should specifically require influencers
to share balanced and honest product experiences including both strengths and limitations,
disclose sponsorship arrangements transparently, and demonstrate real -world product
usage in contextually relevant settings. Electronics firms should also empower influencers
to engage honestly with their audiences about product specifications, thereby building the
informational trust that significantly elevates perceived quality.

ii. Given that influencer professionalism significantly improved perceived quality, electronics
firms should adopt a more rigorous and competence -based approach to influencer
selection, prioritising expertise, technical knowledge, content quality consistency, and
responsive engagement over follower count alone. Partnerships should be developed with
influencers who have demonstrated experience in technology reviews or consumer
electronics, and who maintain consistent professional brand identities on their social media
platforms. Long -term brand ambassadorship arrangements are preferable over one -off
sponsored posts, as they allow influencers to develop deeper product knowledge and
communicate with greater authority and credibility over time.
