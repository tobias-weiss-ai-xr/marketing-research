Digital libraries are organized collections of electronic information resources supported by
technological infrastructure that enables users to search, retrieve, access, preserve, and utilize
scholarly information through digital platforms. Unlike conventional libraries that depend primarily
on printed collections, digital libraries provide users with unrestricted access to information
resources through computers, mobile devices, and internet -enabled technologies. Their
emergence has transformed library ope rations by facilitating remote access, virtual reference
services, digital lending, institutional repositories, electronic databases, online catalogues, and
cloud-based information services. These developments have strengthened the role of academic
libraries as centres for knowledge creation, lifelong learning, and scholarly communication within
tertiary institutions.
Despite the enormous investments made by governments, educational institutions, and
development partners in establishing digital libraries, s tudies have shown that the utilization and
universities, namely the Federal University of Technology, Owerri (FUTO), Alvan Ikoku Federal

Purposive sampling technique was used to select the institutions. Data were collected using a
researcher-developed questionnaire titled Marketing Strategies to Enhance Patronage of
Digital Libraries Questionnaire Scale (MSEPDLQS). The instrument wa s validated by experts in
Educational Measurement and Evaluation and Library and Information Science. The reliability
of the instrument was established using Cronbach's alpha, which yielded a coefficient of 0.80.
Mean and standard deviation were used to analyze the research question. The finding revealed
that social media marketing enhances patronage of digital libraries to a high extent, with a
grand mean of 3.39 and standard deviation of 0.70. Specifically, respondents indicated that
social media increased their awareness of digital library services, motivated them to use digital
libraries more frequently, influenced their decisions to patronize digital library services,
improved awareness of new digital resources, enhanced their understanding of how to access
and use digital resources, encouraged regular usage, increased interest through interactive
content, improved perceptions of digital library usefulness, and increased engagement
through timely announcements. The study concludes that social media repre sents an
important marketing strategy for increasing awareness, engagement and patronage of digital
library resources in tertiary institutions. It was recommended that tertiary institution libraries
should develop systematic social media marketing strategi es, maintain regular and relevant
promotional content, strengthen interactive communication with users, and provide adequate
ICT infrastructure and professional training for librarians.

The emergence of social media has created unprecedented opportunities for organizations to
communicate directly with their target audiences. Social media refers to int ernet-based
applications and digital communication platforms that enable users to create, share, exchange,
and interact with information in real time through text, images, audio, and video. Platforms such
as Facebook, WhatsApp, Instagram, X (formerly Twitter), YouTube, LinkedIn, Telegram, and TikTok
have become integral communication channels in higher education because they facilitate
collaboration, information dissemination, community engagement, and knowledge sharing among
students, lecturers, researcher s, and librarians. Their widespread adoption has transformed the
manner in which institutions market services and interact with users.
Marketing in library and information science has evolved from traditional promotional activities
such as notice boards, p rinted bulletins, exhibitions, newsletters, and orientation programmes to
more interactive digital marketing approaches. Library marketing involves identifying users'
information needs, creating awareness of available resources and services, promoting libr ary
products, and encouraging sustained utilization of library facilities. In the digital era, marketing has
become a strategic management function through which libraries increase visibility, improve user
engagement, strengthen institutional image, and ma ximize the utilization of costly electronic
information resources.
Social media marketing represents one of the most effective digital marketing strategies available
to academic libraries. It involves the deliberate use of social networking platforms to pr omote
library resources, services, programmes, events, electronic databases, institutional repositories,
information literacy programmes, and virtual reference services. Through social media, libraries
can communicate instantly with thousands of users, dis seminate information about newly
acquired electronic resources, announce training programmes, respond to users' enquiries, and
create interactive online communities that strengthen user engagement. Recent studies indicate
that social media enhances awarene ss of library services, increases user participation, and
supports effective marketing of digital resources when strategically deployed. The increasing
popularity of smartphones and affordable internet connectivity has further enhanced the
relevance of soc ial media marketing in tertiary institutions. Undergraduate and postgraduate
students spend a considerable amount of time on social networking platforms for communication,
collaboration, entertainment, and academic activities. Consequently, libraries have recognized the
need to reach users through the same platforms they frequently use rather than relying exclusively
on traditional communication channels. By integrating social media into library marketing
strategies, librarians can deliver timely informatio n, promote electronic resources, encourage
online interactions, and improve patronage of digital library services.

Kirita, and Mwantimwa (2021), carried out a study on the use of social media in marketing library
resources and services at MUHAS and SUA. A mixed research approach was used to process,
collect and analyze data in the s tudy. Library staff and users formed the population of the study
and were conveniently selected, while key informants were purposively selected. Data was mainly
collected using survey methods. The data were analyzed using SPSS. The study found out that
Facebook and WhatsApp are the effectively used platform by both the studied libraries in the
marketing of library services and resources due to its popularity and convenience. However,
notwithstanding this, the media’s usage to market library resources and se rvices has been found
generally ineffective. This has been attributed to different challenges such as lack of a written
policy for social media usage, unreliable internet connectivity, low promotion, content
obsoleteness, and inadequate computer facilities . The study recommended that social media
policies should be formulated, investment in ICT infrastructure development should be made, and
promotion of social media usage among information users should be done.  Kirita and Mwantimwa
(2021), study is similar to the present because both centered on use of social media as a marketing

on the fact that the present study investigated three different digital marketing strategies while
the former studied only one aspect. Eligan -Lectaoa (2023), investigated the influence of library
marketing strategies on the utilization of library virtual reference service. The study determined
the influence of library marketing strategi es to the library users’ utilization of virtual reference
service. The concurrent nested mixed method of research was used. Participants of the study were
the 206 students at a state university in the province of Bukidnon. Descriptive statistics and
regression analysis were used to organize the data of the study. Findings revealed that the
participants accessed as high all the library marketing strategies. And it was found out the
utilization of the library virtual reference service was rated to a high exte nt. Moreover, the
combination of library marketing strategies significantly influenced the variability in the utilization
of virtual reference service. The study concludes that through social media, the library can
encourage library users to avail of its varied information sources. Marketing strategies significantly
influence the library users’ utilization. This confirms the theory of confirmation expectancy which
suggests that perceptions are influenced by pre -use expectations (marketing strategies) and
consequently directly impact confirmation (utilization of the library virtual service). Based on the
findings, the university library may consider other forms of social media such as Google hangouts,
Twitter(X) and YouTube to enhance its virtual services to attract more patrons to use its service.

In Nigeria, tertiary institutions have increasingly embraced digital transformation in response to
global developments in higher education. Many universities, polytechnic, and college libraries now
subscribe to electronic databases, establish institutional repositories, digitize local collections, and
provide remote access to scholarly resources. Nevertheless, the level of patronage of these digital
resources varies considerably among institutions. Several studies have attributed this situation to
inadequate awareness campaigns, inconsistent social media engagement, limited funding,
insufficient digital marketing expertise among librarians, and infrastructural constraints affecting
internet connectivity and electricity supply. Agai nst this backdrop, the present study seeks to
examine the utilization of social media as a marketing strategy to enhance the patronage of digital
libraries in tertiary institutions. Understanding how social media platforms can improve awareness,
engagement, and sustained use of digital library resources will provide evidence for developing
effective digital marketing strategies capable of increasing the visibility and utilization of academic
library services in the contemporary time.

institutional repositories, online databases, and other digital  information resources essential for

educational institutions, and development partners have invested substantial financial and
technological resources in establ ishing and maintaining digital library infrastructures. Despite
these investments, evidence suggests that the level of patronage and utilization of digital library
resources among students and academic staff in many tertiary institutions remains below

One major challenge confronting many academic libraries is the inability to effectively market their
digital resources and services to their target users. Although libraries subscribe to numerous
electronic databases and provide various digital in formation services, many students remain
unaware of these resources or lack adequate knowledge of how to access and utilize them
effectively. Consequently, users often depend on general internet search engines and freely
available online materials instead of credible scholarly resources provided through institutional
digital libraries. This situation undermines the value of investments made in digital library
development and reduces the visibility and impact of academic library services. The widespread
adoption of social media platforms such as Facebook, WhatsApp, Instagram, X (formerly Twitter),
YouTube, LinkedIn, Telegram, and TikTok presents academic libraries with new opportunities to
market their digital resources, increase user engagement, and improve patronage. Through these
platforms, libraries can create awareness, disseminate information on newly acquired electronic
resources, provide virtual reference services, conduct user education, and maintain continuous
interaction with their users. However, d espite the increasing popularity of social media among
students, many tertiary institution libraries have not fully integrated these platforms into their
marketing strategies. In some institutions, social media accounts are either inactive, poorly
managed, or used only for occasional announcements rather than as strategic tools for sustained
user engagement.
Furthermore, previous studies have focused largely on the adoption of social media in libraries,
users' perceptions of digital library services, and th e challenges associated with digital library
utilization. Relatively few studies have comprehensively examined the extent to which the
utilization of social media as a marketing strategy enhances the patronage of digital libraries in
tertiary institutions,  particularly within the Nigerian context. This gap limits the availability of
empirical evidence needed by library administrators and policymakers to formulate effective digital
marketing strategies. If this situation persists, digital libraries may conti nue to experience low
patronage despite the availability of valuable electronic resources. This could result in
underutilization of institutional subscriptions, reduced return on investment, limited access to
high-quality scholarly information, and diminis hed support for teaching, learning, and research in
tertiary institutions. It is against this background that this study seeks to investigate the utilization
of social media as a marketing strategy to enhance the patronage of digital libraries in tertiary
institutions. The study aims to provide empirical evidence that will guide library management,
educational administrators, and policymakers in developing effective social media marketing
strategies capable of increasing awareness, improving user engagement , and promoting greater
utilization of digital library resources.

Methodology
The research design used for the study was descriptive survey research design. The study
employed descriptive survey research design because it enables the researchers to collect a great
deal of information or data in a relatively quickly and easy way. A l arge number of responses can
be obtained quite quickly enabling the researchers to work with a large data (Cozby, 2003; Asika,
2009). The study was carried out in tertiary institutions in Imo State, Nigeria. Imo state is a fairly
large area with (27) local government council areas that make up the state. The population of the
study  comprise all the nine thousand two hundred and one (9,201) final year students in the eight
(8) tertiary institutions in Imo State. Out of these tertiary institutions, two (2) a re public
universities, three (3) are state universities, two (2) polytechnic and one (1) college of Education
The sample was made up 950 final year students drawn from five (5) universities in the study area.
This is made up of Federal University of Techn ology (FUTO = 350), Alvan Ikoku Federal University
of Education (AIFUE=280), Imo State University (IMSU= 350), Kingsley Ozumbambadiwe University
(KOMU=47) and University of Agriculture and Environmental sciences (UAES=38). Purposive
sampling technique was used to select all the five (5) universities in Imo State because they have
the same capacity funding from tetfund and enough ICT facilities that can accommodate the
students.  The data collection instrument for this study was structured questionnaire titl ed
Marketing Strategies to Enhance Patronage of Digital Libraries Questionnaire Scale (MSEPDLQS).
The instrument contains two parts A and B. Section A seeks information on students’ demographic
characteristics, such as gender and level and section B has th ree (3) sub - sections that asked
questions on the variables of the study on a four -point scale with options ranging from Strongly
agree (4 points), Agree (3 points), Disagree (2points) and strongly disagree (1points) the response
scores were reversed for negative items. The face and content validity of the questionnaires was
done by presenting the instrument to two experts in Test and Measurement from Alvan Ikoku
Federal University of Education Owerri and three experts from department of Library and
Information Science in Imo State University to validate and make necessary corrections. They were
asked to make corrections on grammar and ensure each of the items are specific. Their advice will
be sort in terms of scope of coverage, content validity and clarity of expression in the instruments.
It was also revalidated by two library officers to confirm it. They made certain observations and
their corrections were used to review the MSEPDLQS. To determine the reliability index
MSEPDLQS the instrument was found t o be 0.80 using Cronbach alpha.  With the help of the
research assistants, the instrument was distributed to the sampled students to complete. The
students were requested to provide the researchers with the accurate information. The
researchers solicit for  support of the students by briefing those involved in the research. The

Social Media Marketing Enhances Digital Library Patronage
The finding shows that social media provides tertiary institution libraries with an effective avenue
for creating awareness and communicating the value of digital library resources to students. The
high mean score for awareness suggests that students are more likely to know about electronic
resources when libraries communicate information through platforms that students frequently
use. In practical terms, social-media posts can inform students about newly subscribed databases,
e-books, electronic journals, institutional repositories, research -support services and other digital
resources. This is particularly important because the availability of digital resources does not
necessarily result in their utilization if potential users are unaware of their existence or do not know
how to access them.  The finding agrees with Kirita and Mwantimwa (2021), whose study of
MUHAS and SUA found Facebook to be an effectively used social -media platform for marketing
library resources and services because of its popularity and convenience. However, their study also
found that the overall effectiveness of social-media marketing was constrained by factors including
inadequate policies, unreliable Internet connectivity, inadequate promotion, obsolete content and
insufficient computer facilities. it is also consistent with Eligan -Lectaoa (2023), who investigated
library marketing strategies and the utilization of virtual reference services among 206 university
students in the Philippines. The study found that library marketing strategies were rated highly and
that utilization of virtual reference services was also high. actual use of library services. The finding
also has support from Muneja (2024), who assessed the effectiveness of online e -resource
marketing tools in university libraries in Tanzania. The study found that Facebook was the most
commonly used social -media platform for marketing e -resources, followed by Instagram.
However, the study also found that less than 50% of marketing messages were active and  current
and that only a few universities provided instructions on how to access and use e -resources.

Conclusion
Based on the finding of the study, it is concluded that social media is an important and potentially
effective marketing strategy for enhancing the patronage of digital libraries in tertiary institutions
in Imo State. The high grand mean of 3.39 demonstrates that respondents perceived social -media
marketing as contributing substantially to awareness, engagement, accessibility, interest and
utilization of digital library resources. Social media enables libraries to communicate information
about new resources, provide timely announcements, share promotional content, facilitate
interaction with users and demonstrate the usefulness of digital resou rces. Therefore, when
strategically planned, regularly updated and supported with adequate ICT infrastructure and
competent librarians, social-media marketing can strengthen the visibility and patronage of digital
library resources among students in tertiary institutions.

Recommendations
Based on the finding and conclusion of the study, the following recommendations are made:
1. Tertiary institution libraries should develop formal social -media marketing policies. Each
library should establish clear guidelines  specifying the objectives, target audience,
content, posting frequency, responsibilities of staff and procedures for monitoring social-
media activities.

3. Social-media content should be relevant to students' academic needs. Librarians should

should, where technically and institutionally appropriate, include links that take users
