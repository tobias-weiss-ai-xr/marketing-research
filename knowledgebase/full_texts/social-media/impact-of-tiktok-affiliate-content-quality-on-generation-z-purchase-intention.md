Abstract
TikTok has become a major social commerce channel, and it has changed digital marketing practice, most visibly
through the spread of affiliate marketing strategies. Indonesia has one of the largest TikTok user bases in the world,
and Generation Z is a leading consumer group there, heavily engaged with short-form video, particularly in fashion.
This study examines how affiliate content quality affects Generation Z's purchase intention on TikTok Shop, using
the Stimulus-Organism-Response (S-O-R) framework as its analytical basis. More precisely, the research probes
the mediating role of trust alongside the moderating role of perceived risk in shaping purchase intention. A
quantitative research design was adopted, harnessing PLS -SEM. Data were amassed via an on line questionnaire
disseminated to 165 Generation Z respondents in Indonesia who actively use TikTok and have been exposed to
affiliate marketing content. The findings disclose that affiliate content quality exerts a positive and significant effect
upon both trust and purchase intention. Trust, in turn, significantly influences purchase intention and partially
mediates the relationship between affiliate content quality and purchase intention. Moreover, perceived risk
mediates the relationship between trust and purchase intention, such that an escalation in perceived risk weakens
the positive impact of trust on purchase intention. This study augments the S-O-R framework's applicability within
the context of social commerce and furnishes practical implications  for brands and affiliate creators seeking to
cultivate authentic, credible, and high-quality content so as to enhance consumer trust and purchase intention.
Keywords: Content Quality, Perceived Risk, Purchase Intention, TikTok Affiliate Marketing, Trust.

1. Introduction
TikTok, a short-form video-centric social media platform, has undergone considerable
proliferation in recent years and has fundamentally reconfigured the manner in which
individuals, particularly younger cohorts, consume information and entertainment. According
to Rizaty (2025) , Indonesia was ranked as the nation harboring the largest contingent of
TikTok users worldwide as of April 2025, reaching approximately 184.95 million users. This
figure surpasses that of the United States (132.76 million) and Brazil (120.28 million),
accentuating Indonesia's preeminent standing within the global TikTok ecosystem (see Figure
1). Such rapid adoption suggests that TikTok has evolved beyond a mere entertainment
platform and has become a strategically significant channel within the digital marketing and
social commerce landscape.

The transformation of TikTok from an entertainment-based application into a business
platform has become increasingly evident with the introduction of TikTok Shop, which
integrates social interaction with e -commerce activities. This feature facilitates tra nsactions
for both consumers and sellers through mechanisms such as live streaming and embedded
product links within video content (Sa’adah et al., 2022) . Such developments signify a
substantial shift from conventional social media toward social commerce, where consumption
is driven not only by functional needs but also by social and emotional experiences (Mumtaz
& Saino, 2021).
Within this ecosystem, affiliate marketing has emerged as a prominent strategy on
TikTok Shop. Affiliate marketing enables content creators to earn commissions through the
promotion of product links (Karina et al., 2023) . The effectiveness of this strategy has been
demonstrated in the TikTok ecosystem, where affiliate marketing significantly contributes to
consumers' purchase intention during live -streaming activities (Reswara & Wijaya, 2024).
Prior studies indicate that affiliate marketing positively influences consumers' purchase
intention (Brilianita & Sulistyowati, 2023)  and enhances consumer trust through relevant,
authentic, and credible content (Kim & Kim, 2021). However, its effectiveness largely depends
on content quality. Honest, informative, and creative content not only strengthens affiliate
credibility but also fosters trust, which serves as a fundamental determinant of purchasing
decisions (Sokolova & Kefi, 2020).
Generation Z, individuals born between 1997 and 2012, constitute the primary target of
this strategy. As digital natives, they are highly engaged with social media and tend to critically
evaluate marketing messages before making purchase decisions (Djafarova & Bowes, 2021 ;
Sokolova & Kefi, 2020). This generation values transparency, authenticity, and emotionally
engaging content (Soni et al., 2024) . Nevertheless, they are also sensitive to perceived risk,
including concerns related to data security, product quality, and seller credibility (Faza &
Melati, 2025). Studies by Akram et al. (2021)  demonstrate that even when consumers trust
social commerce platforms, h igh levels of perceived risk may still lead them to delay or
reconsider purchasing decisions. Therefore, perceived risk may function as a mediating
variable in the relationship between trust and purchase intention.
The fashion sector represents a particula rly relevant context for social commerce
research, as fashion and apparel products are consistently reported among the most
prominent product categories purchased through social commerce platforms and are strongly
influenced by social interaction and visual content (Sudirman et al., 2025). Due to their visual
nature and reliance on product presentation, consumers often depend on digital content and
online recommendations when making purchase decisions (Djafarova & Bowes, 2021;

Sokolova & Kefi, 2020). Given that fashion products rely heavil y on visual appeal and brand
image, content quality and trust play critical roles in shaping consumers' purchase intention.
Notwithstanding antecedent scholarship that has probed the influence of content quality
and information quality upon consumer trust within social commerce platforms (Hoang et al.,
2022; Senali et al., 2024), together with the nexus between trust and purchase intention (Lee,

Notwithstanding the precipitous growth of Ti kTok Shop and affiliate marketing, it
remains obscure how affiliate content quality contributes to the cultivation of trust and,
ultimately, sways Generation Z's purchase intention. Trust in affiliates may function as a
pivotal determinant guiding consumer s' purchasing decisions, particularly amongst
Generation Z consumers who frequently depend upon influencer recommendations and social
media endorsements when appraising products  (Kumar, 2025) . However, perceived risk
continues to pose a significant challenge, as online transactions are inherently vulnerable to
fraud, product discrepancies, and unsatisfactory shopping experiences.
To gain a deeper understanding of these issues, preliminary interviews were conducted
with a brand owner, an affiliate creator, and a Generation Z consumer. The owner of a local
fashion brand stated that using TikTok Affiliate has not always produced consistent sales; viral
content is not necessarily followed by increased sales, leaving uncertainty about the roles of
content quality, affiliates, and promotional strategy (Mr. T, 25 years). The affiliate creator
emphasized that trust is a key element of affiliate content, built through live product
demonstrations, relevant information, and continued engagement with the audience (Ms. A,
22 years). Meanwhile, the Generation Z consumer stated that she only trusts certain creators
and tends to be cautious toward content perceived as merely promotional, without authentic
product experience (Ms. P, 22 years). These initial findings indicate a gap between exposure
to affiliate content and purchase decisions, making content quality, trust, and perceived risk
important aspects to examine.
Based on these problems, this study aims to analyze the effect of affiliate content quality
on purchase intention and consumer trust, the effect of trust on purchase intention, and the
mediating roles of consumer trust and perceived risk among Generation Z consumers who
purchase fashion products through TikTok Shop in Indonesia.
Theoretically, this study extends the Stimulus-Organism-Response (S-O-R) framework
to the context of TikTok affiliate marketing by positioning content quality as the stimulus, trust
and perceived risk as internal mechanisms, and purchase intention as the behavioral response.
Practically, this study offers implications for fashion brands and affiliate creators in
developing authentic, informative, credible, and audience-oriented content to build trust and
reduce perceived risk. The study is limited to Generation Z consumers in Indonesia who use
TikTok Shop and have been exposed to fashion-product affiliate content. The research focuses
on content quality, trust, perceived risk, and purchase intention within the S-O-R framework,
while other  product categories, demographic groups, and social commerce platforms fall
outside its scope.

2. Literature Review
2.1. Stimulus-Organism-Response (S-O-R) Model
The Stimulus -Organism-Response (S -O-R) framework, originally propounded by
Mehrabian and Russell (1974)  in An Approach to Environmental Psychology , explains how
environmental stimuli shape human behavior through internal psychological processes.
Rooted in environmental psychology, the model posits that individuals do not respond
automatically to external stimuli; rather, stimuli first influence cognitive and affective states
before producing a behavioral response. Conceptually, S -O-R consists of three interrelated
components: stimulus, organism, and response (Djafarova & Bowes, 2021). The stimulus
refers to external cues from the environment, including physical, social, and digital elements
such as advertising messages, promotional content, product presentation, and platform
features. The organism refers to the internal psychological processes that mediate the
influence of the stimulus, while the response is the resulting behavioral tendency,  whether
approach or avoidance (Pandita et al., 2021).
In this study, the S-O-R framework act as the theoretical foundation for explaining the
effect of TikTok affiliate content quality on the purchase intention of Generation Z consumers
in the fashion sector. Content quality is positioned as the stimulus that shapes consumers'
internal evaluations, with trust as the organism variable mediating the relationship between
content quality and purchase intention. Perceived risk is included as an additional mediating
mechanism to explain how consumers' internal evaluations may influence purchase intention.
The S -O-R framework thus provides a theoretical structure for understanding consumer
decision-making in social commerce environments such as TikTok Shop.

2.3. Relationship Between Content Quality and Purchase Intention
Content quality represents a central component of digital marketing strategies,
particularly within social media platforms such as TikTok. Content that is visually appealing,
clearly communicated, and informative can shape consumers' perceptions and increase their
interest in promoted products. High-quality digital content improves consumers' engagement
with marketing messages and positively influences their purchase intentions in social
commerce environments (Djafarova & Bowes, 2021; Sokolova & Kefi, 2020).
Generation Z, as the primary user segment of TikTok, tends to favor content that appears
authentic, transparent, and consistent with their communication style. When affiliate content
aligns with these expectations, it can directly stimulate purchase intention. Consumers may
decide to click affiliate links or proceed with transactions when the content is perceived as
engaging, relevant, and credible. Thus, in the context of TikTok affiliate marketing, content
quality can directly contribute to consumers' purchasing decisions (Sibulan & Limos -Galay,
2024). Empirical findings further buttress this relationship. Al -Qudah (2020) demonstrated
that content quality positively and significantly sways purchase intention within e-commerce
settings. Predicated upon these theoretical considerations and antecedent empirical evidence,
the following hypothesis is proffered:
H1: Content quality has a positive and significant effect on purchase intention.
2.4. Relationship Between Content Quality and Trust
Content quality plays a vital role in shaping consumers' evaluations of both the affiliator
and the promoted product. In digital marketing environments, well -structured, informative,
and authentic conten t enhances the perceived credibility of the message source and
strengthens consumers' confidenc  e in the information presented (A. E. K. Sari & Patrikha,
2024). When affiliate content is delivered clearly and professionally, it reduces ambiguity and
increases the reliability of the promotional message.
Within social commerce platforms such as TikTok Shop, consumers often rely heavily
on content cues to evaluate the trustworthiness of affiliators. High-quality affiliate content
that is transparent, relevant, and experience-based can foster trust by signaling competence,
honesty, and sincerity. From the vantage point of the Stimulus -Organism-Response (S-O-R)
framework, content quality fu nctions as the stimulus that sways consumers' internal
psychological state, namely trust, which epitomizes the organism component in this study.
Empirical studies further support this linkage. Yuliana and Wahyudi (2021) discerned that
content quality exerts a positive and significant influence upon consumer trust within digital
contexts. Drawing from both theoretical reasoning and empirical evidence, the following
hypothesis is proffered:
H2: Content quality has a positive and significant effect on trust.

2.5. Relationship Between Trust and Purchase Intention
Trust is widely recognized as a key determinant of pu rchase intention, particularly in
online environments characterized by uncertainty and information asymmetry. In digital
transactions, consumers often face potential risks related to product quality, payment security,
and the credibility of sellers. Theref ore, trust functions as a critical mechanism that reduces
uncertainty and increases consumers' willingness to engage in purchasing behavior (Mutaufiq
et al., 2025).
Within the ambit of affiliate marketing, trust toward the affiliator and the promoted
brand plays a pivotal role in shaping behavioral intentions. When consumers perceive the
affiliator as forthright, co mpetent, and reliable, they are more disposed to feel assured in
acting upon the promotional message. Conversely, deficient trust may attenuate purchase
motivation and diminish conversion effectiveness  (Piliana & Maradona, 2024) . From a
behavioral vantage point, trust functions as a cognitive and affective evaluation that facilitates
the transition from perception to purchase intention (Mutaufiq et al., 2025). Predicated upon
these theoretical arguments and empirical evidence, the following hypothesis is proffered:
H3: Trust has a positive and significant effect on consumers' purchase intention.
2.6. Perceived Risk as a Mediator between Content Quality and Purchase
Intention
Content quality may influence purchase intention not only directly but also through
perceived risk, an internal evaluative mechanism that reflects consumers' assessment of
potential losses and uncertainty in online transactions, such as product mismatch, p ayment
security, and the risk of fraud. In digital environments where consumers cannot inspect
products in person, clear, transparent, and informative affiliate content can reduce
uncertainty by providing information that supports product evaluation. This is consistent with
Soni et al. (2024), who showed that digital marketing content delivered through social media
influencers affects consumers' purchase intention, particularly among Generation Z.
Conversely, ambiguous or low -quality content may heighten un certainty and perceived risk,
thereby weakening purchase intention.
Within the Stimulus -Organism-Response (S -O-R) framework, content quality acts as
the stimulus, perceived risk represents consumers' internal cognitive evaluation, and purchase
intention is the behavioral response. Perceived risk thus serves as a psychological mechanism
explaining how content quality can influence purchase intention. High -quality content is
expected to reduce perceived risk and ultimately strengthen consumers' willingness to
purchase. Based on this mechanism, perceived risk is expected to mediate the relationship
between content quality and purchase intention.
H4: Perceived risk mediates the effect of content quality on purchase intention.
2.7. Perceived Risk as a Mediator between Trust and Purchase Intention
Although trust is widely recognized as a key determinant of purchase intention in online
environments, its influence may operate through consumers' evaluation of potential risks. In
digital transactions, consumers frequently assess uncertainties and potential losses related to
product quality, transaction security, and seller credibility before making purchase decisions
(Akram et al., 2021). Even when consumers trust an affiliator, their purchase intention may
depend on whether the perceived risks associated with the transaction are sufficiently reduced.
Trust can function as a mechanism that attenuates perceived risk. When consumers
perceive an affiliator as forthright, competent, and reliable, they are more disposed to believe
that the information proffered is accurate and that the transaction process is secure.  As a
result, concerns related to product mismatch, financial loss, or fraud diminish. The reduction

of perceived risk subsequently increases consumers' confidence in proceeding with the
purchase, thereby strengthening purchase intention.
Within the Stimulus -Organism-Response (S -O-R) framework, trust epitomizes an
internal psychological evaluation within the organism component, while perceived risk
reflects a subsequent  cognitive assessment of uncertainty that molds behavioral response.
Within this mechanism, perceived risk elucidates how trust translates into purchase intention.
Therefore, perceived risk is anticipated to mediate the relationship between trust and
purchase intention.
H5: Perceived risk mediates the effect of trust on purchase intention.

3. Methods
3.1. Types and Research Approaches
This study employs a quantitative approach utilizing an explanatory research design.
According to Sugiyono (2017), quantitative research accentuates theory testing through the
measurement of research variables using numerical data, which are subsequently scrutinized
using statistical procedures. The paramount objective of explanatory researc h is to examine
and elucidate causal relationships amongst the investigated variables, encompassing both
direct and indirect effects.
A quantitative approach is adopted because this study concentrates on testing
hypotheses concerning the influence of Conte nt Quality (X) upon Generation Z consumers'
Purchase Intention (Y), with Trust (Z) and Perceived Risk (M) functioning as mediating

variables. Data analysis is conducted using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation
Modeling (PLS -SEM) with the aid of Smar tPLS software. This method is apposite for
predictive research with relatively modest sample sizes (approximately 100 to 150
respondents), non -normally distributed data, and structural models involving multiple
mediation effects (Hair et al., 2024).
3.2. Population and Sample
The population of this study comprises Generation Z consumers (born between 1997 and
2012) who actively use TikTok and have been exposed to affiliate marketing content on the
platform. This population was selected because Generation Z epitomizes one of the most active
social media user segments and exhibits robust engagement with digital content, influencer
marketing, and online shopping activities (Djafarova & Bowes, 2021; Sokolova & Kefi, 2020).
The sampling technique employed in this study is purposive sampling, which entails
selecting respondents predicated upon specific criteria germane to the research objectives
(Sugiyono, 2017). Respondents must meet the following inclusion criteria: (1) aged between
13 and 28 years (Generation Z), (2) possessing an active TikTok account and being an active
user of TikTok Shop, and (3) having purchased fashion products through TikTok Shop, with
at least two purchases, within the past six months.
The minimum sample size was set at 150 respondents. The determination of sample size
follows the minimum requirement in PLS -SEM, which suggests using at least ten times the
maximum number of indicators used to measure a single construct (Hair et al., 2022). Given
that the largest construct in this study comprises seven indicators, the minimum requisite
sample size would be 70 respondents. T herefore, a total of 150 respondents is deemed
adequate to ensure the robustness and statistical validity of the findings.
3.3. Data Collection Method
Primary data were amassed through an online questionnaire administered via Google
Forms. The questionnaire items were formulated based on measurement indicators adapted
from antecedent studies and were translated into Bahasa Indonesia to ensure lucidity and
comprehensibility for respondents. All indicators are gauged using a five-point Likert scale (1
= strongly disagree to 5 = strongly agree), a scale extensively employed in consumer behavior
research to capture perceptions and attitudes with greater precision (Hair et al., 2022).
The questionnaire was disseminated through social media platforms, including TikTok,
Instagram, and WhatsApp groups, targeting respondents who satisfied the inclusion criteria
delineated in Section 3.2. Participation was voluntary, and respondents were apprised that
their responses would remain confidential.
This approach was selected due  to its alignment with the digital nature of the research
context and its efficiency in reaching relevant respondents. Online survey methods are
particularly suitable for studies involving digital consumers because they enable efficient data
collection and broad access to internet-active populations.

3.5. Data Analysis Techniques
Data analysis was conducted employing Partial Least Squares Structural Equation
Modeling (PLS-SEM) with the aid of SmartPLS software, a method aptly suited for predictive
research with relatively circumscribed sample sizes (approximately 100 to 150 respond ents),
intricate structural models involving multiple mediation effects, and data that do not
necessitate stringent assumptions of normal distribution (Hair et al., 2024).
Once these criteria were satisfied, the structural model was appraised to test the
hypothesized relationships amongst latent variables, with the coefficient of determination
(R²) employed to gauge explanatory power and the bootstrapping procedure employed for
hypothesis testing, a hypothesis being deemed supported when the t -statistic exceeds 1.96 at
a 5% significance level (p < 0.05) (Hair et al., 2024). Mediation effects were scrutinized by
assessing the significance of specific indirect effects through bootstrapping, deemed

4.2. Discussion
4.2.1. The Effect of Content Quality on Purchase Intention
The results show that content quality has a positive and significant effect on purchase
intention (β = 0.295; p < 0.001). Th is finding indicates that the more informative, visually
appealing, relevant, and authentic the affiliate content presented on TikTok, the stronger Gen
Z's intention to buy the promoted products. In the context of affiliate marketing, content
quality relates not only to the attractiveness of the presentation but also to the content's ability
to convey product information clearly and in line with consumers' needs. Relevant information
helps consumers understand a product's characteristics and benefits, while visually appealing
presentation can increase attention to the promotional message. Authenticity is also
important because content that looks genuine and not exaggerated can make promotional

69
information easier for consumers to accept. Content quality can t hus act as a stimulus that
directs consumers' attention, understanding, and evaluation of the product until purchase
intention is formed.
The S-O-R framework positions content quality as an external stimulus that consumers
receive through TikTok affiliate content and then process through internal evaluation before
forming purchase intention. The positive relationship found suggests that the quality of the
stimulus is associated with consumers' tendency to consider making a purchase. Relevant,
easily understood content helps reduce the difficulty of obtaining product information, while
visual elements and authenticity enhance the appeal and credibility of the message. These
characteristics matter for Gen Z, who interact intensively with digital content, so th e
effectiveness of affiliate content depends not only on promotional frequency but also on its
ability to integrate relevant, clear, appealing, and authentic information. This finding is
consistent with prior research (Al -Qudah, 2020; Martin et al., 2024; Asadiyah et al., 2023)
showing that content quality plays a role in shaping consumer responses in social commerce
environments. Practically, affiliate creators need to present product information that is
specific, relevant, and authentic, including reasona ble demonstrations or accounts of usage
experience, while avoiding exaggerated claims. For brands, providing accurate product
information that creators can easily use can support the creation of high -quality content.
Content quality therefore functions not  only as a promotional instrument but also as an
informational resource that helps consumers evaluate products and form purchase intention.
4.2.2. The Effect of Content Quality on Trust
The results show that content quality has a positive and significant effect on trust (β =
0.728; p < 0.001), indicating that content quality is a very important factor in shaping
consumers' trust in affiliates. Content that is consistent, transparent, info rmative, relevant,
and authentic gives consumers a stronger basis for judging whether the information conveyed
by an affiliate can be trusted. In the context of TikTok affiliate marketing, content quality
becomes even more important because consumers obtain product information mainly through
short, visual digital presentations. Content that shows real product use, provides explanations
suited to the product's characteristics, and presents the product's strengths and limitations
proportionately can increase consumers' confidence that the affiliate conveys information in
an accountable manner.
The S-O-R framework positions content quality as a stimulus that influences consumers'
internal states, one of which is trust. The positive relationship found suggests t hat clarity,
relevance, and authenticity in delivering information give consumers a basis for assessing the
affiliate's credibility. Conversely, content that is overly promotional, lacks substantive
information, or contains inaccurate claims can weaken trust. Trust is thus influenced not only
by the presence of the affiliate as a communicator but also by the quality of the information
and the way the message is delivered. This finding reinforces Yuliana and Wahyudi (2021) on
the importance of content qualit y in building trust on digital platforms. Practically, affiliate
creators need to prioritize real product demonstrations, specific information, balanced
evaluations, and transparency, while brands need to ensure that the information provided to
affiliates is accurate and consistent. These strategies can strengthen the quality of the stimulus
and support the formation of trust in digital marketing interactions.
4.2.3. The Effect of Trust on Purchase Intention
The results show that trust has a positive and significant effect on purchase intention (β
= 0.218; p = 0.045). This finding indicates that consumers with higher trust in an affiliate tend
to have a stronger intention to buy the promoted products. In the context of TikTok affiliate

information but also whether that information can be trusted and whether the affiliate conveys
it honestly. When consumers regard an affiliate as a trustworthy source of information, the
uncertainty of evaluating products through digital media becomes easier to manage, giving
consumers a stronger basis for considering a purchase.
In the S-O-R framework, trust acts as an internal state of the consumer that forms after
receiving and evaluating sti muli from the environment, including information delivered
through affiliate content, and is then associated with the response of purchase intention. Trust

the affiliate and the consumer. Practically, affiliate creators need to keep their information
consistent, describe their usage experience reasonably, avoid claims that cannot be verified,
and be open about the product's characteristics and the affiliate relationship. Brands also need
to ensure that the product information used in affiliate content is accurate and consistent with
the product consumers receive. These efforts can strengthen consumer trust and create
conditions more conducive to the formation of purchase intention.
4.2.4. The Mediating Role of Perceived Risk between Content Quality and
Purchase Intention
The specific indirect effects analysis shows that perceived risk significantly mediates the
relationship between content quality and purchase i ntention (β = 0.079; p = 0.031). This
finding indicates that the effect of content quality on purchase intention operates not only
directly but also through how consumers evaluate uncertainty and potential risk before
making a purchase. Affiliate content t hat provides clearer, more transparent, relevant, and
detailed product information can give consumers a more adequate basis for assessing the
condition of the product and weighing the consequences of purchase. With more complete
information, consumers have more room to evaluate the product rationally, so the uncertainty
arising from limited information in digital transactions can be managed better.
In the S-O-R framework, content quality acts as a stimulus that influences consumers'
internal states, one of which operates through perceived risk, before producing the response
of purchase intention. When content provides adequate information about a product's
characteristics, benefits, usage instruc tions, and condition, consumers can form risk
assessments based on clearer information.  Yet limited or insufficiently transparent
information can heighten uncertainty and make consumers more cautious when considering
a purchase. The position of perceived risk as a mediator therefore shows that content quality
serves not only to attract consumers' attention but also to help shape the evaluation process
that determines whether consumers feel confident enough to proceed with their purchase
intention.
This finding is consistent with Akram et al. (2021), who emphasized the importance of
risk evaluation in purchase decisions in online and social commerce environments. Practically,
affiliate creators need to present product information completely and transparently, including
product condition, specifications, usage instructions, and relevant usage experience, without
making exaggerated claims. Brands can support this process by providing accurate product
information, clear guarantees or return policies, and transact ion protection mechanisms.

4.2.5. The Mediating Role of Perceived Risk between Trust and Purchase
Intention
The results show that perceived risk significantly mediates the relationship between
trust and purchase intention (β = 0.164; p = 0.013). This finding indicates that consumers'
trust in an affiliate is related not only to a direct increase in purchase intention but also to how
consumers assess uncertainty and potential risk in the purchasing process. When consumers
trust an affiliate, product information tends to be more readily accepted as a basis for
consideration, so uncertainty about product quality, the accuracy of the information, and the
transaction can become more manageable. This condition can ultimately support the
formation of purchase intention.
In the S -O-R framework, trust and perceived risk can be understood as interrelated
internal pro cesses of the consumer in shaping the behavioral response. Trust provides
confidence in the information and the communicator, while perceived risk represents
consumers' evaluation of uncertainty and the possible unwanted consequences of a purchase.
Once trust in the affiliate is established, consumers have a stronger basis for accepting and
evaluating the information delivered, so their assessment of transaction uncertainty can
become more manageable. The position of perceived risk as a mediator therefore s hows that
the effect of trust on purchase intention also operates through consumers' internal evaluation
process before a purchase decision is formed.
Practically, these findings suggest that efforts to build trust in affiliate marketing need
to be accompa nied by the provision of information that helps consumers evaluate purchase
risk. Affiliate creators can keep their information consistent, provide transparent reviews,
show real product use, and avoid claims that cannot be verified. Brands can reinforce t his

transaction mechanisms that protect consumers. The trust formed through interaction with
an affiliate thus does not stop at confidence in the information sourc e but can also help
consumers deal with the uncertainty that arises before making any purchase.

5. Conclusion
This study analyzes the effect of TikTok affiliate content quality on the purchase
intention of Generation Z consumers for fashion products, with tr ust and perceived risk as
psychological mechanisms in decision -making on TikTok Shop. The PLS -SEM results show
that affiliate content quality has a positive and significant effect on purchase intention and
trust, while trust has a significant effect on purchase intention. Perceived risk is also shown to
mediate the relationship between content quality and purchase intention, as well as between
trust and purchase intention. Theoretically, these findings extend the application of the
Stimulus-Organism-Response (S-O-R) framework in the social commerce context by showing
that content quality, as a stimulus, shapes psychological processes through trust and perceived
risk before producing the response of purchase intention. Managerially, the findings offer
guidance for TikTok affiliates and fashion brands to produce content that is relevant, clear,
visually appealing, and authentic in order to strengthen credibility, build trust, and reduce
consumers' perceived risk. Affiliates need to convey product information tr ansparently and
consistently, while brands may consider affiliate credibility in their marketing strategies on
TikTok Shop.
