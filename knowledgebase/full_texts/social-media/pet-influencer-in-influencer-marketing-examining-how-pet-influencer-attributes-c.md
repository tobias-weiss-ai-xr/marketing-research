Abstract : The development of digital marketing has introduced social media influencers (SMIs) as
key players in shaping consumer perceptions and behaviors. Although human influencers remain
more familiar, pet influencers, a specific type of SMI, also hold strong potential to influence customer
responses. However, research on pet influencers remains limited. Although the elaboration
likelihood model (ELM) has been widely applied to explain persuasion in huma n influencer
marketing, its applicability to non -human influencers remains largely unexplored. Using ELM, this
study examines the effectiveness of pet influencers in shaping audiences' purchase intention and
perceived sincerity through three proposed attri butes: authenticity, attractiveness, and
anthropomorphism. Data were collected from 157 audiences who had viewed pet influencer content
at least three times within the past year and analyzed using partial least squares structural equation
modeling (PLS-SEM). The results show that anthropomorphism is the only attribute related to both
purchase intention and perceived sincerity. Authenticity is directly related to perceived sincerity
but not purchase intention, while attractiveness shows no relation to either  outcome. Perceived
sincerity itself does not translate into purchase intention. These results suggest that persuasion in
the pet influencer context is driven more by the ELM peripheral route, centered on emotional and
symbolic cues such as anthropomorphis m, than by the central route typically associated with
authenticity in human influencer marketing. As most measurement items were adapted from human
influencer research, they may not fully capture pet influencer traits, and future studies are
encouraged to develop and validate measures specific to the pet influencer context.

purchase intention by disseminating brand information through their content (Chu,
2023). Audiences respond positively to influencer content, with approximately 68% of

Through their content, influencers can enhance a brand’s image, fostering a closer

Indonesia, brands have begun to recognize the potential of influencers in bridging the gap
between customer engagement and interaction, as evidenced by the fact that around 26%

new types of influencers, one of which is the petfluencer.
Although the petfluencer trend is on the rise (Chitrakorn, 2022) , the term still
sounds unfamiliar to some. Petfluencers are pets  that maintain social media profiles
similar to human influencers, have followers, and can endorse brands. While petfluencers
are primarily known for their visual presence in content, these accounts are indeed
managed by their human owners (Lavertu et al., 2025) . Petfluencers are becoming
increasingly effective in promoting brands (Ngai, 2023), as they can enhance emotional
connections with humans (Lass-Hennemann et al., 2022) . They express happiness that
fosters interpersonal closeness and increases customer engagement (Maddox, 2021) .
However, high levels of distrust toward social media influencers have become a challenge
for influencer marketing effectiveness (Katadata, 2025). Influencers are often perceived
as over-endorsing brands, which triggers audience skepticism (Ki et al., 2023). In contrast,
petfluencers are perceived as more credible than human influencers (Cioccio et al., 2024).
A survey by MarkPlus found that a cheerful personality is the most trusted influencer trait,
with a share of 31% (Validnews, 2022) . This aligns  with the cheerful and joyful
characteristics commonly attributed to petfluencers, highlighting the marketing potential
they possess (Lavertu et al., 2025) . In Indonesia, the growth of petfluencers is also
significant. According to The Jakarta Post (2021) , several petfluencer accounts  such as
Pororo (@pororoofficial96) , Kaori (@fluffykaori), and Duffy (@duffythepoodle)  have
gained millions of views and followers, earning incomes similar to those of human
influencers.
Nevertheless, the success of petfluencer content in influencing audiences is not
solely dependent on the number of followers or viewers, but also on several other factors
such as social presence (Jacobson et al., 2022) , attractiveness  and perceived sincerity
(Lavertu et al., 2025; Wei et al., 2025) , and audience engagement (Myers et al., 2024) .
Compared to human influencers who are often perceived as less credible (Cioccio et al.,
2024), petfluencers are viewed as more sincere in their content delivery (Lavertu et al.,
2025). AlFarraj et al. (2021)  found that attractiveness has a positive effect on purchase
intention. In the case of petfluencers, their appealing and adorable appearance creates a
joyful atmosphere that drives audience intention (Wei et al., 2025). These findings suggest
that authenticity, attractiveness, and anthropomorphism are essential factors in
determining petfluencer effectiveness. However, r esearch on petfluencers remains
limited, and existing studies are predominantly centered on human influencers  (Cioccio
et al., 2024; Hariningsih et al., 2024; Hossain et al., 2025; Jacobson et al., 2022; Lavertu et
al., 2025; Myers et al., 2024; Ren et al., 2023; Shoenberger & Kim, 2023; Wei et al., 2025).
More specifically, although the Elaboration L ikelihood Model has been widely applied to
explain persuasion processes in human influencer marketing, its applicability to non -
human influencers such as petfluencers remains largely unexplored. It is therefore
unclear whether the central and peripheral ro utes of persuasion identified in human
influencer contexts operate in the same way when the source of the message is a pet

To address this gap, this study draws on the Elaboration Likelihood Model (ELM) as
its theoretical framework. The ELM explains that there are two primary routes in the
persuasion process: the central route, which focuses on rational evaluation, and the
peripheral route, whic h emphasizes emotional responses (Bitner & Obermiller, 1985) .
Individuals tend to process information more carefully when they are highly involved,
such as when they are actively in need of the endorsed product. Conversely, when
involvement is low, individuals are more likely to rely on external cues like appearance
rather than the content of the message itself (Teng et al., 2014). In marketing contexts, the
ELM has been widely used as a theory of persuasion in previous studies (Chang et al.,
2015; Liao & Huang, 2021; Markowitz, 2020; Teng et al., 2014)  to identify behavioral
changes driven by message reception (Akgun et al., 2017) . When a petfluencer is
perceived as attractive by the audience, it tends to incr ease emotional engagement. This
aligns with Wei et al. (2025) , who noted that, according to the ELM, attractiveness
influences message reception through the peripheral route. The attractiveness displayed
by petfluencers  functions as an external factor that elicits audience responses without
requiring deep cognitive processing of the message content (Hariningsih et al., 2024) .
Similarly, anthropomorphism can trigger emotional responses in the audience.
In contrast, authenticity operates through the central route of persuasion. When
consumers are in urgent need of a product, they are more likely to pay rational attention
to the quality and relevance of the message (Hossain et al., 2025). Authenticity becomes a
critical issue as it is often scrutinized by the audience when viewing promotional content
(Amini, 2025). The ELM offers a framework for understanding how message processing
influences audience behavior (Hossain et al., 2025) . In the context of petfluencers
promoting brands through content, both the central and peripheral routes of persuasion,
as outlined in the ELM, play a significant role in shaping perceived sincerity and purchase
intention.
Building on this theoretical foundation, the present study examines three key
petfluencer attributes: authenticity, attractiveness, and anthropomorphism. Authenticity
refers to the perceptions that some thing is genuine, valid, and reliable in the eyes of
followers. In the digital context, it is often understood as presenting oneself authentically
to netizens, encompassing both the sender's identity and how that identity is reflected in
communication and behavior (Abdullah et al., 2024; Barta & Andalibi, 2021; Eigenraam
et al., 2021) . Prior studies emphasize that authenticity is not an objecti ve trait but a
perception shaped by audience beliefs within a specific social context, and that it tends to
emerge more strongly when senders engage in explicit self-presentation, such as through
story features and posts (Kreling et al., 2022; Morhart et al., 2015) . Authentic messages
are generally more persuasive because they allow audiences to identify with the sender
and the meaning of the message, particularly when the content aligns naturally with the
influencer's everyday life rather than appearing as overt promotion  (Pöyry et al., 2019;
Ren et al., 2023) . In the context of pet influencers  specifically, the presenc e of animal
characters in promotional content has been shown to  strengthen emotional bonds
between consumers and the promoted product , and higher perceived authenticity has
been linked to a greater likelihood of purchase intention (Hänninen, 2021; Ren et al.,
2023). Based on this reasoning, the following hypotheses are proposed:
H1: Authenticity has a significant positive influence on purchase intention

H2: Authenticity has a significant positive influence on perceived sincerity
Attractiveness refers to the extent to which consumers perceive an individual or
object as able to attract attention and build emotional connections and affection (E. H.
Kim, 2022) . An influencer's attractiveness is one of the main aspects consumers pay
attention to (Davis & Berlianto, 2025). According to AlFarraj et al. (2021), attractiveness
is a key factor in delivering valuable messages, and influencers with a high level of
attractiveness are more likely to shape their followers' purchase intentions. This aligns
with research by Bai et al. (2024), which reveals that a seller's attractiveness can influence
consumer perceptions by capturing attention and encouraging behavioral responses that
lead to increased purchase intention. Furthermore, halo -effect theory suggests that
individuals with attractive appearances are generally evaluated more positively overall;
thus, attractiveness can influence perceptions of an influencer's quality (Kaplan, 1978).
Social psychologists argue that attractiveness often triggers stereotypes, leading to a halo
effect in which attractive individuals are perceived more positively overall (Shang &
Zhang, 2025) , which may subsequently be interpreted as an indicator of perceived
sincerity. When applied to influencers, attractiveness can help shape positive evaluations
that extend to the opinions and brand affiliations they convey (Hariningsih et al., 2024) .
In the context of pet influencers, they tend to perceive positive responses from audiences
because they are perceived as more appealing and capable of telling stories through the
role of humans as the narrator behind the context (Khalisa et al., 2025). For example, pet
influencer Instagram accounts are often seen as promoting happiness rather than toxicity,
thereby both shaping and being shaped by broader social and economic dynamics
(Maddox, 2021) . In terms of content, attractiveness positively influences purchase
intention. This is supported by the findings of Weismueller et al. (2020) , which
demonstrate that an influencer's attractiveness positively affects consumers' purchase
intentions. Therefore, the following hypotheses are proposed:
H3: Attractiveness has a significant positive influence on purchase intention
H4: Attractiveness has a significant positive influence on perceived sincerity
Anthropomorphism is the tendency to assign human -like emotions and actions to
animals or objects. For some individuals, it represents a way to express empathy, connect
more deeply with animals, and show attentiveness to their well -being (Mota-Rojas et al.,
2021). Humans have a psychological inclination to treat non-human entities like animals,
machines, plants, or even supernatural and natural phenomena as if they were human
beings (Festerling & Siraj, 2022) . It involves attributing human -like features, emotions,
thoughts, and behaviors to non-human objects (Ali et al., 2021). Research by Seeger et al.
(2021) shows that increased human likeness in objects enhances perceived liability,
which reflects a sense of connection to anthropomorphism. Studies have explored
consumer responses to various strategies employed in pet social media accounts,
including the use of anthropomorphism (Yoo et al., 2024) . Audiences who
anthropomorphize petfluencers perceive them as more sincere (Lavertu et al., 2025). Lu
(2022) states that advertising appeals involving elements of anthropomorphism can
positively enhance purchase intentions. However, in the study conducted by Wang & Park
(2023), anthropomorphism did not have an indirect effect on the audience's purchase
intention. Based on this reasoning, the following hypotheses are proposed:
H5: Anthropomorphism has a significant positive influence on purchase intention
H6: Anthropomorphism has a significant positive influence on perceived sincerity

Perceived sincerity refers to the audience's judgment of an influencer's honesty,
sincerity, and non-manipulative intentions (M. Lee & Park, 2023). Sincerity plays a crucial
role in building closeness between an influencer and their audience, and it can serve as a
distinctive advantage that sets the influencer apart from others (J. A. Lee & Eastin, 2020).
People who are perceived as sincere are generally believed to possess good moral
character, making sincerity a vital aspect of creating a positive first impression (Lavertu
et al., 2025). According to Page and Clementson (2023), perceived sincerity can influence
how the audience interprets messages. Supporting this, Zhafira et al.  (2022) found that
products promoted by sincere influencers are more likely to generate positive attitudes
toward the brand than those promoted by influencers perceived as less sincere. A
previous study stated that perceived sincerity has a positive and significa nt effect on
customer purchase intention (Demirel & Erdogmus, 2016). Contrary to the prior findings,
J. A. Lee & E astin (2020) showed that perceived sincerity has no significant relationship
with purchase intention.
Based on these considerations, the following hypotheses are proposed:
H7: Perceived sincerity has a significant positive influence on purchase intention
H8: Perceived sincerity mediates the relationship between authenticity and
purchase intention
H9: Perceived sincerity mediates the relationship between attractiveness and
purchase intention
H10: Perceived sincerity mediates the relationship between anthropomorphism
and purchase intention
Purchase intention refers to the extent to which consumers express a desire or
tendency to purchase a product (Peña-García et al., 2020). Santiago and Serralha (2022)
define purchase intention as a consumer's tendency or initial intention to purchase in the
near future, regardless of whether they have already been exposed to the product or
criticism related to it. Before making a purchase decision, consumers generally seek
information first to ensure that the product aligns with their needs and preferences
(Moslehpour et al., 2021) . Reviews or recommendations from influencers, friends, and
family members are considered one of the strongest factors in influencing consumers'
purchase intentions (Meliawati et al., 2023) . Social media influencers have proven
effective in influencing consumer purchase intentions, as shown by reports indicating that
50% of companies choose to use their services in promotional activities (Kurdi et al.,
2022). Interestingly, not all social media influencers are human; some are pets whose
social media accounts are managed by their owners (Myers et al., 2024) . This
phenomenon has attracted well -known brands such as Budweiser, Google, Subar u, and
The Body Shop to include pet influencers as part of their marketing strategy. This
indicates that pet influencers are not limited to promoting pet care products but are also
effective in a broader marketing context (Myers et al., 2024) . When audiences view
content posted by pet influencers, they tend to experience curiosity as a natural reaction,
which in turn can shape attitudes and encourage their purchase intention (Hänninen,
2021). Therefore, this study aims to examine th e effectiveness of petfluencers by
investigating authenticity, anthropomorphism, and attractiveness as independent
variables that influence purchase intention through perceived sincerity as a mediating
variable, using the Elaboration Likelihood Model as the theoretical framework.

RESEARCH METHODS
Research Design
This study adopts a quantitative research design to examine the influence of
petfluencer attributes on purchase intention and perceived sincerity. The research
examines both direct and indirect effects through statistical modeling. Indonesia's
internet penetration rate increased to 79.5% in 2024, with Java Island holding the highest
rate at 83.64% based on region (Katadata, 2024). According to the Indonesian Pet Food
Association (AMHKI), the pet food sector grew by 60% in the Java Island market (Aldianto
& Noviani, 2023). The high number of internet users and the pet food market in Java Island
create opportunities for an increase in the number of followers of petfluencer accounts.
Given the penetration rate and the level of the pet food market, Java Island serves as a
relevant sample for our study. Data were collected through an online survey targeting
respondents in Java Island, Indonesia, who met our three purposive sampling criteria: (1)
aged 18 or older, (2) residing in Java Island, and (3) having been exposed to petfluencer
content at least three times within the past year. The minimum sample size was calculated
using the minimum five observations per indicator rule (Hair et al., 2010). The minimum
sample size is 135, and we collected 157 samples for this research. The profiles of
respondents are shown in Table 1.
Measures
Petfluencers, as one type of social media influencer, can influence audiences'
behavioral intentions (Wei et al., 2025) . In this study, the variables tested include
authenticity, attractiveness, anthropomorphism, perceived sincerity, and purchase
intention. The items for these variables, adapted from previous studies, were measured
using a 5-point Likert scale ranging from “strongly disagree” (1) to “strongly agree” (5).
Data analysis method
The data were analyzed using partial least squares structural equation modeling
(PLS-SEM) with SmartPLS 4 software. PLS-SEM was chosen for its suitability in estimating
complex models with latent variables and its stability with small to medium sample sizes.

Discussion
This study examined how petfluencer attributes, namely authenticity,
attractiveness, and anthropomorphism, influence audiences’ purchase  intention and
perceived sincerity using the Elaboration Likelihood Model (ELM) as the underlying
theoretical framework. Overall, anthropomorphism emerged as the most influential
attribute, significantly shaping both outcomes, whereas authenticity affected  only
perceived sincerity, attractiveness showed no significant effect on either outcome, and
perceived sincerity itself did not translate into purchase intention, leaving all indirect
effects insignificant.  These findings provide important insights into how persuasion
mechanisms may operate differently in the context of non-human influencers.
One of the most notable findings of this study is the strong influence of
anthropomorphism on audiences’ purchase intention. Anthropomorphism refers to the
attribution of human-like characteristics, emotions, and behaviors to non-human entities,
including animals. In the context of petfluencers, anthropomorphism is often manifested
through captions written from the pet’s perspective, emotional storytelling, facial
expressions interpreted as human emotions, and visual representations that portray pets
as having personalities similar to humans. The results indicate that audiences responded
positively to these human-like portrayals, leading to stronger emotional attachment and
higher purchase intention.
This finding supports previous studies suggesting that ant hropomorphism can
strengthen emotional closeness and consumer engagement (Ali et al., 2021; Lu, 2022;

The significance of anthropomorphism also indicates th at audiences tend to
perceive anthropomorphized pets as socially meaningful actors rather than merely
animals appearing in promotional content. This pattern can be explained more precisely
through the concepts of perceived social presence and symbolic comp anionship. When a
petfluencer is portrayed with human -like emotions, intentions, and personality traits,
audiences may experience a stronger sense that the pet is psychologically present as a
social actor rather than a passive object, reflecting perceived social presence. At the same
time, this human -like portrayal allows audiences to relate to the petfluencer as a
companion figure rather than merely a promotional character, reflecting a form of
symbolic companionship that mirrors the growing emotional inte gration of pets into
human social life, where pets are increasingly viewed as family members . Consequently,
when audiences perceive petfluencers as possessing emotions, intentions, and
personalities, they become more psychologically connected to them , and this emotional
proximity, grounded in perceived social presence and symbolic companionship,  may
explain why anthropomorphism significantly influenced both purchase intention and
perceived sincerity.
Interestingly, anthropomorphism also significantly influe nced perceived sincerity.
This suggests that human -like portrayals of pets may increase the perception that
petfluencer content is more genuine, honest, and emotionally sincere. This finding
extends previous research by Lavertu et al. (2025) , who argued that audiences tend to
perceive petfluencers as more trustworthy and sincere compared to human influencers.
One possible explanation is that audiences generally assume pets do not possess
manipulative intentions in the same way humans do. As a result, promotional content
delivered through petfluencers may be perceived as less commercially motivated and
more emotionally authentic.
Turning to authenticity, its effect was more limited than that of anthropomorphism,
remaining significant only for perceived sincerity. This finding partially supports previous
influencer marketing studies that emphasize authenticity as a critical driver of audience
trust and engagement (Hasan et al., 2024; Liu & Zheng, 2024) . In this study, audiences
indeed perceived authentic petfluencer content as more sincere, suggesting that
authenticity remains important for  shaping positive audience perceptions. However,
authenticity alone was insufficient to directly stimulate purchase intention.
From the perspective of ELM, authenticity is conventionally assumed to operate
through the central route of persuasion, in which audiences cognitively evaluate whether
the petfluencer content appears genuine, consistent, and believable. However, the fact
that authenticity only influenced perceived sincerity and not purchase intention raises
the question of whether central route proc essing is actually activated when audiences
encounter petfluencer content. Because petfluencers are fundamentally non -human
entities, audiences may not rely heavily on rational, message -based evaluation when

Another unexpected finding concerns attractiven ess, which showed no significant
effect on either outcome. Previous studies have consistently argued that attractiveness
positively influences persuasive effectiveness through the halo effect mechanism, where
attractive endorsers are evaluated more positiv ely overall (AlFarraj et al., 2021; Kaplan,
1978). In the context of human influencers, physical attractiveness oft en functions as a
strong peripheral cue that increases attention, admiration, and behavioral intention.
However, the present study suggests that attractiveness may not function similarly
in the petfluencer context. This pattern can be interpreted through t he lens of a ceiling
effect, in which most pets featured as petfluencers are already perceived as inherently
cute or visually appealing, leaving little variation for attractiveness to explain. Related to
this, cuteness saturation may also be at play, where  repeated exposure to visually
appealing pet content across social media reduces its novelty and persuasive impact over
time. Together, these mechanisms point toward a broader process of aesthetic
normalization, in which visual appeal has become a default expectation for petfluencer
content rather than a distinguishing feature.  In other words, attractiveness may have
become a baseline expectation rather than a distinctive attribute capable of driving
persuasion. Audiences may instead prioritize emotional storytelling, humor, personality,
or anthropomorphic characteristics over physical appearance alone.
This finding also indicates that the persuasive effectiveness of petfluencers may
depend less on aesthetic presentation and more on emotional connection and symbolic
interaction. While attractive visual appearances may attract initial attention, they may not
necessarily translate into deeper perceptions of sincerity or stronger purchase intentions.
Consequently, marketers relying solely on visually appealing p et content without
meaningful emotional engagement may fail to generate persuasive marketing outcomes.
Perhaps the most theoretically intriguing finding of this study is the insignificant
influence of perceived sincerity on purchase intention. This result contradicts many
previous influencer marketing studies suggesting that sincerity and trustworthiness
positively affect consumer purchasing behavior (Demirel & Erdogmus, 2016; Song & Shin,
2024). Although audiences perceived certain petfl uencers as sincere, this perception did
not significantly influence their intention to purchase endorsed products.
Several explanations may account for this phenomenon. First, audiences may engage
with petfluencer content primarily for entertainment, emoti onal gratification, or
parasocial enjoyment rather than for commercial decision-making purposes. Rather than
approaching petfluencer content as a source of purchase-relevant information, audiences
may consume it more as a parasocial relationship, in which the primary reward is the
emotional connection and enjoyment of following the pet's activities, rather than an
intention to act on any promotional message it carries. Petfluencer accounts are often
associated with humor, cuteness, relaxation, and emotional  comfort. Consequently,
audiences may emotionally appreciate and trust the content without necessarily
developing purchasing intentions.
Second, perceived sincerity may not be sufficient to generate purchase intention in
low-involvement product contexts. A ccording to ELM, behavioral outcomes are
influenced not only by positive perceptions of the message source but also by personal

Third, this finding may suggest that the persuasive mechanisms underlying human

It is also worth noting that the proposed model explained only 27.8 percent of the
variance in purchase intention (R² = 0.278), indicating that a su bstantial proportion of
audience purchase intention remains unaccounted for by authenticity, attractiveness,
anthropomorphism, and perceived sincerity combined. This relatively low explanatory
power suggests that other determinants not captured in the pres ent model may play a
more decisive role in shaping purchase intention toward petfluencer-promoted products,
such as parasocial interaction, emotional attachment to the pet, trust toward the endorsed
brand, and the entertainment value of the content itself.  Incorporating these variables in
future petfluencer studies may help to account for the unexplained variance and provide
a more complete picture of how petfluencer marketing translates into actual purchase
behavior.
Overall, the findings of this study contribute to the growing literature on influencer
marketing by demonstrating that persuasion mechanisms in petfluencer contexts differ
from those observed in human influencer contexts. The study highlights the importance
of anthropomorphism as the dominant p ersuasive attribute in petfluencer marketing. It
challenges the assumption that traditional influencer attributes, such as attractiveness
and authenticity, operate identically across human and non-human influencer categories.
From a theoretical perspective, this study extends the application of ELM into non -
human influencer contexts by illustrating how emotional and symbolic cues may
outweigh rational evaluation in shaping audience responses toward petfluencer content.
The findings suggest that the peripher al route of persuasion may be more dominant in
petfluencer marketing, particularly through anthropomorphic and emotionally engaging
content. At the same time, the results also indicate limitations in applying traditional
influencer marketing constructs dir ectly to petfluencer contexts without contextual
adaptation.

CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS
This study examined the influence of petfluencer  attributes, namely authenticity,
attractiveness, and anthropomorphism, on audiences' purchase intention and perceived
sincerity using the Elaboration Likelihood Model as the theoretical foundation. The
findings demonstrate that anthropomorphism is the mos t influential attribute in
petfluencer marketing, significantly affecting both purchase intention and perceived
sincerity. Authenticity positively influenced perceived sincerity but did not directly affect
purchase intention. Meanwhile, attractiveness did not significantly affect either perceived
sincerity or purchase intention. In addition, perceived sincerity itself did not significantly
influence purchase intention, resulting in insignificant mediating effects across all indirect
relationships. These findings indicate that emotional and symbolic characteristics play a
more important role than conventional influencer attributes in shaping audience
responses toward petfluencer content.
The study contributes theoretically by extending influencer marketing research into
the context of non-human influencers and demonstrating that persuasion mechanisms in
petfluencer marketing differ from those commonly found in human influencer studies.
More specifically, the findings extend the application of the Elaboration Likelihood Model
by showing that the peripheral route, rather than the central route, appears to dominate
persuasion in the petfluencer context. While authenticity, a construct conventionally
associated with central route processing, only shaped perceived s incerity without
translating into purchase intention, anthropomorphism, an emotionally and symbolically
driven cue, significantly influenced both outcomes. This pattern suggests that when the
message source is a non -human entity, audiences may rely less on  rational, credibility-
based evaluation and more on emotional and symbolic cues such as perceived social
presence and symbolic companionship, indicating that the relative weight of ELM's two
routes can shift depending on the nature of the persuasion source . The findings suggest
that audiences are more responsive to emotional engagement and human-like portrayals
of pets than to visual attractiveness or authenticity alone. Practically, the results imply
that marketers and brands should focus on developing emo tionally engaging and
anthropomorphic petfluencer content rather than relying solely on aesthetic appeal.
Creating narratives that portray pets as relatable social actors may enhance audience
connection and improve marketing effectiveness.
This study also presents several recommendations for future research. Future
studies are encouraged to explore additional variables that may better explain audiences’
purchase intention in petfluencer contexts, such as emotional attachment, trust,
entertainment value, par asocial interaction, and content quality. Researchers are also
encouraged to develop measurement instruments specifically designed for petfluencer
contexts, considering that constructs adapted from human influencer studies may not
fully capture audience pe rceptions toward non -human influencers. In addition, future
