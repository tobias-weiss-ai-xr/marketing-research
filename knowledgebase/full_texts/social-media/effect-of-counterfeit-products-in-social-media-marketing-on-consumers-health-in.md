Abstract
The rapid growth of digital platforms has transformed marketing practices globally, providing
unprecedented opportunities for both legitimate and illicit commerce. This paper examines the
effect of counterfeit products in social media marketing and consumer health in Bayelsa state.
The further examined the concept of counterfeit products, concept of social media marketing,
concept of consumer health, forms of social media marketing for counterfeiting, types of
counterfeit products, proliferation of counterfeit products on social media , consumer behavior
factors facilitating circulation and health risks of counterfeit products. The study highlights key
consumer behavior factors, including price sensitivity, perceived trust, peer influence,
aspirational buying, and gaps in digital literacy, which contribute to the circulation and
consumption of counterfeit goods. Findings reveal significant public health risks, including
organ toxicity, therapeutic failures, and pediatric vulnerabilities, exacerbated by algorithm -
driven marketing and influencer promotions. The study underscores the urgent need for
enhanced regulatory enforcement, consumer education, and targeted interventions to mitigate
health risks and curb the spread of counterfeit products.

Keywords: Counterfeit products, social media marketing, consumer health

Concept of Counterfeit Products
Counterfeit products represent a deliberate and systematic violation of intellectual property rights
through the unauthorized manufacture and distribution of goods that imitate genuine brands. It
involves the deceptive planning, creation, and presentation of items ranging from
pharmaceuticals to luxury apparel in ways that mirror the visual identity, packaging, and
trademarks of authentic products to mislead the end user (Ahmed et al, 2020). This illicit practice
is the strategic integration of low -cost production methods, fraudulent labelling, and exploitative
supply chains to ensure that unsuspecting consumers purchase inferior substitutes under the guise
of brand quality. It encompasses activities such as trademark infringement, patent theft, and the
use of substandard raw materials aimed at generating high profit margins at the expense of
consumer safety and economic stability (Smaddar and Menon 2020). Counterfeiting constitutes a
core threat to market integrity, as it directly influences brand erosion, unfair competition, and
severe public health risks. Counterfeit products are market -distorting phenomenon that aligns
deceptive manufacturing with consumer demand for affordability, ensuring that the trade remains
both pervasive and difficult to regulate.
The International Anti -Counterfeiting Coalition (2024) considers counterfeit products as
fraudulent imitations or forgeries of trusted global brands that siphon knowledge from legitimate
companies and foster deep mistrust within the marketplace. OECD (2021) further notes that the
conceptualization of counterfeiting has shifted from simple street -corner forgeries to a highly
sophisticated global industry, providing illicit actors with opportunities to exploit digital supply
chains and reach consumers across borders. Counterfeit products are not merely the assembly of
fake goods, but a comprehensive and intentional criminal approach that undermines the efficacy
of legitimate commerce. It involves careful replication of security features, the use of toxic
chemical fillers in consumables, and the deployment of misleading digital marketing tactics that

Concept of Social Media Marketing
Social media marketing represents a deliberate and systematic process  that structures and
organizes digital engagement, brand messaging, and consumer interaction to achieve specific
commercial or educational outcomes. It involves the planning, creation, organization, and
presentation of promotional content across interactive platforms in ways that enhance brand
visibility, user engagement, and purchase intention (Kaplan & Haenlein, 2020). It is the strategic
integration of algorithmic targeting , multimedia tools, and contextual relevance to ensure that
brands acquire, influence, and retain customers effectively within virtual ecosystems. It
encompasses activities such as influencer collaborations , sponsored advertisements, viral
challenges, and real -time customer support aimed at facilitating immersive brand experiences.
Social media marketing constitutes a core element of modern market penetration, as it directly
influences consumer perception, social trust, and trend adoption. Nana (2025) emphasizes that
social media marketing is a consumer-centered process that aligns digital materials with lifestyle
aspirations, peer influence, and interactive feedback, ensuring that brand communication is both
purposeful and persuasive.
De Assis and Vilela (2024) consider social media marketing as a strategic value co -creation
process that enables businesses and individual vendors to craft narratives that are contextually
relevant and culturally resonant. Statista (2024) further notes that well -structured social media
marketing integrates data analytics with creative storytelling, providing marketers with
opportunities to experiment with real-time engagement and develop digital competencies that are
transferable across global markets. Social media marketing is not merely the assembly of digital
posts, but a comprehensive and intentional approach that enhances the efficacy of market reach.
It involves careful sequencing of content, algorithmic optimization , and the incorporation of
interactive tasks that stimulate active participation and electronic word-of-mouth.

Concept of Consumer Health
Consumer health is a deliberate and systematic state of physical, psychological, and social well -
being that is directly influenced by the safety of products consumed and the accuracy of
information guiding purchase decisions. It involves the access to, evaluation of, and use of goods
and services in ways that enhance bodily integrity, mental stability, and long -term wellness
(Adewale, 2024). It is the strategic integration of personal safety standards , regulatory
protections, and health literacy to ensure that individuals acquire and use products that are free
from toxic or hazardous contaminants. It encompasses activities such as product ingredient
scrutiny, medical consultation, the use of verified pharmaceuticals, and the avoidance of
substandard consumables aimed at maintaining a high quality of life. Consumer health
constitutes a core element of human capital development, as it directly influences economic
productivity and life expectancy. The World Health Organization  (2025) emphasizes that
consumer health aligns public safety with corporate responsibility and government oversight,
ensuring that the marketplace remains a safe environment as a right for all citizens.
Zenone et al. (2025) consider consumer health as a dynamic outcome of the commercial
determinants of health, where marketing strategies on digital platforms can either promote or

Forms of Social Media Marketing for Counterfeiting
Social media marketing platforms are digital environments where fraudulent vendors exploit
social networking technologies to advertise, promote, and distribute imitation products to
unsuspecting consumers. These platforms such as Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, WhatsApp,
Telegram, and YouTube provide mechanisms for targeted communication, peer -to-peer
engagement, and algorithmically enhanced content visibility, which counterfeit actors leverage to
mimic legitimate brands and influence consumer behavior.

Facebook: Facebook is a widely used social networking platform that enables users and
businesses to share text -based posts, images, videos, and advertisements with diverse audiences
(Smith & Anderson, 2018). It is also described as a platform that integrates complex targeting
tools and interactive features, allowing sellers to micro -segment consumer groups and promote
products that mimic authentic brands. For counterfeit vendors, Facebook’s marketplace, pages,
and sponsored ads enhance visibility and facilitate transactions that appear credible to
unsuspecting consumers (Jin & Phua, 2021).

Instagram: Instagram is a visually focused social media platform that supports photo sharing,
stories, reels, and influencer collaborations (Marwick, 2015). It is further defined as an
image-centric environment where visual appeal and trend -based content drive engagement,
enabling counterfeit sellers to replicate premium product aesthetics and brand logos. Through
curated feeds and influencer marketing, Instagram enhances perceived product legitimacy and
influences consumer behavior toward counterfeit goods.

TikTok: TikTok is a short-form video platform centered on user creativity and algorithm -driven
content discovery (Vaterlaus et al, 2021). It is also characterized as a dynamic, trend -propelling
platform where users engage with bite -sized promotional content that spreads rapidly through
“For You” feeds. Counterfeit vendors leverage TikTok’s viral nature to disseminate persuasive
product videos and demonstrations that blur the line between authentic brand messaging and
imitation offerings.

WhatsApp and Telegram:  WhatsApp and Telegram are encrypted messaging platforms that
facilitate private, direct communication between users (Obar & Oeldorf -Hirsch, 2020). These
platforms are also described as closed networks where counterfeit sellers share product catalogs,
promotional links, and purchase instructions via group chats and broadcast messages. The
discreet nature of these communication channels bolsters trust among peer networks and reduces
the visibility of counterfeit content to regulators, increasing consumer exposure to fraudulent

Types of Counterfeit Products
Counterfeit products  is categorise based on their physical composition, intended use, and
associated risks to public safety , particularly within digital marketplaces such as social media
platforms. This classification represents a deliberate and systematic process of identifying
fraudulent goods that range from life -critical medications to everyday lifestyle accessories. It
involves the planning, production, and distribution o f imitation goods including falsified
pharmaceuticals, substandard cosmetics, adulterated food products, counterfeit e lectronics, and
illicit apparel in ways that closely mimic the branding, packaging, and certific ation of trusted
manufacturers. The following categories represent the primary types of counterfeit products
currently proliferating through social media marketing:

Falsified pharmaceuticals and medical devices : This category comprise unauthorized
production of essential drugs and medical devices, including anti -malarials, antibiotics, vaccines,
and diagnostic kits. These products often contain either no active ingredients or toxic substitutes,
rendering them both ineffective and dangerous. Ojo et al. (2021) assert that the consumption of
counterfeit medicines contributes to therapeutic failures, fosters drug -resistant organisms, and
poses significant threats to public health, particularly in regions like Bayelsa State where
regulatory oversight is limited.

Substandard cosmetics and personal care products: Counterfeit cosmetics, skincare products,
and personal care items are designed to mimic established global brands while often containing
harmful chemicals. The National Agency for Food and Drug Administration and Control (2024)
reports that such products frequently include mercury, lead, hydroquinone, and other toxic
substances, which can cause severe dermatological reactions, organ damage, and long -term
toxicity. Social media platforms amplify exposure to these products by using influencer
endorsements, visually appealing content, and targeted advertisements, increasing the risk of
consumer engagement with unsafe products.

Adulterated food and beverages: This type includes counterfeit alcoholic beverages, packaged
foods, and infant formulas adulterated with industrial -grade chemicals or produced under
unsanitary conditions. OECD (2021) notes that the consumption of these products can result in
acute poisoning, metabolic disorders, and other chronic health complications. Profit -driven illicit
actors prioritize revenue over safety, and social media marketing accelerates the circulation of
these high-risk consumables.

Proliferation of Counterfeit Products on Social Media
The proliferation of counterfeit products has been significantly accelerated by the rise of social
media platforms, which provide counterfeit vendors with unprecedented access to large, targeted,
and highly engaged consumer bases. Unlike traditional marketplaces, social media enables
sellers to bypass geographical limitations and regulatory oversight, promoting imitation goods
directly to users through algorithmically curated feeds, influencer endorsements, sponsored
advertisements, and peer -to-peer sharing (Jin & Phua, 2021). This digital environment allows
counterfeit sellers to simulate the branding, packaging, and marketing techniques of legitimate
companies, making it increasingly difficult for consumers to distinguish between genuine and
fake products.
Social media platforms such as Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, WhatsApp, Telegram, and
YouTube serve as both promotional and transactional channels. Facebook and Instagram provide
visually persuasive posts, reels, and marketplace listings, while TikTok leverages short -form
viral videos to showcase counterfeit products in entertaining and relatable ways. WhatsApp and
Telegram offer encrypted, private channels for direct sales and group promotions, bypassing
traditional oversight mechanisms. YouTube enables long -form tutorials, reviews, and unboxing
videos that legitimize counterfeit products through perceived influencer credibility (De Veirman,
Cauberghe, & Hudders, 2017; Marwick, 2015). These platforms collectively form an ecosystem
that supports rapid and widespread dissemination of counterfeit goods, fostering high visibility
and perceived legitimacy among consumers.
Several factors inherent to social media facilitate this proliferation. Algorithmic targeting ensures
that promotional content reaches highly specific demographic groups based on browsing history,
interests, and engagement patterns, increasing the likelihood of consumer interaction (Vaterlaus
et al, 2021). Peer influence and social proof mechanisms, such as likes, shares, and user -
generated content, further amplify engagement by normalizing counterfeit consumption within
online communities. In addition, the influencer culture on these platforms creates aspirational
appeal, encouraging consumers to purchase counterfeit products to align with perceived social
status or lifestyle trends. The rapid circulation of counterfeit goods on social media has profound
implications for consumer behavior, particularly in contexts like Bayelsa State, where high social
media usage and limited regulatory enforcement intersect. Consumers are often exposed to a
continuous stream of persuasive content that normalizes counterfeit consumption and encourages
impulsive buying decisions. UNESCO (2021) highlights that understanding the digital

Consumer Behavior Factors Facilitating Circulation in Bayelsa State
Consumer behavior plays a critical role in the circulation of counterfeit products, particularly in
Bayelsa State, where high social media usage and limited regulatory oversight intersect. One of
the most influential factors is price sensitivity and perceived affordability . Many consumers in
Bayelsa, due to economic constraints, are attracted to counterfeit products as cheaper alternatives
to expensive branded goods. Bian and Veloutsou (2007) emphasize that the price gap between
authentic and imitation products encourages consumers to accept lower -quality substitutes,
sustaining the demand and continuous circulation of counterfeit goods in both physical markets
and online platforms.

Perceived trust in social media platforms  also facilitates the spread of counterfeit products.
Platforms like Instagram, TikTok, WhatsApp, and Facebook use algorithms to tailor content to
users’ interests, often presenting counterfeit products alongside authentic brands. This creates a
perception of legitimacy, particularly when content is endorsed by influencers or peer networks.
Kaplan and Haenlein (2020) note that social media’s ability to simulate authentic brand
messaging reduces consumers’ perception of risk and encourages engagement with potentially
harmful products. In Bayelsa State, where users rely heavily on smartphones and social media
for product information, this factor significantly increases exposure to counterfeit goods.

Social influence and peer pressure  are additional behavioral drivers in the region. Consumers
are highly influenced by online communities, family, and friends who share or endorse products
on social media. Eng and Bogaert (2021) suggest that viral content, user reviews, and influencer
promotions create social proof, normalizing counterfeit consumption and prompting others to
imitate these behaviors. In Bayelsa, where social networks are tightly knit and community
validation carries weight, peer -driven demand contributes to rapid circulation of counterfeit
goods.

Aspirational buying and brand consciousness  further facilitate counterfeit product circulation.
Consumers often purchase imitation goods to achieve perceived social status or lifestyle
alignment associated with premium brands. Wilcox, Kim, and Sen (2009) argue that even when
aware of the products’ illegitimacy, consumers are motivated by symbolic value and desire for
social recognition. In Bayelsa State, this is evident in the purchase of counterfeit cosmetics,
fashion accessories, and smartphones, which are widely advertised on social media as status
symbols.

Digital literacy and awareness gaps  among consumers contribute to the problem. Many users
lack the knowledge or skills to distinguish genuine products from counterfeit items online. Jin
and Phua (2021) highlight that limited understanding of product verification, combined with
persuasive social media marketing, makes consumers more susceptible to deception. In Bayelsa
State, this lack of digital literacy exacerbates the circulation of counterfeit goods, as residents
often rely on visual appeal, peer recommendations, or influencer endorsements rather than
verified product information.

Health Risks of Counterfeit Products
The circulation of counterfeit products in Bayelsa State  has become a major public health
concern. By August 2025, local authorities officially described the influx of fake drugs,
beverages, and cosmetics as a “serious emergency,” linking these products to a marked increase
in chronic organ diseases, preventable illnesses, and pediatric fatalities (GazetteNG, 2025). The
proliferation of these goods through physical markets, such as Swali Market in Yenagoa, and
digital platforms, including Instagram, TikTok, WhatsApp, and Facebook, has created
widespread consumer exposure to toxic substances. These products, though visually
indistinguishable from authentic brands, often contain harmful additives, incorrect active
ingredients, or completely inert compounds, posing severe health risks.
One of the most immediate and serious health consequences of counterfeit products in Bayelsa is
critical organ failure , particularly affecting the liver and kidneys. Local health reports from the
Federal Medical Centre (FMC), Yenagoa  reveal an alarming rise in hospital admissions for
kidney and liver dysfunction, which clinicians attribute directly to the consumption of
substandard beverages, counterfeit pharmaceuticals, and cosmetics (Instagram, 2025).
Counterfeit beverages, including soft drinks, wine, and bottled water, have been found to contain
industrial-grade chemicals and heavy metals such as lead and arsenic. These toxic additives
directly impact liver and kidney function, potentially leading to acute poisoning or chronic organ
damage over time. Similarly, counterfeit cosmetics and personal care products , such as skin -
lightening creams, hair relaxers, and soaps, contain hazardous chemicals like hydroquinone,
mercury, and other unregulated substances. These chemicals are readily absorbed through the
skin into the bloodstream, producing systemic toxicity  that can result in organ damage, skin
rashes, and, in severe cases, cancer (Bendel Mirror, 2025; GazetteNG, 2025).
The pervasive use of such products in daily life combined with the aggressive marketing
strategies employed on social media amplifies these health risks. Influencer endorsements and
visually persuasive advertisements make it difficult for consumers to differentiate genuine from
counterfeit products, particularly in economically constrained communities where affordability
often takes precedence over safety (Kaplan & Haenlein, 2020).
Children in Bayelsa State are particularly vulnerable to the health risks posed by counterfeit
products. Substandard beverages and medications marketed as safe or healthy alternatives have
been implicated in a growing number of preventable pediatric illnesses and deaths (Vanguard,
2025). Parents and caregivers, often influenced by social media content, purchase these items
without verifying authenticity, exposing children to toxic substances and ineffective treatments.
Infant formulas adulterated with unsanitary ingredients or counterfeit nutritional supplements can
lead to malnutrition, gastrointestinal disorders, and developmental complications. These pediatric
health threats underscore the need for targeted public health interventions and parent -focused
awareness campaigns in the region.
Counterfeit pharmaceuticals represent one of the most dangerous categories of fake products in
Bayelsa. Many of these drugs, sourced from national distribution hubs such as Onitsha and
Alaba, lack proper active ingredients or contain incorrect dosages (Remedy Publications, 2025).

healthcare system, with hospitals reporting increased admissions, longer treatment durations, and
the diversion of limited resources to manage preventable conditions (Facebook, 2025). These
systemic pressures compromise the quality of care for other patients, creating broader public
health challenges that extend beyond individual consumers.

Conclusion
The circulation of counterfeit products in Bayelsa State, amplified through social media
platforms like Instagram, TikTok, WhatsApp, and Facebook, poses a clear threat to consumer
health. Counterfeit drugs, cosmetics, beverages, and household items have been linked to organ
damage, therapeutic failures, pediatric illnesses, and the rise of drug -resistant pathogens. Factors
such as price sensitivity, peer influence, aspirational buying, and limited digital literacy make
residents particularly vulnerable to these products.
The combination of persuasive social media marketing and inadequate awareness has created an
environment in which unsafe products reach a broad segment of the population, often without
detection or verification. The socio-economic consequences are equally concerning, with victims
facing financial strain and the healthcare system experiencing increased burdens due to the influx
of counterfeit-related illnesses.

Way forward
The following are the ways forward to combat the effect of counterfeit products in social media
marketing on consumers health
1. Regulatory agencies such as NAFDAC should collaborate with social media platforms to
track and flag counterfeit product advertisements. Algorithms can be developed to detect
suspicious content and remove it before consumers are exposed.
2. Government should strengthen fines, legal prosecution, and public disclosure of offenders
can deter vendors from distributing counterfeit products.
3. Customs and border protection agencies partnerships with neighboring states and national
hubs, like Onitsha and Alaba markets, can help intercept counterfeit goods before they reach
