Abstract
The study examines the influence of social media marketing
dimensions on tourist attraction in Sauraha, Nepal, drawing on
the Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB), Stimulus-Organism-
Response (S-O-R) framework, Information Adoption Model, and
Source Credibility Theory. A quantitative, cross-sectional research
design was employed, and data were collected from 423 domestic
and international tourists through a structured questionnaire. The
hypothesized relationships were tested using Partial Least Squares
Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) in SmartPLS 4. The
structural model demonstrated substantial explanatory power, with an
R² of 0.690 and a Q²predict value of 0.678. The findings indicate that
informativeness had a significant positive effect on tourist attraction
(β = 0.348, t = 6.231, p < 0.001), followed by influencer marketing
(β = 0.334, t = 6.411, p < 0.001) and social media interaction (β =
0.176, t = 2.970, p = .003). In contrast, e-WOM (β = −0.003, p =
0.955) and content quality (β = 0.060, p = 0.304) did not demonstrate
statistically significant effects. The findings suggest that informative
social media content, credible influencer communication, and
meaningful interaction are particularly relevant to tourist attraction
in the Sauraha context, while the effects of e-WOM and content
quality may depend on other contextual or mediating mechanisms.
The study contributes to the growing body of Nepalese tourism
and digital marketing research by providing empirical evidence on
how specific social media marketing dimensions relate to tourist
attraction in a destination-specific context.

Tourism is one of the most dynamic sectors in the world, which significantly contributes to
jobs creation, foreign exchange generation and sustainable regional development. Digital
technologies have rapidly developed, impacting how tourism destinations are marketed and
how tourists choose, research and assess travel destinations. In contrast to conventional
marketing channels, social media platforms like Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, and
X allow for bidirectional interactions between destination marketing organizations (DMOs),
tourism providers, tour influencers, and tourists, which means that the information can be
shared with them in real time and they can create content too. (Baber & Baber, 2023; Wilopo
& Nur Alam, 2025). As a result, travellers are increasingly turning to digital platforms to
make travel decisions.
Social media marketing is now a strategic tool that can be used to improve destination
competitiveness through increasing tourist awareness of the destination, developing the tourist
image of the destination, and changing the tourist behavior towards the destination. Social
media offers a source of authentic, credible information that is perceived as more trustworthy
than traditional advertising, such as through electronic word-of-mouth (e-WOM), online
reviews, travel photos, destination videos, and content created by influencers (Cheung et
al., 2008; Li & Liu, 2023). The Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) (Ajzen, 1991) states that
attitudes and subjective norms affect travelers’ behavior, and social media communications
and recommendations can impact both. Moreover, the Stimulus – Organism – Response
(S – O – R) model proposes that social media marketing activities evoke tourists’ cognitive
and emotional responses, which, in turn, impact the choice of the tourist’s destination (Baber
& Baber, 2023; Mehrabian & Russell, 1974).
Tourism research has focused on a number of dimensions of social media marketing, including
the dimensions of electronic word-of-mouth, content quality, social media interaction,
influencer marketing and informativeness. Positive e-WOM helps to diminish perceived
travel risk and increase destination credibility (Çelik & Aslan, 2024;  Thao et al., 2023) .
Well-crafted social media posts with highly visual images, videos and credible storytelling
enhances the destination image and fosters travel motivations (Abbasi et al., 2023; Han
et al., 2025). Similarly, the level of interaction among destination marketers and potential
tourists enhances engagement and trust, and the use of influencers as an effective marketing
tool has become a practice that influences tourists’ perceptions and visit intention (Han &
Zhang, 2024; Hernández-Méndez & Baute-Díaz, 2024). Additionally, tourists who receive
information on social media regarding the attractions, accommodations, transportation,
safety, and local culture will be able to make informed decisions and make the destination
more attractive (Sussman & Siegal, 2003; Susanto et al., 2023).
Although considerable international literature has built up on these various dimensions, there
is relatively little empirical evidence to date that synthesises these multiple dimensions in
one structural framework, especially in the context of developing a tourism destination like
Nepal. Most previous studies concentrate on specific social media marketing measures, while
ignoring the effects of multiple social media marketing measures on tourist attraction using
adequate multivariate methods like the Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling

(PLS-SEM) (Hair et al., 2022;  Sarstedt et al., 2022) . It is especially vital for Nepal as
tourism activities have become more reliant on digital marketing to attract customers both
domestically and internationally.
Some of the obstacles to tourism development in Nepal are its weak destination management,
the lack of infrastructure, environmental pressure, and the disbalanced distribution of
tourism benefits. Such problems are especially noticeable in Sauraha, where the high
rate of tourism development has led to problems with sanitation, inadequate solid-waste
management, pollution and the strain on natural resources (Sigdel, 2014). Overcrowding,
poorly planned tourism facilities, lack of interpretation facilities, and lack of benefits for
local communities are also reported (Chitwan National Park, 2015). Sustainable Tourism
Theory, which focuses on the integration of economic, environmental, and socio-cultural
aspects, can help to understand such problems. In the same way, the Stakeholder Theory
emphasizes the importance of engaging tourists, local communities, the tourism industry,
and local governments in a process of destination management. According to the evidence
obtained from Sauraha, tourism has positive contribution to employment and culture but
also has negative impacts on environment and socio-cultural aspects (Acharya et al., 2020).
Sauraha, the main gateway to Chitwan National Park, is one of the most popular tourist
places in Nepal, which is famous for wildlife tourism, Tharu cultural heritage, jungle safari
activities and eco-tourism. With the growing competition between the tourism destinations,
it is important for destination managers, tourism entrepreneurs and policymakers to be aware
of the impact of various aspects of social media marketing on tourist attraction. Hence, this
study investigates the impact of electronic word of mouth, informativeness, content quality,
social media interaction, and influencer marketing on tourist attraction in Sauraha, Nepal
through the use of PLS-SEM. The results will help the growing body of literature on digital
destination marketing and be useful to those creating effective social media strategies for
increasing the competitiveness of tourism in Nepal.
Literature Review and Hypothesis Development
Social media marketing has revolutionized the tourism industry by giving destinations a
platform to connect with potential tourists in an interactive digital environment. Social
media offers possibilities for real-time communication, creation of user generated content,
sharing of information and building relationships between destinations and travellers, which
is different from traditional marketing. Social media platforms like Facebook, Instagram,
TikTok, and YouTube have emerged as vital sources for tourists to get information about
travel, assess the appeal of a destination, and share their experiences (Baber & Baber, 2023;
Susanto et al., 2023). The Theory of Planned Behavior (Ajzen, 1991) suggests that external
information and social influences play a significant role in how people make decisions about
their specific behaviours, such as travel decisions. Similarly, according to the Stimulus-
Organism-Response (S–O–R) model, the stimuli of social media marketing will affect
tourists’ cognitive and emotional evaluations in the end, which will lead to destination choice
behavior (Baber & Baber, 2023; Pokharel & Chhetri, 2026). As a result, other factors like
electronic word-of-mouth (e-WOM), content quality, social media interaction, influencer
marketing and informativeness have emerged as major factors that affect tourist attraction.

Electronic Word-of-Mouth (e-WOM)
Electronic word of mouth is consumers’ opinions, recommendations, reviews and experiences
about a destination that are shared through digital platforms (Cheung et al., 2008). Overall,
e-WOM is more believable than traditional advertising, as it comes from independent
travelers and not from businesses. The Information Adoption Model suggests that travelers
are more likely to adopt online information if they believe it to be credible and valuable
when considering their travel options (Çelik & Aslan, 2024; Sussman & Siegal, 2003).
Positive online reviews decrease the perceived risk of travel, enhance the destination image
and boost the destination choice (Li & Liu, 2023). Favorable e-WOM also makes a positive
contribution to destination image, tourist involvement and travel intention, as revealed in
recent tourism studies (Abbasi et al., 2023;  Thao et al., 2023). Thus, visitors who come
across positive destination-related social media content are more prone to visit Sauraha.
H1: Electronic word-of-mouth positively influences tourist attraction toward Sauraha.
Content Quality on Social Media Platforms
Content quality is the level of relevance, accuracy, completeness, creativity, consistency and
visual appeal of information provided on social media (Ducoffe, 1996). Professional photos,
videos, authentic travel stories and destination stories create high-quality tourism content,
which contributes to the perception tourists have of destinations and motivates their travel.
The informativeness and aesthetic appeal of content also lower the information search costs,
and increase destination awareness (Han et al., 2025). Empirical research shows that the
quality of social media content has a positive impact on destination image (Sarif Yono et
al., 2023), tourist engagement (Wilopo & Nur Alam, 2025), and visit intention (Sarif Yono
et al., 2023; Wilopo & Nur Alam, 2025). So, destinations which always serve high-quality
online content will be expected to draw more visitors.
H2: Content quality on social media positively influences tourist attraction toward Sauraha.
Social Media Interaction
Social media interaction is the level of communication and interaction (through comments,
shares, likes, discussions, etc.) between destinations and users. Interactive communication

builds trust, emotional attachment, and customers engagement between tourism marketers
and potential visitors (Abbasi et al., 2023; Pokharel et al., 2026). According to relationship
marketing it is concluded that the more interaction a customer has with a company the
more involved and loyal the customer will be and thus the more likely he is to have positive
behavioral intentions towards the company. Furthermore, social media platforms that allow
for interaction and engagement foster a sense of community and information sharing, which
enhances destination awareness and appeal (Susanto et al., 2023). Prior empirical research
has consistently revealed that social media involvement plays a significant role in tourists’
destination evaluations and travel decisions (Baber & Baber, 2023; Wilopo & Nur Alam,
2025).
H3: Social media interaction positively influences tourist attraction toward Sauraha.
Influencer Marketing
Influencer marketing is about working with social media personalities that have a high level
of credibility and can help shape consumer choices via real-time content development. Travel
experiences are shared through story-telling, photos, short video clips and live broadcasts that
boost destination visibility and credibility (Hernandez-Méndez & Baute-Díaz, 2024). The
source credibility theory states that a credible communicator is able to be more persuasive
if the communicator is credible and appears to be knowledgeable and trustworthy and
attractive (Hovland et al., 1953). Tourism studies in recent years have shown that, when it
comes to travel, the credibility of influencers clearly has a positive effect on the image of the
destination, the perception of risk associated with traveling, and the willingness of tourists
to travel to promoted destinations (Han & Zhang, 2024; Hernández-Méndez & Baute-Díaz,
2024). As a result, it is believed that influencer marketing will have a positive impact on the
tourist attraction to Sauraha.
H4: Influencer marketing positively influences tourist attraction toward Sauraha.
Informativeness
Informativeness refers the quality of social media content that offers timely, accurate, complete
and useful information about a tourist destination. Before making travel decisions, tourists
are increasingly turning to social media to get information about attractions, transportation,
accommodation, pricing, weather, local culture, safety and travel regulations (Sussman &
Siegal, 2003). Informative content decreases uncertainty, perceived travel risk and destination
evaluation (Peng & Huang, 2017). In the context of tourism marketing, several studies have
shown that informative social media content can have a positive impact on tourists’ attitudes,
satisfaction, and behavioral intentions (Pokharel, 2024; Han et al., 2025; Wilopo & Nur
Alam, 2025). In view of this, it is expected that the more informative it becomes, the more
tourists will be attracted towards Sauraha.
H5: Informativeness positively influences tourist attraction toward Sauraha.

Methods
In this study, the researcher used quantitative cross-sectional research design to examine
the effect of social media marketing (SMM) dimensions on tourist attraction in Sauraha,
Nepal. The use of a quantitative approach was deemed appropriate because the researchers
wanted to test the relationship between hypothesized latent constructs using statistical
methods (Creswell & Creswell, 2018; Saunders et al., 2019). The study was conducted
on both domestic and international tourist at the one of the main tourist sites of Nepal
Sauraha. The primary data was obtained by the use of a structured and self-administered
questionnaire by convenience sampling technique at the main tourist attractions, hotels,
restaurants and recreational areas where the tourists were easily accessible. To ensure a wide
range of perceptions and experiences of Sauraha as a tourism destination, both domestic and
international tourists were included in the study. The inclusion was deemed suitable due to
the possibility that the two groups might have different travel motivations, expectations and
perceptions of destination. The differences in sample sizes were due to different availability
and accessibility of respondents during field survey. This may prevent the groups being
compared directly but the overall sample offers a more representative sample of tourists in
Sauraha. The results should therefore be interpreted with caution, especially if differences
between the domestic and international respondents are taken into account. Survey was
conducted in self-administered manner in person, where the respondents were approached
at certain tourist spots in Sauraha and asked to fill out the questionnaire voluntarily after an
explanation of the study and its purpose.

    | 175
The questionnaires were distributed to 450 and 423 questionnaires were received after
excluding 27 questionnaires that were not complete, the effective response rate was about
94%. The questionnaire had two parts. The demographic characteristics of the respondents
such as gender, age, nationality, occupation, and preferred social media platform were
measured in the first section, and the second section measured the study constructs. The
independent variables were Electronic Word-of-Mouth (e-WOM), Content Quality on Social
Media Platforms, Social Media Interaction, Influencer Marketing, and Informativeness and
the dependent variable was Tourist Attraction. All the measurement items were adapted
items in the tourism and social media marketing literature and were assessed by a five-point
Likert scale ranging from 1 (Strongly Disagree) to 5 (Strongly Agree). Some items in the
measurement scales were adopted from existing research on electronic word-of-mouth,
social media marketing, influencer marketing, and destination marketing (Cheung et al.,
2008; Ducoffe, 1996; Katsikari et al., 2020; Abbasi et al., 2023; Baber & Baber, 2023; Han
& Zhang, 2024).
The collected data was analyzed by SmartPLS 4 with the recommended two-stage approach
of Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM). PLS-SEM was chosen
as it is a solid multivariate statistical method for analyzing complex relationships between
multiple latent constructs and is more appropriate for prediction-based research (Hair et
al., 2022; Sarstedt et al., 2022). To determine the validity of the measurement model, the
indicator reliability, internal consistency reliability measured by Cronbach’s alpha, and
composite reliability measured by Average Variance Extracted (A VE) and Heterotrait–
Monotrait (HTMT) ratio (Fornell & Larcker, 1981; Henseler et al., 2015) were assessed.
Path coefficient analysis, coefficient of determination (R²), effect size (f²), predictive
relevance (Q²) and Standardized Root Mean Square Residual (SRMR) were used to assess
the model fit and predictive ability of the structural model. The hypotheses were tested
using the bootstrapping procedure with 5,000 re-sampling to estimate the significance of
the structural relationships (Hair et al., 2022).

has small but meaningful effect size values, while social media interaction (0.029) has a
weak effect size. In contrast, the quality of the content (0.004) and electronic word of mouth

DIScuSSIon
The results revealed that the dimensions of social media marketing do not equally contribute
to the tourist attractions in Sauraha. The model explains a significant amount of variance in
tourist attraction (R² = 0.678); among the individual variables, only informativeness (β =
0.348, p < 0.001), influencer marketing (β = 0.334, p < 0.001) and social media interaction
(β = 0.176, p = 0.003) have significant positive effects. Electronic word of mouth (e-WOM)
(β = −0.003, p = 0.955) and the quality of the content in social media (β = 0.060, p =
0.304) are not directly significant. It indicates that when it comes to social media, tourists
are more likely to be influenced by the usefulness, credibility, and interactiveness of the
communication than just the presence of social media reviews and/or the visual appeal of
the social media content.
The most important attribute in determining tourist attraction is informativeness. The study
is aligned with Nepalese evidence of tourists’ active search for travel related products
and prices information via social media. When Sthapit and Khadka (2016) investigated
travel intermediaries and customers in Kathmandu Valley, they observed that among the
information most desired by customers, they found the product and price information and
that recalled information, comments and reviews had an impact on customer responses. In
a similar line of reasoning, Kunwar et al. (2022) identified social media as an important
information source for their destination related decision making for tourism in Nepal. The
current discovery builds on this research by showing that the informativeness has a direct
and significant relationship with the tourist attraction in a particular tourist destination. This
can be especially important in Sauraha as tourists need to have practical details regarding
activities, lodging, transportation, cost, safety and local experiences with wildlife.
The second strongest predictor is influencer marketing (β = 0.334, p < 0.001). This means that
credible and experience-based communication from influencers in social media can make a
significant impact on destination attractiveness. The result aligns with the study carried out
by Hernández-Méndez and Baute-Díaz (2024) who determined that the influencer marketing
is an important tool for tourism destination promotion. It is also suitable for the Nepalese
context which has been found to have a relation between social media engagement and brand
perceptions related to tourism. For instance, Sharma et al. (2024) determined that social media
interactivity, benefits and incentives played significant roles in the tourism brand image of

Nepal. The current study, however, does not focus on brand image, but instead on tourist
attraction, and thus it may explore the impact of influencer-based communication beyond
brand building to tourists’ immediate perceptions of destination appeal.
Social media interaction is also shown to have a relatively high positive impact (β = 0.176)
but it is less than informativeness and influencer marketing. The finding corroborates Sharma
et al. (2024) who reported that the interactivity of social media has a significant impact on
the formation of tourism brand images in Nepal. It also aligns with the finding of Sthapit
and Khadka (2016) about customer behavior in response to communication through the
social media. Therefore, in the context of tourism, interactive communication can enhance
destination attractiveness as the traveler will be able to clarify their questions, share their
experiences, and feel that tourists are accessible and responsive.
However, e-WOM does not play a significant role in tourist attraction (β = −0.003, p = .955).
This is in contrast to previous studies focusing on the significance of online reviews and
electronic recommendations as part of the tourism decision making process. The disparity
could be due to the fact that the current model incorporates informativeness, influencer
marketing and interaction at the same time. That is, while reviews can serve as one source
of information, the impact of tourists may be less pronounced if other, more specific, forms
of social media communication are included. More importantly, the present data do not
provide evidence that tourists lack the trust in reviews or that fake reviews were responsible
for the negligible relationship; that has to be directly measured.
Content quality also does not significantly directly affect (β = 0.060, p = .304). This result
contrasts with those that focus on the significance of photographs and visual content of
tourism. In Chitwan, Bhatt and Pickering (2022) identified that Chitwan National Park
images communicated wildlife, landscapes, tourism activities and cultural attributes through
the use of online photographs, but that the emphasis of tourists’ photographs and tourism
organizations’ promotional photographs differed. This implies that if the content is visually
appealing, it can help to generate destination visibility; however, this may not be enough
to make the destination a tourist attraction. For Sauraha, visually appealing content could
require some additional information, credible source and interaction with the content to have
an impact on decisions related to the destination.
The results show that social media marketing effectiveness in Sauraha is dimension specific.
There are significant direct relationships between informativeness, influencer marketing,
interaction and tourist attraction, but not for e-WOM and content quality. Results thus add to
the Nepalese tourism literature highlighting the importance of the usefulness and credibility
of social media communication rather than just its volume and visual interest.
Conclusion
This study focused on five social media marketing dimensions, namely, electronic word-
of-mouth (e-WOM), content quality, social media interaction, influencer marketing, and
informativeness, and their effect on tourist attraction toward Sauraha in Nepal, as measured by
PLS-SEM. Results show informativeness, influencer marketing and social media interaction
are significant factors that increase tourist attraction, with informativeness being the most

    | 183
important factor. The findings suggest that social media that delivers accurate, timely and
relevant travel information, through credible social media personalities and user-interactions,
is more likely to attract tourists to a destination. On the other hand, the direct effects of
e-WOM and content quality were not significant, implying that positive online reviews
and content that is visually appealing do not directly affect destination choice, if there is
any. Overall, the research has added to literature on digital tourism marketing by providing
empirical evidence that the various dimensions of social media make different contributions
to the tourist attraction, especially in a developing tourist destination like Sauraha.
Practical and Theoretical Implications
The results have implications for the tourism stakeholders in Sauraha as well as other
similar locations in Nepal. While it is true that destination marketers need to be visible on
social media, the importance of informativeness, influencer marketing, and social media
interaction indicate that it is better to have useful information, credible communications
based on experience, and two-way interaction. Tourism groups, tourist hotels, travel agents
and local government can offer concrete and useful information related to Chitwan National
Park, wildlife experience, the Tharu culture, stay in Sauraha, accessibility and responsible
tourism. This is consistent with the Nepalese context that social media plays a significant
role as a source of tourism information and to make decisions for travelling (Kunwar et al.,
2022; Sthapit & Khadka, 2016).
The large role that influencer marketing plays also suggests there’s opportunity to engage
with relevant travel influencers to convey true destination experiences. In addition, active
responses to the questions, comments, and requests of visitors to social media can also help
to enhance social media interaction and engagement.
The non-significant role of e-WOM and content quality does not mean that these factors
are not important; it can just be that when the other social media dimensions are taken into
account at the same time, the direct impact of e-WOM and content quality might not be
as significant as they seem. Theoretically, the results support the extension of destination-
marketing research findings by highlighting that the dimensions of social media do not
have the same impact on the phenomenon of tourist attraction. The study therefore offers
context-specific evidences from Sauraha which highlights the significance of information,
interaction and influencer mediated communication in the marketing activities of tourism
in Nepal.
Future Research Directions
To further understand the indirect and moderating effect of e-WOM and content quality
on tourist attraction, studies should consider other constructs like destination image, trust,
tourist engagement, perceived value, or even travel motivation. The findings of the study
could be generalised by comparative studies across different tourism destinations in Nepal
and cross-country contexts. Furthermore, longitudinal studies and multi-group analyses by
demographic and domestic or foreign tourists could be useful to gain a deeper understanding
of the evolution of digital tourism behaviour. In addition, future researchers can incorporate
new social media platforms and the new artificial intelligence (AI) marketing techniques to
gain insight into the changing decision-making processes of tourists.
