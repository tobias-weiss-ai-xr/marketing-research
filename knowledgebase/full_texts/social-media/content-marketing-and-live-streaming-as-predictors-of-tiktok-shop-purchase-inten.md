Social commerce platforms increasingly integrate promotional content with real -time seller–
consumer interaction, creating new ways for consumers to evaluate products and develop
purchase intentions. TikTok Shop exemplifies this environment by combining content marketing
and live streaming within a single platform, particularly relevant to Generation Z consumers. This
study examined the associations of content marketing and live streaming with purchase
intention among Generation Z consumers on TikTok Shop. A quantitative cross -sectional
explanatory survey was conducted among 96 active Management students at Universitas
Kristen Maranatha who had TikTok accounts, had been exposed to product-related marketing
content, and had watched product -oriented live streams on TikTok Shop. Participants were
selected using purposive sampling, and the data were analyzed using multiple linear
regression. The results showed that content marketing was positively and significantly
associated with purchase intention ( B = 0.270, β = 0.208, p = .025), while live streaming also
demonstrated a positive a nd significant association (B = 0.754, β = 0.561, p = .001). The larger
standardized coefficient indicates that live streaming was the stronger predictor of purchase
intention within the examined model. Collectively, the two predictors formed a statistical ly
significant model that explained 48.4% of the variance in purchase intention (adjusted R² =
.484). These findings demonstrate that content -based promotion and real -time interactive
selling are both relevant to purchase intention in the examined social commerce context, with
live streaming showing a more prominent statistical contribution. The study highlights the
particular relevance of real-time commercial interaction for understanding purchase intention
among young consumers using TikTok Shop.

I. INTRODUCTION
Social commerce has expanded the role of social
media from a communication channel into a digital
environment where information seeking, social
interaction, promotion, and commercial transactions
can occur within the same platform. TikTok Shop
exemplifies this development by integrating short -form
video content, commerce features, and live streaming
into a single ecosystem. This integration creates a more
interactive marketing environment in which consumers
not only encounter product information but can also
communicate directly with sellers or hosts. Such
characteristics are particularly relevant to Generation
Z, whose consumption of digital content and
engagement with video -based social media make
them frequent recipients of various forms of online
marketing communication. University students
represent an important segment within this generation
because their everyday enga gement with social
media exposes them to both content -based
promotion and interactive selling environments.
An important consumer response within social
commerce is purchase intention, which reflects
consumers’ psychological tendency to consider, plan,
or express willingness to purchase a product after
receiving information or marketing stimuli (Al -Hafizi,
2021). Although purchase intention does not
necessarily translate into an actual purchasing
decision, it represents an important stage preceding
consumer choice. Within TikTok Shop, purchase
intention may be associated with multiple forms of
marketing stimuli, including content marketing and live
streaming. These strategies differ fundamentally in how
consumers experience marketing communication.
Content marketing primarily delivers relevant and
engaging information through digital content that can
be accessed independently, whereas live streaming
combines product information with synchronous
interaction between consumers and sellers or hosts.
Content marke ting focuses on creating and
distributing relevant, valuable, engaging, and
consistent content to attract and retain the attention
of a targeted audience (Yunita et al., 2021). Within
social media environments, this approach enables
businesses to communicate product information
through creative formats aligned with the
characteristics of platform users. Informative, relevant,
engaging, and comprehensible content may facilitate
consumers’ understanding of product benefits and
support their evaluation of avai lable alternatives.
Kędzierska-Szczepaniak and Minga (2022) further
emphasized the role of content marketing in
developing audience engagement and relationships
through valuable information. Content marketing can
therefore function not merely as a promotional
mechanism but also as a marketing stimulus

Live streaming, by contrast, introduces synchronous

strengthen consumers’ sense of proximity, confidence,
and interest in the products being presented.
The relationships among content marketing, live
streaming, and purchase intention can be
conceptually understood through the Stimulus –
Organism–Response (S -O-R) framework. The S -O-R
perspective proposes that environmental stimuli are
associated with individuals’ internal states and
subsequently with behavioral or psychological
responses (E roglu et al., 2001). In the present context,
content marketing and live streaming can be
conceptualized as digital marketing stimuli, while
purchase intention represents a consumer response.
Content marketing provides informational stimuli
through the relevance and quality of the information
consumers receive, whereas live streaming adds
immediacy, interactivity, and real-time communication
to the marketing environment. However, because this
study does not directly measure the organism
component, the S -O-R framework is used as a
conceptual basis for understanding the relationship
between digital marketing stimuli and purchase
intention rather than as a complete empirical test of
the S-O-R mechanism.
The potential relevance of live streaming can be
further understood through the concept of social
presence. Real -time interaction may enhance
consumers’ perception that they are communicating
with another person within a digital environment. The
presence of a host, immediate responses, product
demonstrations, and two-way communication can
reduce the psychological distance commonly
associated with online transactions. Wongkitrungrueng
and Assarut (2020) demonstrated that live streaming in
social commerce can create value and foster
consumer engagement through interactive
experiences. Accordingly, live streaming may be
particularly relevant to purchase intention because it
combines product information with interpersonal
interaction and a sense of social presence. This
characteristic conceptually differentiates live
streaming from content marketing and provides a
basis for examining whether the two forms of digital
marketing communication exhibit different strengths of
association with purchase intention.
Empirical research generally supports a positive
relationship between content marketing and purchase
intention. Fauzi and Fitriyah (2025) reported a positive
and significant relationship between content
marketing and purchase intention among Generation
Z TikTok Shop users. Syaputri et al. (2023) similarly found
that con tent marketing on TikTok was positively
associated with purchase intention, while Hikmah et al.
(2025) reported a positive and significant relationship
between content marketing and purchase intention
on TikTok Shop. Collectively, these findings suggest that
consumers who evaluate marketing content more
favorably may demonstrate a stronger tendency to
consider purchasing the products presented to them.
A relatively consistent pattern has also been
reported for live streaming. Khazainah and Agustina
(2025) i dentified a positive and significant relationship
between live streaming and students’ purchase
intention. Lenggihunusa et al. (2024) found that live
shopping could encourage purchase intention among
TikTok consumers, while Aleyda et al. (2025) reported a
positive relationship between live shopping and
purchase intention for fashion products among
Generation Z consumers. These findings highlight the
potential relevance of direct interaction, host -
delivered product information, and two -way
communication in s haping consumers’ willingness to
consider a purchase. Previous studies have also
examined content marketing and live streaming
simultaneously. Nurani and Nugraha (2025), Arta and
Ahmadi (2025), and Irwanto et al. (2026) reported
significant relationships between these marketing
strategies and purchase intention in TikTok -related
contexts.
Despite the growing body of evidence, an
important empirical question remains regarding the
relative contributions of content marketing and live
streaming when both are co nsidered within the same
model. The research gap does not primarily arise from
contradictory findings, as previous studies generally
report relationships in a similar positive direction.
Rather, existing evidence provides more limited insight
into whether these two marketing stimuli, which differ in
their communication characteristics, exhibit
comparable or substantially different strengths of
association with purchase intention. Content marketing
primarily provides information that consumers can
access asy nchronously, whereas live streaming
combines product information with real -time
interaction and social presence. These differences
provide a conceptual basis for expecting that the two
predictors may not contribute equally to purchase
intention. Examining their standardized contributions
within the same statistical model can therefore provide
a more differentiated understanding than merely
establishing whether each predictor is statistically
significant.
Addressing this issue is particularly relevant among
Generation Z consumers who have experienced both
forms of marketing communication. Rather than
generalizing to the entire Generation Z population, the
present study focuses specifically on Management
students at Universitas Kristen Maranatha who have
been exposed to product -related content marketing
and have watched product -oriented live streams on
TikTok Shop. This relatively specific consumer group
provides a context in which the two marketing stimuli
can be examined among respondents with relevant
exposure to both. Accordingly, the contribution of this
study lies not only in testing whether content marketing
and live streaming are associated with purchase

II. METHOD
This study employed a quantitative approach with
an explanatory cross -sectional survey design to
examine the associations of content marketing and
live streaming with purchase intention among TikTok
Shop users (Sugiyono, 2020). Content marketing and
live streaming were s pecified as predictor variables,
while purchase intention was treated as the outcome
variable. A cross -sectional design was employed
because data on all study variables were collected
from participants within a single period of observation.
Accordingly, the analysis was designed to identify
statistical associations among the variables rather than
to establish definitive causal relationships.
The target population comprised active
Management students at Universitas Kristen
Maranatha. Participants were recrui ted using non -
probability purposive sampling because eligibility
required characteristics relevant to the research
context. To participate, respondents were required to
be active Management students at Universitas Kristen
Maranatha, have a TikTok account, have been
exposed to product -related content marketing on
TikTok, and have watched live streams offering
products through TikTok Shop. Based on these eligibility
criteria, 96 respondents were included in the final
analysis.
The sample -size calculation was based on the
Lemeshow formula using a 95% confidence level ( Z =
1.96), an assumed population proportion of .50, and a
precision level ( d) of .10. These parameters produced
an estimated sample requirement of approximately 96
respondents. The calculation was used as a reference
for determining the required sample size, whereas
participant selection remained based on purposive
sampling and the predetermined eligibility criteria.
Data were collected using a structured
questionnaire measuring three constructs: c ontent
marketing, live streaming, and purchase intention.
Each construct was measured using four items,
resulting in a total of 12 questionnaire items. Content
marketing was represented by items CM.1 –CM.4, live
streaming by items LS.1 –LS.4, and purchase intention
by items MB.1 –MB.4. These measures were used to
capture respondents’ evaluations of the two forms of
digital marketing communication and their purchase
intentions in the TikTok Shop context.
Prior to hypothesis testing, the measurement quality
of the questionnaire was assessed through item validity
and internal consistency analyses. Item validity was
evaluated by comparing the correlation coefficient for
each item with the critical r value of .2006, with
coefficients exceeding this criterion considered
acceptable. Internal consistency was assessed using
Cronbach’s alpha for each construct, with coefficients
above .600 treated as acceptable according to the
criterion adopted in this study. The results of these
measurement assessments are reported in t he Results
section.
Multiple linear regression was used as the primary
analytical technique. Before estimating the regression
model, relevant assumptions were assessed, including
residual normality, homoscedasticity, and
multicollinearity. Residual normality was evaluated
through visual inspection of the histogram to determine
whether the residual distribution approximated a
normal pattern. Homoscedasticity was assessed using
a residual scatterplot by examining the distribution of
residuals around zero and the absence of a systematic
pattern. Multicollinearity was evaluated using
tolerance and variance inflation factor (VIF) statistics,
with tolerance values greater than .10 and VIF values
below 10 used as the predetermined criteria for
identifying problematic multicollinearity.
The multiple regression model was specified as Y = α
+ β ₁X₁ + β₂X₂ + ε, where Y represents purchase
intention, X₁ represents content marketing, X₂
represents live streaming, α represents the intercept, β₁
and β₂ represent the regression coefficients, and ε
represents the residual term. The overall signi ficance of
the regression model was evaluated through the F test
at a significance level of 5% ( p < .05). The unique
association of each predictor with purchase intention
was evaluated using the regression coefficients,
standardized coefficients (β), and c orresponding p
values. Standardized coefficients were additionally
used to compare the relative statistical contributions of
content marketing and live streaming within the same
model. Adjusted R² was used to indicate the proportion
of variance in purchase intention explained
collectively by the predictors after accounting for the
number of predictors included in the model. The
hypotheses were considered empirically supported
when the corresponding statistical tests yielded p
values below .05.

B. Discussion
The findings demonstrate that content
marketing was pos itively and significantly
associated with purchase intention ( B = .270, β =
.208, p = .025), providing empirical support for H1.
This result indicates that more favorable evaluations
of content marketing were associated with higher
purchase intention among  the respondents. In the
context of TikTok Shop, this relationship can be
understood through the informational role of
marketing content. Relevant, engaging,
informative, and accessible content can provide
consumers with information needed to recognize
product attributes, evaluate potential benefits, and
consider available alternatives before developing
an intention to purchase. The finding is consistent
with Fauzi and Fitriyah (2025), Syaputri et al. (2023),
and Hikmah et al. (2025), who similarly reported
positive relationships between content marketing
and purchase intention in TikTok -related contexts.
Collectively, these findings reinforce the relevance
of content marketing as a predictor of purchase
intention among consumers exposed to social
commerce, although its relative statistical
contribution in the present model was smaller than
that of live streaming.
Live streaming was also positively and
significantly associated with purchase intention ( B =
.754, β = .561, p = .001), supporting H2. Respondents
who evaluated their live -streaming experiences
more positively tended to report higher levels of
purchase intention. This finding is consistent with
Khazainah and Agustina (2025), Lenggihunusa et al.
(2024), and Aleyda et al. (2025), whose studies
demonstrated the relevance of live streaming or
live shopping to consumers’ purchase intentions.
Unlike content marketing that consumers can
access asynchronously, live streaming combines
product presentation with synchronous
communication, allowing consumers to observe
demonstrations, obtain information, ask questions,
and receive immediate responses from sellers or
hosts. These characteristics create a more
immediate form of marketing communication and
provide a plausible context for understanding why
live streaming is closely associated with purchase
intention in social commerce.
The stronger association observed for live
streaming represents an important finding of this
study. The standardized coefficient for live
streaming (β = .561) was substantially larger than
that for content marketing (β = .208), indicating that
live streaming exhibited greater relative predictive
strength when both variables were included in the
same regression model. This difference  should not
be interpreted as evidence that live streaming
causally produces a stronger effect on purchase
intention. Rather, it demonstrates that variation in
respondents’ evaluations of live streaming was
more strongly associated with variation in purchase
intention after accounting for content marketing.
This finding extends previous studies that have
primarily established the statistical relevance of the
two marketing strategies by demonstrating that
their relative contributions may differ when
examined simultaneously among consumers who
have experienced both forms of marketing
communication.
The distinction between the two predictors can
also be interpreted in relation to their different
communication characteristics. Content marketing
primarily provides information through prepared
digital content that consumers can view and
process independently, whereas live streaming
integrates information with immediacy,
interpersonal communication, and real -time
interaction. This interactive environment may
provide consumers with opportunities to clarify
product information and experience a greater
sense of connection with the seller or host. Such an
interpretation is consistent with the concept of
social presence and with Wongkitrungrueng and
Assarut (2020), who highlighted the capacity of live
streaming commerce to create value and
consumer engagement through interactive
experiences. The present findings therefore suggest
that, within the examined context, combining
product information with real-time social interaction
may be particularly relevant to purchase intention.
Nevertheless, social presence, engagement, trust,
and related psychological mechanisms were not
directly measured in this study; consequently, their
roles should be regarded as theoretically plausible
explanations rather than empirically established
mechanisms.
The overall regression model was statistically
significant (p = .001), providing support for H3 and
demonstrating that content marketing and live
streaming jointly provided significant explanat ory
information regarding purchase intention. This result
is broadly consistent with Nurani and Nugraha
(2025), Arta and Ahmadi (2025), and Irwanto et al.
(2026), who reported significant relationships
involving content marketing, live streaming, and
purchase intention in TikTok -related settings. The
adjusted R² of .484 further indicates that the two
predictors collectively accounted for 48.4% of the
observed variance in purchase intention among
the respondents. This explanatory capacity is

These findings have practical implications for

demonstrations, immediate clarification, and direct
interaction between consumers and hosts, while
content marketing can continue to provide
information before and after live interactions.

marketing with live streaming. Rather, the two
approaches can serve complementary functions,
with the present results indicating that live
streaming was the more prominent predictor of
purchase intention within the examined model.
The study also contributes to the social

question of whether content marketing and live
streaming are individually associated with purchase

simultaneously and comparing their standardized

evidence regarding their relative statistical

formats on TikTok Shop. At the same time, this
contribution should be interpreted within the
boundaries of the research design. The cross -

consumers. Future resear ch could examine more
heterogeneous consumer populations, employ

incorporate relevant organism variables such as

different social commerce stimuli may be
associated with purchase intention.

IV. CONCLUSION
This study demonstrates that content marketing and
live streaming are positively and significantly
associated with purchase intention among the TikTok
Shop users exa mined, with live streaming exhibiting a
substantially stronger relative association than content
marketing based on the standardized regression
coefficients. Collectively, the two predictors formed a
statistically significant model and explained 48.4% of
the variance in purchase intention, indicating that
both content -based promotion and real -time
interactive communication are relevant to
understanding purchase intention in the examined
social commerce context. The findings extend previous
evidence by demon strating that these two forms of
digital marketing communication may differ in their
relative statistical contributions, with live streaming
emerging as the more prominent predictor while
remaining complementary to content marketing. From
a practical perspective, marketers targeting
consumers with characteristics similar to those
examined in this study may benefit from combining
informative and engaging marketing content with live -
streaming activities that facilitate product
demonstrations, immediate respon ses, and direct
interaction with consumers. Nevertheless, the findings
should be interpreted within the boundaries of the
cross-sectional design and purposive sample of
Management students from a single university and
should not be generalized to the broader Generation Z
population without further evidence. Future research
should therefore examine more diverse consumer
populations and incorporate potential psychological
mechanisms, such as social presence, engagement,
trust, and perceived value, to provide a more
comprehensive explanation of how different social
commerce stimuli are associated with purchase
intention.
