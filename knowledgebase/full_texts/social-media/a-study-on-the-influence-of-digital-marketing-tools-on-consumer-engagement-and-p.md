ABSTRACT
Digital marketing has become an important way for businesses to reach customers, build relationships with them, and influence their purchasing decisions. This
study aims to understand how four major digital marketing tools—social media marketing, email and SMS marketing, search advertising and search engine
optimization(SEO),andinfluencermarketing—affectconsumerengagementandpurchasebehaviour.
The study collected primarydata from 200 respondents through astructured questionnaire usinga five-pointLikert scale. The collected data were analysed using
MicrosoftExcelwithstatisticaltechniquessuchasthechi-squaretestofindependence,one-wayANOVA, andlinearregression.
Theresultsofthe chi-squaretestrevealedasignificantrelationship betweenconsumers'preferreddigitalmarketingtools andtheirpurchasedecisions(χ²=19.34,
df = 3, p < 0.001). A significant association was also found between gender and purchase decisions (χ²= 5.79, df = 1, p = 0.016). The one-way ANOVA results
showed that consumer engagement differed significantly across the digital marketing tool groups (F = 10.56, p < 0.001). However, no statistically significant
differenceinpurchasebehaviourwasobservedamongdifferentagegroups(F=1.86,p=0.137).
The regression analysis further indicated that consumer engagement was the strongest predictor of purchase behaviour, with a standardized beta coefficient of
0.55(p<0.001).Thefive predictorsincludedintheregressionmodeljointlyexplained33.2%ofthevariationinpurchasebehaviour.
Overall, the findings suggest that digital marketing tools mayinfluence consumers' buying decisions by encouraging greaterengagement with brands. Therefore,
businesses should focus not only on promoting their products through digital platforms but also on creating meaningful interactions and positive customer
experiences. The study also highlights opportunities for future research to examine additional factors that may influence consumer engagement and purchasing
decisions.

1.Introduction
1.1 Background of the Study
The spread of internet access, smartphones and social platforms has changed the way consumers discover, evaluate and buy products. Firms that once
relied on one-way mass media now communicate through interactive channels that let consumers respond, share and co-create content. Digital
marketing, broadly understood as the use of internet-based and mobile channels to promote products and build customer relationships, has therefore
movedfromasupplementaryactivitytoacoreelementofmarketingstrategy(Chaffey&Ellis-Chadwick,2019;Kotler&Keller,2016).
Because digital channels generate data on every interaction, marketers can target, personalise and measure at a level that was not possible with
traditionalmedia. At the sametime, consumers facean abundanceofmessages and areselectiveabout thebrands they payattentionto. In thissetting,
engagement(theextenttowhichconsumers interactwith,andemotionallyinvestin,abrand'scommunications)iswidelyregardedasabridgebetween
exposuretomarketingandeventualpurchase(Brodieetal.,2011).

1.3 Consumer Engagement and Purchase Behaviour
Consumer engagement is treated in this study as the degree of attention, interaction and involvement a consumer shows towards a brand's digital
content, for example by clicking, liking, commenting, sharing, subscribingor revisiting. Purchase behaviour refers tothe consumer's tendency to buy,
including the intention to purchase, the likelihood of repeat purchase and willingness to recommend, and, in a simple form, whether a purchase was
actuallymadeafterexposuretodigitalmarketing.
1.4 Need and Significance of the Study
Marketing budgets are increasingly shifted to digital channels, yet managers still need evidence on which tools actually engage consumers and which
translateengagementintosales. Evidencethatdistinguishes theeffectofeachtool, andthattestswhetherengagementisthemechanismthroughwhich
tools influence buying, helps firms allocate budgets more carefully. The study also contributes to the academic literature by testing the relationships
withsimple,replicablestatisticalmethodsthatcanbereproducedinaspreadsheet.
1.5 Statement of the Problem
Firms invest heavily in digital marketing tools, but it is not always clear whether these tools influence consumers uniformly, whether preference for a
tool is linked to actual purchasing, and how much of the effect on purchase behaviour operates through engagement. This study addresses these
questionsempirically.
2.ReviewofLiterature
2.1 Digital Marketing and the Consumer Decision Journey
Kannan and Li (2017) offer a framework in which digital marketing affects consumers at multiple stages of the decision process, and highlight
personalisation,interactivity andthe role of consumer data. Lemon andVerhoef (2016)similarly describethe customer experienceas a journey across
touchpoints before, during and after purchase, and argue that firms must manage these touchpoints in an integrated manner. Both perspectives imply
thattheeffectofanysingletoolshouldbeunderstoodintermsofitscontributiontothewiderjourneyratherthanasanisolatedcauseofsales.
2.2 Social Media Marketing
Kaplan and Haenlein (2010) classify social media into distinct types and discuss both the opportunities and the challenges they present for firms, in
particulartheloss ofcontroloverbrand-relatedconversations.Hollebeek,GlynnandBrodie(2014)developandvalidatea measureofconsumerbrand
engagement in social media contexts, conceptualising it through cognitive, emotional and behavioural dimensions. These works support the view that
social platforms are especially suitable for elicitinginteraction, which motivates the hypothesis thatconsumers who prefer social mediaexhibit higher
engagement.
2.3 Consumer Engagement
Brodie et al. (2011) conceptualise customer engagement as a psychological state that emerges from interactive, co-creative experiences with a focal
brand, and propose that engagement can lead to outcomes such as loyalty, satisfaction and advocacy. This positions engagement as a mediating
constructbetweenmarketingactivityandbehaviouraloutcomes,whichunderpinstheregressionmodelusedinthisstudy.
2.4 Word of Mouth and Influencer Effects
Hennig-Thurau et al. (2004) investigate why consumers articulate themselves on online opinion platforms, showing that electronic word of mouth is
driven by social and functional motives. Influencer marketing can be seen as a commercial extension of this peer-to-peer influence, since
recommendations from a followed individual may be perceived as more credible or relatable than direct brand messages. Whether this perception
translatesintopurchaseisanempiricalquestionaddressedhere.

2.5 Theoretical Foundations
Two theories that support the current study variables and relationship among them were, Theory of Planned Behaviour and Technology Acceptance
Model. TheTheory ofPlanned Behaviour(Ajzen, 1991)proposes thatbehaviourisshapedbyintention, whichinturn dependson attitudes,subjective
norms and perceived behavioural control; digital content and peer recommendations can plausibly shape attitudes and norms towards buying. The
Technology Acceptance Model (Davis, 1989) explains adoption of technology through perceived usefulness and ease of use, and is relevant to
consumers'willingnesstousedigitalchannelssuchasapps,searchandsocialplatformsforshopping.
2.6 Research Gap
Prior work tends to study one channel at a time, or engagement and purchase separately. Fewer studies compare several common tools in the same
sample, test whether the purchase decision depends on the tool a consumer prefers, and assess how far engagement explains purchase behaviour once
toolexposureiscontrolled.Thisstudyaddressesthatgapusinganintegratedsetofchi-square,ANOVAandregressionanalyses.
2.7 Conceptual Framework
The framework treats exposure to four digital marketing tools (social media, email/SMS, search ads/SEO and influencer marketing) as independent
variables. Consumer engagement is modelled both as an outcome of tool exposure and as a predictor of the dependent variable, purchase behaviour.
Demographiccharacteristics(genderandagegroup)aretreatedasgroupingvariablesforthetestsofassociationanddifference.

1. The purchase decision is significantly associated withthe preferred digital tool (χ² = 19.34, p < 0.001). Purchase rates were highest among
socialmedia(64.1%)andinfluencer-marketing(58.1%)preferersandlowestamongsearch-ads/SEOpreferers(26.5%).
2. Gender is significantly associated with the purchase decision (p = 0.016), with a higher purchase rate among women (58.8%) than men
(41.7%).

arethemostengaged.
4. Purchasebehaviourdoes notdiffer significantlyacrossagegroups(p=0.137),suggestingthatdigitalmarketingworksacross agesegments

Taken together, the findings suggest a two-step pattern: tools thatinteract withconsumers (socialmediaand influencers) generate higher engagement,
and engagement, in turn, drives purchase behaviour. This is consistent with the conceptualisation of engagement as an outcome of interactive
experiences thatleads tobehavioural outcomes (Brodieet al., 2011),and withthe view thatthe effect of a marketingtouchpoint should be understood
withinthe broader customer journey (Lemon & Verhoef, 2016). The lack of a significant direct effect of email/SMS and search advertising should be
interpretedcautiously:thesetoolsmaysupportconversionatspecificstagesofthejourneythatageneralquestionnairedoesnotcapture.
7.SuggestionsandManagerialImplications
• Prioritise engagement, not justreach.Since engagement is the strongest predictorof purchasebehaviour, campaigndesign should aimat
interaction(polls,contests,shortvideos,user-generatedcontent,two-wayresponses)andnotonlyimpressions.
• Invest insocial media and influencer collaborations for building engagement,particularly for consumers who showhigh responsiveness
tothesechannels.
• Use email/SMS and search ads for targeted conversion tasks, such as cart reminders, renewals and high-intent search queries, and
evaluatethemwithconversion-basedmetricsratherthanengagementalone.
• Tailorcommunicationby gender,as purchaseresponses differed between men and women, while maintaininga broadage reach because
agedidnotsignificantlyaffectpurchasebehaviour.
• Measure and track engagement metrics (click-through rate, shares, time spent, repeat visits) and link them to sales data to refine budget
allocation.

9.Conclusion
This study examined how digital marketing tools influence consumer engagement and purchase behaviour using chi-square, ANOVA and regression
analyses in Excel. The results indicate that the tool a consumer prefers is linked to whether they buy, that engagement varies by tool, and that
engagementisthestrongestdeterminantofpurchasebehaviour,withthefivepredictorstogetherexplaining33.2%ofitsvariance.Forpractitioners,the
message is that digital marketing pays off when it engages consumers; for researchers, it points towards mediation-based models of the tool-
engagement-purchasepathway
