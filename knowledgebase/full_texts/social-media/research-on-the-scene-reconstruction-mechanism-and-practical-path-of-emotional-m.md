  Abstract
The iterative development of digital technology, the upgrading of content
consumption, and the emergence of the youth consumer group have driven the
transformation of book consumption from knowledge acquisition to emotional
experience, identity recognition, and cultural expression. The development of
short videos, live streaming, e -commerce, and social media has also prompted
book marketing to shift from pr oduct dissemination to scenario- based marketing
centered on user relationships and emotional connections. However, publishing
institutions still face issues such as a prominent product orientation, insufficient
emotional insight, poor connection between pu blic and private domains, and
insufficient accumulation of brand value. Based on Baudrillard's theory of
consumer society, Maslow's hierarchy of needs theory, and the theory of user
lifecycle, this paper constructs an analytical framework of "consumption l ogic
transformation - emotional value generation -  scenario mechanism
reconfiguration - user relationship operation". The study finds that scenarios are
an important intermediary connecting the value of book content with users'
emotional needs, and their r econfiguration is manifested in three levels: spatial
reach, emotional resonance, and relationship building. Publishing institutions can
achieve a transformation from traffic acquisition to user co -creation by emotion
recognition, scenario embedding, and r elationship strengthening, providing a
reference for the emotional, scenario -based, and long-term marketing of books in
the new consumption era.

2. Theoretical Foundation and Research Framework
2.1. Consumption Society Theory: The Shift from Functional Value to
Symbolic Value in Book Consumption
Traditional book consumption focuses on knowledge acquisition and information
satisfaction, with marketing efforts mainly concentrated on content, authors, and
channels. Baudrillard argues that modern consumers not only consume goods
themselves but also their symbolic meanings, cultural values, and social identities.
For books, the text content meets the need for information, while the author's image,
design, cultural symbols, and reading communities also carry emotional and identity
expressions. Especially for the Z Generation, reading has gradually become a way of
cultural expression and group identity. Therefore, in the new consumption era, book
consumption shifts from functional consumption to meaning consumption and
emotional consumption. Publishing marketing should also expand from content
advantages to cultural meaning construction and emotional value creation.
2.2. Demand Hierarchy Theory: Emotional Needs Become an Important
Driving Force
According to Maslow's hierarchy of needs theory, after basic needs are met,
individuals will further pursue higher -level needs such as belonging, respect, and
self-actualization. Books as spiritual cultural products can meet various emotional
needs: reading communities and interest communities enhance users' sense of
belonging; book selection reflects aesthetic preferences, values, and lifestyle; growth,
psychological, and literary books meet the needs for self -improvement and spiritual
experience. Therefore, book marketing should not only focus on "what consumers
buy" but also on "why consumers need it", and connect content value with user
needs through emotional scenarios.
2.3. User Lifecycle Theory: Emotional Connection Promotes Long- Term
Relationship Development
The user lifecycle theory emphasizes that enterprises and consumers do not have a
one-time transaction relationship. The classic AARRR model includes acquisition,

3. The Emotional Shift and Realistic Challenges in Book
Marketing in the New Consumption Era
3.1. Emotional Shift in Book Consumption Logic
The development of digital technology, social media, and content platforms has
driven the transformation of book consumption from functional consumption to
emotional consumption. The emerging consumer group represented by Generation
Z pays more attention to individual expression, interest communities, and emotional
experiences. The value of boo ks has also expanded from knowledge acquisition to
emotional experience, cultural identity, and identity expression. At the same time,
new media such as short videos and live streaming have changed the way users
access books, shifting consumption decisions  from active search to
content-triggered decisions, and from information transmission to
emotion-triggered decisions. Publishing institutions therefore need to shift from
simply emphasizing content advantages to exploring users' emotional needs and
converting textual content into perceptible emotional resources.
3.2. Scenario Transformation of Book Marketing Models
Traditional book marketing follows a linear model of "book production -  channel
distribution - user purchase", while in the new consumption envi ronment, it
gradually shifts to "content reach -  emotional resonance -  social interaction -
consumption conversion". The changes mainly lie in three aspects: First, the
dissemination space expands from fixed places such as bookstores and libraries to
ubiquitous life scenarios such as commuting, home, and socializing; second,
marketing content shifts from author, book description, etc., to emotional narration,
life-oriented expression, and user experience sharing; third, user relationships
extend from one -time transactions to comment interaction, community co -reading,
and content co-creation, etc., for long-term interaction.
3.3. Realistic Challenges under the Absence of Emotional Value
Currently, book marketing still faces four issues: First, there is a stro ng
product-oriented inertia, with insufficient insight into users' emotional needs and
life scenarios; second, there is a disconnection between traffic acquisition and user
retention, and short-term exposure is difficult to be transformed into long-term user

communicable, participatory, and sustainable-operational marketing content.
4. The Scenario Reconstruction Mechanism of Emotional
Marketing in Books
4.1. Spatial Scenario Reconstruction: Shift from product display to user
experience.
Traditional book marketing mainly relied on sales spaces such as bookstores and
e-commerce platforms. However, the development of short videos, social media, and
content communities has enabled books to enter various daily life scenarios such as
commuting, at home, and in social settings. Publishing institutions achieve natural
user engagement through content such as author creation, reader experience, and
work themes, and enhance the experience through physica l spaces like reading
activities and cultural exhibitions. Therefore, the core of spatial scenario
reconstruction is to expand the opportunities for users to interact with books,
creating a foundation for emotional connection.
4.2. Emotional Scenario Recons truction: Shift from content
dissemination to emotional resonance.
Books inherently contain rich emotional resources, but they need to be expressed in
a scenario -based manner to be transformed into marketing value. Marketing
content should shift from "what  this book is about" to "what this book responds to
the users", connecting the work theme, character stories with the users' real -life
situations, enabling consumers to progress from understanding the content to
emotional identification. At the same time, users' reading insights and experience
sharing can spread through social media, converting personal emotions into a
driving force for dissemination.
4.3. Relationship Scenario Reconstruction: Shift from a one -time
transaction to continuous connection.
In the new consumption era, users are not only purchasers but also content
disseminators and brand participants. Publishing institutions can enhance user
stickiness through book review sharing, reading communities, and continuous
interaction, and further unde rstand user needs to form a stable brand relationship,
achieving a transition from single -book operation to cultural ecosystem
construction.

5. Practical Approaches to Emotional Marketing in the New
Consumption Era of Books
5.1. Deepen User Insights to Enhance the Precision of Emotional
Marketing.
Publishing institutions should shift from the trad itional "user profiling" to
"emotional profiling", integrating users' reading preferences, interaction behaviors,
and feedback content to analyze their underlying psychological needs and value
recognition. For instance, growth -oriented books not only meet the need for
knowledge but may also address users' career anxieties and self-growth aspirations.
By extracting the value concepts, life experiences, and emotional elements from the
text, a transformation can be achieved from "finding users through books" t o
"organizing content based on user needs".
5.2. Strengthen the operation of scenario- based content to realize the
translation of emotional value.
The core of emotional marketing is not simply creating emotions, but rather placing

the content of the books  in specific life scenarios, allowing consumers to perceive
the connection between the content and their own experiences. Publishing houses
can express their content through channels such as short videos, social media, and
reading communities, focusing on the author's story, reading experience, and user
feedback, transforming abstract cultural values into perceptible, participatory, and
communicable emotional experiences.
5.3. Build a user relationship ecosystem to promote the continuous
accumulation of emotional value.
The ultimate goal of emotional marketing is not a single purchase, but the
establishment of a long-term user relationship. Publishing institutions can carry out
community operations around authors, works, and reading themes. By conducting
online shared reading, topic discussions, and user interactions, they can enhance the
sense of participation. At the same time, through author brand building, content IP
development, and user sharing, they can drive consumers to transform from
purchasers t o brand disseminators and value creators, achieving continuous
accumulation of emotional value.
6. Conclusion
In the new consumption era, book consumption has shifted from merely obtaining
knowledge to experiencing emotions, developing cultural identity, and expressing
one's self. The traditional marketing model centered on products and channels is
facing the need for transformation. This article, based on the theories of
consumption society, hierarchy of needs, and user lifecycle, analyzes the formation
logic and practical mechanism of emotional marketing for books. The study suggests
that scenario reconstruction is an important mechanism for connecting the value of
book content with users' emotional needs: spatial scenarios enable user access,
emotional scenarios promote value resonance, and relationship scenarios drive user
retention. Together, these three elements constitute the emotional marketing path
of "access - resonance - retention".
Based on this, publishing institutions should promote marketing t ransformation
from three aspects: user emotional insight, scenario- based content expression, and
user relationship operation, achieving a transition from product marketing to user
operation and from traffic acquisition to long -term value creation. In the f uture,
specific publishing cases can be combined to further verify the impact of different
scenarios on consumption behavior and brand value through user research and data
analysis. Overall, in the new consumption era, the core competitiveness of book
marketing is shifting from content advantages to value connection capabilities.
Scenario-based emotional marketing will become an important direction for
publishing marketing innovation.
