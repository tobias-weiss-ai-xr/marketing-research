# Drivers of Brand Image and Consumer Engagement: The Role of User-generated Content, Brand Storytelling and Collaboration on Online Platforms

Abstract
Purpose: This research aims to establish an understanding of the drivers of
brand engagement and brand image on websites and social media platforms,
through independent variables such as brand storytelling, user-generated content
(UGC) and collaboration within the S-O-R framework. Given rapidly changing
trends and simultaneous consumer preferences, understanding the key drivers
behind these shifts is essential.
Methodology: This study uses a standardised online survey tool to gather
data from a diverse group of individuals in the state of Gujarat, India. This
study analyses the data through partial least squares–structural equation mod-
elling (PLS-SEM) to understand the key drivers of brand image and consumer
engagement.
Findings: These findings of the study show positive responses for all the vari-
ables, specifically brand storytelling and UGC in context with social media, which
indicated shaping consumer engagement and brand image. However, collabora-
tion did not necessarily indicate a positive impact on both consumer engagement
and brand image. Also, in the context of websites, UGC did not have a positive
impact on consumer engagement.

Introduction
The digital ecosystem has transformed brand communication into a multidirec -
tional and engaging process through which consumers actively co-create content
with brands. With the emergence of social networks and community-based con-
tent creation on websites, marketers are increasingly required to build content that
demonstrates authenticity through storytelling and collaborative strategies
(Dwivedi et al., 2023). The online industry has seen countless variations in trends,
especially with the expansion of e-commerce and social media platforms, result-
ing in the emergence of several factors that influence consumers’ purchase deci-
sions. With improved technologies and the increased popularity of e-commerce
websites and social media platforms, audiences are continuously exposed to
diverse online stimuli (Davies et al., 2024), making them more influenced by
newer styles, trends and brand narratives on the internet. Consequently, factors
influencing buyers’ purchase decisions online can be highly influenced by multi-
ple external and internal elements, which further contribute to the formation of
brand image and engagement (de Almeida et al., 2025).
Brand image, which has been understood widely in marketing literature as a set
 of associations, perceptions and emotions held by consumers towards a brand
(Keller, 1993), while consumer engagement reflects the degree of cognitive, emo-
tional and behavioural involvement of consumers with the brand-related content
(Brodie et al., 2011). As organisations transition from firm-centric to co-creation-
based communications, platforms such as a brand’s websites, blogs, brand apps
and social media platforms facilitate this transition. Therefore, the expansion of
the digital ecosystem has significantly accelerated community content creation.
Post-COVID studies indicate that consumers increasingly rely on online product
reviews given by users of the product on the internet (Tao Chen, 2022), which
indicates increasing influence and credibility in user-generated content (UGC).
Similarly, Hanadi and Salhab (2023) emphasised that social media is a key driver
for a positive brand image and also affects consumers’ purchase intention signifi-
cantly. Further, celebrity endorsement and collaborations with influencers, who
communicate compelling narratives that resonate with the brand’s audiences
(Pattipeilohy & Riry, 2019), strengthen consumer trust and enhance brand recog-
nition and perception towards the brand (Evan, 2021).

Although prior studies acknowledge the increasing importance of multidimen-
sional online strategies in shaping consumer behaviour (Osorio-Andrade et al.,
2025), existing literature has largely examined UGC, brand storytelling and col-
laborations independently. Limited research has attempted to integrate these
dimensions within a single framework to understand their simultaneous impact on
consumers’ psychological and behavioural responses, such as shaping brand
image and consumer engagement in digital environments. This indicates a signifi-
cant theoretical and contextual gap in understanding the combined role of these
digital communication strategies within the broader online ecosystem.
Grounded in the stimulus–organism–response (S-O-R) framework (Mehrabian
& Russell, 1974), this study conceptualises UGC, brand storytelling and collabo-
rations as stimuli that influence consumers’ internal evaluations, leading to
responses in the form of enhanced brand image and consumer engagement. By
integrating these dimensions into a unified framework, the study seeks to extend
the existing literature on digital marketing communication and provide empirical
insights into how brands can strategically utilise content marketing strategies for
strengthening consumer–brand relationships in the digital ecosystem.
Literature Review
Theoretical Framework: The S-O-R Model
To provide a robust theoretical underpinning for the relationships proposed in this
study, the Stimulus-Organism-Response (S-O-R) framework (Mehrabian & Russell,
1974) is integrated. The S-O-R model posits that external stimuli (S) influence
internal organismic states (O), which subsequently lead to behavioural responses
(R). In the context of digital fashion branding, elements like brand storytelling,
UGC and collaborations act as stimuli (S). These elements are perceived and pro-
cessed by consumers through cognitive and emotional evaluations (O), which then
influence outcomes such as brand image and engagement (R). This framework sup-
ports previous empirical assertions (Hollebeek et al., 2014) that digital content
influences internal psychological states before triggering brand-related behaviours,
thereby justifying the chosen relationships within the proposed model.
Brand Storytelling, Social Media and Websites
For a brand to create and foster a healthy customer base has to emotionally con-
nect with the target audience by fabricating a brand image for their audiences. To
sustain a stable customer base, marketers should aim to develop deeper, long-
lasting and strong brand attachments for consumers (Iveson et al., 2026). For this,
marketers take the assistance of brand storytelling. Brand storytelling is to share
any story or belief with a target group to arouse bonds in people’s minds or emo-
tions so that such a brand becomes more attractive, more recognised and provokes
relationships among people in the organisation and with other stakeholders out-
side the organisation (Kosakarika, 2023). Storytelling has emerged as an effective
content marketing strategy that helps brands build meaningful relationships with

consumers by creating emotionally appealing narratives. Such narratives
strengthen consumer–brand engagement and encourage stronger brand loyalty by
making audiences feel more connected to the brand (Appel et al., 2020). Research
also suggests that emotional engagement developed through storytelling reflects
the extent to which consumers form deeper psychological connections with
brands (Dessart & Pitardi, 2019). In addition, immersive storytelling experiences
supported through interactive and vivid content can further enhance audience
involvement and engagement. When consumers feel absorbed in a brand narra-
tive, their emotional attachment and sense of presence increase, making storytell-
ing an important mechanism for fostering authentic consumer–brand relationships
(Ahmed et al., 2024). A powerful brand story imprints a positive brand idea
towards the target audience. The online industry is fuelled by content, and there -
fore, content marketing appears to be the best for approaching and creating brand
image and personality. With a major involvement of influencers, brands can intro-
duce themselves in a very clean and crafted manner. Brand storytelling is useful
for building strong brands and brand loyalty because it has the power to persuade
consumers, entertain them and imprint a brand in their minds (JungHwa Hong,
2022). Thus, the study hypothesises:
H1: Brand storytelling has a positive relationship with social media usage.
H2: Brand storytelling has a positive relationship with website usage.
Collaboration, Social Media and Websites
In the digital marketing age, where people have a very media-driven lifestyle,
marketers have evolved their marketing approach towards the marketplace. Now,
the content is generated by popular and influential people posting content on the
online space, known as influencers. An influencer is an individual with a large
following and has a powerful influence on their followers, their opinions and
sometimes their decisions. Celebrities, social media stars, industrial experts,
brand ambassadors, content creators—bloggers, videographers/vloggers, artists,
podcasters and photographers—are the influencers of the modern marketplace.
Influencers, due to their large follower base and niche content creation across
social media platforms, foster higher audience interaction and engagement as fol-
lowers often relate to or aspire to the lifestyles and values reflected in their content
(Levin et al., 2025). Previous studies have suggested that collaborations with
influencers who create relatable and informative content, particularly when
aligned with expert perspectives and consistent with their existing values, enhance
authenticity and strengthen audience engagement (Paraskeva et al., 2024). The
online industry was initially governed by very niche fashion setters, but now it has
opened up. The influencers are the driving force of various categories, such as
fashion, tech, beauty, lifestyle and travel, and play a very crucial role in trend-
setting. Due to consistent content posting about trends and recommendations,
they exert an influential force on their followers to engage with their suggestions
(Nurfadila, 2020). Social media and the employment of influencers by brands are

H3: Collaborations have a positive relationship with social media usage.
H4: Collaborations have a positive relationship with website usage.
UGC, Social Media and Websites
Brands may now effectively engage with their audience and create a feeling of
community by using UGC (Ploscă et al., 2024). It has the potential to significantly
impact brand perception, consumer loyalty, brand image and purchasing decisions
in the online space. Gharib et al. (2019) contend that better product development
and consumer comprehension can be achieved by studying UGC. Businesses can
get insights that guide their product design and development process and result in
more effective and relevant offers by analysing the feedback, preferences and
experiences that consumers share (Fu et al., 2024). Similarly, Djafarova and
Bowes (2021) contend that because of its easily navigable design and freely avail-
able UGC, social media, especially Instagram, has grown in popularity as a plat -
form for online purchasing. Because they believe UGC, often referred to as
electronic word-of-mouth (eWOM), to be truthful and grounded in real-world
experiences, consumers are more likely to trust it than traditional marketing com-
munications. UGC is important to drive customer interaction, shape brand percep-
tion and influence purchase decisions (Osorio-Andrade et al., 2025). Through its
insights into consumer preferences, UGC helps firms generate more relevant
products and improve their product development (Gharib et al., 2019).
Furthermore, UGC is beneficial in establishing trust and encouraging customer
loyalty because of its authenticity and legitimacy (Djafarova & Bowes, 2021).
Even though previous studies have recognised the significance of UGC, there is
still a significant knowledge vacuum in understanding how UGC, along with
brand storytelling and collaboration, can help generate positive brand engagement
and develop brand image on websites and social media. Thus, the study
hypothesises:
H7: UGC leads to more social media usage.
H8: UGC strengthens the website’s credibility.
Social Media
Social media has completely changed how companies communicate with their
customers. Social media channels present a distinctive chance for brands to inter-
act with clients, increase brand recognition and cultivate customer loyalty. Kim
and Ko (2012) state that to sustain an exclusive and appealing image, luxury
brands can use social media platforms as a potent tool for storytelling, interaction
and communication with their target audience, while Lu et al. (2022) mention that
social media has developed into an essential tool for B2C companies to commu -
nicate with clients. Crafting compelling communications is still difficult, though.
This review of the literature looks at how customer engagement on social media

is affected by the message source and message content (emojis vs. objective infor-
mation). According to available data, the message’s source and content affect con-
sumers’ perceptions and levels of trust, which in turn affects engagement.
However, knowledge of how these components work together to promote engage-
ment in a business-to-business setting is lacking. Liu et al. (2020) suggest a tech-
nique that combines human judgement with big data from social media networks
to enhance demand forecasts for new items. They illustrate the difficulties in
accurately classifying social media’s influence factors on demand forecasting,
converting insights into judgements that can be implemented and maintaining
data dependability. They also show how effective this method is in a real-world
scenario. The literature review investigates how social media influences con-
sumer experiences and propels brand success. It emphasises how crucial social
media is to the storytelling and engagement of luxury brands, as well as the effi-
cient communication of B2C businesses. However, the study finds a knowledge
vacuum on how message source and content interact to shape customer engage -
ment, especially in business-to-business settings. Subsequent investigations may
probe this field more thoroughly and examine the enduring effects of social media
involvement on customer conduct and brand allegiance.
Websites
In the past 10 years, websites have transformed, becoming a significant element
of digital brand strategy, serving as a dynamic platform that shapes consumer
perceptions and nurtures brand engagement (Shehu et al., 2021). In the fashion
industry, a brand’s website functions as a primary digital touchpoint that has the
ability to influence consumer behaviour through visual aesthetics, interactive
interface and content framework. A coherent, streamlined website with clear navi-
gations and personalised elements can significantly boost the user experience and
nurture consumer retention (Natasha et al., 2021).
As per recent studies, incorporation of brand storytelling, collaborations and
UGC into a website design can increase emotional resonance and engagement
(Krishen et al., 2021). Similarly, when storytelling is embedded into a brand’s
digital interface, it creates a profound psychological connection with the users.
Likewise, collaborations on websites provide authenticity and cultural relevance,
strengthening user trust and involvement (Yin et al., 2026). In the same way,
UGC, on a brand’s website, builds social proof and improves interactivity, dem-
onstrating brand loyalty and authenticity (Jiang et al., 2025).
Thoughtful website functionalities further support brand image formation and
sustained engagement (Xu & Schrier, 2019). Through these dynamics, which
demonstrate websites as aid to strategic conduit, brands can operationalise their
digital narratives and co-create value with consumers.
Social Media, Websites and Brand Image
Brand image is a consumer’s perception of a particular brand, which can be
related to the product quality, services or experiences. The online industry is a

rapidly growing industry where marketers have to leave a lasting impression to
build a good brand image, and this is where engagement and personalisation
play their role in building a brand image. Keller (1993) mentioned that the
reflection that the consumer holds about a particular brand depends on how they
perceive it and what they think about it. Kotler and Keller (2016) believe a
brand image to be the way that customers view a company, shaped by things like
experiences, services and quality of products. They underline that brand image
is vital in creating consumer satisfaction and loyalty. Besides the significant
impact of brand image in building loyalty, it holds a major role in creating
engagement, which also positively impacts brand loyalty (Kim & Ko, 2012).
Prior studies have also validated that the emotional association with a brand
strengthens brand loyalty, which the brands leverage for premium prices
(Diniso, 2025). Similarly, in the fashion sector, customer engagement and loy-
alty are greatly influenced by brand image. Customers’ perceptions of a brand
are greatly influenced by their experiences, services and product quality.
Enhanced consumer satisfaction and loyalty can result from better brand inter -
action achieved through these components (Orús et al., 2026). Furthermore,
brand image enhancement can be achieved through experiences that incorporate
components such as UGC (Lanz et al., 2024), brand storytelling (Ahmed et al.,
2024) and collaboration (Ika Sari et al., 2024). These tactics in influencing con-
sumer perceptions and emotional ties to the brand have been widely examined
in the literature. Through the cultivation of genuine relationships, brands may
bolster their trustworthiness and hence cultivate a more positive brand percep-
tion. Thus, the study hypothesises:
H5: Social media usage strengthens the brand image.
H9: Websites positively impact the brand image.
Social Media, Websites and Brand Engagement
With the rapid changes in online consumption behaviour, brands are under pres-
sure to provide customers with distinctive and customised experiences. To develop
strong brand ties and encourage consumer loyalty, engagement is essential
(Aldhamiri et al., 2024). Hollebeek and Macky (2019) argue that relationships,
trust and brand engagement can only be developed through digital content mar -
keting. It promotes behavioural, emotional and cognitive engagement through the
provision of quality content, which in turn increases favourable attitudes, brand
equity and brand trust. Pizzi et al. (2022) suggest that engagement is a multidi -
mensional construct of immersion, passion and activation and that interaction,
brand attachment, brand trust and brand knowledge all contribute to brand engage-
ment in the metaverse. They contend that these elements eventually influence
consumers’ inclinations to make in-person purchases and are favourably influ-
enced by the immersive experiences provided by the metaverse. Previous studies
have observed that if engagement with the content satisfies the motivation, then
the content marketing can strongly influence the advertising value (Mirzaei et al.,
2024). By examining the distinct engagement drivers, this research aims to close

Research Methodology
Data Collection and Sample Characteristics
To address our research questions, we gathered data through a survey from indi-
viduals in India who are active social media users. We used a convenience sam-
pling approach to reach out to the target population through social media
platforms such as WhatsApp, LinkedIn, Instagram and e-mails, asking them to
share their perspectives. The survey was done from June to October 2024 to
collect the data with the help of a structured self-administered questionnaire
(Malhotra, 2004). The questionnaire was divided into two parts: the first being
the data about the demographics of the respondents, while the second part
addressed the variables of the research model (Figure 1), which were measured
through a 5-point Likert scale, where 1 represented strongly disagree and 5 rep-
resented strongly agree. The power estimation technique was employed to
determine the sample size. G* Power software was used for calculating the
required sample size. With an effect size of 0.15, a power value of 0.95 and a
maximum of 8 predictors, the required sample size was calculated as 74. A total
of 154 responses were collected, of which only 137 were selected for final anal-
ysis after eliminating incomplete responses, missing values and outliers, and the
given sample size is more than the required sample size. These 137 responses
were used for final analysis through partial least squares–structural modelling
(PLS-SEM) in SmartPLS. Table 1 presents the descriptive characteristics of the
sample.

Discussion and Conclusion
The results show that social media platforms, particularly Instagram, play a signifi-
cant role in influencing consumer behaviour and brand perception and consumers
strongly believe that fashion brands can increase engagement by prioritising social
media marketing, making it the most effective strategy, followed by meaningful
brand interactions and building a strong brand image. According to the results,
influencer marketing and UGC, such as reviews and social media posts, are impor-
tant; they are given somewhat less weight than celebrity endorsements, social
responsibility programmes and personal interactions.
Nonetheless, social media engagement with brands through the introduction of
new products and tailored experiences turned out to be important factors in deter-
mining customer satisfaction. It has been determined that elements such as prod-
uct quality, cost and design have a major impact on how consumers perceive a
brand, making them crucial for building loyalty, as per the findings.
The results highlight how UGC, brand storytelling and collaboration shape
consumer engagement and brand image on online platforms. Brand storytelling
has a moderate impact on website interactions (H 2) and a somewhat significant
impact on social media (H1), which supports its function in fostering brand image
and consumer engagement. These findings are consistent with those of Dolan
et al. (2019), who highlighted how well-structured and captivating storylines
impact audience engagement.

When it comes to influencing customer engagement and brand image, collabo-
ration produces mixed results. H 3 is not supported, implying that collaboration
has little effect on social media engagement; however, H 4 is supported, demon-

imply that although collaboration works well in controlled settings like websites,
it cannot be the main factor influencing participation on dynamic social media
platforms (Smith & Taylor, 2019).
Additionally, the impact of social media on brand image (H 5) and consumer
engagement (H6) is demonstrated. H5 is supported, suggesting that social media,
with the help of UGC (supported in results) and brand storytelling, results in
higher levels of customer engagement and boosts brand image, which indicates
that consumers may prefer consuming such content on social media that they can
trust, instead of collaborations where a sign of consumers are pushed to consume
content trying to sell a brand/product. This implies that although social media
improves brand image, further components like content generated by consumers
themselves and emotional associations with a brand through storytelling might be
needed for engagement (Kaplan & Haenlein, 2021).
On the other hand, one of the most important factors shaping consumer percep-
tion and brand image is UGC (H7) because it provides unbiased views to consum-
ers, demonstrating that UGC has a beneficial impact on social media engagement
and strengthens the interactive associations that consumers frame with the brand.
However, H8, which proposed that UGC also impacts websites to build brand
image and consumer engagement, has been rejected. This implies that UGC has a
limited role in static online platforms like websites, in contrast to interactive plat-
forms like social media (Kumar et al., 2020). This can be justified by knowing the
essence of UGC, where it requires consumer–brand interactions, which can be
noticed more on social media platforms.
Similarly, there are conflicting results from websites and their impact on con-
sumer engagement and brand image. Results for H 9 show that websites have a
major impact on brand image, highlighting the significance of well-designed digi-
tal platforms for the growth of brands (Chaffey & Smith, 2021), which implies
that the presence of a well-made brand website can build trust, which transforms
into a soothing brand image. However, H10 is not supported, indicating that web-
sites need to incorporate some interactive elements in their websites to increase
customer engagement.
The results of the study align with Ajzen (1991), which emphasises how social
media usage affects customer attitudes and actions. This study confirms that
developing strong relationships with customers and building a positive brand
image on digital channels requires meaningful storytelling, regular content distri-
bution and interactive participation. High-quality goods and gripping stories can
be combined by brands to produce unforgettable customer experiences that leave
a lasting impact for strengthening brand image and encouraging engagement.
In conclusion, this study highlights the crucial role of social media, particularly
Instagram, in shaping brand image and consumers’ perception of the brand.

Managerial Implications
The findings of this study offer valuable insights for managers and decision-mak-
ers in the online industry, particularly leveraging social media marketing to
enhance consumer engagement and brand storytelling (Godey et al., 2016).
Managers should prioritise social media platforms as key strategic tools for shap-
ing consumer behaviour and brand image (Kaplan & Haenlein, 2010). Focusing
on improving UGC through customer satisfaction and frequent personalised
social media content updates, and collaboration can boost engagement, create
deep brand connections and improve the brand image (Schivinski & Dabrowski,
2016). To keep customers confident and loyal, content must be of the highest cali-
bre, coherently organised and constant (Ashley & Tuten, 2014).
Managers should concentrate on delivering consistent brand stories on all
social media platforms to guarantee a seamless consumer experience and
strengthen brand image. Managers should especially invest in social media that
promotes two-way communication, which can help them deepen customer
engagement. Although influencer marketing and UGC may not hold the highest
priority, they remain influential in building trust and authenticity (Djafarova &
Rushworth, 2017). In addition to encouraging customers to provide evaluations
and material that represent their brand experiences, managers should work with
relevant influencers (Evans et al., 2017). Managers should combine social media
initiatives with e-mail marketing, in-store promotions and loyalty programmes as
part of a larger multi-channel strategy to optimise their impact. E-mail discounts
that are tailored to a recipient’s social media activity, for example, can increase
engagement and conversions (Ashley & Tuten, 2014). By implementing these
strategies, brands can strengthen their market position and foster customer
loyalty, engagement and sustainable growth in today’s dynamic and competitive
digital landscape (Kaplan & Haenlein, 2010).
Theoretical Implications
This study provides crucial theoretical insights into the relation of social media,
personalisation, engagement and consumer behaviour. It highlights the transfor -
mative role of social media platforms, especially Instagram, in shaping consumer
perception of the brand image and driving their buying behaviour (Godey et al.,
2016). By examining key dimensions of UGC, such as content quality, consistent
content and interactivity, the research expands existing frameworks surrounding

consumer experiences (Schivinski & Dabrowski, 2016). The study highlights the
importance of relevant, consistent content and fostering two-way communication
to improve brand engagement and brand image, effectively blending the concepts
of brand storytelling, digital communication (through social media) and consumer
engagement (Ashley & Tuten, 2014). Additionally, it also broadens knowledge of
multi-channel marketing tactics by showing how social media initiatives can sup-
port more conventional platforms like websites to grow on online platforms
(Kaplan & Haenlein, 2010).
The differential impact observed across platforms (i.e., social media versus web-
sites) highlights the organismic filtering process within the S-O-R model. The inef-
fectiveness of UGC on websites, as opposed to social media, underscores that
stimulus effectiveness is context-sensitive and moderated by platform-specific fea-
tures that shape consumer engagement and brand image as a response. Therefore,
the study extends the S-O-R framework by emphasising the relevance of authentic
content in the form of brand storytelling and user-generated content, acting as a
stimulus, generating differing responses of brand image and consumer engagement
through different organism contexts, that is, websites and social media. This model
focuses on content strategy, which represents platform-specific principles, adapt-
able to changing technological advances such as AI and others that will follow.
Limitations and Future Research
The study mainly focuses on the impact of certain factors on social media and
websites to build brand image and consumer engagement. This research may have
overlooked studies on different industries or may have different contexts related
to these factors. The data collection is majorly confined to the area of Gujarat, and
the audience is available through social media channels like Instagram, Facebook
and LinkedIn, resulting in region-specific and platform-specific outcomes; there-
fore, it is not applicable universally. Also, convenience sampling was used, which
may not represent the target audience completely. This article can serve as a foun-
dation for research in the same domain but in different geographical regions.
