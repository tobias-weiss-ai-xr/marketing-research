By Sara Salem Bahbaishi1, Abrar Sameer Khayyat2

ABSTRACT:
Meme-based promotional tactics have become a prevalent strategy in social media marketing;
nonetheless, empirical validation of their cross -cultural effectiveness continues to be scarce. The
current study reapplies and regionally tailors the methodological  design proposed by Rathi and Jain
(2024) to investigate how meme marketing initiatives shape purchase intentions among Saudi social
media consumers, while positioning consumer engagement as the intervening mechanism. Data were
collected via an online, cross -sectional quantitative survey. A total of 284 eligible Saudi participants,
ranging between 18 and 40 years of age, completed a 22 -item survey derived from pre -validated
psychometric measures, which was subsequently rendered into Arabic following a forward-backward
translation protocol. Analytical procedures relied upon Partial Least Squares Structural Equation
Modeling (PLS-SEM). Empirical outcomes indicated that meme marketing practices exert a strong,
positive influence on engagement (β = 0.692, p < .001), and that engagement, in turn, serves as a
significant positive predictor of purchase intention (β = 0.373, p < .001). The observation of a
significant direct path between meme marketing and purchase intention (β = 0.465, p  < .001),
alongside an indirect effect through engagement (β = 0.258, p < .001), provides support for a partial
mediation effect. These results not only reaffirm but also broaden existing scholarly evidence to the
Arabian Gulf region, thereby enriching the  theoretical underpinnings of Uses and Gratifications
Theory and Social Cognitive Theory. On a practical level, the findings deliver data-informed strategic
insights for brand stewards and digital communication practitioners active in the Saudi market.

Keywords: Meme Marketing; Consumer Engagement; Purchase Intention; Social Media Marketing; Saudi Arabia;
Replication Study

1. Introduction

The rapid expansion of social media has fundamentally altered the digital
marketing landscape. Within this environment, meme marketing — the deliberate use of
culturally resonant, shareable digital content to stimulate brand engagement and message
diffusion — has gained substantial commercial traction (Malodia et al., 2022; Razzaq et al.,
2023). Brands such as Heinz, Gucci, and Wendy’s have leveraged meme campaigns to
generate millions of impressions at relatively low cost (Bury, 2016; Malodia et al., 2022).
Compared to the 1% click-through rate associated with standard online ads, meme-driven
social media campaigns generate a substantially higher engagement figure of 30%
(Paquette, 2019).
Despite its growing prevalence, empirical research on meme marketing (MM)
effectiveness remains limited in scope and geographical reach. Most existing studies are

million social media users accounting for 94.3% of the population (Global Media Insight,

outcome of Vision 2030—the country’s overarching modernization initiative—the Saudi
digital marketplace has grown rapidly, and social media platforms have concurrently
become essential vehicles for marketing communications and consumer involvement
(Alharthi et al., 2026). Understanding how digital marketing mechanisms such as meme
marketing influence consumer behavior in this rapidly evolving market is therefore of both
academic and practical significance. Saudi Arabia’s digital landscape is also platform -
specific: the country has among the world’s highest per -capita rates of Snapchat and
Twitter/X usage, alongside rapid growth in TikTok among younger audiences, providing
fertile ground for meme-based marketing content. Moreover, Saudi consumer behavior is
shaped by a distinctive cultural context rooted in Islamic values, collectivist social norms,
and a strong emphasis on family and community; drawing on Hofstede’s (1980) cultural
dimensions framework, Saudi Arabia scores high on collectivism, dynamics that amplify
social modeling effects and give peer endorsement and social proof heightened persuasive
weight (Long & Maisel, 2010). Islamic cultural norms further shape the boundaries of
acceptable marketing communication, as memes respectful of religious and family values
are more likely to resonate positively, while culturally incongruent content may generate
adverse consumer reactions (Sudigdo & Khalifa, 2020). This cultural specificity reinforces
the necessity of context -specific empirical research rather than assuming direct
transferability of findings from non -Muslim majority markets such as India, where the
source study was conducted. Yet, as Alsaadi et al. (2024) note, research on digital marketing
and purchase intention (CPI) in Saudi Arabia remains limited, and no empirical study has
tested the meme marketing–engagement–purchase intention pathway in this market.
    To bridge the existing research gaps, the current study reapplies the framework
established by Rathi and Jain (2024) within the Saudi Arabian context. The empirical
inquiry is structured around three pivotal aims: evaluating the direct influence of mem e-
centric promotional tactics on consumer engagement, determining the subsequent effect
of this engagement on CPIs, and exploring the mediating mechanism through which
engagement transmits the impact of meme marketing to consumer behavior .
Methodologically, it relies on a quantitative approach, gathering primary data through a
cross-sectional survey and employing PLS -SEM for statistical inference. The projected
contributions are twofold: it provides a robust empirical validation of the meme marketing
model in an Arab Gulf setting, while simultaneously offering actionable, data -driven
implications for digital marketing practitioners operating in Saudi Arabia.

2. Theoretical Background

Dawkins (1976) originally introduced the term Internet meme to describe cultural
units disseminated through behavioral copying. Shifman (2013) subsequently refined this
understanding, characterizing internet memes as digitally borne material passed across
individuals, with the potential for modification as it moves through networks. In
marketing, MM refers to the strategic deployment of such content by brands to stimulate
audience interaction and message diffusion (Razzaq et al., 2023). The literature identifies
five key dimensions of MM activities: interaction, trendiness, entertainment,
informativeness, and electronic wo rd-of-mouth (e -WOM; Bilgin, 2018; Sharma et al.,
2021; Yadav & Rahman, 2018).
The advantages of MM are well documented. Meme -based campaigns cost less
than traditional digital promotion (Bury, 2016; Williams, 2000). Paquette (2019) reports a
30% social media engagement rate for MM, compared to just 1% for Google AdWords
click-through rates a striking contrast that reflects the power of relatable, shareable
content. Memes utilizing popular templates lower the cognitive effort required for
audiences to contextualize a brand message (Malodia  et al., 2022), and their creation is
relatively simple, enabling rapid response to trending cultural moments. Crucially, MM
transcends traditional unidirectional communication by positioning consumers as active
participants and co-creators (Kozinets et al., 2008; Merz et al., 2009).
Drawing on established social media marketing literature (Bilgin, 2018; Sharma et
al., 2021; Yadav & Rahman, 2018), Rathi and Jain (2024) operationalized MM activities as
a multi -dimensional construct with five sub -dimensions. Entertainment captures the
enjoyability and interest of brand meme content. Interactiveness  reflects the degree to
which meme content enables information sharing and opinion exchange. Trendiness
measures the currency and cultural relevance of meme content. Informativeness assesses
the utility of memes in conveying accurate product -related information. Finally, e-WOM
drives consumers to share and recommend brand memes, which is how memes spread
organically. This five -factor framework serves as the measurement basis for the
independent variable in the present study.
     Despite the growing commercial adoption of MM, the empirical literature
remains limited, and most early studies were qualitative or case-based in nature (Brubaker
et al., 2018; Chuah et al., 2020). More recent quantitative work has begun to address th is
gap. Meme marketing grabs consumers’ attention and boosts their purchase intentions by
pairing smart, funny content with current cultural events and news. (Lee et al., 2019). Vasile
et al. (2021) found that memes shape consumer attitudes, perceptions, and CPIs in digitally
connected environments. Nanayakkara and Liyanage (2021) provided cross -cultural
empirical evidence from Sri Lanka that MM improves online consumer engagement. Most
comprehensively, Rathi and Jain (2024) the source study for the present replication applied
Structural Equation Modelling (SEM) to a sample of 452 Indian social media users and
confirmed that MM activities have a positive influence on ENG (β = 0.745, p < 0.05) and
CPI (β = 0.257, p < 0.05), with ENG partially mediating the la tter relationship (indirect
effect β = 0.485, p < 0.05). Collectively, this body of evidence establishes a consistent
pattern; however, most of the studies are based in Western or South and Southeast Asian
contexts, leaving the Saudi market entirely unexam ined. Notably, no empirical study to
date has investigated MM within the Saudi cultural context, representing a significant
geographical and cultural gap in the literature that the present study directly addresses.

2.2 Theoretical Underpinning
This study applies two complementary theoretical frameworks, Social Cognitive Theory
(SCT) and Uses and Gratifications Theory (UGT), both employed by Rathi and Jain (2024)
and directly relevant to explaining how MM shapes consumer behaviour on social media.
2.2.1 Social Cognitive Theory
     SCT, developed by Bandura (1986), suggests that individuals gain knowledge, attitudes,
and behaviors through observational learning and social modeling. People observe others,
particularly those perceived as influential or similar, and adopt comparable behaviors when
they anticipate positive reinforcement. In the MM context, SCT explains the viral spread
of brand content: when consumers observe peers eng aging with brand memes, the
embedded social proof legitimizes and encourages similar behavior in others (Das, 2023).
This dynamic is especially potent among Gen Y and Gen Z users and is amplified in
collectivist contexts such as Saudi Arabia, where in -group peer influence strongly shapes
individual behavior (Long & Maisel, 2010). This supports hypothesis 1, as peer modelling
drives consumer engagement with brand memes.
2.2.2 Uses and Gratifications Theory
      UGT frames audiences as active and goal-directed in their media consumption. UGT
was originally conceptualized by Katz and Blumler (1974) for traditional broadcast media
and later extended to social media (Cahya & Triputra, 2017; Das, 2023). Rather than
passively absorbing content, individuals select platforms and specific content types based
on the specific needs they seek to satisf y: entertainment, information, social identity
expression, or community belonging. When brand memes satisfy these needs
simultaneously, they generate deep, voluntary, and intrinsically motivated engagement that
is especially likely to influence purchase inte ntions (Bolos et al., 2016). In Saudi Arabia,
where social media use is driven by high entertainment and social connectivity motivations
(Global Media Insight, 2023), UGT offers a culturally fitting lens for understanding
consumer responses to memes. Toget her, SCT and UGT provide mutually reinforcing
explanations for MM’s influence: SCT accounts for norm-driven behavior change through
social modeling, while UGT explains active, need -driven content engagement. These
frameworks collectively inform hypothesis2, as gratification derived from memes fosters
purchase intention.

2.3 Meme marketing and Consumer Engagement
MM has emerged as an interactive form of social media marketing that enables brands to
communicate with consumers through entertaining, relatable, timely, and shareable
content. In the present study, MM activities comprise informativeness, trendiness,
interaction, entertainment, and e -WOM, reflecting both the content characteristics of
memes and their capacity to stimulate consumer participation. These characteristics can
encourage consumers to visit brand pages, read brand memes, like content, comment, and
interact with brand -related posts. The relationship between MM and consumer
engagement (ENG) can be explained through UGT and SCT. UGT explains that
consumers pick media content based on what they’re looking for, whether that’s
entertainment, learning som ething new, or interacting socially. Since memes can
simultaneously provide entertainment and opportunities for social interaction, consumers

2.4 Consumer Engagement and Purchase Intention
Conceptualized by Vivek et al. (2012), ENG is defined as the level of a customer’s active
mental, bodily, and affective participation in brand -related encounters. Extant research
suggests a positive association between engagement levels and CPIs (Boyer & Hult, 2006;
Rosetta Consulting, 2014). In contrast, CPI, following Wells et al. (2011), denotes the
subjective probability of acquiring a marketed good or service subsequent to  being
exposed to promotional material. Importantly, engagement is posited to influence
consumer purchasing responses by augmenting brand familiarity and cementing the
psychological rapport between the consumer and the brand. When consumers repeatedly
interact with brand content, they may develop more favorable  perceptions and stronger
interest in the brand’s products or services. From a UGT perspective, satisfying
entertainment, informational, and social needs through branded memes can create positive
associations with the brand, which may contribute to CPIs. S imilarly, SCT suggests that
observing the engagement of others with brand -related content can provide social cues
that influence consumers’ own attitudes and behavioral  intentions. Previous research
provides empirical support for the association of ENG and CPI. Boyer and Hult (2006)
found that ENG significantly predicts online CPIs, while  Toor et al.  (2017) demonstrated
the importance of ENG in the relationship between social media activities and CPI. Most
importantly, Rathi and Jain (2024) found a significant positive effect of ENG on CPI.
Therefore, consumers who actively engage with brand-related meme content are expected
to demonstrate stronger CPIs.
H2: Consumer engagement has a significant positive effect on consumer purchase
intention.

2.5 Consumer Engagement Mediation
ENG may serve as an important mechanism through which MM activities influence CPI.
MM can initially attract consumers through entertaining, informative, interactive, and
socially relevant content. When consumers respond to such content by visiting brand
pages, reading memes, liking posts, or commenting on brand content, they become more
actively involved with the brand. This increased engagement can strengthen brand
familiarity, positive affect, and social connection, which may subsequently enhance CPI
(Toor et al., 2017; Vivek et al., 2012). The mediating role of ENG is also supported
theoretically. UGT suggests that people don’t just consume media randomly; they actively
pick content that fulfills their specific desires, whether that’s entertainment, learning, or

socializing (Katz & Blumler, 1974). When MM fulfills these needs, consumers may become
more involved with the brand, creating favorable conditions for purchase -related
responses. Similarly, SCT suggests that social interactions and observed behaviors withi n
online networks can reinforce consumers’ attitudes and behavioral  intentions (Bandura,
1986; Das, 2023). Rathi and Jain (2024) provide direct empirical support for this proposed
mediation relationship. Their study found that ENG partially mediates the relationship
between MM activities and CPI. This indicates that MM ca n influence CPI not only
through direct persuasion but also by first stimulating ENG. The present study extends
this framework by examining whether this mediating mechanism is also applicable among
social media users in Saudi Arabia. Therefore, the following hypothesis is proposed:
H3: Consumer engagement mediates the relationship between meme marketing activities
and consumer purchase intention.
MM may influence CPI directly because memes can communicate product -related
information while simultaneously creating entertaining, relatable, and culturally relevant
brand experiences. Unlike conventional advertising, memes can integrate marketing
messages into content that consumers perceive as socially relevant and enjoyable. Such
characteristics can increase attention toward the brand and influence consumers’
perceptions and purchase-related attitudes (Lee et al., 2019; Vasile et al., 2021). Previous
research provides evidence that meme -based communication can influence consumer
purchase-related responses. Lee et al. (2019) demonstrated that internet memes can
increase consumers’ inclination toward purchasing associated products, while Vasile et al.
(2021) found that memes can shape consumer attitudes and behavioral intentions by
connecting brands with cultural identity and social aspirations. Similarly, Malodia et al.
(2022) showed that MM can create emotional associations between consumers and brands
through humor, relatability, and cultural relevance. Rathi and Jain (2024) provide further
support for the direct relationship between MM activities and CPI. Their study
demonstrated that MM can directly influence CPI, in addition to its indirect influence
through ENG.  This finding suggests that consumers may develop CPIs from the
persuasive, informative, entertaining, and relatable characteristics of meme -based brand
content, even without relying entirely on active engagement behaviors . Accordingly, the
present study proposes that MM activities can directly influence consumers’ CPIs within
the Saudi social media context. Therefore, the following hypothesis is proposed:
H4: Meme marketing activities have a significant positive effect on consumer purchase
intention.
H1 is grounded in UGT and supported empirically by Malodia et al. (2022) and Rathi and
Jain (2024). H2 is supported by Boyer and Hult (2006) and Toor et al. (2017). H3 reflects
the partial mediation pathway identified by Rathi and Jain (2024).

3 Methodology
3.1 Research Design and Context
     A quantitative, cross -sectional survey design was employed, consistent with the
positivist, deductive orientation of replication research (Saunders et al., 2019). The study
targeted active Saudi social media users aged 18 –40 who were familiar with mem es and
meme-based marketing.

3.2  Sampling and Data Collection
This study employed a non-probability, convenience sampling technique to recruit active
Saudi social media users aged 18–40 years who were familiar with memes and meme-based
marketing. Although the non-probability sampling method has limitations in terms of the
generalizability of the findings, it facilitates access to relevant respondents and allows for
a deeper understanding of the research subjects and their perceptions. The survey was
distributed through Instagram, X, and Snapchat. A total of 514 questi onnaires were
distributed; after screening for eligibility, 284 fully completed responses were retained. This
sample is adequate for PLS-SEM in a three-construct model (Hair et al., 2019).
3.3 Measurement Instrument
The instrument replicates Rathi and Jain’s (2024) validated 22-item questionnaire on a five-
point Likert scale. MM activities (second -order construct) were measured by 15 items
across five dimensions adapted from Bilgin (2018) and Sharma et al. (2021). ENG  was
measured by 4 items adapted from Yadav and Rahman (2018). CPI was measured by 3
items adapted from Toor et al. (2017). The instrument was translated into Arabic through
forward translation, back -translation, and expert panel review to ensure cultural
equivalence.
3.4 Data Analysis
Data were analyzed using the PLS-SEM statistical technique in SmartPLS 4.1.1.7 (Hair et
al., 2022). The PLS -SEM analysis constituted a two -stage analysis: measurement model
assessment and structural model assessment. Since data were collected from a single
source, common-method bias was assessed using the full collinearity VIF method (Kock,
2015). The measurement model assessment included reliability and validity of items and
constructs (Henseler et al., 2015). The structural model tested path coefficients. Mediation
was examined via bootstrapping with 5,000 resamples at a 95% confidence interval (Zhao
et al., 2010).

     4.4 Structural Model Assessment

This study set out to examine whether the MM–ENG–CPI model is replicable in the
Saudi market. All three hypotheses were supported, broadly replicating Rathi and Jain
(2024).
The findings support H1, indicating that MM positively influences ENG. This result
is consistent with Rathi and Jain (2024), who reported a similar positive relationship in

interactive, culturally relevant, and shareable meme content can encourage ENG (Lee et
al., 2019; Malodia et al., 2022; Vasile et al., 2021). The finding therefore reinforces the

The positive relationship between ENG and CPI is consistent with previous research
showing that engagement with social media content can strengthen consumers’
purchase-related responses (Boyer & Hult, 2006; Toor et al., 2017). The finding also

contextual or platform-related variation between the Saudi and Indian samples. Saudi
consumers’ engagement with highly visual and socially oriented platforms such as
Snapchat and Instagram may not translate into CPI in the same way as engagement in
other platform environments. Methodological differences, sample characteristics, and
changing patterns of social media consumption may also contribute to differences in
effect magnitude. However, these explanations were not directly tested in the present
study and should therefore be considered possible interpretations rather than established
causes. Future comparative research could explicitly examine cultural context, platform
type, and consumer characteristics as moderators of the engagement–purchase intention
relationship.
The findings support H3, demonstrating that ENG mediates the relationship between
MM and CPI. This result is consistent with Rathi and Jain (2024) and supports the
broader view that engagement can serve as an important mechanism through which
social media marketing influences consumer responses. The significant direct and
indirect relationships further suggest that MM influences CPI both directly and through
ENG, indicating a complementary mediation pattern..
The findings also demonstrate that MM has a significant positive direct influence on
CPI. This finding is consistent with Rathi and Jain (2024), who also reported a significant
direct relationship between MM and CPI among Indian social media users. The finding
further aligns with previous research suggesting that memes can influence consumers’
purchase-related attitudes and intentions by providing relevant information, creating
emotional associations, and making brands more relatable and memorable (Lee et al.,
2019; Vasile et al., 2021). In the Saudi context, this suggests that MM can influence CPI
beyond its role in generating ENG, indicating that well-designed memes may directly
shape consumers’ perceptions and consideration of branded products or services.
     5.1 Theoretical Implications
This study extends the cross-cultural applicability of the MM model to the Arab Gulf
region, addressing an explicitly identified gap (Alsaadi et al., 2024; Rathi & Jain, 2024). It
extends UGT beyond traditional and Western media settings to meme-based marketing
communication in an Arab context, and contributes to SCT by providing evidence that
observational learning and peer modeling operate in the Saudi social media environment.

     5.2 Practical Implications
     The findings of this study provide several practical implications for brand
managers, digital marketers, marketing agencies, and other practitioners operating in the
Saudi market. First, meme marketing demonstrated a large effect on consumer
engagement, indicating substantial practical relevance for generating engagement. Brands
can therefore consider meme-based content as part of their social media marketing
strategies, particularly through entertainment, interaction, trendiness, informativeness,
and e-WOM. Second, e-WOM demonstrated the highest outer loading among the meme
marketing dimensions (λ = 0.886), indicating that shareability is particularly prominent
within the overall meme marketing construct in this sample. Marketers may therefore
prioritize content that encourages consumers to share and recommend brand-related
memes. However, this finding reflects the relative importance of e-WOM as an indicator
of meme marketing rather than direct evidence that sharing behavior independently
increases purchase intention. Third, meme marketing showed a moderate effect on
consumer purchase intention, while consumer engagement demonstrated a smaller but
meaningful effect on purchase intention. These results indicate that the relationships are
not only statistically significant but also substantively relevant for marketing decision-
making. The significant direct effect of meme marketing on purchase intention, together
with the significant indirect effect through consumer engagement, suggests that
practitioners may adopt a two-pronged strategy: creating engaging meme content while
also incorporating useful and persuasive product or service information. Finally, the large
effect of meme marketing on engagement and moderate effect on purchase intention
support its potential strategic relevance within the social media marketing mix in the
Saudi context. However, these findings concern purchase intention rather than actual
purchasing behavior. Therefore, the results should not be interpreted as evidence that
meme marketing will produce a corresponding increase in actual sales or purchases.
Instead, they indicate that meme marketing has practical potential for strengthening
consumer engagement and purchase intentions. Future research using behavioral
measures such as click-through rates, conversion rates, or actual purchase data could
provide stronger evidence of its commercial impact.
5.3 Limitations and Future Research Directions
This study has several limitations that provide directions for future research. First, the
use of non-probability convenience sampling facilitated access to relevant Saudi social
media users and enabled a deeper understanding of their perceptions; however, it may
introduce selection bias and limit the generalizability of the findings. This limitation is
particularly relevant given the predominance of female and younger respondents in the
sample. Future studies should therefore prioritize probability-based sampling and larger,
more demographically diverse samples to improve representativeness and external
validity. Second, the cross-sectional design captures respondents’ perceptions at a single
point in time and therefore does not establish temporal or causal relationships among
meme marketing, ENG, and CPI. Longitudinal research should consequently be
prioritized to examine how these relationships develop over time and provide stronger

evidence regarding temporal effects. Third, the study measures consumer purchase
intention through self-reported responses rather than actual purchasing behavior. Future
research should therefore incorporate objective behavioural indicators, such as actual
purchase data, click-through rates, engagement metrics, or conversion rates, to determine
whether meme marketing translates into measurable consumer behaviour. Finally, the
study focuses exclusively on Saudi social media users, which limits the extent to which
the findings can be extended to other cultural and market contexts. Cross-GCC
comparative studies, particularly involving the UAE, Kuwait, and Qatar, could therefore
examine whether the relationships identified in this study remain consistent across
different national and cultural settings. Future research may also extend the model by
incorporating additional constructs, such as brand trust, brand equity, and brand loyalty,
to provide a broader understanding of the mechanisms through which meme marketing
influences consumer responses.
6. Conclusion
This study examined the impact of meme marketing on CPI, mediated by consumer
engagement, among 284 Saudi social media users. PLS-SEM confirmed that meme
marketing significantly influenced consumer engagement (β = 0.692) and purchase
intention (β = 0.465 direct; β = 0.258 indirect), with partial mediation confirmed. These
findings replicate Rathi and Jain (2024) in a Gulf Arab context, extending UGT and SCT
to Arab digital consumer behavior and providing practical evidence for Saudi-based
marketers. Limitations include convenience sampling, a predominantly female sample,
and a cross-sectional design. Future research should employ probability sampling,
longitudinal designs, objective behavioral measures, and cross-GCC comparative studies.
