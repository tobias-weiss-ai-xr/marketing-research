ABSTRACT
The rapid growth of social media has transformed modern marketing strategies, yet the
mechanisms through which social media marketing activities influence consumer behavior
toward sustainable consumption remain underexplored. This study investigates how so cial
media marketing activities affect consumers' purchase intention toward circular economy
products, with consumer attitude functioning as a mediating variable. Based on theory of
planned behavior, social media marketing activities are conceptualized as a higher -order
construct comprising five dimensions: entertainment, interaction, trendiness, customization,
and word-of-mouth. A quantitative research design was employed, with data collected from
Vietnamese consumers. A two -stage approach was adopted to a nalyze the higher -order
construct, ensuring robust and accurate measurement of its multidimensional nature. Structural
equation modeling (SEM) was applied to examine hypothesized relationships. The findings
demonstrate that social media marketing activitie s significantly and positively influence
consumer attitude toward circular economy products, which in turn drives purchase intention.
Consumer attitude is confirmed as a significant mediator in this relationship. These findings
contribute to sustainable ma rketing literature and provide actionable insights for businesses
and policymakers promoting circular economy consumption through social media platforms
in emerging markets.
Keywords: Circular economy products, purchase intention, social media marketing activities,
sustainable consumption, higher order construct.
1. INTRODUCTION
Social media platforms have changed the way consumers find, compare, and buy
products. Appel et al. [1] argue that social media is no longer just an additional marketing tool
but has become a key force that shapes how consumers make decisions. Today, brands spend
significant resources on social media marketing activities (SMMAs) to increase awareness,
change consumer attitudes, and encourage purchase behavior [2]. At the same time, growing
environmental concerns have made the circular economy an increasingl y important concept.
The circular economy focuses on reducing waste, extending the life of products, and
encouraging sustainable consumption behaviors [3], [4].
The relationship between social media marketing and circular economy consumption is
a research area that has not been fully explored, especially in emerging economies like
Vietnam. Vietnam is a suitable context for this study because the country has seen rapid growth
in social media use, with over 72.70 million active users as of January 2024, representing

Social media marketing activities include a wide range of brand -led actions on social

The theory of planned behavior [11] is a well -established framework that explains how
attitudes lead to behavioral intentions. Many studies on sustainable consumption have
confirmed that attitude is one of the strongest predictors of purchase intention [12 ], [13].
Despite this, very few studies have examined the full SMMA –attitude–purchase intention
relationship in a developing country context. Arora et al. [14] confirmed that attitude plays a
central mediating role between social media use and sustainable purchase intentions but did
not treat SMMA as a higher -order construct (HOC), which allows for a more accurate and
complete measurement of the concept.
Based on these gaps, the main research question of this study is: How do social media
marketing activities affect consumers' purchase intention toward circular economy products,
with consumer attitude as a mediating variable, within the framework of the theory of planned
behavior?
To answer this question, this study treats SMMA as a higher-order construct (HOC), with
the five sub -dimensions identified by Kim and Ko [8] serving as lower -order components
(LOCs). An embedded two -stage approach within PLS -SEM is used to test the propose d
relationships.

2. LITERATURE REVIEW
2.1. Theory of planned behavior and circular economy products
This study uses the Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) as its main theoretical framework.
The TPB was developed by Ajzen [11], [15] and is one of the most used theories for predicting
human behavioral intentions. According to the TPB, a person's intention to perform a behavior
is shaped by three main factors: their attitude toward the behavior, the subjective norms they
feel from people around them, and their perceived behavioral control , that is, how easy or
difficult they think it is to perform the behavior. Among these three factors, attitude has been
identified as one of the strongest predictors of behavioral intention [12], including in
sustainable and environmental consumption [16].
The circular economy is a new way of thinking about production and consumption that
moves away from the traditional linear model of "take -make-dispose." Instead of throwing
products away at the end of their life, the circular economy focuses on keeping pro ducts and
materials in use for as long as possible through strategies such as reuse, repair,
remanufacturing, and recycling [3]. Shevchenko et al. [4] argue that making the circular
economy work in practice requires the active participation of consumers at  every stage of a
product's life, from buying and using it to disposing of it responsibly. Their product -centric

Many studies have already applied the TPB to investigate consumer purchase intention
toward circular economy products, and the results consistently support the TPB.
Kongarchapatara and Hanpanit [17] used the TB to study Thai consumers' purchase intention
toward circular economy products with 559 respondents. Their results showed that attitude
toward the behavior had the strongest influence on purchase intention, compared to subjective
norms and perceived behavioral control, which also showed positive but smaller effects. In a
similar study, Pisitsankkhakarn and Vassanadumrongdee [18] used the TPB to examine
purchase intention toward remanufactured automotive products in Thailand. They found that
attitude and subjective norms were the two key factors that significantly and positively
influenced purchase intention, and that product quality and price factors helped to strengthen
consumer attitude toward these products. At a larger scale, Hanliang Fu et al. [19] conducted
a meta-analytic study combining 32 empirical studies with a total of 14,032 respondents. Their
findings confirmed that within the TPB framework, consumer attitude plays an important
mediating role in connecting environmental concern, social value, and product knowledge to
consumers' willingness to pay for circular products. Together, these studies show that TPB is
a strong and widely supported framework for studying circular economy purchasing behavior,
and that attitude is the most consistent and powerful predictor in this context.
In the specific context of circular economy products, attitude refers to how positively or
negatively a consumer evaluates the idea of buying products that are designed to be reused,
repaired, remanufactured, or recycled to reduce waste. Vidal -Ayuso et al.  [3] confirm that
attitude is the most impactful variable affecting purchase intention in the circular economy
context. They also note that positive attitudes not only directly increase purchase intention but
also help reduce the negative effect of price s ensitivity on buying decisions, which further
encourages circular purchasing. This conclusion is broadly consistent with the meta -analytic
evidence of Armitage and Conner [12], who reviewed 185 independent studies across many
types of behavior and found th at, within the TPB model, subjective norm is generally the
weakest predictor of intention, implying a comparatively stronger and more consistent role for
attitude and perceived behavioral control. Additionally, Zaremohzzabieh et al. [16] conducted
a meta-analytic path analysis and found that consumer attitude has a strong and consistent
positive effect on green purchase intention across many different product types and cultural
contexts, further confirming that attitude is the key psychological driver of su stainable
purchasing behavior.
Based on the theoretical arguments and empirical evidence presented above, this study
proposes the following hypothesis:
H2: Consumer attitude positively influences purchase intention toward circular economy
products.
2.2. Social media marketing activities
Social media marketing activities (SMMAs) refer to a set of brand actions carried out on
social media platforms to positively influence how consumers think and behave [20]. Kim and
Ko [8] identified five key dimensions of perceived SMMA: entertainment, int eraction,
trendiness, customization, and word-of-mouth. This five-dimension structure has been widely
used and tested across many different contexts. For example, Chen and Lin [9] showed that
SMMAs influence how consumers identify with a brand community and how much value they
perceive, which in turn shapes their satisfaction and behavioral intentions. More recently,
Yang et al. [21] confirmed that this five-dimension structure is valid and useful across different

countries and product types, showing that trendiness, interaction, and word -of-mouth were

In this study, SMMA is conceptualized as a higher-order construct (HOC), meaning that
the five dimensions above are treated as lower -order components (LOCs) that together make
up one single, broader concept of SMMA. This HOC approach is supported by the
methodology proposed by Hair et al. [22] and the two-stage embedded approach of Ringle et
al. [23], and it provides a more accurate and complete picture of how SMMA as a whole affects
consumer outcomes. Treating SMMA as an HOC also reduces model complexity while
capturing all the important aspects of how brands communicate through social media.
SMMA and consumer attitude
A growing body of research shows that SMMA has a significant positive influence on
consumer attitude, including toward green and sustainable products. Nekmahmud et al. [7]
used an extended TPB model with 785 respondents and found that social media marketin g
has a strong and significant positive association with consumer attitude toward green
products, which in turn leads to green purchase intentions. Their study showed that social
media marketing works partly by increasing consumers' green product knowledge , which
then strengthens their attitude. Similarly, Sun and Wang [24] found that social media
marketing positively shapes consumers' attitudes and subjective norms toward green
products in China, and that attitude, in turn, significantly and positively pre dicts purchase
intention. Zafar et al. [25] confirmed the relationship by showing that social media use builds
sustainable purchasing attitudes in consumers, and that this effect is strengthened when
consumers trust the information they see on social media . Arora et al. [14] provided
empirical evidence that social media is a key antecedent of consumer attitude toward
sustainable products, confirming that social media shapes how favorably consumers evaluate
the idea of buying sustainable or green products.
These findings are especially relevant to the context of circular economy products.
Although direct empirical evidence specifically linking SMMA to circular economy product
attitudes is still limited [3], the theoretical logic is clear: just as social medi a builds positive
attitudes toward green products in general, it can also shape consumers' evaluations of circular
economy products by providing entertaining, informative, and persuasive content through its
five dimensions.
Based on evidence, this study proposes:
H1: Social media marketing activities (SMMA) positively influence consumer attitude
toward circular economy products.
Attitude as a mediator between SMMA and purchase intention
Beyond the direct effect of SMMA on attitude, a key question in this study is whether
attitude mediates the relationship between SMMA and purchase intention. This mediation
logic is grounded in the TPB [11], which places attitude as the key psychological mechanism
that links external stimuli to behavioral intention. Chen and Lin [9] showed clearly that SMMA
does not directly affect consumer behavioral outcomes such as satisfaction; instead, its effects
work through intermediate psychological variables like perceived value and social
identification. This indirect pathway strongly supports the idea that attitude mediates the
SMMA–purchase intention relationship.
Empirical support for this mediation is also provided by Arora et al. [14], who confirmed
that attitude plays a central mediating role between social media exposure and sustainable
purchase intentions. Jamil et al. [10] provided further evidence that SMMA dimensions
positively influence consumer intentions through attitudinal and value-based pathways, rather
than through a direct route. Nekmahmud et al. [7] showed through PLS -SEM that the path

3. METHODOLOGY
Research design and data collection
This study uses a quantitative research design, which is a common approach in social
science and marketing research [26], [27]. Data were collected through an online survey
distributed on Vietnamese social media platforms and through university networks ov er four
weeks from December of 2025. Respondents were screened to confirm that they were regular
social media users and had at least a basic understanding of circular economy or sustainable
products. After removing incomplete responses, 450 valid responses  were retained. This
sample size meets the minimum requirement of 10 observations per parameter for PLS -SEM
[22]. The sample was 54.2% female and 45.8% male, with 72.3% aged between 18 and 35.
Over 61% held at least a university degree. Monthly income was distributed as follows: 28.4%
earned below 10 million VND, 55.6% between 10 and 50 million VND, and 16.0% above 50
million VND.
SMMA was measured using 11 items across five lower -order constructs (LOCs)
entertainment, interaction, trendiness, customization, and word-of-mouth, which adapted from
Kim and Ko [8]. Consumer attitude (3 items) and purchase intention (3 items) were both
adapted from Chen & Lin [9]. All items used a five-point Likert scale (1 = Strongly disagree;
5 = Strongly agree). Full measurement items are listed in Appendix 1.
Data analysis followed the embedded two -stage approach for higher -order construct
(HOC) modeling [2 2], [2 3]. The measurement assessment and structural assessment were
analysed by using PLS-SEM in SmartPLS 4.

5. DISCUSSION
5.1. H1: SMMA positively influences consumer attitude
The support for H1 extends prior research on SMMA's role in attitude formation [9], [8]
into the circular economy domain. The five dimensions of SMMA, entertainment, interaction,
trendiness, customization, and word -of-mouth, collectively shape how consumers evaluate
circular economy products. Entertaining and trendy content positions these products as
desirable, interactive features deepen consumer engagement, personalized messaging
increases relevance, and word-of-mouth creates social proof. Together, these dimensions build
a favorable attitude that consumers would not easily develop through traditional marketing
channels alone.
This finding is consistent with Nekmahmud et al. [7], who demonstrated that social media
marketing shapes green purchase attitudes within an extended TPB framework, and with Arora
et al. [14], who confirmed that social media usage is a key antecedent of sustainable purchase
attitudes. In the Vietnamese context, where social media penetration is high and environmental
awareness is still growing [5], SMMA represents a particularly effective vehicle for attitude
change toward circular economy products.

5.2. H2: Consumer attitude positively influences purchase intention
The support for H2 is consistent with the core logic of the theory of planned behavior
[11], which positions attitude as the primary driver of behavioral intention. In the circular

sustainable consumption research.
Importantly, this finding also highlights the attitude -behavior gap as a key challenge.
Vidal-Ayuso et al. [3] noted that despite generally positive consumer attitudes toward circular
economy practices, actual engagement often remains low. The significant attitude-to-intention
path in this study suggests that building genuinely favorable attitudes, rather than just
superficial awareness, is a critical first step in bridging this gap.
5.3. H3: Consumer attitude mediates the SMMA–purchase intention relationship
The mediation analysis revealed complementary partial mediation: consumer attitude
significantly mediates the SMMA and purchase intention relationship, while SMMA also
retains a smaller but significant direct effect on purchase intention. This result is theoretically
important and aligns with Zhao et al.'s [36] taxonomy of mediation types.
The primary route is attitudinal: SMMA first changes how consumers evaluate circular
economy products, and this changed attitude then drives purchase intention. This is
consistent with Chen and Lin [9], who found that SMMA does not directly affect behavior al
outcomes but works through intermediate psychological mechanisms. In the circular
economy context, this confirms that SMMA must do more than inform consumers, it must
shift their evaluative stance.
The secondary direct route suggests that some dimensions of SMMA, particularly word-
of-mouth and entertainment, can also motivate purchasing more immediately through social
activation and emotional resonance, without necessarily requiring deep attitudinal change.
This two-route structure means that circular economy marketing strategies can operate at both
levels: long-term attitude building through educational and narrative content, and shorter-term
direct behavioral activation through peer recommendations and engaging calls to action.
5.4. Theoretical and managerial implications
Theoretically, this study contributes to the literature in three ways. First, it confirms and
extends the TPB -based SMMA –attitude–intention chain to the circular economy product
domain in an emerging market, where such evidence was previously limited [3]. Second, it
demonstrates the value of conceptualizing SMMA as a higher -order construct (HOC), which
provides a more complete and parsimonious representation of how its five dimensions operate
together as a unified marketing force [22], [23]. Third, the iden tification of complementary
partial mediation enriches the understanding of SMMA's behavioral mechanisms, showing
that both attitudinal and direct activation pathways are at work.
From a managerial perspective, the findings offer clear guidance for brands in Vietnam's
circular economy space. Since attitude is both the main outcome of SMMA and the primary
driver of purchase intention, marketing strategies should prioritize attitude -building content
such as educational storytelling, emotional narratives, and sustainability -focused brand
positioning rather than purely promotional or transactional messaging. This is consistent with
the recommendation by Dwivedi et al. [2] that firms use social media as a relationship-building
instrument. Word-of-mouth and entertainment dimensions should receive particular emphasis,
as these create social proof and positive emotional associations that are most effective in
building favorable attitudes.
For policymakers in Vietnam, the evidence that SMMA can shift consumer attitudes
toward circular economy products provides a rationale for public -private partnerships.

6. CONCLUSION
This study investigated the role of social media marketing activities in shaping
Vietnamese consumers' purchase intention toward circular economy products, with consumer
attitude as a mediating variable. Using PLS-SEM with an embedded two-stage HOC approach,
all three hypotheses were supported. SMMA positively influences consumer attitude (H1),
consumer attitude positively influences purchase intention (H2), and consumer attitude
partially mediates the SMMA –purchase intention relationship as complementary p artial
mediation (H3). The model explained approximately 49.0% of the variance in consumer
attitude and 49.6% of the variance in purchase intention.
These findings contribute to the sustainable marketing literature by confirming the TPB-
based SMMA –attitude–intention mechanism in a circular economy product context in
Vietnam, and by demonstrating that the HOC approach to SMMA measurement is both
empirically valid and theoretically appropriate. The complementary mediation finding adds
knowledge by showing that SMMA influences purchase intention through two distinct
pathways, primarily through attitude, and secondarily through direct behavioral activation.
This study has several limitations. The cross-sectional design limits causal inference. The
sample was predominantly young, educated, and urban, which may restrict generalizability to
other demographic groups. The model also focused on attitude as the sole  mediator; future
research could incorporate other TPB components (subjective norms, perceived behavioral
control) or additional variables such as environmental concern, perceived value, and green
trust. Replication across other Southeast Asian markets wou ld also strengthen the
generalizability of the findings.
Despite these limitations, this study provides timely and practically relevant evidence
that social media marketing is a powerful tool for promoting circular economy consumption
in emerging markets. As sustainability imperatives and digital marketing capabilities continue
to converge, understanding how SMMA builds the psychological foundations of circular
economy behavior will remain an important area of research.
