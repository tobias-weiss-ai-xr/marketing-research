The rapid development of artificial intelligence (AI) is increasingly shaping the
information consumers receive and their decision-making on social commerce platforms.
This study compares the effects of AI-curated electronic word-of-mouth (e-WOM), in
which consumer-generated information is selected and personalized by algorithms, and
organic e-WOM, which is voluntarily shared based on consumer experiences, on purchase
decisions among Generation Z TikTok Shop users in Surabaya. It also examines customer
trust as a mediating variable, using the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) as the
theoretical foundation. A quantitative survey of 229 respondents was analyzed using PLS-
SEM with SmartPLS 4. The results show that AI-curated e-WOM significantly affects
customer trust (β = 0.539; p < 0.001) and purchase decisions (β = 0.368; p < 0.001),
whereas organic e-WOM significantly affects purchase decisions (β = 0.142; p = 0.009)
but not customer trust (β = 0.148; p = 0.060). Customer trust significantly influences
purchase decisions (β = 0.462; p < 0.001) and mediates the relationship between AI-
curated e-WOM and purchase decisions (β = 0.249; p < 0.001), but not between organic
e-WOM and purchase decisions (β = 0.069; p = 0.061). These findings indicate that AI-
curated e-WOM builds customer trust while influencing purchases, whereas organic e-
WOM directly influences purchase decisions without significantly strengthening trust.
The study contributes to digital marketing literature by demonstrating distinct
mechanisms through which algorithmically curated and organic e-WOM shape consumer
behavior in social commerce.

1. INTRODUCTION
1.1. Research Background
Social commerce integrates commercial transactions with social
interaction, user-generated content, and platform-mediated
recommendations. Its design therefore changes not only where
consumers buy but also how they discover, evaluate, and validate
product information [1]  This issue is particularly salient on
TikTok Shop, where product discovery is embedded in short-
form video, live commerce, comments, and algorithmically
personalized feeds.
Electronic word-of-mouth (e-WOM) can reduce information
asymmetry and influence consumers’ purchase decisions by
shaping the perceived usefulness, relevance, and credibility of
product information [3].  Recent evidence also indicates that
information quality, credibility, and quantity remain relevant to

increasing role of platform algorithms in determining which

This study distinguishes the recommender system from its
informational outcome. A recommender system represents the
algorithmic infrastructure that predicts, ranks, and personalizes
content, whereas AI-curated e-WOM refers to consumer-
generated product information that users encounter after being
selected, prioritized, or personalized through such algorithmic
processes. In contrast, organic e-WOM refers to voluntary,
experience-based product information communicated by
consumers without algorithmic personalization as the primary
mechanism through which the information is presented. Although
the visibility of organic e-WOM may still be influenced by
platform algorithms, its defining characteristic in this study lies
in its consumer-originated and non-personalized informational
pathway. This distinction matters because algorithmic relevance
and interpersonal authenticity may build consumer trust through
different mechanisms.
From a technology-acceptance perspective, the Technology
Acceptance Model (TAM) provides an overarching theoretical
foundation for understanding the technological context in which
AI-supported information is delivered and consumed. TAM
proposes that perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use
shape technology acceptance [8] . However, these core TAM
constructs are not operationalized in the present study because the
research does not examine technology acceptance as its empirical
outcome. Instead, TAM provides the theoretical context for
understanding the role of AI-enabled technology, while the
empirical relationships are developed from the literature on e-
WOM, customer trust, and purchase decision.
1.2. Research Gap and Contribution
Prior studies establish that e-WOM, trust, and platform
characteristics affect social-commerce behavior, but three
unresolved gaps remain. Theoretically, few studies model
algorithmic curation and consumer-originated communication as
distinct stimuli operating through the same psychological
mechanism. Empirically, evidence is limited on whether trust
carries the effects of both information routes among Generation
Z users in an emerging-economy city. Practically, platforms and
sellers lack clear evidence on whether personalization or peer-
authored content should be prioritized when designing
trustworthy product discovery.
This study addresses those gaps by Ref. (1) operationally
separating AI-curated from organic e-WOM; (2) testing customer
trust as a mediator of both routes; and (3) examining a bounded
sample of Generation Z TikTok Shop users in Surabaya.
Surabaya provides a relevant urban social-commerce setting,
while the bounded context avoids claiming national
representativeness. The contribution is not a formal test of
whether one coefficient is statistically larger than another; rather,
it identifies distinct direct and indirect patterns that can inform
theory and platform practice

1.4. Literature Review and Hypothesis Development
The study uses the Stimulus–Organism–Response (S-O-R)
framework as its primary explanatory foundation. AI-curated and
organic e-WOM are information stimuli, customer trust
represents the internal organism state, and the purchase decision
is the behavioral response. The Technology Acceptance Model
(TAM) is retained only as a complementary lens for
understanding why algorithmically organized information may be
experienced as useful or easy to process; its core constructs are
neither measured nor tested in the structural model.
1.4.1.  Theoretical Foundation: S-O-R and the
Supporting Role of TAM
The Stimulus–Organism–Response (S-O-R) framework provides
the primary explanatory foundation for this study. Originally
proposed by Ref. [6] the S-O-R framework explains how external
environmental stimuli influence individuals’ internal cognitive or
affective states, which subsequently shape behavioral responses.
In social commerce, information, technological, and social cues
can function as stimuli that influence consumers’ internal
evaluations, trust, and subsequent behavioral responses [1]..In the
present study, AI-curated e-WOM and organic e-WOM are
conceptualized as external information stimuli, customer trust
represents the consumer’s internal evaluative state, and purchase
decision represents the resulting behavioral response.
This theoretical mapping provides the basis for examining the
mediation mechanism proposed in the study. AI-curated e-WOM
may provide information that is selected, prioritized, and
personalized according to users’ characteristics and behavioral
patterns, potentially increasing the perceived relevance and
accessibility of product information. In contrast, organic e-WOM
provides experience-based information and social validation
originating from other consumers. Both forms of e-WOM may
influence purchase decisions directly, while their effects may also
operate indirectly by shaping consumers’ trust in the information,
product, seller, or platform.
The Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) is retained as a
complementary theoretical lens for contextualizing consumers’
interaction with AI-enabled recommendation technology. TAM
proposes that perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use are
fundamental determinants of technology acceptance [8]. In the
present study, TAM is not employed as the empirical explanatory
model because perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use are
not included as research constructs or operationalized in the
measurement instrument. Accordingly, the structural model
estimates no TAM pathways. Instead, TAM provides contextual
support for understanding why consumers may be receptive to
information organized and delivered through algorithmically
supported recommendation systems.
Taken together, S-O-R explains the empirical stimulus–trust–
purchase mechanism tested in this study, whereas TAM provides
complementary theoretical context for consumers’ interaction

1.4.2. AI Curated E-WOM Electronic (Word of Mouth)
AI-curated e-WOM is defined here as consumer-generated
product information that a platform's AI recommender system
selects, ranks, and personalizes for a particular user. The AI does
not author the e-WOM; it controls exposure to existing reviews,
videos, comments, or other consumer content. This operational
boundary distinguishes the construct from an AI-generated
recommendation and from the recommender system itself.
Algorithmic curation can increase perceived relevance by
matching content to behavioral traces, prior interactions, and
inferred preferences. This may reduce search costs and cognitive
overload, but it can also create concerns about opacity,
manipulation, and bias. Research on AI acceptance shows that
trust is central to users' willingness to rely on AI-enabled
technologies, while explainability and transparency help users
evaluate whether reliance is warranted [10].
In human-AI interaction, perceived competence alone is
insufficient: users also evaluate whether outputs are
understandable, controllable, and aligned with their goals [11].
Accordingly, AI-curated e-WOM may encourage purchase when
the selected information appears relevant and credible, and may
build trust when personalization is accompanied by consistent
and transparent platform behavior.
On TikTok Shop, the recommender system can prioritize
product videos, reviews, and comments based on search history
and interaction patterns. When users perceive this curated
information as useful and congruent with their needs, it can
simplify product evaluation and increase confidence in a
purchase. Thus, AI-curated e-WOM is expected to influence both
the behavioral response and the trust mechanism.
1.4.3. Organic E-Wom Electronic (Word of Mouth)
Organic e-WOM refers to voluntary, consumer-authored
communication based on product or service experience rather
than seller-authored or sponsored promotion. It includes reviews,
comments, and user videos perceived as spontaneous and
experience based. Unlike algorithmic curation, its persuasive
value is primarily associated with source credibility,
diagnosticity, and social proof. The term does not imply that
platform distribution is entirely free from algorithms. E-WOM
research shows that information quality, credibility, usefulness,
and the receiver's need for information shape information
adoption and purchase intention [3].
Evidence among Generation Z similarly indicates that
credible and useful social-media e-WOM supports online
purchase intention [5].. Organic e-WOM may therefore reduce
uncertainty by providing concrete accounts of product
performance and use. On TikTok Shop, users encounter
experience-based product videos and comments from other
consumers. Positive and diagnostic content can affect a purchase
directly through social proof; however, anonymous sources,
commercial incentives, information overload, and suspected
manipulation may weaken its capacity to build trust. These
competing mechanisms make empirical testing necessary.
1.4.4. Customer Trust
Customer trust is the consumer's willingness to rely on a seller,
platform, or information source based on perceived competence,
integrity, and benevolence. Trust is especially important in digital
transactions because consumers cannot inspect products or sellers
directly and must tolerate uncertainty about information
accuracy, fulfilment, and data use [13].
In social commerce, trust can be directed toward multiple
objects: the platform, seller, recommender system, or consumer-
generated information. This construct captures customers' overall
confidence that the information and transaction environment is
dependable. AI-curated e-WOM may build that confidence
through consistent relevance, whereas organic e-WOM may build
it through authentic peer experience.
Algorithmic trust should not be treated as automatic
acceptance. Explainability, transparency, feedback, and user
control help consumers calibrate reliance on AI recommendations
[10].  If curation appears intrusive or commercially manipulative,
personalization may reduce rather than increase trust. This
conditional logic is important when interpreting a positive
average effect.
Within the S-O-R framework, trust is the organism state that
translates external information cues into a response. It can reduce
perceived risk and increase confidence that acting on a
recommendation will produce an acceptable outcome.
Accordingly, trust is expected to influence purchase decisions and
may mediate the effects of both e-WOM routes.
The two routes nonetheless differ conceptually. Trust in AI-
curated e-WOM depends on perceived algorithmic competence
and responsible curation, whereas trust in organic e-WOM
depends more heavily on source authenticity and message
credibility. Comparing their indirect effects can reveal whether
the same psychological mechanism operates across both forms of
information exposure.
1.4.5. Purchase Decision
A purchase decision is the consumer's selection of a product and
commitment to buy after evaluating available alternatives and
information [19]. In social commerce, product attributes interact
with information quality, recommender-system cues, social
influence, and trust. Consequently, a purchase decision may
follow a deliberative trust-building process or a more immediate
response to relevant information and social proof. For Generation
Z TikTok Shop users, product discovery, evaluation, and
purchase can occur within the same content stream. Recent
studies report that e-WOM, perceived value, ease of use, and trust
are associated with purchase decisions in TikTok Shop contexts
[15].  These findings support including both information stimuli
and trust, while the present study advances the comparison
between algorithmically curated and organic e-WOM.

2. METHODOLOGY
2.1. Research Design
A cross-sectional quantitative survey examined the relationships
among AI-curated e-WOM, organic e-WOM, customer trust, and
purchase decisions. The design tests associations within a
theoretically specified model; because the data are cross-sectional
and nonexperimental, the study interprets causal language
cautiously.
The model was estimated with partial least squares structural
equation modeling (PLS-SEM) in SmartPLS 4. PLS-SEM was
selected because the study emphasizes prediction, includes latent
reflective constructs, and tests indirect effects [18. We assessed
statistical significance using nonparametric bootstrapping.
2.2.  Population and Sample
This study's population consists of Generation Z consumers who
use TikTok Shop in Surabaya, Indonesia. Generation Z was
chosen because this demographic is one of the most active user
groups in social commerce, characterized by high digital literacy
and frequent exposure to AI-based recommendation systems.
This study uses purposeful sampling techniques. Respondents
are required to meet the following criteria: belong to Generation
Z, have used TikTok Shop, have previously received AI-based
product recommendations or product-related information on
TikTok Shop, and have purchased or are considering buying
products through TikTok Shop. We collected the data through a
self-administered online questionnaire distributed via Google
Forms for approximately one month (May 2026). We obtained
229 valid responses for the final analysis after excluding
incomplete questionnaires. In terms of gender, the sample
includes men and women aged 17 to 27 years.
The sample size is considered adequate for PLS-SEM
analysis. According to Ref. [18], PLS-SEM is suitable for studies
with relatively small to medium sample sizes and complex
research models. In addition, the study meets the rule 10 times,
which recommends that the minimum sample size should be at
least ten times the number of the largest structural pathways
directed to any endogenous construction. Since the maximum
number of arrows pointing to a single construct in the proposed
model is three, the recommended minimum sample size is 30
respondents. Therefore, the final sample of 229 respondents
significantly exceeded the recommended minimum and provided
sufficient statistical strength for hypothesis testing.
2.3. Instrument Measurement and Development
All constructs were measured using five-point Likert-type scales
ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). The
instrument was adapted from established e-WOM, algorithmic
trust, online trust, and consumer-decision literature and
contextualized for TikTok Shop. Each reflective construct
comprised five indicators, as evidenced by the reported
measurement model.

4. DISCUSSION
The findings reveal two distinct information-processing routes in
AI-enabled social commerce. AI-curated e-WOM affects
purchase decisions both directly and indirectly through trust,
whereas organic e-WOM affects purchase decisions directly but
not through trust. This pattern refines research that treats e-WOM
as a single undifferentiated cue and is consistent with the S-O-R
proposition that different stimuli can activate different organism
states before producing a response.

5.1 AI-Curated e-WOM Builds Customer Trust

AI-curated e-WOM positively affects customer trust (β =
0.539, p < 0.001). A plausible mechanism is informational fit:
ranking based on prior behavior can surface reviews and product
content that users perceive as timely and personally relevant. This
reduces search effort and helps users organize an otherwise
excessive information environment. The finding is consistent
with research showing that trust is central to AI acceptance and
that users evaluate algorithmic competence when deciding
whether to rely on an AI-enabled system [11].
The result should not be interpreted as unconditional
endorsement of opaque personalization. Explainable-AI research
suggests that transparency, comprehensibility, and opportunities
to recognize bias help users calibrate trust [10].  Thus, relevance
may explain the positive average relationship, while transparency
and user control likely serve as boundary conditions for
sustainable trust. The study extends the S-O-R account by
identifying algorithmically organized consumer information as a
distinct technological-information stimulus.

5.2 Organic e-WOM Doesn't Always Build Customer Trust

Findings H4 showed that organic e-WOM had no statistically
significant effect on Customer Trust (β = 0.148; t = 1.879; p =
0.060). These results show that, in the context of TikTok Shop,
consumer-generated reviews alone are not enough to build
customer trust. One possible explanation is information overload
among Generation Z consumers. TikTok Shop users are

rapidly evolving nature of AI-based social commerce, where
personalised recommendations are increasingly competing with
traditional peer-generated reviews. Therefore, while organic e-
WOM continues to provide valuable social proof, its ability to
directly reinforce customer trust appears to be declining in an
environment where algorithmic recommendation systems
dominate consumer information exposure.
From a theoretical perspective, these findings challenge the
traditional assumption that all forms of electronic word-of-mouth
communication affect consumer trust similarly. Instead, the study
shows that the mechanisms that generate and deliver information
play an important role in shaping trust. In AI-based social
commerce, trust is influenced not only by interpersonal
communication but also by consumer trust in intelligent
recommendation systems that are able to filter and personalize
information according to individual needs.

5.3 Customer Trust as the Strongest Predictor of Purchase
Decision

Findings H5 showed that Customer Trust had a positive and
significant effect on Purchase Decisions (β = 0.462; t = 8.720; p
< 0.001), suggesting that trust was the strongest determinant of
purchasing behavior among TikTok Shop Generation Z users.
These findings suggest that while consumers are exposed to a
variety of digital information sources, including AI-curated
recommendations and organic consumer reviews, purchasing
decisions ultimately depend on whether they perceive the
information, vendors, and platforms as trustworthy. In a digital
environment characterized by uncertainty and limited
opportunities for direct product evaluation, trust serves as a
psychological mechanism that reduces perceived risk and
increases consumer confidence in completing online transactions.
These findings are consistent with previous studies that
emphasized the important role of trust in social commerce. Ref.
[13]  argue that trust allows consumers to overcome uncertainty
in digital transactions by increasing trust in both information
sources and online vendors. Similarly, [22] show that customer
trust significantly influences consumers' willingness to engage in
online purchasing behavior in social commerce platforms.
In practical terms, these findings imply that social trading
platforms should prioritize strategies aimed at strengthening
customer trust. Improving recommendation accuracy, improving
algorithm transparency, ensuring information credibility, and
maintaining a secure transaction environment are likely to drive
stronger purchasing behavior among digital consumers.

5.4 Mediation, Theoretical Contribution, and the Unexpected
Pattern

Trust partially mediates the AI-curated e-WOM effect but does
not mediate the organic e-WOM effect. This asymmetry indicates
that algorithmic relevance may first strengthen confidence and
then support purchase, while peer-authored content may trigger a
more immediate response through social proof, informativeness,
affect, or urgency. These alternative mediators were not tested
and should be examined in future research.
Theoretically, the study contributes by differentiating two
information stimuli and locating trust as an organism mechanism
only for the AI-curated route. It does not demonstrate that the AI
coefficient is statistically larger than the organic coefficient
because no formal difference test was conducted. TAM remains
a supporting explanation for receptivity to the recommender
interface, whereas S-O-R provides the tested causal ordering.
This tighter boundary resolves the mismatch between the
previous theoretical claims and the measured constructs.
5. CONCLUSIONS, IMPLICATIONS, AND
LIMITATIONS
6.1 Conclusion

Among the surveyed Generation Z TikTok Shop users in
Surabaya, AI-curated e-WOM is associated with both trust and
purchase decisions. Organic e-WOM is associated directly with
purchase decisions but not significantly with trust. Customer trust
strongly predicts purchase decisions.
Trust provides complementary partial mediation for AI-
curated e-WOM and no mediation for organic e-WOM. The
evidence therefore supports differentiated rather than uniform
treatment of e-WOM in AI-enabled social commerce. The
conclusions are limited to the sampled context and do not
establish that algorithmic curation is universally superior to
organic consumer communication.

6.2 Theoretical implications

The study extends e-WOM research by separating authorship
from algorithmic exposure, applies S-O-R to a dual-stimulus
model, and shows that trust carries only the AI-curated route. It
also clarifies TAM's limited role: usefulness and ease-of-use
concepts provide background for technology receptivity, but the
model tests no TAM constructs. Future models can explicitly add
perceived usefulness, perceived ease of use, social presence,
information diagnosticity, and source credibility.

6.3 Managerial and Policy Implications

These findings provide several practical implications for social
commerce platforms, online businesses, digital marketers, and
policymakers. For platform providers like TikTok Shop,
improving the quality of AI recommendation systems should be
a strategic priority. Recommendation algorithms must
continuously improve personalization accuracy, information

6.4 Limitations and Future Research

The cross-sectional, self-report, nonprobability design limits
causal inference and generalizability. The single-city, single-
platform setting may not represent other cohorts or markets, and
researchers must assess common-method and nonresponse bias
using the available raw data. The operational distinction between
curated and organic e-WOM should be validated further because
even consumer-authored content can be algorithmically
distributed. Future research should use experiments or
longitudinal designs, probability or stratified sampling, multi-
group analysis, and formal tests of coefficient differences;
distinguish trust targets; and evaluate transparency, privacy
concern, perceived manipulation, social presence, and review
authenticity as moderators or alternative mediators.
