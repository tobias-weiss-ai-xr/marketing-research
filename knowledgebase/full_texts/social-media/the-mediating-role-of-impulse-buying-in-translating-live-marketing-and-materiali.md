Abstract
TikTok Live Shopping has revolutionized social commerce, yet the joint
impact of external marketing and internal values on consumer intentions
remains under-explored. This study examines the roles of social media
marketing (SMM) and materialism in driving purchas e intentions for
TikTok Live Shopping, mediated by impulse buying. Utilizing a
quantitative, cross -sectional design, data were collected via
questionnaires from 143 business students selected through simple
random sampling. Structural Equation Modeling (SE M-PLS) was
employed to test the hypotheses. The structural model reveals that SMM
(β=0.385) and materialism (β=0.356) significantly trigger impulse
buying. Furthermore, impulse buying exerts the strongest direct impact
on purchase intention (β=0.412) and s uccessfully mediates both SMM
(β=0.159) and materialism (β=0.147) pathways. Unlike traditional static
e-commerce studies, this research offers a novelty approach by
integrating the Stimulus -Organism-Response (S -O-R) framework to
demonstrate how real-time, high-FOMO digital marketing interacts with
internal psychological traits to accelerate impulse -driven transactions.
Practically, social commerce managers must transition from passive
broadcasting to interactive, relationship -driven hosting. Theoretically,
the study warns that modern social commerce platforms can exploit
materialistic vulnerabilities, highlighting the urgent need for consumer
digital well-being education.

Keywords: Impulse Buying, Live Shopping TikTok, Materialism,

INTRODUCTION
A preliminary survey involving 20 students from the Faculty of Economics and Business, Bandar
Lampung University, revealed worrying patterns of consumer behavior in TikTok Live Shopping.
Although 90% of respondents report frequent exposure to TikTok Live S hopping and 70% have made a
purchase through the platform, purchasing decisions are largely driven by external stimuli rather than
rational evaluation. Flash promotions (80%) and hosts or influencers (60%) emerged as the main factors

driving purchases. More importantly, 75% of respondents admitted to feeling pressured to make a quick
purchasing decision during a live session, while 55% experienced post -purchase regret. Despite these
negative experiences, 65% still expressed an intention to repurchase via TikTok Live Shopping.
According to Efendİoğlu, (2019), purchase intention represents a consumer's willingness to
purchase a particular product in the future and their plans to do so. Purchase intention can be influenced
by several factors, such as social media marketing, materialism, and impulse buying. Acco rding to Phan
et al. (2024), social media marketing is defined as the effectiveness of providing valuable content,
interacting with customers, and building relationships with them (Awanis et al., 2022). Previous research
consistently shows that social media marketing has a positive effect on impulse buying. Putra et al., (2025)
found that social media marketing significantly increases impulse buying behavior, while Iqbal et al.,
(2024) found that social media marketing activities have a positive effect on electronic impulse buying.
These findings suggest that engaging and interactive social media marketing stimulates spontaneous
buying behavior. However, Singh et al., (2023) noted that although social media advertising significantly
influences impulse buying intentions, the social media community dimension has no significant effect.
These inconsistent findings suggest that the influence of social media -related factors on impulse buying
can vary across contexts.
Previous research generally indicates that social media marketing has a positive effect on
purchase intentions. Dolega et al, (2021) found that social media marketing significantly increases
consumer purchase intentions and brand loyalty in the Indonesian banking industry. Similarly, Alalwan,
(2018) stated that social media marketing activities have a positive effect on consumer purchase intentions
by strengthening customer engagement and brand relationships. However, empirical findings remain
inconsistent. Ngo et al., (2024) found that social media marketing had no direct effect on purchase
intentions. According to Antiniene et al., (2021), materialism is defined as the degree to which consumers
orientate toward possessions as a measure of status or life satisfaction. Previous research consistently
reports that materialism positively influences impulse buying. Sen & Nayak, (2019) found that consumers
with stronger materialistic values exhibit a higher tendency to make impulsive purchases. Similarly,
Dogan, (2025) demonstrated that materialism significantly increases impulse buying in digital shopping
environments. However, previous research has primarily focused on conventional online shopping or
general e-commerce contexts.
Previous research consistently reports that materialism positively influences consumer purchase
intentions. Kim & Oh, (2022) demonstrated that materialism positively influences consumers' purchase
intentions for luxury products by strengthening their desire for status. These findings suggest that
consumers with stronger materialistic values tend to develop purchase intentions  for products that
enhance their self -image (Adityawan & Kusuma, 2018) . However, previous research has largely
examined the relationship between materialism and purchase intentions in luxury products and
conventional online shopping contexts.
According to Anoop & Rahman, (2025), impulse buying is spontaneous purchasing behavior that
occurs without prior planning, usually triggered by emotional impulses and external stimuli. Previous
research consistently shows that impulse buying positively influences purchase intentions. Iqbal et al.,
(2024) found that electronic impulse buying significantly increased purchase intentions in social
commerce, suggesting that impulsive buying behavior serves as an important predictor of consumer
purchase decisions.  However, empirical evidence remains limited regarding whether impulse buying
drives purchase intentions in TikTok Live Shopping, where real-time interactions, live streaming features,
and host involvement may shape consumer purchase decisions differently. Therefore, further research is
needed to examine this relationship in the context of live social commerce.
Although previous research has demonstrated a significant relationship between social media
marketing, materialism, impulse buying, and purchase intention, several methodological limitations
remain. Most previous studies have examined these relationships separately, focusing on the direct effects
of social media marketing or materialism on consumer behavior, particularly in the context of
conventional e-commerce, luxury products, or social commerce in general. Furthermore, research that
simultaneously integrates external marketing (social media marketing) and internal psychological factors
(materialism) into a single structural model while examining the mediating role of impulse buying is
limited. Previous research has overlooked the unique characteristics o f TikTok Live Shopping, where
real-time interactions, live broadcasts, flash promotions, and host involvement can alter consumer
decision-making processes. Therefore, this study addresses this methodological gap by using an

integrated mediation model to examine the direct and indirect effects of social media marketing and
materialism on purchase intention through impulse buying in the context of TikTok Live Shopping.
The Stimulus–Organism–Response theory, originally proposed by (Mehrabian & Russell, 1974),
explains that an individual's behavioral responses are influenced by external environmental stimuli
through internal cognitive and emotional processes (Ming et al., 2021) . Stimulus (S) refers to external
environmental cues that trigger a consumer's psychological reaction, Organism (O) represents the
consumer’s internal cognitive and affective state, and Response (R) refers to the resulting behavioral
intention or action (Soemiyati, 1986). The Stimulus–Organism–Response theory has been widely adopted
in consumer behavior and social commerce research to explain how marketing stimuli shape consumer
emotions and subsequently influence purchasing behavior. In the context of this research, soci al media
marketing serves as an external stimulus through engaging content, interactive communication, and
promotional activities, while materialism reflects consumers' internal value orientations that increase
susceptibility to marketing stimuli  (Guo et al., 2023) . These factors stimulate impulsive buying as an
organism (emotional and psychological response), which then leads to purchase intention as a behavioral
response. Therefore, the Stimulus –Organism–Response theory provides an appropriate theoretical
foundation to explain the mechanisms by which marketing stimuli and consumers' psychological
characteristics influence purchase intentions in TikTok live shopping (Li et al., 2022).
The urgency of this research stems from the rapid growth of TikTok Live Shopping as a live
social commerce platform that has fundamentally changed consumer purchasing behavior. Initial findings
indicate that although college students are highly exposed to TikTok Live Shopping and consistently
express strong purchase intentions, many purchase decisions are driven by external stimuli such as flash
promotions and event hosts rather than rational evaluation, resulting in impulse purchases and post -
purchase regr et (Guo et al., 2023) . This phenomenon raises concerns about the quality of consumer
decision-making, particularly among younger consumers who are increasingly influenced by interactive
digital marketing (Istijanto & Handoko, 2023). Given the inconsistent findings of previous research and
the limited empirical (Guo et al., 2023)  evidence examining the impact of social media marketing,
materialism, and impulse buying in the unique context of TikTok Live Shopping (Dogan, 2024), this
research is crucial for providing an understanding of consumer behavior in live social commerce. This
dynamic demonstrates that live social commerce operates on psychological mechanisms distinct from
traditional e-commerce, where constant real-time interaction continuously lowers the cognitive threshold
required for impulse buying (Singh et al., 2022).
This study contributes to the digital marketing literature. It extends previous research by
examining purchase intentions in the emerging context of TikTok Live Shopping, where real -time
interactions, instant promotions, and live host engagement can create a distinct consumer decision-making
environment than conventional e -commerce and social media platforms  (Phan et al., 2024) . This study
develops an integrated framework by combining external marketing stimuli, namely social media
marketing, with internal psychological factors, namely materialism, to explain consumer purchase
intentions. This study also differs from previous re search that examined the direct influence of these
variables. This study explains the underlying mechanisms by which impulse buying mediates the
relationship between social media marketing, materialism, and purchase intentions, thus providing a more
comprehensive understanding of consumer behavior (Li et al., 2022).
This study aims to 1. Empirically test the influence of social media marketing on impulse buying,
2. Empirically test the influence of materialism on impulse buying, 3. Empirically test the influence of
social media marketing on purchase intention, 4. Empirically test the influence of materialism on purchase
intention, 5. Empirically test the influence of impulse buying on purchase intention, 6. Empirically test
the influence of impulse buying in mediating the influence between social media marketing on pur chase
intention, 7. Empirically test the influence of impulse buying in mediating the influence between
materialism on purchase intention.
This research provides both theoretical and practical benefits. Theoretically, this research can
enrich the digital marketing and consumer behavior literature by integrating social media marketing and
materialism into one conceptual framework and by clarif ying the mediating role of impulse buying in
shaping purchase intentions in the context of TikTok Live Shopping. The findings of this study also
provide empirical evidence that expands the current understanding of consumer decision -making in live
social co mmerce, thus serving as a foundation for future research on digital purchasing behavior.
Practically, the results of this study offer valuable insights for marketers, live shopping merchants, content
creators, and e-commerce platforms in designing interactive marketing strategies that encourage purchase

intentions while minimizing excessive impulse buying. The findings of this study can also assist educators
and policymakers in promoting more responsible and rational digital consumption among young

There are seven hypotheses proposed in this study, namely H 1. Social media marketing has a
positive and significant effect on impulse buying, H2. Materialism has a positive and significant effect on
impulse buying, H 3. Social media marketing has a positive and significant effect on purchase intention,
H4. Materialism has a positive and significant effect on purchase intention, H 5. Impulse buying has a
positive and significant effect on purchase intention, H6. Impulse buying mediates the influence between
social media marketing on purchase intention, H 7. Impulse buying mediates the influence between
materialism on purchase intention.  While existing literature has separately examined social media
marketing (SMM) in traditional e -commerce (Dolega et al, 2021) and materialism in luxury retail (Y.
Kim & Oh, 2022), a major empirical gap remains. Specifically, very little research integrates external
social media stimuli with internal psychological constructs (like materialism) into a single structural
model within  the real-time, interactive environment of live social commerce. TikTok Live Shopping
operates on immediate feedback loops, time -constrained discounts, and high peer -influence. Therefore,
the urgency of this research lies in addressing this gap by examinin g how external digital triggers and
internal materialistic values of Gen-Z consumers interact in this highly immersive, high -pressure digital
ecosystem to produce impulsive purchasing decisions.
RESEARCH METHOD
This research uses a quantitative approach which was carried out over a period of 6 months and
the research location was at the Faculty of Economics and Business, Bandar Lampung University.  The
population in this study was all 222 followers of the Faculty of Economics and Business, Bandar Lampung
University, class of 2022. The sample consisted of 143 respondents who participated in TikTok live
shopping, calculated using the Slovin formula wit h a 5% error rate. The sampling technique used was
simple random sampling.
The research procedure began with a literature review to develop a conceptual framework and
formulate research hypotheses. Next, a structured questionnaire was designed by adapting validated
measurement scales from previous studies and reviewed to ensure c ontent validity and clarity. The
questionnaire was then distributed online via Google Forms to respondents who met the predetermined
research criteria. After data collection, returned questionnaires were screened for completeness,
consistency, and feasibil ity for analysis. The collected data were then analyzed using SEM PLS. The
analysis was conducted in two stages: first, evaluation of the measurement model to assess validity and
reliability; second, evaluation of the structural model to examine the proposed hypotheses, direct effects,
indirect effects (mediation), and the predictive ability of the research model.
This study uses a quantitative research approach with a cross-sectional research design. Primary
data were collected from respondents through a structured online questionnaire administered via Google
Forms. The research instrument consisted of 5 dimensions  with 10 statement items for each variable, so
that the total statement items in the questionnaire were 40 statement items with scales that had been
validated in previous studies. Social media marketing was adopted and modified from research by (Godey
et al., 2016) ; (A. J. Kim & Ko, 2012) ; (Seo & Park, 2018) ; (Pasi & Viviani, 2020) . Materialism was
adopted from research by (Richins & Dawson, 1992) , impulsive buying with 10 statement items was
adopted and modified from research by (Verplanken & Herabadi, 2001), and purchase intention with 10
statement items was adopted from research by (Dodds et al., 1991) ; (Pavlou, 2003); (Ajzen, 1991). All
constructs were measured using a five-point Likert scale ranging from 1 meaning strongly disagree to 5
meaning strongly agree. The online survey technique was chosen to facilitate efficient data collection
from respondents who have experience using TikTok Live Shopping. To ensure the internal consistency
and reliability of the measurement scales before proceeding to structural modeling, Cronbach's alpha
values were calculated for each construct. The reliability testing yielded highly satisfactory coefficients:
Social Med ia Marketing (α=0.891), Materialism (α=0.895), Impulse Buying (α=0.902), and Purchase
Intention (α=0.898). All constructs exceeded the widely accepted academic reliability threshold of 0.70,
indicating excellent internal consistency. All constructs were me asured using a five -point Likert scale

Data were analyzed using SEM PLS to examine the influence between social media marketing,
materialism, impulse buying, and purchase intention. SEM PLS was chosen because it is suitable for
simultaneously analyzing complex structural relationships consistin g of several variables and mediation
effects. The analysis was conducted in two stages: first, the measurement model was evaluated by
assessing the reliability of the indicators through outer loading, internal consistency reliability using
Cronbach's alpha and composite reliability (CR), convergent validity using average variance extracted
(AVE). Second, the structural model (inner model) was assessed by examining collinearity (VIF),
coefficient of determination (R²).

Despite its valuable contributions, several limitations of this study must be acknowledged by: 1)
Geographical and Demographic Constraints: The sample was limited to college students from the Faculty
of Economics and Business at Universitas Bandar Lampung,  Indonesia. This constraints the
generalizability of the findings to broader age demographics, different socio -economic backgrounds, or
highly distinct cultural environments  (Sharma et al., 2022 ; Lim & Yoon, 2023 ); 2) Cross -Sectional
Research Design: This study utilized a cross -sectional design, capturing data at a single point in time
(Zaman et al., 2025) . Consequently, it cannot account for long -term behavioral changes or capture how
post-purchase regret eventually impacts future purchase intentions and brand loyalty; 3) Reliance on Self-
Reported Data: The data collection relied entirely on self -reported questionnaires, which may introduce
social desirability bias  (Choudhary et al., 2023; Podsakoff et al., 2024) . Respondents might have been
hesitant to fully acknowledge their actual levels of materialistic values or impulsive buying tendencies.
Based on the limitations identified above, the following recommendations are proposed for future
research. Future researchers should expand the demographic scope by involving diverse age groups,
professions, and geographical regions to enhance the external validity and generalizability of the
structural model, Combining quantitative PLS-SEM analysis with qualitative methods (such as in-depth
interviews or focus group discussions) is highly recommended to explore the deep -seated emotional
states, cognitive dissonance, and post -purchase regret experienced by social commerce shoppers and
Conducting Longitudinal Inv estigations: Future studies should employ longitudinal designs to observe
how consumer interactions and shopping behaviors on live-streaming platforms evolve over time, and to
test the moderating effects of constructs like self-control or digital literacy in reducing compulsive buying
behaviors.
CONCLUSION
The findings indicate that social media marketing and materialism each have a positive and
significant influence on impulse buying and purchase intention. Furthermore, impulse buying has a
positive and significant influence on purchase intention and is the  strongest direct predictor among the
structural relationships examined. The results of this study indicate that both external marketing stimuli
and consumers' internal psychological values play a significant role in shaping purchase decisions in a
live social commerce environment.  Furthermore, this study confirms that impulse buying significantly
mediates the relationship between social media marketing and purchase intention and between
materialism and purchase intention. These findings suggest that consumers' emotional and spontane ous
buying responses serve as important psychological mechanisms through which marketing activities and
materialistic values are transformed into stronger purchase intentions. Overall, this study extends the
application of the Stimulus –Organism–Response theory to the context of TikTok Live Shopping by
demonstrating that real-time interactions, engaging promotional activities, and consumers' materialistic
orientations jointly influence purchase intention through impulse buying. These findings contribute to the
literature on digital consumer behavior and provide practical insights for marketers in designing more
effective and responsible live shopping strategies.
Future research is encouraged to expand this study by examining additional psychological and
contextual variables that may influence consumer behavior in live social commerce, such as trust,
perceived value, fear of missing out (FOMO), hedonic motivation, or streamer credibility. Researchers
could also investigate moderating variables, including gender, age, shopping experience, or digital
literacy, to better understand differences in consumer purchasing behavior across market segments.
Furthermore, future research could use a broader and more diverse sample beyond university students to
increase the generalizability of the findings. Comparative research across different live shopping
platforms, such as Shopee Live, Instagram Live, and YouTube Live, or acros s different countries and
