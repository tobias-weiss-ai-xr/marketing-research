ABSTRAK
This study investigates how social media quality and personal branding influence brand
image and electronic word of mouth (E -WoM) in the surgical clinic context. Grounded  in
the Stimulus –Organism–Response (S –O–R) framework and Social Credibility Theory, it
examines how digital stimuli shape patients’ perceptions and behavioral responses. A
quantitative explanatory design was employed. Data were collected from 150 active
followers of a surgical clinic’s social media accounts and analyzed using Partial Least
Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM). The findings reveal that social media
quality comprising clarity, interactivity, and relevance and personal branding compr ising
authority and authenticity are critical drivers in constructing a robust brand image and
encouraging patients to engage in E -WoM. These results suggest that in high -risk
healthcare settings, patients do not respond merely to promotional content; they evaluate
the credibility, usefulness, and relational value of digital touchpoints, which together
shape institutional perceptions and willingness to share information. Theoretically, this
study contributes by integrating social media quality and personal branding within a high -
risk healthcare context, thereby addressing a gap in digital branding literature and
extending the S–O–R framework and Social Credibility Theory to surgical clinic marketing.
Practically, it offers strategic insights for surgical clinic managers to optimize digital
communication strategies, leverage physician credibility, and strengthen patient trust and
institutional reputation. The study also highlights the importance of managing both
platform-level and person -level digital cues to foster favorable brand outcomes and
positive E-WoM..

Keywords: Digital branding, healthcare marketing, personal branding, social media quality

INTRODUCTION
The rapid digitalisation of communication has fundamentally altered how
healthcare providers build identity and interact with patients. Social media platforms
such as Instagram and YouTube have evolved into strategic instruments for professional
image construction and health service promotion (Suryani et al., 2021) . In Indonesia,
where social media penetration reaches over 50% of the population, the opportunity for
medical institutions to utilise these channels for two -way communication with patients is
immense. Through educational content, transparent procedure demonstrations, and
patient testimonials, doctors and clinics can shape public pe rception and foster trust
even before a clinical encounter occurs (Siddiqui et al., 2021).
Surgical clinics are among the healthcare providers increasingly utilizing digital
marketing—specifically social media and personal branding —in recent times. This use of
digital marketing serves not merely promotional purposes but also aims to educate
patients and build trust. Patients frequently seek information online regarding services
and perceive social media as fostering a positive sense of connection with their doctors
(Benetoli et al., 2018). Surgical services are  perceived as high -risk; from a consumer
behavior perspective, this perception drives consumers to seek comprehensive
information from various sources (Suryani et al., 2022). Social media provides insights
into surgical services and offers medical advice, thereby increasing patient confidence in
undergoing medical procedures (Andraus Quintero et al., 2025).
Beyond utilizing social media for general content, doctors are increasingly
engaging in personal branding to educate the public and promote their surg ical clinics.
Studies indicate that the information shared and the behavior exhibited by doctors on
social media influence trust os patients (Fatollahi et al., 2020). These trust came from
positivebrand  image which can be shaped by the quality of social media (Suryani et al.,
2020b). Therefore, it is important to examine the impact of social media quality and
personal branding in this context, as a positive clinic image fosters patient tru st.Patient
trust encourages adherence to medical advice and is crucial for fostering long -term
patient relationships. Furthermore, a doctor's creativity in personal branding —linked to
the surgical clinic where they practice —is vital for the clinic's sustainability; attracting
more patients can boost revenue, which in turn allows for the upgrading of facilities
(Dornelles et al., 2025).

This study addresses these gaps by integrating social media quality and personal
branding as distinct yet complementary stimuli within the S -O-R paradigm, positioning
brand image as the central organism and E -WoM on Google Reviews as the behavioural
response(Rahmah & Riyadi, 2025) . Additionally, Social Credibility Theory is employed to
unpack the dimensions of personal branding that establish a doctor’s trustworthiness and
expertise. By focusing on surgical clinics in Surabaya metropolitan setting with high digital
engagement the resear ch provides novel empirical evidence that can inform both theory
and practice. The objectives of this research  are  to examine: (1)  the  relationship of
social media quality  on  brand image and Electronic Word of Mouth (E -WoM) at Surgeon
Clinic and the role of brand image as a mediating variable in that relationship; (2) the
relationship  of personal branding on brand image and Electronic Word of Mouth (E -WoM)
at Surgeon Clinic and the role of brand image as a mediating variable in that relationship.

METHODS
This study was used a quantitative approach, and explanatory research design
with a cross sectional approach. This study employed a survey method involving the
distribution of questionnaires to respondents. To ensure adherence to research ethics,
the following ethics rules were implemented: (1) respondents provided informed consent
regarding the study's objectives, benefits, and procedures; (2) participation was voluntary;
and (3) respondent anonymity and data confidentiality were maintained  (Cooper &
Schindler, 2021).
The research aims to examine the relationships among social media quality  and
personal brandingon   brand image, and  the impac on electronic word of mouth (E WOM)
within the cont ext of surgical clinic services in Surabaya, Indonesia. Data were collected
at a single point in time to capture the perceptions of active social media followers
regarding the digital strategies employed by clinics and physicians.
The study was conducted in Surabaya, Indonesia, focusing on surgical medical
services that actively use social media (Instagram and YouTube) for patient education and
branding. The target population consists of all active followers of social media accounts
operated by surgical cli nics or physicians practicing in Surabaya who have accessed
digital health -service information. Respondents were selected using a non -probability,
purposive sampling technique based on the following inclusion criteria: (1) minimum age

A self -administered online questionnaire served as the primary data collection

clinic's official digital channels, a Surabaya -based health forum, and snowball samplin g.
Prior to full -scale distribution, a pre -test was conducted with 30 respondents to evaluate

questions were easy to understand. These respondents  involved in pre -test, excluded the
respondents who involved in this research. The instrument also underwent an evaluation
by four digital marketing experts prior to the pre -test. All measurement items were rated
using a five-point Likert scale, ranging from 1 (Strongly Disagree) to  5 (Strongly Agree) (Koo
& Yang, 2025). Participation was voluntary and anonymous, and respondents provided
informed consent at the beginning of the survey.
The measurement instruments were adapted from previously validated scales and
tailored to the healt hcare context. Social Media Quality (X ₁) was operationalised through
four dimensions: clarity, attractiveness, interactivity, and relevance, drawing on the
SOME-Q model developed by (Suryani et al., 2020a) . Eight items were used (two per
dimension). In this study, the research instrument was modified by converting dimensions
into items, taking into account the research context while ensuring the representation of
the dimensions.  Personal Branding (X ₂) captured authority and credibility, consistency
and identity, as well as education and closeness, based on the framework of (Nurhadi et
al., 2023)  . Six items were used.  Brand Image (Z)  was measured through functional,
emotional, and symbolic associations following (Suryani et al., 2021) Six items were used
(two per dimension).  Electronic Word of Mouth (Y)  assessed review intensity, review
content, and digital recommendation propensity, adapted from (Watkins, 2018)  and

Data were analysed using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling
(PLS-SEM) with SmartPLS software. PLS -SEM was selected for its robustness in handling
complex models with multiple indicators and mediating paths, as well as its flexibility with
modest sample sizes (Hair & Hult, 2021) .The analysis followed a two -stage procedure:
evaluation of the outer (measurement) model and evaluation of the inner (structural)
model.
In the outer model evaluation, convergent validity was assessed through indicator
loadings (expected ≥0.70) and Average Variance Extracted (AVE ≥0.50) (Watkins, 2018) .
Internal co nsistency reliability was examined using Cronbach’s Alpha and Composite
Reliability (both ≥0.70). Discriminant validity was verified using the Fornell -Larcker
criterion (square root of each construct’s AVE exceeding its correlations with other
constructs) and the Heterotrait-Monotrait ratio (HTMT <0.90).
The inner model was evaluated by examining the coefficient of determination (R²)
for each endogenous construct, the path coefficients (β) and their significance via
bootstrapping with 5,000 subsamples (t -statistic >1.96, p -value <0.05), and effect sizes
(f²). Mediation analysis followed the four -step procedure: establishing a significant direct
effect from the exogenous variable to the endogenous variable; confirming a significant
path to the mediator; verifying the mediator’s effect on the endogenous variable; and
evaluating the change in the direct effect after the mediator was included. Descriptive
statistics (means, standard deviations) were computed to provide a preliminary overview
of respondents’ perceptions.

DISCUSSION
In line with the Stimulus -Organism-Response (S -O-R) framework, the stimuli in
this study consist of social media quality and personal branding; these influence the
"organism" (patient perception) —reflected in the surgical clinic's image —and
subsequently impact E -WOM as the "response." The results indicate that higher social
media quality leads to a stronger brand image for the surgical clinic. The magnitude of the
predictive ability of Social Media Quality towards Brand Image ( β = 0.371) is greater than
towards E-WoM ( β = 0.214). These findings are consistent with previous empirical studies

conducted by Suryani et al. (2021) and Indriyani (2022). Both studies demonstrated that
social media quality —indicated by clarity (regardin g medical messages and image
quality), attractiveness (content visuals and the doctor's delivery style), interaction
availability, and content relevance to patient needs —is associated wirh the surgical
clinic's brand image.
The findings of this study also confirm those of previous studies, indicating that
the strenght of brand image is strongly associated with —and plays a role in driving —
behavioral responses in the form of E -WOM (Chairani, n.d.; Khoirunnisa, 2023). As quality
improves, patient perception of the surgical clinic also improves.   Furthermore, the
results show that higher social media quality at the surgical clinic increases the likelihood
of patients engaging in E -WOM. Patients are more inclined to write reviews, share them,
and offer recommendations to others regarding a surgical clinic they perceive as having a
strong image. These findings align with earlier empirical research reported by Nurhadi et
al. (2023) and Yusuf et al. (2018), both of which demonstrated that effective social media
management—particularly through the presentation of educational content —significantly
influences the formation of E-WOM behavior among surgical clinic service users.
The analysis of the relationship between personal branding and the brand image of
a surgical clinic reveals a positive path coefficient of 0.432. This indicates that an
enhancement in personal branding is accompanied by an improvement in the surgical
clinic's brand image. Personal branding —shaped by factors such as the doctor's
background, specialized experience, active engagement in patient education, and the
authenticity of their messaging —strengthens the professional image, thereby elevating
the clinic's brand image.   According to perception theory, brand image is shaped by
experiences, observations, and associations derived from various relevant perceptions; it
can therefore be understood as the product of interconnected perceptions (Suryani &
Faikar Cordova, 2023) . A strong personal brand for a doctor may be linked to a more
positive perception of the clinic's brand image.

The implication of this research is that personal branding is very import ant to be
managed well in order to enhance the brand image and word of mouth (WOM) of patients
at surgical clinics. First, clinics should move beyond static information updates (mean =
3.77) and prioritise real -time interactivity (responsiveness, active Q&A, mean = 4.05).
Second, the lowest brand -image item, “feeling cared for by medical staff” (mean = 3.72),
requires improved service SOPs (active listening, clear explanations) to enhance patient
comfort. Third, the weakest personal -branding item, “doctor a ctively provides health
education” (mean = 3.63), can be addressed by structured content calendars and
dedicated media support. Fourth, because formal Google reviews scored lowest among
E-WOM items (mean = 3.49), clinics should encourage satisfied patients to leave
platform-based feedback by making the process simple and reciprocal. Fifth, the strong
effect of personal branding on brand image suggests surgical clinics should actively
cultivate and promote physicians’ personal brands through training and con tent support.
Sixth, the mediating role of brand image highlights the importance of building and
maintaining a strong institutional brand by integrating both social media quality and
physician personal branding into a cohesive digital presence.

CONCLUSION
This study confirms that social media quality and physician personal branding
positively influence brand image and electronic word of mouth in surgical clinic services
in Surabaya, with brand image acting as a partial mediator. The findings advance the
Stimulus-Organism-Response framework application in high -risk healthcare digital
branding, demonstrating that digital stimuli operate through both direct and organism -
mediated pathways. Practically, surgical medical services should invest in both platform
content quality and physician personal branding, as each independently drives patient
advocacy. However, the cross -sectional design limits causal inference, the Surabaya -
based sample restricts generalisability, and self -reported E -WOM intention may not
reflect actual behaviour. Future research should employ longitudinal designs, incorporate
objective behavioural data such as actual Google Reviews, and examine moderators like
patient risk perception or health literacy to clarify when direct versus mediated paths
dominate. Extending this model to other healthcare services would further test its
generalisability across the medical sector.
