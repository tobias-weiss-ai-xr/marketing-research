Abstract

Influencer marketing is now among the most widely used promotional strategies in digital markets.
However, how it actually reaches a purchase is somewhat underexplored , as arguably much of the
literature stops at purchase intention, treating a stated willingness to buy as though it settled the matter.
This deserves the inquiry of how much of the final decision intention really accounts for. With that
inquiry, this study examines the relationship between perceived influencer marketing quality, purchase
intention, and purchasing decisions among Generation Z consumers, with purchase intention modelled
as a mediating variable. In this case, Indonesia serves as the empirical setting because it combines a
large young population, high mobile internet penetration an d a fast -growing creator economy, a
configuration now found in many emerging and mature digital markets. Data came from an online
survey of 200 social media users aged 18-27 who follow influencers. It was analysed using partial least
squares structural equ ation modelling (PLS -SEM). Among the construct correlations, purchase
intention tracks purchasing decisions more closely (r = 0.69) than perceived influencer marketing
quality does (r = 0.61). More importantly, the association between influencer marketing and purchasing
decisions is much larger than a purely indirect route would imply: 0.61 against 0.44. Influencer
marketing in the end seems to influence purchasing in two ways at once, building intention beforehand
and assisting the decision at the point of  choice. Because the pattern follows from general theories of
source credibility, and not from anything peculiar to Indonesia, this paper could benefit markets with
comparable demographic and platform conditions. The study in the end offers practical guida nce to
brands selecting and managing influencer partnerships.

Introduction
Social media has reshaped the global marketing landscape by letting brands reach consumers in
ways that are more interactive, more personalised and more persuasive than conventional advertising
ever managed (Laradi et al., 2026) . As digital environments matured, a new kind of opinion leader
emerged. Social media influencers recently increasingly shape how consumers see products and brands,
and their knack for producing relatable content, building online communities and sustaining a sense of
personal connection has placed them between brands and consumers as novel intermediaries. Influencer
marketing followed, and it is now among the most widely adopted promotional strategies of the digital
era (Chen et al., 2024).
In this regard, Generation Z, born between the mid-1990s and the early 2010s, is among the most
digitally connected consumer groups anywhere. Its members, studies suggest,  spend a substantial share
of their waking hours on Instagram, TikTok and YouTube, consuming and resp onding to influencer
content (Priporas et al., 2017) . What shapes their purchasing decisions goes beyond product
information, including how credible, knowledgeable and authentic they judge the influencers they
follow to be. Nevertheless, arguably the literature does not investigate beyond purchase intention. They
treat a stated willingness to buy as equivalent to buying, whereas the two are related but distinct (Adaba
et al., 2025) . A consumer may form a favourable intention and never act on it; another may reach a
decision at the point of choice with little prior intention at all. Working out how much of the influence
of influencer marketing passes through intention, and how much reaches the decision directly, matters
theoretically. It also matters for campaign design, because the two routes call for different content at
different moments.
Indonesia is a to some scholars, a useful place to examine the question as it is one of the world’s
largest social media markets, with a young population and high mobile internet penetration, and
influencer-led campaigns have spread quickly across its industries (Syamsuar & Witarsyah, 2025). Even
so, empirical work on what specifically shapes Generation Z purchasing decisions in this market
remains scarce (Erwin et al., 2023; Saroyini & Putri PA, 2024). The value of such a study is not confined
to Indonesia. The conditions that make the market interesting - a digitally native young majority and
mobile-first consumption in an expanding creator economy, are now present in many other countries
(Mai et al., 2023). To that end, framing the analysis around a general mechanism, which is the angle of
this paper, could arguably inform comparable cases elsewhere.
This study therefore examines the relationship between perceived influencer marketing quality,
purchase intention and purchasing decisions among Generation Z consumers, using a quantitative
design and partial least squares structural equation modelling (PL S-SEM). Two questions drove the
study: (1) how strongly is each construct associated with the others, and (2) how much of the path from
influencer marketing to the purchasing decision runs through intention? The findings should contribute
to the literature  on influencer marketing and offer practical guidance to brands designing digital
communication strategies.

Literature Review and Research Model
Theoretically, influencer marketing is a strategy in which brands work with people who hold
significant sway on social media to promote products or services (Chen et al., 2024). Influencers come
from varied backgrounds: celebrities, bloggers, video creators, and ordinary users who have built large
followings on Instagram, TikTok or YouTube. The practice has evolved quickly, both as social media
use has grown and as the way consu mers interact with brands has changed. Followers often treat
influencers as role models or sources of inspiration, which lends their recommendations real weight,
and that standing is built through content that is consistent, authentic and relevant to the audience (Gong
et al., 2024). Because influencers usually share personal experience with a product, followers tend to
read their reviews as more honest than advertising.

Collaboration takes many forms. Product reviews, unboxing videos, tutorials, giveaways and
sponsored posts are all common, and such content is typically more creative than conventional
advertising and woven into the influencer’s everyday life, which makes promotion feel organic instead
of intrusive (Gong et al., 2024). Brands generally pick influencers whose audiences match their target
market. Influencer marketing also reaches demographic groups that traditional media struggle to touch.
In this sense, campaign success is usually measured through follower growth, engag ement rate, click-
through and sales conversion. Choosing influencers whose values and public image fit the brand
remains essential, as does conducting collaborations transparently (Musiyiwa & Jacobson, 2023) .
Consequently, audiences are quick to detect content that feels inauthentic or too commercial, so an
influencer’s integrity is central to retaining their trust.
Unlike traditional celebrities, influencers are generally seen as more relatable and more
accessible, which strengthens the persuasive power of their messages (Freberg et al., 2011). Their use
has grown as consumers have become more resistant to conventional advertising formats, letting brands
communicate in a more personalised and interactive way (De Veirman et al., 2017) . Follower count
alone does not determine effectiveness. Engagement rate, perceived authenticity and the fit between
influencer and brand matter at least as much.
At the heart of this influencer marketing environment is a layer of society named  Generation Z,
or more commonly known as Gen Z (Cao et al., 2025). This generational cohort comprises those born
between the mid-1990s and the early 2010s and is widely described as the first generation to grow up
in a fully digital environment (Priporas et al., 2017). Its members have never known a world without
internet access, smartphones and social media, and the way they interact, learn and decide reflects that.
They handle digital technologies fluently, using Instagram, TikTok, YouTube and Snapchat to socialise,
to find information, to seek entertainment and to shop. As consumers, they are more critical than earlier
cohorts (Francis & Hoefel, 2018). They place a high value on authenticity and transparency, and they
trust peer-generated and influencer-generated content more readily than advertising (Francis & Hoefel,
2018). Scepticism about conventional advertising runs deep, and what earns their attention is content
they judge to be honest, including content in which brands and influencers admit a product’s limitations.
They also engage actively through likes, comments and  sharing, and brands that build genuine
communities tend to earn higher loyalty from them.
Research shows that reviews and recommendations from influencers they follow carry
considerable weight, and Generation Z consumers are more likely to buy on a trusted influencer’s
recommendation than on the strength of traditional advertising (Fitriasari et al., 2025; Saputra et al.,
2025). Social and environmental issues matter to them too: they tend to favour brands that show a
commitment to sustainability, and they value products and experiences that can be adapted to personal
preference (Francis & Hoefel, 2018) . All of this makes Generation Z a distinctive segment, and
influencer marketing, with its emphasis on authenticity and engaging content, is well suited to reaching
it.
In the traditional marketing literature, source credibility theory holds that the persuasive force of
a message depends on how the audience judges its source, along three dimensions: expertise,
trustworthiness and attractiveness (Ohanian, 1990). Applied to influencer marketing, this implies that
audiences respond less to exposure itself than to their assessment of the person delivering the message.
Influencers seen as knowledgeable and honest produce better responses than those who are merely
visible (Cao et al., 2025) . Among Generation Z consumers in particular, confidence in an influencer
develops through consistent content, transparency about sponsorship and the sharing of genuine
personal experience (Lou & Yuan, 2019). Where that confidence exists, recommendations reduce the
perceived risk of a purchase. Recent Indonesian evidence points the same way, finding that perceived
credibility affects purchase decisions partly through brand-related perceptions (Rahmawaty & Purnama,
2025).

With that reasoning, this study treats perceived influencer marketing quality as a single construct
whose indicators capture the informational and credibility -related attributes just described: the
usefulness of the information provided, apparent expertise , trustworthiness and the authenticity of the
content. Physical attractiveness, the third dimension of Ohanian’s scale, is not measured separately here.
As purchase Intention generally influences purchasing decisions, the classic account of consumer
decision-making runs through five stages: need recognition, information search, evaluation of
alternatives, the purchase decision and post -purchase behaviour (Kotler & Keller, 2016) . Purchase
intention belongs to the evaluation stage and denotes a consumer’s stated willingness or likelihood to
buy (Dodds et al., 1991). The purchasing decision belongs to the stage that follows. It denotes the choice
actually made.
The distinction matters because the two do not coincide. Scholars found that an intention is a
disposition formed in advance, whereas a decision is taken at a specific moment under constraints,
among them price, availability, time and the presence of alter natives, that may not have applied when
the intention was formed (Adaba et al., 2025) . Treating intention as a proxy for purchasing therefore
risks overstating what marketing communication achieves. Modelling intention as a mediator, not an
outcome, separates the two routes: the influence that accumulates before the moment of choice, and the
influence that operates at that moment (Adaba et al., 2025).
For Generation Z , both routes run through digital information sources, online reviews and
influencer endorsements. Influencers act as intermediaries between producer and consumer. By
describing a product’s features, explaining its benefits and commenting on whether the price is justified,
they reduce the uncertainty a consumer faces. Evidence indicates that influencer content which is both
informative and entertaining strengthens emotional engagement, which in turn raises purchase intention
and actual purchasing behaviour (Djafarova & Rushworth, 2017). Indonesian studies report comparable
patterns across product categories, including skincare (Fitriasari et al., 2025) , food and beverages
(Sispurwanto et al., 2025) and TikTok -based promotion among university students (Saputra et al.,
2025).
The evidence on influencer marketing and purchasing outcomes is broadly positive, particularly
among younger consumers. Influencers affect Generation Z by building confidence, shaping favourable
brand attitudes and raising purchase intention (Sokolova & Kefi, 2020). Pinto and Paramita (2021) go
further and find that purchase intention mediates the relationship between social media influencers and
brand loyalty among Generation Z, which supports the mediating role adopted here. Related work
shows the effect can also travel through electronic word of mouth (Y unitasari et al., 2025), and that
consumers evaluate macro -influencer content differently from user -generated content once they
recognise persuasive intent (Sarhour, 2025) . Wijaya et al. (2024) and Ishak et al. (2025) report
comparable associations in other Indonesian samples.
To this end, what remains unsettled is arguably the relative weight of the two routes. If the
influence of influencer marketing were exhausted by its effect on intention, campaign design could
concentrate entirely on pre -purchase persuasion. If a direct as sociation survives once intention is
accounted for, then content that supports the decision itself, through comparison, reassurance or risk
reduction at the point of choice, carries independent value. This study addresses that question directly.
This all d irected this study to propose a rese arch model , pictured in Figure 1 , that relates three
constructs. Marketing Influencer (MI) denotes the perceived quality of influencer marketing as the
consumer experiences it, operationalised through the informativeness, apparent expertise,
trustworthiness and authenticity of the influencer’s content. Purchase Intention (PI) denotes the stated
willingness to buy products endorsed by influencers. Purchasing Decision (PD) denotes the consumer’s
account of choices actually made under influencer influence.
In this model, this study found that favourable perceptions of an influencer’s content should raise
willingness to buy, since useful, expert and authentic content lowers the perceived risk of a purchase.

Methodology
This study used a quantitative explanatory design as done by previous scholars (Syamsuar &
Witarsyah, 2025). An online survey went to 200 respondents aged 18 -27 who follow social media
influencers, using a five -point Likert scale. Following studies by (Syamsuar & Witarsyah, 2025) ,
respondents were recruited by purposive sampling, the criteria being membership of the Generation Z
age range and active use of at least one of Instagram, TikTok or YouTube.
Analysis was carried out in SmartPLS using PLS -SEM, chosen for three reasons: the study is
predictive rather than confirmatory in orientation, the method tolerates survey data that is not normally
distributed, and it suits models built from a modest number of reflective constructs (Hair et al., 2019).
The analysis ran in two stages. Section 4.1 reports the measurement model; Section 4.2 examines the
relationships among constructs. Participation was voluntary and anonymous, and every respondent gave
informed consent. All three constructs were measured with reflective indicators on a five -point Likert
scale, from 1 (“strongly disagree”) to 5 (“strongly agree”). The items were adapted from validated prior
studies and modified to suit influencer marketing and Generation Z consumers.

Veirman et al. (2017) and Lou and Yuan (2019).

Relationships Among the Constructs
Every correlation in Table 7 is positive and moderate to strong, which supports the general
direction of the model. Perceived influencer marketing quality is associated with purchase intention at
r = 0.64, supporting H1. Consumers who judge an influencer’s content to be informative, expert,
trustworthy and authentic report a greater willingness to buy what that influencer endorses.
The strongest association in the model runs between purchase intention and the purchasing
decision, at r = 0.69, supporting H2. That ordering is what the stage model predicts: intention is the
immediate antecedent of the decision, so the two should sit clo ser to each other than either does to an
upstream perceptual construct.
Perceived influencer marketing quality is also associated with the purchasing decision directly,
at r = 0.61, supporting H3. Note how little that falls short of its association with intention. Influencer
content evidently bears on the decision itself, and not merely on the disposition formed beforehand.
The pattern across all three correlations speaks to H4. Were the influence of influencer marketing
on the purchasing decision to run entirely through purchase intention, so that mediation were complete,
the correlation between MI and PD would approximate the product of the other two: 0.64 x 0.69 = 0.44.
The observed value is 0.61, a good deal higher. Holding intention constant, the association between MI
and PD that remains is 0.30, a moderate residual. H4 is therefore supported in part. Purchase intention
carries a share of the relationship, but a meaningful direct association persists alongside it, which makes
partial mediation the right conclusion here.
One however, must note that these figures describe associations, not estimated causal effects.
This means that further analysis would be needed to put the mediation claim on a formal setting ensuring
causal, if needed, be investigated. The ordering among them is nonetheless consistent and interpretable
hence the value of this paper’s argument.

Discussion
The results above substantially indicate that influencer marketing reaches the purchasing decision
by two routes at once. Part of its influence accumulates in advance, as favourable perceptions of an
influencer build a disposition to buy. Another part operates at the moment of choice, where influencer
content gets consulted for comparison, reassurance or confirmation. Studies that treat purchase intention
as the endpoint capture only the first of these (Adaba et al., 2025) . Based on the result of this study,
these previous studies seem to understate what influencer content contributes.
The result is significantly inline with source credibility theory, which places persuasive weight
in the audience’s judgement of the source rather than in exposure as such (Ohanian, 1990). This is based
on which indicators load most strongly on the Marketing Influencer construct: trustworthiness at 0.86
and expertise at 0.84, with informativeness at 0.81 and authenticity at 0.79 a little behind. The attributes
carrying the most weight are judgements about the person, not about the message alone. That aligns
with evidence that message value and perceived credibility, rather than reach, explain consumer trust in
branded social media content (Lou & Yuan, 2019), and with work questioning follower count as a proxy
for influencer effectiveness (De Veirman et al., 2017).
For Generation Z specifically, the pattern reflects a documented scepticism towards overtly
commercial communication (Francis & Hoefel, 2018) . Where audiences actively look for signs of
persuasive intent, disclosure and candour become assets. They signal that the influencer is not
concealing the commercial relationship. The persistence of a direct association between influencer
content and the purchasing decision fits that reading, since a consumer who returns to an influencer at
the point of choice is treating that influencer as a reference source, not an advertisement.
The mechanism identified above in the end , sheds light also that it may travel beyond the
Indonesian context, as it rests on source credibility theory, which has been applied across widely
differing cultural and institutional settings (Cao et al., 2025). In this sense, this paper may benefit any
context that shares three features. Firstly, a large and digitally native young consumer base. Secondly,

Nevertheless, one must note that what is likely to vary across these settings is not the direction
of the relationships but their relative weight. Studies have argued that where discovery and purchase
happen in the same session, the direct route to the decision should dominate (Mai et al., 2023). Where
purchases are deliberated over time, the mediated route through intention should carry more.
Comparative replication would test that directly, and would turn a single-country finding into a general
account of when each route prevails.

Conclusion
This study examined the relationship between perceived influencer marketing quality, purchase
intention and purchasing decisions among Generation Z consumers. The measurement model satisfies
the conventional standards for indicator reliability, internal co nsistency, convergent validity and
discriminant validity, so the three constructs can be treated as reliably measured and empirically distinct.
Importantly and conclusively, the relationship between influencer marketing and the purchasing
decision is considerably stronger than a purely indirect route would imply, which points to partial rather
than full mediation.
Further, the substantive conclusion of this paper is that influencer marketing works on Generation
Z consumers in two ways simultaneously. It builds a disposition to buy before the point of choice, and
it assists the choice itself. As argued in the results, what drives both is the audience’s judgement of the
influencer, principally perceived trustworthiness and expertise, rather than exposure or visibility. To this
end, campaigns designed only to generate intention leave part of the available influence unused.
As such, two recommendations follow for brands, agencies and platforms operating in these
markets. Firstly, select and evaluate influencers on evidence of credibility rather than on reach. Follower
count is a poor proxy for the attributes that actually mov e consumers. In this study the strongest
indicators were perceived trustworthiness and expertise, which suggests that brands and agencies should
be assessing category expertise, consistency of past content, the balance between organic and sponsored
posts, the tone of audience comments, and the influencer’s record of disclosure. Contracts can give
those criteria force by specifying disclosure standards, permitting creators to acknowledge a product’s
limitations honestly, and linking part of the fee to engage ment quality rather than impressions alone
(Musiyiwa & Jacobson, 2023) . Measurement should shift accordingly. Saved and shared posts,
comment sentiment, branded search and repeat purchase track the mechanism identified here far more
faithfully than reach does.
Secondly, design for the point of decision as well as for the build -up of intention. A direct
association between influencer content and the purchasing decision survives once intention is accounted
for, which means campaigns that stop at awareness and desire leave influence on the table. In practice
this means commissioning content that is useful at the moment of choice: side -by-side comparisons,

Nevertheless, there are several limitations of this paper. The analysis reports the measurement
model and the correlations among constructs; estimated path coefficients, R² values and a bootstrapped
test of the indirect effect would put the mediation claim on a stronger and formal argumentation. It may
allow the relative size of the two routes to be stated imprecisely. The design is cross -sectional, so it
captures associations at a single point in time which cannot show how perceptions of an influencer
accumulate or erode over the course of a relationship with an audience. The purchasing decision
measure is self-reported rather than observed, which leaves the familiar gap between stated and actual
behaviour.
Relevant to the above discussion, future relevant research could focus on estimating the structural
model with bootstrapped confidence intervals would quantify the direct and indirect effects separately.
It means that decomposing the Marketing Influencer construct into its component dimensions, physical
