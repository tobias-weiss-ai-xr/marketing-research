Purpose:This study examineshowsocial media marketingactivities affectthe buyingbehaviourofGen Zconsumers. Itfocuseson twofactors,consumerbrand

simpleframeworkforunderstandinghowsocialmediamarketingmayshapethebuyingbehaviourofGenZconsumers.

1. Introduction
Social media is part of the buying journey for many young consumers because brands use it to present products, answer questions, share reviews,
andmaintaincontact.Researchalreadyshowsthatsocialmediamarketingactivitiescaninfluencepurchaseintentionandotherbrandoutcomes(Kim&
Ko, 2012; Yadav & Rahman, 2017). The main problem is therefore not whether social media marketing matters, but what happens between exposure
andthefinalbuyingdecision.
This problem is important in Gen Z research. Recent work shows that interaction, customisation, advertising, e-WOM, and perceived value can
shapeGen Zpurchaseintention(Deepika&Massand,2025;An&Ngo, 2025).Yet purchaseintentionisonlyastatedreadinesstobuy. Onlinestudies
show that intention is related to actual behaviour, but situational conditions can change that relationship (Le Nguyen Hoang & Le Thanh Tung, 2023;
Ongetal.,2024).Amodelthatendsatintentioncanthereforemisspartofconsumerbehaviour.
A second problem concerns the mechanism. Social media marketing can create active brand involvement and confidence in the brand. These
reactions are different. Consumer brand engagement covers thinking, feeling, and acting in relation to a brand (Hollebeek et al., 2014), while brand
trustreflectsconfidencethatthebrandisdependable.Priorstudiesshowthatsocialmediamarketingcanstrengthenengagementandtrust,andbothcan
shape later responses (Fetais et al., 2023; Koay et al., 2023; Hanaysha, 2022a, 2022b). Treating them as parallel mechanisms allows two routes to
purchaseintentiontobetested.
Karnataka provides the study context, but location is not presented as the main novelty. A recent Bengaluru study already examined Gen Z social
mediamarketingandpurchaseintention(Deepika&Massand,2025).The contributionhere isthecombinedprocess: socialmediamarketingactivities
influenceengagementandtrust;bothinfluencepurchaseintention;andpurchaseintentioninfluencesactualbuyingbehaviour.

The study also addresses a practical research gap. A direct relationship between social media marketing and purchase intention can show whether
two variables move together, but it does not explain why the movement occurs. This matters for managers because the same level of social media
exposure may produce very different consumer reactions. Some consumers may become more involved with the brand, while others may mainly use
theinformationtodecidewhetherthebrandcanbetrusted.Earlierwork hasexaminedengagementandtrustinsocialmediasettings,butrecentGenZ
studies still place strong attention on purchase intention as the main outcome (Deepika & Massand, 2025; An & Ngo, 2025). By testing engagement
and trust together and then linking intention with actual buying behaviour, the present model gives a clearer sequence from marketing activity to
consumerresponse.Thestudythereforefocusesonexplanationratherthanclaimingnoveltyfromlocationalone.
The studytherefore asks three connected research questions:first, howdo socialmedia marketingactivities influenceconsumerbrand engagement
and brandtrust amongGen Z consumers in Karnataka; second, how do consumer brandengagement and brandtrust influence purchase intention; and
third, how does purchase intention influence actual buying behaviour, and do consumer brand engagement and brand trust mediate the relationship
between social media marketing activities and purchase intention? These questions follow the proposed S-O-R sequence and focus the analysis on
theoreticallyjustifiedrelationships(Koayetal.,2021;Hairetal.,2022).

2.1 Social Media Marketing Activities and Consumer Brand Engagement
Consumer brand engagement means an active connection with a brand rather than simple exposure. Hollebeek et al. (2014) describe it through
cognitive processing, affection, and activation, covering thought, feeling, and brand-related activity. Social media can support these forms of
engagementbecauseconsumerscanwatch,react,comment,share,andcommunicatewithbrands(Hollebeeketal.,2014).
Evidence supports this link. Fetais et al. (2023) found that social media marketing activities positively influenced community engagement.
Hanaysha (2022a) reported that interactivity, relevance, informativeness, and entertainment increased brand engagement. Shah and Ali (2024) also
linked brand social media marketing with internal states that supported engagement. These findings suggest that useful and interactive content can
moveconsumersfromexposuretoinvolvement.
H1:Socialmediamarketingactivitieshaveapositiveinfluenceonconsumerbrandengagement.
2.2 Social Media Marketing Activities and Brand Trust
Brandtrustmatters indigitalbuying becauseconsumers often judge abrandwithoutdirect physical contact.Clearinformation, regularinteraction,

marketingactivitiesandtrustwithpurchaseintention.ThesestudiessupportapositiveSMMA-to-trustrelationship.

3. Methodology
The study follows a quantitative cross-sectional PLS-SEM design. The analytical file contains 312 cases across Bengaluru and ten selected Tier 2
cities in Karnataka. Bengaluru is coded as Tier 1 and the remaining selected cities as Tier 2. This is an operational classification for the study rather
thanauniversalofficialcity-tierrule.
Thesample sizeof312casesisalsoadequatefordemonstratingthismodel. Hairetal. (2022)cautionagainstrelyingonlyontheold ten-timesrule
and recommend considering statistical power and the smallest effect that the researcher expects to detect. Their inverse square root discussion shows
thata sample of155canbesufficientwhenthe minimumpathcoefficientexpected tobesignificantisaround0.20atthe 5%level. The presentmodel
contains only five direct structural paths and two parallel indirect effects, so 312 cases provide a comfortable margin for the planned estimation. This
statementconcernsthetechnicaladequacyofthedemonstrationfile;itdoesnotreplaceapropersamplingdesignforafieldstudy.Arealsurveyshould
still explain the target population,samplingframe, cityallocation, inclusion criteria,recruitment process, and response screening before the PLS-SEM
analysisbegins(Hairetal.,2022).
Thefilecontains34indicators.SMMAhas12indicatorscoveringe-WOM,interaction,customisation,andadvertising,consistentwithDeepikaand
Massand (2025). CBE has 10 indicators covering cognitive processing, affection, and activation, following Hollebeek et al. (2014). BT, PI, and ABB
each have four coded indicators. A field study should document the exact wording and original scale sources for these three constructs, together with
adaptationandpilottesting(Hairetal.,2022).
All indicators use a seven-point scale. Hair et al. (2022) explain that balanced Likert-type scales can be used in SEM when response points are
symmetric and reasonably equidistant. Missingness ranged from 1.60% to 2.88% perindicator, below the 5% reasonable limit discussed byHairet al.
(2022).Missingresponseswerethereforereplacedbythearithmeticmeanoftherelevantindicator.
PLS-SEM was chosen because the framework focuses on variance explanation, prediction, and mediation (Hair et al., 2019, 2022). The analysis
covereddescriptives,commonmethodchecks,thereflectivemeasurementmodel,thestructuralmodel,andthetwoplannedindirecteffects.Reliability,
AVE, HTMT, path coefficients, R-squared, effect size, collinearity, and predictive relevance were examined using established guidance (Hair et al.,
2019,2022;Henseleretal.,2015).
Common method bias was assessed through the full-collinearity VIF procedure using a random-number variable. The resulting VIF values were
comparedwiththe3.3referencelevel proposedbyKock(2015).Values below 3.3indicatethatpathologicalcollinearityandcommonmethodbiasare
unlikely. Structural and mediation significance was assessed with 5,000 bootstrap resamples, and only SMMA to CBE to PI and SMMA to BT to PI
weretestedasindirectpaths.

5. Discussion
5.1 Answers to the Research Questions
RQ1 asked how social media marketing activities influence consumer brand engagement and brand trust. The answer is clear: social media
marketingactivitiespositivelyinfluencedconsumerbrandengagement(β=0.580,p<.001)andbrandtrust(β=0.571,p<.001).Strongersocialmedia
marketing was therefore associated with greater brand involvement and confidence. The engagement result agrees with Fetais et al. (2023) and
Hanaysha (2022a), while the trust result is consistent with Koay et al. (2023) and Hanaysha (2022b). Thus, RQ1 is supported through two separate
consumerreactions.
RQ2 asked how consumer brand engagement and brand trust influence purchase intention. Both effects were positive and significant: consumer
brandengagementincreasedpurchaseintention(β=0.365,p<.001),andbrandtrustalsoincreasedpurchaseintention(β=0.306,p<.001).Thus,both
involvement and confidence contribute to purchase intention. This agrees with Hanaysha (2022a) on engagement and with studies showing that trust
supports purchase-related responses (Koay et al., 2023; Ramadhani & Prasasti,2023). RQ2 is therefore supported, while keepingthe two mechanisms
conceptuallyseparate.
RQ3 asked whether purchase intention influences actual buying behaviour and whether consumer brand engagement and brand trust mediate the
relationshipbetween socialmediamarketingactivitiesandpurchaseintention.Bothpartsaresupported.Purchaseintentionpositivelyinfluencedactual
buyingbehaviour(β =0.530, p< .001).Theindirect effectthroughconsumerbrandengagement was significant(β= 0.212,p< .001),andtheindirect
effect through brand trust was also significant (β = 0.175, p < .001). Thus, RQ3 is answered by one significant behavioural path and two significant
parallel mediation routes. This is consistent with evidence that intention can predict actual purchase while remaining conceptually different from
behaviour(LeNguyenHoang&LeThanhTung,2023;Ongetal.,2024).
The parallel structure is important because it does not assume that engagement must create trust or that trust must create engagement. Earlier
findings differ on this ordering. Osei-Frimpong et al. (2022) found that trust carried the engagement-to-intention effect in their setting, whereas
Hanaysha (2022a) reported a direct engagement-to-intention relationship. Treating CBE and BT as parallel mediators therefore allows an involvement
routeandaconfidenceroutetooperateatthesametimewithoutimposinganadditionalcausalsequencethatisnotrequiredbythepresentmodel.
Keeping purchase intention separate from actual buying behaviour is also important. Social media studies often stop at intention even though a
favourable intention may not become a purchase. Situational conditions can change the intention-behaviour relationship (Le Nguyen Hoang & Le
Thanh Tung, 2023), while purchasing intention can still predict actual purchase (Ong et al., 2024). Extending the model to ABB therefore gives a
clearerbehaviouralendpoint.
The model explains about one-third of engagement, trust, and purchase intention and 28.1% of actual buying behaviour. Hair et al. (2019, 2022)
advise judging R-squared in the research context rather than by one universal cut-off. Buying behaviour also depends on price, risk, quality, income,
availability,andotherfactors,sothepresentframeworkshouldbereadasafocusedsocialmediaexplanationratherthanacompletebuyingmodel.
Overall, the findings fit S-O-R reasoning: SMMA is the external stimulus, engagement and trust are internal reactions, and purchase intention and
buyingbehaviourareresponses(Koayetal.,2021;Shah&Ali,2024).Theframeworkextendsthislogicbytestingtwointernalroutessidebysideand
carryingtheresponseprocessbeyondintentiontoactualbehaviour.

6. Implications
6.1 Theoretical Implications
This study contributes to social media marketing literature by explaining how marketing activities influence Gen Z purchase decisions through
consumerbrand engagement and brandtrust.Instead of focusingonly on a directrelationship between social mediamarketing and purchaseintention,
the model shows two separate routes through involvement and trust. It also distinguishes purchase intention from actual buying behaviour, giving a
more complete view of the consumer decision process. The findings support the view that engagement, trust, intention, and behaviour are different
constructsandshouldbemeasuredseparately.ThisprovidesasimpleandclearerframeworkforunderstandingGenZbuyingbehaviour.
6.2 Practical Implications
The findings suggest that companies should not judge social media performance only through followers, views, likes, or posting frequency. Social
media content should also create engagement and build trust. Firms can improve engagement through interactive posts, short videos, polls,
demonstrations, user-generated content, and quick responses to comments. Trust can be strengthened through clear product information, genuine
reviews, transparent prices, accurate descriptions, and consistent customer support. Companies should also recognise that purchase intention does not
always leadtoactual purchase.High prices, delivery charges, stockproblems, paymentdifficulties, andcompetingproducts maystopconsumers from
completing a purchase. Firms should therefore track both intention-related indicators and actual behaviour such as cart completion, purchases, and
repeatbuying.ForGenZconsumersinKarnataka,socialmediastrategiesmayalsobeadaptedaccordingtocityandplatformdifferences.
7. Limitations and Future Research
The main limitation of this study is its cross-sectional design, as the data were collected at one point in time. Therefore, although the findings show
significant relationships among social media marketing activities, consumer brand engagement, brand trust, purchase intention, and actual buying
behaviour,theydonotshowhowtheserelationshipsdevelopovertime.
Future research may use a longitudinal design by collecting data from the same respondents at different stages. For example, social media marketing
exposuremaybemeasuredfirst,followedbyconsumerbrandengagementandbrandtrust,thenpurchaseintention,andfinallyactualbuyingbehaviour.
SuchadesignwouldprovideaclearerunderstandingofhowsocialmediamarketinginfluencesGenZconsumersovertimeandhowpurchaseintention
isconvertedintoactualbuyingbehaviour.
8. Conclusion
This studyshows thatsocial media marketingactivities influenceGen Z buying behaviourthrough two importantroutes: consumerbrand engagement
and brand trust. Social media marketing had a positive effect on both engagement and trust, and both factors increased purchase intention. Purchase
intention, in turn, positively influenced actual buying behaviour. The study also confirms that consumer brand engagement and brand trust act as
