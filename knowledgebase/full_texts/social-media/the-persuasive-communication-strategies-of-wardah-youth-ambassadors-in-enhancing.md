Abstract
Purpose: Th is study aim s to analyze the persuasive communication strategy implemented by the Wardah Youth Ambassado r in
increasing Wardah's brand awareness on social media, referring to the persuasive communication strategy theory of Melvin L. DeFleur
and Sandra J. Ball-Rokeach.
Methodology: Th is study use s a descriptive qualitative approach with a case study design. Da ta were collected through in-depth
interviews with three Wardah Youth Ambassado r members and three Wardah consume rs, observation of soc ial media activity, and
literature review. Data analysis was conducted using the Mile s and Huberman interactive model, which include s data reduction, data
display, and conclusion drawing and verification, supported by technique triangulation to maintain data credibility.
Results: The result s show tha t the Wardah Youth Ambassado r succeeded in increasing Wardah' s brand awareness through three
persuasive communication approaches. The psychodynamic approach is realized through storytelling and personal experience that build
emotional closeness; the sociocultura l approach is applied through the adjustmen t of language, communication sty le, and the use of
social media trends in accordance with the character of the younger generation; and the meaning construction approach is built through
message consistency and Wardah's distinctive visual identity, so that audiences can recognize, recall, and form a positive perception of
the brand.
Applications/Originality/Value: This study provide s an empirica l contribution to the developmen t of community-based persuasive
communication strategies fo r brand ambassadors on social med ia, while also serving a s a practical reference fo r beauty marketing
practitioners in designing effective and sustainable brand ambassador programs for the younger generation.
Keywords: Persuasive Communication, Brand Awareness, Social Media, Wardah Youth Ambassador, Brand Community.
Introduction
The digital era has transformed marketing communication by making social media the main channel  for building
brand awareness, since persuasive  communication  there  is  two-way  and  interactive,  allowing  audiences  to  actively
participate rather than passively receive  messages (Luthfiyah &  Sari, 2025). Its effectiveness in persuading and shaping
audience attitudes depends heavily on the accuracy of the communication strategy used (Hajar & Anshori, 2021); as of
February 2025, social media users in Indonesia reached 143 million identities, or about 50.2% of the population (Riyanto,
2025), making platforms such as TikTok, X, and Instagram the primary channels through which the beauty industry builds
closeness with, and shapes the purchasing behavior of, young consumers (Fadillah, 2024; Mandasari et al., 2025).
Recent data shows that social media users in Indonesia reached 143 million identities in February 2025, or about 50.2%
of the total population of 285 million people (Riyanto, 2025), confirming social  media's position as the  main space for
interaction, information,  and  entertainment for  Indonesian society. This  figure  also  reflects that more  than  half  of
Indonesia's population is now actively connected to various digital platforms, so that social media is no longer merely a
complement but has become the primary communication infrastructure for both industry and consumers. Interactions on
social media enable a brand to build a more personal closeness  with its consumers (Fadillah, 2024), especially through
visual-based platforms such as TikTok, X, and Instagram, which have become favorite channels for the beauty industry in
reaching young consumers,  while also influencing  consumer  behavior  patterns in  seeking information before  making
purchasing decisions (Mandasari et al., 2025). This interactive characteristic of social media is what drives various local
beauty brands to design communication strategies that are adaptive to the digital habits of the younger generation as the
dominant market segment.
One local beauty brand that actively implements a community-based persuasive communication strategy is Wardah,
a skincare and cosmetics company founded in 1995 by Nurhayati Subakat and now under PT Paragon Technology and
Innovation (PTI),  which  originally  started its  business as a home  industry  in Cibodas,  West  Java  (Suhartini,  2020).
Wardah's position  as  a halal-certified  product  is also  a major  factor  in  its  sales  growth in  Indonesia,  with  consumer
consideration of Wardah's halal label increasing from 32% in September 2023 to 46% in August 2025 (Redaksi, 2025).
This increase shows that halal value is not merely a product attribute but has become one of the emotional and religious

considerations that also shape consumer loyalty toward the brand. With a primary focus on the  young women's segment,
Wardah utilizes various digital platforms such as Instagram, YouTube, and TikTok as its main means of product promotion
activities (Wardatus Shalihah et al., 2025).
To strengthen its persuasive communication strategy on social media, Wardah introduced a special program called
Wardah Youth Ambassador, a brand community formed in 2024 with a focus on inclusively empowering the younger
generation by providing a learning space and network that supports the creation of content that inspires kindness. Wardah
Youth Ambassador is an active platform aimed at empowering young people with a passion for becoming beauty
influencers and content creators, as reflected in its vision and mission to make Wardah Youth Ambassador the ultimate
beauty squad that gives rise to a new generation of beauty content creators. This case is particularly instructive because it
differs from the individually managed influencer or micro-celebrity accounts examined in prior studies (e.g., Luthfiyah &
Sari, 2025; Liwa & Mardiana, 2025): Wardah Youth Ambassador operates as a national, community-based ambassador
structure in which many members simultaneously represent one brand identity, so that persuasive communication must
be consistent enough to protect a single bra nd image while still being personal enough for each member's content to feel
authentic. Through creative content such as get ready with  me and a day in my life, each community member brings a
distinctive persuasive communication style and creativity, enabling Wardah to reach a wider audience in line with the
interests of the younger generation.  It is precisely this tension between collective brand consistency and individual
authenticity that makes Wardah Youth Ambassador a strategically important case for examining how a persuasive
communication strategy is designed and sustained at scale, distinct from single -influencer or one -off endorsement
contexts.  The use of such a brand community aligns with the finding that members' closeness and commitment to a brand
can enhance consumers' ability to seek and process brand information on social media (Amalia Maretta Fadhila et al.,
2024). Competition in the local cosmetics industry through social media is also becoming increasingly competitive, so that
a brand is not enough to merely be present, but  is required to be able to convey persuasive, relevant messages that build
long-term emotional engagement with the audience (Ramadhana & Syam, 2025). Although Wardah has utilized Wardah
Youth Ambassador as a brand rep resentation, the success of this role has not been systematically understood in terms of
the persuasive communication strategy used, and it is this gap that underlies the urgency of this study.
Brand awareness, as one of the fundamental concepts in marketing and communication studies, refers to a prospective
buyer's ability to recognize or recall that a brand is part of a particular product category (Saputri et al., 2020). Brands that
are widely known and embedded in consumers' memory tend to have a significant competitive advantage, especially in
highly competitive markets, so that increasing brand awareness becomes a top priority for many companies to sustain their
business (Sumarni et al., 2024). This level of brand awareness plays an important role because consumers tend to choose
known brands for a sense of security and to avoid risks associated with products (Kholifah et al., 2023), while strong brand
awareness also forms a positive perception through user recommendations and strengthens trust through intense interaction
between consumers and brands (Fidiana et al., 2024). Without adequate brand awareness, no matter how high the product
quality, it tends not to be considered by consumers in making purchasing  decisions (Ramadayanti, 2019). This concept
confirms that brand awareness is not merely an early stage of marketing, but an important foundation that determines
whether a product will be considered by consumers or instead overlooked amid the many other brand choices in the market.
Social media, as the channel through which the entire persuasive communication process takes place, is not merely
a technology platform but a social space with unique characteristics that distinguish it from traditional mass media, namely
as an interaction space that connects one internet user with another to obtain information while also participating in creating
and sharing that information (Muhammad Atha Ramaputra & Subhan Afifi, 2021). The most relevant characteristics of
social media in the persuasion process include interactivity and dialogic communication, in which audiences can actively
respond through likes, comments, and discussions, creating engagement that serves as a strong indicator of audience
attention and message processing. In addition, the phenomenon of user generated content and electronic word -of-mouth
also plays an important role, because a consumer's satisfaction or dissatisfaction greatly influences the information
conveyed to other consumers, thereby sha ping a positive or negative impression of a company (Agustina & Mahendri,
2023). Social media also enables the formation of parasocial interaction, in which audiences feel they have a personal bond
with an ambassador figure even without knowing them direct ly, as well as algorithmic curation that actively helps
audiences discover content relevant to their interests, thereby strengthening repeated exposure to persuasive messages
(Nurfajri et al., 2025).
To understand how persuasive messages are received differently by each individual audience member, this study uses
the persuasive communication strategy theory framework proposed by Melvin L. DeFleur and Sandra J. Ball -Rokeach in
Theories of Mass Communication, which states that the effectiveness of a pers uasive message greatly depends on the
internal and external factors of the message recipient, and divides persuasive communication strategy into three main
approaches (DeFleur & Ball-Rokeach, 1989). The psychodynamic approach is a theoretical framework that pays attention
to factors that influence behavior unconsciously, given that each individual has a unique psychological structure, so that
pre-existing factors such as attitudes, beliefs, values, and needs will mediate how a person responds to a persuasiv e
message; the same message can produce different effects on different people, and persuasion only occurs if the message is
able to interact with and internally change psychological factors. The sociocultural approach is based on the assumption
that human behavior is influenced by external factors, in which every individual cannot be separated from the influence of
others or their social environment; individuals within a particular social group, for example based on age, gender, or

education level, tend to have similar thought patterns and behavior because they share the same norms and values, so a
persuasive message will be more effective if it is tailored to the characteristics and norms of its target social category.
Meanwhile, the meaning construction approach emphasizes the design of words, thoughts, and language to attract consumer
attention, based on the belief that words can be positively manipulated to create new meaning, so that the relationship
between knowledge and behavior is achieved insofar as something can be remembered by a person; when a person's
knowledge changes, their behavior also has the potential to change.
A number of previous studies have examined persuasive communication strategies in various marketing contexts.
Setiawan and Arviani (2024) analyzed Pandawara Group's strategy in raising Indonesian public awareness of
environmental cleanliness through social media, and found that Pandawara Group attracted public attention through three
main persuasive communication strategies, namely ps ychodynamic, sociocultural, and meaning construction, realized
through extensive social media use, the involvement of public figures, and an emotional approach that captivates the
audience. Sherin and Yusniah (2025) examined the Wardah Youth Ambassador Med an community and found that the
community proved able to increase brand recognition, brand recall, up to achieving top of mind, while also highlighting
the community's role in increasing brand awareness through various educational activities and promotions on social media,
particularly in the Medan area. However, that study has not yet thoroughly discussed the persuasive communication
strategy used by Wardah Youth Ambassador in a broader and more comprehensive social media context. Another related
study is that of Liwa and Mardiana (2025) on the persuasive communication strategy of TikTok live streaming marketing
hosts on the @somethincofficial account, which heavily emphasizes verbal and nonverbal techniques in capturing audience
attention, but does not specifically examine the role of brand ambassadors such as Wardah Youth Ambassador. The study
by Luthfiyah and Sari (2025) on the Instagram account @Adheliamakeup also shows that visual feed consistency and
informative captions effectively build brand imag e, but it focuses on an individual makeup artist's account, not on a
national-scale ambassador community like Wardah Youth Ambassador.
Based on the description above, there is a research gap regarding how the persuasive communication strategy
comprehensively implemented by Wardah Youth Ambassador plays a role in increasing brand awareness on social media,
particularly when examined through DeFleur and Ball-Rokeach's three approaches in an integrated manner. Based on the
background of the problem described above, the research problem can be formulated as follows: how is the persuasive
communication strategy implemented by Wardah Youth Ambassador in increasing the brand awareness of Wardah
cosmetics on social media. This study aims to determine the persuasive communication strategy implemented by Wardah
Youth Ambassador in increasing the brand awareness of Wardah cosmetics on social media. This study is expected to
contribute to the development of communication science, particularly in the field of persuasive communication, and is also
expected to serve as a reference for similar future research, as well as provide students with an understanding of the analysis
of the persuasive communication strategy used by Wardah Youth Ambassador in increasing brand awareness on social
media, while also providing practical input for optimizing community-based brand ambassador persuasive communication
strategies in the future.
Method
This study uses a descriptive qualitative approach, in which the data collected take the form of words or images rather
than numbers, and are used to obtain in -depth data containing meaning. Qualitative research is conducted intensively,
with the researcher participating by carefully recording what occurs, conducting reflective analysis of the documents
found, and compiling an in-depth research report (Sugiyono, 2019). This approach was chosen because it aims to gain an
in-depth
understanding of the persuasive communication strategy used by Wardah Youth Ambassador in increasing brand
awareness on social media. The type of research used is a case study, chosen because it focuses on a specific case, namely
the practice of Wardah Youth Ambassador as part of the Wardah brand, allowing the researcher to explore the context,
process, and impact of the persuasive communication strategy in depth. The object of this study is the persuasive
communication strategy implemented by Wardah Youth Ambassador through content on social media, with the aim of
increasing brand awareness of the Wardah brand.
The research data sources consist of primary and secondary data. Primary data sources are data obtained directly from
the research subjects through interviews, observations, or reports in the form of unofficial documents that are then
processed by the researcher; in this study, the primary data sources are three members of Wardah Youth Ambassador and
three consumers of Wardah cosmetics. Participants were selected purposively based on the following criteria: for Wardah
Youth Ambassador members, active status in the community for at least six months, a demonstrated track record of
producing brand-related content (e.g., product reviews, get -ready-with-me, or day -in-my-life formats), and a combined
follower reach sufficient to generate observable audience interactio n; for consumers, self -reported familiarity with
Wardah Youth Ambassador content and prior exposure to or engagement with at least one member's social media account.
Three informants per group were considered sufficient because the study is designed as an in-depth case study rather than
a study aiming for statistical generalization: this number allowed data collection to continue until responses across
informants converged on recurring, non -novel themes (data saturation), consistent with the descriptive qua litative case-
study design adopted in this research (Sugiyono, 2019).  Secondary data sources are data not obtained directly from the

3931

data collectors, for example from posts on the Wardah Youth Ambassador social media accounts, news articles about
Wardah ca mpaigns, and previous study journals on Wardah's persuasive communication on social media, used to
complement and verify the primary data.
Data collection techniques were carried out in three ways. First, in-depth interviews, which are meetings between two
people to exchange information and ideas through questions and answers, allowing meaning on a particular topic to be
constructed, and enabling the researcher to gain deeper insight into participants' interpretation of experiences,
perspectives, and unders tanding of the research topic (Sugiyono, 2019).  Interviews were conducted face -to-face at
locations agreed upon with each informant between March and April 2026, lasting approximately 45 –60 minutes per
session, guided by a semi -structured interview protoco l organized around the three approaches of DeFleur and Ball -
Rokeach's persuasive communication theory (psychodynamic, sociocultural, and meaning construction), with follow -up
probing questions used to explore themes as they emerged. All interviews were aud io-recorded with informants' consent
and transcribed verbatim prior to analysis.   Second, observation was conducted to directly understand the interaction
between Wardah Youth Ambassador, Wardah cosmetics, and audiences on social media platforms, so that t he researcher
could discover things not revealed during the interviews and gain a more comprehensive view of the data context. .
Observation was carried out over a four -week period on the Instagram and TikTok accounts of the three Wardah Youth
Ambassador informants, during which the researcher systematically logged content format, captions, visual style, and
patterns of audience engagement (likes, comments, shares) for all posts published during the observation window,
supplemented by non-participant monitoring of comment sections to observe audience response in situ. Third, literature
review, namely the process of collecting, reading, and analyzing various relevant sources of information such as books,
scientific journals, articles, and research reports rel ated to the communication strategy used by Wardah Youth
Ambassador.
Data analysis was carried out using the Miles and Huberman (1984) interactive model, which explains that the
qualitative data analysis process takes place through a series of interrelated activities that continue throughout the study
until the researcher obtains clarity of meaning and reaches a point of data saturation. This analysis technique consists of
four stages, namely data collection from interview results, in -depth observation, and literature review; data reduction
through the selection of key points, focusing on important matters, and simplifying raw data related to the implementation
of the persuasive communication strategy; systematic data display so that the patterns of persuasiv e communication
strategy used by Wardah Youth Ambassador can be seen and conclusions can be drawn and conclusion drawing and
verification, which is still tentative at the beginning of the study but becomes credible once valid and consistent evidence
is found during the subsequent data collection process.  Concretely, coding proceeded deductively as well as inductively:
interview transcripts and observation notes were first coded using the three approaches of DeFleur and Ball -Rokeach's
theory (psychodynamic, sociocultural, meaning construction) as initial thematic categories, after which excerpts within
each category were re-read to identify recurring sub-themes inductively (for example, "audience -needs observation" and
"storytelling/personal experience" emerg ing as sub -themes within the psychodynamic category, or "non -coercive
language" and "trend adaptation" within the sociocultural category). Sub -themes were then cross -checked across
informants and against observation data to confirm that a pattern recurred rather than reflecting a single informant's
idiosyncratic account, before being finalized as the thematic structure presented in the Results and Discussion section.  To
maintain data credibility, this study applies technique triangulation, namely the use o f several different data collection
methods in the form of observation, in-depth interviews, and literature review, which complement each other in exploring
information from the same data sources (Sugiyono, 2019).
Results and Discussion
Based on the result s of data analysis obtained from six informants, consisting of three Wardah Youth Ambassador
members and three Wardah Youth Ambassador audience members, analyzed using Melvin L. DeFleur and Sandra J. Ball-
Rokeach's persuasive communication strategy theory,  it was found that the persuasive communication strategy
implemented by Wardah Youth Ambassador in increasing brand awareness on social media can be mapped into three
main approaches, namely the psychodynamic approach related to individual psychological fa ctors, the sociocultural
approach related to the social environment, and the meaning construction approach related to the formation of meaning
through the messages conveyed. Differences in experience, perception, and communication strategy in conveying and
receiving messages related to Wardah products on social media were also found; Wardah Youth Ambassador members
tend to play an active role in building persuasive communication through content, interaction, and the use of trends, while
consumers show varying responses that reflect their level of interest, trust, and decisions regarding the products offered.
The research results are presented thematically based on these three approaches, including direct quotations from the
interviews as empirical evidence that is analyzed in depth.

Psychodynamic Strategy: Emotional Closeness through Personal Experience

3932

Observation of Audience Needs as the Psychological Basis of Content
Before creating content, Wardah Youth Ambassador members conduct in -depth observation of  the needs and
problems of the audience, both through general observation and analysis of digital interaction data. This was conveyed by
informant 1 in an interview conducted at the Psychology Hall of Universitas Muhammadiyah Surakarta:
“When I pay attenti on to my audience, I usually just observe my followers, especially what struggles they're facing right
now….so I observe first, or look at what my audience needs, and then I start applying that to the content I'm going to upload ”
(informant 1, WYA member, 03/03/2026).
This quote shows that the content production process does not begin solely from the brand's interests, but from an
effort to first understand the audience's psychological struggles. From the psychodynamic perspective of DeFleur and
Ball-Rokeach, this step is relevant because the effectiveness of a persuasive message is greatly determined by the extent
to which the message is able to interact with the needs, attitudes, and values already present within the audience  without
such observation, the message risks becoming generic and failing to touch the internal psychological factors that mediate
the audience's response. What this finding extends beyond DeFleur and Ball -Rokeach's original, largely one -directional
model of message design is the addition of a feedback loop: rather than the communicator designing a message based on
an assumed psychological profile of the audience, Wardah Youth Ambassador members treat platform -level interaction
data as a continuous, empirical proxy for audience psycholog y, effectively closing the gap between the sender's
assumptions and the receiver's actual internal states before the message is even produced. Understanding of the audience
is further deepened through analysis of digital interaction data, as explained by i nformant 2, who was interviewed at
Cooken Café & Resto Surakarta:
“I understand the audience from observing interactions in comments, DMs, Instagram story polls, and also looking at insights,
likes, shares, and comments….so the content becomes more relatable and feels more trustworthy” (informant 2, WYA member,
05/03/2026).
This statement shows that the psychodynamic strategy carried out does not solely rely on personal intuition, but is also
supported by empirical data on audience behavior in the comments section, direct messages, polls, and insight features.
This data-based approach strengthens the accuracy in touching the audience's psychological factors, because the resulting
content becomes more targeted and has a greater chance of being well received, as it is based on real interaction patterns
rather than merely one -sided assumptions from the communicator.  In this sense, the platform's own engagement metrics
function as an informal, ongoing audience -psychology diagnostic that the ambassadors use to cal ibrate their messages —
a mechanism that is largely implicit in DeFleur and Ball -Rokeach's original formulation of the psychodynamic approach,
since their model predates the interactive data affordances of contemporary social media.
Storytelling and Honest Reviews as Triggers for Emotional Closeness
Personal experience is used as the main basis for conveying messages to the audience. Informant 1 explained that the
product reviews shared are based on her personal experience using skincare products:
“What I usually do, based on my own experience too, Sis….so from the content I upload, I really do give a review of the products
I've used, so I'm not the type to pressure the audience too much to follow me, but I personally show it directly to the audie nce,
so the audience can judge it for themselves” (informant 1, WYA member, 03/03/2026).
Conveying messages based on personal experience without any element of coercion aligns with the psychodynamic
principle that emphasizes that audience attitude change occurs thro ugh their own internal process, not through external
pressure. By giving the audience room to judge the effectiveness of the product themselves, the communicator respects
the audience's psychological autonomy, making the message conveyed feel more honest a nd credible. The storytelling
approach based on personal narrative is used to foster a deeper emotional bond, as expressed by informant 2:
“I use a storytelling approach, for example by telling a personal experience of dealing with a certain skin concern…. then I
create content about my acne-prone skin, and I also share my skincare routine or Wardah makeup products….that content gets
a lot of comments because the audience experiences the same thing, and feels the same emotion, like 'I've been in your position
too,' which makes the message feel easier to accept” (informant 2, WYA member, 05/03/2026).
This quote explicitly shows the psychodynamic mechanism, namely that the same message in the form of content
about acne -prone skin produces strong emotional reson ance because it successfully interacts with the psychological
experience of an audience that has gone through a similar struggle. The phenomenon of “I've been in your position too”
depicts the process of emotional identification that is at the core of the psychodynamic approach, namely that attitude
change occurs because the message successfully touches the audience's pre -existing internal psychological factors.
Informant 3 added that personal daily content also receives a positive response:
“I suggest that if daily content attracts a lot of interest, it's because it often feels really relatable to our lives, like sharing a
story” (informant 3, WYA member, 12/03/2026).
Audiences also confirmed the effectiveness of this psychodynamic approach. One audience me mber stated that the
communication built felt like a friend pouring out their heart, not a brand promotion:

 “It doesn't feel like a brand ambassador account, it's more personal. It's like a friend telling their experience, not promot ing,
so I feel like I trust it more, like having a heart-to-heart with a friend” (WYA audience, 03/03/2026).
This audience perception shows that the success of the psychodynamic strategy is measured not only from the
communicator's side, but also from how the message truly influences the recipient's psychological structure, in this case
a shift in perception from a “promotional account” to a “friend telling their story,” which in turn fosters trust in a more
organic way.
Transparency and Consistency as Reinforcers of Psychological Trust
Audience trust is built and maintained through transparency in the use of Wardah products, as explained by informant
“The way I maintain audience trust is by consistently showing people that I also use the product….so I show the transparency
directly, from before I use the product, and then after I use it, I also film the results, so maybe that's how I build trust w ith my
audience” (informant 1, WYA member, 03/03/2026).
This before-and-after transparency serves as concrete psychological evidenc e for the audience, strengthening their
internal beliefs without the need for excessive persuasive arguments. Informant 1 also emphasized that trust depends on
the audience's personal judgment, so that the communication carried out does not pressure the au dience to absolutely
follow the communicator's opinion:
“Because trust depends on each individual, right? If they ask, I give information based on my experience, so Sis, if I say it's
good, it's good, and vice versa….So the message isn't forced to conform, it's not like an advertisement, it's based on experience”
(informant 1, WYA member, 03/03/2026).
Informant 2 also maintains the authenticity of her communication as an official representative of the community:
“So as part of the official WYA community, I need to convey accurate and not exaggerated information….I still maintain
authentic communication, so that the audience sees that I am consistent, not just following the endorsement trend” (informant
2, WYA member, 05/03/2026).
“While also seeing myself as a brand representative who has been given trust. But that trust must still be maintained through
consistency, transparency, and good, intense interaction as well” (informant 2, WYA member, 05/03/2026).
These two quotes confirm that trust within the psycho dynamic framework is built gradually and cumulatively, not
instantly, through the communicator's consistent attitude over time. Informant 3 also added that her involvement in WYA
activities shared through stories also attracts audience attention:
“Every time I take part in a WYA event, I always share it through stories first, and on average people are interested” (informant
3, WYA member, 12/03/2026).
“Yes, because I myself often use Wardah products, and they often ask things like, what skincare is suitable for this type of skin,
so they trust the products I use more” (informant 3, WYA member, 12/03/2026).
This pattern shows that the ambassador's routine use of the product strengthens personal credibility in the eyes of the
audience, which in turn forms a psychological foundation in the form of trust in the recommendations given. Audiences
also confirmed that the communication built was felt as sharing an experience, not merely a sale:
“I feel like they're not just selling a product, but also selling their own experience with it” (WYA audience, 03/03/2026).
This finding is in broadly consistent with the study by Setiawan and Arviani (2024), who likewise found storytelling
effective in building persuasive credibility through content authenticity, and with Meilani et al. (2024), who found that
content reflecting brand values authentically influences target audiences. However, the present findings extend this line
of work in one respect specific to a multi-member ambassador structure: whereas Setiawan and Arviani (2024) examined
storytelling from a single, unified campaign voice (Pandawara Group), and Meilani et al. (2024) examined it within one
brand's own official account, this study shows that authenticity in the psychodynamic sense — the audience perceiving
the message as unforced, self-disclosing, and internally motivated — must be achieved independently by each ambassador
while still contributing to one shared brand image. The mechanism identified here (transparent before -and-after
demonstration, combined with an explicit refusal to pressure audience judgment) is therefore not simply a restatement of
"storytelling builds t rust," but a specific tactic for reconciling individual -level authenticity with collective -level brand
consistency, a tension the community -based structure of Wardah Youth Ambassador raises but that a single -voice
campaign or single -account context does no t. Overall, the psychodynamic strategy carried out by Wardah Youth
Ambassador succeeded in changing the audience's internal psychological factors, ranging from the need for validation of
experience, a sense of emotional identification, to personal trust, into an important foundation for the formation of deeper
and more lasting brand awareness.
Sociocultural Strategy: Adaptation to the Character and Norms of the Younger Generation

3934

Non-Coercive Communication as a Social Norm of the Younger Generation
The sociocultural approach of DeFleur and Ball-Rokeach emphasizes that a persuasive message will be more effective
if it is adjusted to the norms and values held by the target social group, in this case the younger generation of social media
users who tend to avoi d communication that feels forceful or excessive. Informant 1 explained that the product's
advantages are conveyed naturally, without exaggeration:
“The content where I mention the product's advantages, I don't oversell it too much, I just mention the standard effects I've felt
from the product” (informant 1, WYA member, 03/03/2026).
Informant 2 added that calls to action are chosen by considering the audience's comfort in making decisions:
“When it comes to that, I avoid instant -sounding sentences; I prefer to use invitational phrases, this approach makes the
audience feel respected in making decisions” (informant 2, WYA member, 05/03/2026).
This avoidance of sentences that promise instant results reflects sensitivity to the social norms of the younger
generation, who are skeptical of excessive marketing claims, so the communicator chooses a more equal invitational
approach that respects the audience's autonomy. Informant 3 also expanded the persuasive reach through her closest social
circle:
“I usually receive a lot of products from Wardah, and the new ones I receive I share right away with my close friends, so they
become interested in using Wardah products too” (informant 3, WYA member, 12/03/2026).
This practice of sharing products with the closest s ocial environment is a concrete manifestation of the sociocultural
approach, in which the spread of persuasive messages utilizes informal social networks and trust between friends, which
according to DeFleur and Ball -Rokeach is more effective than one -way communication because audiences within the
same social group tend to influence each other's norms and preferences. Audiences also confirmed the effectiveness of
this non-coercive approach:
“The way they convey it isn't forceful, they explain the product while sharing their personal experience, so it feels more honest,
not like it's being oversold” (WYA audience, 03/03/2026).
A similar point was emphasized by informant 5 as another audience member, who felt that Wardah's consistent
presence without a forceful impression actually made the brand easier to remember:
“The communication carried out by Wardah Youth Ambassador is consistent….so for me personally, Wardah is a brand that
doesn't push too hard, but it's still present. That's exactly what makes it easier to remember, and in my opinion, through Wardah
Youth Ambassador, Wardah has succeeded in conveying its message in the form of persuasive communication” (WYA audience,
03/03/2026).
This statement reinforces the argument that the social norms of today's yo unger generation value a brand presence
that is consistent but not aggressive, so the low-pressure persuasion strategy applied by Wardah Youth Ambassador aligns
with the digital communication culture of the younger generation, who value authenticity more than hard-selling. Wardah's
widely known reputation also strengthens social acceptance of the recommendations conveyed, as expressed by informant
4:
“Because it looks natural, Sis, not forced, and Wardah already has a name in Indonesia, and there are a lot of good reviews,
so I trust this brand” (WYA audience, 03/02/2026).
This quote shows that sociocultural factors do not only come from direct interaction between the ambassador and the
audience, but also from the brand's own social reputation, which has bee n collectively built within society, thereby
strengthening the acceptance of persuasive messages conveyed through the Wardah Youth Ambassador community.
Composing Captions, Hooks, and Language Adaptive to Digital Culture
Adjusting language style and conten t structure to the digital culture of the younger generation is another important
element of the sociocultural strategy. Informant 1 explained her approach to writing captions:
“For captions, I don't write them too long. But the main point is that I invite the audience to try the Wardah product I'm showing
in the content” (informant 1, WYA member, 03/03/2026).
Informant 2 explained a more systematic caption structure, starting from a thought -provoking question to a casual
call to action:
 “I've always used the first structure, starting with a question or statement, then the content with a story or main informati on,
and closing with a call to action; I also adjust the tone to the audience's character. For example, using a friendlier and le ss
rigid language style” (informant 2, WYA member, 05/03/2026).
This caption structure reflects sociocultural adaptation to the reading habits of the younger generation on social media,
who tend to prefer concise yet still informative narratives. Informant 3 added the importance of using a hook as a content
opener:

3935

“I usually use a hook; if the hook is interesting, it will make people watch the content all the way to the end, so I focus my effort
mainly on the hook” (informant 3, WYA member, 12/03/2026).
The use of this hook is relevant to the fast -paced and competitive content consumption culture of the younger generation,
in which the content opener becomes crucial so that the persuasive message is not simply overlooked. This finding
reinforces the results of the study by Sari, Kusumawijayanti, Taufiqurohman, and An Najwa (2025), which states that
caption composition is an effective digital branding strategy, as well as the study by Panjaitan and Simanjuntak (2024),
which confirms that a relatable communication style is more effective in attracting the attention of the younger generation
on digital platforms, particularly through visual and creative content that arouses their curiosity and interest.  Beyond
confirming that adaptive language matters, this patte rn points to a specific theoretical refinement of the sociocultural
approach for social media contexts: DeFleur and Ball -Rokeach's original formulation frames sociocultural adaptation
mainly in terms of matching a message's content to a group's norms and v alues, whereas the caption structure described
here (question/hook, narrative, invitational call to action) suggests that on algorithm -mediated platforms, sociocultural fit
also operates at the level of message form and sequencing  a message must first surv ive the platform's attention economy
(via the hook) before its content-level cultural fit can have any persuasive effect at all. This suggests format-level adaptation
functions as a precondition for, rather than merely an accompaniment to, the sociocultura l persuasion process on social
media.
Trend Utilization and Two-Way Interaction as Social Environment Adaptation
Wardah Youth Ambassador's ability to keep up with the latest skincare and makeup trends popular on social media
is also a particular draw for audiences, as expressed by informant 4:
“The content done by Wardah Youth Ambassador is more widely known by many people. The content on Wardah Youth
Ambassador's social media always updates on skincare and makeup trends for Wardah products on social media, but it doesn't
feel outdated either, Sis” (WYA audience, 03/02/2026).
The alignment of content with current trends confirms that Wardah Youth Ambassador members actively observe
and adapt to the dynamics of the digital social environment in which their audience interacts, in line with the sociocultural
principle that persuasive messages need to adapt to a continuously changing social context in order to remain relevant.
This is reinforced by the study by Sari, Kusumawijayanti, Taufiqurohman, and An Najwa ( 2025) and Nafisa, Muazzah,
Tristania, Zuhdi, and Triyanto (2026), which shows that content adaptive to digital trends can increase audience
engagement and interest in a brand. Besides following trends, two -way interaction through comments, direct messages,
and Instagram polls also strengthens the sociocultural dimension of the persuasive communication strategy, as previously
mentioned by informant 2 in the section on understanding the audience. The study by Nurhadi (2025) explains that the
higher the audien ce engagement with content shared by an ambassador, the greater the chance for the audience to
recognize and remember the promoted brand, especially through interactive content such as quizzes and Q&A sessions.
The quality of such interaction, as confirmed by Sakinah, Murtiadi, and Arista (2025), plays an important role in building
emotional closeness and audience loyalty, so that brand strength does not only come from the visual aspect or content
frequency, but from the quality of the communication itself,  which is able to build a sustainable emotional connection,
trust, and engagement between the audience and the brand ambassador. The study by Maulana, Dudayef, Balqis, and
Amanda (2026) also confirms that the presentation of personal content close to the a udience's daily life, such as
representations of lifestyle and product usage experience, is a widely used approach in responding to social changes that
emphasize emotional closeness between a brand and its audience.
This research finding is consistent with DeFleur and Ball -Rokeach's persuasive communication strategy, which
explains that the sociocultural approach emphasizes that individual behavior is influenced by the surrounding social and
cultural environment, so that the use of language suited to th e character of the younger generation and the utilization of
social media trends shows that persuasive communication is carried out by continuously adapting to the audience's social
conditions. However, the present findings suggest a refinement worth highl ighting: in the original theory, "social
environment" is treated largely as a relatively stable set of group norms and values that a communicator adapts to. In this
social-media case, by contrast, the relevant social environment is dynamic and platform driven trends shift on a weekly or
even daily basis, and two -way interaction features (polls, comments, DMs) mean the sociocultural context is partly co -
created by the audience itself in real time rather than existing prior to the communication act. Adapting to "the character
of the younger generation," in this setting, therefore requires not a one -time calibration to a fixed social category, but
continuous, iterative recalibration to a moving target a distinction that becomes visible specifically because this  study
examines an ongoing, multi-member ambassador community rather than a single campaign with a fixed message.

Meaning Construction Strategy: Message Consistency and Visual Identity

3936

The meaning construction approach according to DeFleur and Ball-Rokeach emphasizes that words and symbols can
be positively designed to create new meaning that becomes embedded in the audience's memory, so that changes in the
audience's knowledge have the potential to change their behavior toward a brand. In the context of Wardah Youth
Ambassador, meaning construction is reflected in how they build the audience's perception that Wardah is a brand relevant
to the needs of the younger generation, namely by first raising skin issues, skincare needs, and the audience's makeup
trends before introducing Wardah products as the solution. Informant 5 explained that although each member has a
different communication style, the visual tone and brand message remain consistently maintained:
 “There is indeed a consistent style and tone fr om the brand, like a clean visual and a positive message….but each Wardah
Youth Ambassador still has their own distinctive characteristics in how they speak, create content, and tell stories, so it's more
like operating within the same overall area, but th e delivery itself remains different according to their personality” (WYA
audience, 03-03-2026).
This quote clearly illustrates how meaning construction works collectively yet remains personal: Wardah's brand
identity is maintained through a uniform message  and visual framework, while each individual ambassador is still given
room to reinterpret it through their own personal style, so that the audience receives a consistent yet not monotonous or
forced brand meaning. The visual identity most embedded in the audience's memory is Wardah's signature color, as
expressed by one audience member:
“Actually, what's most memorable is Wardah's visual identity, which is characterized by light blue, where that color looks
really striking, so it's deeply embedded in my mind, with the color blue being Wardah” (WYA audience, 03/03/2026).
This statement is concrete evidence of the success of meaning construction through visual elements, in which a color
has successfully been positively manipulated into a symbol that represents Wardah's entire brand identity in the audience's
mind. This process aligns with DeFleur and Ball -Rokeach's principle of meaning construction, that audience knowledge
of a symbol, in this case the color light blue, can be remembered and automatically asso ciated with a particular brand
without requiring additional verbal explanation, thereby accelerating the audience's brand recall process. This finding is
in line with the study by Panjaitan and Simanjuntak (2024), which states that messages aligned with audience experience
are able to build a positive perception of a brand, as well as the study by Meilani et al. (2024), which confirms that content
composed authentically and relevant to daily life has a significant influence on the target audience. Visual identity has an
important influence in building brand awareness on social media (Panjaitan & Simanjuntak, 2024), which also supports
the study by Sari, Kusumawijayanti, Taufiqurohman, and An Najwa (2025), showing that message and visual consistency
are able to strengthen audience memory of a brand.
Overall, the three persuasive communication approaches, namely psychodynamic, sociocultural, and meaning
construction, do not stand alone, but reinforce one another in gradually shaping Wardah's brand awareness, starting from
brand recognition, brand recall, and potentially reaching top of mind, as found in the context of a similar community in
Medan (Sherin & Yusniah, 2025). Wardah's widely known reputation as well as its position as a halal product also
strengthens audience trust in the recommendations conveyed by Wardah Youth Ambassador (Redaksi, 2025), so that the
ambassador's role functions not only as an information disseminator but also as a mediator that bridges the brand's values
with the emotional, social, and cognitive needs of the younger generation audience simultaneously.

Conclusion
This study concludes that Wardah Youth Ambassador has successfully implemented a persuasive communication
strategy to increase Wardah's brand awareness on social media through three complementary approaches. The
psychodynamic approach is realized through observation of the audience's psychological needs, storytelling based on
personal experience, and transparency and consistency of attitude that gradually build emotional  closeness and audience
trust. The sociocultural approach is applied through non-coercive communication, adjustment of language style and caption
structure, the use of hooks and social media trends, and intense two-way interaction with the younger-generation audience,
in accordance with the norms and culture of digital communication developing among them. Meanwhile, the meaning
construction approach is built through message consistency and Wardah's distinctive visual identity, especially the use of
the color light blue as a brand symbol strongly embedded in the audience's memory, which helps the audience recognize,
remember, and form a positive perception of the brand. As these conclusions are drawn from a small, purposively selected
sample within a single b rand community, they should be read as analytically grounded interpretations of this particular
case rather than as generalizable claims about brand ambassador communication as a whole.
The persuasive communication strategy theory of Melvin L. DeFleur and  Sandra J. Ball -Rokeach, which includes the
psychodynamic, sociocultural, and meaning construction approaches, has proven relevant in explaining the communication
practices carried out by Wardah Youth Ambassador. The three approaches together contribute to  strengthening brand
recognition, closeness with the audience, and Wardah's positive image among young consumers. This study is expected to
serve as a reference for the development of community -based brand ambassador persuasive communication strategies,
while also encouraging further research that explores the effectiveness of similar strategies in different brand contexts or
social media platforms. Academically, these observations suggest that future applications of DeFleur and Ball -Rokeach's
framework to social media contexts may benefit from explicitly incorporating platform interactivity and temporal

dynamism as moderating conditions on the psychodynamic and sociocultural approaches, rather than treating the original
1989 formulation as directly transfer able without adaptation. Practically, for beauty marketing practitioners designing
brand ambassador programs, these findings point to concrete design considerations: (1) equipping ambassadors with
regular access to audience engagement data, rather than bra nd messaging guidelines alone, to support the psychodynamic
observation process described by informants; (2) allowing individual ambassadors interpretive latitude in tone and
storytelling within a shared visual and messaging framework, so that collective b rand consistency (meaning construction)
does not come at the cost of the perceived authenticity that appeared central to audience trust (psychodynamic approach);
and (3) building content workflows flexible enough to respond to trend cycles on a short time horizon, given that the
sociocultural fit identified in this study depended on continuous adaptation rather than a fixed communication template.
Future research could examine whether these patterns hold across other Wardah Youth Ambassador regional communities,
other product categories, or other social media platforms, to establish the extent to which the strategy identified here
generalizes beyond the present case.
