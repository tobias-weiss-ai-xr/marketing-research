Abstract. The rapid development of information technology has driven the transformation of con-
ventional marketing strategies into digital ones. This study aims to analyze the influence of Social Media
Marketing (SMM) and Influencer Endorsement on the purchase decision of Le Minerale Bottled Water
products among Generation Z in Semarang Regency, as well as to test the role of Trust as a moderating
variable. This type of research is quantitative, using a survey method with a Google Form-based ques-
tionnaire. The research sample was determined to be 140 Generation Z respondents residing in Sema-
rang Regency, selected through a purposive sampling technique. The analysis tool used was the IBM
SPSS Statistics 25 program with a Moderated Regression Analysis (MRA) approach. The results of the
first-stage linear analysis showed that partially, SMM (X_1) has a positive and significant effect on the
purchase decision (Y) with a significance value of 0.000. Influencer Endorsement (X_2) also has a
positive and significant effect on t he purchase decision with a significance value of 0.007. However,
the results of the second-stage MRA test proved that the Trust variable (M) is not capable of moder-
ating the relationship between either SMM or Influencer Endorsement and consumer purchase d eci-
sions (the interaction significance values are 0.994 and 0.910, respectively). Trust in this study acts as a
pure predictor variable (predictor moderator) that directly exerts a strong impact on the purchase de-
cision loyalty of Generation Z.

Keywords: Generation Z ; Influencer Endorsement; Purchase Decision; Social Media Marketing;
Trust.
1. Introduction
The bottled drinking water industry in Indonesia is growing rapidly, driven by rising
public awareness regarding health and product hygiene (Suryani &  Lestari, 2023). Amidst
intense market competition, the Le Minerale brand has distinguished itself through its natural
mineral content and a massive marketing communication strategy (Ananda & Handayani,
2023). A key strategic target market today is Generat ion Z—consumers known as "digital
natives" who are critical, independent, and possess unique consumption preferences (Kant-
arci & Ali, 2022; Wijaya, 2020).
To capture this market, the Fast -Moving Consumer Goods (FMCG) industry widely
employs social media marketing and influencer endorsements to shape positive perceptions
among young consumers (Ardianti & Widiartanto, 2019; Tuten & Solomon, 2017). Digital
platforms allow brand messages to be communicated visually and interactively to spark inter-
est (Kaplan & Haenlein, 2010). However, in an era of open digital information, consumer
trust is often considered a crucial determinant before a final choice is made (Lim et al., 2017).
Within the consumer behavior cycle, the final purchase decision represents the convergence
of external marketing stimuli and the consumer's internal psychological state (Kotler & Keller,
2016; Schiffman & Wisenblit, 2019). Therefore, this article aims to empirically examine the
actual contribution of these variables within the market ecosystem of Semarang Regency.

2. Preliminaries or Related Work or Literature Review
This section provides a state-of-the-art overview of the variables employed in this study.
Prior research has yielded diverse findings regarding how digital marketing approac hes
influence consumer behavior. Social media marketing and influencer endorsements constitute
two key pillars of modern digital business communication (Tuten & Solomon, 2017). The
presence of influencers on social media not only extends the reach of promotional efforts but
also transfers personal credibility to the image of the advertised product (De Veirman et al.,
2017).
While the majority of existing literature positions trust as either a mediating variable or
a direct antecedent (Pratama & Ramadani, 20 21), this study introduces a distinct structural
approach by designating trust as a moderating variable. This approach aims to examine in
depth whether trust can alter or reinforce the influence of external marketing efforts on the
purchasing decisions of Generation Z within the fast -moving consumer goods (FMCG)

3. Research Methods
This explanatory quantitative study was conducted in Semarang Regency, targeting
Generation Z consumers aged 17–29 who had previously purchased Le Minerale products
(Sugiyono, 2019). A valid sample size of 140 respondents was obtained using purposive sam-
pling based on predetermined criteria (Sugiyono, 2019). Primary data collection was carried
out using a digital, structured questionnaire with a Likert scale, distributed online.
Statistical analysis was performed in two main stages to test the model hypotheses
(Ghozali, 2021). Stage I employed Multiple Linear Regression to measure the direct influence
of independent variables on the dependent variable . Stage II utilized Moderated Regression
Analysis (MRA) with an interaction term approach to accurately test the moderating interac-
tion effect of the Trust variable (Baron & Kenny, 1986; Sharma et al., 1981).

The criteria for selecting respondents via purposive sampling include: 1) Respondents
officially reside within the administrative area of Semarang Regency; 2) Aged between 17
and 28 years old (representing Generation Z); 3) Have actual experience purchasing and
consuming Le Minerale products

4. Results and Discussion
Among the 140 respondents, the gender distribution was balanced between males
(51.4%) and females (48.6%). Regarding age demographics, the sample was predominantly
composed of the 21–29 age group, accounting for over 81% of the total.

Preliminary instrument testing confirmed the validity of all questionnaire items, as cor-
relation coefficients exceeded the critical threshold > 0.1660), and demonstrated reliability

also satisfactorily met, with residuals showing a normal distribution (p = 0.200) and the model

of the interaction terms in Stage II revealed  of 0.994 for the X1 Z interaction and 0.910 for
the X_2Z interaction. As both values significantly exceeded the threshold ($p > 0.05), it can
be statistically concluded that Trust does not moderate the relationship of Social Media Mar-
keting or Influencer Endorsement with the purchase decision for Le Minerale products. Alt-
hough the moderating interaction effect was not supported in the structural analysis, the co-
efficient of determination (R-Square) showed a significant increase from 70.0% to 81.3% (a
net rise of 11.3%). Mathematically, this shift is attributed to the substantial direct effect coef-
ficient of the Trust variable acting independently (beta = 0.719; p = 0.000). The phenomenon
observed in the field data theoretically classifies the Trust variable (Z) as a predictor-moder-
ator (independent predictor) type; in this role, the variable does not interact with the inde-
pendent variable but instead functions solely as a standard, standalone predictor variable
within the estimation model (Baron & Kenny, 1986; Sharma et al., 1981).

Empirical results prove that SMM plays the most dominant role in forming Generation
Z's purchase decisions toward Le Minerale products (Sig. 0.000). Likewise, Influencer En-
dorsement is proven to provide a significant positive contribution (Sig. 0.007).
However, the most essential finding from this MRA hypothesis test is the rejection of
Trust's function as a moderating variable. The jump in the coefficient of determination (R^2)
from 70.0% to 81.3% is purely driven by the independent effect of the Trust var iable acting
as a new, very strong independent variable (individual significance value M=0.000). This
places Trust into the typology of a Predictor Variable (Predictor Moderator).
5. Comparison
Compared to previous studies in which trust typically strengthens online marketing
campaigns as a quasi-moderator or homologous moderator (Pratama & Ramadani, 2021), this
study provides a structurally different paradigm. In this specific market segment (Generation
Z consumers of FMCG products), trust acts purely as an independent backbone (Kantarci &
Ali, 2022). Generation Z evaluates product reliability and health benefits directly based on
objective information, making trust an absolute requirement in its own right, rather than a
conditioning variable that strengthens or weakens the function of external marketing efforts
(Hennigs et al., 2012).
6. Conclusions
This study concludes that social media marketing and influencer endorsements have a
positive and significant direct influence on purchasing decisions for Le Minerale products
among Generation Z consumers in Semarang Regency.

Meanwhile, the Trust variable was not proven to function as a moderating variable but
instead acted purely as a predictor/moderator due to its very dominant direct influence on
consumer purchasing decisions for daily food products.
Producers are advised to continue to maintain the consistency of product physical qual-
ity, the honesty of mineral claims, and packaging hygiene to maintain the already established
strong base of consumer trust. For future researchers wishing to examine similar consumer
behavior topics, the Trust variable should be positioned directly as the primary independent
variable or tested as an intervening (mediating) variable within a structural analysis framework.
