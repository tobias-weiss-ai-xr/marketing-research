Abstract
Background: Social media networking has become an increasingly important tool for
influencing consumer behavior, especially among Gen Z, who are highly engaged with social
media networks. In Nepal, limited research has examined how v arious dimensions of social
media network marketing influence purchasing behaviors. This study seeks to examine the
influence of entertainment, customization, interaction , and trendiness on purchasing behavior
among Gen Z, with word of mouth as a mediating variable.
Methods: The study employed an explanatory research design with data collected from 488
Gen Z consumers in Kathmandu Valley. The data collected were analyzed using Partial Least
Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS SEM) to examine the relat ionships between
dimensions of social network marketing, Word of mouth, and Purchasing behavior.

Introduction
The rapid diffusion of social me dia has fundamentally transformed how consumers discover,
evaluate, and purchase products. Social platforms are no longer confined to interpersonal
communication and entertainment; they increasingly function as commercial environments
where product information, brand communication, peer interaction, and purchasing decisions
converge. This transformation is occurring at a considerable rate (Cunningham & Craig,
2019; Timsina & Bhattarai, 2025 ). In 2025,  approximately 5.24 billion active social media
user identities were recorded worldwide, representing more than 60% of the global
population ( Kemp, 2025 ). The commercial significance of this transformation is equally
substantial. A global survey of 24,000 online shoppers across 24 countries found that 70%
had already purchased through social media, while 70% expected social platforms to become
their primary shopping destination by 2030 ( DHL Group , 2025 ). Social  media has
consequently evolved from a communication channel into an important component of the
contemporary consumer decision environment (Appel et al., 2020).
This transformation is particularly consequential for consumer purchas ing behavior, which
encompasses how individuals identify needs, seek information, evaluate alternatives, make
purchasing decisions, and respond to products and brands  (Ramchandran et al., 2025 ).
Although purchasing behavior has traditionally been explained through information search,
perceived value, attitudes, and social influence, digital environments have expanded these
processes by embedding consumers within continuous streams of personalized content and
social interactio n. Social media enables consumers to encounter products, compare
alternatives through user -generated content, observe others' experiences, and communicate
directly with brands before purchasing  (Kim & Johnson, 2016 ). Recent eviden ce indicates

This transformation is particularly salient for Ge neration Z, whose consumer experiences

Within this environment, word o f mouth (WOM) provides an important mechanism for
explaining how social media exposure may translate into purchasing behavior. Consumer
decisions are rarely based exclusively on firm -generated promotional messages;
recommendations, reviews, experiences, and opinions communicated by other consumers can
provide social validation and reduce uncertainty (Chen et al., 2025 ; Li et al., 2026 ). Social
media has amplified this process by enabling consumer -generated infor mation to circulate
rapidly across interconnected networks. Research on social commerce demonstrates that
social interaction and social support can significantly influence consumers' intentions to buy
(Hajli, 2015), while subsequen t research shows that the quality, credibility, usefulness, and
adoption of electronic word of mouth can shape consumers' purchase intentions ( Erkan &
Evans, 2016 ). Thus, WOM represents more than an additional communication outcome ; it
can constitute a social mechanism through which consumers interpret marketing information
and form purchasing responses.
The relevance of this phenomenon is increasingly evident in Nepal, where digital
connectivity and social -media use have expanded s ubstantially. Kemp (2025) reports that
Nepal had approximately 16.5 million internet users in January 2025, representing 55.8%
internet penetration, while 14.3 million social media user identities were recorded, equivalent
to 48.1% of the population ( Dahal & Kreps, 2026 ). This expanding digital environment
provides businesses with increasing opportunities to communicate with consumers through
social platforms while simultaneously exposing consumers to peer -generated
recommendations, reviews, and commercial content  (Sarkis et al., 2026 ). Consequently,
Nepal provides an emerging -market setting in which the relationship between social -media
marketing and consumer purchasing behavior warr ants closer examination  (Perdana &
Gultom, 2026).

Despite growing evidence that social media is reshaping consumer decision -making, the
process through which social media marketing influences Gen Z purchasing behavior through
WOM remains insufficiently integrated, particularly in emerging -market contexts. Existing
research has established that social media marketing activities can influence consumer
purchase intentions ( Yadav & Rahman, 2017 ; Alalwan, 2018 ), while other studies have
demonstrated the importance of e -WOM in shaping purchase intentions ( Erkan & Evans,
2016). However, less is known about how specific social -media marketing activities work
collectively through WOM to shape Gen Z purchasing behavior in Nepal. This issue is
theoretically important because it helps clarify the social mechanism linking marketing
stimuli with consumer responses; it is also practically important because marketers need to
understand whether social -media activities influence purchasing directly or partly by
stimulating consumer-to-consumer communication.
Research Objectives
Against this background, the present study examines the impact of social media marketing on
Gen Z p urchasing behavior, with particular emphasis on the mediating role of WOM.
Specifically, entertainment, customization, interaction, and trend are examined as dimensions
of social media marketing, while WOM is positioned as the mechanism linking these
marketing activities with purchasing behavior.
By integrating these elements within a single framework, the study seeks to provide a more
comprehensive explanation of Gen Z consumer responses in an emerging digital market. The
study is therefore timely given t he continuing expansion of social commerce, the growing
commercial importance of Gen Z, and the rapid transformation of Nepal's digital consumer
environment.

Literature Review
Generation Z as Socially Embedded Digital Consumers
Generation Z (Gen Z) repre sents a distinctive consumer cohort whose formative experiences
have unfolded alongside the rapid expansion of the internet, smartphones, social networking
platforms, and digitally mediated communication  (Barbosa & Real de Oliveir a, 2026 ;
Chapagain et al., 2025 ). According to Rosenberg et al. (2025) , birth boundaries vary across
studies; Gen Z is commonly positioned as the cohort born from the late 1997 to the early
2010s. Rather than treating generational membership merely as a demographic characteristic,
contemporary research increasingly conceptualizes Gen Z through the distinctive
technological and sociocultural environment in which its members have developed. Their
continuous exposure to digital technologies has shaped not only how they communicate and
access information but also how they construct preferences, evaluate alternatives, interact
with brands, and make consumption decisions (Theocharis & Tsekouropoulos, 2025).
A defining characteristic of Gen Z consumption is therefore the integration of social media
into everyday decision -making. Unlike earlier consumer cohorts, for whom digital media
often represented an additional information source , Gen Z encounters social, informational,

media consequently operates simultaneously as a space for identity expression, peer
interaction, entertainment, product discovery, and consumption  (Fisher et al., 2016 ). Recent
research demonstrates that consumption -related social media peer communication is
positively associated with Gen Z's online shopping intentions, with peer communication
influencing purchasing through mechanisms such as perceived brand quality and brand

purchasing be havior cannot be adequately understood through conventional models of

The relationship between social media use and purchasing behavior is parti cularly important
because Gen Z consumers do not simply receive marketing messages; they actively interact
with, evaluate, reproduce, and circulate them. Social media transforms consumers from
passive recipients of marketing communication into participants  in a networked marketplace
where recommendations, reviews, comments, experiences, and trends become part of
purchase evaluation (Wang, 2021). Wang et al. (2012) demonstrated that peer communication
through social media can influence purchase decisions directly through peer conformity and
indirectly by strengthening product involvement. More recent evidence similarly shows that
social-media peer communication has significant implications for Gen Z online s hopping
intentions (Huwaida et al., 2024).
Importantly, Gen Z's purchasing behavior should therefore be viewed as socially mediated
rather than merely digitally enabled. The influence of social media may arise not simply from
exposure to brand content but from the interaction between marketing stimuli and consumer -
to-consumer communication ( Schivinski & Dabrowski, 2016 ). Electronic word of mouth,
peer recommendations, user -generated content, and socia l validation can reduce uncertainty
and influence product evaluations and purchase intentions. Consequently, Gen Z provides a
theoretically meaningful cohort for examining how social media marketing is translated into
purchasing behavior through interperso nal communication (Duffett & Maraule, 2024 ). In the
context of Nepal, where social -media use is expanding rapidly, examining this intersection
offers an opportunity to understand how a digitally immersed generation negotiates
marketing information, peer influence, and purchasing decisions within an emerging social -
commerce environment.

marketing activities shape consumers’ purchasing behavior by emphasizing that individuals
engage in behaviors that they perceive as capable of satisfying fundamental psychological
needs, including belonging, freedom, fun, and power  (Bradley, 2024; Ebrahimi et al., 2022 ).
In social media environments, entertainment, interaction, customization, and trendiness can
create need-satisfying experiences that enhance consumers’ psychological engagement with
brands and influence  their behavioral choices. Such experiences may further encourage

Entertainment and Gen Z Purchasing Behavior
Entertainment represents the extent to which social media content provides consumers with
enjoyable, interesting, and pleasurable experiences, thereby increasing their engagement with

behaviors that satisfy fundamental needs, particularly the need for fun, suggesting that
entertaining social media experiences can encourage consumers to respond favorably to
brands and their offerings  (Lou et al., 2019 ). Empirical research has demonstrated that
entertainment embedded in social media marketing enhances consumer engagement and can
positively shape consumption -related behaviora l responses, indicating that enjoyable brand
experiences may translate into stronger purchase tendencies (Chung & Jeong, 2024). For Gen
Z, whose consumption experiences are highly embedded in interactive and content-rich social

Customization and Gen Z Purchasing Behavior
Customization refers to the extent to which social media content, information, and
promotional messages are tailored to consumers’ individual preferences, needs, and interests
(Olsen & Pracejus, 2020 ). Customized marketing can enhance consumers’ sense of freedom
and personal control by enabling them to encounter information that is more relevant to their
individual preferences, thereby making purchase -related choices more mea ningful and
purposeful (Yildirim et al., 2026) . Prior empirical evidence suggests that customized social
media marketing enhances consumer engagement, perceived value, and satisfaction by
increasing the relevance of brand communications to individual consum ers (Khan, 2026 ;
Rehman et al., 2025 ). Such personalized experiences can reduce information overload while
strengthening consumers’ perceptions that a product or brand is responsive to their specific
needs. Thus, greater customization in social media marketing is expected to encourage more
favorable purchase decisions among Gen Z consumers.
H2: Customization positively influences Gen Z purchasing behavior.
Interaction and Gen Z Purchasing Behavior
Interaction captures the extent to which social media enables reciprocal communication and
information exchange between consumers and brands, as well as among consumers
themselves (Simon & Tossan, 2018 ). Interaction can satisfy consumers’ ne eds for belonging
and social connection by allowing them to express opinions, exchange experiences, and
participate actively in brand -related conversations  (Simon & Tossan, 2018 ). Previous
empirical studies indicate that social med ia interaction facilitates consumer engagement,
information sharing, and the exchange of product -related experiences, which can
subsequently influence purchase -related decisions  (Gautam, 2024 ). This mechanism is
particularly relevant to Gen Z because social media provides an environment in which brand
evaluation is increasingly shaped through dialogue, peer interaction, and participatory
communication rather than one -way promotional messages  (Zhang & Lin, 20 15). Therefore,
higher levels of social media interaction are expected to strengthen Gen Z’s propensity to
purchase.
H3: Interaction positively influences Gen Z purchasing behavior.

Trendiness reflects the extent to which social media provides consumers with current, timely,
and fashionable information about brands, products, and consumption practices
(Rostamzadeh et al., 2025) . Exposure to trending information can support consumers’ need
for belonging and social relevance by helping them remain informed about products and
consumption patterns valued within their social environment  (Dabija, 2018 ). Prior research
indicates that consumers use social media to monitor e merging trends, acquire product
knowledge, obtain pre -purchase information, and discover new sources of inspiration,

Mediating Role of Word of Mouth
Social media marketing activities can generate need -satisfying experiences that encourage
consumers to communicate their brand and product experiences with others ; WOM is an
important social mechanism linking marketing stimuli to purchasing behavior  (Wang & Yu,
2017). Entertainment may stimulate WOM by creating enjoyable and emotionally engaging
experiences, while customization can encourage sharing by making brand communications
personally relevant and responsive to individu al preferences (De Keyzer et al., 2022 ; Sharma
et al., 2024 ). Similarly, interaction facilitates the exchange of opinions and consumption
experiences, creating opportunities for consumers to generate and disseminate product-related
information within their social networks. Hollebeek and Macky (2019)  state that trendiness
can also stimulate WOM by providing timely and socially relevant information that
consumers share to remain info rmed, connected, and socially aligned with emerging
consumption patterns. Through these processes, WOM can strengthen product awareness,
provide socially derived information, reduce perceived uncertainty, and influence consumers’
evaluations and purchase d ecisions (Ngo et al., 2024 ). Thus, the four dimensions of social
media marketing (i.e., entertainment, customization, interaction, and trendiness) are expected
to influence Gen Z purchasing behavior indirectly by stimulating WOM, con sistent with
Choice Theory’s proposition that purposeful, need -satisfying experiences shape subsequent
behavioral choices. Accordingly, the study proposes that WOM mediates the relationships
between entertainment, customization, interaction, trendiness, an d Gen Z purchasing
behavior.

Valley, Nepal. The study conceptualizes social media marketing as a multidimensional
construct comprising entertainment, customization, interaction, and trends, with WOM
specified as the mediating variable and consumer purchasing behavior (CPB) as the
dependent variable. The target population comprised Generation Z consumers residing in
Kathmandu, Bhaktapur, and Lalitpur, and primary data were collected through a structur ed,
self-administered online questionnaire using KoboToolbox. Given the absence of a
comprehensive sampling frame for the target population and the accessibility of digitally
active Gen Z respondents, convenience sampling was employed, resulting in 488 usa ble
responses for the final analysis.
The measurement instrument comprised 31 items covering the study constructs. Social media
marketing dimensions (i.e., entertainment, customization, interaction, and trend ) and WOM
were measured using five items for eac h construct, adapted from established measures
reported in prior literature, including Ebrahimi et al. (2022) , where conceptually appropriate.
Consumer purchasing behavior was assessed using a six-item scale adapted from Kim and Ko
(2010) and Ebrahimi et al. (2022) . All measurement items were evaluated using a five -point
Likert scale, ranging from 1 = strongly disagree to 5 = strongly agree, providing a consistent
response form at across the constructs. Before the main survey, a pilot study involving 40
respondents was conducted to assess the clarity, comprehensibility, sequencing, and
preliminary reliability of the questionnaire. Feedback obtained from the pilot assessment was
used to refine ambiguous wording and improve the overall suitability of the instrument before
full-scale data collection.
Ethical principles were maintained throughout the research process. Participation was
voluntary, respondents were informed about the pu rpose and academic nature of the study,
and informed consent was obtained before questionnaire completion. Respondents were also
assured that their participation was anonymous, their responses would be treated
confidentially, and the information collected would be used solely for academic purposes.
Data analysis was conducted in two stages. Descriptive statistics were first used to summarize
respondents’ demographic characteristics and the distribution of the study variables.
Subsequently, Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) was employed
for inferential analysis to assess the measurement model and structural model and to test the
hypothesized direct and indirect relationships. The PLS -SEM approach was particularly
appropriate because the study seeks to explain predictive relationships among multiple latent
constructs and examine the mediating effect of WOM within the proposed conceptual
framework.

the constructs. The assessment included indicator loadings, Average Variance Extracted
(AVE), Composite Reliability (CR), and Cronbach’s alpha. Indicator loadings of 0.708 or
above are generally considered acceptable, as  they indicate that the indicator explains a
substantial proportion of the variance in its respective construct ( Hair et al., 2022 ). An AVE
value of 0.50 or higher indicates adequate convergent validity, while CR values between 0.70
and 0.95 indicate satisfactory internal consistency reliability ( Fornell & Larcker, 1981 ; Hair
et al., 2022 ). Similarly, Cronbach’s alpha values of 0.70 or above are considered acceptable
for establishing internal consistency reliability.
As presented in Table 2 all indicator loadings range from 0.713 to 0.852, exceeding the

recommended minimum threshold of 0.70, confirming satisfactory internal consisten cy
reliability. Therefore, the measurement model demonstrates adequate indicator reliability,
convergent validity, and internal consistency reliability.

corresponding inter-construct correlations, confirming adequate discriminant validity.
The HTMT ratio was also examined as a more rigorous assessment of discriminant validity.

effects on Gen Z consumers’ purchasing behavior. Trendiness emerged as the strongest

elements as important mechanisms influencing consumer engagement and behavioral

Massand (2025)  reported significant effects of customization and interaction on Gen Z

Spain, likewise found positive effects of customization, entertainment, and trendiness on
purchase intentions, although the magnitude of effects differed across cultural cont exts.
Collectively, these studies support the present finding that Gen Z purchasing responses are
shaped by informational relevance, experiential value, interaction, and social-media trends.
However, the findings also diverge from some previous evidence. Wijaya et al. (2021) ,
studying Indonesian smartphone consumers, found that entertainment, interaction, and
trendiness did not significantly influence purchase intention, whereas customization was
significant. Similarly, Cheung et al. (2020)  reported that interaction, eWOM, and trendiness
significantly influenced consumer–brand engagement, while entertainment and customization
were not significant. These differences suggest that the effectiveness of indivi dual social
media marketing dimensions may depend on consumer characteristics, product category,
cultural setting, platform, and outcome variable. The present study therefore extends prior
research by demonstrating that, within the Kathmandu Valley context , these four dimensions
collectively have significant behavioral relevance among Gen Z consumers.
The mediation results provide an additional contribution by demonstrating that Word of
Mouth significantly transmits the effects of entertainment, customizati on, interaction, and
trendiness on purchasing behavior. This suggests that social media marketing influences
consumers not only directly but also by stimulating interpersonal communication,
recommendations, and information exchange. The finding is consiste nt with Ismagilova et al.
(2020), whose meta-analysis of 69 studies established the significant influence of eWOM on
purchase intention. Synthesizing 1,532 effect sizes from 96 studies, similarly found a positive
relationship between eWOM and sales. Evidence specifically involving Gen Z reinforces this
mechanism. Ngo et al. (2024),  using Vietnamese Gen Z respondents, found that eWOM
information quality, quantity, and usefulness significantly contribute to  online purchase
intention, while Appel et al. (2025)  demonstrated that eWOM positively influences Gen Z
purchase intention. Thus, the present findings align with substantial empirical evidence
positioning WOM as an important socia l mechanism through which social -media stimuli are
converted into purchasing responses.

From the perspective of Glasser’s Choice Theory, the findings provide a theoretically
meaningful explanation of these relationships. Choice Theory proposes that behavio r is
purposeful and directed toward satisfying fundamental needs, including fun, love and
belonging, freedom, and power ( Glasser, 1998 ). In the present context, entertainment
corresponds to the need for fun; interaction and WOM f acilitate belonging and social
connection; customization provides greater freedom and personal choice; and trendiness may
enhance social recognition and influence associated with power. These relationships
represent theoretically informed interpretations rather than direct empirical tests of individual
psychological needs. Nevertheless, the significant direct effects indicate that social media
marketing becomes influential when it provides experiences that are personally meaningful
and socially rewarding. M ore importantly, the mediating role of WOM demonstrates that
these experiences can be socially communicated and reinforced through interpersonal
exchange. Accordingly, the study extends the application of Choice Theory to contemporary
social commerce by sh owing that Gen Z purchasing behavior may be understood as
purposeful choice shaped by need -relevant digital experiences and socially mediated
information.

Conclusion and Implications
This study examined the impact of social media marketing on Gen Z consum ers’ purchasing
behavior with particular emphasis on the dimensions of entertainment, customization,
interaction, and trendiness, and to assess the mediating role of Word of Mouth. The findings
demonstrate that all four dimensions of social media marketing  have positive and significant
effects on Gen Z purchasing behavior, with trendiness emerging as the strongest predictor,
followed by customization, entertainment, and interaction. Furthermore, WOM significantly
mediates the relationships between all four social media marketing dimensions and
purchasing behavior, indicating that social -media influence operates both directly and
through consumers’ interpersonal communication and recommendations. Thus, the findings
convey that for Nepalese Gen Z consumers, so cial media is not merely a promotional
medium but an interactive and socially embedded consumption environment in which
contemporary content, personalized experiences, entertainment, and interaction can stimulate
purchasing responses and encourage WOM. The key takeaway is that social media marketing
becomes more influential when it is current, personally relevant, engaging, and socially
shareable, while WOM strengthens the conversion of these digital experiences into
purchasing behavior. The findings theref ore extend the application of Glasser’s Choice
Theory to social commerce by suggesting that Gen Z’s purchasing choices can be shaped
through digital experiences that respond to needs for fun, belonging, freedom, and social
recognition.

implications.  The study contributes to social media marketing literature by demonstrating the
relevance of entertainment, customization, interaction, and trendiness in explaining G en Z

Limitations and Future Research
This study is limited to Gen Z consumers in Kathmandu Valley and therefore may not fully
represent consumers from other geographical areas or generations in Nepal; its cross -
sectional design also restricts conclusions ab out changes in purchasing behavior over time.
Future studies could employ longitudinal or mixed -method approaches and compare Gen Z
with Millennials and other generations to examine generational differences in social -media-
driven consumption. Researchers c ould also extend the model by examining platform -
specific effects, influencer marketing, social -media engagement, brand trust, perceived
credibility, or electronic WOM, while testing the model across different cities, product
categories, and cultural conte xts. Such research would provide broader and more pivotal
evidence regarding how social media marketing continues to shape the purchasing behavior
of Nepalese consumers.
