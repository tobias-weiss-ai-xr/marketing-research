 This study examines the eﬀects of social media marke ting and marketplace
marketing on purchase intention and customer engagem ent. A quantitative
approach was employed, involving 100 active social media and marketplace
users aged 19–35 years. Data were collected through  questionnaires and
analyzed using SmartPLS 4. The results indicate that social media marketing has
positive and statistically signiﬁcant eﬀects on customer engagement (β = 0.339,
p < 0.001) and purchase intention (β = 0.273, p = 0.012). Marketplace marketing
also has positive and statistically signiﬁcant eﬀects on customer engagement (β
= 0.295, p = 0.009) and purchase intention (β = 0.2 89, p = 0.005). In contrast,
purchase intention has a negative and statistically signiﬁcant eﬀect on customer
engagement (β = −0.224, p = 0.035). The indirect eﬀ ects of social media
marketing and marketplace marketing on customer engag ement through
purchase intention are not statistically signiﬁcant  (p = 0.122 and p = 0.138,
respectively). These ﬁndings highlight the importan ce of social media and
marketplace marketing in shaping consumer purchase in tention and
engagement, while indicating that the proposed medi ating role of purchase
intention is not supported by the results.

II.  Literature Review

2.1.  Social Media Marketing

Social media marketing refers to the strategic use of social media platforms to communicate brand
messages, facilitate consumer interactions, and sup port marketing objectives. Unlike traditional promo tional
channels, social media enables consumers to respond  to brand communications, share experiences, and
generate content that may inﬂuence other consumers.  Li et al. (2021) conceptualize social media market ing
strategy as a structured process involving strategi c drivers, inputs, activities, and outputs. Their f ramework
emphasizes that social media should be integrated i nto a ﬁrm's broader marketing strategy rather than
treated merely as an additional promotional channel . The eﬀectiveness of social media marketing depend s
on how businesses develop and manage interactions w ith their audiences. Kim and Ko (2012) demonstrate
the relevance of social media marketing activities to consumer responses in the luxury fashion context. Godey
et al. (2016) similarly examine social media market ing eﬀorts and their relationships with brand equit y and
consumer behavior. These studies suggest that socia l media marketing can contribute to brand-related
outcomes by facilitating communication, information  exchange, and consumer participation. However, the
strength of these relationships may depend on the p latform, product category, audience characteristics , and
quality of the marketing activities. Social media marketing may also inﬂuence purchase intention by providing
information, facilitating electronic word-of-mouth,  and enabling consumers to evaluate products throug h
other users' experiences. Erkan and Evans (2016) de monstrate the importance of information adoption in
explaining how electronic word-of-mouth on social m edia relates to purchase intention. Accordingly, so cial
media marketing is expected to be positively associated with purchase intention and customer engagemen t,
although these relationships require empirical test ing within the speciﬁc research context.

2.2.  Marketplace Marketing

Marketplace marketing concerns marketing activities  conducted through online marketplace
platforms, where businesses present products to consumers who can search, compare, evaluate, and purchase
available oﬀerings. Unlike social media environment s, which often emphasize communication and social
interaction, marketplace platforms are closely conn ected to product discovery and transaction-related
activities. Nevertheless, the two channels may comp lement each other because consumers can encounter
promotional content on social media before searchin g for or purchasing products through a marketplace.
Research on electronic commerce provides a theoreti cal basis for understanding consumer responses to
marketplace activities. Pavlou (2003) integrates tr ust and perceived risk into an acceptance model of
electronic commerce, demonstrating the importance o f consumer beliefs in online transaction decisions.
Gefen et al. (2003) also establish the relevance of  trust in online shopping environments. These studi es
indicate that consumers' willingness to engage in o nline transactions depends not only on product
information but also on their perceptions of the on line environment and the reliability of the transac tion
process. Social commerce research further demonstra tes that digital platforms can inﬂuence purchasing
decisions through social support, information exchange, and consumer interaction. Hajli (2015) examines the
relationships between social commerce constructs, t rust, and consumers' intention to buy. Hajli and Si ms
(2015) also discuss how social commerce can change the relationship between sellers and buyers by
empowering consumers to access and share information. Although these studies provide relevant theoretical
foundations, marketplace marketing should not be assumed to operate identically to social commerce. In this

2.3.  Purchase Intention

Purchase intention represents a consumer's stated l ikelihood or willingness to purchase a particular
product or service. It is an important outcome in consumer research because it captures a behavioral tendency
that may precede an actual transaction. However, in tention and behavior are conceptually distinct, and
purchase intention should not be interpreted as dir ect evidence of completed purchases. Ajzen's (1991)
Theory of Planned Behavior provides a foundational explanation of behavioral intention. The theory proposes
that intention is shaped by attitudes, subjective norms, and perceived behavioral control. In digital marketing
contexts, these factors may be associated with cons umers' evaluations of products, recommendations fro m
other users, and perceptions of their ability to co mplete online transactions. Although the theory was  not
developed speciﬁcally for marketplace marketing, it  oﬀers a useful basis for explaining why consumer
evaluations and social inﬂuences may contribute to purchase intention.
Empirical research on electronic word-of-mouth furt her demonstrates the relevance of online
information to purchasing decisions. Cheung et al. (2008) investigate the adoption of online opinions in
customer communities, while Erkan and Evans (2016) examine information adoption and purchase intention
in social media environments. Sokolova and Keﬁ (202 0) also show that inﬂuencer credibility and parasoc ial
interaction are relevant to purchase intentions in social media contexts. Taken together, these studies indicate
that consumers' purchase intentions may be shaped b y the information and relationships they encounter
online. Within the present framework, purchase inte ntion is treated as the dependent variable inﬂuence d by
social media marketing and marketplace marketing. T he proposed relationships reﬂect the expectation th at
useful marketing information, accessible product oﬀ erings, and relevant digital interactions may stren gthen
consumers' willingness to purchase. Nevertheless, t he magnitude and statistical signiﬁcance of these
relationships must be established empirically.

2.4.  Customer Engagement

Customer engagement is a multidimensional construct  describing consumers' cognitive, emotional,
and behavioral investment in interactions with a brand or ﬁrm. Brodie et al. (2011) conceptualize engagement
through interactive experiences and value co-creati on, distinguishing it from related concepts such as
participation and involvement. This perspective is particularly relevant to digital marketing because social
media and online marketplaces provide multiple oppo rtunities for consumers to interact with brands,
respond to content, and participate in brand-relate d activities. Hollebeek et al. (2014) develop and v alidate a
consumer brand engagement scale for social media contexts, identifying cognitive processing, aﬀection, and
activation as important dimensions. Their work supports the view that engagement extends beyond exposure
to promotional messages. Consumers may process bran d-related information, develop emotional responses,
and undertake actions such as commenting, sharing c ontent, or interacting with brand accounts. Vivek e t al.
(2012) similarly conceptualize customer engagement as a broader form of participation in organizationa l
oﬀerings and activities. Customer engagement is als o relevant to the customer experience across digita l
touchpoints. Lemon and Verhoef (2016) explain that customer experience develops throughout the customer
journey, involving multiple interactions across channels. Dessart et al. (2015) examine consumer engagement
in online brand communities and distinguish its cog nitive, emotional, and behavioral dimensions. These
perspectives suggest that digital marketing activit ies may contribute to engagement when they provide
relevant information and opportunities for meaningf ul interaction. However, engagement should not
automatically be equated with purchase intention, b ecause consumers can actively interact with a brand
without necessarily intending to buy its products.

2.5.  Relationships Among the Constructs

Social media marketing may inﬂuence customer engage ment by providing opportunities for
interaction, content sharing, and participation in brand-related activities. The strategic framework d eveloped
by Li et al. (2021) emphasizes the importance of in tegrating social media activities into marketing st rategies
to build consumer relationships. Evidence from Kim and Ko (2012) and Godey et al. (2016) also supports  the
relevance of social media marketing activities to c onsumer and brand-related outcomes. Accordingly, a
positive relationship between social media marketin g and customer engagement is theoretically plausibl e.
Marketplace marketing may also be associated with c ustomer engagement when platform features
encourage consumers to explore products, access inf ormation, and interact with sellers or other users.
However, because marketplace activities often empha size product search and transactions, the relations hip
may diﬀer from that of social media marketing. The eﬀect of marketplace marketing on customer engagement
therefore requires empirical examination rather tha n being inferred solely from the broader social med ia
literature.
The relationship between marketing activities and p urchase intention can be explained through the
availability of information, consumer evaluations, and the reduction of uncertainty during online deci sion-
making. Pavlou (2003), Gefen et al. (2003), and Haj li (2015) provide theoretical and empirical foundat ions for
understanding how trust, perceived risk, and social  commerce mechanisms relate to online purchasing
decisions. Cheung et al. (2008) and Erkan and Evans  (2016) further demonstrate the relevance of inform ation
adoption in online consumer behavior. These ﬁndings support testing the direct relationships of social media
marketing and marketplace marketing with purchase i ntention. Customer engagement may also be
considered a potential mechanism linking marketing activities to purchase intention. Engaged consumers
may pay greater attention to brand-related informat ion and participate more actively in brand interact ions,
which could be associated with stronger purchase intentions. Nevertheless, engagement does not necessarily
result in purchase intention, and purchase intentio n does not guarantee a transaction. The proposed
mediating role of customer engagement must therefor e be evaluated using a model in which the indirect
eﬀects are explicitly speciﬁed and tested.

This study employed a quantitative research approac h to examine the relationships between social

determine the sample size were not speciﬁed.

4.2.  Discussion

a.  The Relationship Between Social Media Marketing and  Customer Engagement
The results indicate that social media marketing has a positive and statistically signiﬁcant relationship
with customer engagement (β = 0.339, p < 0.001). This ﬁnding suggests that social media marketing activities
are associated with greater customer engagement in the context examined. Social media can provide
opportunities for consumers to interact with brands , respond to content, share information, and partic ipate
in brand-related conversations. These activities ar e consistent with the view that social media market ing has
evolved beyond one-way promotion toward more intera ctive and participatory communication (Appel et al. ,
2020; Dwivedi et al., 2021). The ﬁnding also corresponds with the conceptualization of customer engagement
as a multidimensional construct involving cognitive , emotional, and behavioral responses to brand-rela ted
interactions. Brodie et al. (2011) explain that customer engagement develops through interactive experiences
and the co-creation of value, while Hollebeek et al . (2014) operationalize consumer brand engagement i n
social media through cognitive processing, aﬀection, and activation. From this perspective, marketing content
may encourage engagement when it provides consumers  with meaningful information and opportunities to
interact with a brand.
The result is also broadly consistent with empirica l research on social media marketing. Godey et al.
(2016) demonstrate that social media marketing eﬀor ts are associated with brand equity and consumer
behavior, while Kim and Ko (2012) identify the rele vance of social media marketing activities to custo mer
responses in the luxury-brand context. However, these studies examine speciﬁc settings and constructs. Their
ﬁndings provide theoretical support for interpretin g the present relationship, but they do not establi sh that
every form of social media marketing will produce t he same eﬀect in all markets. The practical implica tion is
that businesses should evaluate social media market ing not only in terms of reach or posting frequency  but
also in terms of the quality of consumer interactio n. Relevant content, opportunities for dialogue, an d
consistent brand communication may help encourage engagement. Nevertheless, because the present study
does not separately estimate the eﬀects of individu al content characteristics, these mechanisms should  be
treated as plausible explanations rather than as di rectly tested causal pathways.

e.  The Negative Relationship Between Purchase Intentio n and Customer Engagement
An important and unexpected ﬁnding is the negative relationship between purchase intention and

customer engagement across consumer settings. Rathe r, it represents the relationship estimated within the
particular model and sample used in this study. One  possible theoretical consideration is that purchas e
intention and customer engagement represent diﬀeren t aspects of consumer responses. Purchase intention
concerns a consumer’s stated readiness or likelihoo d of buying, whereas customer engagement concerns
brand-related cognitive, emotional, and behavioral activity. Brodie et al. (2011) and Hollebeek et al.  (2014)

intention to complete a transaction. A consumer may therefore intend to purchase a product without actively
interacting with a brand, while another consumer may engage extensively with brand content without having
an immediate intention to buy.
Customer engagement can also vary across stages of the customer journey. Lemon and Verhoef
(2016) explain that customer experience develops th rough multiple touchpoints and across pre-purchase,
purchase, and post-purchase stages. Verhoef et al. (2010) similarly discuss the importance of managing  the

is negative in the present study. The negative coeﬃ cient therefore requires additional scrutiny. First , the
researchers should verify whether the items measuri ng customer engagement and purchase intention were
coded and interpreted consistently. Second, the mea surement model should be reviewed to ensure that th e
constructs are empirically distinct. Third, the res earchers should check the speciﬁcation and directio n of the
structural paths, particularly because the conceptu al framework describes customer engagement as a
mediator while the reported indirect paths place pu rchase intention between marketing and customer
engagement. The ﬁnding may also reﬂect unobserved v ariables or sample-speciﬁc characteristics. For
example, consumers may engage with brands for infor mation, entertainment, or social interaction withou t
intending to purchase immediately. Yet these explan ations cannot be conﬁrmed using the current results
alone. Accordingly, the negative relationship should be reported transparently as an unexpected ﬁnding that
requires replication rather than presented as a uni versal consumer behavior pattern.

consumer engagement and purchase intention more com prehensively.
The reported SRMR value is 0.083, slightly above th e commonly used reference value of 0.08. This
diﬀerence is small, but it warrants cautious interp retation. The SRMR should be considered alongside o ther
model-assessment evidence rather than used in isola tion to declare the model acceptable or unacceptabl e.
Moreover, Henseler et al. (2015) discuss the use and limitations of model-ﬁt assessment in PLS path modeling,

ﬁndings indicate that social media marketing and ma rketplace marketing have positive and statistically

nonsigniﬁcant indirect eﬀects, the limited explanat ory power, and the inconsistency in the speciﬁed
mediation direction require careful interpretation.  The main contribution of the ﬁndings is therefore not that
all proposed relationships are supported, but that the results distinguish the direct associations fro m the
unsupported indirect pathways and identify importan t issues for further theoretical and methodological
examination.

V.  Conclusion

This study examined the relationships between socia l media marketing, marketplace marketing,
purchase intention, and customer engagement. The ﬁndings indicate that social media marketing is positively

intention has a negative and signiﬁcant relationshi p with customer engagement (β = −0.224, p = .035).
Furthermore, neither the indirect eﬀect of social m edia marketing nor that of marketplace marketing on
customer engagement through purchase intention is s tatistically signiﬁcant. These ﬁndings highlight th e

The ﬁndings suggest that businesses should integrat e social media marketing and marketplace
marketing while recognizing that the two channels m ay serve diﬀerent functions in the consumer journey .

purchase intention and customer engagement suggests  that an intention to purchase should not
