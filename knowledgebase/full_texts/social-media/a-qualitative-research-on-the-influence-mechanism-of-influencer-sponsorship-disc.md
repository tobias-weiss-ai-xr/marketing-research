Abstract.The prevalence of hidden grass -root marketing advertisements posted by social -media
influencers has prompted platforms and regulators to mandate sponsorship disclosure labels for
sponsored content. Nevertheless, existing quantitative empirical studies have yielded completely
contradictory conclusions regarding the communication effe cts of such disclosures. Traditional
questionnaire-based quantitative research presupposes causal relationships among variables and
struggles to restore consumers’ genuine, multi -layered native psychological experiences when
confronted with promotional labels; accordingly, there is a lack of bottom-up qualitative explorations
in this research field. This study conducted one -on-one online semi -structured interviews with 40
social-media users meeting predefined screening criteria. Among them, the transcripts of the first 32
interviews served as core materials for coding analysis, and the remaining eight transcripts were
used to test theoretical saturation. Adopting the classic groun ded-theory approach, this paper
completed three-stage coding including open coding, axial coding and selective coding. The findings
indicate that influencer sponsorship disclosure simultaneously triggers two parallel but opposite
psychological processing logics for consumers. On the one hand, drawing on  their accumulated
persuasion knowledge, audiences quickly identify the commercial profit -seeking motives behind
influencers’ posts, perceive that their consumption decisions are being deliberately manipulated, and
consequently generate psychological reactance, which reduces their acceptance and repurchase
intentions toward the corresponding br and. On the other hand, explicit and clear sponsorship
disclosure acts as transparent self -disclosure behaviour, which significantly improves audiences’
perceived authenticity of influencers and shortens parasocial -emotional bonds between followers
and bloggers, thereby positively fostering consumers’ favourable attitudes toward promoted brands.
Furthermore, the fit between an influencer and the promoted product (in terms of personal persona
and product category) functions as a core situational constraint that moderates the strength of the
two psychological paths. Under high-fit conditions, perceived manipulation induced by disclosure is
greatly weakened, and the positive gain effect brought by perceived authenticity is further amplified.
In low-fit cross-border promotion scenarios, audiences are more likely to detect marketin g tactics,
and the trust -enhancing effect of transparent disclosure almost disappears. From a qualitative
perspective, this study reconciles conflicting conclusions from prior quantitative research,
compensates for the deficiency of existing literature that over-emphasises quantitative analysis, and
provides solid qualitative empirical evidence for corporate influencer marketing placement and
compliant advertising governance on social-media platforms.
Keywords: Influencer marketing , Sponsorship disclosure, Hidden advertising, Grounded theory,
Psychological reactance.
1. Introduction
Driven by the rapid development of new -media social platforms such as Douyin, Xiaohongshu
and Kuaishou, hidden influencer grass-root marketing advertisements built upon bloggers’ personal
personas and parasocial relationships with followers have become a dominant brand-communication
mode. This type of promotional content embeds commodity information into daily-life-themed texts
and short-video posts while concealing the commercial nature of paid brand collaborations, blurring
the boundary between organic user content and commercial advertisements and violating consumers’
right to know. Consequently, domestic and international advertising r egulators and social -media
platforms have successively introduced regulatory rules requiring all paid grass -root marketing

content to carry standard disclosure labels such as #Ad and #Sponsored to clearly inform audiences
of the commercial nature of posts. However, academic literature presents obvious divergences over
the actual communication effects of sponsorship disclosure. Some scholars have conducted
quantitative experiments based on psycho logical-reactance theory, arguing that disclosure labels
explicitly expose marketing -inducement purposes, activate consumers’ persuasion -knowledge
reservoirs, trigger audiences’ resistant psychology and weaken brand favourability . Other scholars
adopt an interpersonal-transparency perspective and propose that voluntarily disclosing commercial
collaborations can demonstrate influencers’ candid personalities and improve audience trust and
brand evaluations. Most existing empirical s tudies adopt questionnaire -driven quantitative designs
that presuppose causal, mediating and moderating relationships between variables, and cannot capture
consumers’ subtle and dynamic real-world psychological changes. Moreover, prior literature mostly
tests either the positive or negative effects of disclosure in isolation, lacking integrated analyses of
psychological mechanisms, and pays insufficient attention to the critical situational co nstraint of
influencer-product fit.
Given the above research gaps, this paper adopts grounded theory as an exploratory qualitative
method, without setting pre -established research hypotheses, and constructs concepts and theories
bottom-up purely from original interview data. Two core research questions are addressed: first, what
differentiated inner feelings are evoked among consumers by influencer sponsorship-disclosure labels,
and how do such psychological perceptions shape consumers’ brand-purchase preferences; second,
how does the fit between influencers and promoted products constrain and alter the above-mentioned
psychological processes. This study builds a holistic qualitative interpretive framework based on
native interview data to resolve contradictory findings in previous research and deliver practical
references for corporate influencer marketing and platform advertising governance.
2. Literature Review and Theoretical Foundations
A review of domestic and international literature on influencer hidden advertising, sponsorship
disclosure and consumer perceptions reveals a broad consensus on the definition of influencer hidden
advertising: influencers embed products into daily -life posts on social -media accounts without
marking paid collaborations, featuring two core attributes of concealment and content integration.
Such advertising leverages parasocial interaction to lower audiences’ advertising defences, yet its
concealment of commercial partnerships violates regulatory requirements for advertising
transparency [1]. Existing research on sponsorship disclosure mostly focuses on quantitative effect
tests and defines sponsorship disclosure as compliant behaviour in which bloggers proactively mark
commercial-cooperation hashtags or text labels as required by regulators [2]. Prior empirical studies
separately examine two consumer -psychological constructs: perceived manipulation and perceived
authenticity [3]. Perceived manipulation refers to consumers’ subjective perception that marketing
agents deliberately induce their consumption decisions [4], whereas perceived authenticity represents
audiences’ comprehensive judgement regarding whether bloggers’ sharing motives and personal
dispositions are candid  [5]. Influencer-product fit describes the alignment between an influencer’s
specialised domain and personal persona and the positioning and usage scenarios of a brand’s product,
constituting an important situational factor for advertising persuasion [6].
This study takes psychological -reactance theory as its core theoretical pillar and adopts
persuasion-knowledge theory for theoretical dialogue [7]. Psychological-reactance theory holds that
individuals intrinsically value their right to autonomous choice and develop negative resistant
emotions once they perceive their decisions are deliberately interfered with or induced by external
forces [8], which explains the negative psychological path triggered by disclosure labels.
Persuasion-knowledge theory posits that consumers accumulate sophisticated marketing-recognition
cognition through long-term exposure to diverse advertisements. When confronted with the explicit
commercial cue of sponsorship disclosure, consumers may form two distinct modes of information
processing: interpreting disclosure either as manipulative sales tactics or as evidenc e of an

negative effects of disclosure instead of constructing an integrated interpretive framework for dual

intrapersonal consumer traits while overlooking the critical external constraint of influencer-product
fit[6]. Accordingly, drawing on the two core theories, this paper adopts the grounded -theory
qualitative approach to fill multiple gaps in extant research.
3. Research Methodology and Data Collection
This research employs classic grounded theory, whose core principle is to suspend all
pre-formulated research hypotheses. Starting purely from primary interview transcripts, the study
implements open coding, axial coding and selective coding in sequence to extract basic concepts,
generalise categories, sort out logical relationships between cat egories and construct a holistic
theoretical interpretive framework. Nvivo 12 qualitative -analysis software is utilised  for text
decomposition, concept classification and coding documentation. Clear inclusion criteria were set for
interviewees: participants aged 18 -50 years who frequently use Douyin, Xiaohongshu or Kuaishou,
regularly follow consumption-oriented influencers covering beauty, apparel, food and digital-product
sectors, and have encountered #Ad and #Sponsored disclosure labels and can distinguish posts with
and without commercial markers. Participants covering students, young professionals and
middle-aged groups were recruited to ensure sample heterogeneity. In total, 40 one -on-one online
semi-structured voice interviews were completed. Open -ended questioning was strictly followed
throughout the interviews to avoid leading prompts, and follow -up questions were used to explore
deep-seated psychological motivations behind respondents’ answers. Each interview lasted
40-55 minutes. Prior to recording, participants were informed that data would be used solely for
academic coursework, would remain anonymous and that audio recordings would only be transcribed
into written text; recording commenced only after obtaining consent. All audio files were transcribed
within 24 hours after each interview; personally identifiable information such as account names and
real-world names was removed, and respondents were coded sequentially from S1 to S40. Theoretical
saturation was achieved after interviewing the 32nd participant, as no new concepts or categories
emerged from subsequent interview content. The transcripts of the  remaining eight interviews were
used for secondary theoretical -saturation verification. The full semi -structured interview outline is
provided in the appendix. All qualitative -analysis materials originate exclusively from primary
interview transcripts, without supplementary data such as online public comments or secondary
textual sources.
4. Three-stage Coding Procedure and Research Findings
The full three-stage coding procedure was carried out on the 40 interview transcripts. First, open
coding was performed: sentence-by-sentence decomposition of all interview content was conducted
to extract native oral statements from respondents; semantically redundant and substantively
meaningless sentences were eliminated, yielding 43 basic native concepts. Items were clustered
according to similarities in sentiment, behaviour  and events, generating 29 initial categories.
Representative native statements for negative perception include “When I see a sponsorship label, I
feel the blogger only wants commission and tries to trick me; I feel resistant and do not want to buy”.
Representative statements for positive perception include “Marking advertisements voluntarily is
honest; there is no concealment, making me more inclined to trust recommendations”. Statements

Second, axial coding was implemented. The 29 initial categories were further merged and
summarised to derive five core main categories: influencer sponsorship disclosure (external
commercial stimulus cue), perceived manipulation and psychological reactance  (negative
psychological-processing procedure), perceived authenticity and parasocial bonding (positive
psychological-processing procedure), influencer -product fit (situational -constraint condition), and
consumers’ brand -purchase preference (attitudinal -behavioural outcome). Logical relationships
among categories were identified: sponsorship disclosure acts as an external  informational stimulus
that simultaneously activates two independent and parallel psychological -processing pathways.
Influencer-product fit moderates the strength of both pathways, and the two types of psychological
perceptions jointly shape consumers’ brand preferences in opposite directions.
Third, selective coding was conducted. “Dual consumer -perception effects of influencer
sponsorship disclosure” was identified as the core category that unifies all main categories, and a
complete theoretical storyline was constructed: when influencers attach sponsorship-disclosure labels
to grass -root-marketing posts, audiences develop two diametrically opposed inner interpretations
based on their persuasion knowledge. On the negative path, disclosure exposes bloggers’
profit-driven motives, leading consumers to perceive deliberate manipulation of their consumption
choices and triggering psychological reactance, which reduces brand acceptance and repurchase
intentions. On the positive path, voluntary transparent disclosure mani fests influencers’ candid
dispositions, enhances audiences’ perceived authenticity and strengthens parasocial-emotional bonds
between followers and bloggers, thereby improving consumers’ brand attitudes. Influencer -product
fit alters the magnitude of both pathways: under high -fit conditions, perceived manipulation is
substantially weakened, and the positive effect of perceived authenticity is enhanced; under low -fit
cross-border-promotion conditions, audiences are mo re likely to detect marketing tactics, and the
trust-building benefit brought by transparent disclosure nearly disappears.
The remaining eight saturation-check interview transcripts were recoded using the full three-stage
coding workflow; no new concepts, categories or logical relationships emerged, confirming the
stability and reliability of the theoretically derived framework. Two core conclusions are drawn: first,
sponsorship disclosure simultaneously activates two  parallel psychological -processing pathways
exerting opposite effects on consumers’ brand preferences; second, influencer-product fit constitutes
a key situational constraint that bidirectionally moderates the magnitude of negative and positive
psychological effects triggered by sponsorship disclosure.
5. Theoretical Contributions and Practical Implications
This study makes three key theoretical contributions. First, it supplements existing literature from
a methodological perspective. Most empirical studies on sponsorship disclosure adopt quantitative
questionnaires; this paper applies grounded -theory qualitative analysis and builds a theoretical
framework bottom-up from consumers’ native interview experiences, enriching qualitative empirical
materials in this research domain and remedying the limitation of quantitative research that cannot
capture subtle psychological processes. Second, it advances mechanism -oriented research by
integrating conflicting findings from prior studies and identifying two parallel opposite
psychological-processing pathways triggered by disclosure, constructing a holistic dual -perception
interpretive framework that overcomes the limitations of one -sided unidirectional research
perspectives. Third, it expands the boundary of situational research by incorpor ating
influencer-product fit as a critical constraining condition and extending the application scope of
psychological-reactance theory and persuasion -knowledge theory to the social -media-influencer
hidden-advertising context. Corresponding practical implications can be derived for brands,
social-media platforms and industry regulators. For brands: priority should be given to collaborating
with vertical influencers whose personal personas are highly aligned with product positioning to

mandatory-disclosure rules, regulators can disseminate practical insights concerning the mitigating
role of influencer -product fit to market participants while enforcing compliance requirements,
balancing consumer-rights-protection objectives with legitimate corporate-marketing demands.
6. Research Limitations and Future Research Directions
This study has three notable limitations. First, the sample exhibits structural bias: interviewees are
dominated by young students and working-age participants aged 18-30 years, while middle-aged and
elderly respondents are under -represented; accordingly, the explanatory power of the theoretical
framework for middle -aged and elderly consumer groups requires further validation. Second,
qualitative-data sources are singular: only online voice -based semi-structured interviews were used
to collect primary data, and triangulation with platform -public comments or offline focus -group
interviews was not performed, limiting data diversity. Third, the study does not differentiate effects
across platform types or product categories, and does not compare the magnitude of dual -sided
disclosure effects for Xiaohongshu text -based posts versus short -video content on Douyin , or for
product sectors including beauty, digital goods and food. Based on these limitations, three avenues
for future research are proposed. First, mixed -methods research can be conducted: scenario -based
questionnaires and experiments can be designed on the basis of this qualitative theoretical framework
to quantitatively test the magnitude of effects for the dual -perception pathways and fit -related
constraints. Second, comparative qualitative interviews across segmented scenarios can be
implemented to contrast consumer perceptions of sponsorship disclosure under different platforms
and product categories. Third, additional intrapersonal situa tional variables such as consumers’
persuasion-knowledge level and parasocial -attachment intensity can be incorporated for further
qualitative exploration to refine the complete set of theoretical boundary conditions.
