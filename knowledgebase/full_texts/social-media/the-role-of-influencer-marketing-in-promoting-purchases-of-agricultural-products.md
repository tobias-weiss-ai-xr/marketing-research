# The role of influencer marketing in promoting purchases of agricultural products in the direct-to-consumer (D2C) model

A BSTRACT
The Direct-to-Consumer (D2C) model is reshaping agricultural marketing in India by allowing farmers,
agri-businesses and Farmer Producer Organisations (FPOs) to reach consumers directly through digital
and social media channels. Within this environment, influencer marketing has emerged as an important
promotional tool, but its specific contribution to agricultural D2C outcomes remains under-researched.
This study examines the role of influencer marketing in creating awareness of agricultural products,
identifies the influencer types and social media platforms preferred by consumers, and evaluates the in-
fluence of influencer credibility and content quality on consumer trust and purchase intention. A de-
scriptive and analytical research design was adopted, and primary data were collected through a struc-
tured Google Form questionnaire from 120 farmers selected by convenience sampling. Data were ana-
lysed using percentage analysis, mean score analysis, correlation analysis and multiple regression
analysis. The results show that influencer posts or videos were the first source of awareness for 50.83
per cent of respondents, and 67.50 per cent agreed that influencer marketing increased their awareness
of agricultural D2C brands. Micro-influencers were the most trusted influencer type (50.83%), while
agricultural experts/scientists were the most influential category for purchase decisions (35.00%).
Regression analysis showed that influencer credibility and content quality jointly explained 83.09 per
cent of the variation in consumer trust (𝑅2 = 0.8309, p < 0.01), with influencer credibility the stronger
predictor.
Influencer marketing also explained 75.39 per cent of the variation in consumer purchase intention (
𝑅2 = 0.7539, p < 0.01). The study concludes that influencer marketing is a statistically significant driver
of awareness, trust and purchase intention in agricultural D2C markets, and recommends that agricul-
tural businesses build long-term partnerships with credible micro-influencers and agricultural experts
using informative, authentic content on platforms such as Instagram and YouTube.
Keywords: Influencer Marketing, Direct-to-Consumer (D2C), Agricultural Products, Consumer Trust,

Introduction
Agriculture remains a cornerstone of the Indian economy, contributing significantly to employment genera-
tion, rural livelihoods and national income. The agricultural marketing system has traditionally relied on
physical markets and layers of intermediaries, but the growth of digital technologies and social media has
created new opportunities for producers and agri-businesses to communicate directly with consumers.
Platforms such as YouTube, Instagram, WhatsApp and Facebook now enable agricultural stakeholders to
demonstrate products, share information and build relationships with consumers beyond geographical
boundaries.
The Direct-to-Consumer (D2C) model has strengthened this shift by allowing agricultural and food busi-
nesses to sell directly to consumers rather than depending entirely on conventional distribution channels.
Within this digital marketing environment, influencer marketing has emerged as an important promotional
mechanism. Farmers, agricultural experts, food creators and lifestyle creators can share product information,
demonstrations, reviews and recommendations that shape consumer awareness, trust and purchase intention.
Because agricultural products often require product-specific and technical knowledge before a purchase de-
cision is made, the credibility of the person delivering that information becomes particularly important.
Although social media's role in agricultural information dissemination and general influencer-marketing re-
search on consumer trust and purchase intention have both been studied separately, there remains limited re-
search that connects influencer marketing specifically with agricultural products sold through the D2C
model. This creates a research gap at the intersection of agricultural digital marketing and influencer-market-
ing literature, which the present study seeks to address.

Objectives of the study
The present study was undertaken with the following objectives:
1. To study the role of influencer marketing in creating awareness
of agricultural products in Direct-to-Consumer (D2C) models.
2. To study the different types of influencers and platforms in pro-
moting agricultural products.
3. To examine the impact of influencer credibility and content
quality on consumer trust towards agricultural products.
4. To evaluate the effect of influencer marketing on consumer pur-
chase intention in D2C agricultural markets.
Hypotheses
H0: Influencer credibility and content quality have no significant in-
fluence on consumer trust. H1: Influencer credibility and content
quality have a significant influence on consumer trust. H0: Influencer
marketing has no significant effect on consumer purchase intention in
D2C agricultural markets.
H1: Influencer marketing has a significant effect on consumer pur-
chase intention in D2C agricultural markets.
Research methodology
The study adopted a descriptive and analytical research design to un-
derstand consumer perceptions of influencer marketing and to exam-
ine statistical relationships among the study variables. Primary data
were collected through a structured questionnaire administered via
Google Forms to 120 farmers, selected using convenience sampling.
The questionnaire used categorical, multiple-response and five-point
Likert-scale items (1 = Strongly Disagree to 5 = Strongly Agree) to
capture respondent demographics, awareness of D2C agricultural
brands, influencer and platform preferences, influencer credibility,
content quality, consumer trust and purchase intention. Secondary
data were drawn from research papers, journals, reports and websites
relevant to influencer marketing, social media marketing and agricul-
tural digital marketing.
The collected data were analysed using percentage analysis, mean
score analysis, correlation analysis and multiple regression analysis.
Percentage analysis summarised categorical and demographic re-
sponses; mean score analysis measured average perception levels on
Likert-scale items; correlation analysis examined the direction and
strength of association among variables; and multiple regression
analysis tested the two hypotheses by evaluating the influence of in-
fluencer credibility and content quality on consumer trust, and the ef-
fect of influencer marketing on consumer purchase intention.
Results and Discussion
The findings are presented under five sections: respondent profile, the
role of influencer marketing in creating awareness, preferred influ-
encer types and platforms, the influence of influencer credibility and
content quality on consumer trust, and the effect of influencer market-
ing on consumer purchase intention.

Micro-influencers were the most trusted influencer type (50.83%),
ahead of nano- and macro-influencers (23.33% each), while celebrity
or mega influencers were the least trusted (2.50%), suggesting that re-

Findings
Influencer posts/videos were the leading first source of awareness of
D2C agricultural brands (50.83%), and 67.50 per cent of respondents
agreed that influencer marketing increased their awareness; 80.00 per
cent could recall a brand promoted by an influencer. Educational con-
tent was the most effective format for building awareness (34.17%),
and 74.17 per cent found influencer content more attention-grabbing
than traditional advertisements. Micro-influencers were the most
trusted influencer type (50.83%), while agricultural experts/scientists
were the most influential category for purchase decisions (35.00%);
73.33 per cent of respondents said their influencer preference varies
by product. Instagram (30.00%) and YouTube (29.17%) were the
most effective platforms for agricultural influencer promotion.
Regression analysis confirmed that influencer credibility and content
quality jointly and significantly explain 83.09 per cent of the variation
in consumer trust, with credibility the stronger predictor, and that in-
fluencer marketing significantly explains 75.39 per cent of the varia-
tion in consumer purchase intention. Both hypotheses tested in the
study were supported at the 1 per cent level of significance.
Conclusion
The study concludes that influencer marketing plays a statistically
significant and practically important role in agricultural D2C market-
ing. It builds awareness by serving as consumers' primary point of
discovery for D2C agricultural brands, and it generates strong brand
recall. There is no single “best” influencer type or platform: micro-in-
fluencers are the most trusted for relatability and authenticity, while
agricultural experts and scientists carry the greatest weight in pur-
chase-related decisions, and influencer suitability varies with the
product being promoted. Influencer credibility and content quality are
strong, significant drivers of consumer trust, with credibility the more
influential of the two, while influencer marketing as a whole is a
strong, significant driver of purchase intention. Taken together, these
results
indicate that influencer marketing in the agricultural D2C context
works through a chain of awareness, credibility-driven trust and pur-
chase intention rather than through visibility alone. D2C agricultural
businesses, farmers and Farmer Producer Organisations are therefore
advised to build long-term partnerships with credible micro-influ-
encers and agricultural experts, prioritise informative and authentic
content — including educational posts, testimonials and genuine
product demonstrations — over purely promotional messaging, main-
tain transparency in paid partnerships, and concentrate promotional
efforts on Instagram and YouTube while linking influencer campaigns
to simple, convenient D2C purchasing options to convert awareness
and trust into actual sales.
