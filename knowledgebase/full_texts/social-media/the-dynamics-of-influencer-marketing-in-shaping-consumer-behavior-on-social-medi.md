ABSTRACT
The rapid expansion of social media has transformed influencer marketing into a relationship -
oriented form of digital communication that can shape consumer perceptions and decisions. This
study aims to examine how parasocial interaction influences marketing effectiveness through
consumer trust and purchase intention, how influencer -generated content contributes to brand
polarization and consumer loyalty, and what factors determine effectiveness in consumer decision -
making. A Systematic Literature Review (SLR) following PRISMA guidelines was conducted using
Scopus and supplementary searches. From 82 initially identified articles, screening, retrieval, and
eligibility assessment resulted in 20 articles for thematic synthesis. The findings show that parasocial
interaction is a central mechanism linking influencer communication with trust, engagement, and
purchase intention. Influencer-generated content can simultaneously strengthen loyalty and generate
resistance because consumers interpret messages according to their values, identities, experiences, and
perceptions of influencers. Influencer –brand congruence, credibility, content quality, authenticity,
engagement, transparency, and perceived value also determine marketing effectiveness. The study
concludes that influencer marketing effectiveness depends increasingly on relational credibility and
meaningful audience interaction rather than visibility alone. These findings provide implications for
marketers seeking to develop authentic, transparent, and strategically aligned influencer campaigns.

Keywords: Brand Polarization, Consumer Behavior, Influencer Marketing, Parasocial Interaction.

INTRODUCTION
The rapid development of social media in recent years has significantly transformed
the marketing landscape, particularly in the way companies communicate with
consumers. Digital platforms no longer function merely as channels for information
sharing but h ave evolved into interactive spaces that enable more personalized
relationships between brands and audiences to emerge (Chen et al., 2024). In this context,
influencer marketing has emerged as a strategic approach that leverages individuals with
social influence to deliver brand messages in a more persuasive and relevant manner to
target markets ( Migkos et al., 2025). Influencers no longer serve solely as information
disseminators; they also function as opinion leaders capable of shaping consumer
perceptions, attitudes, and decision -making within the digital ecosystem (Zaki et al.,
2025).
The influencer marketing industry has experienced consistent growth alongside
increasing internet access and social media usage worldwide. The global influencer
marketing industry was valued at USD 13.8 billion in 2021 and is projected to continue
expanding as influencer -based communication becomes increasingly integrated into
digital marketing strategies (Hoque et al., 2025). This growth is supported by the ability
of influencers to reach targeted audiences and generate engagement compared with
conventional marketing approaches (Tartaraj et al., 2024). In Indonesia, the widespread

adoption of social media has positioned influencers as important actors in shaping

A major appeal of influencer marketing lies in its ability to create a perceived sense of
closeness between influencers and followers. This relationship is commonly
conceptualized as parasocial interaction, in which audiences perceive emotional closeness
and attachment to influencers despite the absence of direct interpersonal interaction (Wei
et al., 2022; Aldlimi et al., 2025 ). Recent research by Sheng et al. (2025) further
emphasizes that parasocial interaction can develop into stronger parasocial relationships
that influence consumer responses over time. Such perceived closeness allows influencer-
delivered messages to be received with greater trust and can strengthe n consumer
engagement and purchase intention (Garg & Bakshi, 2024). Other characteristics,
including credibility, attractiveness, and value similarity, can further reinforce these
responses (Rahman & Akter, 2025; Xu & Deng, 2026).
Despite these advantages, influencer marketing does not always generate uniform
consumer responses. Influencer -generated content may elicit different interpretations
depending on individual experiences, preferences, values, and perceptions. This condition
can contribute to brand polarization, whereby consumers develop sharply divergent
attitudes toward the same brand. Within the open and interactive environment of social
media, positive opinions can be reinforced among supportive audiences, while negative
perceptions can spread rapidly and generate resistance toward the promoted brand
(Nadia, 2026). Thus, influencer-generated content may contribute not only to consumer
loyalty but also to divergent brand evaluations and responses.
Previous studies by Ao et al. (2023) and Gu and Duan (2024) have demonstrated the
significant role of influencer marketing in shaping consumer behavior through trust,
engagement, and purchase intention. Influencer credibility, content quality, authenticity,
and influencer –brand congruence have also emerged as impor tant determinants of
campaign effectiveness ( Khalfallah & Keller, 2025). Studies have further examined
parasocial relationships, virtual influencers, live -streaming, and consumer engagement
across different digital contexts (Diao et al., 2025; Tseng & Ou, 2025). However, much of
the existing literature remains  concentrated on direct relationships between specific
variables, leaving insufficient understanding of how parasocial interaction, influencer -
generated content, brand polarization, and multiple effectiveness determinants
collectively explain consumer resp onses in social media environments. This gap is
particularly relevant in the Indonesian context, where influencer marketing continues to
expand across diverse consumer segments and digital platforms.
Therefore, this study offers a more integrated perspective by examining three
interconnected dimensions of influencer marketing effectiveness . The role of parasocial
interaction in building consumer trust and purchase intention, the role of influencer -
generated content in triggering brand polarization and shaping consumer loyalty, and the
factors that determine influencer marketing effectiveness i n consumer decision -making.
Therefore, this study aims to examine how parasocial interaction influences influencer
marketing effectiveness through consumer trust and purchase intention, how influencer -
generated content triggers brand polarization and shapes consumer loyalty on socia l
media, and what factors determine influencer marketing effectiveness in influencing
consumer decision -making. The study contributes to the literature by integrating
relational, content -based, and strategic perspectives to provide a more comprehensive
understanding of influencer marketing effectiveness in social media contexts.

Influencer Marketing
Influencer marketing is a digital marketing strategy in which individuals with
established credibility, expertise, or social influence use social media content to
communicate brand -related messages to followers. Unlike traditional advertising,
influencer marketing relies on perceived authenticity, relatability, and ongoing interaction

consumer decision-making, from product awareness and evaluation to purchase intention
and post-purchase engagement. Research by Khalfallah and Keller (2025) and Migkos et
al. (2025) also indicates that authenticity, transparency, and relational value are
increasingly important for sustaining consumer trust and engagement. These
characteristics distinguish influencer marketing from one -way promotional
communication and e stablish influencers as social actors who can shape consumer
perceptions and behaviors. Thus, understanding influencer marketing requires attention
not only to promotional outcomes but also to the psychological and social mechanisms
underlying consumer responses.

Parasocial Interaction
Parasocial interaction describes a perceived interpersonal connection in which
audiences experience familiarity, intimacy, or emotional closeness with media figures
despite the absence of direct reciprocal relationships. In social media environments, this
concept has become particularly relevant because influencers continuously share personal
experiences, opinions, routines, and everyday activities with followers. Such repeated
exposure can create a perception that followers know influencers personally, the reby
strengthening psychological closeness and identification (Sijabat et al., 2022; Sheng et al.,
2025). Unlike conventional advertising relationships, parasocial interaction develops
through ongoing communication that combines personal storytelling, informal language,
visual self -disclosure, and interactive features. Garg and Bakshi (2 024) show that
parasocial interaction is closely associated with trust and purchase intention in influencer
marketing, indicating that perceived relational closeness can enhance the persuasive
influence of influencer recommendations. Parasocial interaction provides an important
mechanism through which influencer marketing can affect consumer responses.
The influence of parasocial interaction extends beyond perceived closeness because it
can shape how consumers evaluate information and respond to promoted products.
When followers perceive influencers as trustworthy, relatable, and authentic, parasocial
bonds can increase engagement and reduce psychological distance between the influencer
and audience. This relationship may strengthen consumers ’ willingness to accept
recommendations and develop favorable behavioral intentions (Garg & Bakshi, 2024).
Recent research by Zhang et al. (2025) and Omeish et al. (2025) further suggests that
parasocial relationships remain relevant in emerging forms of influencer marketing,
including virtual influencers, where perceived credibility, attractiveness, and social
presence can contribute to consumer responses. The literature t herefore positions
parasocial interaction as a central psychological mechanism linking influencer-generated
communication with consumer trust and purchase intention. However, its effectiveness
depends on the perceived authenticity and credibility of the in fluencer, suggesting that

Brand Polarization
Brand polarization refers to sharply divided consumer evaluations in which audience
groups express strong support for or opposition to a brand. Social media can intensify this
process because consumers can immediately express opinions, interact with others , and
redistribute brand -related content. Based on Ao et al. (2023) and Chen et al. (2024),
influencer-generated content contributes to polarization because influencers
communicate with diverse audiences whose responses depend on their values, identities,
prior experiences, and perceptions of the influencer. Influencer credibility and perceived
value can shape consumer evaluations, but these effects may differ across audience
groups. When an influencer’s identity strongly aligns with a brand, communication may
strengthen positive evaluations and loyalty, whereas perceived inconsistency can generate
negative reactions. The relational characteristics of influencer marketing may therefore
create both favorable and unfavorable consumer responses.
Brand polarization can become more pronounced when influencer-generated content
involves controversial issues, emotional topics, or distinctive social identities. Interactive
features such as comments, reposts, and user-generated content allow divergent reactions
to spread rapidly, potentially strengthening consumer loyalty among audiences who
identify with an influencer while encouraging rejection among those who perceive a
mismatch with their values. Authenticity and transparency are particularly importan t
because consumers may respond negatively when promotional communication is
perceived as manipulative or inconsistent (Garg & Bakshi, 2024; Khalfallah & Keller,
2025). Koivunen et al. (2025) and Stocchi and Harrigan (2026) found that influencer -
generated narratives can also influence brand perceptions during crises, while emerging
practices such as de -influencing may challenge established consumption preferences.
Thus, brand polarization illustrates how influencer marketing can simultaneously
strengthen loyalty and generate negative consumer responses in fragmented social media
environments.

RESEARCH METHODS
This study employs a Systematic Literature Review (SLR) approach to
comprehensively analyze the literature on the dynamics of influencer marketing in
shaping consumer behavior on social media. This method was selected because it enables
researchers to systematically identify, evaluate, and synthesize findings from prior studies,
thereby generating a deeper conceptual understanding of the phenomenon under
investigation. The implementation of the SLR in this study follows the Preferred
Reporting Items for Sys tematic Reviews and Meta -Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines to
ensure that the identification, screening, and selection processes are conducted in a
structured, transparent, and replicable manner.
The literature search was conducted through the Scopus database using the keyword
phrase “Influencer Marketing Consumer.” The initial identification process yielded 82
articles. A preliminary screening was then performed based on predefined inclusion and
exclusion criteria, including publication years ranging from 2020 to 2026, topic relevance
to the research focus, availability of abstracts and full-text articles, and journal publication
quality. At this stage, 10 articles were excluded, consisting of 1 ar ticle published outside
the specified time range and 9 articles that did not meet the required journal quality
standards based on tier classification. Consequently, 72 articles proceeded to the screening
stage.
The screening stage involved evaluating article titles, abstracts, and substantive
relevance to the research focus. As a result, 44 articles were excluded due to insufficient
relevance to the study topic. Subsequently, 28 articles entered the retrieval stage; however,
only 10 articles were successfully obtained in full -text format and met the criteria for

RESULTS
Parasocial Interaction and Influencer Marketing Effectiveness
The analysis of the 20 selected articles indicates that the dynamics of influencer
marketing on social media are shaped by three primary themes: parasocial interaction,
brand polarization, and the determinants of influencer marketing effectiveness. These
themes consistently emerged across the reviewed studies and collectively explain how
influencers shape consumer perceptions, engagement, and purchasing behavior within
digital environments. Influencer marketing has therefore evolved beyond promotional
communication into a social and psychological mechanism that influences how
consumers interpret information, build trust, and develop emotional connections with
brands (Gu & Duan, 2024; Joshi et al., 2025; Sheng et al., 2025).
Across the reviewed literature, parasocial interaction appeared as the most dominant
mechanism underlying influencer marketing effectiveness. Consumers often perceive
influencers not merely as content creators but as relatable social figures whose opinions
and recommendations resemble advice from peers or close acquaintances (Garg & Bakshi,
2024; Wang et al., 2025 ). Unlike traditional advertising, influencer -generated content
creates a more personal communication atmosphere through storytelling, daily -life
exposure, and interactive engagement with followers. This perceived intimacy can
strengthen emotional attachment and increase consumers’ willingness to trust influencer
recommendations and perceive them as credible. The importance of this relational
mechanism is also supported by research linking parasocial relationships with consumer
engagement and behavioral responses (Ao et al., 2023; Sheng et al., 2025).

The reviewed studies further show that parasocial interaction is strengthened through
repeated exposure to influencers’ personal content and communication styles. Influencers
who consistently share their personal lives, values, routines, and experiences are more
likely to cultivate emotional closeness with audiences. Such proximity enables followers
to feel psychologically connected despite the absence of direct interpersonal interaction.
Wang (2025) and Nadroo et al. ( 2026) found that stronger parasocial identification
increases trust and engagement toward promoted products, while Garg and Bakshi (2024)
and Rolando (2025) showed that parasocial interaction positively influences purchase
intention when influencers are perceived as trustworthy and authentic. Research on
virtual influencers similarly indicates that parasocial relationships and credibility
contribute to consumer responses in digital environments (Zhang et al., 2025; Omeish et
al., 2025). Thus, emotional connection functions as an important mechanism linking
influencer-generated content with consumer behavioral responses.
Another recurring pattern concerns the role of authenticity in strengthening parasocial
interaction. Audiences tend to respond more positively to influencers who present
themselves naturally, transparently, and consistently across digital platforms. Conversely,
overly commercialized or excessively promotional content can trigger skepticism.
Authenticity therefore becomes central to sustaining audience trust and long -term
engagement. Several studies indicate that consumers increasingly value honest product
experiences, transparent endorsements, and realistic representations of influencers’ daily
lives (Migkos et al., 2025; Khalfallah & Keller, 2025). This finding is consistent with Cao
et al. (2025) , and Zaki et al. (2025) show that source credibility and perceived value
contribute to consumer engagement and purchase intention. Influencer effectiveness is
therefore strongly connected to perceived sincerity rather than solely to popularity or
follower size.
The findings also indicate that parasocial interaction extends beyond purchase
intention to broader consumer behavior. Emotional attachment to influencers has been
associated with increased consumer engagement, stronger brand recall, and greater
willingness to participate in online discussions concerning products or brands (Diao et al.,
2025; Ingriana & Rolando, 2025). Influencer marketing consequently contributes not only
to transactional outcomes, such as purchasing decisions, but also to relational outcomes
involving emotional commitment and social participation within digital communities.
Interactive communication and consumer participation further reinforce this relationship
(Gu & Duan, 2024; Tartaraj et al., 2024). Influencers function not only as promotional
agents but also as social actors capable of shaping digital consumer experiences and
influencing consumer trust and purchase intention.

Influencer-Generated Content, Brand Polarization, and Consumer Loyalty
Brand polarization can emerge within influencer marketing activities when influencer-
generated content produces divergent responses among audiences, resulting in both strong
support for and resistance toward brands. In social media environments characterized by
rapid information exchange and interactive communication, consumers do not interpret
influencer messages uniformly. Instead, their responses are shaped by individual beliefs,
values, experiences, and perceptions of influencer credibility. As a result , the same
influencer campaign may strengthen loyalty among one audience segment while
simultaneously generating negative sentiment among another. This dynamic reflects the
increasingly interactive nature of influencer marketing, in which consumer engageme nt
and personal interpretation shape responses to promotional content (Ao et al., 2023; Gu
& Duan, 2024).
Several studies suggest that brand polarization becomes more visible when influencers
are associated with controversial opinions, sensitive issues, or highly distinctive personal
identities (Yap et al., 2025). In such situations, audiences may evaluate the  promoted
brand not only based on product quality but also on their perceptions of the influencer ’s
personality, values, and behavior. When consumers perceive alignment between the
influencer and the brand, influencer marketing can reinforce positive brand attitudes and

lifestyles, and social affiliations. Influencer marketing therefore creates segmented

influencer’s persona and communication style. Emerging practices such as cause-oriented

consumers to support, question, or reject established consumption practices (Koivunen et

cannot be generalized universally across all audience groups. Instead, its impact depends
on how consumers identify with the influencer, interpret the content, and rel ate the
influencer’s message to their own values and brand perceptions.

Determinants of Influencer Marketing Effectiveness in Consumer Decision-Making
Influencer–brand congruence was repeatedly identified as a key determinant of
campaign effectiveness. Consumers tend to respond more positively when an influencer’s
identity, lifestyle, and values align with the image and positioning of the promoted brand.
Such alignment can strengthen perceived authenticity and increase the persuasive value
of influencer recommendations (Cao et al., 2025; Duffett & Mxunyelwa , 2025).
Conversely, poor alignment between influencers and brands may reduce message
credibility and generate perceptions of opportunistic endorsement. This finding indicates
that successful influencer marketing depends heavily on strategic compatibility between
brand identity and influencer characteristics (Pratama & Nelloh, 2025).
Content quality and engagement-oriented communication also play an important role
in determining influencer marketing effectiveness. Influencer -generated content that is
visually appealing, interactive, informative, and emotionally engaging tends to produc e
stronger consumer responses than highly promotional or repetitive content. Tartaraj et al.
(2024) found that engagement with influencer content significantly contributes to
consumer behavior, particularly on highly interactive platforms such as TikTok.
Similarly, interactive and live -streaming environments can strengthen purchasing
responses by creating real -time interaction and emotional stimulation among audiences
(Pramesti et al., 2025; Vinkóczi et al., 2026). These findings suggest that content quality
and audience interaction are essential components of effective influencer communication.

practices. Consumers are becoming more aware of sponsored endorsements and

DISCUSSION
The effectiveness of influencer marketing appears to depend not merely on the
exposure generated by promotional content but on the psychological and emotional
relationships established between influencers and their audiences. Consumers in social
media envi ronments actively interpret and evaluate marketing messages rather than
passively receiving them. Influencer-generated content can become more persuasive when
it is perceived as relatable, authentic, and personally relevant. This interpretation is
consistent with Chen et al. (2021), who found that parasocial identification enhances
consumer engagement and trust, and Ao et al. (2023)  confirmed the influence of social
media influencers on engagement and purchase intention. These relationships help
explain why influencer communication can be perceived as more personal and socially
relevant than conventional advertising.
Parasocial interaction provides an important explanation for this relational influence.
Continuous exposure to influencers ’ daily activities, opinions, routines, and personal
experiences can create familiarity and perceived emotional closeness, even without direct
interpersonal communication. Followers may consequently interpret recommendations
as forms of personal advice rather than purely commercial messages. Sheng et al. (2025)
explain that repeated parasocial interactions can develop into stronger parasocial
relationships, while Garg and Bakshi (2024) demonstrate that parasocial interaction and
trust contribute to purchase intention. This relational mechanism also extends beyond
immediate purchasing outcomes. Emotional attachment can encourage engagement,
brand-related discussions, and commitment to influencer -associated brands, consistent
with Zhang et al. (2025), who highl ight relational and parasocial influences in virtual
influencer contexts.
The strength of these relationships, however, depends substantially on perceived
authenticity. Influencers who communicate naturally, provide credible experiences, and
maintain consistency between their online persona and promoted products are more likely
to preserve audience trust. In contrast, excessive commercialization can create skepticism
because audiences may question the motives behind promotional recommendations. This
interpretation is supported by Khalfallah  and Keller (2025), who identify authenticity,
ethics, and transparency as important foundations of consumer trust and engagement.
Migkos et al. (2025) likewise demonstrate that influencer characteristics and
communication practices shape consumer behavior al responses. Thus, authenticity
functions not simply as a desirable characteristic but as a relational resource that helps
influencers maintain credibility and sustain audience engagement.

The relational nature of influencer marketing also helps explain the emergence of
brand polarization. Because social media audiences differ in their values, identities,
experiences, and perceptions of influencers, the same influencer -generated content may
produce contrasting responses. Alignment between an influencer ’s identity and a brand
can strengthen favorable attitudes, emotional attachment, and loyalty, whereas perceived
inconsistency or controversy can stimulate resistance. This dynamic is consistent with
Yap et al. (2025), who show that influential consumers ca n shape brand narratives and
public perceptions during brand crises. Koivunen et al. (2025) similarly demonstrate that
cause-oriented influencers and de -influencing can challenge established consumption
practices. Brand polarization therefore reflects the fragmented nature of digital audiences,
where influencer communication can simultaneously strengthen relationships with some
consumer groups and weaken them with others.
These contrasting responses also demonstrate why influencer selection should extend
beyond follower size and popularity. Credibility, expertise, attractiveness, perceived
value, audience characteristics, communication style, and influencer –brand congruence
collectively shape how consumers evaluate influencer -generated messages. Zaki et al.
(2025) emphasize the role of credibility and perceived value in influencing purchase
intentions, while Duffett and Mxunyelwa  (2025) demonstrate the importance of
influencer characteristics in shaping Generation Z ’s purchase intentions. Influencer –
brand congruence is particularly important because recommendations appear more
persuasive when they fit naturally with the influencer ’s identity and lifestyle. Similarly,
content that is relevant, interactive, informative, and emotionally engaging can strengthen
consumer responses, as reflected in studies of TikTok and live-streaming environments.
Transparency reinforces the relational nature of influencer marketing by shaping
consumers’ perceptions of both sponsored products and endorsement motives.
Transparent disclosure can reduce perceived manipulation and strengthen honesty when
combined with authentic communication (Chen et al., 2021; Khalfallah & Keller, 2025).
This reflects a shift from visibility -oriented promotion toward relationship -oriented
practices, where effectiveness increasingly depends on trust, authenticity, engagement,
credibility, transparency, and audience identification. Gu and D uan (2024) emphasize
consumer engagement and psychological needs, while Joshi et al. (2025) highlight more
strategic and relational influencer practices. This shift also strengthens the role of micro -
influencers and niche creators in fostering engaged comm unities. Influencers therefore
function not merely as promotional intermediaries but as social actors who shape brand
meanings, loyalty, and consumer decision-making.

CONCLUSION
This study concludes that influencer marketing effectiveness is shaped by relational,
content-based, and strategic factors rather than exposure alone. Parasocial interaction
emerged as a central mechanism linking influencer communication with consumer trust,
engagement, and purchase intention, while authenticity strengthens these relationships.
Influencer-generated content can also produce brand polarization because audiences
interpret messages differently according to their values, identities, experiences,  and
perceptions of influencers. At the same time, influencer –brand congruence, credibility,
content quality, engagement, transparency, and perceived value determine how
effectively influencer communication shapes consumer decision -making and loyalty.
These findings imply that marketers should select influencers based on credibility,
audience compatibility, authenticity, and brand fit rather than follower size alone.
Campaigns should also prioritize interactive, relevant, transparent, and relationship -
oriented content to strengthen trust and minimize negative consumer responses.
This study is limited by its reliance on 20 articles selected through a systematic
literature review, the accessibility of full -text sources, and the heterogeneity of contexts,
platforms, and consumer groups represented in the literature. Future research s hould
therefore examine these relationships using empirical and cross-cultural designs, compare
influencer types and platforms, and investigate how parasocial interaction, brand
