Abstract
With	the	development	of	the	digital	entertainment	industry,	"growth‐idol"	has	gradually
attracted	 more	 attention	from	young	people.	Unlike	traditional	idols,	these	idols	show
their	growth	process	to	fans	through	social	media,	live	streaming	platforms,	and	variety
shows.	That	allows	fans	to	see	their	transformation	from	trainees	to	established	artists.
This	 public	 display	 of	 their	 growth	 attracts	 a	 large	 number	 of	 Generation	 Z	 fans	 and
promotes	 their	 consumption	 behavior.	 Therefore,	 researching	 the	 factors	 influencing
fans'	continued	willingness	to	pay	is	a	great	significance.	This	study	aims	to	explore	the
impact	of	growth	visibility	perception	on	Generation	Z	fans'	continued	willingness	to	pay.
Based	on	interaction	theory	and	customer	engagement	theory,	the	study	uses	emotional
attachment	and	fan	sense	of	accomplishment	as	mediating	variables.	And	the	study	adds
fan	participation	into	the	research	framework.	A	questionnaire	survey	was	used	to	collect
201	 valid	 questionnaires.	 And	 SPSS	 was	 used	 for	 data	 analysis.	 The	 results	 show	 that
growth	visibility	perception	significantly	increases	fans'	emotional	attachment	and	fan
sense	 of	 accomplishment.	 Meanwhile,	 emotional	 attachment	 and	 a	 sense	 of
accomplishment	among	fans	significantly	increase	their	willingness	to	pay.	This	indicates
that	 when	 fans	 can	 clearly	 see	 their	 idol's	 growth	 progress,	 they	 are	 more	 likely	 to
establish	 an	 emotional	 connection	 with	 their	 idol	 and	 gain	 a	 sense	 of	 accomplishment
from	 accompanying	 their	 idol's	 growth	 activities.	 Then	 fans	 will	 be	 more	 willing	 to
provide	long‐term	support	and	spending.	This	study	contributes	to	enriching	research	on
the	 fan	 economy	 and	 can	 also	 provide	 a	 reference	 for	 entertainment	 companies	 in
developing	fan	management	and	marketing	strategies.
Keywords

3. Research	Methodology
3.1. Research	Design
This study employed quantitative research methods and collected  data by questionnaires.
Quantitative research can analyz e large amounts of data from re spondents. This method
collected and analyzed basic info rmation and consumption behavi ors, ultimately quantifying
respondents' opinions, backgroun d characteristics, and behavior s as percentages and
frequencies.
This study also is the category of descriptive research. It foc us on describing the basic
characteristics of respondents, such as gender, age, fan experience, and spending amount. This

3.2. Sampling	Method	and	Sample	Size
This study employs a non-probability sampling method. It use co nvenience sampling to select
respondents. Due to time constraints and the specific characteristics of the research population,
convenience sampling is more suitable for this study.
The target respondents for this study are Gen Z individuals. Th ey have followed idols during
their trainee period and have made paid purchases, such as buyi n g  a l b u m s ,  m e m b e r s h i p s ,
merchandise, voting, fan support items, or participating in other paid activities supporting their
idols. Therefore, two screening questions were included at the beginning of the questionnaire.
Screening questions ensure that respondents met the research requirements.
This research employs quantitative research. It collected data through questionnaires and used
SPSS for frequency analysis, descriptive analysis, correlation analysis, and multiple regression
analysis. In addition, a number of respondents' data support the analysis results. These data are
reliable and representative.

5. Discussion	and	Conclusion
5.1. Research	Discussion
T h i s  s t u d y  m a i n l y  h a s  f i v e  f i n d i n g s .  T h e  f i r s t  o n e  i s  t h a t  g r o wth visibility perception has a
positive impact on emotional attractiveness (β = 0.400, p < .001). This finding proves that when
fans can clearly see the growth process of idols will create st ronger emotional connection, the
growth idol model is easier to make emotional connection than traditional idol model, and fans
will see themselves as a part of idol’s growth process. This result is consistent with researches
of Yin, Y. (2021), Özer, M., Özer, A., Ekinci, Y., & Koçak, A. (2022), Zheng, L., & Xu, Y. (2024).
Second one is growth visibility perception has a positive impact on fan sense of accomplishment
(β = 0.397, p < .001). This result discusses that fans will hav e pride after seeing idol’s growth,
fans also think that their support enhances the success of idol , and fans will receive
psychological satisfaction by companing with idol’s growth. Customer engagement theory and
fan economy theory support this result.
The third finding is that emotional attachment has a positive impact on continuous willingness
to pay (β = 0.393, p < .001). This finding explains that if emotional connection is stronger, then
continuous willingness to pay is higher. And fan consumption is  not only buying products, but
also to express emotional support. This finding also proves tha t emotional attachment will
enhance long-term loyalty.
The fourth one is fan sense of accomplishment has a positive impact on continuous willingness
to pay (β = 0.377, p < .001). It discusses that fans can feel a ccomplishment from the success of
idol, accomplishment drives continuous willingness to pay and fans all think that consumption
is a continuous investment. This finding is supported by  Huang, Q., & Zou, Y. (2025) , Kim, M. S.,
Wang, S., & Kim, S. (2023) and Yoo, S. C., Fan, H., Piscarac, D., & Puentes, S. T. (2025).
The final finding is that fan participation significantly moder ates the relationship between
growth visibility perception and emotional attachment. It shows that high engagement fans are
easier to transform perceived growth visibility into emotional attachment, high engagement
behaviors increase investment cost of time, money and energy, a nd high engagement fans are
easier to have a sense of belonging. This finding is supported by customer engagement theory.
