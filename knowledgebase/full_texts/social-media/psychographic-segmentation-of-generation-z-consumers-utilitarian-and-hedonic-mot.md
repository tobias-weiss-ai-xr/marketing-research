Abstract
This	study	 examines	 psychographic	 diversity	 among	 Generation	 Z	 consumers	 within	 Indonesia's	 e-
commerce	 ecosystem,	 focusing	 on	 behavioral	 patterns	 across	 traditional	 marketplaces	and	 social
commerce	platforms.	Despite	marketing	literature	commonly	treating	Generation	Z	as	a	homogeneous
demographic	 group,	 this	 study	 identifies	 distinct	 psychographic	 segments ,	along	with	their
corresponding	 shopping	 motivations	 and	 behavioral	 tendencies.	 Data	 were	 collected	 from	 210
respondents	via	structured	 online	 surveys	that	 measured	psychographic	 characteristics	 using	 an
expanded	 Activities,	 Interests,	 and	 Opinions	 framework,	 recruited	through	purposive	 and	 snowball
sampling	 among	 Generation	 Z	 individuals	 with	 active	 accounts	 on	 Shopee	 or	 TikTok	 Shop.	 Three
psychographic	segments	were	identified:	Passive	Disengaged	Consumers,	Rational	Value	Seekers,	and
Digital-Native	 Enthusiasts,	 each	 exhibiting	 notably	 different	 shopping	 motivations,	 with	 hedonic
motivation	showing	the	strongest	influence.	Psychographic	characteristics	proved	reliable	predictors	of
both	 utilitarian	 and	 hedonic	 motivations,	 with	 hedonic	 motivation	 yielding	 considerably	 stronger
correlations.	Behavioral	analysis	revealed	that	utilitarian	motivation	predicted	higher	expenditure	per
transaction,	 whereas	 hedonic	 motivation	 anticipated	 longer	 browsing	 duration.	 Contrary	 to	 initial
expectations,	 platform	 type	 did	 not	 significantly	 moderate	 the	 relationship	 between	 psychographic
characteristics	and	shopping	motivations,	suggesting	individual	differences	carry	greater	weight	than
platform-specific	features.	These	findings	contribute	to	understanding	Generation	Z	consumer	diversity
and	offer	actionable	guidance	for	developing	segment-specific	e-commerce	marketing	strategies.

INTRODUCTION
The	e-commerce	market	in	Indonesia	has	changed	substantially	in	recent	years,	accelerated	by
the	 COVID-19	 pandemic.	 This	 growth	 has	 introduced	 two	 distinct	 platform	 types:	 traditional
marketplaces	 like	 Shopee	 that	 focus	 on	 systematic	 transactions	 and	 price	comparison,	 and	 social
commerce	platforms	like	TikTok	Shop	that	integrate	shopping	with	entertainment-driven	social	media
experiences	(Peng	&	Lu,	2024).
Generation	Z,	born	between	1997	and	2012,	represents	approximately	74	million	people	or	27%
of	Indonesia’s	population	(Thangavel	et	al.,	2022),	and	has	emerged	as	a	dominant	consumer	group	in
this	platform	landscape.	As	genuine	digital	natives,	Generation	Z	exhibits	consumption	expectations	and
buying	behaviors	that	differ	significantly	from	those	of	other	generational	cohorts	(Theocharis	et	al.,
2025).	Nevertheless,	a	widespread	misconception	persists	that	Generation	Z	represents	a	monolithic
demographic	 group.	 Empirical	 evidence	challenges	this	 view:	Thangavel	 et	 al.	(2022)	identified	 four
distinct	Gen	Z	shopping	segments,	underscoring	the	need	for	psychographic	segmentation	approaches
that	account	for	digital-native	behavioral	patterns	(Vyncke,	2002;	Wang	et	al.,	2022).
Studies	 investigating	 online	 shopping	 incentives	 have	 delineated	 two	 principal	 dimensional
constructs:	utilitarian	and	hedonic	motivations	(Childers	et	al.,	2001).	Utilitarian	motivation	embodies
logical,	 goal-oriented	 perspectives	 that	 prioritize	 efficiency,	 convenience,	 and	 the	 maximization	 of
economic	value.	Hedonic	motivation	pertains	to	emotional	and	experiential	orientations	that	prioritize
entertainment	value,	sensory	stimulation,	and	social	fulfillment	from	shopping	activities,	irrespective	of
transactional	outcomes	(Babin	et	al.,	1994).	Recent	research	by	Ang	(2024)	on	Generation	Z	indicates
that	 both	 motives	 significantly	 affect	 buying	 habits,	 with	 utilitarian	 motivation	 serving	 as	 a	 stronger
predictor	of	purchase	decisions	and	hedonic	motivation	predicting	ongoing	platform	participation.	It	is
crucial	 to	 understand	how	 psychographic	 traits	 consistently	 affect	 these	 two	 types	 of	 motivations	 in
order	to	come	up	with	good	methods.
Platform	Affordances	Theory	(Treem	&	Leonardi,	2013)	posits	that	platform’s	unique	aspects
consistently	 affect	user	 behavior.	Conventional	 marketplaces	 with	 search-oriented	 interfaces	 may
disproportionately	 appeal	 to	 consumers	 with	 strong	 utilitarian	 motives,	 whereas	 social	 commerce
platforms	with	content-driven	interfaces	may	be	better	suited	for	consumers	with	heightened	hedonic
orientations.	 Examining	 whether	 platform	 factors	 influence	 the	 links	 between	 psychographic
differences	 and	 incentives	 yields	 significant	 theoretical	 insights	 and	 practical	 assistance	 for	 platform
positioning	tactics.
Even	though	research	on	Generation	Z	shopping	behavior	is	growing,	several	important	gaps
remain.	 First,	 although	 existing	 research	 recognizes	 the	 significance	 of	 Generation	 Z,	 the	 majority	 of
studies	utilize	conventional	segmentation	methods,	frequently	overlooking	the	incorporation	of	modern
digital	 habits	 that	 define	 this	 generation.	 For	 instance,	(Thangavel	 et	 al.,	 2022)	employed	 K-means
cluster	 analysis	 based	 on	 nine	 traditional	 shopping	 orientation	 factors	 without	 incorporating	 digital
behavior	 dimensions	 such	 as	 social	 media	 usage,	 content	 consumption,	 or	 platform -switching
behaviors.	Similarly,	the	foundational	Activities,	Interests,	and	Opinions	(AIO)	Framework	(Plummer,
1974;	Vyncke,	2002)	was	developed	prior	to	the	digital	era	and	lacks	constructs	specific	to	digital	native
consumption	 patterns.	 The	 traditional	 Activities-Interests-Opinions	 Framework	 need	 significant
enhancement	 to	 include	 digital	 behavior	 aspects	 pertinent	 to	 digital	 native	 people	(Vyncke,	 2002).
Second,	 the	 theoretical	 mechanisms	 by	 which	 psychographic	 features	 predict	 utilitarian	 and	 hedonic
motivations	remain	insufficiently	understood,	particularly	in	identifying	psychographic	antecedents	of
these	dual	motivational	orientations	(To	et	al.,	2007).	This	gap	is	particularly	salient	in	the	Indonesian
context,	 where	 recent	 evidence	 indicates	 that	 Generation	 Z's	 engagement	 with	 social	 commerce	 is
shaped	 by	 distinct	 psychosocial	 and	 platform-specific	 factors	 that	 remain	 poorly	 captured	 by
conventional	segmentation	approaches	(Huwaida	et	al.,	2024).

Third,	 no	 prior	 research	 has	 examined	 whether	 platform	 type	 moderates	 psychographic-
motivation	relationships	(Peng	&	Lu,	2024).		Fourth,	empirical	behavioral	validation	of	psychographic
segmentation	outcomes	remains	scarce	in	the	Indonesian	Generation	Z	context.	Fifth,	Indonesia	as	an
emerging	 market	 is	 significantly	 underrepresented	 in	 Generation	 Z	 e-commerce	 research,	 with	 most
studies	focusing	on	Western	or	East	Asian	contexts.
To	address	these	gaps,	this	study	integrates	three	complementary	theoretical	frameworks:	the
Extended	AIO	Framework	(Vyncke,	2002)	for	psychographic	segmentation	incorporating	digital	native
behaviors;	 Dual	 Value	 Theory	(Babin	 et	 al.,	 1994;	 Childers	 et	 al.,	 2001)	to	 explain	 differences	 in
utilitarian	 and	 hedonic	 motivations	 across	 segments;	 and	 Platform	 Affordances	 Theory	(Treem	 &
Leonardi,	2013)	to	examine	how	platform-specific	characteristics	moderate	psychographic-motivation
relationships.
This	 study	 offers	 various	 unique	 additions	 that	 enhance	 current	 knowledge.	 First,	 it	 offers
systematic	 empirical	 confirmation	 of	 Generation	 Z	 heterogeneity	 within	 the	 Indonesian	 emerging
market	scenario	through	psychographic	segmentation	methods,	hence	extending	the	AIO	Framework
specifically	 for	 digital	 native	 populations.	 Second,	 it	 enhances	 Dual	 Value	 Theory	 by	 empirically
pinpointing	 psychographic	 features	 as	 fundamental	 antecedents	 of	 utilitarian	 and	 hedonic	 shopping
motives,	 so	 addressing	 the	 gap	 concerning	 the	 individual	 difference	 variables	 that	 predict	 both
motivational	 orientations.	 Third,	 it	 offers	 new	 empirical	 evidence	 about	 the	 relative	 significance	 of
personal	 versus	 situational	 aspects	 in	 shaping	 shopping	 impulses	 by	 analyzing	 platform	 moderation
effects,	 so	 contributing	 to	 the	 discourse	 on	 person-situation	 interactionism	 theory.	 Fourth,	 it	 offers
behavioral	 validation,	 showing	 that	 psychographic	 segments	 display	 unique	 shopping	 habits	 that	 go
beyond	 differences	 in	 attitudes,	 proving	 that	 they 	may	 be	 used	 to	 make	 predictions.	 Fifth,
methodologically,	 it	 combines	 many	 ways	 of	 analyzing	 data	 into	 one	 big	 framework,	 which	 makes	 it
possible	to	test	hypotheses	in	a	strong	way	from	many	different	angles.
The	specific	research	objectives	are:	(1)	to	identify	and	characterize	psychographic	segments
among	 Generation	 Z	 e-commerce	 consumers	 in	 West	 Java	 based	 on	 an	 extended	 AIO	 framework
incorporating	 digital	 behaviors;	 (2)	 to	 analyze	 systematic	 differences	 in	utilitarian	 and	 hedonic
shopping	motivations	across	psychographic	segments,	understanding	which	characteristics	associate
with	each	motivational	orientation;	(3)	to	test	whether	psychographic	segments	differ	in	preferences
for	 traditional	 marketplace	 versus	 social	 commerce	 platforms;	 (4)	 to	 investigate	 whether	 platform
usage	 moderates	 relationships	 between	 psychographic	 characteristics	 and	 shopping	 motivations,
examining	whether	platform	context	influences	how	individual	differences	manifest	into	motivational
orientations;	(5)	to	provide	behavioral	validation	by	examining	relationships	between	motivations	and
observable	 outcomes	 including	 spending	 per	 transaction	 and	 browsing	 duration;	 and	 (6)	 to	 derive
actionable	 theoretical	 insights	 advancing	 understanding	of	 Generation	 Z	 consumer	 behavior	 while
generating	 practical	 recommendations	 for	 developing	 effective,	 targeted	 strategies	 recognizing
Generation	Z	heterogeneity.
This	 research	 holds	 value	 across	 various	 dimensions.	 Theoretically,	 it	 enhances	 academic
comprehension	of	Generation	Z	as	diverse	rather	than	uniform,	critiques	oversimplified	demographic
targeting,	expands	psychographic	segmentation	models	for	digital	natives,	elucidates	the	precursors	of
dual	 shopping	 motivations,	 and	 evaluates	 person-situation	 interaction	 theories	 within	 e-commerce
settings.	 In	 practice,	 the	 findings	 provide	 immediate	 actionable	 insights	 for	 platform	 operators
concerning	positioning	strategies,	feature	development,	and	interface	design;	for	marketers	regarding
audience	segmentation,	message	customization,	and	platform	selection	optimization;	and	they	enhance
consumer	self-awareness	and	improve	shopping	experiences.	By	concentrating	on	West	Java,	Indonesia
the	nation's	most	populated	province	with	significant	digital	adoption	this	research	yields	regionally-
specific	 insights	 while	 enhancing	 comprehension	 of	 rising	 market	 dynamics	 and	 Southeast	 Asian	 e-

HYPOTHESES	DEVELOPMENT
This	study	grounds	its	hypotheses	in	three	integrated	theoretical	frameworks:	the	Extended	AIO
Framework	(Plummer,	1974;	Vyncke,	2002),	updated	for	digital	consumer	contexts	by		Theocharis	et	al.
(2025)	and	 further	 validated	 for	 cluster-based	 psychographic	 segmentation	 by	 Ameyibor	&	Saini
(2024);	Dual	Value	Theory	(Babin	et	al.,	1994;	Childers	et	al.,	2001),	empirically	extended	by	Wang	et	al.
(2022)	in	social	commerce	settings;	and	Platform	Affordances	Theory	(Bucher	&	Helmond,	2018;	Treem
&	 Leonardi,	 2013)	to	 examine	 how	 platform-specific	 characteristics	 moderate	 psychographic-
motivation	relationships.
Recent	empirical	investigations	have	reinforced	the	continued	relevance	of	these	frameworks	in
contemporary	 digital	 consumer	 behavior	 contexts.	Lim	 et	 al.	 (2017)	confirmed	 that	 psychographic
lifestyle	 dimensions	 remain	 robust	 predictors	 of	 online	 shopping	 engagement	 among	 digital	 native
populations,	while	Peng	&	Lu	(2024)	demonstrated	that	platform	structural	affordances	systematically
influence	 motivational	 formation	 across	 social	 commerce	 and	 traditional	 marketplace	 settings.	Ang
(2024)	further	 validated	 that	 utilitarian	 and	 hedonic	 motivations	 produce	 distinct	 and	 measurable
behavioral	 outcomes	 among	 Generation	 Z	 consumers,	 providing	 recent	 empirical	 grounding	 for	 the
behavioral	 outcome	 pathways	 examined	 in	 this	 study.	 The	 integration	 of	 these	 three	 frameworks
enables	 a	 theoretically	 comprehensive	 examination	 of	 how	 individual -level	 psychographic
characteristics	 interact	 with	 platform-level	 situational	 factors	 to	 produce	 differentiated	 motivational
orientations	and	behavioral	outcomes.

Empirical	 support	 for	 this	 psychographic–motivation	 linkage	 has	 been	 demonstrated	 across
multiple	 recent	 studies.	Ang	 (2024)	confirmed	 that	 distinct	 consumer	 groups	 within	 Generation	 Z
exhibit	systematically	divergent	utilitarian	and	hedonic	motivation	profiles,	with	lifestyle	orientation
emerging	 as	 a	 significant	 individual -difference	 predictor.	Theocharis	 et	 al.	 (2025)	similarly
demonstrated	 that	 psychographic	 characteristics	 encompassing	 technology	 adoption	 attitudes,	 social
influence	 sensitivity,	 and	 novelty	 seeking	 significantly	 predicted	 hedonic	 engagement	 with	 digital
commerce	 platforms	 among	 Generation	 Z	 consumers.	To	 et	 al.	 (2007)	established	 that	 efficiency-
seeking	 consumers	 exhibit	 reliably	 higher	 utilitarian	 motivations,	 while	 social	 and	 exploratory
consumers	exhibit	reliably	higher	hedonic	motivations	a	motivational	bifurcation	mechanism	that	this
study	hypothesizes	will	manifest	across	identified	psychographic	segments.	This	dual-value	structure
has	 since	 been	 corroborated	 across	 contemporary	 store,	 online,	 and	 mobile -app	 shopping
environments,	confirming	its	continued	relevance	for	digitally	mediated	retail	contexts	(Picot-Coupey
et	al.,	2021).	Furthermore,	Guo	&	Li	(2022)	demonstrated	that	consumer	psychographic	orientations
toward	social	connection	and	experiential	engagement	predict	sustained	hedonic	engagement	in	social
commerce	contexts.	Cross-generational	comparisons	further	indicate	that	these	motivational	patterns
are	not	uniform	across	cohorts,	with	Generation	Z	displaying	a	distinctly	stronger	hedonic	orientation
than	 older	 generational	 cohorts	(Nwobodo	 &	 Weissmann,	 2024),	 while	 social-media-based	 purchase
intention	models	confirm	that	psychographic	and	behavioral	antecedents	jointly	shape	Generation	Z's
engagement	with	digital	commerce	platforms	(Roy	et	al.,	2025),	while	individual-difference	factors	such
as	gender	and	environmental	awareness	have	been	shown	to	further	moderate	Generation	Z's	shopping
motivations	 and	 channel	 preferences	(Harantová	 &	 Mazanec,	 2025).	 Ethical	 and	 value-driven
considerations	further	differentiate	segments,	as	Generation	Z's	awareness	of	ethical	and	environmental
issues	 does	 not	 uniformly	 translate	 into	 consistent	 purchasing	 behavior	(Djafarova	 &	 Foots,	 2022),
while	emerging	technological	factors	such	as	AI	exposure	(Bunea	et	al.,	2024),	digital	media	disruption
(Chang	&	Chang,	2023),	and	platform-switching	tendencies	driven	by	overload	and	dissatisfaction	(Fan
et	 al.,	 2024;	 Ryu	 et	 al.,	 2023)	add	 further	 behavioral	 heterogeneity	 that	 a	 purely	 demographic
segmentation	 approach	 cannot	 capture.	 These	 converging	 theoretical	 mechanisms	 and	 empirical
precedents	support	the	following	hypotheses:
H1:	 Psychographic	 segments	 differ	 significantly	 in	 utilitarian	 shopping	 motivation,	 with	 efficiency-
oriented,	practical,	and	goal-directed	segments	exhibiting	higher	utilitarian	motivation.
H2:	 Psychographic	 segments	 differ	 significantly	 in	 hedonic	 shopping	 motivation,	 with	 experience-
seeking,	entertainment-oriented,	and	socially-engaged	segments	exhibiting	higher	hedonic	motivation.

Shopee	and	TikTok	Shop	were	selected	as	focal	platforms	because	they	represent	structurally
divergent	 e-commerce	 models	 with	 contrasting	 affordance	 profiles.	 Shopee's	 interface	 architecture
organized	around	systematic	product	catalogues,	advanced	search	and	filtering	tools,	price	comparison
mechanisms,	and	goal-oriented	transactional	flows,	is	structurally	aligned	with	the	informational	and
efficiency	 needs	 of	 utilitarian	 consumers	(Peng	 &	 Lu,	 2024).	 For	 efficiency-oriented	 consumers	 who
enter	the	shopping	process	with	predetermined	needs	and	minimal	tolerance	for	informational	friction,
Shopee's	search-centric	design	substantially	lowers	the	cognitive	effort	associated	with	need	fulfillment,
making	it	the	psychographically	congruent	platform.
TikTok	Shop's	interface	architecture	operates	on	fundamentally	different	affordance	principles:
algorithmic	 short-form	 video	 content,	 live	 streaming	 commerce,	 and	 social	 discovery	 mechanisms
create	 an	 immersive,	 entertainment-driven	 shopping	 environment	 in	which	 product	 exposure	 is
embedded	within	lifestyle	content	consumption	(Putri	et	al.,	2024).	For	experience-seeking	consumers
who	 derive	 intrinsic	 value	 from	 discovery,	 social	 engagement,	 and	 novelty	 exposure,	TikTok	 Shop's
content-driven	 architecture	 provides	 a	 psychographically	 resonant	 environment	 that	 transforms

shopping	 from	 a	 transactional	 task	 into	 a	 hedonic	 activity,	 particularly	 through	 influencer-driven
content	exposure	(Lim	et	al.,	2017;	Putri	et	al.,	2024).
Platform	Affordances	Theory	(Treem	&	Leonardi,	2013)	posits	that	individuals	gravitate	toward
platforms	 whose	 structural	 affordances	 align	 with	 their	 psychological	 orientations.	 Person -
environment	 fit	 theory	(Kristof-Brown	 et	 al.,	 2023)	further	 establishes	 that	 consumers	 experience
greater	 satisfaction	 and	 sustained	 engagement	 in	 environments	 congruent	 with	 their	 values	 and
behavioral	dispositions.	Theocharis	et	al.	(2025)	and	Guo	&	Li	(2022)	confirm	that	entertainment	value
and	 social	 discovery	 features	 disproportionately	 attract	 experience-seeking	 consumers.	 Collectively,
these	mechanisms	support	the	following	directional	hypotheses:
H3:	 Efficiency-oriented,	 systematic,	 and	 task-focused	 psychographic	 segments	 exhibit	 stronger
preferences	for	traditional	marketplace	platforms	(Shopee).
H4:	Entertainment-seeking,	socially-engaged,	and	exploratory	psychographic	segments	exhibit	stronger
preferences	for	social	commerce	platforms	(TikTok	Shop).

Motivational	 orientations	 systematically	 predict	 observable	 behavioral	 outcomes	 through
distinct	 causal	 mechanisms.	 Utilitarian	 motivation	 predicts	 higher	 spending	 per	 transaction	 because
goal-directed	consumers	enter	purchasing	situations	with	predetermined	needs	and	clearly	bounded
decision	 criteria,	 producing	 decisive	 and	 relatively	 high-value	 purchase	 outcomes	(To	 et	 al.,	 2007).
Utilitarian	consumers'	deliberate	information	search	and	systematic	price	comparison	behaviors	also
tend	to	concentrate	purchases	toward	fewer,	more	considered	transactions	rather	than	frequent,	low-
value	 impulse	 purchases,	a	 behavioral	 pattern	 that	 manifests	 as	 elevated	 average	 transaction	 value
(Ang,	2024).
Hedonic	 motivation	 predicts	 extended	 browsing	 duration	 because	 consumers	 motivated	 by
experiential	rewards	derive	intrinsic	value	from	the	shopping	process	itself	(Akram	et	al.,	2018;	Babin
et	al.,	1994).	The	hedonic	shopping	experience	characterized	by	discovery,	novelty	exposure,	and	social
engagement,	functions	 as	 an	 intrinsically	 rewarding	 activity	 that	 sustains	 behavioral	 engagement
beyond	 any	 specific	 transactional	 need.	Yastuti	 &	 Irawati	 (2023)	confirmed	 in	 an	 Indonesian	 e-
commerce	context	that	hedonic	shopping	motivation	is	significantly	associated	with	extended	platform
engagement	 and	 exploratory	 browsing	 behavior	 among	 Generation	 Z	 consumers.	Guo	 &	 Li	 (2022)
further	demonstrated	that	social	commerce	features	amplifying	hedonic	value	extend	session	duration
and	return	visit	frequency.	These	mechanisms	support	the	following	hypotheses:
H5:	Utilitarian	shopping	motivation	positively	predicts	spending	per	transaction.
H6:	Hedonic	shopping	motivation	positively	predicts	browsing	duration.

Person-situation	 interactionism	 theory	(Endler	 &	 Magnusson,	 1976)	posits	 that	 behavior
emerges	from	ongoing	interactions	between	individual	characteristics	and	situational	contexts.	Applied
here,	platform	type	represents	the	situational	context	that	may	amplify	or	attenuate	how	psychographic
traits	translate	into	motivational	orientations.
Shopee's	 utilitarian	 affordances	 should	 amplify	 the	 psychographic-utilitarian	 motivation
relationship	 for	 efficiency-oriented	 consumers,	 while	 TikTok	 Shop's	 hedonic	 affordances	 should
strengthen	 the	 psychographic-hedonic	 motivation	 relationship	 for	 experience-seeking	 consumers
(Peng	&	Lu,	2024).	This	leads	to:
H7:	 Platform	 type	 moderates	 the	 relationship	 between	 psychographic	 characteristics	 and	 utilitarian
motivation,	 such	 that	 the	 positive	 relationship	 is	 stronger	 on	 traditional	 marketplace	 platforms
(Shopee)	versus	social	commerce	platforms	(TikTok	Shop).
H8:	 Platform	 type	 moderates	 the	 relationship	 between	 psychographic	 characteristics	 and	 hedonic
motivation,	such	that	the	positive	relationship	is	stronger	on	social	commerce	platforms	(TikTok	Shop)
versus	traditional	marketplace	platforms	(Shopee).

METHOD
This	study	utilized	a	quantitative	methodology	with	a	descriptive-explanatory	research	design
to	examine	the	psychographic	segmentation	of	Generation	Z	consumers	on	e-commerce	platforms	in
West	 Java,	 Indonesia.	 The	 descriptive	 aspect	 sought	 to	 identify	 and	delineate	 specific	 psychographic
segments	 within	 Gen	 Z	 consumers	 utilizing	 cluster	 analysis	 methodologies,	 whereas	 the	 explanatory
facet	 investigated	 the	 interrelations	 among	 psychographic	 traits,	 shopping	 motivations,	 platform
preferences,	and	behavioral	outcomes	through	multivariate	statistical	techniques.	This	methodological
approach	was	chosen	because	it	allows	for	systematic	measurement	of	psychographic	variables	utilizing
validated	 scales,	 while	 concurrently	 evaluating	 theoretical	 claims	 regarding	 correlations	 between
constructs	through	stringent	hypothesis	testing	methodologies.
The	 study	 population	 comprised	 all	 Generation	 Z	 consumers	 (born	 between	 1997	 and	 2012,
aged	13	to	28	years	at	the	time	of	the	research)	residing	in	West	Java	and	actively	utilizing	e-commerce
platforms,	notably	Shopee	and	TikTok	Shop.	West	Java	was	selected	as	the	research	site	due	to	its	status
as	 Indonesia's	 most	 populous	 province,	 with	 a	 population	 of	 approximately	 49.93	 million	 people
representing	 18.1%	 of	 the	 national	 total	(BPS,	 2024),	 characterized	 by	 elevated	 internet	 penetration
rates	exceeding	73%	and	a	substantial	Generation	Z	population	comprising	approximately	27%	of	the
provincial	demographic	composition	(APJII,	2024).	This	demographic	concentration	ensures	sufficient
representativeness	 across	 urban,	 peri-urban,	 and	 rural	 settings,	 as	 well	 as	 diverse	 socioeconomic
backgrounds.	Due	to	the	considerable	and	vaguely	delineated	population	size,	this	study	employed	non-
probability	sampling	through	purposive	and	snowball	sampling	methods.
The	 combination	 of	 purposive	 and	 snowball	 sampling	 offers	 complementary	 methodological
advantages.	Purposive	sampling	enables	precise	alignment	between	respondent	characteristics	and	the
study's	 theoretical	 requirements,	 ensuring	 that	 all	 participants	 genuinely	 belong	 to	 the	 Generation	 Z
cohort	 and	 actively	 use	 the	 specified	 platforms,	thereby	 maximizing	 construct	 validity.	 Snowball
sampling	 extends	 reach	 into	 networks	 that	 are	 otherwise	 inaccessible	 through	 formal	 channels,
particularly	among	younger	digital	communities	where	personal	referral	constitutes	the	primary	trust
mechanism	 for	 research	 participation.	 However,	 this	 combined	 approach	 carries	 acknowledged
limitations:	 it	 does	 not	 permit	 probability-based	 statistical	 inference	 to	 the	 broader	 Generation	 Z
population,	 and	 snowball	 sampling	 may	 introduce	 network	 homogeneity	 bias	 whereby	 respondents
share	 similar	 social	 networks,	 values,	 and	 behavioral	 patterns,	 potentially	 underrepresenting
psychographic	diversity	at	the	extremes	of	the	distribution.	These	limitations	are	partially	mitigated	by
multi-channel	distribution	across	geographically	dispersed	institutional	networks	and	the	diversity	of
recruitment	 settings	 ranging	 from	 campus	 environments	 to	 professional	 workplaces	 and	 community
organizations,	ensuring	 reasonable	 representativeness	 across	 demographic	 sub-groups	 within	 the
target	population.

Following	measurement	model	validation,	the	structural	model	was	tested	using	Partial	Least

subsamples.	Unlike	the	separate	regression	models	previously	used,	PLS-SEM	simultaneously	estimates
all	 structural	 paths	 in	 a	 single	 integrated	 model,	 allowing	 assessment	 of	 the	 overall	 structural

relationships	between	latent	variables,	directly	addressing	the	reviewer’s	concern	about	silo-analysis
(Hair	et	al.,	2019).
The	 structural	 model	 examined	 four	 hypothesized	 paths	 simultaneously.	 Psychographic
characteristics	 significantly	 predicted	 hedonic	 motivation	 (H1:	 β=0.772,	 t=18.83,	 p<0.001,	 f²=0.437

with	MANOVA	results	(η²=0.438	vs	0.111).

Discussion
This	 section	 discusses	 the	 research	 results	 within	 a	 broader	 theoretical	 and	 empirical
perspective,	 integrating	 findings	 with	 pertinent	 theories	 and	 prior	 investigations	 to	 elucidate	 their
theoretical	and	practical	significance.

Psychographic	Heterogeneity	of	Generation	Z
The	 delineation	 of	 three	 separate	 psychographic	 segments	 Passive	 Disengaged	 Consumers,
Rational	 Value	 Seekers,	 and	 Digital-Native	 Enthusiasts	 furnishes	 robust	 empirical	 evidence	 that
Generation	Z	is	not	a	monolithic	entity,	contrary	to	prevalent	assumptions	in	marketing	practice.	This
discovery	 significantly	 corroborates	 and	 expands	 upon	 the	 research	 conducted	 by	Thangavel	 et	 al.,
(2022),	which	 also	 identified	 notable	 variability	 among	 Generation	 Z,	 albeit	 with	 a	 different
segmentation	 framework	 (four	 segments	 compared	 to	 three	 segments	 in	 the	 present	 study).	 The
disparity	in	the	number	of	segments	can	be	elucidated	by	variations	in	the	utilized	variables,	geographic
and	cultural	setting,	and	sample	makeup.

The	 existence	 of	 the	 Passive	 Disengaged	 segment	 comprising	 over	 a	 third	 of	 the	 sample
challenges	 the	 universal	 assumption	 that	 Gen	 Z	 is	 uniformly	 tech-savvy	 and	 digitally	 engaged.	 As
(Yastuti	 &	 Irawati,	 2023)	similarly	 observed	 in	 an	 Indonesian	 Gen	 Z	 context,	 digital	 disengagement
coexists	 alongside	 digital	 nativity	 even	 within	 the	 same	 generational	 cohort.	 Socioeconomic	 status,
educational	background,	geographic	context,	and	personal	disposition	produce	considerable	variation
in	 digital	 involvement,	 underscoring	 that	 generational	 membership	 is	 a	 contextual	 moderator	 rather
than	a	primary	determinant	of	consumer	behavior.
Theoretically,	 these	 findings	 extend	 generational	 cohort	 theory	 by	 demonstrating	 that	 intra-
generational	 variability	 can	 be	 as	 pronounced	 as	 inter-generational	 differences.	 The	 three-segment
structure	illustrates	that	generational	cohort	membership	provides	a	contextual	backdrop	but	does	not
determine	individual	motivational	orientation	or	behavioral	tendency.	Marketing	strategies	premised
on	 generational	 homogeneity	 will,	 by	 design,	 fail	 to	 connect	 meaningfully	 with	 the	 majority	 of	 the
consumer	population	they	target.

Psychographic	Characteristics	as	Motivation	Antecedents
The	 finding	 that	 psychographic	 characteristics	 serve	 as	 significant	 antecedents	 of	 both
utilitarian	 and	 hedonic	 motivations	 provides	 a	 substantial	 contribution	 to	 dual	 value	 theory.	 Prior
studies	have	thoroughly	examined	motivational	consequences	on	behavior	(Babin	et	al.,	1994;	Childers
et	 al.,	 2001;	 To	 et	 al.,	 2007),	 	yet	 the	 determinants	 of	 motivational	 orientation,	particularly
psychographic	antecedents	remain	underexplored.	This	study	demonstrates	that	consumers’	lifestyle
patterns,	values,	interests,	and	digital	behaviors	are	systematically	correlated	with	their	motivational
orientations,	 establishing	 psychographic	 characteristics	 as	 upstream	 drivers	 within	 the	 motivational
architecture	of	online	consumer	behavior.
Notably,	the	psychographic–hedonic	linkage	is	substantially	stronger	than	the	psychographic–
utilitarian	linkage	that	is	theoretically	meaningful.	This	asymmetry	implies	that	psychographic	traits	are
more	 influential	 for	 experiential,	 pleasure-seeking	 orientations	 than	 for	 goal-directed,	 task-oriented
ones.	Utilitarian	motivation	is	more	universal	and	anchored	in	functional	need;	hedonic	motivation	is
more	variable	and	personality-dependent.	Consumers	who	are	more	open	to	new	experiences,	socially
connected,	and	digitally	engaged	naturally	seek	novelty,	variety,	and	social	satisfaction	in	their	shopping
encounters	(Ang,	 2024).	 The	 psychographic	 profile	 of	 a	 consumer	 is	 therefore	 a	 far	 more	 powerful
predictor	of	the	degree	to	which	they	seek	pleasure	in	shopping	than	of	their	desire	for	transactional
efficiency.
The	segmentation	analysis	reinforces	this	pattern:	the	divergence	in	hedonic	motivation	across
segments	 is	 far	 more	 pronounced	 than	 the	 divergence	 in	 utilitarian	 motivation.	 Digital -Native
Enthusiasts’	 elevated	 hedonic	 orientation	 can	 be	 interpreted	 as	 a	 behavioral	 extension	 of	 their
overarching	 lifestyle	 one	 that	 prioritizes	 experience,	 social	 engagement,	 and	 self-expression.	 These
results	 enhance	 dual	 value	 theory	 by	 establishing	 psychographic	 characteristics	 as	 systematic
antecedents	 of	 motivational	 orientations,	 demonstrating	 that	 motivations	 are	 not	 simply	 static
individual	traits	but	are	embedded	within,	and	predictable	from,	broader	lifestyle	configurations.
These	results	open	productive	research	avenues	into	how	life	transitions	such	as	moving	to	a
new	 city,	 changing	 employment,	 or	 entering	 a	 new	 relationship	 may	 affect	 shopping	 motivations	 by
reshaping	 lifestyle	 habits.	 They	 also	 imply	 that	 interventions	 targeting	 lifestyle	 dimensions	 may
indirectly	and	meaningfully	influence	motivational	orientations.

Asymmetric	Motivational	Pathways	to	Behavioral	Outcomes
One	of	the	most	theoretically	significant	findings	of	this	study	is	the	asymmetric	pattern	through	which
motivational	orientations	translate	into	behavioral	outcomes.	While	hedonic	motivation	demonstrates
a	 meaningful	 pathway	 to	 behavioral	 characteristics,	utilitarian	 motivation	 does	 not	 a	 result	 that

challenges	the	symmetric	framing	often	implied	in	dual	value	theory	(Babin	et	al.,	1994;	Childers	et	al.,
2001)	and	merits	sustained	theoretical	attention.
The	failure	of	utilitarian	motivation	to	predict	behavioral	outcomes	in	this	integrated	structural
model	 can	 be	 understood	 through	 two	 theoretical	 lenses.	 First,	 from	 a	 person -environment	 fit
perspective	(Kristof-Brown	et	al.,	2023),	utilitarian	motivation	represents	a	stable	disposition	toward
efficiency	and	goal	attainment,	but	behavioral	outcomes	such	as	purchase	frequency	and	spending	are
jointly	determined	by	motivational	disposition	and	external	constraints,	principally	economic	capacity
and	 habitual	 platform	 use	 patterns.	 A	 highly	 utilitarian	 consumer	 may	 strongly	 desire	 efficient
transactions	yet	remain	constrained	by	budget	or	prior	loyalty,	diluting	the	direct	motivation–behavior
link.	Second,	utilitarian	motivation	functions	more	as	a	threshold	construct	than	a	magnitude	construct:
once	a	minimum	level	of	utilitarian	satisfaction	is	achieved,	additional	motivation	does	not	translate	into
additional	 behavioral	 output.	 Consumers	 “complete”	 their	 utilitarian	 shopping	 when	 needs	are	 met,
unlike	 hedonic	 shoppers	 who	 remain	 behaviorally	 activated	 as	 long	 as	 the	 experience	 continues	 to
reward	them.
Hedonic	 motivation's	 significant	 behavioral	 pathway	 is	 theoretically	 coherent	 with	 the
characterization	of	shopping	as	an	intrinsically	rewarding	process	(Akram	et	al.,	2018;	Babin	et	al.,	1994;
Zhang	et	al.,	2018):	consumers	who	derive	pleasure	from	shopping	are	more	likely	to	extend	and	repeat
the	 behavior,	 producing	 observable	 differences	 in	 transaction	 patterns	 and	 engagement	 depth.	 This
asymmetric	 finding	 also	 demonstrates	 an	 important	 analytical	 advantage	 of	 the	 integrated	 PLS-SEM
approach:	by	estimating	all	structural	relationships	simultaneously,	the	model	reveals	that	utilitarian
motivation’s	 apparent	 behavioral	 effect	 is	 substantially	 attenuated	 when	 hedonic	 motivation	 and
psychographic	 characteristics	 are	modeled	 jointly,	a	 finding	 that	 remains	 concealed	 within	 isolated
single-equation	analyses.	The	structure	of	motivational	influence	on	consumer	behavior	is	inherently
multivariate,	and	partial	approaches	risk	misattributing	variance	that	properly	belongs	to	correlated
predictors.

Platform	Role	and	Moderation	Effects
The	 marginally	 non-significant	 platform–segment	 preference	 association	 and	 non-significant
PLS-MGA	findings	suggest	that	the	platform	role	is	more	restricted	than	initially	proposed	a	finding	that
is	 somewhat	 unexpected	 given	 the	 structural	 differences	 between	 Shopee’s	 transaction-oriented
interface	and	TikTok	Shop’s	content-driven	social	commerce	architecture	(Peng	&	Lu,	2024).	Several
theoretical	explanations	illuminate	this	null	moderation	effect.
First,	 ongoing	 platform	 convergence	 may	 be	 substantially	 eroding	 functionally	 meaningful
distinctions.	 Shopee	 has	 progressively	 integrated	 live	 streaming,	 short-form	 video	 content,	 and
community	features,	while	TikTok	Shop	has	developed	structured	product	catalogs,	expedited	logistics,
and	 formal	 transactional	 infrastructure.	 As	 both	 platforms	 migrate	 toward	 comparable	 functional
repertoires,	 the	 affordance-based	 differentiation	 theoretically	 expected	 to	 moderate	 motivation
formation	becomes	less	tenable.
Second,	 individual	 differences	 appear	 to	 exert	 greater	 influence	 on	 motivational	 orientation
than	 do	 situational	 platform	 characteristics.	 The	 strong	 predictive	 power	 of	 psychographic
characteristics	 over	 both	 motivational	 dimensions,	particularly	 hedonic	 motivation,	suggests	 that
lifestyle-based	dispositions	are	deeply	embedded	and	relatively	impervious	to	platform-context	effects.
In	the	framework	of	person-situation	interactionism	(Endler	&	Magnusson,	1976),	these	results	indicate
dominant	 person	 effects	 relative	 to	 situation	 effects,	 consistent	 with	 trait	 theories	 predicting	 that
personality	 and	 lifestyle	 are	 more	 powerful	 behavioral	 predictors	 than	 contextual	 factors	 when
behaviors	are	trait-relevant.

Third,	while	the	omnibus	platform–segment	association	falls	just	short	of	statistical	significance,
the	 directional	 trend	 is	 theoretically	 meaningful:	 Digital-Native	 Enthusiasts	 exhibit	 noticeably	 higher
social	commerce	adoption	than	the	other	two	segments.	This	shows	that	people	who	are	very	active
online	are	more	open	to	social	commerce.	Even	within	this	segment,	however,	the	majority	still	prefer
Shopee,	suggesting	that	conventional	marketplaces	retain	a	strong	and	durable	value	proposition	even
for	the	most	digitally	engaged	consumers,	reflecting	the	enduring	importance	of	trust,	product	diversity,
and	price	competitiveness	in	platform	selection	decisions.
From	a	platform	affordances	standpoint,	the	absence	of	significant	moderation	effects	indicates
that	affordances	may	be	less	impactful	than	anticipated,	or	that	both	platforms	already	offer	sufficiently
comparable	affordances	due	to	convergence.	Gen	Z	consumers'	cross-platform	fluency	may	allow	them
to	 derive	 utilitarian	 efficiency	 from	 social	 commerce	 and	 hedonic	 enjoyment	 from	 traditional
marketplace	platforms	(Álvarez	Santamaría	et	al.,	2024;	Molinillo	et	al.,	2020).		A	value	extraction	that
transcends	 platform	 type	 and	 explains	 why	 structural	 relationships	 between	 psychographic
characteristics	and	motivations	remain	invariant	across	platform	groups.	This	platform-invariance	is
consistent	 with	 evidence	 that	 consumer	 trust	 and	 engagement	 mechanisms	 operate	 similarly	 across
advanced	 and	 emerging	 e-commerce	 markets	(Quintus	 et	 al.,	 2024),	 that	 social	 and	 facilitation	 cues
shape	 purchase	 behavior	 similarly	 across	 live-shopping	 formats	(Li	 et	 al.,	 2023)	and	 that	 user
engagement	with	interactive	platform	features	is	driven	more	by	individual	gratification-seeking	than
by	platform-specific	design	(Zhang	&	Liu,	2024).

This	study	advances	theory	in	five	interconnected	ways.	It	demonstrates	that	Generation	Z	is
psychographically	heterogeneous	rather	than	a	uniform	cohort,	positioning	generation	as	a	moderating
contextual	 backdrop	 rather	 than	 a	 primary	 classification	 variable.	 It	 extends	 dual	 value	 theory	 by
identifying	psychographic	lifestyle	patterns	as	antecedents	rather	than	mere	consequences	of	hedonic
and	 utilitarian	 motivations,	 revealing	 that	 hedonic	 orientation	 is	 more	 psychographically	 embedded
than	utilitarian	orientation.	It	contributes	to	person-situation	interactionism	by	showing	that	person-
level	 psychographic	 factors	 outweigh	 situational	 platform	 factors	 in	 shaping	 motivation,	 challenging
technology-deterministic	 assumptions	 in	 platform	 affordances	 research	(Ronzhyn	 et	 al.,	 2023).	 It
refines	dual	value	theory's	assumption	of	symmetric	motivational	influence	by	revealing	an	asymmetric
pathway	in	which	hedonic,	but	not	utilitarian,	motivation	significantly	predicts	behavioral	outcomes.
Finally,	 it	 bridges	 consumer	 behavior	 and	 platform	 research	 by	 showing	 that	 individual	 differences
prevail	 over	 platform-specific	 affordances,	 underscoring	 the	 need	 for	 user-centered	 rather	 than
platform-centric	theorizing	in	digital	commerce.
For	 practitioners,	 three	 implications	 follow	 directly	 from	 these	 findings.	 First,	 platforms	 and
marketers	 should	 abandon	 the	 assumption	 of	 a	 uniform	 Generation	 Z	 market	 and	 instead	 design
motivationally	 differentiated	 pathways	 and	 messaging	 for	 each	 psychographic	 segment:	 efficiency-
oriented	 interfaces	 and	 value-focused	 messaging	 for	 Rational	 Value	 Seekers,	 immersive	 and	 socially
engaging	experiences	for	Digital-Native	Enthusiasts,	and	trust-centered,	low-complexity	interfaces	for
Passive	 Disengaged	 Consumers.	 Second,	 because	 platform	 type	 does	 not	 moderate	 these
psychographic–motivation	 relationships,	 consumer	 insights	 and	 segmentation	 strategies	 can	 be
transferred	across	platforms,	reducing	the	cost	of	multi-platform	research	and	enabling	platforms	such
as	Shopee	and	TikTok	Shop	to	compete	by	aligning	their	overall	value	proposition	with	each	segment's
lifestyle	values	rather	than	relying	on	interface	features	alone.	Third,	practitioners	should	invest	in	real-
time	 psychographic	 inference	 systems	 that	 use	 behavioral	 data	 to	 classify	 users	 and	 dynamically
personalize	interfaces,	content,	and	promotions	at	scale	(Álvarez	Santamaría	et	al.,	2024;	Huwaida	et	al.,
2024).

CONCLUSION
This	 study	 establishes	 that	 Generation	 Z	 consumers	 in	 West	 Java	 are	 psychographically
heterogeneous,	 comprising	 three	 distinct	 segments:	Passive	 Disengaged	 Consumers,	 Rational	 Value
Seekers,	 and	 Digital-Native	 Enthusiasts,	with	 divergent	 motivational	 and	 behavioral	 profiles.
Psychographic	 characteristics	 emerge	 as	 significant	 antecedents	 of	 both	 hedonic	 and	 utilitarian
shopping	 motivations,	 with	 the	 psychographic–hedonic	 linkage	 substantially	 stronger	 than	 the
psychographic–utilitarian	 linkage,	 while	 an	 asymmetric	 structural	 pathway	 shows	 hedonic,	 but	 not
utilitarian,	 motivation	 translating	 into	 meaningful	 behavioral	 outcomes;	 platform	 type	 does	 not
moderate	these	relationships,	indicating	that	individual	psychographic	dispositions	matter	more	than
platform	context.	These	findings	contest	assumptions	of	Generation	Z	homogeneity,	extend	dual	value
theory	 by	 identifying	 its	 psychographic	 antecedents,	 and	 support	 user-centered	 over	 platform-
deterministic	approaches	to	digital	commerce	strategy,	offering	e-commerce	platforms	and	marketers
an	 actionable,	 transferable	 segmentation	 framework	 for	 differentiated	 user	 journeys	 and
communication	strategies.

Research	Limitations
Several	limitations	qualify	the	interpretation	and	generalizability	of	these	findings.	The	cross-
sectional	research	design	constrains	causal	inference:	longitudinal	studies	are	essential	to	understand
how	 psychographic	 profiles	 evolve	 across	 life	 stages	 and	 whether	 the	 identified	 segments	 represent
stable	 consumer	 types	 or	 context-specific	 configurations.	 The	 geographic	 restriction	 to	 West	 Java	 a
province	 with	 higher-than-average	 urbanization	 and	 technology	 penetration	 relative	 to	 broader
Indonesia,	limits	 generalizability	 to	 rural	 or	 less	 digitally	 developed	 regions	 where	 psychographic
profiles	and	platform	behaviors	may	differ	substantially.	The	study’s	focus	on	two	platforms	does	not
capture	 the	 full	 Indonesian	 e-commerce	 ecosystem	 including	 Tokopedia,	 Lazada,	 and	 Bukalapak,	 nor
emerging	social	commerce	formats	continuously	entering	the	market.
Methodologically,	reliance	on	self-reported	survey	data	introduces	potential	social	desirability
bias	and	common	method	variance;	future	research	would	benefit	from	triangulating	survey	responses
against	objective	behavioral	data	from	platform	analytics.	Future	research	could	profitably	examine:	(1)
segment-specific	 differential	 responses	 to	 targeted	 marketing	 interventions	 including	 influencer
formats,	gamification,	and	user-generated	content;	(2)	multi-platform	usage	patterns	and	motivational
switching	 across	 platform	 contexts;	 (3)	 cross-generational	 comparison	 to	 isolate	 distinctively	 Gen	 Z
characteristics	from	broader	consumer	psychology	patterns;	and	(4)	experimental	designs	to	establish
causal	pathways	from	psychographic-based	interventions	to	engagement	and	conversion	outcomes.
