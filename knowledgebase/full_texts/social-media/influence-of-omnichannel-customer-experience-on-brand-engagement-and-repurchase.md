Abstract
The explosive growth of digital technology, e-commerce platforms, mobile apps, social commerce and brick-and-
mortar stores has radically changed consumer behaviour in India. The omnichannel customer experience has
become a strategic strategy in this changi ng marketplace, integrating different customer touchpoints to create
smooth, consistent and customised brand encounters. This study investigates the impact of omnichannel customer
experience on brand engagement and repurchase intention of Indian consumers. The key characteristics of
omnichannel customer experience i.e. channel integration, service consiste ncy, convenience, personalisation,
information accessibility and responsiveness are discussed. Brand engagement refers to the cognitive, emotional
and behavioural engagements of customers with brands; repurchase intention refers to consumers’ readiness to
buy products or services from the same brand in the future. Special focus is given to Indian consumer context
which is marked by increasing smartphone penetration , increasing use of digital payments, diversified customer
preferences and cohabitation of traditional retail and digital purchasing channels. The study suggests that a
favourable and integrated omnichannel experience promotes consumers’ psychological connection with
companies, increases engagement and stimulates repeat purchasing behaviour. In addition, brand engagement is
identified as a potential mediating variable that explains the effect of an omnichannel customer experience on
repurchase intention. The proposed research may use a quantitative approach with structured questionnaires
administered to Indian customers who have experience of both online and offline purchasing channels and
appropriate statistical tools to evaluate the correlations among the dimensions. The results are intended to offer
useful insights for marketers and retail  managers interested in developing customer -centric omnichannel
strategies, strengthening brand relationships and improving customer retention in the highly competitive Indian
market.

I. Introduction
The Indian retail sector has been swiftly altered by digital technologies, changing the way consumers
discover, assess, purchase and connect with brands. The rising convergence of e -commerce websites, mobile
applications, social media platforms, digital pa yment methods and physical shopfronts has increased the
significance of omnichannel customer experience. Omnichannel retailing focuses on the integration of different
channels to deliver a seamless and easy customer experience, whereas multichannel retaili ng is focused on
operating through several channels (Verhoef et al., 2015). The customer experience includes several touchpoints
and stages of the purchasing process and its management is a strategic focus for contemporary companies (Lemon
& Verhoef, 2016).

The omnichannel customer experience is about how customers feel about convenience, customisation,
consistency of information, speed of service response, and interaction between online and offline channels. The
research illustrates that the combination of channels can affect customers’ perception, satisfaction and behavioural
intentions (Hossain et al., 2020; Mishra et al., 2022). We are observing a rising trend in India of people mixing
their digital and physical shopping journeys, including looking up things online before purchasing them at a store,
or using mobile apps to o rder after physically inspecting the product. These behaviours indicate the need for
integrated shopping journeys that respond to the shifting expectations of consumers. The Indian omnichannel
research has looked at the impact of technological and behavioural aspects on purchase intention (Singla et al.,
2022).
Brand engagement is the level to which consumers are cognitively, emotionally and behaviourally  committed to
a brand. Consumers may interact with the brand again through product evaluations, involvement on social media,
referrals and other brand -related actions if they have good experiences with a company. Customer experience
management is thus intimately connected to the establishment of meaningful interactions between consumers and
enterprises (Lemon & Verhoef, 2016). Moreover, a consistent and integrated brand experience can reinforce
customers’ views of value and make them more inclined to contin ue engaging with a brand .
Repurchase intention is the consumer’s willingness to buy again from the same brand. It is a key
determinant of the likelihood of retaining customers and of the long-term performance of the firm. Recent studies
show that the combination of online and offli ne channels may enhance patronage intentions through increased
customer empowerment and satisfaction (Mishra et al., 2022).
However, greater analysis is required to understand the relationship between omnichannel customer
experience, brand engagement and repurchase intention in the heterogeneous Indian consumer market. A better
knowledge of this link can help firms design integ rated marketing strategies which will lead to increased
engagement, foster repeat purchase and strengthen long-term customer relationships (Mishra et al., 2021; Sharma
et al., 2025). Thus, the current study explores the impact of omnichannel customer exper ience on brand
engagement and repurchase intention among Indian customers, emphasising the relevance of consistent,
convenient and customised interactions across channels.
Omnichannel customer experience is an essential part of contemporary marketing management. This is
due to the rapid development of digital platforms and changing consumer expectations. While Indian customers
are interacting with brands across websites, mobile apps, social media and physical outlets, providing a seamless,
consistent and personalised  experience across various touchpoints remains a key problem for businesses.
Inconsistent messaging, broken customer journeys and service level differences can erode brand engagement and
lower consumer repeat purchase intent. The expanding significance of omnichannel strategies calls for additional
empirical research on the connection between omnichannel customer experience, brand engagement, and
repurchase intention in the Indian consumer context. Moreover, there is not enough understanding of how great
omnichannel experiences reinforce the cognitive, emotional and behavioural linkages of customers with
companies. Therefore, the present study attempts to investigate the impact of omnichannel customer experience
on brand engagement and repurchase intention of Indian consumers. The findings of the study can be useful f or
the development of effective customer -centric marketing strategies and establishment of sustainable brand
relationships.

1. Examining the influence of various dimensions of omnichannel customer experience on brand
engagement among Indian consumers.
Discussion of the Objective 1
With firms interacting with consumers through websites, mobile applications, social media platforms, and
physical retail outlets, omnichannel customer experience has become an essential component of modern marketing
strategy. As opposed to traditional multichannel marketing, omnichannel shopping integrates various touchpoints
to provide a seamless and convenient consumer journey (Verhoef et al., 2015). Customer experience is built up
through various encounters across the whole customer journey, so the synchronisation of these touchpoints is vital
for good customer relationship management (Lemon & Verhoef, 2016).

Channel integration is a key factor of the omnichannel customer experience, allowing consumers to move
seamlessly between online and offline platforms with minimal disturbance. Research shows that channel
integration influences consumer experience, perceiv ed convenience, contentment and behavioural  intentions
(Chung et al., 2022; Gao et al., 2021). Moreover, integration quality, such as content consistency, service
availability, and process coordination, can increase consumer engagement and stimulate repurchase intention (Lee
et al., 2019).

Another important feature is personalisation. Consumers increasingly want appropriate product
recommendations, personalised offers and communication in line with their preferences. Effective customisation
increases perceived value and reinforces the psychological link between consumers and brands (Lemon &
Verhoef, 2016). Similarly, convenience, access to information and consistent delivery of service reduce customer
effort and lead to good shopping experie nces. An omnichannel customer experience multidimensional approach
also considers customer service, product availability, pricing consistency, privacy, shipping and return processes
as major evaluative factors. Brand engagement is the cognitive, emotional and behavioural investment of
consumers into the brand. It extends the purchase and includes engagement with brand content, sharing
experiences, recommending items, and participating in brand communities (Brodie  et al., 2011; Hollebeek et al.,
2014). Positive, integrated customer experiences may function to enhance such connections through improving
trust, contentment and perceived relational worth. However, integration does not ensure a great experience by
itself, as the quality and relevance of individual touchpoints also matter (Lemon & Verhoef, 2016).
In India, where internet shopping coexists alongside traditional retail, an exploration of these
characteristics may offer significant insights into various consumer expectations. Therefore, this purpose is to
identify the dimensions of omnichannel customer experience that have a substantial impact on brand engagement
among Indian consumers. The insights should help marketers prioritise customer -centric integration, appropriate
customisation, and consistent service delivery to develop better brand relationships.

2. Assessing the impact of omnichannel customer experience on repurchase intention among Indian
consumers.
Discussion of the Objective 2
Omnichannel customer experience has become an important determinant of consumer choice and
repurchase behaviour in today’s shopping. Consumers can interact with brands at connected touchpoints through
websites, mobile apps, social media, digital payment systems and physical shopfronts. Studies have demonstrated
that the successful integration of online and physical  channels can improve consumer experience, contentment,
and patronage intentions (Verhoef et al., 2015; Chung et al., 2022). Repurchase intention is the consumer's
intention to buy from the same brand again, indicating the prospect of continued customer connections. The
elements of channel integration, convenience, consistency of information, customisation , service quality and
reliability of fulfilment are significant to impact repurchase intention. This means more flexibility and
convenience in purchasing when consumers can look for things online, check them out in real stores and finish
their purchases th rough their preferred channels. Research showed that the quality of channel integration
positively affects consumer engagement and, consequently, enhances repurchase intention and positive word -of-
mouth (Lee et al., 2019). The combination of channel and logistics services can also improve customer satisfaction
and drive repurchase (Shi et al., 2020). Omnichannel experiences can convert into repurchase intention through
fundamental mechanisms such as customer happiness and trust.
Consistent product information, transparent pricing, reliable delivery, fast customer assistance and
hassle-free returns can lower perceived risk and increase confidence in a company. Moreover, the customer
journey viewpoint additionally points out that th e experiences before, during and after a purchase all contribute
to shaping the subsequent consumer behaviour (Lemon & Verhoef, 2016).
The Indian consumer market provides an appropriate context to explore these linkages, as internet
commerce is increasingly co -existing with traditional retail models. Consumers can compare pricing on e -
commerce platforms, examine products in real locations , and purchase via mobile applications. But disjointed
information, unreliable service, delayed delivery and complex return procedures may dissuade the customer from
buying again. In India, recent studies have explored the relationship between omnichannel retailing and consumer
purchase intention, emphasising the significance of integration, convenience, trust, and perceived value (Joseph,
2025).

3. Studying the Relationship between Brand Engagement and Consumers Repurchase Intention
Interacting with Brands via Integrated Online and Offline Channels

Discussion of the Objective 3
With the development of contemporary marketing, brand engagement has become an important term to
denote the cognitive, emotional and behavioural involvement of consumers with brands. Omnichannel retailing is
where consumers interact with brands across websites, mobile apps, social media and physical stores, giving
several opportunities to form meaningful relationships. Customer involvement is view ed as a multidimensional
construct that goes beyond purchase to encompass active participation and relationship connections (Brodie et al.,
2011; Vivek et al., 2012).
The intention to repurchase is the willingness of consumers to buy items or services from the same brand
in future. Brand interaction may increase this desire by enhancing emotional attachment, perceived value, brand
familiarity and trust. Research indicat es that engaged customers are more likely to engage in brand -related
activities and create deeper bonds with the firm (Hollebeek et al., 2014; Pansari  & Kumar, 2017). Ongoing
interactions across digital and physical touchpoints in multichannel contexts can even further strengthen these
relationships. The empirical data has demonstrated that the quality of channel integration promotes consumer
engagement, which then affects repurchase intention and positive word-of-mouth (Lee et al., 2019).
Relationship marketing theory can also be used to explain the relationship of engagement and repurchase
intention. Trust and commitment are the factors that lead consumers to maintain important relationships with
brands (Morgan & Hunt, 1994). Similarly, po sitive past experiences and customer satisfaction might also boost
the repurchase intentions (Oliver, 1999). However, engagement does not automatically lead to repurchase, since

4. Exploring  the mediatin g effect of brand engagement on omnichannel customer experience and
repurchase intention of Indian consumers

Discussion of Objective 4
As companies merge digital platforms, mobile apps, social media and real retail outlets, the omnichannel
customer experience has become a key strategic driver of consumer behaviour . Consumers’ views, emotional
responses and their future buying decisions can be affected by a frictionless experience across various touchpoints
(Verhoef et al., 2015; Lemon & Verhoef, 2016). However, the impact of customer experience on repurchase
intention may be partly mediated by consumers’ psychological and behavioural engagement with a brand.
Brand engagement refers to the cognitive, emotional and behavioural participation of customers in
encounters with the brand (Brodie et al., 2011; Hollebeek  et al., 2014). Positive experiences of ease,
personalisation, consistent information and prompt service may motivate customers to engage more often with
companies, engage in online communities, exchange recommendations and establish deeper brand relations hips.
The research on omnichannel shopping suggests that the quality of channel integration can improve consumer
engagement, which in turn leads to repurchase intention and positive word -of-mouth (Lee et al., 2019).
The relationship marketing and customer experience perspectives can be used to understand the mediating
role of brand engagement. Effective cross -channel interactions can build trust, contentment and perceived value,
and, in turn, motivate customers to pay  more attention and emotional connection to a brand (Morgan & Hunt,
1994; Pansari & Kumar, 2017). This involvement can enhance consumers’  repurchase intention as they build
more meaningful ties with brands rather of assessing each transaction in isolation. Moreover, recent empirical

The Indian consumer market, characterised by coexistence of traditional retail and fast-growing digital commerce,
is a useful backdrop to study these linkages. Consumers can find businesses on social media, compare products
online, visit brick -and-mortar stores and buy through mobile apps. When such interactions are integrated and
made interesting  they might lead customers to establish brand relationships and see potential purchase
opportunities. But the power of this mediating mechanism needs to be empirically tested and not assumed.
Therefore, the present study attempts to investigate the mediating role of brand engagement on the
relationship between omnichannel customer experience and repurchase intention of Indian consumers. The
findings could help marketers understand how seamless customer experiences translate into repeat purchasing
behaviour and design more effective, engagement-oriented omnichannel strategies.
Methodological Recommendation To assess this aim, a mediation model can be used, where omnichannel
customer experience is the independent variable, brand engagement is the mediator and repurchase intention is
the dependent variable. The indirect effect can  be examined using structural equation modelling (SEM) or
PROCESS Model 4 with bootstrapped confidence intervals. The empirical study needs to distinguish between
direct, indirect and total effects to be able to identify the presence of mediation and if it  is partial or full.

5. Identifying the Key Dimensions of Omnichannel Customer Experience Such as Channel Integration,
Convenience, Personalisation and Service Consistency That Have Substantial Impact on Brand
Engagement and Repurchase Intention
Discussion of Objective 5
Omnichannel customer experience is a multifaceted entity influencing consumers’ interactions,
emotional connections, and future purchasing decisions. The convergence of physical shopfronts, e -commerce
platforms, mobile applications and social media has alt ered contemporary retailing through seamless consumer
journeys (Verhoef et al., 2015; Beck & Rygl, 2015). Knowledge of relative significance of the experiential
elements is vital in formulating effective customer  centric marketing strategies (Lemon & Verhoef, 2016;
Homburg et al., 2017).
Channel integration helps in the seamless movement between online and offline channels with
synchronised product information, inventory availability, price and transactions. Successful integration improves
consumer experience and builds brand relationships (Herhausen et al., 2015; Gao et al., 2021). The research further

Convenience is the degree to which consumers can obtain products, information, transactions and services with
the least amount of effort. Integrated shopping environments can increase perceived convenience, satisfaction,
and patronage intentions (Chung et al., 2022; Huré et al., 2017). Likewise, positive shopping experiences could
enhance perceived value and drive continuing brand engagement ( Rose et al., 2012; Grewal et al., 2017).
Personalisation includes personalised advice, suitable promotional material and individualised services on
consumer preferences. These kinds of experiences can increase consumers' cognitive and emotional involvement
with brands (Hollebeek et al., 2014; Brodie et al., 2011). Personalised inter actions can also result in greater
customer relationships and engagement -related behavioural outcomes (Pansari & Kumar, 2017; Rahman et al.,
2025).
Service consistency is about delivering consistent information, reliable support, transparent policies, and
reliable fulfilment across channels. Consistent service delivery may reduce perceived uncertainty and increase
consumer confidence (Neslin et al., 2 006; Piotrowicz & Cuthbertson, 2014). Research on omnichannel customer
experience has also stressed the importance of consistency, customer service and fulfilment as experiential
characteristics (Rahman et al., 2022).
In the Indian environment where internet commerce is co -existing with traditional retailing, these factors
may affect the consumers differently. Hence, the aim is to empirically test their respective impact on brand
engagement and repurchase intention. The  insights may enable marketers to focus impactful omnichannel
operations, improve consumer relationships, and support sustainable repeat purchase behaviour.

II. Conclusion
Omnichannel customer experience is a driver of brand engagement and repurchase intention in the Indian
market. Channel integration (online and offline), ease, customisation and service consistency can all contribute to
seamless customer journeys and meaningful brand encounters. Brand engagement could be a potential mediating
mechanism through which great customer experiences could promote repeat purchasing behaviour  and create
long-term customer connections. Digital technologies, along with tailored services and dependable customer
support, are the key to addressing all the different consumer demands in India’s expanding retail space. The
proposed study builds a conc eptual framework of the links between omnichannel customer experience, brand
engagement and repurchase intention. Empirical validation will assist uncover the most influential factors and
enable effective customer-centric marketing strategies for lasting competitive advantage.
