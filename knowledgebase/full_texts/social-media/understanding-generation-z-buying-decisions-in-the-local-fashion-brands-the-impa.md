Abstract
The increasing competition in Indonesia’s local fashion industry requires brands to strengthen
their marketing strategies and better understand consumer behavior. Despite its rapid store
expansion, Suko experienced declining sales, indicating a gap between business growth and
consumer buying decisions. Furthermore, previous studies have reported inconsistent findings
regarding the impact of social media marketing on buying decisions. Grounded in the Theory
of Planned Behavior (TPB), this study examines how social media marketing, brand image,
and product quality impact the buying decisions of Generation Z consumers in Surabaya
toward Suko products. A quantitative method was conducted through a survey questionnaire
involving 145 respondents aged 17–29 years in Surabaya who had previously buying and used
Suko clothing products. The respondents were selected through purposive sampling and were
processed using PLS -SEM. The results show that social media ma rketing, brand image, and
product quality simultaneously and partially have a positive and significant impact on buying
decisions, with brand image having the strongest impact. These findings extend the
application of the TPB framework in the context of local fashion brands and provide practical
insights for Suko to strengthen its marketing strategies by enhancing social media
engagement, a positive brand image, and reliable product quality to shaping Generation Z
buying decisions.

LITERATURE REVIEW
Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB)
The Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB)
proposed by Ajzen (1991) was developed
to address behaviors that are not entirely
under an individual’s con trol by incur-
porating the concept of perceived beha -
vioral control. This theory assumes that
behavior is primarily influenced by beha -
vioral intention, which reflects an indi -
vidual’s readiness to perform a parti cular
action. TPB consists of three key
components: attitude toward behavior,
subjective norm, and perceived behavioral
control. Attitude toward behavior describes
how a person evaluates whether a behavior
is positive or negative. Subjective norm
refers to social pressure felt from people
around t hem, while perceived behavioral
control reflects how easy or difficult a
person believes it is to perform  a certain
behavior. According to Ajzen & Fishbein
(2005), these three components are shaped
by underlying beliefs, namely behavioral
beliefs, normative beliefs, and control
beliefs. Combined, these aspects offer a
broad explanation of how personal
judgment and social influences affect the
way individual make decisions.

Social Media Marketing
Social media marketing can be defined
as the use of o nline platforms to promote
products, services, or brands through
interactive and content -based marketing
strategies. According to Gunelius (2011),
social media marketing aims to build
awareness, recognition, and consumer
memory by leveraging various platfo rms
such as social networks and content
sharing media. Beyond promotion, it
emphasizes engagement and relationship -
building between brands and their
audiences.
Furthermore, Meliawati et al. (2023)
highlight that social media marketing
focuses on establish ing meaningful
connections with target consumers’ to
foster trust, understanding, and long -term
loyalty. This approach enables brands to
communicate more personally and
responsively compared to traditional
marketing channels. Key indicators of
social media marketing include content
creation, content sharing, connect ing, and
community building (Gunelius, 2011).

Brand Image
Brand image represents consumers’
perceptions and beliefs about a brand,
formed through associations stored in their
memory. According to Keller (1993), these
associations shape how consumers’ under-
stand and judge a brand, which later affects

Product Quality
Product quality is commonly unde r-
stood as the ability of a product to meet or
exceed consumer expectations. Garvin
(1988) defines product quality from a user
based perspective, emphasizing that
quality is determined by consumers’
subjective evaluations based on their expe -
riences and preferences. As consumer
needs vary, perceptions of quality are
influenced by how well a product’s attri -
butes satisfy those needs.
In the context of fashion products,
quality is often reflected in tangible attri -
butes such as  fabric type, stitching,
durability, and comfort. These attributes
directly affect user experience and  overall
satisfaction. According to Gutkevych et al.
(2025), key indicators of product quality
include performance, reliability, durability,
and aesthetics. These indicators highlight
both functional and visual aspects of a
product.

Buying Decisions
Buying decision is generally described
as a psychological process in which
consumers’ pass through several stages,
starting from recognizing a problem
recognition, search ing for information,
evaluating available alternatives, making
the final buying, and ending with post-
buying evaluation. Firms are expected to
develop a comprehensive understanding of
how consumers’ make decisions, starting
from how they learn about the products,
evaluate alternatives brands, and
eventually decide where to make their
buying (Kotler et al., 2022).
Moreover, consumer buying decisions
are shaped by both internal and external
determinants. Internal determinants include
a personal aspects such  as beliefs, know -
ledge, attitudes, personality, lifestyle,
personal figures, and social status. Mean -
while, external determinants are associated
with the surrounding environ ment,
including social class, cultural influences,
and reference groups, which al so strongly
affect consumer behavior (Hanaysha,
2022; Sun et al., 2023).

that social media marketing does not
significantly impact buying decisions.
These inconsistent findings suggest that
the further investigation is needed
effectiveness of social media marketing
may vary across different industries and
consumer characteristics. Furthermore,
limited research has examined this
relationship in local fashion brands
targeting Generation Z consumers.

Brand Image on Buying Decisions
Previous research indicates that brand
image is closely associated with con -
sumers’ buying decisions. In increasingly
competitive markets, firms are required to
differentiate their offerings  by developing
unique product characteristics that
distinguish them from competitors, thereby
strengthening brand image and ultimately
enhancing buying decisions (Chaudhry &
Kumar, 2024). In line with these results,
Yuliani et al. (2026) also confirms that
brand image significantly influences
Generation Z’s buying decisi ons through
both experiential and digital mechanisms.
The experiential aspect comes from direct
interactions with product quality that shape
brand perceptions, while the digital aspect
is driven by social media engagement that
supports brand image formation.
However, Suardana et al. (2024) have
found that brand image does not signi -
ficantly impact buying decisions. These
inconsistent findings suggest that the
effectiveness of brand image may different
across product categories, industries, and
consumer grou ps. Moreover, limited
research has focused on local fashion
brands and Generation Z consumers.
Therefore, the following hypothesis is
proposed:
H2: Brand Image is expected to have a
positive and significant impact  on buying
decisions.

Product Quality on Buying Decisions
Previous studies by Naufal et al. (2025)
shows that product quality has a positive
and significant impact on buying decisions.
Their findings indicate that consumers’ are
more likely to choose fashion products that
provide comfort, durability, and attractive
design, highlighting the important role of
perceived quality in influencing buying
decisions. Similarly, product quality  has a
positive and significantly impact on
buying decisions, emphasizing that fashion
products should not only follow trends but
also align with consumer preferences and
use high quality materials (Singh, 2025).
However, Ramadiana  & Paludi (2022)
have found  that product quality does not
have a significant impact on buying
decisions. These inconsistent findings
suggest that the influence of  product
quality may differ depending on  the
industry and consumer preferences. There-
fore, further investigation is needed,
particularly within the local fashion
industry, to better understand how product
quality affects the purch ase decisions of
Generation Z consumers. Therefore, the
following hypothesis is proposed:
H3: Product Quality is expected to have
a positive and significant impact on buying
decisions.

RESEARCH METHODS
Research Design
This research uses a quantitative
method, where all variables are measured
using an ordinal Likert scale (Sugiyono,
2023). Data were collected through a
hybrid survey questionnaire distributed
online via various social media platforms
using Google Forms and offline by visiting
all Suko stores in Surabaya for four weeks.
A pre-survey was also conducted by
involving 30 respondents to assess the
validity and reliability of the questionnaire.
The population of this study was Suko
consumers in S urabaya with an infinite
population size. Purposive sampling
technique was used because the population
could not be clearly identified and
respondents had to meet specific criteria.
The sample criteria include Generation Z
respondents aged 17 -29 in Surabay a who
had previously buying and used Suko
clothing products, and also had seen Suko’s
social media content. Following the ten-
times rule suggested by Hair et al. (2022), a
minimum sample size was determined by
multiplying the number of indicators. A
total of 145 respondents were collected to
ensure sufficient data for analysis and to
anticipate potential outliers.

Data Analysis Method
The analysis was conducted using
Partial Least Squares -Structural Equation
Modeling (PLS -SEM) with supported by
SmartPLS 4,  which is suitable for testing
complex variables relationships even with a
relatively limited number of samples (Hair
et al., 2022). The variables examined in this
study are latent constructs that cannot be
observed directly and are therefore
measured through multiple indicators.
According to Hair et al. (2022), PLS-SEM
consists of two main components: the outer
model, which evaluates the relationship
between constructs and their indicators and
the inner model, which assesses the
relationships among constructs. This study
adopts a reflective measurement model,
where the indicators ar e considered
representations of the latent construct.

Measurement of Variables
This study measured constructs by
operationalizing them into specific indica -
tors to enable systematic measure ment.
Each indicator was assessed using an
ordinal Likert scale t o capture respondents
perceptions in a positive or negative way,
ranging from “strongly agree” to “strongly
disagree”. In the qualitative assessment,
each option for responses is also given a
numeric value from 1 to 5 to facilitate the
analysis of the data.
According to Nurhaliza &  Zawawi
(2025), there are four indicators that can be
used to measure social media marketing
strategies, including (1) Content Creation:
Suko’s ability to create informative,
engaging, and trend aligned content on
social media, (2 ) Content Sharing: Suko’s
social media content that attracts Genera -
tion Z consumers’ and encourages them to
share it within their communities, (3)
Connecting: Suko’s ability to establish
two-way communication with its followers
by actively responding to comments,
questions, and feedback from Generation Z
consumers’, (4) Community Building:
Suko’s initiative in fostering engagement
among social media followers through
interaction, discussion, and active parti -
cipation, thereby strengthening the rela -
tionship between the brand and Generation
Z consumers’.
Moreover, according to Nareswari &
Arindita (2024), brand image has three
indicators that represent consumer percep-
tions, including (1) Strength of Brand
Associations: Generation Z consumers’
ability to recognize and evaluate Suko
through its logo, products, and overall
appearance, which influences their percep-
tions and trust, (2) Favorable of Brand
Associations: Positive evaluations of the
Suko brand among Generation Z
consumers’ based on attributes such as
product quality, brand trust, and reputation,
(3) Uniqueness of Brand Associations:

Hypotheses Testing
In this study, hypothesis testing was
conducted using the bootstrapping method
with decision criteria used a significance
level of 5% (α = 0.05). If the t -statistic is
more than 1.96 and the p -value is less than
or equal to 0.05, then a hypothesis is
accepted (H1). On the other hand, a hypo -
thesis is disproved (H0) if the t -statistic
value is less than 1.96 and the p -value is
higher than or equal to 0.05.
The hypothesis testing results revealed
that all independents variables have a
positive and significant impact on Buying
Decisions. First, Social Media Marketing
had a path coefficient of 0.334, with a T -

Marketing has a positive and significant

that Brand Image also has a positive and

Discussion
The Impact of Social Media Marketing
on Buying Decisions
According to the hypothesis analysis in
this study, it is clearly show that Social
Media Marketing has a positive and
significant impact on Generation Z consu -
mers’ in Surabaya’s buying decisions for
Suko products. This outcome in line with
Gunelius (2011), who argues that marke -
ting activities through social media
platforms aim for raising brand awareness,
recognition, and customer response
towards products.
Previous studies by Angelica & Tj
(2025); Nurs iti & Giovenna (2022);
Pradana et al. (2025); Pervin & Khan
(2025) also support this finding, which
found that Social Media Marketing has
significant impact on buying decisions
through attractive contents, strategic
promotions, and active consumer engage -
ment. However, the result differs from
Rakhmawati (2023) who found that there
is no significant impact between social

TIONS
Conclusion
This study confirms that Social Media
Marketing, Brand Image, and Product
Quality are key elements that affected to the
Generation Z consumer’s in Surabaya
buying decisions toward Suko products.
Social Media marketing is impactive in
attract consumer interest, boosting
curiousity, and build engagemengt through
interactive content and communication
social media platforms. Moreover, Brand
Image is crucial in influencing how
consumers’ view  products. A strong,
favorable, and unique brand image allows
Generation Z consumers’ to  recognize
Suko easily, develop trust in the brand, and
feel assured in their choice of its products
over those of competitors.  Product Quality
is also plays a vital role, as characteristics
like comfort, durability, and appealing
designs contribute to customer satisfaction
and enhance the buying decisions.
Among the three variables, brand image
shows the strongest impact on buying
decisions. This indicates that for
Generation Z consumers, how perceptions
and symbolic value attached to the brand
carry more weight than promotional
exposure or even product attributes alone.
A strong brand image builds trust, reduces
uncertainty, and creates emotional
connection, making consumers more
confident in their choices. These findings
also extend the Theory of Planned
Behavior by illustrating how buying
decisions are shaped not only by individual
perceptions, but also by the combined
influence of external marketing stimuli.

Limitations of the Study
This research presents several limi ta-
tions that need to be recognized. First, the
research focuses on a limited range of
factors, specifically Social Media Marke -
ting, Brand Image, and Product  Quality,
while neglecting other potentially influen-
tial factors such as price, promotion, and
lifestyle in the analysis. Second, the scope
of respondents is relatively narrow, as this
study only involves Generation Z
consumers’ of Suko products who are
domiciled in Surabaya. Consequently, the
findings may not accurately reflect
consumer behavior in different geographi -
cal areas or among various demographic
segments.

Managerial Recommendations
Suko is encouraged to strengthen its
marketing and product development
strategies by enhancing product quality
through better material selection, improved
durability, and designs that align with
consumer preferences. At the same time, it
is important to maintain a clear and
distinctive brand identity, while also
strengthening engagement on social media
through more interactive content that
encourages audience participation. These
efforts are expected to impr ove customer
satisfaction, shape stronger brand percep -
tions, and ultimately encourage more
positive buying decisions among Genera -
tion Z consumers.

Policy Recommendations
Stakeholders in the local fashion
industry are encouraged to support
programs that help small and emerging
brands improve product quality standards
and strengthen their digital marketing
capabilities. This can be done through
training initiatives, certification support,
and better access to creative industry
resources. Such support would  help local
businesses become more competitive while
also building stronger consumer trust and a
more sustainable fashion ecosystem.

Academic Recommendations
This study contributes to the discussion
on consumer behavior by highlighting how
social media ma rketing, brand image, and
product quality can significantly impact
buying decisions among Generation Z.
Future research is encouraged to extend the
current model by incorporating additional
variables that may influence buying
