Social media influencer marketing has emerged as a dominant promotional
strategy in the food industry, yet the academic literature on its effects on
Generation Z (Gen Z) consumers remains fragmented across disciplines. This
review systematically synthesizes existing empirical and theoretical evidence
on how social media influencer marketing shapes Gen Z consumer behaviour
in the food industry, with a focus on identifying key mechanisms of influence,
boundary conditions, and gaps in current knowledge. A syst ematic literature
review following PRISMA 2020 guidelines was conducted across seven
academic databases covering the period 2015 -2025. After screening 253
initial records and removing duplicates, 20 peer-reviewed studies meeting all
inclusion criteria were retained for full synthesis. Three thematic dimensions
emerged: (1) consumer attitudes, purchase intentions, and food choices; (2)
influencer credibility, trust, and parasocial relationships; and (3) content,
platform, and endorser characteristics. Findin gs consistently indicate that
influencer effects on food behaviour are predominantly indirect, mediated by
parasocial relationships, trustworthiness, and consumer attitude. Micro -
influencers are comparably effective to macro -influencers for informational
content, and food type moderates engagement. Platform diversity remains
under-researched, with Instagram dominating 70% of included studies.
Influencer marketing exerts a significant but context-dependent influence on
Gen Z food behaviour. Food brands shoul d prioritise endorser
trustworthiness over follower scale. Critical gaps persist around TikTok, male
Gen Z consumers, cross -cultural contexts, longitudinal designs, and ethical
dimensions of marketing unhealthy food to young audiences.

1. Introduction
The digital revolution of the 21st century has fundamentally changed the marketing communications
landscape and social media have become the dominant channels through which brands communicate
with consumers. Marketing through social media has grown from a speciality strategy to an industry
worth billions of dollars, transforming brand-audience engagement [24]. The food industry is a prime
example of this transformation, as visually compelling content, lifestyle storytelling, and the social

dimension of eating combine to create fertile ground for influencer -driven campaigns. Instagram,
TikTok, and YouTube are dominated by food -related content, from recipe tutorials to restaurant
reviews to “what I eat in a day” vlogs [21].
Generation Z (born 1997-2012) represents a large and commercially significant consumer population.
As digital natives who have never known a world without the internet, Gen Z consumers are sceptical
of mainstream advertising, prefer authenticity and transp arency, and place considerable trust in
recommendations from peers and the influencers they follow . The food attitudes of this cohort are
equally distinctive — they are more conscious about health, sustainability, and ethical food
consumption, and are drawn to new, visually shareable food experiences. Therefore, the specific
impact of influencer market ing on Gen Z’s attitudes, purchase intentions, and actual consumption
habits is of critical importance both academically and for industry practitioners.
Although influencer marketing scholarship is advancing rapidly, the body of research remains
disjointed, especially at the intersection of the three specific domains of interest — influencer
marketing, Gen Z consumer behaviour, and food. Various studies ha ve investigated how influencer
credibility influences purchasing intentions [ 5] and the food media consumption of Gen Z [7], but
fewer studies have systematically combined these two aspects within the food context. Food poses
unique challenges for influencer marketing — food choices are highly personal, culturally situated,
and sometimes connected to health outcomes, making me chanisms of influence (e.g., trust,
parasocial relationships, perceived authenticity) more complex than in other product categories [11].
Furthermore, there has been insufficient research on how content type, platform affordances, or the
ethical aspects of marketing potentially unhealthy foods to young audiences moderate this
relationship [14].
This systematic review is aimed at bringing together and critically evaluating the fragmented
evidence on this subject. The major focus is to offer a structured and evidence -based picture of the
state of the art and to highlight existing relationships, con troversial results, and gaps that require
further investigation. Against this background, this review is guided by the following research
questions:
RQ1: What is the nature and direction of the effect of social media influencer marketing on Gen Z
consumers’ food-related attitudes, purchase intentions, and actual food choices?
RQ2: Through what psychological mechanisms — specifically influencer credibility, trust, and
parasocial relationships — does influencer marketing exert its effect on Gen Z food consumers?
RQ3: How do content characteristics, platform affordances, and endorser type moderate the
effectiveness of influencer marketing on Gen Z food behaviour?
RQ4: What are the key gaps in the existing literature and what directions should future research
prioritise?
This review is important for several reasons. First, it offers a solid foundation for further empirical
studies by identifying key theoretical models and methodological approaches. Second, it provides
actionable insights for food companies, marketing agencies, and policymakers. Third, it advances the

2. Methodology
This systematic literature review was conducted following the PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for
Systematic Reviews and Meta -Analyses) guidelines to ensure transparency, rigour, and
reproducibility [19]. The methodology was designed to comprehensively capture and critically
appraise the existing evidence on how social media influencer marketing influences Gen Z consumer
behaviour within the food industry.
2.1 Review Protocol
The search strategy targeted peer -reviewed papers published from 2015 to 2025 to capture the
formal rise and swift development of influencer marketing as a promotional discipline. Seven major
academic databases were systematically searched: Scopus (selecte d as the primary database for its
comprehensive coverage of peer -reviewed articles from social sciences, business, and health
sciences), Web of Science (for top journals in communication and management), ScienceDirect (for
full-text articles in business, m arketing, and food science), SpringerLink (for social science and
behavioural research), IEEE Xplore (for human -computer interaction and digital media technology
fields), ACM Digital Library (for computing and social media systems research), and PubMed (fo r

2.2 Thematic Dimensions for Analysis
Four thematic dimensions were identified as shown in  the Fig 2  to organise the synthesis: (1)
Consumer Attitudes, Purchase Intentions, and Food Choices — research examining effects on
evaluative judgments, stated likelihood to purchase, or food selection behaviours; (2) Influencer
Credibility, Trust, and Parasocial Re lationships — research exploring how influencers gain influence
through expertise, trustworthiness, attractiveness, and one -sided emotional bonding; (3) Content,
Platform, and Endorser Characteristics — studies examining the moderating and mediating effects of
message features, platform affordances, and endorser type; and (4) Others — studies not clearly
fitting the major three categories, such as ethical issues or methodological innovations.

2.3 Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria
Studies were included if: published from 2015 -2025; directly addressing social media influencer
marketing and its correlation with food industry purchasing behaviour; focused on Generation Z or
youth consumers (generally 18 -27 years); comprising quantitati ve, qualitative, or mixed -methods
designs; published as peer-reviewed journal articles or conference papers indexed in Scopus or Web
of Science; and available in English. Studies were excluded if: published before 2015; lacking a social
media context; focu sed solely on influencer marketing in other product categories (fashion,

constituting editorials, opinion articles, commentaries, book chapters, dissertations, or industry

2.4 Study Selection Process
The selection process followed the PRISMA flow diagram. The initial database search yielded 253
total records. Removing 73 duplicates left 180 unique records for screening. Two independent
reviewers screened all 180 records against pre-defined criteria, rejecting 128 for being clearly out of
scope (covering general celebrity endorsement, non-food industries, and older age categories). This
left 52 records for full-text retrieval, of which 7 were inaccessible via institutional library systems. The
remaining 45 were assessed for full -text eligibility, resulting in the exclusion of 25 studies due to
insufficient focus on Gen Z, lack of methodological rigour, or predominant focus on constructs outside
the scope. Thus, 20 studies were included in the final systematic review as presented in the Fig 3.

3.3 Consumer Attitudes, Purchase Intentions, and Food Choices
The findings of the compiled studies indicate that the most commonly analysed outcome variables
are consumer attitudes, purchase intention, and actual or reported food choice. The evidence base
shows high heterogeneity in both the quality and direction of effects, depending on a variety of
moderating and mediating factors.
A wide array of studies concludes that influencer marketing can positively shape Gen Z consumers’
food-product perceptions and purchase intentions. A study of Instagram health and wellbeing
influencers showed that a positive attitude toward the influencer directly and significantly predicted
a positive attitude toward the promoted brand, which in turn predicted intention to buy organic food
[6]. This mediating effect of consumer attitude was confirmed by studies on food influencers, where
positive perception of the usefulness of influencer recommendations positively affected purchase
intent both directly and indirectly through consumer attitude [22]. Digital influencer marketing on
Instagram for healthy food also reported that attitude toward advertising on Instagram was a major
determinant of purchase intention [ 2]. These results collectively imply that influencer marketing
effectiveness is predominantly indirect, operating through positive consumer evaluations as a
psychological stepping stone to purchase behaviour.
The type of food being promoted is an important moderating variable. A study investigating the effect
of healthy versus unhealthy food images in social media found that healthy food images produced
more engagement and increased the likelihood of trying the  recommended product, attributed to
greater consumer identification with the person in the image [ 1]. This suggests that health -related
food content may be more persuasive, resonating with Gen Z’s aspirational self-image. In contrast, a
study of Gen Z and conspicuous food consumption found no statistically significant relationship
between type of social media use and propensity to engage in conspicuous food consumption, even
though participants acknowledged being affected by social media and influencers [20], highlighting
the complexity of influencer effects on status-oriented food purchasing.
Multiple studies confirm the link between exposure to influencer marketing and dietary choices. A
cross-sectional study found a significant relationship between BMI and viewing food advertisements
on ready -to-eat foods and food delivery apps among Gen Z [17]. A separate study found that
exposure to food vlogs statistically significantly impacted dietary behaviours and preferences, and
that the more people watched food vlogs, the less they engaged in physical activity [4]. These results
indicate that influencer marketing may shape not only food preferences but also the broader lifestyle
patterns that influence health.

Consumer involvement in the food category further moderates influencer impact. A study on
sustainable food choice found that consumers’ engagement in sustainable consumption mediated
the relationship between influencer-follower engagement, social norms, and sustainable food choice
[25]. Similarly, parasocial relationship and perceived congruence with the influencer were key drivers
of organic food adoption intentions through their effect on consumer attitude [12]. These studies
point to the fact that influencer marketing works most effectively when it activates and reinforces
pre-existing consumer values.
There are multiple mechanisms through which influencer content impacts attitudes and intentions.
Celebrity endorsers inspired greater pleasure and arousal compared to food experts on Instagram,
leading to more positive purchase intention implications [13], though this effect was moderated by
visual complexity. Additionally, a popular real fit influencer elicited stronger parasocial interactions
than a fictitious fit influencer, and parasocial interaction completely mediated the effect of influencer
type on product attitude and purchase intention [9], underscoring that authenticity and a sense of
interpersonal relationship play a central role in persuasive impact. Celebrity endorsement for healthy
food, however, showed a relatively low direct impact on purchase intention, with brand attitude
emerging as a stronger driver [18], indicating that celebrity status alone may not be sufficient in the
food domain.
3.4 Influencer Credibility, Trust, and Parasocial Relationships
One of the most prevalent themes in this systematic review relates to the underlying psychological
mechanisms through which social media influencers affect Gen Z consumers’ eating behaviours.
Credibility, trust, and parasocial relationships were consistently conceptualised as key mediators and
moderators contributing to the effectiveness of influencer marketing in the food industry.
Rooted in classical persuasion theory, the concept of source credibility has been widely validated.
Perceived credibility had a significant direct positive influence on purchase intention, with fully
mediated indirect influence through consumer involvement  and advertising attitude in digital
influencer marketing for healthy food promotion [ 2]. Research on health and wellbeing influencers
found that follower attitude toward the influencer — shaped by credibility, congruence, and physical
attractiveness — ultimately predicted brand attitude and intention to buy organic food, with physical
attractiveness showing greater impact on female consumers [6]. Some studies, however, challenged
the universally positive effect of credibility: celebrity endorsement for healthy food had a much
weaker direct impact on purchase intent [18], suggesting credibility dimensions may be more
effective for micro - or niche influencers than for celebrities in food contexts.  Fig.4 synthesises this
pattern into an emerging sequential pathway linking credibility, parasocial relationship, and trust."

Trustworthiness has been evidenced as a very strong mediator in the influencer -follower
relationship. A study investigating food influencer marketing on Instagram found that the interactive
and authentic nature of influencing had no significant direct effe cts on brand trust; all relationships
were fully mediated through the follower’s perceived trustworthiness of the influencer . This implies
that trustworthiness is the critical gateway through which influencer characteristics translate into
brand-related outcomes. Similarly, follower trust in the information provided by influencers was a
positive direct predictor of sustainable food purchasing [25]. The mediating role of trust was further
confirmed by a study demonstrating that perceived trust significantly mediated the link between
follower-influencer similarity and food choices and brand advocacy [26].
Parasocial relationships — one-sided yet emotionally intimate connections formed between media
users and on-screen figures — have been highlighted as a vital mediator. An experiment on healthy
food promotion confirmed that parasocial interaction fully medi ated the effect of influencer type
(real vs. fictitious fit influencer) on product attitude and purchase intention; those who followed a
real influencer exhibited higher levels of parasocial interaction, leading to more favourable product
perception [9]. The importance of parasocial relationship was also confirmed in the study of organic
food adoption, where it significantly predicted customer attitude, which in turn strongly drove
organic food adoption intent [12]. Table 2 summarises the key theoretical frameworks, mediating

There appears to be a sequential flow between credibility, parasocial relationships, and trust. The
literature indicates a pathway where an influencer’s attractiveness and expertise attract followers
and foster a parasocial relationship through intimacy an d emotional attachment; this parasocial
relationship in turn increases trustworthiness toward the influencer, which then shapes consumer
attitude and buying behaviour. Perceived congruence between influencer and follower enhances
both parasocial relationship and trustworthiness — for example, perceived congruence significantly
increased food choices and brand advocacy, with trust as a mediator [26]. A study further showed
that the content about food influencers substantially impacted food -related consumer behaviour
through trust and credibility [23].
3.5 Content, Platform, and Endorser Characteristics
The characteristics of food influencer marketing can be broken down into content type, platform
type, and endorser type, as illustrated in Table 3. The evidence shows how these variables interact
to determine campaign effectiveness.
Regarding content type, there is evidence suggesting that images displaying healthy food (compared
to unhealthy food) increase consumer engagement and willingness to try products [ 1]. Concerning
the entertainment versus information distinction, while macro-influencers were more effective when
content was viewed as entertainment, micro -influencers could deliver an equally effective message

content, they engage in systematic processing and focus more on content quality and source
credibility. Visual complexity also modulates effectiveness — while celebrities generally generated
more pleasure than food experts, experts showed higher pleasure values in low -complexity visual
settings, suggesting that expert credibility is accentuated when visual distractions are minimised [13].
Platform affordances influence overall campaign success. Instagram dominated the literature, with
its visual character leading to studies on visual complexity of food content [ 6,14]. TikTok, known for
short-form video and algorithm -driven virality, has begun attracting research attention [ 3]. A
significant gap in the literature is the absence of systematic comparative studies between different
platforms and how they differently influence consumer perceptions of food influencer marketing.
Regarding endorser type, differences exist between micro -, macro-, and celebrity endorsers, with
varying levels of impact contingent on content type and platform. For informational content, the
impact of macro - and micro -influencers is found to be similar [16]. Celebrity endorsers increase
arousal and pleasure, but experts can generate more influence in low-complexity contexts [13]. Real
fooders on Instagram, who act as food -domain experts advocating for healthily prepared foods,
exemplify how endorsers can build a trustworthy image through domain expertise and targeted
content strategies [10].

4. Conclusions
Drawing on a systematic literature review of twenty studies, this review explored the process by
which social media influencer marketing influences the food purchase behaviour of Gen Z consumers,
establishing that such influence is mediated by the complex interplay of credibility, trust, parasocial
relationship, and content -platform-endorser fit. The persuasiveness of influencer campaigns is

predominantly implicit — consumer responses are driven more by parasocial bonds and endorser
trustworthiness than by direct exposure — thereby debunking the widespread assumption that high
follower counts lead to immediate consumer impact. The review estab lishes clear boundaries of
endorser effectiveness, with content type, processing goals, and food categories contributing
significantly to the moderating process.
The implications for theory suggest extending classical persuasion frameworks — including source
credibility and parasocial interaction theory — into digital food environments by developing
integrated models that demonstrate the conditional and sequential interaction between credibility,
parasocial interaction, and trust. The pragmatic implications offer guidance to food marketers on
establishing authenticity and domain expertise over reach, aligning content strategies with consumer
processing goals, and ta iloring visual complexity with endorser type. For policymakers, this review
serves as a call to action to address consumer vulnerability to marketing messages — particularly
regarding body image and unhealthy food consumption — with increased scrutiny on d isclosure
requirements and restrictions on marketing to children.
Significant gaps need to be addressed in future research. Longitudinal studies that track actual dietary
behaviour change would lend critical evidence on the relationship between advertising exposure and
purchasing. Systematic platform comparisons — particularly involving TikTok and short -form video
content — are urgently needed. A greater focus on the ethical dimensions of marketing, on male Gen
Z consumers, and on cross -cultural contexts would greatly broaden understanding. Overall, the
findings of this r eview offer a foundation for academic researchers, marketers, and policymakers
striving to navigate this complex and rapidly evolving field of study.
