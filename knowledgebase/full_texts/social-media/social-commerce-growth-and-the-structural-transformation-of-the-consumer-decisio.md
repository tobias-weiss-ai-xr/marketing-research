Abstract
Social commerce the integration of social networking platforms with electronic commerce infrastructure
represents a fundamental transition in digital retail, shifting online shopping from search based,
intentional purchasing to discovery driven, community mediated consumption. This literature review
and research synthesis examines the global expansion of social commerce and evaluates how embedded
social media features reconfigure consumer decision making mechanisms. Synthesizing core
frameworks including the Stimulus Organism Response (S O R) theory, Social Support Theory, the
Consumer Decision Journey (CDJ), and Swift Guanxi, this study analyzes the psychological, affective,
and relational drivers of adoption. Global macroeconomic data projects the social commerce sector to
grow from a 2025 baseline valuation of 1.48 to 1.65 trillion to multi trillion dollar thresholds by the early
2030s, propelled by Asia Pacific market dominance, live streaming innovations, and algorithmic
personalization engines. The findings indicate that platform features compress the traditional sales
funnel by substituting deliberate information search with observational learning, parasocial creator
attachment, real time social proof, and embedded transactional checkouts. Consequently, high
involvement evaluation pathways are frequently bypassed in favor of affect driven impulse buying or
complex cross platform decision migrations. This paper unifies these dynamics into an integrated
theoretical framework, offering insights for information systems research and strategic guidance for
digital commerce practitioners.

1. Introduction
The digital commercial landscape is undergoing a structural paradigm shift driven by the convergence of
Web 2.0 social architectures and electronic commerce transactional systems [1].  Historically, electronic
commerce operated as an intentional, utility driven channel where consumers executed targeted search
queries to fulfill established purchasing needs. In contrast, social commerce (s commerce) redefines
retail as an interactive, relational, and experience centric process mediated by social media
environments. By integrating product discovery, peer recommendations, live multimedia streams, and
friction free payment settlement directly within social platforms, s commerce merges social engagement
with commercial transaction[2].
This functional convergence has catalyzed global market growth across both developed and emerging
digital economies. Valued between 1.48 trillion and 1.65 trillion globally in 2025, the social commerce

2. Theoretical Foundations of Consumer Behavior in Social Commerce
Scholars examining consumer action within social commerce synthesize theories from environmental
psychology, sociology, relationship marketing, and information systems  [8]. These complementary
theoretical lenses clarify how platform affordances transform into internal psychological states and
downstream purchasing behaviors.

3. The Stimulus Organism Response Paradigm
The Stimulus Organism Response (SOR) framework serves as a central theoretical paradigm for social
commerce literature [9]. Rooted in environmental psychology, SOR posits that environmental inputs (S)
influence an individual's internal cognitive and affective evaluation (O), which subsequently executes a
behavioral reaction (R) [10].
In social commerce, environmental stimuli (S) extend beyond traditional digital storefront cues or 4P
marketing inputs. They encompass dynamic social media affordances, including algorithmic content
feeds, real time live video, peer recommendations, verified user reviews, scarcity indicators, and creator
entertainment value [11]. These environmental stimuli concurrently trigger complex internal organismic
states (O):
Cognitive organismic processes involve perceived usefulness, review credibility, platform trust, and
situational normality assessments. Affective organismic processes encompass emotional arousal,
positive affect, parasocial attachment, a sense of virtual community belonging, and the Fear of Missing
Out (FOMO) [12]. Experiential organismic processi ng centers on the state of flow a psychological state
of deep environmental immersion and focused engagement where temporal awareness decreases.
The interaction among these organismic states determines consumer behavioral responses (R), driving
decisions such as immediate impulse purchasing, native content sharing, brand community participation,
or cross platform decision migration.

4. Social Support Theory and Social Commerce Constructs
Social Support Theory asserts that individuals in digital networks fulfill psychological needs and
mitigate uncertainty by exchanging informational and emotional resources with peers. In social
commerce platforms, social technologies provide specific operational capabilities designated as Social
Commerce Constructs (SCCs) [13].
These constructs comprise three primary platform features: Ratings and Reviews, which aggregate user
feedback to reduce product quality uncertainty; Recommendations and Referrals, which leverage
interpersonal social capital to validate purchase intentions; and Forums and Communities, which host
peer to peer discussions, shared interests, and collective product evaluation.
These social commerce constructs facilitate two distinct forms of digital social support. Informational
support consists of advice  [14], recommendations, and product usage insights shared by peer networks,
which directly reduce cognitive search costs and risk perception [15]. Emotional support involves
expressions of empathy, care, encouragement, and community approval, which enhance psychological
safety and elevate relationship quality with both the platform and merchant communities.

5. Swift Guanxi and Relational Trust Dynamics
While established relational frameworks focus on long term trust accumulation, social commerce
transactions frequently occur in volatile, fast paced environments requiring rapid decision making. To
explain these short term interactions, scholars adapt relational network theory into the concept of Swift
Guanxi[16]. Swift Guanxi describes the rapid formation of mutual understanding, psychological
closeness, and transactional trust between buyers and sellers within social platforms, particularly during
live stream broadcasts or conversational chat exchanges.
Swift Guanxi is established through antecedents including situational normality, interpersonal presence,
communication responsiveness, and perceived host authentic familiarity[17]. When live stream sellers or
micro merchants display high social presence, immediate responsiveness, and personal disclosure,
consumers experience a rapid reduction in psychological distance. This fast relational bonding bypasses
lengthy trust building cycles, directly increasing purchase intentions and word of mouth sharing
behaviors.

6. The Integrated Social Commerce Theoretical Architecture
To consolidate these distinct theoretical perspectives, recent academic literature advances the
comprehensive Social Commerce (SC) Framework  [18]. This architecture synthesizes five
interconnected dimensions that govern social commerce research and implementation:
The Social Dimension encompasses peer networks, community governance, user generated content co
creation, and word of mouth dynamics. The Commerce Dimension incorporates business models (B2C,
C2C, social reselling) [19-20], native checkouts, promotional strategies, and order fulfillment logistics.
The Technology Dimension addresses Web 2.0/3.0 architectures, interactive live streaming affordances,
mobile user interfaces, and artificial intelligence recommendation engines [21]. The Behavioral
Dimension focuses on the consumer psychological journey, capturing initial attention, emotional
arousal, cognitive trust, impulse buying, and post purchase community feedback. Finally, the Research
Dimension specifies quantitativ e and qualitative methodologies such as Partial Least Squares Structural
Equation Modeling (PLS SEM), weight analyses, and meta analytic modeling —used to evaluate
platform dynamics [22].
7. Structural Transformation of the Consumer Decision Journey
The integration of interactive social media features into digital commerce platforms fundamentally alters
the traditional five stage Consumer Decision Journey (CDJ). The linear progression from Need
Recognition to Information Search, Alternative Evaluation, Purchase Decision, and Post Purchase
Behavior has evolved into an iterative, highly compressed, and non linear journey.

In traditional e commerce, Need Recognition stems primarily from internal physical depletion or
intentional web search queries. In social commerce, need recognition is systematically activated through
predictive algorithmic curation and immersive visual content streams. Social platforms a nalyze user
engagement patterns such as view duration, interaction frequency, and gesture velocity to present highly

During the Information Search stage, consumers historically relied on merchant catalogs, official
product descriptions, and formal corporate advertisements. Social commerce replaces merchant
dominated information channels with peer driven crowdsourced validation and observational learning.
Consumers gather information by monitoring real time viewer interactions, reading uncurated  peer
comment threads, and reviewing aggregated user ratings. Through observational learning, individuals
evaluate product utility by watching the live purchasing behaviors and real time reactions of peer
consumers, significantly reducing cognitive search effort.
The Alternative Evaluation stage transitions from structured attribute matrix comparisons (such as cross
site feature and price matching) to parasocial trust alignment and community consensus heuristics.
Endorsements from micro creators, live stream demonstrations, and user social proof act as cognitive
shortcuts. The perceived authority and authenticity of the creator substitute for extensive feature by
feature evaluation. Trust transfers from the abstract corporate brand to the human creator, compressing
the evaluation phase into moments of emotional connection.
The Purchase Decision stage experiences structural funnel compression through platform affordances.
Embedded shopping tags, native in app checkouts, one click digital wallet integrations, and live flash
sales eliminate operational friction between product discovery and financial settlement. When c ombined
with affective triggers such as scarcity counters, real time stock drops, and host promotions the transition
from impulse urge to financial commitment occurs within seconds [23]. Rational, deliberative evaluation
is frequently bypassed, producing high levels of impulse buying driven by emotional arousal and flow
states.
The Post Purchase Behavior stage no longer marks the termination of the customer journey. Post
purchase satisfaction or dissatisfaction feeds directly back into the platform's social content ecosystem.
Consumers transform into active content co creators  by posting unboxing videos, tagging product
handles in aesthetic posts, writing detailed reviews, and participating in community Q&As. This user
generated content serves as the environmental stimulus (S) that initiates discovery and need recognition
for subsequent consumers, creating a continuous loop of social discovery and conversion.

8. The Risk Paradox and Cross Platform Migration Mechanics
Despite the growth of platform native shopping, empirical research reveals a complex behavioral
phenomenon known as the Social Commerce Risk Paradox [24]. Studies examining cross platform
consumer journeys demonstrate that high perceived risk on social media pl atforms stemming from data
privacy concerns, merchant legitimacy doubts,  and transaction security fears does not necessarily cause
consumers to abandon their purchasing journey.
Instead, perceived risk on social platforms paradoxically accelerates cross platform migration . Gen Z
and digitally native consumers frequently initiate product discovery (S), emotional engagement, and peer
evaluation (O) within immersive social media channels such as TikTok, Instagram, or Xiaohongshu .
However, when evaluating purchase risk, consumers migrate across digital environments to complete the
transaction on established central e marketplaces (such as Amazon or Shopee) or official brand
storefronts. Centralized e marketplaces provide buyer protection guarantees, established fulfillment
tracking, and reliable refund infrastructure that offset social platform transaction risks. Consequently,
social platforms serve as primary interest generation channels, while transactional completion depends
on the total trust architecture across connected digital ecosystems.

9. Feature Level Affordances and Psychological Mechanisms
To understand how social platforms alter consumer behavior, specific technological affordances must be
mapped to their underlying psychological constructs and empirical outcomes.
9.1.Video Commerce and Live Streaming Affordances
Live streaming and short form video commerce represent the fastest growing channels within global
social commerce, capturing 43.22% of total market revenue  [25]. Live video platforms combine real
time visual demonstration, host banter, dynamic chat feeds, and time sensitive promotional cues.
High visual quality and host entertainment value generate a psychological state of flow, characterized by
heightened sensory focus and enjoyment. In a flow state, consumers exhibit reduced price sensitivity and
diminished analytical reflection, increasing purchase openness. Concurrent real time chat interactions
and host shout outs foster strong social presence—the perception of warm, direct human contact within a

9.2.Algorithmic Personalization Engines
Modern social media platforms use machine learning algorithms to curate individualized content feeds
based on real time engagement telemetry. Algorithmic recommendation systems assess micro behaviors
such as video rewatch loops, pause duration, comment interactions, and direct shares to infer latent
consumer preferences [26].
This continuous personalization creates persona perception alignment, where consumers encounter
products that match their lifestyle, aesthetic preferences, and subcultural identities. Algorithmic curation
presents products seamlessly within organic entertainment feeds, lowering cognitive defense barriers.
Consumers experience discovery as serendipitous personal alignment rather than commercial targeting,
accelerating the shift from passive viewing to active purchase consideration.
9.3.Ratings, Reviews, and Peer Community Features
Platform features designed around user generated feedback function as fundamental trust building
mechanisms in social commerce. Aggregate star ratings, unfiltered customer review threads, and user
uploaded media reduce product quality uncertainty prior to purchase.
Peer reviews deliver informational social support by offering real world product usage evaluations,
sizing feedback, and performance critiques  [27]. This peer generated intelligence mitigates the sensory
absence inherent in online shopping. Furthermore, high review volumes and active community
discussions establish social proof, reassuring potential buyers through demonstrated peer consensus and
lowering perceived risk.
9.4.Creator Economies and Parasocial Affordances
Digital creators and micro influencers serve as central relational intermediaries in social commerce
ecosystems. Regular content publishing and personal disclosure foster parasocial interaction, where
followers experience an asymmetric feeling of personal friendship and psychological connection with
the creator.
When creators demonstrate products, endorsements benefit from emotional attachment and parasocial
trust. Consumers often transfer trust from the creator directly to the recommended brand. This parasocial
connection shifts consumer cognitive processing from central, systematic evaluation to peripheral, trust
based heuristics. Consequently, follower purchase decisions are driven by personal alignment with the
creator rather than objective product feature analysis.
9.5.Conversational Messaging and Chat Commerce

WeChat, and direct messaging to guide consumers through discovery, inquiry, payment, and post

conversational AI bots can instantly answer product queries, send custom catalog links, and offer
tailored promotional incentives. This two way communication builds Swift Guanxi by demonstrating

10. Regional Heterogeneity and Regulatory Compliance Dynamics
The evolution of social commerce displays operational heterogeneity shaped by regional digital
infrastructure, cultural expectations, and national regulatory oversight.
In the Asia Pacific region, platform architecture favors super app models that integrate content, social
networking, live streaming, and native digital payments into unified applications. China exemplifies this
native ecosystem integration, where applications like WeChat, Douyin  [28], and Xiaohongshu facilitate
complete, enclosed consumer decision journeys. In contrast, emerging South Asian markets like India
demonstrate a messaging led commerce model. Indian social commerce frequently initiates through
discovery on visually oriented channels (Instagram, Facebook) but hands off execution to WhatsApp for
personalized merchant interaction, catalog negotiation, and transaction fulfillment. This conversational
format aligns with traditional retail preferences for assisted selling and personal merchant relationships.
In Western markets (North America and Western Europe), social commerce historically developed
through referral based social advertising and creator affiliate storefronts. However, Western platforms
are rapidly deploying platform native shops (such as TikTok Shop) to capture full conversion funnels.
Simultaneously, the scaling of social commerce has attracted increased regulatory scrut iny globally.
Regulatory bodies such as the Advertising Standards Council of India (ASCI) and Western fair trade
authorities enforce strict disclosure mandates requiring creators to clearly label sponsored content, paid
partnerships, and affiliate links. Non compliance and undisclosed commercial endorsements undermine
consumer trust and create legal exposure for platforms [29].
Additionally, regulatory institutions and platform operators are implementing rigorous seller vetting
procedures, anti counterfeiting tracking systems, and quality certification standards. In social commerce,
where the gap between peer recommendation and checkout is minimal, counterfeit products or
unfulfilled orders quickly undermine platform trust. Establishing robust buyer protection architectures,
data privacy compliance, and transparent merchant vetting is essential for sustaining long term consumer
participation.

11. Strategic Implications and Research Agenda
The transformation of consumer decision making in social commerce requires commercial enterprises to
re align their digital strategies while opening new avenues for academic inquiry.
For commercial enterprises, digital retail strategies must adapt to discovery led shopping models. Brands
should prioritize shoppable media formats, live streaming programs, and short form video feeds that
capture attention within algorithmic recommendation systems. Marketing strategies should leverage
Social Commerce Constructs by embedding authentic consumer reviews, peer referral incentives, and
user generated media across all digital touchpoints. Partnering with creators who possess genuine
parasocial trust within niche subcultures allows brands to access established trust networks, lowering
customer acquisition costs. Furthermore, to manage the Social Commerce Risk Paradox , enterprises
must ensure multi channel consistency, offering secure native checkouts alongside reliable order
tracking, robust customer support, and seamless cross platform integration. Finally, commercial

Conclusions
Social commerce represents a structural transformation in electronic commerce, shifting consumer
behavior from deliberate, intent driven shopping to continuous, discovery based engagement. By
integrating interactive social features —such as live streaming video, algorithmic personalization, peer
feedback mechanisms, creator endorsement s, and conversational messaging social platforms
fundamentally reconfigure the Consumer Decision Journey.
Theoretical frameworks spanning Stimulus Organism Response, Social Support, and Swift Guanxi
clarify how platform affordances operate as environmental stimuli that trigger affective arousal, flow
immersion, parasocial connection, and swift trust  1 . These psychological states compress traditional
decision funnels, frequently transforming need recognition directly into affect driven impulse buying  2 .
As global social commerce expands toward multi trillion dollar  valuations, sustained commercial
leadership will depend on an enterprise's ability to combine engaging visual content with transparent
trust architectures, regulatory compliance, and seamless cross platform execution
