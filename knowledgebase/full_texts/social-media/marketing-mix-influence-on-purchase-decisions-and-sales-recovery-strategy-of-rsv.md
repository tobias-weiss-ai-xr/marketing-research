 Viral social commerce campaigns frequently generate significant
attention without producing sustained sales performance. The RSV SC
Hijab Series, a hijab -compatible motorcycle helmet produced by PT
Helmindo Pratama Indonesia, attracted 23 million video views during
its September 2025 launch; however, sales volume declined by 52.5%
within five months. This study examined the effects of the seven -
element marketing mix on purchase decisions and formulated a sales
recovery strategy. A questionnaire consisting of 32 Likert -scale items
was administered to 221 verified buyers selected through purposive
sampling. Data were analyzed  using multiple linear regression,
Importance–Performance Matrix Analysis, and the 5A consumer
journey framework. The seven marketing mix elements collectively
explained 58.5% of the variance in purchase decisions (F = 42.930, p <
0.001). Process, Physical  Evidence, People, Price, and Place had
significant partial effects, while Product and Promotion did not, despite
achieving the highest performance scores. Process demonstrated the
strongest effect (β = 0.391), while its performance score remained
relatively low at 2.80 out of 5. A verified price audit positioned the
product within the mid -range category among competitors. Therefore,
the low Price score indicated reduced perceived value rather than an
incorrectly priced offering. The study recommends prioritizing post -
purchase reliability improvements rather than relying primarily on price
discount strategies.

INTRODUCTION
Social commerce has made consumer attention inexpensive to acquire but increasingly
difficult to convert into sustained sales. A single short-form video can reach millions of viewers
at a negligible marginal cost; however, firms frequently observe that the resulting sales growth
declines within months. Metrics commonly used to indicate campaign success, such as views,
reach, and engagement, do not necessarily reflect the indicators required to sustain revenue.
The gap between attention generation and purcha se conversion is therefore rarely diagnosed
with sufficient precision (Ban et al., 2024; Khoshaim, 2025; Wang et al., 2021; Zhou et al.,
2025).
The case examined in this study illustrates this gap clearly. In September 2025, PT
Helmindo Pratama Indonesia launched the RSV SC Hijab Series, a helmet designed with an
interior cavity that accommodates a hair bun for women who ride while wearing a hijab. Based
on conventional campaign indicators, the launch achieved substantial visibility: it generated 23
million video views, with 87.3% of product page visits originating from algorithmic
recommendations rather than direct search, and the audience composition shifted from

market continued to expand, motorcycle ownership remained high, and internet penetration
continued to increase (Malik, 2024; Putri et al., 2025; Wells & Xenias, 2015) . Therefore, the
explanation is likely associated with factors under the firm’s control.

The marketing mix consists of controllable instruments that firms combine to generate
desired responses from target markets (Kotler & Keller, 2016). McCarthy’s original
formulation included four elements —product, price, place, and promotion —which were
considered adequate for conventional manufactured goods. Booms and Bitner (1981) argued
that this framework was insufficient for offerings containing service components and
introduced three additional elements: people, process, and physical evidence. These add itions
resulted in the seven -element marketing mix framework, which remains widely applied in
services marketing and experience-based industries (Wai Yan Ko, 2024).
This distinction is particularly important in online retail environments. In marketplace
transactions, consumers evaluate not only the product itself but also the entire service
experience, including response speed to inquiries, order accuracy, and deliver y condition. A
four-element framework cannot fully capture these service -related interactions. This issue is
relevant to the present study because previous marketing mix research on modest fashion
products in Indonesia has generally adopted the four -element framework, including studies
involving Marchemode, Toko Vivi Jilbab, and UKM Tamyakila, resulting in limited
consideration of service-related attributes.
Perceived value provides the mechanism linking service quality and purchase decisions.
It reflects consumers’ evaluations of received benefits relative to the sacrifices incurred,
including non-monetary costs such as waiting time, uncertainty, and effort ( Blut et al., 2024).
This concept explains why a product with a objectively competitive price may still be perceived
as expensive: when non -monetary sacrifices increase, perceived value decreases even if the
price remains unchanged. Previous studies have linked perceived price, delivery quality, and
perceived value wi th repurchase intention in e -commerce contexts (Ali & Bhasin, 2019).
Reference price theory provides a complementary explanation, suggesting that consumers
evaluate current prices against internal reference standards developed from previous price
experiences; consequently, frequent discounting may alter price expectations and influence
future price perceptions (Asia Pacific Journal of Marketing and Logistics, 2024).
Research on electronic service quality has identified responsiveness, fulfillment
accuracy, and post-purchase handling as important determinants of online repurchase behavior,
with customer satisfaction often serving as a mediating factor (Dandis  et al., 2026; Vu &
Nguyen, 2025). However, these findings have largely developed separately from traditional
marketing mix research, limiting their integration into a comprehensive strategic framework.
This study addresses this limitation by incorporating responsiveness as an indicator of People,
fulfillment reliability as an indicator of Process, and delivery condition as an indicator of
Physical Evidence.
The 5A framework —Aware, Appeal, Ask, Act, and Advocate —models the consumer
journey as a sequence in which advocacy can contribute to renewed awareness (Kotler et al.,
2021). In this study, the framework is applied diagnostically to identify the stage at wh ich the
consumer journey weakens, as regression analysis indicates the magnitude of relationships but
does not reveal the specific stage where conversion declines. Previous research on viral content
indicates that engagement decreases rapidly after algorithmic distribution declines (Sangiorgio
et al., 2025), while studies on user -generated content demonstrate that consumer reviews can
replace campaign-driven attention by strengthening trust (Van Dat et al., 2025; Sustainability,

Based on previous studies, a research gap exists regarding the application of the seven -
element marketing mix to modest fashion products in Indonesia, particularly within the context
of viral social commerce campaigns. Although previous studies have examin ed the four -
element marketing mix framework in modest fashion retail, including Marchemode, Toko Vivi
Jilbab, and UKM Tamyakila, limited research has integrated service-related elements (People,
Process, and Physical Evidence) or investigated why viral campaigns fail to generate sustained
sales. This gap is important because Indonesia’s modest fashion market continues to expand,
while social commerce has become an increasingly important sales channel. Therefore, this
study provides empirical evidence regarding the determinants of purchase decisions and offers
practical recommendations for firms experiencing post-viral sales decline. The novelty of this
research lies in integrating the seven -element marketing mix with the 5A consumer journey
framework to provide a more comprehensive diagnosis of conversion challenges. The findings
are expected to contribute theoretically to marketing  mix and electronic service quality
literature and practically provide evidence-based recommendations for PT Helmindo Pratama
Indonesia and similar firms.
This study pursued two interconnected objectives. The first was explanatory, namely to
identify which marketing mix elements influenced purchase decisions for the RSV SC Hijab
Series and determine the strongest influencing factors. The second was prescriptive, namely to
translate empirical findings into a structured sales recovery strategy, ensuring that managerial
priorities were based on measured impacts rather than implementation convenience
Product quality provides the functional foundation of the offering, and previous
Indonesian studies on modest fashion retail have reported significant effects of product quality
on purchase decisions. However, within the helmet industry, Regulation of the Minister of
Industry No. 59 of 2024 requires mandatory safety certification for helmets distributed in
Indonesia, which may reduce perceived differences among competing products in terms of
basic safety compliance.
H1. Product has a significant effect on the purchase decision.
Price influences the decision through perceived sacrifice, not through the nominal figure
alone. Where discounting has been sustained since launch, reference price effects may amplify
that influence further (Kwon & Hartley, 2026).
H2. Price has a significant effect on the purchase decision.
Place determines access and the conditions under which a transaction can be completed,
including the range of channels through which a buyer can reach the seller.
H3. Place has a significant effect on the purchase decision.
Promotion drives awareness and appeal. Where a category is saturated with promotional
content, however, exposure may cease to differentiate between competing offers.
H4. Promotion has a significant effect on the purchase decision.
People captures the interpersonal service encounter, occurring in a marketplace setting
primarily through seller responsiveness to enquiries and complaints — an established
determinant of online repurchase behaviour.
H5. People has a significant effect on the purchase decision.
Process captures the reliability of the transaction sequence: order accuracy, delivery
consistency against the promise made, and clarity of return and warranty procedures. Electronic
service quality research positions these as decisive in online settings.
H6. Process has a significant effect on the purchase decision.
Physical Evidence encompasses tangible cues surrounding the offer, including
packaging, arrival condition and visible certification, which serve as quality signals where the
product cannot be inspected before purchase.

METHOD
This study employed a quantitative problem-solving research design consistent with the
marketing research approach proposed by Malhotra et al. (2017), in which measurement was
conducted to support decision -making. The independent variables consisted of the  seven
marketing mix elements, while the dependent variable was the actual purchase decision rather
than purchase intention. This distinction was important because intention -based measures
could not fully capture post-purchase service experiences examined in this study.
Respondents were selected using purposive sampling based on three criteria: hijab -
wearing women, motorcycle riders or passengers, and previous purchasers of the RSV SC
Hijab Series. The minimum sample size was determined as five times the number of indicators,
resulting in a minimum requirement of 135 respondents. The study targeted 200 respondents,
and 221 respondents successfully passed the screening process. Data collection was conducted
online through Google Forms using sales channels where the product was distributed, primarily
Shopee and TikTok Shop, which together accounted for 79.6% of revenue, supplemented by
the official website and buyer database. Most respondents were within the 25 –44 age range,
and nearly half had used the product for three to s ix months, allowing them to evaluate post -
purchase experiences rather than only initial impressions.
The research instrument consisted of 32 statements measuring eight constructs using a
five-point Likert scale ranging from strongly disagree (1) to strongly agree (5). Indicators for
Price, People, Process, and Physical Evidence were operationalized based on the theoretical
framework discussed previously, focusing on perceived sacrifice, responsiveness, fulfillment
reliability, and product arrival condition. Product was measured through perceived quality,
safety features, and suitability for hijab use; Place was measured through channel availability
and accessibility; Promotion was measured through content informati veness and
persuasiveness; and Purchase Decision was measured through realized purchase behavior and
related determinants.
Validity was assessed using corrected item -total correlation against a critical value of
0.132 (n = 221, α = 0.05), while reliability was evaluated using Cronbach’s alpha with a
threshold of 0.70. Classical assumption tests were conducted before interpreting the regression
coefficients, including the Kolmogorov –Smirnov test for normality, variance inflation factor
(VIF) analysis for multicollinearity, the Glejser  test for heteroscedasticity, and the Durbin –
Watson test for autocorrelation. Multiple linear regression was then performed using the mean
scores of each marketing mix element through SPSS. The findings were subsequently mapped
using an Importance –Performance Matrix, which compared standardized coefficients with
mean performance scores, and were interpreted through the 5A framework using campaign
reports, ten months of transaction records, and a verified competitor price audit conducted
across official marketplace stores in July 2026 as supporting evidence.

Validity and reliability
Of the 32 items, 31 satisfied the validity threshold. One Promotion item failed, returning
a corrected item -total correlation of −0.047. That item measured exposure — whether the
respondent had encountered campaign content — whereas the remaining Promotion items
measured judgements about that content. The failure is substantive, not merely technical:
having seen the content and having been assisted by it do not covary i n this sample, an early

Five partial hypotheses are supported and two are not. Process carries the largest

Interpreting the rejected hypotheses against previous research
The rejection of H1 and H4 contradicts prior Indonesian studies of modest fashion retail,
which report significant effects of product quality and promotion on purchase decisions in
comparable categories. Two explanations reconcile the divergence. Both are specific to this
context, and neither refutes that earlier work.
First, the non-significance of Product coincides with the highest performance score in the
study (4.00). This combination is not contradictory once regulatory context is considered. Since
Regulation No. 59 of 2024 made national safety certification mandato ry for all helmets in
circulation, the attribute that once distinguished a certified helmet from an uncertified one has
become an entry requirement met evenly across the competitor set. Product remains necessary
but no longer differentiates, and a variable  that does not vary meaningfully across the
alternatives a buyer considers cannot explain variance in choice among them. The earlier
studies were conducted in categories without an equivalent mandatory standard, where product
quality retained discriminating power.
Second, the non-significance of Promotion is consistent with the measurement anomaly
reported in Section 3.1. The item that failed validity testing measured exposure, and it did not
covary with items measuring judgements about content quality. Respondents who had seen the
campaign were not thereby more likely to find it helpful. Read alongside the 5A diagnosis
below, this suggests that promotion in this case generated awareness without supplying
decision-relevant information, so its effect terminates before the purchase decision. The effect
is not absent; it simply stops early. This is consistent with evidence that viral engagement
decays once algorithmic distribution ends (Sangiorgio et al., 2025), and extends it by showing
the decay is observable in realised sales, not only in engagement metrics.
Conversely, the support for H5, H6 and H7 aligns closely with electronic service quality
research (Dandis et al., 2026; Vu & Nguyen, 2025), and the present study extends that literature

by demonstrating these effects inside a marketing mix specification instead of alongside one.
That Process dominates all other elements is a stronger result than the e -service quality

intention, and post-purchase failures are invisible to respondents who have not yet transacted.
Importance–Performance mapping and consumer journey diagnosis
Crossing standardised coefficients against performance scores places Process, People
and Price in the priority quadrant, combining high effect with weak performance, while
Product, Physical Evidence and Place occupy quadrants that call for maintenance, not  further
investment. Notably, the two elements the firm had already acted upon — through price
discounting and a reallocation of promotional focus — both sit in the low-effect quadrant. This
explains why two apparently reasonable corrective actions failed to arrest the decline: both
targeted elements with small measured effects, leaving the binding constraint untouched.
Interpreted through the 5A framework, the journey breaks at identifiable points. Aware
and Appeal were built strongly and at low cost, as campaign metrics confirm. Ask was left
unaddressed: the campaign generated attention without answering the questions p rospective
buyers raise concerning sizing, fit with different hijab styles, and safety. Act was obstructed by
the fulfilment and responsiveness weaknesses registered in the Process and People scores.
Advocate never formed, so once algorithmic distribution ended no substitute source of demand
existed. On this reading the 52.5 per cent decline is a structural consequence of the journey's
shape. Timing had little to do with it.
The findings also relocate the pricing problem. A verified price audit across official
marketplace stores places the RSV SC Hijab Series in the middle of its competitor set and
below the most premium comparable product, contradicting the firm's own initial  assumption
that its price was approximately double that of competitors. A fair price position coexisting
with a low Price  score ceases to be contradictory once perceived value is considered: what
buyers surrender includes waiting time, the risk of receiving an incorrect item, and unclear
return procedures. Sustained discounting compounds this by lowering the internal referen ce
price, so that a 28.8 per cent rise in the received price index registers with buyers as a price
increase, not as a return to normal levels. Lowering price further would therefore address a
symptom while aggravating the underlying condition.
Theoretical implications
Three implications follow for theory. First, the results support integrating electronic
service quality into the marketing mix instead of maintaining two parallel literatures. Locating
responsiveness, fulfilment accuracy and arrival condition within People, Process and Physical
Evidence produced a specification in which service elements dominate — an outcome a four-
element model would have been structurally incapable of detecting. For research on modest
fashion retail in Indonesia, where four -element specifications remain common, this suggests
reported findings may systematically understate the role of service.
Second, the absence of multicollinearity between People and Process indicates that
buyers distinguish the interpersonal service encounter from transactional execution. This
argues against aggregating them into a single service construct and supports treati ng them as
separate managerial levers with distinct remedies.
Third, the study demonstrates the analytical value of pairing a variance -explaining
method with a stage-diagnostic framework. Regression established that Process dominates but
could not indicate where in the consumer journey its effect dissipates; the 5A reading supplied
that location. Neither method alone would have produced the conclusion that the decline is
structural.
Managerial implications
For managers, the overarching implication is that campaign reach and conversion
capacity are separable capabilities, and that further investment in the former yields little when

the latter is the binding constraint. Five priorities follow from the measured effects, ordered by
impact rather than ease of execution.
•  Treat post -purchase reliability as an operating standard, not a project. Order accuracy,
delivery consistency against the promise made, and a single clear return procedure applying
identically across channels address Process and People, the elements com bining the largest
effect with the weakest performance. Where switching costs approach zero, a single
fulfilment failure is sufficient to lose a buyer permanently.
•  Withdraw routine discounting and restore the reference price. Discounting should be phased
out gradually and replaced with benefits that raise value without lowering the price figure,
while product benefits are communicated in concrete and verifiable te rms. Abrupt
withdrawal risks a volume shock; continued discounting perpetuates the distortion.
•  Supply verifiable evidence at the information-search stage. Where national certification has
become mandatory for all competitors, it ceases to differentiate; additional international
certification and material addressing recurrent sizing and fit questi ons, placed on product
pages across every channel, populate a stage the campaign left empty.
•  Manage buyer reviews as a marketing asset, not as passive feedback. Reviews substitute for
campaign-driven attention once distribution ends. This priority depends on the first: reviews
will only be favourable once the purchase experience has improved, s o sequencing matters
more than speed.
•  Reduce channel concentration. With 79.6 per cent of revenue arising from two marketplaces,
the transaction experience is largely determined by third -party rules. Activating direct
channels and physical stores as trial and fitting points transfers a port ion of the customer
relationship back to the firm.
Two observations bear on implementation. The priorities are interdependent. Attempting
them at once risks degrading execution quality across all of them, so a phased sequence in
which reliability precedes advocacy is preferable. In addition, most indicator s required to
evaluate these priorities — response time, order accuracy, return rates, review volume — had
never been recorded by the firm, meaning the element most determining the purchase decision
was also the element measured least. Establishing baselin e measurement is a precondition for
evaluating any priority above, not an administrative afterthought. Feasibility rests on a finding
from the internal analysis: nearly every capability required is already held by the firm and
simply not organised towards this end. The binding constraint is organisational.
Limitations
Five limitations bound these findings. The data represent conditions during a single
observation period, and shifting platform algorithms and shopping habits may alter both the
pattern of relationships and the relative importance of elements in later perio ds. Respondents
were restricted to buyers, so the conclusion that the information -search stage was left empty
rests on inference, since those who considered the product and declined it were never surveyed.
Cost and margin data were unavailable, so profitab ility was not calculated and success is
assessed as marketing performance, not financial return. The heteroscedasticity violation on
Product means the reported significance pattern warrants confirmation under robust standard
errors. Finally, the study exam ines one product line at one firm using purposive sampling, so
the analytical logic transfers more readily than the specific coefficients.
Directions for further research
These limitations define the most productive avenues for future work. The model should
be re -estimated under robust standard errors to confirm the significance pattern. Perceived
value should be tested as a mediator between the service elements and the pur chase decision,
which would confirm or correct the recommendation against price reduction. Prospective
buyers who did not purchase should be surveyed directly, which would test the information -
search conclusion instead of inferring it. Engagement and trans action time series should be

CONCLUSION
This study examined why a viral product launch failed to generate sustained sales growth
and addressed this issue by measuring the seven -element marketing mix against realized
purchase decisions for the RSV SC Hijab Series. The marketing mix explained 58.5 % of the
variance in purchase decisions. Process demonstrated the strongest effect despite having one
of the lowest performance scores, placing it alongside People and Price as a priority area for
managerial intervention. Meanwhile, Product and Promotion, which achieved the highest
performance scores, did not demonstrate significant differentiating effects. Therefore, the sales
decline appeared to occur after the purchase decision stage, particularly in fulfillment accuracy,
service responsiveness, and the clarity of return procedures. The decline was not primarily
associated with product attributes, nominal price levels, or market demand conditions.
The practical implication is a shift in managerial priorities. For PT Helmindo Pratama
Indonesia and other firms experiencing similar post -viral sales declines, the findings indicate
that resources should be redirected from generating additional attention toward improving the
conversion and retention of existing customer interest. The capab ilities required for this
transition were already available within the firm but required better organization and
integration.
