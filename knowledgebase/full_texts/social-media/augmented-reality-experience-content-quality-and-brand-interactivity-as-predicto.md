Abstract: The rapid expansion of social commerce has increased the need to
understand how technological, informational, and relational features jointly shape
consumers’ purchase evaluations. This study examines Augmented Reality (AR)
experience, Content Quality, and Brand Interactivity as predictors of Purchase
Intention among Indonesian Instagram Shopping users. A quantitative cross -sectional
survey was conducted with 202 respondents aged 18 –35 years who had used
Instagram Shopping and encountered an AR-supported shopping experience during the
preceding three months. Measurement quality was assessed using item -level
diagnostics, Cronbach’s alpha, exploratory factor analysis, and parallel analysis,
followed by multiple linear regression with HC3 heteroskedasticity-consistent standard
errors. The regression model was statistically significant, F(3, 198) = 134.863, p < 0.001,
and explained 67.1% of the variance in Purchase Intention. Content Quality showed
the strongest positive association (β = 0.439, p < 0.001), followed by AR experience (β
= 0.341, p < 0.001), whereas Brand Interactivity showed a positive but nonsignificant
association (β = 0.111, p = 0.276). Measurement diagnostics indicated limited construct
differentiation: reliability coefficients ranged from 0.616 to 0.651, and parallel analysis
retained one dominant factor. Accordingly, the results are interpreted as exploratory
predictive evidence rather than confirmatory validation of fully distinct latent
constructs. Within this predictive framing, decision-relevant content and functional AR
visualization were more strongly associated with Purchase Intention than Brand
Interactivity considered as an independent predictor. The study contributes to social -
commerce resea rch by jointly examining technological, informational, and relational
predictors while providing practitioners with a cautious empirical basis for prioritizing
content quality and evaluation-oriented AR.

INTRODUCTION
The paradigm of digital commerce has undergone a substantial transformation, moving beyond
static transactions toward immersive and participatory consumer journeys. In the contemporary retail
landscape, Instagram has become a prominent social-commerce environment in which consumers may
encounter AR -supported shopping experiences through virtual try -on tools, branded filters, AR
advertisements, spatial product visualization, or links to external AR services . Such experiences may
partially reduce the uncertainty caused by consumers’ inability to inspect products physically before
purchase (Ngo et al., 2025) . Indonesia represents a critical market in this evolution, as Meta’s
advertising tools indicated that Instagram had a potential advertising reach of 103 million users in the
country in early 2025, equivalent to 36.3% of the national population  (Kemp, 2025). Nevertheless,
extensive platform reach does not necessarily eliminate consumer hesi tation. A 2025 survey by the
Indonesian Internet Service Providers Association found that 38.72% of respondents preferred
shopping at physical stores because they considered direct shopping more convincing than online

help address. This "reality gap" stems from critical information asymmetry between digital
representations and the physical reality of products.
The adoption of immersive shopping technologies is commonly explained through the
Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), which emphasizes perceived usefulness and perceived ease of
use (Davis, 1989) . Recent empirical evidence suggests that AR experiences significantly bolster
purchase intentions by merging physical reality with real -time virtual elements (Guo & Zhang, 2024).
Features such as Virtual Try-On (VTO) play a pivotal role in this process by creating a "flow" experience
that reduces perceived risk and enhances cognitive trust  (Khan & Fatma, 2024; Putri & Salehudin,
2022; Uhm et al., 2022) . Furthermore, content quality acts as a vital informational bridge that
translates technological capabilities into meaningful consumer value (Windasari & Santoso, 2022; Yang
& Lin, 2024) . This technological and informational synergy is further strengthened by brand
interactivity and social presence, which differentiate brand identities in multi-channel environments
(Alimamy & Al -Imamy, 2022; Yim et al., 2017; Zare Ebrahimabad et al., 2024) . The success of
augmented shopping reality increasingly depends on balancing accuracy and entertainment effects to
satisfy both utilitarian and hedonic consumer needs (Hoffmann & Mai, 2022; Zhou et al., 2025). Recent
AR marketing research further demonstrates that augmentation quality, reflected in design,
interaction, and embedding quality, can strengthen local presence and contribute to purchase-related
outcomes (Schein et al., 2026).
The technological perspective alone does not fully capture purchase evaluation in social
commerce. Instagram Shopping also requires consumers to assess the quality of product information
and interact with brands through digitally mediated communication. Accordingly, this study draws on
three complementary theoretical lenses. The Technology Acceptance Model provides the
technological basis for examining AR-supported shopping experiences (Davis, 1989); the information-
quality perspective derived from the DeLo ne and McLean Information Systems Success Model
explains the role of accurate, relevant, understandable, and useful product information (DeLone &
McLean, 2003 ; Wang & Strong, 1996 ) while Social Presence Theory explains how responsive
communication and socially rich digital cues can create psychological proximity between consumers
and brands (Short et al., 1976; Lu et al., 2016). Recent evidence from Instagram-based social commerce
further indicates that platform sociability, enjoym ent, functionality, and trust are relevant to
consumers’ purchase intentions  (Aziz & Poureisa, 2025). Together, these perspectives represent
technological, informational, and relational dimensions of the social-commerce experience.
Despite growing research on digital commerce, these dimensions have often been examined
separately or within broader e -commerce settings rather than compared within a single Instagram -
based predictive model. Indonesian studies have addressed AR adoption an d other purchase-related
determinants, while other work has emphasized factors such as brand ambassadors and price
discounts (Anifa & Sanaji, 2022; Faritzal et al., 2023; Khoirunnisa & Sugiharti, 2024; Liu & Napitupulu,
2020; Iisnawati et al., 2023; La i et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2025) . Consequently, less is known about the
relative predictive contribution of immersive visualization, decision -relevant information, and
reciprocal brand communication when these mechanisms are considered simultaneously. Augmented
Reality, Content Quality, and Brand Interactivity were therefore selected because they represent these
three complementary mechanisms and allow their unique associations with Purchase Intention to be
evaluated within the same social-commerce model.
Drawing on the Technology Acceptance Model, an information-quality perspective derived from
the DeLone and McLean Information Systems Success Model, and Social Presence Theory, this study
proposes three directional relationships. Augmented Reality enables consumers to visualize products
within realistic and interactive environments, thereby supporting product evaluation and reducing
uncertainty associated with the absence of physical inspection. Accordingly, H1: Augmented Reality

To address these theoretical and empirical gaps, this study examines Augmented Reality (X1),
Content Quality (X2), and B rand Interactivity (X3) as predictors of Purchase Intention (Y) within the
Instagram Shopping context. Using multiple linear regression, the study analyzes data from 202
Indonesian respondents aged 18 –35 years who reported prior experience with Instagram S hopping
and AR-supported shopping during the preceding three months. The analytical framework evaluates
the unique contribution of each predictor while assessing the overall explanatory capacity of the model
through the coefficient of determination (R²). B y simultaneously considering technological,
informational, and relational dimensions of the shopping experience, the study investigates whether
immersive product visualization, decision -relevant content, and reciprocal brand communication are
differentially associated with consumers’ purchase intentions in a social-commerce environment.
The theoretical framework of this study integrates three complementary perspectives, each
providing a distinct rationale for one dimension of the proposed predictive model. The Technology
Acceptance Model provides the technological foundation for Augmented  Reality experience by
emphasizing how consumers’ evaluations of technology -enabled usefulness and ease of use shape
their responses to digital systems. However, technology-related evaluations alone do not fully capture
purchase evaluation in social-commerce environments, where consumers also depend on the quality
of the information presented through the interface. The information-quality perspective derived from
the DeLone and McLean Information Systems Success Model therefore provides the informational
foundation for Content Quality by emphasizing the relevance, accuracy, understandability, and
usefulness of information for decision -making. This perspective is particularly relevant to AR -
supported shopping because the value of an immersive interface depend s not only on the availability
of the technology itself, but also on the quantity, presentation, diagnosticity, and controllability of the
product information conveyed through the shopping experience (Hoffmann et al., 2022) . Social
Presence Theory complements these technological and informational perspectives by addressing the
relational dimension of social commerce. Because Instagram also functions as an interpersonal
communication environment, responsive brand communication and socially rich digital cue s may
create a stronger sense of human contact and psychological proximity, thereby providing the
theoretical basis for Brand Interactivity. This layered framework is consistent with evidence showing
that consumer responses to AR are shaped by technologica l perceptions and value -creation
mechanisms such as virtuality, informativeness, interactivity, and the development of utilitarian and
hedonic value (Dogra et al., 2023; Negm, 2024) , as well as evidence that AR -supported content and
digitally mediated co mmunication can contribute to consumer engagement and trust formation
(Sunenti & Santoso, 2025) . Accordingly, the three perspectives are treated as complementary rather
than competing explanations: the Technology Acceptance Model informs the technological dimension
represented by Augmented Reality, the information -quality perspective informs the informational
dimension represented by Content Quality, and Social Presence Theory informs the relational
dimension represented by Brand Interactivity . The study therefore contributes to social -commerce
research by examining these three theoretically grounded dimensions simultaneously within a single
predictive framework rather than treating them as isolated determinants of consumer evaluation.

RESEARCH METHOD
This study employed a quantitative, cross -sectional research design to examine Augmented
Reality experience, Content Quality, and Brand Interactivity as predictors of Purchase Intention within
the Instagram Shopping context. Multiple linear regression was selected because the primary analytical
objective was to estimate the unique predictive association of three continuous predictors with a
single continuous outcome while statistically controlling for the remaining predictors. Each variable
was represented by a composite mean score calculated from its corresponding indicators. This
analytical choice was deliberate and aligned with the predictive objective of the study rather than
being adopted as a default estimation procedure. The measurement diagnostics re ported below did
not recover four clearly differentiated empirical factors, indicating that the observed data did not
provide sufficiently strong evidence of construct separation to justify treating the variables as fully
distinct latent constructs. Under these conditions, imposing a confirmatory latent -variable model
would imply a degree of measurement differentiation that the data do not support. Multiple linear
regression using theory -defined composite scores was therefore considered an appropriate and
transparent approach for exploratory prediction. Because the heteroskedasticity diagnostics produced
mixed evidence, including a statistically significant Breusch –Pagan test, HC3 heteroskedasticity -

This adjustment provides inference that is less sensitive to nonconstant error variance than
conventional ordinary least-squares standard errors. Given the nonexperimental and cross -sectional
design, the estimated coefficients were interpreted as conditional predictive associations rather than
causal effects. An a priori power analysis was conducted for a fixed -model multiple linear regression
with three predictors. Assuming a medium effect size of f² = 0.15, a significance level of α = 0.05, and
statistical power of 0.80, the minimum required sample size was 77 respondents (Faul et al., 2009) .
The final analytical sample of 202 respondents therefore exceeded the minimum sample requirement.
Data were collected  between 13 and 29 January 2026 through an online questionnaire
administered using Google Forms. A non -probability purposive sampling technique was employed to
recruit Indonesian Instagram users aged 18 –35 years with relevant experience in Instagram -based
shopping. The questionnaire was disseminated through personal WhatsApp messages, WhatsApp
Status, close -friend and family networks, university class groups, Facebook student communities,
Twitter, and Instagram Story. Recruitment information provided examples of AR-supported shopping
experiences, including virtual try -on applications for cosmetics, fashion products, glasses, and
accessories; spatial product placement; AR advertisements; and branded filters used in a shopping -
related context. These examples w ere provided to help prospective respondents distinguish an AR -
supported shopping experience from ordinary Instagram browsing.
Before accessing the demographic and measurement sections, prospective respondents
completed screening questions concerning infor med consent, active Instagram use, previous use of
Instagram Shopping, and self -reported experience with an AR -supported shopping feature through
Instagram during the preceding three months. The three -month eligibility period was applied to
increase the li kelihood that respondents could recall the shopping experience when evaluating the
questionnaire statements. Eligibility was based on self-report; the study did not collect platform logs,
screenshots, brand names, product categories, or the exact AR format  used by each respondent.
Consequently, respondents should not be assumed to have experienced an identical AR feature or
product category. A total of 203 submissions were received. One submission was excluded because
the respondent reported neither previou s use of Instagram Shopping nor an AR -supported shopping
experience and did not complete the demographic and measurement sections. The final dataset
therefore consisted of 202 complete and eligible responses.
All measurement items were assessed using a fiv e-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (“strongly
disagree”) to 5 (“strongly agree”). The structured questionnaire comprised 20 indicators distributed
equally across four theoretical constructs, with five indicators assigned to each construct. The
Augmented R eality items were developed and contextually adapted with reference to established
measures of perceived ease of use, vividness, usefulness, AR-supported product evaluation, and retail
user experience (Poushneh & Vasquez-Parraga, 2017). Within the present study, these items captured
respondents’ evaluations of the usefulness, ease, clarity of product visualization, purchase -decision
support, and overall value of their self-reported AR-supported Instagram Shopping experience.
The Content Quality item s were informed by established information -quality frameworks and
social-media content-quality measures and captured the clarity, relevance, usefulness, visual appeal,
and decision-support value of product information (Dabbous & Barakat, 2020). The Brand Interactivity
items were developed with reference to Dabbous and Barakat (2020) and represented accessibility of
brand communication, responsiveness, opportunities for consumer participation, perceived attention
from the brand, and enjoyment associated wi th brand interaction. Purchase Intention was measured
using indicators contextually adapted from prior e -commerce and AR -supported shopping research
and reflected purchase interest, product consideration, purchase likelihood, preference, and
recommendation intention. All items were presented in Indonesian and contextually worded to reflect
respondents’ experiences with Instagram-based shopping and AR-supported product evaluation.

Data analysis proceeded in three stages. First, the dataset was screened for e ligibility,
completeness, and coding consistency, followed by descriptive analysis of respondent characteristics
and the study variables. Item -level measurement quality was assessed using Pearson item –total
correlations and corrected item–total correlations. A corrected item–total correlation of approximately
0.30 or higher was used as a general indicator of adequate item discrimination, while lower values
were evaluated together with theoretical relevance and reliability diagnostics rather than being treated
as an automatic deletion criterion (Hair et al., 2019). Internal consistency was subsequently assessed
using Cronbach’s alpha and alpha -if-item-deleted diagnostics. Because deleting individual indicators
did not raise any scale to the conventional reliability benchmark of 0.70, all 20 items were retained to
preserve their theoretical coverage, while the observed reliability limitations were taken into account
in the interpretation of the subsequent analyses.
Second, exploratory factor analysis was cond ucted to examine the empirical structure of the
measurement items. Principal axis factoring was employed because the analysis focused on shared
variance among the indicators, with sampling adequacy and factorability evaluated using the Kaiser –
Meyer–Olkin statistic and Bartlett’s test of sphericity. Factor retention was assessed primarily through
parallel analysis, in which the observed eigenvalues were compared with those generated from 1,000
random datasets (Horn, 1965). Because the theoretical framework specified four potentially correlated
constructs, a theory-constrained four-factor solution with direct oblimin rotation was also examined.
Factor loadings of approximately 0.40 or higher were used as a general criterion for interpreting item–
factor relationships. The factor analysis was used diagnostically to evaluate construct differentiation
rather than as confirmatory evidence of measurement validity.
Third, composite scores were calculated by averaging the five indicators assigned to each
theoretically specified construct. Multiple linear regression was then performed with Purchase
Intention as the dependent variable and Augmented Reality, Content Quality, and Brand Interactivity
as predictors. Overall model performance was evaluated using the multiple correlation coefficient, R²,
adjusted R², and the overall F-test, whereas individual predictors were assessed using unstandardized
coefficients (B), standardized coefficients (β), confidence intervals, and p -values. Multicollinearity,
residual normality, heteroskedasticity, residual independence, and influential observations were
examined using tolerance and variance inflation factor values, graphical and formal residual
diagnostics, the Glejser and Breusch–Pagan tests, the Durbin–Watson statistic, standardized residuals,
leverage, and Cook’s distance. Because the heteroskedasticity diagnostics produced mixed evidence,
including a statistically significant Breusch –Pagan test, HC3 heteroskedasticity -consistent standard
errors were used for the final inferent ial evaluation of the regression coefficients. HC3 adjusts the
estimated covariance matrix and provides inference that is more robust to nonconstant error variance
than conventional ordinary least -squares standard errors (MacKinnon & White, 1985) . A directional
hypothesis was considered supported when the corresponding coefficient was positive and
statistically significant at p < 0.05 based on HC3 -adjusted inference. Standardized coefficients were
additionally used to compare the relative predictive m agnitudes of the three predictors within the
fitted model. Given the cross -sectional design and the measurement limitations identified in the
reliability and exploratory factor analyses, all regression results were interpreted as predictive
associations rather than evidence of causal effects.

CONCLUSION
This study examined Augmented Reality experience, Content Quality, and Brand Interactivity as
predictors of Purchase Intention among 202 Indonesian Instagram Shopping users. The regression
model explained 67.1% of the observed variance in Purchase Intention, with Content Quality showing
the strongest positive and statistically significant association (β = 0.439, p < 0.001), followed by
Augmented Reality experience (β = 0.341, p < 0.001), whereas Brand Interactivity show ed a smaller
positive but statistically nonsignificant association (β = 0.111, p = 0.276); accordingly, H1 and H2 were
supported, while H3 was not supported. The study contributes to social-commerce research by jointly
examining technological, informationa l, and relational dimensions within an exploratory predictive
framework, while the measurement findings indicate that these dimensions were not empirically
differentiated as clearly as the theoretical model assumed. For practitioners, the results provide a
cautious basis for prioritizing accurate and decision -relevant product content, complemented by AR
features that facilitate product evaluation, while responsive brand communication should remain part
of the customer -engagement strategy even though its ind ependent association with Purchase
Intention was not statistically supported after the other predictors were controlled. These findings
should be interpreted in light of several limitations, including modest internal consistency, the
retention of one domin ant factor in parallel analysis, heterogeneous and self -reported AR exposure,
and the use of a cross -sectional purposive sample, all of which limit causal inference and broader
generalization. Future research should therefore refine and validate the measurement structure using
independent samples, document specific AR formats and product categories, replicate the model
across different social -commerce platforms, and employ longitudinal or experimental designs to
examine whether mechanisms such as brand trust, perceived risk, customer engagement, or consumer
flow explain the relationships between AR-supported shopping experiences and Purchase Intention.
