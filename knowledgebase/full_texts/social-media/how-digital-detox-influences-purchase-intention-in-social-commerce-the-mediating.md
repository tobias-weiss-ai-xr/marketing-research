# How digital detox influences purchase intention in social commerce: the mediating role of digital fatigue and the moderating role of mindfulness

ARTICLE
How digital detox in ﬂuences purchase intention in
social commerce: the mediating role of digital
fatigue and the moderating role of mindfulness
Hussein Khalifa 1,2
The paper examines the mediating role of digital detox (DD) between purchase intention
(PI)-digital fatigue (DF)-mindfulness (MF) as moderators in the context of social commerce.
The research utilized a sample of 297 participants in the fashion and retail sector in the UAE
to develop empirical data through self-administered questionnaire. The structural equation
modeling (SEM) was used to test the connections between hypotheses and was strong
enough to test for complicated mediation-moderation. The results show that DD has a
signiﬁcant positive effect on PI both in direct and indirect effect due to reduced DFs. In
addition, MF strengthens the estimated positive connection between DD-PI and weakens the
negative in ﬂuence of DF on the formation of MF as a moderator. The article advances the
digital consumption and social commerce literature because it has de ﬁned DD as a primary
driver of consumer well-being and behavioral intention, particularly in terms of its application
in emerging-market contexts. In a practical manner, the research offers meaningful per-
spective into managers and marketers to come up with consumer engagement policies that
integrate digital balance and MF practises. Although the results produced are signi ﬁcant both
in theoretical and managerial contributions, the data is tied to limited cultural and industry-
speciﬁc generalizability boundaries, implying the need to employ cross-cultural and long-
itudinal studies in further investigations.

Introduction
S
ocial commerce has developed a phenomenal pace, chan-
ging the process of buying into a more digital phenomenon
with the online space becoming a focal point of interaction,
communication, and trade. The digital stimuli of social net-
working, in ﬂuencer marketing, peer recommendations, and
content provided by algorithms are now available to consumers
around the clock. Although these advancements have made life
easier and more interactive, the digital media usage over the years
has had the same impact of creating an increased DF awareness.
DF is perceived as a mental condition that is typi ﬁed by cognitive
overload, mental exhaustion, irritation, and emotional strain due
to over-exposure to information and constant digital connectivity.
The adverse effects of the digital overload are already established
in the literature on the subject. Previous ﬁndings are consistent
with that DF has a negative impact on cognitive functioning, well-
being, and the quality of decisions and decreased engagement,
satisfaction, and trust in digital platforms (Bakri, 2023; Gary,
2025). Nonetheless, despite the fact that such studies evidently
prove the detrimental effect of excessive digital exposure under
the name of fatigue, they mostly treat it as a ﬁnal condition.
Consequently, little is known about the consumer response to DF
by coping mechanisms especially in a manner that reinstate
motivation and have an overall positive effect on behavioral
consequences like PI. As a reaction to the growing amounts of
digital overload, the idea of DD, which is temporary dis-
connectivity or a conscious restriction of using digital platforms,
has become viral as a means of re-establishing cognitive balance
and mental health (Aguvaveedi et al., 2025). The available lit-
erature primarily de ﬁnes DD as a wellness-based behavior that
improves the psychological well-being or work-life balance (Ji
et al., 2025; Aguvaveedi, 2025). Nevertheless, its increasing topi-
cality notwithstanding, DD has not received a suf ﬁcient investi-
gation concerning consumer decision-making, especially in the
framework of social commerce. This is an important gap to
consider since the voluntary disengagement in the digital sphere
can potentially lead to the elimination of fatigue as well as allow
the consumer to be more mindful of the digital space, which
probably result in an increased purchase intent instead of
decreasing it. In spite of the fact that a part of the previous
investigations explored the digital consumption behavior, the
existing body on DD, DF, and their in ﬂuence on PI is disjointed,
conceptually restricted, and geographically limited. The available
literature has continuously emphasized the negative impact of DF
on psychological functioning without much association with
consumer intentions in social commerce settings. DF studies have
mainly dwelled on exhaustion and its adverse effects of social
media on engagement, satisfaction, and trust (Bakri, 2023; Gary,
2025), but not on purchase-seeking behaviors. Such a gap
undermines the theoretical accounts of the ways in which psy-
chological exhaustion can transfer into low motivation to engage
in transactional activities in social commerce systems. Moreover,
although DD is being increasingly discussed as the solution to
information overload, there is little empirical research that con-
centrates on the effect of DD on the consumer outcomes,
including PI. There is limited theoretical synthesis in the litera-
ture on how the paradox is established in temporary disengage-
ment of digital platforms as means of stabilizing or even
improving consumer intention due to fatigue and restoration of
attentional focus. This lack of mediation-based explanations is a
very crucial gap, because DF can be one of the most important
psychological processes with the help of DD can affect PI it is the
assumption which was not adequately studied in previous models.
Besides DD and DF, MF has also been found as a signi ﬁcant
psychological construct leading to the alleviation of stress, the
development of self-regulatory control, and the quality of
decisions made (Binh, 2025; Hu et al., 2024). Nevertheless, there
is a lack of research empirical studies that include MF as a
moderating variable in the relationship between DD, DF and
consumer behavior. According to the existing research, MF is
frequently viewed as a direct measure of well-being without
considering its buffering capability in online shopping settings
with the high rate of platform usage and information over-
saturation. Thus, the impact of MF on the association between
digital experiences-PI is not well comprehended as to whether it
enhance or undermine the relationship. The other signi ﬁcant
weakness of the existing body of knowledge is its high level of
Westernization. The majority of the empirical investigations have
been carried out in individualistic cultures like the United States
and the European nations with relatively stable institutional fra-
mework and established digital markets. In comparison, col-
lectivist and high-uncertainty environments, including the UAE,
have not been suf ﬁciently represented even though they have seen
a dramatic increase in the digital commerce. Social norms, peer
pressure, and group membership are more in ﬂuential on con-
sumer behavior in collectivist societies whereas in high uncer-
tainty context sensitivity to psychological strain and risk is
increased. The theoretical importance of these aspects of culture
is that they are likely to affect the experience of DF by the con-
sumer, embrace DD strategies, and transform these conditions
into PI. This is especially with the fashion retail industry in the
UAE, where an empirical environment is offered to study such
dynamics. The retail of fashion has witnessed an unmatched
digital boom in the UAE digital economy and is ﬁrmly linked to
the social commerce platforms. Social media, where young con-
sumers are eager to be inspired, validated, and socially accepted
are becoming the new channels of Shopper brand interactions.
Meanwhile, the vigor of the promotional materials, in ﬂuencer
advertising, and peer recommendations provide an environment
that is very favorable to DF. Nevertheless, this has seen the
fashion retail being given little attention in past study which
has mainly concentrated on banking and technology industries.
Such an oversight restricts industry-speci ﬁc understanding of
the nature of DF interacting with the expression of identity and
social consumption. The discipline is also limited by methodo-
logical gaps. A signi ﬁcant percentage of the current research is
based on cross-sectional research designs, which measure digital
behavior at one time and are only limited in terms of causation.
Besides, there is a lack of research that combines DD, DF, and PI
in a single system of analysis that can be used to test the med-
iation and moderation possibilities. The lack of these industry-
sensitive and methodologically strong methods enabled previous
studies to generate generalized results that cannot be used to
answer the highly subtle relationships among disengagement,
fatigue, and PI in competitive online markets. The combination of
these gaps is the reason why a more detailed study that integrate
DD, DF, and MF into a moderated mediation framework is
necessary and implemented to an under-researched ﬁeld and
setting the fashion retail industry in the UAE. The investigation is
likely to help not only the development of the theory but also the
practical advice to companies that conduct their activities in
highly dynamic and unstable digital settings where it is still a
challenge to maintain purchase intention in case of the DF. To
this end, the following is a model oriented sequence of the
objectives of this research:
1. To examine the direct impact of DD on DF and PI within
the context of social commerce.
2. To analyse the direct in ﬂuence of DF on PI.
3. To test the mediating role of DF in the relationship between
DD-PI.

Theoretical background
The fast growth of digital technologies has completely changed
the way people experience social commerce. Although unbroken
connectivity has improved accessibility, convenience, and
engagement, it has also created the circumstances of mental
overload and emotional exhaustion, and as time goes on, sus-
tained engagement in social commerce becomes more and more
challenging. The resulting psychological burden has stepped up
the academic interest in DF and in DD as a coping mechanism.
This increasing interest notwithstanding, the manner in which
consumers handle fatigue and still remain PI has not been well
theorized, especially in social commerce set ups. The current
research contributes to the development of the theory by com-
bining Conservation of Resources (COR) theory (Hobfoll, 1989)
and the Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) (Ajzen, 1991)t o
determine the role of DD and MF in in ﬂuencing PI via DF.
Combining the two theories offers an equivalent explanatory
basis: COR offers an explanation as to why consumers feel fati-
gued and need to disengage, whereas TPB offers an explanation as
to how alterations in the psychological resources are converted
into purposeful consumer behavior. The COR theory states that
people have a drive to obtain, defend, and renew the valuable
resources, including attention, cognitive capacity, emotional
energy, and self-control. In case of threat or depletion of these
resources, people are stressed and strive to avoid the further loss
of these resources. Consumers in social commerce settings are
actively spending limited psychological resources by being bom-
barded with advertisements, content by in ﬂuencers, comparisons
with peers, and algorithmic suggestions. The empirical studies
have proved that at least this continuous digital stimulation cause
mental fatigue, frustration, and distraction, which are the fun-
damental features of DF (Al ﬁna et al., 2024; Li et al., 2021).
According to the COR perspective, DF is a resource depletion
state, in which attentional/emotional resources are depleted. In
turn, consumers can use DD as a self-defense and recuperative
measure. In this paper, a conceptualization of DD understand it
as a strategic, temporary and deliberate consumer decision that
seeks to reduce digital usage to safeguard and restore exhausted
resources. The previous empirical data suggests that the con-
sumers practicing DD feel more focused, less stressed, and their
psychological well-being is higher (Ardyan and Sanapang, 2023;
Hoang et al., 2024). DD allow consumers to reclaim cognitive and
emotional resources by involving reallocation of the resources to
more intentional and meaningful decision-making, such as pur-
chase related behavior. This process is especially relevant to the
UAE fashion retail market, where consumers are under the heavy
pressure of advertisements and high social standards in terms of
appearance and the need to follow trends. Although COR
explains the processes of resource depletion and recovery, it fails
to explain the processes of converting restored resources into PI.
This change can be explained by the TPB. TPB assumes that three
major factors in ﬂuence PI, which include attitude to behavior,
behavioral control, and subjective norms. DD may have a bene-
ﬁcial impact on these TPB elements. To begin with, removing the
excessive digital stimuli, consumers can learn to have more
positive attitudes to online purchasing, viewing it as a less
stressful, more entertaining process. Second, DD increases the
perceived control of behavior by supporting the strength of the
belief that consumers are able to control the digital usage instead
of being forced by the omnipresence. Empirical research indicates
that consumers are more predisposed to develop positive PI
caused by autonomy than required when they feel that they have
more control over their digital behavior (Su et al., 2024; Ismail
and Siddiqui, 2025). Resource restoration (COR) in this respect
feeds directly onto the attitudinal and control beliefs (TPB),
giving a very explicit way in which psychological recovery is
converted into behavioral intention. Therefore, the relationship
between COR and TPB is serial: COR describes the recovery of
resources based on DD, and TPB describes how the said recovery
inﬂuences PI. MF also helps to reinforce this theoretical frame-
work as a psychological boundary condition that in ﬂuences the
effectiveness of resources restoration and utilization. MF can be
described as the non-judgmental, present-focused awareness and
is found to decrease stress, increase cognitive clarity, and self-
regulation in consumer decision-making (Peng et al., 2022;H u
et al., 2024). In the COR-TPB system, MF is a moderator that
inﬂuences the fatigue experience and the effectiveness of DD.
High MF complements the advantages of DD by making sure that
the restored resources are diverted into deliberate, value-similar
decision-making, which makes PI stronger. On the other hand,
MF counters the ill-effect of DF by allowing consumers to process
digital stimuli with more balance and emotion control. Despite
the fact that MF has been recognized as a predictor of well-being,
its mediating impact on a social commerce setting has been
underutilized, especially in the case of PI. Although DD can
mitigate fatigue and replenish resources, there is a countering
mechanism that should be taken into consideration. Less digital
engagement can also lead to loss of brand exposure, brand recall,
and availability of promotional information by consumers which
might have an adverse impact on PI. This contradiction points to
the paradox of DD: in as much as it reinstates psychological
resources, too much disengagement can diminish market visibi-
lity and transactional possibilities. The proposed framework
explains this paradox by including MF as a moderating variable,
so that mindful consumers can have a more balanced way of
disengaging and re-engaging selectively. This combined model
has been found to be especially relevant to the fashion retail
industry in the UAE. Collectivist character of UAE culture pre-
disposes to DF as consumers are not only overwhelmed with
information but also subjected to powerful social in ﬂuences to

Digital detox and purchase intention . The connection between
DD-PI in social commerce can be interpreted through the lens of
the restoration of psychological resources and the intentionally
conscious consumer behavior as a result of temporary dis-
connectivity to digital platforms. The literature is consistent on
the assumption that excessive exposure to digital stimuli produces
cognitive overload and decision fatigue that in turn impairs the
capacity of consumers to effectively process information and
make purchase decisions (Li and Ma, 2025; Vihari et al., 2022).
To that extent, DD itself is a self-regulating mechanism that
unloads and provides consumers with a new clarity and focus.
Support on media and technology behavior demonstrates that
persons who every now and then decelerate on online channels
are more probable to report enhanced focus, well-being, and
prudent choices, which is an indication that detox indirectly
supports consumer ratings of brand and buying prospects (Sali-
mah and Rufaidah, 2025; Ji et al., 2025). There are also empirical
studies that show that consumers who have more control over
their usage of digital technology also experience a more positive
sense of autonomy that positively affects their PI (Tan et al.,
2025). Similarly, studies on online wellness assume that a state of
disengagement through excessive use of online technology is
linked to more purposeful and value-congruent consumption
decision-making since they less prone to making unreasonable
purchases and more prone to align their decisions with personal
preference and satisfaction (Sun et al., 2022). It may be con-
sidered avoidance but detox may also be a tactical action in social
business settings where peer pressure and promotional noise are
likely to dominate consumer behavior (Shaik and Indupalli,
2025). Collectively, these results imply that DD through exposure
minimization and restoration of cognitive and affective equili-
brium, can positively in ﬂuence consumers in their ability to
consider alternatives and develop more robust PI. This argument
results into the following hypothesis:
H1: There is a positive impact of DD on PI in social commerce.
Digital detox and digital fatigue . DD has come to be more seen
as a behavioral reaction to the omnipresent pressure of being
digitally connected. In the heart of this reaction lies the struggle to
deal and alleviate DF, the condition of mental fatigue and
annoyance caused by information over-load and constant com-
munication with online platforms. It has already been established
by previous research that overconsumption of social media and
other digital platforms consumes cognitive resources, disrupted
attentional control, and caused emotional exhaustion (Hu et al.,
2024; Hoang et al., 2024). Detox practices, such as short pauses or
longer disconnections, become practices in such settings that
enable an individual to conserve or restore depleted psychological
resources (Li et al., 2021). Empirical evidence proves that a break
in digital space can relieve stress and concentrate attention and
reduce the overwhelm of stimuli, reducing the level of fatigue. To
demonstrate that, (Sun et al., 2022) demonstrated that structured
periods of detoxing increased focus and perceived digital fatigue,
and (Vihari et al., 2022) established that even short social media
breaks made people feel more mentally coherent and less irri-
tated. On the same note, research on workplace digital overload
veriﬁes that voluntary avoidance of emails and platforms has a
direct beneﬁt to burnout and cognitive load (McLean et al., 2025).
All these ﬁndings highlight the protective role of DD, not in the
sense of avoidance, but in the sense of active coping to the
increasing issue of DF. The danger of fatigue is increased in the
context of the fashion retail sector in the UAE, where advertising
content and campaigns with in ﬂuencers and peer-to-peer
schemes are consumed by consumers at an unremitting pace.
In this case, detox is a strategic tool of resource conservativeness
that protects a consumer against content overload and saves their
cognitive-emotional energy. The process of disengagement can
more effectively enable people to control their exposure and help
to minimize the chances of exhaustion that impairs their ability to
engage in meaningful digital interactions. On the basis of this
theoretical argument and empirical evidence, the hypothesis is as
follows:
H2: DD negatively affects DF.
Digital fatigue and purchase intention . DF has become one of
the most urgent psychological effects of extended use of online
resources, especially where promotional messages are abundant,
as well as interpersonal communication. DF can be de ﬁned as a
condition of cognitive fatigue, irritability, and lack of motivation
caused by persistent experiences on the digital platform and
impairs effectiveness in information processing and willingness to
perform goal-oriented behaviors in users (Zhong and Gu, 2024;
Suryawan, 2025). In the framework of social commerce, the
exhaustion has a direct effect, as it undermines attention and
reduces trust in the digital interactions and general consumer
satisfaction (Tan et al., 2025). The evidence-based results are
always pointing towards the adverse consequences of DF on the
consumer behavior. It has been demonstrated that stressed-out
consumers tend to wear out more and get less engaged with
digital platforms, less attentive to marketing materials, and
develop lower motivation to buy (Ji et al., 2025; Sun et al., 2022).
On the same note, research on the internet advertisement and
e-commerce setting indicates that cognitive responsiveness is
diminished due to overstimulation and constant use of promo-
tional information and this results in decreasing PI (Ismail and
Siddiqui, 2025). The results are also consistent even in working
and learning settings, where DF has been a focus of study,
showing that exhaustion negatively affects the performance of the
task and the achievement of the goals, representing the more
generalized behavioral outcomes of this phenomenon (Al ﬁna
et al., 2024). The impacts of DF are especially relevant in the case
of the UAE fashion retail industry. In this industry, customers are
inundated with digital stimuli in the form of in ﬂuencer market-
ing, ﬂash sales, among other forms of protracted stimulus that not
only wears their attention out but also creates avoidance and
skepticism. The exhaustion here is in terms of opposition to
platform use, distrust toward promotional messages, and thus low
purchasing probability. The sum of all this is the indication that
DF is a potent inhibitor of PI in the social commerce settings.
Depending on these arguments, as well as previous empirical
evidence, the hypothesis is the following:
H3: DF as a negative effect on PI in social commerce.
Mediating effect of digital fatigue . The interaction between DD,
DF and PI could be more comprehensively viewed through the
prism of mediation where fatigue is the psychological process that
clariﬁes why and how consumers behave in response to detox in
the purchasing process. The available literature proves that the
constant experience of the digital platform exhausts the cognitive

Moderating effect of mindfulness . Although DD is a way by
which consumers regain psychological resources and their
autonomy in digital settings, the level to which this restoration
correlates to an increase in PI is unlikely to be consistent from
person to person. There is emerging evidence that the critical
factor in ﬂuencing the way consumers bene ﬁt from the resource-
restoration aspects of DD is the use of MF — non-judgmental,
present-moment awareness. Studies already conducted show that
MF has consistently been found to improve self-regulation,
decrease stress levels, and increase thoughtful and deliberate
decision-making (Hu et al., 2024; Aguvaveedi, 2025). So, it means
highly mindful consumers are more capable of using the psy-
chological resources they gained from detox in a conscious
manner and directing them towards conscious buying decisions.
In particular, mindful consumers have more ability to stop and
consider other options and their decisions of what to buy in terms
of their values and long-term objectives. This is supported by
empirical research which found that the mindful possess better
impulse control and higher congruence between values and
purchases (Shaik and Indupalli, 2025). Furthermore, it has been
demonstrated that MF can also enhance the ability of consumers
to distinguish information in an information-rich context and
inform their purposeful decision making even in the presence of
persuasive overload (Vihari et al., 2022). On the other hand, low
MF individuals are less inclined to bene ﬁt from DD, due to their
unsuited cognitive and emotional ability to use the recovered
resources in purposeful purchasing actions. Instead, they may
return to a reactive engagement pattern that does not positively
contribute to PI. This is especially relevant in the case of the
fashion industry in the UAE, where an individual is constantly
bombarded with advertising campaigns, trendsetters, and strong
cultural norms about appearance and fashionability. For high MF,
DD offers a psychological playground to re ﬂect, refocus and
reengage with brands in a way that would be aligned with their
preference and identity, enhancing PI. The bene ﬁts of detox,
however, may be less pronounced for less discerning consumers
who are unable to effectively channel resources back to strategic
purchasing behavior. Combined, these arguments suggest that the
positive relationship between DD-PI is greater for consumers
with higher MF. Thus, we hypothesize:
H5: MF moderates the relationship between DD-PI, such that
the positive effect of DD on PI is stronger when MF is high than
when it is low.
The negative impact of DF has been consistently demonstrated
on consumer outcomes in social commerce contexts, such as PI
(Zarantonello et al., 2024; Su et al., 2024). However, importantly
this negative relationship is not always robust, depending on the
level of psychological resources which consumers have, with the
most obvious being MF. Although MF is generally acknowledged
as a protective factor against stress, recent literature has suggested
that its functions in the context of digital commerce are more
complex. Highly mindful people are able to step back psycho-
logically from the fatigue which can result from excessive digital
use, due to their ability to observe the feelings arising, regulate
them, and not “catch them in the act ”. The ability to respond with
MF, to regulate the feelings that arise, and to not “catch them in
the act ” enables highly mindful people to not get caught up in
their exhaustion from digital overload, limiting the extent to
which fatigue affects purchase-related thinking. Importantly, this
does not mean that DF is ever good and/or positively affects PI.
Conversely, MF serves as a buffer which reduces the negative
impact of DF on PI: The negative impact of fatigue on PI is
smaller for consumers with high MF than for consumers with low
MF. This buffering mechanism is explained by the fact that
mindful consumers have better attentional control and emotional
balance, allowing them to process digital information in a
balanced way and to not completely disengage from content
related to commercial activities even when they feel tired (Peng
et al., 2022; Hu et al., 2024). Whereas consumers with low MF do
not have these regulatory capacities, and when they have DF, the
negative effects of low MF on consumers ’ PI are exacerbated
because the effects of DF are more likely to be translated into
avoidance, skepticism, and reduced motivation to purchase. Such
a moderated logic is especially important because of the UAE
fashion retail environment, as high-intensity digital marketing
and high levels of social conformity together increase fatigue. In
this environment, mindful consumers can keep their purchase
engagement from fashion retail platforms within reasonable
bounds, while less mindful consumers are likely to either lose
interest in the platform completely or become increasingly weary
of even considering purchasing from it. Accordingly, we
hypothesize:

Methodology
The empirical context of this study is the UAE fashion retail
industry and consumers who are active in social commerce
websites with experience in online shopping in the fashion
industry. The country has seen high levels of digital penetration, a
high culture of in ﬂuencers and a strong fashion consumption
culture, making this setting an ideal choice. The ﬁrst screening
factor was active users of social commerce platforms and the
second screening factor was people with previous online shopping
experience of fashion products, as indicated in the study, a pur-
posive convenience sampling method was adopted to target active
social commerce platform users, and fashion products online
shopping experience. This sampling approach enabled access to a
digitally-engaged consumer group that is relevant to the research
questions but, as with all sampling, suffers from the constraint of
sampling bias, thereby limiting the generalizability of the results
to the wider population of fashion retailers in the UAE. A total of
297 complete and valid responses were obtained, which was
considered suf ﬁcient for SEM, especially to test the complex
model with the moderating effects. Data were gathered using a
combination of on-line survey distribution and on-line intercept-
based approaches in the major metropolitan centers of the UAE.
This two-pronged approach was used to increase demographic
diversity and also minimize bias from using a single data col-
lection platform. As for the respondent information, while most
of the respondents are from the UAE fashion industry, part of the
respondents’ sample is South Asian expatriates, who form a sig-
niﬁcant and active part of the UAE retail ecosystem. All income
values in this study are presented in UAE Dirhams (AED) in
order to be consistent and contextually appropriate. Future stu-
dies could further standardize income reporting practices to make
it easier to compare studies. A structured questionnaire was used
with both online and of ﬂine administration to ensure high
accessibility and participation by covering a broad age span,
occupational level and digital engagement. The high percentage of
return (78.4%) indicated a high level of participation and sup-
ported the strength of the sample. To maintain linguistic and
cultural equivalence, a forward-backward translation method was
used following the procedures used in previous cross-cultural
research. The pilot study with 30 respondents of the total
population yielded some minor changes in wording and sequence
of items to make them clearer and easily understood. Key con-
structs were operationalised through the use of existing scales
from the literature. To ensure the cultural relevance and/or
ambiguity of items was removed and the conceptual integrity of
each construct maintained, item reduction and contextual
adaptation were performed. The revised scales were also exam-
ined by academics with expertise in consumer behavior and
ecommerce to enhance content validity. While a full-scale psy-
chometric revalidation was not conducted in this study, the use of
previously validated scales, along with pilot testing, suggests that
construct validity is reasonably ensured. The main analytical
technique was SEM, which was particularly well-suited for the
analysis of complex relations among several constructs and
interaction effects. In this research, for example, AMOS (Analysis
of Moment Structures) has been used instead of PLS-SEM
because it is a research with con ﬁrmatory purpose. AMOS is a
covariance based SEM tool that is ideal for rigorously testing
theory and con ﬁrming models, allowing for the use of global
goodness-of-ﬁt indices (CFI, TLI, RMSEA) to assess whether the
hypothesized model ﬁts the observed data. PLS-SEM has been
mainly developed for exploratory and predictive studies, however,
in the context of the present study, where well-established theo-
retical relationships are tested, this approach is not the most
suitable choice. Furthermore, AMOS explicitly models measure-
ment error, which increases the accuracy of estimates of the
parameters extracted from self-reported and contextually adapted
scales. The software also allows you to analyze moderated-
mediation effects, which are important in the investigation of MF
as a moderating variable. The number of respondents in the study
is 297 which is suitable for the AMOS-based SEM to obtain
reliable parameter estimates for the model in the study. The cross
sectional design of the data highlights the associations that are
statistically signi ﬁcant and not causality. Despite the methodo-
logical rigor employed, there are a few limitations recognized. The
sampling method is purposive and non-probability based, thus,
the ﬁndings from this study cannot be generalized to the whole
population of UAE fashion retail consumers. Secondly, the cross-
sectional nature of the design precludes causal inference, although
exploratory effort testing of theories is appropriate. Third, how-
ever, the use of self-reported data creates the potential for
response distortion even though measures were taken to correct
for common method bias (CMB) in the procedure and in the
statistics. Fourth, the cross-cultural construct equivalence of
established scales is questionable as they are adapted and reduced
in the context. Future studies may work to overcome these lim-
itations by incorporating probability sampling, longitudinal
designs, and formal, cross-cultural scale validation procedures.
However, the high return rate, the validated measurement scales
and pilot testing, along with the rigorous SEM analysis with
AMOS, give strong empirical support for the proposed inter-
relationships between variables for UAE fashion retailers.

Discussion
The present results support the hypothesis that DD is an
important factor in the formation of PI in social commerce, both
directly and indirectly through the mediator of DF and moder-
ated by MF. The ﬁndings con ﬁrm existing evidence and also
critically re ﬂect on contemporary discourse on how consumers
regulate their digital engagement in the increasingly enticement
and information overlord online environment. The positive
association between DD-PI aligns with the theoretical under-
pinning of the research on digital well-being and how intentional
disengagement serves as a restorative process (Hu et al., 2024;
Mursaleen et al., 2024). Signi ﬁcantly, our results build on this
literature by showing that the restorative properties of DD have
psychological consequences, but also measurable behavioral
consequences, which impact directly consumer decision-making
in social commerce environments. This challenges the assump-
tion, implicit in the existing social commerce research, that
continuous, uninterrupted engagement is good. Our ﬁndings,
however, suggest that withdrawal can actually improve the quality
and purpose of engagement that follows, all while being tem-
porary and strategic (Su et al., 2024; Zarantonello et al., 2024). A
central psychological mechanism that links DD to PI is fatigue as
a mediator, according to the mediating role of DF. Traditionally,
DF has been seen as an accumulation of negative effects from
digital over-exposure while our results show that DD can be a
positive intervention to mitigate fatigue and therefore promote
PI. This rede ﬁnition addresses not only the passive nature of
digital environments as an outcome of digital behaviors, but
posits DF as an intervening variable that in ﬂuences consumer
choice and connects the literature on digital overload
(Aguvaveedi, 2025) with consumer engagement theory. Speci ﬁ-
cally, the results corroborate the decrease in DF from DD (in
support of H2) and this decrease in fatigue, in turn, increases PI
(in support of H3 and H4). Fatigue is therefore not only a psy-
chological cost of digital over-engagement, it is also a channel, in
which the restorative digital behaviors translate into economic
results; a subtle nuance that enriches existing knowledge of the
cognitive and emotional side of social commerce. The range or
conditions within which DD is most effective at improving PI is
described by the moderating effect of MF. As noted in previous
studies on digital overload and compulsive use (Zhong and Gu,
2024; Binh, 2025; McLean et al., 2025; Li and Ma, 2025), those
with high MF scores show greater self-regulation and adaptive
coping. Analytically, we have shown that the positive impact of
DD on PI is far more pronounced with high MF consumers and
much less pronounced (and not signi ﬁcant) with low MF con-
sumers. This differential impact calls into question assumptions
of universalistic effectiveness of DD, highlighting the importance
of dispositional factors with regard to whether and how disen-
gagement can lead to favorable engagement outcomes. Besides, in
the low-MF group, the pathway of reduction of fatigue becomes a
relatively larger pathway towards stimulating PI, indicating that
experimental reduction of fatigue —rather than the detox beha-
vior itself —plays a comparatively larger role in driving
improvements in purchase-related outcomes for low-MF con-
sumers. This study builds on an integrated perspective of DF as a
mediator and MF as a moderator, creating a process-oriented
model that merges different lines of research related to technol-
ogy fatigue, digital overload and self-regulation. The current
model reveals that in contrast to previous studies, which have
investigated consumer well-being or consumer behavior sepa-
rately, consumer well-being and commerce results are inter-
dependent, with the effectiveness of digital disengagement
strategies determined jointly by mindfulness and fatigue. Overall,
these ﬁndings offer three main theoretical contributions: (1) they
provide evidence of the quanti ﬁable and economically relevant
effects of DD that are mediated by psychological mechanisms; (2)
they reveal that individual differences (indicated by MF) mod-
erate the impact of DD; and (3) they bring together previously
independent pieces of literature into a moderated-mediation
framework that explains how the psychological processes of dis-
engagement, fatigue reduction, and self-regulation interact with
one another to in ﬂuence consumer behavior in the context of
social commerce.
Theoretical contributions . The research has several speci ﬁc
theoretical contributions, which include extending digital con-
sumer research and combining it to existing theory, the COR
theory and the TPB. First, the present study makes a contribution
to the research of DD. Prior studies mainly conceptualize DD as a
mechanism of stress reduction or enhancing attention (Sun et al.,
2022; Ji et al., 2025), our results show that DD can also have
measurable downstream effects on consumers ’ behavior, namely
on PI in social commerce. This helps to ﬁll the divide between
digital well-being research and consumer behavior research by
demonstrating positive psychological outcomes in addition to
deliberate, goal-oriented consumer behavior outcomes of
resource-restorative practices from a COR perspective. In the
COR paradigm, DD can be conceptualized as a replenishing
process; DD stimulates the re-in ﬁltration of depleted cognitive
and emotional resources which enhances consumers ’ decision
making ability to make deliberate purchase intentions. Second,
from the perspective of information systems and marketing, the
study provides novel ﬁndings, as it proposes to consider DF as an

the end, the moderated-mediation model proposed here brings
together other well spread studies from psychology, information

COR and TPB, by presenting a unifying framework that inte-
grates previously established constructs (DD, DF, MF and PI) and
provides a better understanding of digital behaviors as resource
management processes in online consumer contexts (extending
COR) as well as demonstrating how individual differences and

dispositional traits, cognition and intention formation; and a
moderated-mediation model is developed that extends the

social commerce. As a whole, these essays help to develop the
theory’s accuracy in digital consumer research, as they shed light

characteristics of an individual and the conscious act of con-
sumption in a digital market.
Managerial implications . From the study ’s results, there are
three implications for managers, marketers, and policy makers in
social commerce. The ﬁrst step should be for retailers and digital
platform creators to take steps to reduce DF and ensure a
“regulated” disengagement. Some examples include break
reminders, design features that make it easy for users to take short
breaks without completely leaving the platform, and individua-
lized noti ﬁcation settings. Strategies to balance the novelty of
content and its relevance to users, including the selection of
content volume, recommendation algorithms and interface
designs, can facilitate user engagement while reducing cognitive
overload and its impact on PI. Secondly, marketers can shape the
promotion of mindful consumption by creating campaigns that
take into account moments of re ﬂection and allow products to
become the sign of an intentional and meaningful action. Well-
ness partners or more message-oriented approaches could be
beneﬁcial for fostering consumer trust and loyalty, especially
when it comes to highly mindful consumers who bene ﬁt more
from DD practices. Third, companies can be involved in pro-
moting digital well-being by educating employees about the
importance of MF and “mindful disconnection. Responsible use
of platforms not only improves the consumer experience, but is
also in line with the general direction of society in the ﬁeld of
digital health, thus supporting the brand image and ethical
positioning. Each of these strategies provides opportunities for
companies to keep customers engaged, prevent information
overload and help develop trust-based, long-term relationships.
Limitations and future research directions . This study helps to
ﬁll gaps in the literature in the areas of DD, DF, MF, and PI;
however, there are a number of limitations and avenues for fur-
ther research. First, this study relies on a cross-sectional survey
design and analysis of the data through SEM analysis with
moderated mediation. The design is cross-sectional and not sta-
tistically valid to draw ﬁrm causal conclusions. Longitudinal or
experimental designs are recommended for future investigation of
the development of DD behaviors and the development of PI,
depending on the level of digital exposure. These types of designs
would also allow for a dynamic analysis of proposed resource
recovery processes from the COR theory. Second, the respondents
were mainly consumers from the UAE. Cultural norms, digital
usage patterns and the presence and type of MF may differ from
region to region and country to country. The model needs to be
replicated in various cultural and economic settings, including
developed and emerging markets, to evaluate its generalizability
or cultural speci ﬁcity of the proposed relationships. Third, a
procedural and statistical approach was used to mitigate possible
CMB, but the self-reported measures are still subject to social
desirability and recall biases. There is a need to further validate
the self-reported data with behavioral data from platform ana-
lytics (e.g., actual time spent online, purchase histories, observed
detox behaviors) to improve construct validity and reduce bias in
future research. Combining methods such as survey, experiment
and digital trace data would provide a more holistic view of
consumer behavior. Fourth, other individual and contextual fac-
tors could further shape the relationships between DD, DF and PI
beyond moderation by MF. Digital trust, perceived in ﬂuencer
authenticity, psychological resilience, cultural values and platform
design features may be considered mediators/moderators in
future research. Fifth, the study did not distinguish between
short-term micro-detox behaviors (e.g., going to sleep without
checking noti ﬁcations) and longer term disconnection (e.g., tak-
ing extended social media breaks). Further research would
examine the differences between different types and lengths of
detox along with voluntary and enforced disconnection (e.g.,
technical outages) to improve theoretical understanding of the
effects of DD on fatigue and purchase behavior. Sixth, this study
was narrow in scope, targeting only social commerce platforms
that feature integrated in ﬂuencer marketing, peer communication
and shopping features. Future studies could explore the gen-
eralizability of the observed relationships to other digital envir-
onments, including traditional e-commerce platforms, mobile
gaming systems, or online learning environments. Overall, the
small R² values achieved in this study suggested that the existing
model was only a partial solution to the variance of PI and DF
and it was only theoretically meaningful. This reveals the
potential for omitted variables, and the need for more complex
models in future studies. Future research needs to include further
predictors, including trust, perceived value, platform engagement
intensity, and personality traits, to further enhance the explana-
tory power. Overall, future studies are recommended to focus on
methodological innovation, such as the use of longitudinal and
experimental design, incorporating behavioral and platform data

Conclusion
The aim of this study was to test the hypothesis on how DD can affect
PI with references to the moderated effect by DF and the mediated
effect by MF. The outcomes symbolize DD as having both direct and
indirect effects on consumer behavior. More speciﬁcally, DD lowers
DF, and such decrement DF increases PI, thus conﬁrming mediation.
Moreover, the strength of these relationships was moderated by MF,
whereby DD MP PI was MF high and DF MP PI was MF low. Overall,
these ﬁndings illustrate the intricate and multi-faceted nature of the
inﬂuence of digital well-being practices on consumer behavior in
digital marketplaces. From a theoretical standpoint, the study adds to
the body of work on social commerce and digital consumption by
proposing DD as a signi ﬁcant PI predictor and DF to explain psy-
chologically. It proposes MF as one of the critical boundary conditions,
which adds to the understanding of consumer well-being and self-
regulation during online interactions. In the practical domain the
ﬁndings inform the social commerce managers and marketers that it is
fundamental to prepare plans that alleviate the consumer’s need to rest
in order to balance MF-aimed stra tegies to foster engagement and
trust. At the same time, cross-sectional design study limitations, helps
to enhance customers wellbeing and business sustainability.
