Abstract
This study aims to reveal how digital convenience, online promotions, and instant
gratification tendency influence repurchase intention among students of the Faculty of Islamic
Economics and Business at State Islamic University of North Sumatra who actively use
TikTok Shop. Alongside the rapid growth of social commerce, consumer behavior patterns
have undergo ne significant transformation, consequently making repurchase intention a
critical indicator of platform sustainability in the digital marketplace. To address this
objective, the study employed a quantitative approach based on explanatory research. Data
were collected through questionnaires distributed to 108 respondents selected purposively
according to predetermined criteria. The analytical technique applied was Partial Least
Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM). The results showed that digital
convenience and instant gratification tendency exerted a positive and significant influence on
repurchase intention, while online promotions did not confirm a statistically meaningful
effect. In conclusion, digital convenience and instant gratification pro ved to be dominant,
while online promotions were not significant. These findings suggest that strengthening the
quick-shopping experience is more crucial than promotional gimmicks in maintaining
consumer loyalty on social commerce platforms.  Theoretically, this study extends the
application of the Theory of Planned Behavior to the social commerce context by
demonstrating how digital convenience corresponds to perceived behavioral control and how
instant gratification tendency reflects attitude toward behavi or, offering novel insights into
consumer behavior on short-form video-based platforms.

LITERATURE REVIEW
This study is grounded in the Theory of
Planned Behavior (TPB) proposed by Icek
Ajzen as the primary framework for
understanding individual behavior. This
theory is essentially an extension of the
Theory of Reasoned Action  (TRA), which
initially explained that an individual's
actions are largely determined by their
intentions and attitudes toward the beha -
vior. In its expansion, Ajzen incorporated
the dimension of perceived behavioral
control as a variable representing the
extent to which an individual feels capable
and able to perform a behavior. With this
addition, it becomes evident that behavior
is not merely a product of attitudes and
social pressures, but is also influenced by
an individual's beliefs regarding their ow n
competence (Liao et al., 2007) . Grounded
in TPB, actual actions are derivatives of
behavioral intention that emerge through
cognitive evaluation processes. Such inten-
tions do not arise spontaneous ly; rather,
they are shaped by three main pillars:
attitude toward behavior, subjective norm,
and perceived behavioral control, which
synergistically form an individual's readi -
ness to act.
Within the Theory of Planned Behavior
(TPB), attitude toward behav ior describes
an individual's evaluation of a particular
behavior based on the perceived benefits or
consequences of that action. Subjective
norm confirms the extent to which an
individual perceives influence or expecta -
tions from their social environment regard-
ing the decision to be made. Meanwhile,
perceived behavioral control reflects an
individual's beliefs about their capability
and the ease of performing a given action
(De Groot & Steg, 2007) . The greater an
individual's belief that a behavior can be

Repurchase Intention
Repurchase intention describes consu -
mers' desire to repurchase products or
services through the same platform after
having prior transaction experiences. In
marketing studies, this concept is widely
utilized as an indicator to evaluate the level
of consumer loyalty toward a brand or
digital plat form. Repurchase intention
generally emerges after consumers eva -
luate the experiences gained during the
consumption process. Referring to the
cognitive, affective, and conative model,
this process begins with a cognitive
assessment of product information or
attributes, followed by the formation of an
affective response, which subsequently
drives the emergence of behavioral inten -
tion in the form of repurchase (Chien et al.,
2025). Meanwhile, according to the
Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) deve -
loped by Icek Ajzen, repurchase intention
is perceived as behavioral intentio n,
namely an individual's readiness to per -
form an action. This intention is formed
through three main constructs: attitude
toward behavior, subjective norm, and
perceived behavioral control. These three
factors collectively influence an indivi -
dual's deci sion-making process before
deciding to make a repeat purchase on the
same platform.
Within the e -commerce domain, the
desire to repurchase does not arise
suddenly, but rather through a series of
cognitive and evaluative processes
grounded in consumers' pas t experiences.
Various recent studies reveal that variables
such as perceived value, satisfaction levels,
and assessments of digital service quality
play significant roles in shaping repurchase
intention. A study conducted by Etha et al.
(2024) indicated t hat online repurchase
intention is significantly influenced by
perceived value and consumers' views
toward the platform used. When the
benefits obtained exceed expectations,
consumers' tendency to transact again
becomes higher  (Etha et al., 2024) .
Meanwhile, Nurbaiti  et al. (2024) found
that reviews and ratings from other
customers on TikTok Shop influence
students' purchase interest. This situation
confirms that consumer decisions are not
solely derived from individual experiences,
but also from the collective evalua tions of
other users (Nurbaiti et al., 2024) . Further-
more, Risma et al. (2024) confirmed that
digital shopping experiences and electronic
service quality positively contribute to
online repurchase intention  (Risma et al.,
2024). These findings align with the
Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB), which
emphasizes that behavioral intention is
formed through considerations and beliefs
that emerge before an individual actually
makes a decision. In this study, repurchase

Digital Convenience
Digital convenience refers to an
individual's assessment of the ease,  speed,
and efficiency offered by a digital system
in supporting transaction activities. This
concept reflects the extent to which users
perceive that the process of information
searching, application navigation, and
payment completion can be carried out
practically and without obstacles (Zeithaml
et al., 2002).
According to the Theory of Planned
Behavior (TPB), digital convenience
directly corresponds to the construct
of perceived behavioral control, which
reflects an individual's  perception of how
easy or difficult it is to perform a particular
behavior (Ajzen, 1991) . The higher the
perceived level of convenience and
efficiency of the digital system, the greater
the individual's belief that they can control
the t ransaction process, thereby
strengthening their intention to repurchase
(Najela, 2019) . Empirical research in
recent years supports this relationship.
Perceived behavioral control has been
shown to have a positive and significant
influence on online purcha se intention
within the Indonesian e-commerce domain,
confirming that the ease of using digital
systems strengthens individuals' tendency
to make purchases (Bangun et al., 2023).
System convenience and digital service
efficiency have be en shown to signi -
ficantly impact repurchase intention among
TikTok Shop users in Indonesia. These
findings reinforce the results of previous
studies on UINSU students, which
revealed that transaction systems such as
cash on delivery (COD) and interface

Instant Gratification Tendency
Within the Theory of Planned Behavior
(TPB), instant gratification tendency
primarily influenc es the  attitude toward
behavior component, as individuals' emo ti-
onal and affective drives shape their
overall evaluation of purchasing activities
as enjoyable and satisfying (Ajzen, 1991).
Consumers with high instant gratification
tendencies tend to form positive attitudes
toward repeat purchases because they
associate the behavior with immediate
emotional rewards and satisfaction. Instant
gratification tendency is an individual's
inclination to obtain satisfaction quickly
without delaying desires, despite potential
long-term consequences (Shiddiq &
Taufik, 2024; Sholikhah, 2025) . This
characteristic relates to strong affective
drives in the consumption decision-making
process, particularly within digital environ-
ments that offer convenience and transac -
tion speed.
Empirical research supports this rela -
tionship. Instant gratification signifi cantly
influences purchase intention in social
commerce, confirming that emotionnal
drives to immediately obtain satisfaction
are important factors in strengthening
purchase intentions in digital environments
(Xu et al., 2022) . In addition, positive
emotional experiences in e -commerce
significantly contribute to incre ased repur-
chase intention. This situation confirms
that the faster and more enjoyable the
satisfaction perceived by consumers, the
greater the likelihood that they will make
repeat purchases (Bhatti et al., 2022) . In
this study, instant gratific ation tendency is
measured through several indicators,
namely: tendency to purchase without
delay, preference for short -term satisfac -
tion over long -term considerations, emo -
tional drives in purchase decision -making,
and difficulty resisting urges when see ing
promotions or limited-time offers.
Within the TikTok Shop domain, which
emphasizes flash sales, time -limited
promotions, and checkout convenience,
instant gratification characteristics can
encourage repurchase tendencies if
previous experiences provide d quick and
enjoyable satisfaction. Thus, the higher an
individual's instant gratification tendency,
the greater the likelihood of repurchase
intention formation. The e -commerce
environment, with its various stimuli such
as time -limited promotions, transac tion
convenience, and ease of product access,
can strengthen impulsive purchasing
tendencies due to drives to obtain satis -
faction quickly, making consumers tend to
make immediate purchases. This situation
reinforces that instant grati fication tenden-
cy i s a psychological factor that plays a
role in shaping consumer behavior in
digital environments  (Nurbaiti et al.,
2024).
Empirical evidence regarding the role of
instant gratification tendency in shaping
consumer behavior presents a complex
picture. Studies by Xu et al. (2022) and
Bhatti et al. (2022) found that instant grati -
fication significantly enhances purchase
intention and repurchase intention in
digital environments, as consumers derive
emotional satisfaction from immediate
consumption experiences. Conversely,
other researchers have cautioned that
instant gratification tendencies may lead to
impulsive purchases that subsequently
trigger post -purchase regret, which can
ultimately undermine repurchase intention
(Arif & Manggabarani, 2026) . Further -
more, Venawat (2025) argued that while
instant gratification effectively drives
initial purchases in quick commerce, its
influence on repeat purchases is condi -
tional upon the extent to which consumer
expectations are confirmed. These conflict-
ing perspectives highlight the necessity of
investigating how instant gratification
tendency operates within the TikTok Shop
context, where rapid transactions and time-
limited promotions are prevalent
(Venawat, 2025).

UINSU st udents who have previously

with several criteria, namely: (1) active
students at FEBI State Islamic University
of North Sumatra, (2) have m ade at least

(3) aged between 18 and 23 years, which
falls within the Generation Z category. The

DISCUSSION
Digital Convenience and Repurchase
Intention
From the data processing results using
the PLS-SEM approach, a path coefficient
of 0.333 was obtained, with a t -statistic
value of 4.127 (> 1.96) and a p -value of
0.000 (< 0.05). These figures indicate that
digital convenience has a positive and
significant influence on repurchase inten -
tion. In other words, the higher the
perceived level of convenience users expe -
rience when transacting on TikTok Shop,
the stronger their d rive to repurchase on
the same platform. This finding reinforces
that aspects of convenience in the transac -
tion process play an important role in
shaping consumer behavior patterns in the
digital domain.
From the perspective of the Theory of
Planned Behavior (TPB) developed by
Ajzen, digital convenience is related to the
construct of perceived behavioral control
(Ajzen, 1991). This construct explains that
individuals tend to perform a beh avior
when they feel they have the ability and
control to execute it. Within the context of
this study, ease of navigation, transaction
speed, and payment system efficiency can
strengthen users' perceived control over the
purchasing process. When students per-
ceive that transactions on TikTok Shop are
easy to perform without requiring
significant effort, their belief in making
repeat purchases will increase. Thus,
digital convenience can strengthen repur -
chase intention through enhanced per -
ceived behavioral control.
These findings align with previous
studies that confirmed that perceived ease
of use and digital service quality have a
positive influence on repurchase intention
(P. Sari, 2024) . The results are also
consistent with the research of Kamilah
and Yenni Samri (2024), which explained
that digitalization can enhance service
efficiency while contributing a better user
experience. The easier the access to digital
systems, the higher the convenience
perceived by users, consequently encoura -
ging continued u se of the service. In this
study, ease of application navigation,
payment processes, and information access
on TikTok Shop represent forms of digital
convenience that contribute to the forma -
tion of repurchase intention (Kamilah &
Nasution, 2024). In line with this, ease of
use in digi tal systems can strengthen
repurchase intention through more positive
user experiences. This finding reinforces
that utilitarian factors in technology play
an important role in shaping consumer
behavior in the digital era.
Within the context of FEBI State
Islamic University of North Sumatra stu -
dents, digital convenience is a highly
relevant factor because students, as part of
the digital generation, prioritize practicali -
ty and efficiency. Students tend to choose
platforms that offer fast, easy, and uncom -
plicated transaction experiences. When
TikTok Shop can meet these expectations,
students are more likely to make repeat
purchases without considering many alter -
natives. This situation confirms that high
perceived behavioral control among stu -
dents directl y contributes to increased
repurchase intention.
The finding that digital convenience
significantly influences repurchase inten -

Online Promoti ons and Repurchase

Within the Theory of Planned Behavior

toward behavior, namely an individual's
evaluation of a behavior as valuable or
beneficial. Various forms of promotions
such as discounts, cashback, free shipping,
and f lash sales can shape positive con -
sumer perceptions toward purchasing
activities because they provide economic

transactions. When individuals evaluate
that these promotions are beneficial,
positive attitudes towar d purchasing beha -
vior tend to form. However, within the
context of repurchase, positive attitudes
influenced by promotions are not always
strong enough to strengthen repurchase
intention, especially if consumers place
greater consideration on prior usage expe-
riences.
The findings of this study differ from
several previous studies that suggested
online promotions have a significant
influence on repurchase intention. On the
other hand, there are also studies that
confirm that promotions are more effective
in the short term, particularly for attracting
initial purchases, compared to maintaining
consumer loyalty. This situation indicates
that promotions have limitations in
building long -term relationships with
consumers. These differing findings can be
compared with the research of Nurhayati et
al. (2023), which found that promotional
strategies through social media significant -
ly influence audience decisions. This
discrepancy is presumably due to differ -
ences in research objects, where Nurhayati
et al.'s study  focused on the effectiveness
of digital communication strategies,
whereas this study examines the influence
of online promotions on repur chase
intention among TikTok Shop users. Thus,
digital promotions tend to be more
effective in attracting attention a nd
encouraging initial decisions compared to
strengthening repeat purchases  (Nasution
et al., 2023).
Within the context of FEBI State
Islamic University of North Sumatra stu -
dents, these findings confirm that students
tend no longer to rely on promo tions when
making repeat purchases. Students as
consumers who already have prior expe -
rience place greater considera tion on other
factors such as ease of use and product
satisfaction. In addit ion, the high intensity
of promotions across various platforms
leads to promotion fatigue, consequently
causing promotions to lose their appeal.

Theoretical and Practical Implications
This study makes several theoretical
contributions to the existing literature.
First, it extends the application of the
Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) to the
social commerce context by demonstrating
how digital convenience corresponds to
perceived behavi oral control and how
instant gratification tendency reflects
attitude toward behavior. Unlike previous
studies that applied TPB to conventional e-
commerce settings (Bangun et al., 2023) ,
this research shows that the theory is
equally ap plicable in short -form video -
based social commerce platforms, where
consumer behavior is influenced by both
rational evaluations and emotional
impulses. Second, this study contributes to
the growing body of knowledge on social
commerce by providing empirical evidence
that psychological factors (instant gratify-
cation) exert a stronger influence on
repurchase intention than utilitarian factors
(digital convenience) or marketing stimuli
(online promotions). This finding chal -
lenges the conventional assumption  that
platform convenience and promotional
offers are the primary drivers of customer
loyalty in digital commerce.
From a practical perspective, the
findings offer valuable insights for TikTok
Shop managers and business practitioners.
First, given that dig ital convenience
significantly influences repurchase inten -
tion, platform developers should prioritize
enhancing usability features such as
streamlined navigation, efficient payment
systems, and clear product information.
Second, while online promotions di d not
have a significant effect, this does not
mean they should be abandoned; rather,
they should be strategically designed to
avoid promotion fatigue by offering varied
and personalized incentives rather than
repetitive discounts. Third, the dominant
influence of instant gratification tendency
suggests that businesses should focus on
creating shopping experiences that provide
immediate satisfaction, such as faster
delivery, instant discounts, and interactive
shopping features that evoke emotional
engagement. In addition, customer reten -
tion strategies should move beyond promo-
tional activities and emphasize building
emotional connections with consumers
through personalized content and experien-
tial shopping experiences.

for causal inferences. A longitudinal
approach would be valuable to examine
how the relationships among the variables
evolve over time, particularly in response
to changing promotional strategies and
platform features. Third, the study focused
on only three independent variables: digital
convenience, online promotions, and
instant gratification tendency, leaving
other potential determinants of repurchase
intention, such as trust, product quality,
social influence, and customer satisfaction,
unexplored. Fourth, the reliance on self -
reported questionnaire data may introduce
social desirability and recall bias, as
respondents might overstate or understate
their actual behavior and attitudes.
Although resp ondents were assured of
anonymity and confidentiality, future
research could complement survey data
with behavioral data or experimental
designs to enhance objectivity and validity.

Suggestions
Based on the limitations identified
above, several directions  for future
research are proposed. First, to enhance the
generalizability of the findings, future
studies should extend the sample to include
diverse demographic groups, geographic
regions, and educational backgrounds
beyond students of Islamic higher educ a-
tion institutions. Comparative studies
across different social commerce plat -
forms, such as TikTok Shop, Shopee Live,
Instagram Shopping, and WhatsApp
Business, would also be valuable to
identify platform -specific determinants of
repurchase intention and  to examine
whether the findings hold across different
digital environments.  Second, future
research should incorporate additional
variables that may mediate or moderate the
relationships observed in this study. For
instance, customer satisfaction could be
examined as a mediating variable between
digital convenience and repurchase inten -
tion, while trust could be investigated as a
moderator that strengthens or weakens the
effect of online promotions on repurchase
behavior. Other potential variables include
product quality, social influence, perceived
risk, and brand image, which may provide
a more comprehensive understanding of
the mechanisms underlying repurchase
intention in social commerce con -
texts. Third, a longitudinal research design
is recommended to  examine how the
relationships among the variables evolve
over time and to capture the dynamics of
consumer behavior in response to changing
platform features, promotional strategies,
and market conditions. Such a design
would also allow for the examinatio n of
causal relationships more rigorously than
the cross -sectional approach employed in
this study.  Fourth, the use of mixed -
methods approaches, combining quantita -
tive surveys with qualitative interviews or
focus groups, would provide richer insights
into the underlying motivations, attitudes,
and experiences of social commerce users.
This would allow researchers to explore
not only what factors influence repurchase
intention but also why and how these
factors operate in different contexts.  Fifth,
given th e dominant role of instant
gratification tendency observed in this
study, future research could explore how
specific platform features, such as flash
sales, live streaming, personalized recom -
mendations, and gamification, interact with
consumer psychologic al traits to shape
repurchase behavior. Experimental designs
could be particularly useful in this regard,
allowing researchers to manipulate
platform features and observe their effects
on consumer attitudes and inten -
tions. Finally, cross -cultural studies com-
paring consumer behavior across different
countries or cultural contexts would
provide valuable insights into how cultural
values and norms influence the relation -
ships between digital convenience, online
promotions, instant gratification tendency,
and repurchase intention.
