ABSTRACT
This study clarifies the fundamental distinctions between traditional e-commerce,
social media marketing, and social e -commerce, establishing a conceptual
framework that explains their convergence and divergence in theory and practice.
It examines international experiences where social e -commerce has been
successfully implemented, highlighting Facebook and Amazon as representative
models of virtual enterprises operating in this domain. Using a descriptive –
analytical methodology, data were collected from books, academic articles,
reports, websites, electronic platforms, and personal observations. The findings
indicate that social e-commerce represents an advanced form of e -commerce that
enhances transactions through social interaction, trust building, and user
engagement. Virtual enterprises within this environment create global markets
unconstrained by time and plac e, while promoting digital awareness and
adaptation to rapid technological change. The results demonstrate that social e -
commerce strengthens national economies by encouraging diversification,
improving market efficiency, and supporting sustainable develop ment. The study
concludes that it offers a practical model for developing countries, particularly
Algeria, to advance digital commerce.

Introduction
The modern world is experiencing  swift and significant technological advancements, marked by ongoing
innovations that generate new competitive edges for investors and economic participants. These advancements
encompass sophisticated information systems, electronic monitoring and sensing technologies, and artificial
intelligence, all of which have played a crucial role in shifting business operations from traditional frameworks
to electronic formats and markets from physical locations to virtual settings (Laudon & Traver, 2021; Turban et
al., 2018). This shift became particularly pronounced in the wake of the COVID -19 pandemic, which expedited
digital adoption and dramatically altered consumer behaviour on a global scale (UNCTAD, 2021) .
Social networking platforms such as Facebook, Instagram, Pinterest, and others have become primary channels
for following news, communicating, and sharing personal content. Over time, these platforms have evolved
beyond social interaction to support commer cial activities, enabling users to engage directly in buying and
selling processes (Kaplan & Haenlein, 2010; Hajli, 2015; Liang & Turban, 2020; Li et al., 2019). As a result of
the widespread use of social media, direct selling through these platforms has emerged, giving rise to what is
known as social e -commerce, one of the most recent and rapidly growing trends in electronic commerce
(Busalim & Asadi, 2025; Dincer & Dincer, 2023; Wang et al., 2025).
The concept of social commerce was initially put forth by Yahoo in 2005 to characterize the utilization of user -
generated content (UGC), including reviews and ratings, associated with products, services, and vendors on its
shopping platform. Nevertheless, comparable functionalities had already been adopted by platforms like
Amazon and eBay in the late 1990s, even though they were not explicitly referred to as social commerce during
that period (Wang & Zhang, 2012). The rise of social networking sites (SNSs) and social media applications
greatly facilitated user  participation in commercial endeavours, thereby hastening the advancement of social
commerce. Concurrently, traditional e -commerce platforms swiftly adjusted by incorporating social features,
which further propelled the progression of social commerce (Bus alim & Asadi, 2025). As a result, e -commerce,
Web 2.0 technologies, and social media can be regarded as the three essential pillars that contributed to the
establishment and expansion of s -commerce (Huang & Benyoucef, 2013). Social commerce signifies a nov el
type of commerce where enhanced connections and interactions among users are prioritized in firms’ strategies,
enabling organizations to cultivate closer relationships with customers, tap into the potential of social
interactions, and promote enduring customer loyalty (Li et al., 2019; Alhumud & Elshaer, 2024).
Social e-commerce enables consumers to complete the entire purchasing journey directly within social media
platforms, eliminating the need to be redirected to external online stores. This sets it apart from conventional e -
commerce models, which generally depend on independent websites to finalize transactions (Zhang &
Benyoucef, 2016). Social e -commerce leverages moments of heightened purchase intention and emotional
involvement, promoting immediat e buying decisions through smooth and interactive shopping experiences
(Hajli, 2015). By incorporating social interaction, user -generated content, and peer influence, this model fosters
trust and diminishes perceived risk, allowing consumers to experience comfort, excitement, and entertainment
throughout the shopping process (Kim & Park, 2013).
Prior to making a purchase, social media acts as a significant source of inspiration and product discovery for
consumers. A survey conducted in 2024 revealed that fo ur out of ten social media users indicated that these
platforms aided them in discovering new products and brands. Regarding actual purchases, nearly 25% of global
shoppers identified Facebook as their favoured social commerce platform in 2024, with Instag ram following
closely behind at 20% of respondents. Both platforms, which are owned by Meta, are also recognized for
providing the most favourable social commerce experiences. On a global scale, the product categories most
commonly purchased through social  networks encompass apparel, personal care items, groceries, and food
(Statista, 2025). Social commerce has gained traction across all age groups, with younger consumers exhibiting
the highest engagement. Gen Z, having grown up in the digital era, and mill ennials, possessing greater financial
independence, are particularly active social media shoppers. Both demographics, however, share a strong
preference for entertainment and trustworthiness, valuing the overall shopping experience over mere
transactions. This makes them highly receptive to recommendations from content creators and more likely to
purchase products endorsed by influencers and celebrities. A 2024 study further found that promotions and
streamlined purchasing processes are key factors that enc ourage consumers to engage more actively in social
commerce (Statista, 2025).
In spite of its significant growth, social e -commerce encounters various challenges and barriers that impede its
successful adoption, especially in developing nations  (Boukhedimi & Hammache, 2026).  These challenges
encompass concerns regarding digital trust, data privacy, regulatory frameworks, logistics, a nd digital literacy
(OECD, 2020 ). While a multitude of studies and statistical analyses underscore the achievements of virtual
enterprises within this sector, social e -commerce is still considered a relatively nascent economic phenomenon

1. Research Problem
Despite the rapid growth of social e -commerce and the increasing number of virtual enterprises operating within
this environment, significant variation exists in the levels of success achieved by these enterprises. While some
virtual firms have managed to leverage social media platforms effectively to create value, build trust, and
achieve sustainable competitive advantages, others face structural, technological, and organizational challenges
that hinder their performance. This raises the need to identify and analyse the critical factors that contribute to
the success of virtual enterprises in the field of social e-commerce. Accordingly, the following research question
is proposed:
RQ: What factors contribute to the success of virtual enterprises in social e -commerce, as evidenced by the
cases of Facebook–Instagram and Amazon?
2. Literature Review
The notion of social e -commerce has arisen from the intersection of traditional e -commerce and social
networking technologies. Initial research on e -commerce primarily concentrated on online transactions carried
out via dedicated websites and electronic marketplaces, highlighting efficiency, cost reduction, and market
growth (Turban et al., 2018; Laudon & Traver, 202 1). Nevertheless, with the swift proliferation of Web 2.0
technologies and social media platforms, researchers started to investig ate how social interactions, user
engagement, and online communities affect commercial practices (Kaplan & Haenl ein, 2010; Dincer & Dincer,
2023; Alhumud & Elshaer, 2024; Mubdir et al., 2025).
2.1 Social Electronic Commerce: Concept and Evolution
The concept of social e-commerce was initially articulated by Liang and Turban (20 20), who characterized it as
a branch of e -commerce that leverages social media, online communities, and social networks to enable buying
and selling transactions. In contrast to  conventional e -commerce, social e -commerce incorporates social
interaction, collaboration, and content sharing into the purchasing experience, thus improving consumer
engagement and trust (Hajli, 2015). Social e-commerce can be described as a type of e-commerce that utilizes
established Internet-based social networks to engage and collaborate with prospective customers, differentiating
it from traditional e-commerce.
Moreover, social e -commerce encompasses both specialized e -commerce platforms (such as  Own.com) and
conventional e-commerce marketplaces or retailer websites that integrate social or interactive elements into the
sales process. For instance, Amazon introduced Amazon Live to boost consumer engagement by incorporating
live streaming events into the shopping experience, facilitating real-time interactions between sellers and buyers.
Conversely, Social Media Commerce pertains to traditional social media platforms that integrate commerce
functionalities directly within their interfaces. A notable  example is Instagram, which launched Instagram Shops
and in-app checkout features to create seamless purchasing experiences. A practical illustration is Fresh Beauty,
which employed product tags to enhance its Black Friday campaign across its Instagram pr oduct catalogue.
Consequently, social e -commerce represents a significant economic and social phenomenon that could serve as
a key catalyst for growth in developing nations. It has the capacity to enhance the rate of product turnover,
broaden economic and cultural awareness, and minimize both efforts and expenses. Nevertheless, several
challenges must be tackled to fully harness the potential of social e-commerce in these regions. These challenges
include:
• The need for political will and financial resour ces to invest in the development of the necessary infrastructure,
such as reliable internet access and secure payment systems.
• The need to train a workforce with the skills necessary to operate in the social e -commerce sector.

2.2. The Difference between Traditional Electronic Commerce and Social Electronic
Commerce
While social e -commerce shares some features with traditional e -commerce, there are several distinct
differences. The major differences can be summarized as follows:
2.2.1. Human Participation (Customer Engagement)
In conventional e -commerce, transactions conducted online, including purchasing and selling, primarily take
place without significant human interaction. Engagement from customers is typically low, often restricted to
systems for ratings and reviews that lack interactive dialogue. Con versely, social commerce is fundamentally
dependent on social networks for the processes of buying, selling, and advertising products and services.
Customer feedback, including reviews, testimonials, ratings, and referrals, is instrumental in shaping the
purchasing choices of consumers. Furthermore, customer insights are actively utilized to inform product design,
marketing approaches, and promotional initiatives. Social commerce significantly benefits from viral marketing,
wherein customers disseminate inf ormation regarding products or services within their social circles, quickly
generating extensive interest akin to a viral message.
2.2.2. Shopping Experience
The shopping experience in conventional e -commerce is typically a solitary endeavour, often defin ed by a
straightforward "click and buy" mechanism. Consumers tend to make purchases on their own, with minimal
interaction with others throughout the buying journey. Conversely, social commerce redefines shopping as a
communal experience, significantly sha ped by the exchange of information and interactions among participants
within social networks. Shoppers are able to make better -informed purchasing choices based on collective
insights and recommendations, thereby enhancing the engagement and enjoyment of the shopping process.
2.2.3. Branding Management
E-commerce encounters difficulties in establishing customer relationships and effectively managing branding
due to restricted communication. The absence of comprehensive information about customers can imped e
marketers' comprehension of the target market, complicating the customization of branding initiatives. In
contrast, social commerce utilizes social networks to enhance the development of stronger customer
relationships. Marketers can readily obtain direc t feedback from customers concerning their shopping
experiences, promptly address inquiries and concerns, and acquire deeper insights into customer needs and
preferences. This interactive dynamic enables marketers to better understand and cater to their au dience, thereby
reinforcing brand loyalty and customer satisfaction.
Numerous studies emphasize that social e -commerce represents not only a technological innovation but also a
social phenomenon influenced by peer impact, electronic word -of-mouth (e-WOM), and user-generated content
(Kim & Park, 2013; Zhang & Benyoucef, 2016). These elements are pivotal in shaping consumers' purchasing
intentions and decision -making processes, transforming social platforms into significantly influential
commercial arenas. S pecifically, consumer engagement through e -WOM has been recognized as a key factor
driving social commerce, impacting brand perception and purchasing behaviour. In exploring this dynamic,
Boukhedimi (2025) examined the effects of demographic factors on e -WOM among Algerian online shoppers
and found no significant differences based on gender (p = 0.382), age (p = 0.078), education level (p = 0.833),
or marital status (p = 0.998). These findings indicate that demographic traits may exert limited influence on
online engagement, suggesting that e -WOM behaviours are likely more influenced by platform design, social
dynamics, or content quality than by individual demographic factors.
2.3. Virtual Enterprises in the Digital Economy
The notion of virtual enterprises  predates social e -commerce and originates from the broader literature on
networked organizations and digital collaboration. Davidow and Malone (1992) described virtual enterprises as
flexible, network -based organizations that rely on information technologies to coordinate activities across
organizational and geographical boundaries. Such enterprises are characterized by agility, scalability, and
minimal dependence on physical infrastructure.
In the digital economy, virtual enterprises have become increasingly feasible due to advances in cloud
computing, digital platforms, and communication technologies. These technologies support flexible, network -
based collaboration, enabling organizations to operate efficiently without heavy reliance on physical
infrastructure (OECD, 2020). These enterprises can rapidly enter and exit markets, adapting swiftly to changes
in demand or competitive pressures. They are able to collaborate efficiently with partners and suppliers around
the globe, leveraging digital pla tforms and communication technologies to coordinate operations across

2.4. Social Media Platforms as Enablers of Virtual Enterprises
Social media platforms are pivotal in facilitating the functioning and prosperity of virtual businesses in the
realm of social e -commerce. Platforms like Facebook, Instagram, and Amazon offer cohes ive ecosystems that
merge marketing, communication, payment solutions, and customer feedback systems (Alam et al, 2022;
Mubdir et al, 2025; Alhumud & Elshaer, 2024).Through these platforms, virtual businesses can engage directly
with consumers, foster online communities, and tailor their offerings according to consumer data.
Research consistently demonstrates that trust is a crucial element in determining success in social e -commerce
environments, especially for virtual enterprises that lack a physical pres ence or do not engage in direct
interactions. Trust provides consumers with assurance that a platform will meet its commitments regarding
product delivery, protect personal data, and provide reliable support, thus influencing their likelihood of
engaging in online transactions (Lee, 2025).
Studies examining the factors that cultivate trust in social commerce indicate that the quality of platform design,
perceived security, information quality, and social presence are key factors that influence consumer trus t and
their willingness to use social commerce services (Al -Kfairy et al., 2024). Moreover, trust in social commerce is
shaped by concerns regarding privacy (Al -Kfairy & Shuhaiber, 2022; Tseng, 2023). Activities associated with
information sharing and social support highlight the importance of social interactions and the exchange of
information among users within social networks (Al-Kfairy et al., 2024).
Furthermore, Anantharaman et al.  (2023) suggested that the elements of social presence theory and us ers'
emotional connection to social commerce platforms positively influence their perceived trustworthiness.
Moreover, studies indicate that social media marketing, social engagement, and perceived value play a crucial
role in enhancing trust and consumer confidence within social commerce environments by fostering familiarity
and alleviating transaction-related uncertainties (Afridi et al., 2025). Additionally, it has been demonstrated that
influencer marketing can bolster followers' trust in both products and platforms by enhancing perceived
credibility and value, which in turn positively influences purchase intentions (Taj et al., 2025). These insights
emphasize that in the absence of a physical presence, social commerce settings must utilize trust -building
strategies including secure systems, features for social interaction, authentic communication, and credible
endorsements to cultivate consumer confidence and facilitate the successful operation of virtual enterprises.
2.5. Success Factors of Virtual Enterprises in Social Electronic Commerce
Numerous empirical studies have pinpointed essential success factors for virtual enterprises engaged in social e -
commerce. These factors encompass technological preparedness, proficient utilization of social interaction tools,
a robust brand presence, safeguarding data privacy, and an ability to respond to customer feedback (Zhang &
Benyoucef, 2016; Turban et al., 2018). Furthermore, the capacity to harness big data analytics and artificial
intelligence allows virtual enterprises to gain deeper insights into consumer behaviour and refine their marketing
strategies (Laudon & Traver, 2021).
Recent empirical studies conducted in developing economies further highlight the pivotal role of knowledge
generation and I CT capabilities in facilitating organizational growth. Sada oui et al.  (2025), through their
examination of Mobilis in Bechar (Algeria), illustrate that entrepreneurial opportunities have a significant
impact on strategic growth when mediated by effective k nowledge generation within the ICT sector. Their
results bolster the assertion that digital knowledge, learning capacity, and ICT infrastructure are essential
success factors for virtual enterprises functioning in socially enabled digital environments. In the realm of social
e-commerce, these components foster innovation, platform adaptability, and competitive positioning, especially
in emerging markets.

3. Methodology
This study employed a descriptive survey  method complemented by an analytical approach  to explore the
concept, practices, and challenges of social e -commerce, as well as the success factors of virtual enterprises
operating in this field. The combination of these methods allows for a comprehensive understanding of both
theoretical and practical aspects, while providing a structured framework for analysing real -world experiences.
These platforms were selected because they represent different operational models of social e -commerce and are
among the most influential digital ecosystems globally.
3.1 Research Approach
The study follows a descriptive–analytical research approach , which is particularly suitable for investigating
relatively recent phenomena, such as social e -commerce and virtual enterprises. The descriptive aspect focuses
on presenting theoretical frameworks, definitions, and conceptual distinctions , while the analytical aspect
examines data and case studies to identify patterns, success factors, and obstacles affecting the adoption of
social e-commerce in different  contexts. This dual approach ensures that both the  conceptual foundations  and
practical implementations are thoroughly examined.
3.2 Research Design
The research design is founded on a survey of secondary data, complemented by case study analysis. In contrast
to primary surveys that gather new data from participants, this study utilized documentary research and
previously published empirical studies to consolidate knowledge regarding social e -commerce and virtual
enterprises. Notably, studies such as L iang and Turban (2011), which posited that social commerce transcends
mere transactional efficiency by highlighting social support, trust -building mechanisms, and community
engagement, that affect consumer decision -making processes, as well as Boullenois ( 2022), which explored the
cultural and social dynamics of e -commerce in rural China, offered significant empirical insights. Furthermore,
the research incorporates case models of successful virtual enterprises, with Facebook and Amazon
exemplifying these c ases. These organizations were chosen due to their global leadership in merging social
media with e -commerce, their innovative business models, and their comprehensive documentation in both
academic and industry reports.
3.3 Data Collection
Data for this study were collected from diverse secondary sources, including:
1. Academic literature: Peer-reviewed journals, books, and conference papers on e -commerce, social e -
commerce, digital marketing, and virtual enterprises (e.g., Turban et al., 2018; Liang  & Turban, 2020;
Laudon & Traver, 2021; Hajli, 2015; Hammache et al., 2022).  Industry reports  including reports by
consulting firms and organizations such as UNCTAD and OECD.
2. Case studies: Analysis of documented experiences of Facebook, Amazon, and other virtual en terprises
that have successfully integrated social commerce.
3. Official platforms and websites : Examination of digital tools, marketing strategies, and consumer
interaction mechanisms used by virtual enterprises.
This multi-source approach ensures triangulation, thereby enhancing the reliability and validity of the study’s
findings. Through this process, the study identifies key dimensions of success for virtual enterprises, including

4. Results and Discussion
4.1. Tools for Effective Social Electronic Commerce
Social e-commerce has grown rapidly as brands leverage social platforms to engage consumers and drive sales.
There are sev eral tools and features increasingly implemented on both social media platforms and e -commerce
sites to enhance the shopping experience:
4.1.1. Digital Stores
Digital retail interfaces allow consumers to explore and purchase a brand’s products directly on social media
platforms or websites. Supporting features include product catalogues and in -app checkout options. For
example, Clinique uses Instagram Checkout to enable users to shop items directly within the platform,
streamlining the purchasing process.
4.1.2. Conversational Commerce
Conversational interfaces allow consumers to interact directly with brands to find and purchase products.
Features include direct messaging, chatbots, and voice support. The Lego Group launched the chatbot Ralph on
Messenger, assisting shoppers in selecting the right gifts and completing purchases seamlessly.  It should be
mentioned that Ralph is a conversational commerce tool , it was a Facebook Messenger chatbot launched by The
LEGO Group.
4.1.3. Livestream Commerce
Livestream commerce enables brands to engage with consumers in real time through video -streaming events.
Features often include live Q & A, product demonstrations, and direct checkout options. Walmart launched its
own livestreaming platform, where influencers promot e new products interactively, driving engagement and
sales.
4.1.4. Influencer Commerce
Influencer commerce leverages content creators to inspire consumers to purchase products. Features include

4.1.5. AR Commerce (Augmented Reality)
Augmented reality interfaces allow consumers to virtually explore and “try on” products before purchasing.

4.1.7. Group Buying
Group buying models offer consumers discounts when purchases are pooled collectively. Pinduoduo in China
provides discounts to consumers who buy in groups, encouraging social sharing and collective purchasing.
4.1.8. Mini Programs
Mini programs are small apps that operate within larger platforms, offering integrated shopping experiences. For
example, WeChat mini programs allow brands to showca se product catalogues, educate consumers, and enable
purchases without leaving the app.

4.2.1. Advantages of Social E-Commerce
With the advent of internet technologies, social commerce has become an integral part of the online shopping
experience. Before making a purchase, customers often check product reviews on e -commerce portals or social
networking sites such as Facebook. Businesses, in turn, strive to meet customer needs by  maintaining product
quality, offering competitive pricing, and providing post -purchase support. These practices help ensure positive
reviews, increase sales, and build brand reputation. The four key advantages of social commerce for businesses
are described below:

4.2.1.1. Increased Engagement
Social commerce allows customers to provide reviews and ratings of their purchases on social networking sites,
increasing their engagement with the brand’s online community. Businesses can use this feedback to better meet
customer requirements, enhance the shopping experience, and refine marketing and promotional activities.
4.2.1.2. Trust Building
Building trust is a major challenge for online businesses. Social commerce makes this easier, as potential
customers can see reviews and experiences of other buyers. Since negative reviews can have a detrimental effect
on sales, businesses are motivated to fulfil their promises, particularly regarding product quality and pricing,
ensuring customer satisfaction and trust.
4.2.1.3. Greater Reach
Social commerce enables customers to share their purchases on social media platforms such as Facebook and
Twitter, giving businesses greater exposure to potential customers. This organic sharing is cost -effective and can
rapidly increase brand visibility. Endorsements from satisfied customers also improve the likelihood of
converting potential buyers into actual customers.
4.2.1.4. Higher Search Engine Visibility
Higher search engine visibility is essential for online busi nesses to be easily discovered by potential customers
using search engines like Google, Yahoo, or Bing. Social media interactions, including customer reviews,
comments, and shares, contribute to better search engine optimization (SEO), increasing visibilit y and driving
more traffic to e-commerce sites.
Social commerce has been promoted extensively by platforms such as Facebook, Instagram, and Pinterest,
contributing to its exponential growth. Facebook was the first social commerce platform to allow businesses to
open individual shops. Instagram engages y ounger consumers effectively using high -quality visual content,
while Pinterest allows users to purchase products directly via buyable pins. Although Pinterest has not matched
the overall commercial impact of Facebook or Instagram, it is preferred by mobil e shoppers for online
purchases.
4.3. Barriers Hindering Social E-Commerce Adoption in Developing Countries
While social e -commerce offers considerable prospects for economic advancement and digital inclusion, its
uptake in developing nations is still constrained by various interconnected obstacles. These issues can be
generally classified into infrastructural, technological, socio-cultural, socioeconomic, logistical, and political
dimensions. Furthermore, these barriers frequently interact with one another, thereby creating a complicated
landscape that hinders the spread of social e-commerce.
Infrastructural challenges represe nt a significant hindrance to the adoption of social e -commerce. Developing
nations often lack sufficient foundational infrastructure, such as dependable digital payment systems and
widespread availability of credit cards. Additionally, inadequate distribu tion and delivery logistics diminish the
effectiveness of online transactions and adversely impact consumer satisfaction. The lack of large, reputable
online retailers further erodes user trust. Moreover, insufficient legal and regulatory frameworks do not  provide
adequate consumer protection. Limited broadband capacity and poorly developed telecommunication networks
further limit access. As a result, issues related to Internet security, challenges with product returns, limited

Technological obstacles also restrict effective engagement in social e -commerce. Numerous developing

Telecommunication (network) barriers constitute some of the most significant challenges. Internet access is

Limited access to computing devices further impedes participation in so cial e-commerce. A significant number

infrastructure is not enough; fostering trust and enhancing digital literacy are equally vital for successful
adoption.
Socio-cultural barriers have a profound impact on perceptions regarding social e -commerce. A significant
number of users exhibit a lack of confidence in technology and online transactions, leading them to favour
traditional face-to-face shopping experiences. Additionally, language barri ers and a scarcity of locally relevant
digital content hinder user engagement. Furthermore, low trust levels in institutions and e -commerce platforms
contribute to scepticism and resistance towards the adoption of social commerce practices.
Socioeconomic b arriers also limit the growth of social e -commerce. Low income levels, a limited GDP per
capita, and high technology costs diminish consumers' purchasing power. Moreover, urban -rural disparities
exacerbate these issues. Insufficient ICT and business educat ion impede users' capacity to effectively engage
with social e -commerce platforms. Furthermore, underdeveloped payment systems, characterized by minimal
credit card usage and inefficient banking infrastructure, present additional challenges to secure onlin e
transactions. Furthermore, logistics barriers represent another significant obstacle to the adoption of social e -
commerce. Effective delivery systems are crucial for successful online transactions; however, many developing
nations struggle with inadequat e postal and logistics infrastructure. As a result, delays or unreliable deliveries
diminish customer satisfaction and trust, thereby discouraging repeat usage of social e -commerce platforms.
Lastly, political and governmental barriers are instrumental in shaping the landscape of social e -commerce.
Weak telecommunications policies and market monopolies restrict competition and innovation. Additionally,
the lack of comprehensive national ICT and Internet policies hampers infrastructure development. High impo rt
duties on IT equipment and weak regulatory enforcement further escalate adoption costs. Consequently, robust
government intervention is vital for the advancement of ICT infrastructure, the establishment of effective
regulatory frameworks, and the encouragement of widespread social e-commerce adoption.

Conclusions
This paper aimed to elucidate the conceptual differences and connections among traditional e -commerce, social
media marketing, and social e -commerce, while also pinpointing the essential factors that account for the
success of virtual businesses functioning within socially integrated digital landscapes. Utilizing a descriptive –
analytical methodology and international case studies, particularly from Facebook –Instagram and Amazon, the
results indicate that social e -commerce represents a structurally  unique and more sophisticated variant of
electronic commerce, which integrates commercial transactions with social interactions, trust -building
processes, and user engagement.
The findings affirm that social e -commerce goes beyond mere transactional effic iency by converting shopping
into a socially embedded and emotionally resonant experience. Elements such as user -generated content,
electronic word -of-mouth, influencer marketing, and interactive platform functionalities significantly mitigate
perceived ri sks and enhance consumer trust, thus expediting purchasing decisions and nurturing long -term
loyalty. Furthermore, the comparative analysis of global platforms illustrates that successful virtual enterprises
strategically align themselves along a spectrum between commerce -oriented and social -oriented models,
adjusting their digital capabilities in response to evolving consumer expectations and technological
advancements.
From a theoretical standpoint, this research adds to the existing literature by clarify ing the conceptual
uncertainties associated with social e -commerce, framing it as a hybrid socio -technical system instead of simply
an extension of conventional e -commerce. The results bolster social presence theory and trust -based models by

Furthermore, this study expands upon virtual enterprise theory by demonstrating how digitally interconnected

that safeguard consumers, ensure data privacy, and promote confidence in online transactions.
Notwithstanding its contributions, this study presents limitations that pave the way for future research. The
dependence on secondary data and descriptive analysis constrains causal inference and generalizability.
Subsequent studies ought to utilize empirical and mixed -method approaches to investigate the relationships
among social interaction, trust, and consumer behaviour within social e -commerce environments. Furthermore,
the emphasis on a restricted number of global platforms may not adequately reflect the variety of social
commerce practices across different regions and industries.
Future research is urged to undertake cross -country comparative studies, especially between developed and
developing nations, to explore how institutio nal quality, culture, and digital infrastructure influence the adoption
of social e-commerce. Additional exploration into small and medium -sized enterprises (SMEs) and local virtual
businesses, particularly in Algeria, would yield significant insights into  organizational capabilities and
limitations. Lastly, emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence, augmented reality, and blockchain
deserve closer scrutiny regarding their potential to transform trust, personalization, and governance within soci al
e-commerce ecosystems.
In summary, social e -commerce signifies a transformative model that redefines digital trade by merging
economic exchange with social interaction and trust. For developing economies, it presents a strategic
opportunity to expedite digital transformation, foster entrepreneurship, and improve global market integration.
Achieving this potential necessitates the synchronization of technological innovation, managerial strategy, and
public policy, ensuring that social e -commerce progresses in a sustainable, inclusive, and ethically responsible
manner.
