ABSTRACT:
De-influencing, a new trend that involves social media personalities suggesting not to purchase specific items, is the opposite of traditional influencer marketing.
Thisstudy investigatesthe relationshipbetween de-influencercredibilityandconsumertrust, aswellasconsumertrust andthe de-influencerauthenticity, andthe
latter relationship between consumer trust and purchase intention. Primary data was obtained from a structured questionnaire administered to 120 social media
users which is mainly composed of young Instagram users. The instrument showed acceptable to excellent reliability (Cronbach’s α = 0.76 to 0.92). Results
indicated that chi-square tests revealed that gender and daily social media use and how often they see de-influenced content are significantly associated with
purchase-intention level. An analysis using one-way ANOVA demonstrated that purchase intention varied between age groups, but not by platform, while
consumer trust varied by platform. All hypotheses were supported by linear regression, with the credibility hypothesis predicting consumer trust (β = 0.69), the
authenticity hypothesis predicting consumer trust (β = 0.71), and the relationship between consumer trust and purchase intention (β = 0.76), and between
credibilityandpurchase intention (β=0.73).Credibility, authenticity and trustaccountedfor67.8%ofthe variance inthe purchase intention, with trust beingthe
strongest predictor. The results indicate that brands can reap rewards of collaborating with credible, authentic and transparent creators, not just as a threat of de-
influencing.

Introduction:
Socialmediainfluencersare afocalpointfor consumerstolearnaboutandassessproducts.However, paidpromotions arebecomingmore apparentto
followers, leadingtoa growingsenseofscepticism towardsendorsements thatseem scriptedorcommercial.In thisregard,de-influencinghasbecome
athing.Itisatypeofcontentwherecreatorsdiscouragefollowingcertainproducts,criticizeover-hypedproducts,questionunwarrantedpurchases,and
suggestmorebudget-friendlyoptions.
De-influencing is a double-edged sword for marketers. Information that dissuades buying could lower the demand for the good or service but it also
would increase the credibilityof the creatorand the trust of the audiencein whatthe creator has tosay. Expertise andtrustworthiness are identified as
key elements of persuasion (Ohanian, 1990) and trust is seen as one of the salient bases of favourable behaviour toward an exchange partner in the
context of relationship-marketing research (Morgan & Hunt, 1994). It is an empirical question whether these mechanisms are also in play for de-
influencers,andwhethertheseleadtopurchaseintention.
Thisstudythus investigates effectsof the credibilityand authenticityof thede-influencer on consumertrust, consumertrust on purchaseintention and
demographics and platform-based differences. The rest of the paper provides the conceptual framework, hypotheses, methodology, analysis and
conclusions.

Conceptual framework:
The conceptual framework is de-influencing marketing (honest review, product criticism and transparency) → consumer skepticism (doubt, trust and
authenticity) → consumer purchase intention (purchase intention and buying decision). The framework signifies that consumers make judgements
aboutthecreatorandproductsbeingpromotedbased onthehonesty, critical, andtransparentde-influencingcontent,andthesejudgementsaffect what
consumerswanttopurchase.
Theframeworkwasoperationalisedintothefollowinghypotheses:H1,H2,H3andH4forempiricaltesting.ThecredibilityandauthenticityoftheDe-
influencer are treated as antecedents for consumer trust, while credibility and trust are treated as antecedents of the purchase intention. The degree of
transparency,productcriticismandconsumerskepticismwasalsoassessedandispresentedinthedescriptiveandcorrelationresults.

Conclusion
In this paper, the relationshipbetween de-influencingcontent and consumer trust and purchase intention was analyzed. Support was found for all four
hypotheses: credibility and authenticity increased consumer trust and both credibility and trust were positively related to purchase intention. The
combination of credibility, authenticity and trust accounted for 67.8% of the variance in purchase intention. There were no significant differences
amongintenttopurchasebyplatform,andtrustwasfoundtobehigherforFacebookusersthanInstagramusers.
ManagerialImplications
De-influencing should not be interpreted as just a threat, but as a signal of trust building for brands. Working with creators who are seen as credible,
authentic and open about brand relationships is likely to make audiences more receptive to product information. Authenticity made the biggest
difference in predicting trust, so brands should stay away from scripted or over-managed collaborations. As purchase intention did not differ
significantlybyplatform,itisirrelevanttoconsiderdifferencesinpersuasionandplatformchoiceshouldnotbebasedonthem.Therearedifferencesin
ageandusage,indicatingthatcampaignsshouldbegearedtowardsyoungeraudiencesandmoderateusersasopposedtoveryheavyusers.
Futureresearchandlimitations
The sample is small, non-random, young, student dominated and focused on Instagram, therefore results can't be generalized to all consumers. The
Facebook group is very small. Data are self-reported and cross-sectional and regression results indicate association, not causation. Items were not
factored into constructs, but were simply classified by content. Larger and more diverse random sample, testing de-influencing to include consumer
skepticism as a mediator with structural equation modelling, confirmatory factor analysis scales, and an experimental comparison between de-
influencingandtraditionalinfluencerendorsements.
