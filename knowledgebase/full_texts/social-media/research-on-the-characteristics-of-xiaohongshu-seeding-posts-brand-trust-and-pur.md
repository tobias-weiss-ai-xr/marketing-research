Abstract. With the spread of mobile internet and the flourishing of Xiaohongshu's "seed-
planting economy", user-generated seed-planting posts have become essential, high-quality
positive online word-of-mouth that significantly changes consumer behaviour. Most of the
previous studies have assumed that "information attributes directly cause a change in
purchasing behaviour" and have failed to consider the various psychological changes that
people experience in their minds after being exposed to digital information.To address this
problem, the current study will focus on the unique UGC ecosystem of Xiaohongshu and
introduce "brand trust" as a mediator. Divide the features of seed-planting post information
into two types: credibility and richness, and then explore how they build consumer trust in
the brand. It will also investigate how this accumulated psychological resource and sense of
security reduce perceived risk to promote actual purchase behaviour. Ultimately, this
framework introduces the "black box" of social commerce conversion and explains how
information quality affects long-term brand equity and behaviour intention.
Keywords: Xiaohongshu, Seed-planting posts, Online review characteristics, Brand trust,
Purchase intention
1. Introduction
1.1. Research background
With the rapid development of mobile Internet technology and the deep application of Web 2.0, the
global digital economy is changing at a high speed. Under these general conditions, the "social
commerce" model of content sharing and social interaction has gradually appeared. Consumers are
no longer passive recipients of product information in the ecosystem of social commerce; instead,
they have begun to create online word-of-mouth (eWOM) and user-generated content (UGC).
Among social commerce platforms, Xiaohongshu (RED) has created a unique "Seed-planting
Economy" (Zhongcao Economy) by using a decentralized content distribution model and strong
social characteristics. The so-called "seed planting" is a psychological phenomenon in which, after
experiencing a good product or lifestyle content and reading others' reviews, one develops a wish for
that particular product or service. On Xiaohongshu, these graphic or video media that carry authentic
experiences and recommendation information are widely referred to as "seed-planting posts".

Seed-planting posts are not traditional brand advertisements; they are positive, personalised
online reviews. By using real shares from a large number of Key Opinion Consumers (KOCs), Key
Opinion Leaders (KOLs) and ordinary users, an excellent information-filtering network has been
built among consumers to significantly affect public consumption psychology and final purchasing
decisions.
Although existing literature has shown that attributes of information in online reviews (such as
credibility and richness) are necessary conditions for altering consumer attitudes and motivating
purchase, the internal mechanism by which seed-planting posts bypass consumers' psychological
defenses to drive actual purchase intention in complex commercial chains has not been fully
investigated. Therefore, this study will focus on Xiaohongshu and the "seed-planting post" model to
investigate how the information characteristics of these posts drive consumers' purchase intention
through the accumulation of brand trust as an intangible asset.
1.2. Literature review and current research status
1.2.1. Related research on social commerce platforms and "seeding" marketing
• Evolution of Social Commerce and User-Generated Content (UGC)
With the spread of mobile Internet, the model of e-commerce has changed significantly; that is to
say, it is no longer product-oriented but rather people- and content-oriented social commerce [1]. In
the ecosystem of social commerce, User-Generated Content (UGC) has gradually taken on the role
of traditional brand promotion and is now one of the main sources of information that consumers
learn from to learn about and choose products for purchase.
• Xiaohongshu's Community Ecosystem and the Essence of the "Seeding" Mechanism
Of all the social platforms, Xiaohongshu has built a very vibrant community of content sharing
around its core value proposition of "Marking My Life". Recently, scholars have begun to study the
special commercial logic of Xiaohongshu. According to the above research, the high effectiveness of
Xiaohongshu's "seeding" mechanism lies in its ability to provide both instrumental value for
"information search" [2] and an "emotional value of community identity" [3]. Given that "everything
can be seeded", what particular features help a "seeding post" spread information effectively has
become an urgent research problem for empirical study.
1.2.2. Current research status on online review characteristics (credibility and richness)
• Research Status on Online Review Credibility
Online review credibility refers to the extent to which consumers believe that the source and
content of information spread through online word-of-mouth (eWOM) are genuine, unbiased, and
trustworthy [4].
At the source end, factors such as the poster's historical reputation, number of followers, and
professional identity badges all affect how the audience perceives the credibility of the source [5].
Regarding content, adding some balanced two-sided information (i.e., objectively pointing out
minor product defects instead of praising it) may increase consumers' trust in the review
unexpectedly [6].
• Research Status of Online Review Richness
According to the theory of media richness, these communication tools have varying degrees of
information transmission and uncertainty handling capability. Richness of online reviews generally
refers to the extent of detail and sensory information (e.g., text-image combinations, video

1.2.3. Research on the antecedents and consequences of brand trust
• Concept and Essence of Brand Trust
Chaudhuri and Holbrook defined it as a consumer's psychological readiness to be led by a brand
in times of risk and uncertainty, based on positive expectations of the brand's ability, dependability,
and underlying values [7].
• Drivers of Brand Trust in Social Media Environments
According to the above research, online word-of-mouth (eWOM) is a leading cause of the rapid
spread of brand trust [8]. Repeatedly seeing positive online reviews from other people about a
certain brand causes a strong "halo effect" and compensates for the lack of personal experience.
1.2.4. Research status on purchase intention and its transmission mechanism
Purchase intention refers to a consumer's subjective likelihood or behaviour of purchasing a
particular product or service under certain circumstances, based on the collection, processing, and
internal assessment of external information [9]. According to the original theory of consumer
behaviour, a person's demand for a purchase is closely related to their actual purchase.
1.3. Research questions
Although scholars have broadly recognized the leading role of user-generated content (UGC) in
guiding consumer decisions, there is a lack of empirical evidence on the specific psychological
transmission path through which positive graphic-and-text electronic word-of-mouth (eWOM)
bypasses audience psychological defenses and leads to a desire for consumption in a particular,
highly active ecosystem of Xiaohongshu's "content community".
The present study will focus on the core information features of Xiaohongshu "seeding posts" in
terms of online review credibility and online review richness within the Xiaohongshu platform
ecosystem. Systematically explore how all parts of information quality affect consumers' trust in a
brand and, as a result, motivate a desire to purchase.

2. Theoretical foundation and hypothesis development
2.1. Theoretical foundation
2.1.1. Theory of Reasoned Action
The Theory of Reasoned Action (TRA) in consumer behaviour and psychology that explains how a
person's attitude leads to a desire for behaviour is the Theory of Reasoned Action (TRA), proposed
by Fishbein and Ajzen in 1975 [10]. At the bottom of this theory is the idea that people's behaviour
is driven by their intentions; these intentions, in turn, are determined by how they feel about a
particular behaviour and the "subjective norm" established in society.
Consumers' exposure to 'seeding posts' on Xiaohongshu effectively acts as a strong external
information trigger. The Quality of the intrinsic information in these posts first shapes consumers'
perceptions, and consumers will be motivated to buy the brand. Therefore, the Theory of Reasoned
Action offers theoretical support for studying the psychological process through which external
word-of-mouth stimuli lead to a final consumer decision.
2.1.2. Customer-Based Brand Equity theory
In 1993, Kevin Lane Keller first put forward the theory of Customer-Based Brand Equity (CBBE)
[11]. In short, brand equity is the difference in people's responses to the same marketing efforts that
results from different levels of their knowledge of a particular brand.
As a third-party independent source of information, "seeding posts" are peer-endorsed additions
to a brand's cognitive equity pool, thus shortening the consumer decision-making cycle. Therefore,
CBBE theory can be used to explore the process of change in consumers' perceptions of a brand and
how this affects their behaviour.

2.3. Hypothesis development and conceptual model construction
2.3.1. Impact of online review credibility on brand trust (H1)
A person's first impression of a brand in a decentralized social media environment is often formed
by third-party network information. When consumers see a "seeding post" on Xiaohongshu and
believe it is credible, their highly credible electronic word-of-mouth (eWOM) generates a strong
positive halo effect. Therefore, consumers have formed a positive reliability expectation of the brand
in their minds. Grounded in this logic, the following hypothesis is proposed:
H1: Online review credibility positively and significantly affects brand trust.

2.3.2. Impact of online review richness on brand trust (H2)
According to the theory of media richness, a relatively rich medium with many types of sensory
cues can be used to reduce communication ambiguity and uncertainty caused by information
asymmetry. The richness of a "seeding post" on Xiaohongshu includes detailed breakdowns of
ingredients, step-by-step application instructions, and multi-modal high-definition images.
Grounded in this logic, the following hypothesis is proposed:
H2: Richness of online reviews positively and significantly impacts brand trust.
2.3.3. Impact of brand trust on purchase intention (H3)
In the context of the traditional decision-making system and the Stimulus-Organism-Response (S-O-
R) model, people's thoughts and emotions serve as the reasons for behaviour. Since the purchase will
involve money and other risks of error, people need to feel psychologically safe before buying. After
building trust in the hearts of the people, they will no longer be suspicious of the brand and will
choose to use it. Therefore, the following hypothesis is derived from this logic:
H3: Brand trust significantly and positively affects purchase intention.
2.3.4. Conceptual model construction
Based on the above theoretical deductions, this paper introduces "brand trust", an intangible
cognitive resource, as the mediating path. Build an empirical model to fully illustrate the behaviour-
decision path of high-quality information input and brand trust accumulation, and finally determine
whether a purchase intention is triggered.

4.2. Regression analysis and hypothesis testing
Having verified the high reliability and construct validity of the measurement instruments, this study
will now conduct hypothesis testing. To quantify the effect of different characteristics of
Xiaohongshu "seeding posts" on audience attitude and behaviour, a series of multiple linear
regression models were constructed.
4.2.1. The effect of seeding post characteristics on brand trust
• Main Effect Testing and Discussion for Online Review Credibility (H1)
Regression empirical results show that online review credibility positively relates to brand trust at
the statistical level. The standardized regression coefficient (β) is 0.252 and significant at the 0.05
level (p<0.05). According to the above data, for every one-standard-unit increase in consumers'
perceived credibility of a Xiaohongshu seeding post, their trust in the recommended brand rises by
0.252 units. Therefore, the first hypothesis (H1) has been verified empirically.
• Main Effect Testing and Discussion for Online Review Richness (H2)
Online review richness is also significantly positively correlated with brand trust in the same
regression model. The standardised regression coefficient (β) is 0.285, and it is significant at the
0.01 level (p<0.01). Therefore, an increase of one unit in the perceived richness of a seeding post is
expected to raise brand trust by 0.285 units, as concluded in Table 6.Therefore, Hypothesis H2 has

5. Conclusion and implications
5.1. Overview of research results
Based on the analysis of the above quantitative data, all three main hypotheses in this paper have
been supported empirically. The particular research results can be summarized in the following two
main directions:
First, the high-quality informational characteristics of "seeding posts" serve as a direct driving
force for accumulating brand trust as an intangible asset. Based on the above empirical results,
online review credibility (β=0.252, p<0.05) and online review richness (β=0.285, p<0.01) are
positively related to brand trust. When an image-text post has high authenticity and provides
detailed, in-depth information, this high-quality third-party endorsement can effectively bypass
people's psychological defences. As a result, it has gained the attention of the public and is now
closely associated with a sense of security for those who use it.
Second, based on the above empirical results, high-quality information can build brand trust and
motivate consumers to buy. Based on the data, brand trust is positively and significantly correlated
with the intention to purchase (β=0.456, p<0.001). This finding validates that only when trust is built
among consumers to reduce the perception of risk can a purchase impulse and behaviour be
effectively triggered.
5.2. Theoretical contributions
Based on the previous research in digital marketing and consumer behaviour, this paper will add to
the theory and practice in two areas:
First, it has expanded the scope of explanation and application for electronic word-of-mouth
(eWOM) communication models. Most of the previous empirical research on online reviews has
focused on transaction-oriented e-commerce platforms, such as Taobao and JD.com, and has
concentrated on easy-to-quantify, macro-structural indicators, such as the number of reviews and
star ratings. This paper innovatively applies the classic theoretical system to the context of
Xiaohongshu, a new "decentralised, content-driven" user-generated content (UGC) community.

Beyond the macro-level numerical data, this study will delve into the textual and visual texture

5.3. Research limitations and future directions
Although it is hoped to be both theoretically and empirically sound, there are also some deficiencies
in this study due to objective and subjective reasons that need to be addressed in future research.
First, there are problems of sample size and coverage. After all the data cleaning, the final
number of valid samples in this study is N=97. Although the size of this sample meets the basic
statistical requirements for carrying out Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA) and multiple regression
analysis in SPSS, it is relatively small compared with the large number of social network users
across the country, and thus less representative. In the future, one can increase the research funds or
extend the time for data collection to conduct a larger-scale random sample.
Second, the research context did not consider different degrees of product involvement. This
study considered "seeding posts" evaluated by respondents as an all-encompassing concept without
dividing the specific product categories being recommended, but consumers may have different
error tolerances and information processing depths when purchasing low-involvement products
(e.g., inexpensive snacks or fast-moving cosmetics) compared with high-involvement products (e.g.,
consumer electronics costing thousands of yuan). Therefore, in the future, researchers can add
"product type" or "product involvement" as moderators and offer more specific and targeted vertical
marketing suggestions for brands.
