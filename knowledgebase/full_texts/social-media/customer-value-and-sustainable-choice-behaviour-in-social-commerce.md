The emergence of Social Commerce, facilitated by social media plat-
forms such as Facebook, Snapchat, and Instagram, has revolutionized
electronic commerce, reshaping consumer behavior and business
dynamics. This study investigates the connection between customer
value and sustainable choice behavior in Social Commerce, focusing
on individuals aged 18 and above actively engaged with these plat -
forms. Employing a convenience sampling method and a survey of
383 participants, a structural equation model was utilized to assess
various hypotheses. The findings highlight the significant impact of
word of mouth, intention, offers, and trust on purchase decisions with-
in Social Commerce. These factors play pivotal roles in shaping cus -
tomer perspectives, emphasizing the importance of integrating online
brand communities into marketing and communication strategies. By
leveraging social media, vendors can facilitate the discovery, sharing,
recommendation, and transaction of goods and services, enhancing
customer value and promoting sustainable choices. This study con -
tributes to the understanding of consumer behavior in the context of
Social Commerce, offering insights for businesses to adapt and thrive
in an increasingly digital marketplace.

1. INTRODUCTION
In recent years, the digital revolution has fundamentally transformed the way we shop, con -
nect, and interact with businesses. The rise in social networking has significantly impacted
the way we approach online shopping ( J. Chen, 2015). This has provided birth to a new kind
of e-commerce called social commerce, which harnesses Web 2. O technology to construct
customer-centric enterprises. At the core of this transition lies the developing notion of social
commerce. Social commerce, a feature of electronic commerce (e-commerce), harnesses social
networking platforms for facilitating numerous interactions and activities related to purchasing
goods via the internet (Liang & Turban, 2011). This dynamic junction of social interactions and
economic transactions has experienced tremendous expansion in recent years, heralding a new
age of business potential. Projections show that the social commerce sector was prepared to rise
to an anticipated value of around US$80 billion by 2020, emphasizing its extraordinary ascen-

sion in digital commerce (Zafar et al., 2021). This increase illustrates not just the pervasiveness
of social commerce but also its revolutionary potential in transforming the landscape of online
retail and consumer participation. As social media platforms continue to expand and diversify,
the synergistic link between social interactions and business is set to play an increasingly prom-
inent role in the global economy. With the support of social media, consumers may now build
relationships more promptly and participate in new types of engagement on numerous social
platforms ( Hennig-Thurau, 2004 ). This enables consumers to share information about their
shopping experiences, interact with others during the purchase process, and even make direct
purchases through some social commerce platforms (Liu et al., 2019).
Social commerce platforms also encourage customers to review, rate, recommend, and share
their purchases, influencing others’ purchase decisions ( Hajli, 2015). Despite these improve -
ments, there are minimal research that study the influence of social information on consumer
buying choices. In the area of social commerce, reviews and ratings play a key role, providing
consumers with a forum to offer their product opinion. This interchange of distinct opinions and
experiences helps customers to make well-informed choices (Shadkam & O’Hara, 2013). This
dynamic interaction not only develops a feeling of community but also increases the openness
and trustworthiness of the social commerce ecosystem, eventually benefitting both customers
and sellers. These social commerce platforms depend on social recommendations from custom-
ers who have firsthand experience with the products ( Li et al., 2013 ). The notion of purchase
intention, which signifies a consumer’s cognitive inclination towards acquiring a particular
product or service, is also pertinent in the realm of social commerce (Ling et al., 2010).
Customer engagement behaviors on social media have emerged as significant factors that
strengthen relationships between businesses and customers ( Ajiboye, 2019). This has become
a focal point in both academic and business circles ( Brodie et al., 2011). Social commerce has
converted clients into proactive participants inside the digital marketplace, allowing them the
capacity to join in promoting, selling, comparing, curating, and acquiring things ( Shin, D. H.,
2013). Prominent social networking sites like LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook, and Twitter have
been important in producing fresh company chances and boosting electronic sales transactions
in the realm of social commerce ( Liang & Turban, 2011 ). The growth of social commerce
continues to surge each year, with social media, an integral part of Internet 2.0, revolutionizing
information and communication flow and reshaping how users interact and build relationships
(V ohra & Bhardwaj, 2019).
Sustainable consumption is described as “the utilization of goods and services that meet essen-
tial needs and enhance quality of life, while minimizing the consumption of natural resources,
toxic substances, and the generation of waste and pollutants throughout the product or service’s
life cycle, in order to safeguard the needs of future generations” (Kilbourne et al., 1997). Over
time, a rising number of consumers have expressed a willingness to embrace sustainable buying
patterns (Tanner & Kast, 2003 ). However, this predisposition has not consistently converted
into real purchase behavior ( Young et al., 2010 ). Numerous research has revealed a limited
association between customers’ positive sentiments towards sustainable purchasing and their
actual purchase behavior (Bar, 2003; Magnusson et al., 2001).
In Asian nations, research on sustainable buying behavior is sparse ( Lee, 2014; Wang et al.,
2014). Previous studies in the nation of India had largely focused on consumers’ environmen -
tal attitudes ( Joshi & Rahman, 2015 ). Only a few scholars have delved into the sustainable
shopping habits of Indian consumers. To the best of the author’s knowledge, there has been
scarce research exploring consumer values and ethical decision-making in the realm of social
commerce.

2. LITERATURE REVIEW
The development of electronic commerce in India is notable, with more people choosing to
shop online due to discounts, convenience, and a wide selection of products. E-retail businesses
use discounts as a key strategy to attract customers, employing various tactics like discount
codes and incentive-based discounts ( Nero & Raman, 2020 ). Personalizing sales offers based
on purchasing habits is another method to entice clients.
Social networking sites play a crucial role in consumers’ purchase decisions, as users share
their views and experiences related to products or services online ( Hajli, 2015). Social media
‘likes’ significantly influence purchase decisions and recommendations on connected e-com -
merce sites. However, relationships between consumers and sellers in the social commerce
community can be difficult to sustain, leading to the “social bubble” phenomenon ( Zhang et
al., 2023). Research by Wang et al. (2014) delved into sustainable consumption determinants,
including individual values, environmental awareness, social norms, and government policies.
Similarly, Saeed et al. (2019) found that information about sustainability shared on social net -
works impacts consumers’ willingness to purchase sustainable products. Consumers actively
seeking sustainability-related information rely heavily on social media, influencing their pur -
chasing intentions.
Nero & Raman (2020) highlighted the growing preference for online shopping due to discounts,
streamlined purchasing processes, and a broad range of products. E-commerce enterprises use
discounts and innovative strategies to attract a larger customer base. Hamli & Sobaih (2023)
noted that product variety, payment methods, and psychological factors significantly influenced
online shopping behaviors during the COVID-19 pandemic, whereas convenience and trust had
a lesser impact. Saranchana Asanprakit & Tanpat Kraiwanit (2023) revealed that social, moti -
vational, technological, platform, and economic factors influence the adoption of online social
commerce platforms, with motivational factors being key mediators. These findings highlight
the importance for stakeholders to adapt to the evolving s-commerce landscape, particularly
in emerging economies like Thailand. Calvin et al. (2023) found that sales and promotion,
reputation, and user experience positively influence consumer behavior in e-commerce, with
after-sales service having less impact.
Sofiane Laradi et al. (2024) emphasized understanding customer behavior in social commerce,
focusing on utilitarian and hedonic value, perceived privacy/security, customer trust, and pur -
chase intention. Despite extensive research on the emotional, psychological, and societal factors
affecting ecologically and socially aware buying patterns, there remains a gap in understanding
the comprehensive factors influencing online s-commerce adoption ( Saranchana Asanprakit
& Tanpat Kraiwanit, 2023). Sofiane Laradi et al. (2024) also identified a gap in examining the
impact of perceived privacy and security on trust and purchase intentions, calling for further
research across different cultural contexts.
Additionally, there is a notable gap regarding sustainable choice behavior and consumer val -
ues in social commerce, particularly in ethical decision-making and sustainable buying habits
of Indian consumers. This research gap highlights the need for comprehensive studies in this
area. Moreover, the potential effect of spirituality on consumer behavior remains unexplored,
presenting an opportunity to understand the interplay between spiritual beliefs and sustainable
consumption choices. This underscores the necessity of considering a broader spectrum of in -
fluences, including spirituality, in guiding consumer decisions in the evolving digital landscape.

3. CONCEPTUAL FRAMEWORK AND HYPOTHESES
DEVELOPMENT
The proposed research framework seeks to investigate the relationship between customer choice
and sustained purchasing intention, drawing on insights from previous studies. Specifically, the
framework integrates key constructs established in prior research, namely trust, purchasing
behavior, and word-of-mouth (WOM) intentions, as highlighted by Kim & Park  (2013). Ad-
ditionally, the service recovery model, introduced by Spreng (1995), provides further theoret-
ical underpinnings by elucidating the connection between satisfaction, repeat purchases, and
WOM intentions. Building upon these foundations, the suggested model comprises four major
constructs: convenience, intention, offerings, and trust. These constructs serve as the pillars
of analysis, aiming to uncover the intricate interplay between customer preferences, purchase
intentions, and sustained loyalty in the context of consumer decision-making.
To sufficiently present the framework for this investigation, Figure 1 visually illustrates the
interrelationships among the four major constructs: convenience, intention, offerings, and
trust. Each construct represents a critical dimension influencing customer choice and sustained
purchasing intention. By delineating these relationships, the framework provides a structured
approach to understanding the factors that drive consumer behavior and influence long-term
purchasing decisions. Furthermore, the inclusion of theoretical insights from previous studies
enhances the robustness of the framework, grounding it in established literature and contribut-
ing to its theoretical validity. Thus, the proposed research framework offers a comprehensive
lens through which to analyze the dynamics of customer choice and sustained purchasing in -
tention, paving the way for a deeper understanding of consumer behavior in the contemporary
marketplace.
3. 1. CONVENIENCE
Convenience is undeniably one of the primary factors driving online shopping. Unlike tradition-
al offline shopping, online shopping offers various advantages centered around ease and effi -
ciency, making it a significant incentive for customers to opt for digital purchasing (Omotayo &
Omotope, 2018). Studies by Tarhini et al. (2018) and Omotayo and Omotope (2018) highlight
that online customers are particularly attracted to the convenience offered by online shopping.
One of the key aspects of convenience in internet marketing is the provision of home delivery
services. This feature allows consumers to receive their goods directly at their doorstep, elim -
inating the need to visit physical stores and thereby saving valuable time and effort. The ease
and simplicity provided by online shopping have significantly contributed to the growth and
popularity of e-commerce, making it an attractive option for customers seeking efficient and
hassle-free shopping experiences.
Moreover, online shopping platforms often offer features such as easy navigation, quick check-
out processes, and personalized recommendations, further enhancing the convenience factor.
These features collectively reduce the time and effort required for shopping, making it a more
appealing alternative compared to traditional shopping methods. The ability to shop at any
time, access a wider variety of products, and compare prices effortlessly adds to the overall
convenience, driving more consumers to prefer online shopping.
In essence, the convenience provided by online shopping is a major driver of its popularity. It
transforms the shopping experience by offering time-saving and effortless options that align
with the fast-paced lifestyles of modern consumers. This shift towards convenience is a critical
factor in the sustained growth and widespread adoption of e-commerce.

3. 2. INTENTION
The concept of purchase intention, rooted in the theory of planned behavior, has found signif -
icant applicability across various contexts. Typically used as a key metric to predict consumer
behavior, it is important to recognize that convincing a customer to purchase a product can set
off a series of subsequent actions (Shin, 2013). Social commerce platforms play a crucial role in
this process by showcasing products that attract significant interest from potential consumers.
This phenomenon is closely linked to several underlying motivations. One of these is the desire
for social validation, where individuals seek approval or affirmation from their peers regarding
their purchasing decisions. Additionally, consumers often wish to educate others about products
that have piqued their interest, sharing information and personal experiences that can influence
the purchasing decisions of others. This educational aspect is particularly pronounced in social
commerce, where user-generated content and reviews play a pivotal role.
Furthermore, social commerce fosters a sense of connection among users. By engaging with
others on these platforms, consumers can build a community around shared interests and pref -
erences, enhancing their overall shopping experience. This sense of connection can lead to
increased trust and loyalty towards the platform and the products it offers. The act of purchas -
ing and discussing products on social commerce platforms can manifest a feeling of pride.
Consumers often take pride in their knowledge of products and their ability to make informed
decisions. Sharing their purchases and experiences can be a way for them to express this pride
and establish their identity within their social circles (Zhang & Benyoucef, 2016).
In summary, purchase intention in the realm of social commerce is driven by a complex inter -
play of factors including the desire for validation, the aim to educate others, the fostering of
connections, and the expression of pride. Understanding these motivations is crucial for busi -
nesses looking to effectively engage with consumers and influence their purchasing behavior
on social commerce platforms.
H2: Intention exerts a positive influence on the customer value and sustainable choice be - haviour in social commerce.
3. 3. OFFERS
In the context of social media, an “offer” refers to a promotional deal, discount, or incentive
that businesses and brands share across various social platforms. This strategic approach aims
to engage the target audience, attract potential buyers, and motivate them to perform specific
actions, such as making a purchase or subscribing to a service.
These social media offers are typically communicated through posts, stories, or advertisements
on platforms like Facebook, Instagram, Twitter, LinkedIn, and others. Users can interact with
these offers by clicking on provided links, using exclusive discount codes, or following the in-
structions in the post or advertisement. Several strategies are employed to captivate customers
and influence their purchasing decisions in the dynamic realm of social commerce, including:
Discount Codes/Coupons, Giveaways/Contests, Flash Sales, Free Samples. These tactics lever-
age the interactive and viral nature of social media to shape purchasing choices and enhance
customer engagement. By utilizing the unique features of each platform, such as Instagram’s
visually appealing stories or Facebook’s targeted advertising, brands can effectively reach and
influence their audience.
Social media offers are essential tools for businesses to engage with consumers, boost sales, and
enhance brand visibility. Through well-crafted promotions, discounts, and incentives, brands
can create compelling reasons for customers to interact with their products and services, ulti -
mately driving growth and loyalty in the competitive landscape of social commerce.
H3: Offers makes a positive impact on the customer value and sustainable choice behaviour in
social commerce.

3. 4. TRUST
Trust serves as a vital pillar in the relationships between customers and sellers (Wu et al., 2010).
According to Groß (2015), interpersonal trust is the cornerstone of commercial relationships.
Nicolaou and McKnight (2006) argue that a shopping website providing timely, accurate, and
responsive information fosters perceptions of competence, reliability, and compassion, thereby
building trust in the source. Additionally, Bart et al. (2005) demonstrated that website interface
qualities, such as style, presentation, and lack of errors, correlate with increased customer trust.
Customers often perceive websites that project a professional image and offer quick, easy navi-
gation as reputable. Consequently, higher interface quality—characterized by extensive and rel-
evant content, a well-crafted layout, and visual appeal—supports heightened customer trust in
social media platforms. Numerous studies have indicated that trust positively impacts purchase
intention in the realm of social commerce (Akman & Mishra, 2017; Al-Adwan & Kokash,
2019; C. Liu et al., 2019).
Trust is fundamental in the relationship between customers and sellers, especially in social
commerce. Websites that provide accurate information, exhibit a professional image, and offer
user-friendly interfaces are more likely to earn customer trust, which in turn enhances purchase
intentions.
H4: Trust makes a positive impact on the customer value and sustainable choice behaviour in
social commerce.

challenge. This research is both descriptive and analytical in nature. The survey method is being
used to gain valuable insights into customer value and sustainable choice behavior in Social
Commerce. The primary data were collected from a total of 400 responses from individuals
aged 18 and above, all of whom actively engaged with Facebook and other social commerce
platforms. This initial sample aimed to capture a broad spectrum of user experiences and behav-

4. 2. SAMPLE AND DATA COLLECTION
Convenience sampling techniques were utilized for data collection, specifically targeting indi -
viduals who actively engaged with Facebook and other social commerce platforms on a regular
basis. To gather responses, a web-based questionnaire was designed using Google Forms. This
questionnaire was then distributed to respondents to capture their input.
In addition to distributing the questionnaire directly to respondents, the online form was also
widely disseminated through social media channels and email. This strategy aimed to reach a
broad and relevant audience, ensuring that the sample included individuals who were frequent
users of social commerce platforms. By leveraging these digital distribution methods, the study
was able to efficiently collect a substantial number of responses from the target demographic.
This approach not only facilitated data collection but also enhanced the relevance and applica-
bility of the findings to the population of interest.

5. DISCUSSION
The fundamental purpose of this present study is to increase our grasp of the elements that
drive sustainable consumer decision-making inside social commerce networks, an area that has
received minimal earlier research. This paper delivers major theoretical and practical advances
in this sector. The investigation found that customer decision-making is particularly connected
with four important determinants: convenience, intention, offers, and trust. The primary pur -
pose of this study was to obtain insight into the elements affecting sustainable customer deci -
sions in social commerce networks.
The findings confirm that consumers are more inclined to choose the first option due to its
convenience. They feel comfortable when making a purchase because the product’s usage is
easy to understand. Additionally, the wide selection of products allows for easy comparisons,
and the presentation of comprehensive information in a single window contributes to this com-
fort in decision-making. The factor variables, namely “I feel comfortable in buying”, “Easy to
understand the usage of the product”, “I feel wide selection of product”, “Easy comparison of
product”, “I feel more information in one window” collectively fall under the domain of con -
venience.

Second, the result demonstrated that intention factor significantly enhancing consumer choice
behaviour in social commerce. Moreover, the study’s results underscore the major influence of
the intention element on moulding customer decision-making in the setting of social commerce.
Several factor variables were identified as influential, including the receipt of valid discount
codes, a sense that the offers reached them at the right time, the perceived good quality of the

6. CONCLUSIONS
India stands as one of the world’s largest consumer markets with substantial potential for sus -
tainable goods. However, sustainable marketers encounter challenges in engaging Indian shop-
pers due to a scarcity of data concerning sustainable purchasing practices (Joshi & Rahman,
2019). This present study contributes to existing research by examining the factors that impact
sustainable consumer decision-making in social commerce networks. The authors advocate that
sustainable purchasing behavior should be ingrained in societal mindset, rather than merely
treated as a marketable or advertisable concept.
This study has explored the intricate interplay between customer value and sustainable choice
behavior within the dynamic landscape of social commerce networks. Through a comprehen -
sive analysis of factors such as convenience, intention, offers, and trust, we have gained valu -
able insights into the mechanisms that drive consumers’ decisions in this evolving digital era.
The outcomes of this investigation add to a greater understanding of the dynamics at play.
The rise of social commerce has marked a paradigm shift, placing consumers at the centre of
the digital marketplace. Our analysis underscores the paramount significance of customer con-
venience in influencing purchasing choices. Convenience is not only a convenience element
but a vital motivator that impacts how customers perceive and interact with goods and services.
The ease of understanding product usage and the availability of comprehensive information in
a single window significantly impact consumer comfort, encouraging them to make informed
choices.
The study illuminates the pivotal role of consumer intentions in shaping sustainable choice

behavior. Consumers’ intentions act as a powerful force, driving their preferences and actions
within the realm of social commerce. The alignment of businesses’ marketing strategies with
consumers’ intentions can lead to more resonant engagement, higher satisfaction, and a more
meaningful connection between brands and consumers.
The impact of offers and promotional strategies on consumer choice behavior is evident. The
attraction of discounts, festival offers, and product quality performs a key part in attracting
customer attention and influencing their selections. The timing of offers and their relevance
to consumer needs further highlight the importance of a well-planned promotional approach.
However, it’s noteworthy that while offers may attract initial attention, trust and perceived val-
ue are essential to sustain customer loyalty beyond a single transaction.
Perhaps among the more striking outcomes of this research is the unquestionable influence of
trust in affecting customer behavior. Trust is the bedrock on which sustainable relationships be-
tween businesses and consumers are built. Establishing trustworthiness is not confined to prod-
uct quality alone but extends to factors such as authenticity, familiarity, and reliability. Social
commerce platforms that prioritize creating a sense of trust are more likely to foster enduring
customer relationships and encourage repeated sustainable choices.
6. 1. IMPLICATIONS FOR PRACTICE AND POLICY
The implications derived from this study are substantial for businesses navigating the complex
landscape of social commerce. Strategic marketing initiatives should centre around enhanc -
ing convenience, aligning with consumer intentions, crafting irresistible offers, and cultivating
trust. Online brand communities offer a platform for direct engagement, which can aid in build-
ing trust and familiarity.
From a policy standpoint, promoting transparent and ethical practices within the social com -
merce sphere is imperative. Regulatory bodies can work towards creating an environment where
consumer rights are upheld, misleading practices are curbed, and sustainable consumption is
encouraged.
6. 2. FUTURE A VENUES FOR RESEARCH
This work, in other words has offered useful insights into essential components of sustainable
decision-making in social commerce, there are interesting topics for additional exploration.
Exploring the fundamental psychological and emotional elements driving consumer decisions,
investigating the impact of influencers and peer recommendations, and assessing the implica -
tions of developing technological trends provide promising paths for future research.
In conclusion, our research deepens our comprehension of the delicate interaction between
consumer value and sustainable decision-making in social commerce networks. Through an
in-depth assessment of convenience, intention, offers, and trust, we contribute to a thorough
knowledge of how customers traverse this digital terrain. The information obtained from this
research gives practical assistance for companies, improves scholarly discourse, and supports a
more sustainable and consumer-centric approach to social commerce.
