Abstract
This study examines how perceived personalization in TikTok Shop ads influences trust through the
mediating role of perceived emotional support, grounded in Affective Events Theory (AET). Despite
the widespread adoption of AI-driven personalization, empirical findings on its influence on consumer
trust remain inconsistent, as prior research has largely overlooked affective mechanisms, focusing
instead on cognitive pathways such as relevance and usefulness. This study addresses this gap by
proposing that per ceived personalization functions as an affective event that triggers positive
emotional reactions specifically perceived emotional support which subsequently builds consumer
trust. A quantitative survey was conducted with 270 active TikTok Shop users in In donesia who had
been exposed to personalized advertisements, and the data were analyzed using Partial Least Squares
Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM). The results demonstrate that perceived personalization has
a strong positive effect on perceived emo tional support, which in turn significantly influences trust.
Furthermore, perceived emotional support partially mediates the relationship between perceived
personalization and trust, with a variance accounted for (VAF) of 44.6%, indicating that both affective
and cognitive processes contribute to trust formation. Theoretically, this study extends AET into AI -
based digital marketing and resolves the inconsistent personalization -trust findings by identifying
emotional support as the missing affective link. P ractically, the findings emphasize that marketers
should prioritize creating emotional support through personalization strategies, as content that makes
consumers feel understood and cared for is more effective in building trust. This study contributes to
the growing literature on AI personalization in social commerce contexts, particularly in developing
countries.

INTRODUCTION
The rapid advancement of artificial intelligence (AI) has transformed modern marketing
through AI-driven personalization, enabling companies to deliver relevant, adaptive, and real-time
content based on consumer behavioral data on platforms such as TikTok (Y. Gao & Liu, 2023). TikTok
Shop leverages personalization algorithms to present product recommendations tailored to users'
viewing history and interactions, creating a highly personal and immersive shopping space (Cai &
Suh, 2025; W. He et al., 2021). However, empirical evidence regarding personalization's impact on
consumer trust remains inconsistent. Some studies find that personalization increases trust through
enhanced relevance and personalized experiences (Tuan et al., 2025; Whang & Im, 2018), while

information quality (An & Ngo, 2025; Nilashi  et al., 2016). However, a purely cognitive approach
may be insufficient, as consumers also experience affective reactions that shape attitudes and
behaviors (Samson & Voyer, 2012; Strack & Deutsch, 2004). TikTok Shop is an entertainment-driven
platform wh ere hedonic motivations and emotional experiences often dominate cognitive
deliberation (Cai & Suh, 2025; W. He et al., 2021). In such contexts, emotional responses particularly
feelings of being understood and supported may play a more decisive role than functional
evaluations.
TikTok Shop's unique characteristics algorithmic For You Page, short -form video, and social
commerce features create an environment where emotional connection is paramount. Unlike
traditional e-commerce, TikTok users discover products through algorithmical ly curated content
(W. Gao & Liu, 2023). When personalization successfully anticipates consumer preferences, it
generates a profound sense of being understood conceptualized as perceived emotional support.
This feeling is particularly powerful on TikTok, w here the algorithm operates in real-time, creating
an intimate interaction (P. Shen, Wan, & Zhu, 2023). Nevertheless, previous studies have often
overlooked these affective mechanisms (Deveci, 2025; H. Y. Kim & Lee, 2020).
Indonesia represents a relevant context as one of TikTok Shop's largest and fastest -growing
markets (Sriwidadi & Prabowo, 2025). With over 100 million active users, understanding Indonesian
consumers' responses to AI-based personalization is crucial. Indonesian consumers predominantly
young and digitally native may exhibit different affective responses compared to  consumers in
developed countries, where privacy concerns are more pronounced (Butt et al., 2022). This cultural
distinctiveness makes Indonesia an ideal setting to examine the emotional support pathway of
personalization.
This study employs Affective Events Theory (AET) as its primary framework. AET explains that
environmental events such as personalized advertisements trigger affective reactions that shape
attitudes and behaviors (Christensen et al., 2023; Weiss & Cropanza no, 1996). The AET sequence
is: events → affective reactions → attitudes → behaviors. Thus, perceived personalization
influences trust through the emotions it elicits. Specifically, this study tests perceived emotional
support as the positive affective mecha nism mediating the relationship between perceived
personalization and trust a pathway overlooked by An  & Ngo (2025) and most prior studies.
Perceived emotional support refers to consumers' perception that personalized advertisements
provide a sense of being noticed, understood, and emotionally accompanied (Liang et al., 2011;
Thürmel, 2021). When personalization delivers content matching consumers' needs, individuals
feel the platform "cares" about their comfort (A. -Z. He et al., 2021; P. Shen, Wan, & Li, 2023). This
fosters a positive affective bond and enhances trust (Tseng, 2023; Zhang et al., 2021). Prior research
demonstrates that emotional support from digital interfaces increases trust (Jeon, 2024; J. K. Kim
& Lee, 2020).
Two main gaps underpin this research. First, the personalization -trust relationship remains
inconsistent, indicating an unexplored affective mediation mechanism. Second, AET application in
AI-based personalization on algorithmic platforms remains limited. This study addresses these gaps
by examining perceived emotional support as an overlooked affective mechanism. This study offers
three contributions: introducing perceived emotional support as an affective mediator, extending
AET into AI-based TikTok Shop advertising, and testing this model in a developing country context.

LITERATURE REVIEW AND HYPOTHESIS FORMULATION
Affective Events Theory (AET) as the Theoretical Foundation
 Affective Events Theory (AET) posits that environmental events trigger affective
(emotional) reactions, which subsequently shape individual attitudes and behaviors, following the
sequence: events → affective reactions → attitudes → behaviors (Christensen et al., 2023; Weiss &
Cropanzano, 1996). Originally formulated for organizational behavior, AET has been extended to
consumer behavior and digital marketing, where environmental stimuli similarly evoke emotional
responses that shape decision -making (Samson & Voyer, 2012). In the context of AI-driven TikTok
Shop advertising, personalized advertisements constitute a salient affective event (Y. Gao & Liu,
2023; P. Shen, Wan, & Li, 2023). Unlike traditional advertising, AI -based personalization creates a
highly relevant and contextually situa ted experience, making the affective event more personally
significant and emotionally charged (Bhuiyan, 2024). Consequently, emotional responses elicited
by perceived personalization can shape consumers' subsequent attitudes toward the platform.
A key distinction of AET compared to purely cognitive models is its emphasis on spontaneous
affective reactions as mediators between environmental events and attitudinal outcomes (Strack &
Deutsch, 2004). While cognitive models emphasize rational evaluation of relevance and usefulness
(An & Ngo, 2025) , AET acknowledges that emotional responses often precede and even override
cognitive deliberation, particularly in hedonic contexts such as TikTok Shop (Jeon, 2024; P. Shen,
Wan, & Zhu, 2023). This study adapts AET by positioning perceived personalization as the affective
event that triggers a specific positive affective reaction: perceived emotional support consumers'
subjective feeling that personalized advertisements convey care, understanding, and emotional
companionship (Liang et al., 2011; Thürmel , 2021). This positive affective reaction subsequently
shapes trust the attitude toward the platform, reflecting consumers' belief that the platform is
reliable, honest, and has their best interests at heart (An & Ngo, 2025; Lăzăroiu et al., 2020).
Critically, perceived emotional support represents the affective reaction (transient emotional
state), while trust represents the attitude (stable evaluative judgment) (Weiss & Cropanzano ,
1996). Thus, the AET sequence is operationalized as: Perceived Personalization (Event) → Perceived
Emotional Support (Affective Reaction) → Trust (Attitude).
The Effect of Perceived Personalization on Perceived Emotional Support
 Perceived personalization is defined as the extent to which consumers feel that advertised
content has been tailored to their preferences and behaviors (An & Ngo, 2025; Komiak & Benbasat,
2006). In TikTok Shop, this is realized through algorithms presenting products based on viewing
history and interactions (W. Gao & Liu, 2023). Perceived emotional support reflects consumers'
perception that personalized advertisements provide a sense of being noticed, understood, and
emotionally accompanied (Liang et al., 2011; Thürmel, 2021). According to AET, an environmental
event can trigger positive affective reactions if interpreted as supportive of individual needs (Weiss
& Cropanzano, 1996). When personalization delivers content aligned with consumers' preferences,
individuals feel that the platform "cares" about their comfort, fostering a positive affective bond
(A.-Z. He et al., 2021; P. Shen, Wan, & Zhu, 2023). Prior research confi rms that appropriate
personalization increases feelings of being emotionally supported (Liang et al., 2011; J. Shen et al.,
2025; Thürmel, 2021). In TikTok Shop, where users engage for entertainment and social interaction,
the emotional benefits of personalization are particularly salient (Cai & Suh, 2025). Therefore,

Direct Effect of Perceived Personalization on Trust
 Trust is defined as consumers' belief that the platform will act reliably, honestly, and
without harming their interests (An & Ngo, 2025). Beyond the affective mechanism, perceived
personalization may also build trust directly. AET acknowledges that some e vents may directly
influence attitudes without strong emotional mediation, particularly when individuals cognitively
evaluate the consistency and competence of the system (Weiss & Cropanzano , 1996). The
consistent delivery of accurate and relevant recommendations serves as a heuristic cue for system
competence and technological sophistication (Nilashi et al., 2016). Even without intense emotional
engagement, consumers can rationally appreciate the algorithm's utility and accuracy, reinforcing
their cognitive belief in the platform's reliability consistent with the dual -processing perspective
(Samson & Voyer, 2012). Previous research shows that perceived personalization increases trusting
beliefs (Tuan et al., 2025; Whang & Im, 2018). Thus, higher perceived personalization is expected
to increase trust.
H2: Perceived personalization has a positive effect on trust.
The Effect of Perceived Emotional Support on Trust
High perceived emotional support creates a sense of affective closeness and the perception
that the platform has good intentions toward consumers. According to AET, repeated positive
affective reactions shape positive attitudes toward the object that trigg ered them (Weiss &
Cropanzano, 1996). When consumers feel emotionally supported by personalized advertisements,
they tend to believe the platform is trustworthy because it has demonstrated attention to their
individual needs (Tseng, 2023; Zhang et al., 2021). Previous research demonstrates that emotional
support from digital interfaces increases trust (Jeon, 2024). Furthermore, emotional support
reduces the uncertainty and perceived risk associated with online transactions (Ho Nguyen et al.,
2022). When consumers feel emotionally suppo rted, they perceive the platform as benevolent,
enhancing their willingness to rely on its recommendations. Therefore, high levels of perceived
emotional support are expected to increase trust.
H3: Perceived emotional support has a positive effect on trust.
The Mediating Role of Perceived Emotional Support
Perceived emotional support acts as a mediator that translates the effect of personalization
on trust. AET explicitly supports the notion that affective reactions mediate the relationship
between events and attitudes (Christensen et al., 2023; Weiss & Crop anzano, 1996). When
consumers perceive high personalization, they interpret this as the platform understanding their
needs, generating feelings of emotional support being noticed, understood, and cared for. These
positive feelings build trust because consumers f eel the platform has their best interests at heart.
In the TikTok Shop context, this mediation unfolds through three sequential stages: First, perceived
personalization signals successful algorithmic mapping of consumer preferences (Event); Second,
this generates an internal affective state of perceived emotional support (Affective Reaction); Third,
this positive state signals platform benevolence, reducing perceived risk and fostering trust
(Attitude) (Ho Nguyen et al., 2022). Unlike cognitive mediators requiring conscious evaluation, this
affective mediation occurs spontaneously, making it particularly powerful on entertainment-driven
platforms like TikTok Shop (Strack & Deutsch, 2004). Therefore, perceived emotional support is
predicted to positively mediate the relationship between perceived personalization and trust.
H4: Perceived emotional support mediates the effect of perceived personalization on trust.

RESEARCH METHODS
Research Design
This study employed a quantitative causal -explanatory survey design to empirically test the
relationships among perceived personalization, perceived emotional support, and trust within the
framework of Affective Events Theory (AET) in the context of TikTok  Shop advertisements. The
cross-sectional design allowed for collection of respondents' perceptual data to estimate the
structural model using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) (Guntu et al.,
2022). This method was selected for three reasons: generalizability to TikTok Shop users in
Indonesia, efficiency in collecting data from a large sample, and suitability for testing theoretically
formulated causal relationships (Sriwidadi & Prabowo, 2025).
Population and Sample
The population comprised all active TikTok Shop users in Indonesia who had received and
viewed AI-based personalized advertisements within the last three months. Purposive sampling
was employed with inclusion criteria: (1) active TikTok Shop users who had engaged in shopping or
viewed product content at least twice in the past month; (2) had seen personalized advertisements
based on their activity history; and (3) willing to participate voluntarily, verified through screening
questions (Etikan, 2016). Sample size followed the inverse square root approach (Kock & Hadaya,
2018), with a minimum of 160 respondents based on a pilot test of 30 respondents (95% confidence
level, 5% margin of error). To anticipate incomplete responses, data were collec ted from 270
respondents, exceeding the minimum requirement and satisfying the 10 -times rule for PLS -SEM
(Hair, Risher, et al., 2019). Respondents came from various regions in Indonesia.
Data Collection and Instrument Development Techniques
Data collection was conducted during April –May 2026 using an online questionnaire
distributed via Google Form across social media platforms (WhatsApp, Instagram, Telegram, TikTok)
and TikTok Shop community groups. The questionnaire included an information sheet (study
purpose, confidentiality, voluntary nature), screening questions, and informed consent. Completion
was anonymous, took approximately 10 –15 minutes, and followed ethical research standards
(voluntary participation, right to withdraw, no identifiable information collected).
All measurement items were adopted from previous studies: perceived personalization (4
items) fromAn & Ngo (2025) and Komiak & Benbasat (2006); perceived emotional support (4 items)
from Liang et al. (2011) and Thürmel (2021); and trust (5 items) from An &  Ngo (2025) and Nilashi
et al. (2016). Items were adapted using translation -back translation by two bilingual academics
Deviandri et al. (2024), measured on a 5 -point Likert scale (1 = Strongly Disagree to 5 = Strongly
Agree). A pilot test with 30 respondents confirmed language clarity and initial reliability (Cronbach's
alpha > 0.70), with no items requiring removal.

Data Analysis Techniques
Data were analyzed using PLS -SEM with SmartPLS 4.0, selected for its predictive objectives,
mediation testing capability, and robustness to non -normal data (Hair & Alamer, 2022). Analysis
proceeded in three stages. First, the measurement model was evaluated for validity and reliability.
Convergent validity was assessed through factor loadings (ideal ≥ 0.70; values 0.50–0.70 retained if
AVE remained ≥ 0.50) and Average Variance Extracted (AVE ≥ 0.50). Reliability was measured using
Cronbach's Alpha and Composite Reliability (≥ 0.70). Discriminant validity was assessed using HTMT
(< 0.90) (Henseler et al., 2015). Second, the structural model was evaluated. Path coefficients (β)
were estimated using bootstrapping with 5,000 subsamples; hypotheses were supported if t -
statistic > 1.96 or p-value < 0.05. R², f², and Q² were calculated to assess explanatory and predictive
power. Demographic variables (age, gender, income, TikTok usage frequency) were included as
controls. Third, mediation testing for H4 used specific indirect effects with 95% bootstrap
confidence intervals; mediation was significant if the interval excluded zero (Preacher & Hayes,
2008; Tibbe & Montoya, 2022). To address common method bias, non -statistical approaches
(anonymity, item separation) and statistical tests were employed. Harman's Sing le Factor Test
(41.01% < 50%) and VIF values (< 3.3) confirmed data were free from serious CMB (Kock, 2017;
Podsakoff et al., 2003).

Predictive relevance (Q²) was confirmed for all endogenous constructs (PES = 0.347; Trust = 0.249),

Discussion
The results of this study indicate that perceived personalization acts as a strong and
consistent affective event in shaping consumers' positive affective reactions (perceived emotional
support) (An & Ngo, 2025; Thürmel , 2021). This study found that the positive affective route
through perceived emotional support had a dominant and significant influence on trust. Internal
psychological processes that are positively affective, such as perceived emotional support, have a
stronger and consistent effect in driving trust compared to purely cognitive evaluations (Jeon, 2024;
Tseng, 2023). In other words, in this research context, the positive mechanism effectively translated
personalization into trust. On TikTok Shop, consumers  tend to experience the emotional benefits
of personalization (feeling noticed and supported), making the affective mechanism work
effectively (Samson & Voyer, 2012; Strack & Deutsch, 2004).
Perceived personalization functions as a very dominant affective event in the TikTok Shop
context because the personalization algorithm delivers highly relevant advertisement content
based on consumers' real -time behavior and preferences, leading consumers  to quickly feel that
the platform understands their needs (W. Gao & Liu, 2023). In this situation, successful
personalization increases feelings of being noticed and emotionally supported, which in turn builds
trust (J. Shen et al., 2025). Unlike conventional advertisements that are general and untargeted, AI-
based personalization on TikTok Shop creates a more personal and interactive shopping experience,
making consumers feel that the platform "cares" about their comfort (A.-Z. He et al., 2021; P. Shen,
Wan, & Zhu, 2023). Therefore, perceived personalization in this context does not only serve as a
technical stimulus but also as a trigger for layered psychological processes that simultaneously
activate positive affective reactions (perceived emotional support) and build trust (Cai & Suh, 2025).
The findings show that perceived emotional support acts as a consistent affective mechanism

rather than as a privacy violation. In developing countries such as Indonesia, consumers prioritize
immediate benefits such as convenience, relevance, and emotional support over long -term
concerns about data use (Butt et al., 2022). Additionally, the characteristics of respondents who are
accustomed to TikTok's algorithmic ecosystem make them more tolerant of personalization
because they understand that relevant content results from behavioral data collection (W. Gao &
Liu, 2023).
The results also show that trust is a central mechanism linking positive affective reactions to

2012), where both rational and emoti onal pathways contribute to attitude formation, but the
emotional pathway is particularly influential in hedonic and entertainment -driven contexts like

The findings of this study provide important conceptual implications for the AI -based
personalization and AET literature by affirming that perceived personalization is better understood
as a value -enhancing mechanism that provides emotional support and com fort to consumers,
especially on social media platforms already familiar with algorithmic personalization (W. Gao &

TikTok Shop as a shopping environment that is not only transactional but also emotionally
supportive.

functions as an affective event that triggers spontaneous emotional responses, thereby offering an
alternative to purely cognitive models of personalization -trust relationships (An & Ngo, 2025).

This study examined the effect of perceived personalization on trust through the mediating
role of perceived emotional support within the framework of Affective Events Theory (AET) in the
context of TikTok Shop advertising in Indonesia. The empirical findi ngs provide strong support for
all four hypotheses, confirming that perceived personalization functions as a salient affective event
that triggers positive affective reactions specifically perceived emotional support which
subsequently builds consumer trust.
The results demonstrate that perceived personalization has a strong and significant direct
effect on perceived emotional support (H1) and trust (H2), while perceived emotional support
significantly influences trust (H3). Most importantly, perceived emotion al support significantly
mediates the relationship between perceived personalization and trust (H4) with a variance
accounted for (VAF) of 44.6%, indicating partial mediation. This partial mediation pattern reveals
that trust formation in AI -based personal ization is driven by a combination of affective (feeling
supported) and cognitive (recognizing system accuracy) processes, consistent with the dual -
processing perspective, though the affective pathway remains particularly influential in
entertainment-driven platforms like TikTok Shop.
Theoretically, this study makes three distinct contributions. First, it extends Affective Events
Theory into the domain of AI -based algorithmic advertising, demonstrating that personalization
operates as an affective event triggering spontaneous emotional responses. Second, it resolves the
inconsistent findings in the personalization -trust literature by identifying perceived emotional
support as the missing affective link that translates personalization into trust. Third, by testing this
model in a developing country context (Indonesia), it provides empirical evidence that cultural and
generational factors specifically low digital privacy concerns and high algorithm familiarity among
young consumers strengthen the positive affective route of personalization.
Practically, the findings emphasize that marketers should design personalization strategies
that prioritize creating emotional support rather than merely achieving targeting accuracy, as
personalized content that makes consumers feel understood and cared for is proven more effective
in building trust. For the TikTok Shop platform, this implies maintaining deep personalization that
fosters emotional connections while providing transparency about algorithmic processes. For
regulators, the findings indicate th e need for continuous digital privacy awareness education, as
low concern about data misuse could become a vulnerability for data exploitation in the future,
necessitating a balanced approach that encourages responsible data use while gradually educating
consumers about privacy rights.
Limitation and Further Research
While this study provides valuable insights, several limitations should be acknowledged,
which also suggest promising directions for future research. The cross -sectional design limits the
ability to capture dynamic consumer responses over time, and relianc e on self-reported measures
may introduce bias, though common method bias was within acceptable limits. The sample's
demographic homogeneity predominantly young Indonesian adults restricts generalizability, and
the focus on a single platform (TikTok Shop) calls for replication across other e-commerce or social
media contexts. Furthermore, the exclusive focus on the positive affective route leaves the negative
route (emotional creepiness) unexplored, inviting future investigations into how both routes
interactively influence trust. Additionally, trust was treated as unidimensional, whereas future
research could benefit from distinguishing between cognitive and affective trust, and as this study
did not measure actual purchase behavior, subsequent research sho uld track whether the
personalization-support-trust pathway predicts real purchasing outcomes. Overall, advancing this
line of inquiry through experimental designs, multi -platform comparisons, cross -cultural studies,
and transparency-focused interventions will be critical to balancing the dual affective responses to
AI-driven personalization.
