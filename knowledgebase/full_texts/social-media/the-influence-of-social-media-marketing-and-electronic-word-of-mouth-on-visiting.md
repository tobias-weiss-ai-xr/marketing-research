Abstract
Digital transformation has changed how tourists gather information and evaluate travel decisions, making digital
sources such as social media increasingly important in shaping visi tors’ perceptions and behavioral intentions.
However, the roles of Social Media Marketing (SMM) and Electronic Word of Mouth (E-WOM) in shaping Visiting
Interest (VI) through Perceived Value (PV) remain underexplored in local heritage destinations. This st udy
examines the effects of SMM and E-WOM on VI and PV, the effect of PV on VI, and the mediating role of PV, at
Kampoeng Kauman Heritage. Using an explanatory quantitative approach with a cross -sectional design and
purposive sampling, 219 initial responses were collected; after eliminating 108 responses with constant patterns
(STDEV = 0), 111 responses were analyzed using PLS-SEM in SmartPLS, yielding a final measurement model of 19
indicators with outer loadings of 0.739-0.861. The results show that SMM significantly and positively affects both
PV (β = 0.685) and VI (β = 0.279), while E -WOM significantly affects VI (β = 0.277) but not PV (β = 0.114); PV
significantly affects VI (β = 0.387), and PV significantly mediates the SMM-VI relationship (β = 0.265) but not the
E-WOM-VI relationship. Five of seven hypotheses are supported, with the model explaining 59.0% and 69.9% of
the variance in PV and VI, respectively. These findings highlight the importance of credible, value-communicating
social media content a nd authentic E -WOM in strengthening digital marketing strategies for heritage tourism
destinations.
Keywords: Electronic Word of Mouth, Heritage Tourism, Perceived Value, Social Media Marketing, Visiting Interest.

1. Introduction
Digital transformation in the tourism sector has caused information about destinations
to originate not only from destination managers, but also from user interactions and
experiences across various digital platforms. Social Media Marketing (SMM) functions as a
source of information managed by the destination, whereas Electronic Word of Mouth (E -
WOM) provides information originating from users’ experiences. Both sources of information
can influence how prospective tourists evaluate the value of a destination and form a tendency
to visit.
Previous studies have shown that Social Media Marketing is related to tourists ’ visiting
interest and intention. Baber and Baber (2022) found that social media marketing activities
and electronic reputation play a role in shaping intention to visit through a digital information
mechanism. Juliana et al. (2022) also showed that social media marketing is related to visiting
interest through the role of perceived value. However, other studies still use varying outcomes

such as visiting decision, revisit intention, and tourist satisfaction (Fakari et al., 2023; Sasmita
et al., 2026), so that the relationship between Social Media Marketing and Visiting Interest in
the context of local heritage destinations still needs further examination.
In addition, Electronic Word of Mouth has different characteristics compared with
information managed by the destination. Information from users can provide a more
authentic experience and serve as a form of social validation for prospective tourists. Studies
by Aziza and Purwanto (2022) as well as  Jamilah et al.  (2022) showed that E -WOM can
increase visiting interest or visiting decisions. However, some studies have mostly examined
the direct effect of E-WOM on tourist behavior, while the mechanism by which E-WOM shapes
destination value evaluation through Perceived Value still shows varying results across
different research contexts.
Perceived Value is an important aspect because it explains tourists ’ internal evaluation
process regarding the benefits obtained compared with the sacrifices that must be incurred.
Digital information can help tourists estimate the benefits, costs, time, effort, and risk prior to
a visit. However, the strength of Perceived Value’s role as a mediator may differ depending on
the type of information source received. Official information from destination managers may
be more capable of explaining the destination ’s benefits and characteristics, whereas user -
generated information plays more of a role as social experience and validation.
On the other hand, research on Visiting Interest still uses a variety of terms such as visit
intention, visiting decision, and revisit intention. These terminological differences indicate
that the behavioral intention construct in tourism has different measurement contexts. In this
study, Visiting Interest is used as a construct that describes an individual ’s tendency to seek
information, consider, and plan a visit, and is therefore not equated with  actual visiting
decisions or repeat-visit behavior.
Based on these differences in previous research findings, there is a need to examine
Social Media Marketing and Electronic Word of Mouth as two sources of digital information
stimuli within a single rese arch model, with Perceived Value as the internal evaluation
mechanism. This study was conducted at Kampoeng Kauman Heritage as a local heritage
destination context that is still developing its use of digital media to introduce historical,
cultural, and tourism-activity value.
This study contributes to the tourism digital marketing literature in three ways. First, it
integrates Social Media Marketing and Electronic Word of Mouth as two distinct digital
information stimuli within a single model. Second, it examines Perceived Value as an internal
evaluation mechanism that may explain how these stimuli shape Visiting Interest. Third, it
provides empirical evidence from Kampoeng Kauman Heritage, extending the application of
digital marketing and behavioral intention research to the context of a local heritage tourism
destination.

2. Literature Review
2.1. Theoretical Foundation
The concept of behavioral intention refers to Ajzen (1991). The Theory of Planned
Behavior (TPB) explains behavioral intention as an individual ’s readiness to perform a
behavior, influenced by attitude toward the behavior, subjective norm, and perceived
behavioral control (Ajzen, 1991). This study uses TPB to interpret Visiting Interest as a form
of behavioral intention. SMM, E-WOM, and PV are not directly equated with the original TPB
constructs; rather, they are positioned as information and evaluation factors th at may relate
to the formation of intention.

The S -O-R framework has been applied in tourism research to explain how external
information influences tourists ’ internal evaluations and behavioral responses. Baber and
Baber (2022) showed that social media marketing efforts and e-reputation act as stimuli that
influence tourists’ perceptions and intention to visit. Therefore, this study adopts the S -O-R
framework by positioning Social Media Marketing and Electronic Word of Mouth as stimuli,
Perceived Value as the internal evaluation, and Visiting Interest as the behavioral response.
Although TPB provides a strong explanation of behavioral intention formation, it
primarily focuses on internal determinants of intention rather than explaining how external
information sources influence individuals’ evaluations. Therefore, this study integrates the S-
O-R framework to explain the mechanism through which digital stimuli, such as Social Media
Marketing and Electronic Word of Mouth, shape Perceived Value before generatin g Visiting
Interest. The combination of TPB and S-O-R enables this study to explain both the intention
formation process and the psychological mechanism underlying digital information
processing.
2.2. Social Media Marketing and Visiting Interest
SMM encompasses online community, interaction, content sharing, accessibility, and
information credibility (Gaffar et al., 2021). Informative and credible destination content can
reduce uncertainty, help prospective tourists form initial evaluations, and strengthen the
tendency to plan a visit. A positive relationship between SMM and visiting interest or visit
intention has been found across various tourism contexts (Baber & Baber, 2022; Islami et al.,
2023; Juliana et al., 2022).
H1: Social Media Marketing has a positive effect on Visiting Interest.
2.3. Electronic Word of Mouth and Visiting Interest
E-WOM is information about a product, service, or destination conveyed by users
through the internet. Reviews, comments, recommendations, and user experiences can serve
as references for evaluating the quality, safety, and suitability of a destination. Such
information also provides social validation that can increase visiting interest (Aziza &
Purwanto, 2022; Jamilah et al., 2022).
H2: Electronic Word of Mouth has a positive effect on Visiting Interest.
2.4. Social Media Marketing and Perceived Value
SMM provides information about a destination ’s attractions, facilities, access, safety,
and experiences. Clear and credible information helps prospective tourists compare expected
benefits with costs, t ime, effort, and risk. Social media marketing activities have been found
to be related to Perceived Value in tourism contexts (Juliana et al., 2022; Iriadi & Achmadi,
2024).
H3: Social Media Marketing has a positive effect on Perceived Value.
2.5. Electronic Word of Mouth and Perceived Value
E-WOM provides additional information that can help prospective tourists assess the
benefits, quality, risks, and sacrifices of a visit. Relevant reviews can clarify the experience
likely to be obtained prior to a visit. A n umber of studies indicate that user -generated
information can contribute to the evaluation of Perceived Value, although its strength may
depend on the context and credibility of the reviews (Pratama & Azizah, 2022; Iriadi &
Achmadi, 2024).
H4: Electronic Word of Mouth has a positive effect on Perceived Value.

2.6. Perceived Value and Visiting Interest
Perceived Value is an overall assessment of the expected benefits compared with the
sacrifices required (Zeithaml, 1988). When a destination’s benefits are judged to be worth the
cost, time, effort, and risk, the tendency to plan a visit is expected to increase (Juliana et al.,
2022; Iriadi & Achmadi, 2024).
H5: Perceived Value has a positive effect on Visiting Interest.
2.7. The Mediating Role of Perceived Value
Within the S -O-R framework, SMM and E -WOM represent information stimuli, while
PV represents the internal evaluation process that precedes Visiting Interest. However, the
mediating mechanism may differ according to the type of stimulus, outcome, and respondent
characteristics. Official information provided by destination managers may shape perceived
value differently from user -generated information  (Dewi et al., 2022; Juliana et al., 2022;
Sa’adah et al., 2025).
H6: Perceived Value mediates the effect of Social Media Marketing on Visiting Interest.
H7: Perceived Value mediates the effect of Electronic Word of Mouth on Visiting Interest.

3. Methods
3.1. Research Design
This study uses an explanatory quantitative approach with cross -sectional data
collection. The relationships among variables were analyzed using Partial Least Squares
Structural Equ ation Modeling (PLS -SEM) through SmartPLS. The cross -sectional design
evaluates statistical relationships within a single observation period and is not used to draw
absolute causal conclusions.
3.2. Population, Sample, and Data Screening
The exact size of the t arget population is unknown, so the study uses nonprobability
sampling with a purposive sampling technique. The inclusion criteria were established prior
to data collection in accordance with the research design and were not changed retrospectively
after the data had been collected. The inclusion criteria used were: (1) respondents residing in
Purwokerto or Banyumas Regency, and (2) respondents who had seen or been exposed to
social media content about Kampoeng Kauman Heritage. Respondents who did not meet
either of these criteria were not included in the questionnaire distribution. Exposure to social
media content about Kampoeng Kauman Heritage was an inclusion criterion as described
above, whereas direct visiting experience at the destination was recorded as a respondent
characteristic and was not used as an elimination criterion.
Lemeshow et al. (1990) explain that the minimum sample size for an unknown
population can be calculated using the formula = Z2P(1−P)/d2. With a 95% confidence level (Z
= 1.96), P = 0.5, and margin of error d = 0.1, n = 96.04 is obtained, rounded up to 97
respondents. The study set at least 100 respondents as the practical minimum threshold, and
the final model analyzed 111 respondents.
The study obtained 219 initial responses. A data quality screening was performed on all
indicator responses. As shown in Table 1, a total of 108 responses showed a fully constant
answer pattern across all indicators, resulting in STDEV = 0, and were excluded from the
analysis. This left 111 response s (Table 1) for the PLS -SEM analysis. This screening did not
change the purposive sampling criteria and served only as a check of response pattern quality.

3.4. Data Analysis Technique
Evaluation of the measurement model covers convergent validity, internal reliability,
discriminant validity, and indicator collinearity. Outer loading ≥ 0.70 was used as the main
criterion for convergent validity, while AVE was required to be ≥ 0.50. Reliability was
evaluated through Cronbach’s Alpha, rho_A, and Composite Reliability. All indicators in the

 Muchammad Agung Setyo Widodo et al. | Volume 5 No. 1 2026
113
final model were required to meet this outer loading criterion; indicators that did not meet the
model evaluation were not retained (Hair et al., 2021).
In line with the study ’s evaluation guid ance, discriminant validity was primarily
examined through cross loading and the Fornell -Larcker criterion. Each indicator was
required to have its highest loading on its own construct, while the square root of each
construct’s AVE was required to be highe r than its correlation with other constructs. HTMT
was presented as a supplementary analysis to show the closeness between constructs and is
discussed as a limitation when its value is high (Henseler et al., 2015).
Evaluation of the structural model covers inner VIF, R -square, adjusted R -square, f-
square, path coefficients, and specific indirect effects. Significance testing used the latest
bootstrapping results with a criterion of p -value < 0.05. P -values displayed by SmartPLS as
0.000 are reported as p < 0.001.

4.2. Discussion
4.2.1. Social Media Marketing and Visiting Interest
H1 is supported with β = 0.279, t = 2.714, p = 0.003, and f-square = 0.086. This means
that the more positive respondents’ perception of the quality of Kampoeng Kauman Heritage’s
SMM activities, the higher their Visiting Interest, although the additional contribution falls

4.2.2. Electronic Word of Mouth and Visiting Interest
H2 is supported with β = 0.277, t = 3.591, p < 0.001, and f-square = 0.134. E-WOM has
a direct positive effect on Visiting Interest, and its additional contribution falls into the small
category, relatively close to the medium category. Reviews, comments, recommendations, and
user experiences provide social evidence that can strengthen prospective tourists’ confidence
or curio sity. In S -O-R, E -WOM functions as a digital stimulus that can produce a direct
response. In the TPB framework, int er-user information can be related to social norms and
behavioral beliefs, without asserting that E-WOM is identical to subjective norm.
This finding is consistent with Aziza and Purwanto (2022) , Fitri et al. (2023), Choirisa
et al. (2021) , González-Rodríguez et al. (2022) , and Jamilah et al. (2022). In the context of
Kampoeng Kauman Heritage, authentic experiences of the village atmosphere, interactions
with residents, culinary offerings, and cultural activities can serve as a more personal reference
than official information. Managers can encourage visitors to voluntarily share their
experiences, provide photo spots and hashtag information, and respond to reviews
professionally without controlling or manipulating review content.
4.2.3. Social Media Marketing and Perceived Value
H3 is supported with β = 0.685, t = 6.832, p < 0.001, and f-square = 0.611. This path is
the strongest direct effect in the model and has a large effect size. The results show that
destination-managed information is the main source in shaping the evaluation of benefits
compared with sacrifices. In S -O-R, SMM as a stimulus is processed into PV as an internal
condition. From a TPB perspective, information ab out benefits, access, cost, time, and
activities can help form a positive evaluation of visiting behavior.
This finding is consistent with Juliana et al. (2022) , Dzhahabiyah and Prassida (2025)
and Iriadi and Achmadi (2024). At Kampoeng Kauman Heritage, content explaining historical
value, cultural experiences, local MSME products, facilities, access, visit duration, and costs
can make the destination’s benefits more concrete. The implication is that strategy priorities
should not rely solely on attractive visuals; every piece of content needs to answer prospective
tourists’ questions about what they will gain, what the sacrifice will be, how to get there, and
what activities are available.

4.2.4. Electronic Word of Mouth and Perceived Value
H4 is not supported, with β = 0.114, t = 0.983, p = 0.163, and f -square = 0.017. The
coefficient is positively directed, but the statistical evidence is not yet sufficient to state that E-
WOM increases PV in the 111 -respondent model. In S-O-R, not every stimulus automatically
produces a change in the internal condition; reviews that are brief, varied, or do not address
costs and benefits in a balanced way may not yet be sufficient to form a value evaluation. From
a TPB perspective, social information can also influence intention dire ctly without first
forming a strong PV evaluation.
This result differs from the findings of Pratama and Azizah (2022)  and Iriadi and
Achmadi (2024), who showed a relationship between digital information or E -WOM and
Perceived Value in other contexts. Filieri et al. (2020) explain that the effectiveness of
electronic word of mouth strongly depends on the credibility and usefulness of the information
received. Three characteristics of user reviews may explain this gap. First, in terms of quality,
reviews of heritage destinations such as Kampoeng Kauman Heritage tend to be short a nd
impression-oriented, focusing on atmosphere, photo spots, or culinary offerings, while rarely
detailing costs, travel time, access, or visit risks, which are the elements needed to weigh
benefits against sacrifices. Second, in terms of credibility, pros pective tourists often cannot
verify the reviewer’s identity, experience, or motive, so reviews may be treated as supporting
impressions rather than reliable evidence for a value judgment. Third, in terms of depth, a
destination with a relatively limited volume of detailed reviews offers fewer comparative and
balanced accounts, so respondents may rely more on official information when assessing
whether a visit is worthwhile.
The difference may stem from the object, the depth of the reviews, respondents ’
experience, and information availability.  At Kampoeng Kauman Heritage, E -WOM may
function more as a trigger for attention and social validation than as a structured source of
information about the equivalence of benefits and sacrifices. The implication is that managers
still need to encourage E-WOM, but value formation must be strengthened through detailed
and easily verifiable official content.
4.2.5. Perceived Value and Visiting Interest
H5 is supported with β = 0.387, t = 4.224, p < 0.001, and f -square = 0.204. PV  has a
positive effect with a medium effect size on VI. When the benefits of the heritage experience,
historical knowledge, area atmosphere, culinary offerings, and local activities are judged to be
worth the cost, time, effort, and risk, visiting interest increases. In S-O-R, PV as the organism
produces a response in the form of VI. In TPB, the evaluation of the equivalence between
benefits and sacrifices can support a more positive attitude toward visit plans.  This path
should be interpreted with particular caution, because the PV-VI pair has the highest HTMT
value in the model (0.967), which suggests that respondents may not have clearly separated
the evaluation of value from the interest to visit.
These results are consistent with Juliana et al. (2022)  and Iriadi and Achmadi (2024).
At Kampoeng Kauman Heritage, perceived value is not only economic in nature but can also
include knowledge, cultural, social, and experiential value. The practical implication is that
managers need to package a clear visiting experience, ensure accurate facility and access
information, and demonstrate uniqueness that is not easily found at other destinations.

4.2.7. The Mediating Role of Perceived Value in the Effect of Electronic
Word of Mouth on Visiting Interest
H7 is not supported, with a specific indirect effect of β = 0.044, t = 0.937, p = 0.174, and

mediating effect is not significant. At the same time, the direct effect of E -WOM on VI is

perspective, social validation and recommendations can be directly related to intention, while
value evaluation requires more structured information.
This result shows a different mechanism from the SMM path. Previous studies have also
shown that the mediating effect of digital information can vary according to the stimulus,
outcome, and context (Dewi et al., 2022; Sa ’adah et al., 2025). At Kampoeng Kauman
Heritage, user reviews may directly encourage curiosity or trust, but do not yet sufficiently
explain the equivalence of benefits and sacrifices. The implication is that E-WOM needs to be
managed as a source of authen ticity and social proof, while the information that shapes PV
still needs to be systematically provided by managers.
4.2.8. Discriminant Validity Limitation and Recommendations for Re -
screening
The HTMT values involving VI (0.929 -0.967) are the main limitation of  the
measurement model and should not be read as a minor caveat. Two responses are
recommended for future work. First, the retained and excluded indicators should be compared
systematically. The loadings, cross loadings, and HTMT contributions of the 28 ex cluded
indicators should be reported next to those of the 19 retained indicators, so that readers can
judge whether the staged screening improved construct separation or only removed indicators
that overlapped with other constructs. This comparison is not reported in the present study.
Second, the instrument should be revised and the data re-screened. The revision should
(a) add attention -check items to identify careless or patterned responses, (b) add a small
number of reverse -coded items, especially for VI and PV, to reduce common resp onse
patterns, (c) rewrite the VI items so that they refer to specific visit plans (for example, timing,
companions, and budget) rather than general evaluation, and (d) retain at least four indicators
per construct where possible, so that VI is not reduced  to three items. After re -collection,
HTMT (preferably with bootstrapped confidence intervals) should be re -estimated as the

primary discriminant validity criterion. If VI and PV remain empirically indistinguishable, the
two constructs may need to be merge d or the model respecified. Until then, the findings for
H1, H2, H5, H6, and H7 are best treated as preliminary evidence.

5. Conclusion
This study analyzed 111 final responses (108 of 219 were removed for STDEV = 0) using
a 19-indicator measurement model with all outer loadings ≥ 0.70. SMM significantly affects
VI and PV, E -WOM significantly affects VI but not PV, and PV significantly affe cts VI. PV
partially and complementarily mediates the effect of SMM on VI but does not mediate the
effect of E-WOM on VI, so H1, H2, H3, H5, and H6 are supported while H4 and H7 are not.
Theoretically, the findings extend S-O-R by showing that different digital stimuli work through
different mechanisms (SMM directly and through PV, E -WOM mainly directly), and
complement TPB by showing that digital information and value evaluation can be contextual
antecedents of VI. Practically, managers of Kampoeng Kauman Heritage should prioritize
credible, informative social media content that pairs visual appeal with details on history,
activities, access, facilities, time, and cost, while encouraging voluntary, informative reviews
through photo spots, a hashtag, and pro mpts on cost, access, and time, since PV cannot rely
on user reviews alone.
These conclusions are limited by the cross -sectional design, purposive sampling in a
single context with 111 respondents, STDEV = 0 screening that does not detect other careless
responding, and the removal of 28 of 47 initial indicators. HTMT values for VI with E -WOM
(0.956), PV (0.967), and SMM (0.929) exceed 0.90, so findings involving VI (H1, H2, H5, H6,
and H7) are best treated as provisional. Future research should refine the i nstrument, re-
estimate HTMT on a more diverse sample, use a longitudinal design, and apply more
comprehensive response-quality screening.
