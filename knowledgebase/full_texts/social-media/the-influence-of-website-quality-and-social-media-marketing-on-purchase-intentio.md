 This study aimed to examine the effects of website quality and social
media marketing on purchase intention, both directly and indirectly
through the mediating role of brand awareness, among business -to-
business (B2B) customers of PT Sumberparts Berindo, a  distributor of
mechanical components, including bearings and power transmission
products, in Jakarta. Unlike the initial research design, which also included
corporate reputation as a moderating variable, the model presented in this
study was simplified to a mediation model to provide a more parsimonious
examination of the relationships between digital marketing channels and
purchase intention based on the Stimulus –Organism–Response (S-O-R)
framework. A quantitative causal research design was employed, and
questionnaires were administered to 1 73 respondents selected through
purposive sampling. The data were analyzed using partial least squares
structural equation modeling (PLS -SEM) with SmartPLS 4. The results
showed that website quality and social media marketing had positive and
statistically significant effects on brand awareness. Social media
marketing and brand awareness also had positive and statistically
significant direct effects on purchase intention, whereas the direct effect
of website quality on purchase  intention was not statistically significant.
Brand awareness significantly mediated the relationship between website
quality and purchase intention and partially mediated the relationship
between social media marketing and purchase intention. The research
model explained 71.9% of the variance in purchase intention (R² = 0.719)
and demonstrated substantial predictive relevance (Q²predict = 0.634).
These findings indicate that, in the B2B mechanical components market,
improving website quality may contribute to purchase intention primarily
                    by strengthening brand awareness.

INTRODUCTION
PT Sumberparts Berindo (SPB) is a distributor of mechanical components, particularly
bearings and power transmission products, that has operated in Jakarta for more than 30 years
(Desyanti et al., 2025a, 2025b). The company serves a network of business-to-business (B2B)
industrial customers and distributes products from various international bearing brands,
including NTN, NSK, SKF, FAG, Timken, and Nachi. Because SPB is not exclusively
associated with a single product brand, its marketing strength depends substantia lly on its
corporate brand rather than solely on the brands of the products it distributes (IJAZ, 2024; Neha
& Kumar, 2024; Nguyen et al., 2026).
During the four -year period from 2022 to 2025, SPB consistently failed to achieve its
management-established sales targets, with sales -target attainment declining from 91.7% in
2022 to 71.9% in 2024 before improving slightly to 73.5% in 2025. This persistent gap between
actual sales and management targets indicates a business-performance challenge that warrants

further investigation into factors associated with B2B customers' purchase intention (Lasrado
et al., 2023; Pratama, 2025). At the same time, SPB has undertaken a digital marketing
transformation through website development and social media marketing activ ities to
complement some of its conventional marketing practices.
In the B2B context, websites serve not merely as information channels but also as digital
representations of a company's professionalism, credibility, and capabilities and may constitute
an important initial point of contact for prospective customers before they interact with sales
representatives. Social media marketing, meanwhile, can function as a communication channel
through which a company presents its technical expertise and engages more interactively with
industry stakeholders. These digital channels may influence purchase intention both directly
and indirectly by strengthening brand awareness. This mechanism is particularly relevant in
B2B purchasing contexts, in which customers may undertake extensiv e evaluation before
selecting vendors for relatively high-value procurement decisions.
Previous studies examining the effects of digital platform quality and brand awareness
on purchase intention have predominantly focused on business -to-consumer (B2C) contexts,
such as fashion retail, beauty products, and property, whereas research addressi ng these
relationships in the B2B mechanical components industry remains limited. This research gap
provided the basis for the present study, which integrated website quality and social media
marketing as stimuli, brand awareness as the organism and mediating construct, and purchase
intention as the response, consistent with the Stimulus –Organism–Response (S -O-R)
framework proposed by Mehrabian and Russell (1974).
The initial research model also included corporate reputation as a moderating variable in
the relationship between brand awareness and purchase intention. However, to maintain model
parsimony and clarify the mediation mechanisms central to the study's contribution, the model
presented in this article focused exclusively on mediation and excluded corporate reputation as
a moderator. The novelty of this study lies in applying the S -O-R framework to the relatively
understudied B2B mechanical components industry and examining brand awareness as a
mechanism through which website quality and social media marketing are assoc iated with
purchase intention. Accordingly, this study aimed to examine (1) the effect of website quality
on brand awareness; (2) the effect of social media marketing on brand awareness; (3) the effect
of website quality on purchase intention; (4) the effect of social media marketing on purchase
intention; (5) the effect of brand awareness on purchase intention; and (6) the mediating role
of brand awareness in the relationships between website qua lity, social media marketing, and
purchase intention among B2B customers of PT Sumberparts Berindo. Theoretically, this study
contributes to the literature on digital marketing in B2B contexts, particularly by extending the
application of the S -O-R framework to the mechanical components industry. Practically, the
findings may provide a basis for management to develop more effective digital marketing
strategies aimed at strengthening brand awareness and supporting customer s' purchase
intention.

METHOD
Research Design
This study employed a quantitative explanatory research design to examine the direct and
indirect relationships among the study variables, including the mediating role of brand
awareness.
Population, Sample, and Sampling Techniques
The research population is decision -makers, purchasing managers, or company owners
who are active corporate customers or prospective partners of PT. Sumberparts Berindo. Given
that the size of the B2B population is infinite, the sample size was determined referring to the
PLS-SEM guidelines (Hair et al., 2021), which is 10 times the maximum number of structural
pathways leading to one endogenous variable. The research sample was set as 173 respondents
dominated by customers from the Purchasing, Engineering, and Production division of PT.
Sumberparts Berindo.
The sample drawing technique uses non -probability sampling with the purposive
sampling method, with the criteria of respondents: (1) being a decision maker or recommender
for the procurement of bearing components (Purchasing Manager, Operational Manager,
Engineering/Maintenance Manager, or Director); (2) the respondent's company has transacted
or is exploring cooperation with PT. Sumberparts Berindo; and (3) the respondent has
interacted with the company's digital platform (official website or social media account).
Data Collection and Measurement
Primary data was collected online through an electronic questionnaire (Google Form)
that was personally linked via WhatsApp Business and corporate emails to customer
purchasing contacts, held in June–August 2026. All indicators were measured using a 5-point
Likert Scale (1 = Strongly Disagree to 5 = Strongly Agree). Before being massively deployed,
the instrument was pre -tested to 30 respondents outside the main sample to test validity
(Pearson correlation > r-table at 5% significance) and reliability (Cronbach's Alpha > 0.60).
Website Quality (X1) is measured through 6 indicators adapted from DeLone and
McLean (2003) and Lee and Kozar (2006), including the dimensions of system quality,
information quality, and service quality. Social Media Marketing (X2) is measured through 5
indicators adapted from Tarabieh (2022), including interactivity, trendiness, and customization.
Brand Awareness (M) is measured through 3 indicators adapted from Aaker (1991) and Hien
and Nhu (2022), including brand recall, brand recognition, and top-of-mind. Purchase Intention
(Y) is measured through 4 indicators adapted from Lee, Goh, and Mohd Noor (2019) and Hien
and Nhu (2022), including transactional, referential, and preferential intention.
Data Analysis Techniques
Data were analyzed using Structural Equation Modeling -Partial Least Squares (SEM -
PLS) through SmartPLS 4 software (Ringle et al., 2024), which was chosen for its advantages
in handling exploratory research models without requiring strict data normality assumptions
(Hair et al., 2021). Evaluation of the measurement model (outer model) included convergent
validity (outer loading > 0.70 and AVE > 0.50), discriminant v alidity (Fornell-Larcker and
HTMT criteria < 0.90), and construct reliability (Cronbach's Alpha and Composite Reliability
> 0.70) (Hair et al., 2021; Chin, 1998). The evaluation of the structural model (inner model)
included the determination coefficient (R²), effect size (f²), predictive relevance (Q²predict

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Respondent Characteristics and Descriptive Statistics
The questionnaire was successfully collected and processed from 173 respondents of PT.
Sumberparts Berindo customers. Based on the field of work, the majority of respondents came
from the Purchasing division (128 people; 73.99%), followed by Sales (11 people; 6.36%),
Engineer (9 people; 5.20%), and Production (5 people; 2.89%), indicating that most of the
respondents were procurement decision -makers who interacted directly wit h the company's
products and services. Based on the position level, the most respondents were at the Staff level
(125 people; 72.25%), followed by Managers (26 people; 15.03%), Supervisors (15 people;
8.67%), and Directors (7 people; 4.05%), so that the data represents perceptions from the level
of technical implementers to those in charge of strategic policies.
Descriptively, all research variables were in the High to Very High category: Website
Quality (mean = 4.279; SD = 0.795), Social Media Marketing (mean = 4.132; SD = 0.808),
Brand Awareness (mean = 4.335; SD = 0.772), and Purchase Intention (mean = 4.278; S D =
0.766). The highest score on Brand Awareness lies in the brand recall indicator ("directly
remembering the name of PT. Sumberparts Berindo  when needing bearings"; mean = 4.439),
while Purchase Intention lies in the actual purchase plan (mean = 4.399), indicating that
customers have high confidence in the quality and operational reputation of the company.
Evaluation of Measurement Models (Outer Model)
The convergent validity test showed that all indicators (WQ1 –WQ6, SMM1 –SMM5,
BA1–BA3, PI1–PI4) had an outer loading value between 0.805 and 0.929, well above the 0.70
threshold, so no indicators needed to be eliminated from the model (Table 1). The Average
Variance Extracted (AVE) value of the entire construct was also above 0.50 (0.720 –0.809),
which means that each latent construct was able to explain more than 70 % of the variance of
its constituent indicators. The reliability test showed that Cronbach's Alpha (0.847–0.941) and
Composite Reliability (0.907 –0.955) values of the entire construct were well above the 0.70
threshold, so that all instruments were declared reliable (Table 1).

Evaluation of Structural Models (Inner Model)
Evaluation of the structural model showed an R² Brand Awareness value of 0.577 (R²
Adjusted = 0.572), which means that Website Quality and Social Media Marketing
simultaneously explain 57.7% of the variance of Brand Awareness (moderate category). The
R² Pu rchase Intention value was 0.719 (R² Adjusted = 0.714), which means that the three
exogenous and mediating variables explain 71.9% of the Purchase Intention variance (strong
category, close to the threshold of 0.67 according to Chin, 1998) (Table 4).
Effect size (f²) showed that Website Quality made a moderate contribution to Brand
Awareness (f² = 0.182), while Social Media Marketing made a small contribution (f² = 0.047).
On the path to Purchase Intention, Brand Awareness made a moderate contribution (f² = 0.255)
and Social Media Marketing made a small contribution (f² = 0.143), while Website Quality
directly contributed almost non -existent (f² = 0.014), consistent with insignificant direct
hypothesis test results (Table 3). The collinearity test showed that  all VIF values of the inner
model were below 5 (1.73–4.17), so that the model was free from multicollinearity problems.
The model fit test yielded an SRMR value of 0.069, below the threshold of 0.08, which
indicates that the model has a good fit.

Hypothesis Testing
Hypothesis testing was carried out through the Bootstrapping procedure with 5,000
subsamples at a significance level of 5% (Table 6). The results of the direct influence test
showed that Website Quality had a positive and significant effect on Brand Awaren ess (β =
0.521; T = 4.952; p = 0.000), so that H1 was accepted. Social Media  Marketing also had a
positive and significant effect on Brand Awareness (β = 0.266; T = 2.519; p = 0.012), so that
H2 was accepted. On the other hand, the direct influence of Website Quality on Purchase
Intention was not significant (β = 0.127; T = 1.193;  p = 0.233), so H3 was rejected. Social
Media Marketing had a positive and significant effect on Purchase Intention (β = 0.385; T =
4.138; p = 0.000), so H4 is accepted. Brand Awareness has a positive and significant effect on
Purchase Intention (β = 0.412; T = 5.256; p = 0.000), so H5 is accepted.

Findings H1 and H2 confirm that both digital -owned media channels — websites and
social media — successfully act as a stimulus (S) within the framework of S -O-R that forms
the cognitive retention of B2B customers (organisms/O in the form of brand awareness), in line
with Budiarti and Oktama (2025) and Tarabieh (2022). The contribution of Website Quality (f²
= 0.182) to Brand Awareness is greater than that of Social Media Marketing (f² = 0.047), which
indicates that in high-value and risky B2B purchases, the clarity of technical specifications and
the reliability of official websites are more trusted as a primary source of information than
social media content, which tends to act as an amplification channel.
The insignificance of Website Quality's direct influence on Purchase Intention (H3
rejected) shows that in the B2B context of mechanical components, website quality alone is
not enough to instantly drive corporate customer purchase intent; its functional v alue is only
realized when brand awareness has been established first, as confirmed by the full mediation
role in H6. These findings are consistent with Hien and Nhu (2022) who stated that B2B buyers,
who represent institutions with strict financial accountability, tend to choose vendors with clear
brand awareness to avoid procurement risks.
On the other hand, Social Media Marketing has been shown to have a significant effect
on Purchase Intention both directly (H4) and through Brand Awareness mediation (H7),
showing a complementary pattern of partial mediation. This indicates that social media content
that is relevant to customer operational needs (highest score on the SMM4 indicator = 4.191)
is able to trigger psychological impulses of purchases more directly than websites, in line with
Akgün (2020) who emphasizes the role of social media in stimulating buying interest through
strengthening perceived usefulness.
Brand Awareness proved to be the most influential variable on Purchase Intention (β =
0.412; f² = 0.255), reinforcing its position as a risk filter for purchase decisions (H5), in line

CONCLUSION
Based on the PLS -SEM analysis of data from 173 B2B customers of PT Sumberparts
Berindo, the study concluded that (1) website quality had a positive and statistically significant
effect on brand awareness; (2) social media marketing had a positive and statistically
significant effect on brand awareness; (3) website quality did not have a statistically significant
direct effect on purchase intention; (4) social media marketing had a positive and statistically
significant direct effect on purchase intention; (5) brand awareness had a positive and
statistically significant effect on purchase intention; (6)  brand awareness fully mediated the
relationship between website quality and purchase intention; and (7) brand awareness partially
mediated the relationship between social media marketing and purchase intention. The model
explained 71.9% of the variance in purchase intention (R² = 0.719) and demonstrated
substantial predictive relevance (Q²predict = 0.634). From a managerial  perspective, PT
Sumberparts Berindo should (1) integrate its website -based digital catalog with its enterprise
resource planning (ERP) system to provide real-time inventory information and technical data
sheets and incorporate Request for Quotation (RFQ) and live-chat features, particularly because
73.99% of respondents worked in purchasing functions; (2) strengthen educational and technical
social media content, such as guidance on identifying bearing failures and preventing machine
downtime, while using LinkedIn to support professional engagement with industry customers;
and (3) conduct regular technical webinars or bearing clinics to strengthen brand awareness
and top -of-mind recall among industrial customers. Fut ure research should (1) expand  the
geographical coverage or compare B2B sectors beyond the mechanical components industry;
(2) incorporate additional variables relevant to B2B purchasing, such as e-trust, perceived risk,
customer relationship management, and corporate reputation, based on appropriate theoretical
roles as mediators or moderators; (3) employ longitudinal designs to examine whether purchase
intention translates into actual purchasing behavior over the relatively  long B2B purchasing
cycle; and (4) complement PLS-SEM with qualitative methods, such as in-depth interviews with
procurement decision -makers, to provide a more comprehensive understanding of B2B
purchasing and negotiation processes.
