Abstract:
This study investigates the entrepreneurial strategies used by aesthetic entrepreneurs in Limpopo
Province in order to enhance the sustainability and profitability of their businesses , focusing on the
innovative approaches they adopt to enhance their market presence in the province. The study
employed qualitative research methods within an exploratory design framework; semi -structured face-
to-face interviews were conducted with key participants from the industry. Findings indicate that
business management training  such as marketing, financial management, strategic management and
usage of innovation which is focused on integrated marketing strategies utilizing social media platforms,
such as Instagram and Facebook, along with word -of-mouth referrals and an informative website, is
particularly effective. The data suggests that businesses that focus on aesthetic procedures can benefit
from digital marketing avenues by promoting special offers, sharing positive customer testimonials, and
actively engaging users through interactive content. The study highlights the potency of training
business owners in digital marketing in r eaching a wide, tech -knowledge audience, thus emphasizing
its relevance across various sectors, including aesthetics. The recommendations include expanding
feedback mechanisms beyond traditional suggestion boxes to improve visibility and foster deeper
connections with clients, which will enhance their sustainability and profitability.

Introduction and Background
Entrepreneurship is seen as an engine of long-term economic growth as it creates new businesses and
jobs (Mehta, Qamruzzaman and Serfraz 2022). Despite initiatives to support entrepreneurship, South
Africa is challenged  with low entrepreneurial activity and high unemployment compared to other sub -
Saharan countries (Ngcobo and Khumalo, 2022). One of the biggest problems that business owners in
South Africa lack is training in entrepreneurial skills on how to conduct their business to ensure their
long-term sustainability and profitability, especially aesthetic busines ses which are the focus of this
article.
In the recent years, there has been growth in the aesthetic industry in most regions in South Africa
including Limpopo province. The city of Polokwane is the capital city of Limpopo Province and it serves
as a hub for aesthetic businesses in the province, providing residents with various services ranging from
hair and nail salons and wellness spas. Giyani on the other hand, is a town situated further north in the
province and serves as an emerging centre for aesthetics businesses. Entrepreneurs in both Polokwane
and Giyani have recognised the potential growth of the aesthetics market , its sustainability and
profitability by making efforts to create unique, culturally influenced experiences for customers.
Therefore, this study focused on these two areas within the Limpopo Province.

Aesthetic Entrepreneurship in South Africa

entrepreneurial acumen to create compelling brands and experiences (Wiratno  and Callula, 2024).
South Africa's aesthetics and personal care business, worth $3.2 billion, is one of the largest on the
continent (Lanto, 2021). The need for cosmetic operations is rising in South Africa, and patient -centred
care is becoming more and more important in this area. During doctor -patient communication, the
aesthetic practitioners' notions of beauty are crucial, as are their understanding of and assessment of

quarter of 2021, respectively. This demographic landscape underlines the critical role that employed
individuals play in driving economic and social practices. The reliance on human capital is evident across
numerous sectors, where effective practices depend not only on the availability of workers but also on
their skills and engagement.

McClelland’s Need for Achievement Theory
Psychological theories such as those developed by McClelland pay attention to personal traits,
motivation, and incentives of an individual and conclude that entrepreneurs have a strong need for
achievement (Virtanen, 2000; Pramudita, 2021).  Entrepreneurs have been defined by numerous
authors as individuals who are relentlessly focused on identifying opportunities either a new or existing
business. They seek to create something worthwhile considering the foreseeable risk and rewards
associated with the effort ( Olsson, and Bernhard, 2021 ). From this definition, it is clear that
entrepreneurs are individuals with a high level of need for achievement. To every individual who exerts
his/her energy towards accomplishing a particular task, we often say “This individual is motivated”
(Osemeke, and Adegboyega, 2017); therefore, this analogy applies  to entrepreneurs as they are
individuals who are likely to display the following five leading attributes of entrepreneurs:
• Self-efficacy
• Need for achievement
• Locus of control
• Need for autonomy
• Risk taking

and fluctuating consumer preferences. Those with a pronounced Need for Achievement are likely to

themselves in managing the new types of digital innovation processes that emerge. The nature of these
processes forces firms to challenge prior assumptions about their product and service portfolio, their

Aesthetic Entrepreneurs and Entrepreneurial Strategies

(Atiyeh, Rubeiz, and Hayek, 2020). Aesthetic surgery is currently experiencing both a severe ethical
conundrum and an identity crisis. A more thorough examination from an ethical perspective reveals that

Research Methodology
This study utilised an exploratory research design. Exploratory research is a methodology approach that
investigates research questions that have not previously been studied in depth. Exploratory research is
often qualitative and primary in nature (Scârneci-Domnispru, 2024). The topic of the study has not been
examined in depth in Limpopo province, as the aesthetics industry is quite new and less popular in the
province.  Exploratory studies are used when a phenomenon is not yet clearly defined, when variables
are not well established, and when there is limited empirical evidence to guide structured investigation.
In this situation, the aesthetics business which includes b eauty salons, skin care clinics and spas
operate within a unique socio -economic and under -regulated sector; therefore, exploratory design is
relevant for the study.
This study has utilised an exploratory design, which was aim ed at investigating a relatively under -
researched phenomenon of entrepreneurial strategies of aesthetics entrepreneurs in Limpopo province.
Therefore, interpretivism is particularly suitable for such studies because it allows for open-ended inquiry
through the usage of semi-structured interviews which enables discovery and generation of new insights
rather than test pre -existing hypotheses (Van Burg et al., 2020). Since entrepreneurial strategies in
aesthetics businesses in Limpopo are not well documented, an interpretivist approach supports the
identification of emerging patterns and practices.
 According to Haven and Van Grootel (2019), qualitative research attempts to answer 'how', 'why' and
'what' questions about phenomena. Qualitative research often uses language as data, whether written
or spoken, but photographs, videos, or other behavioura l recordings can also be used. The study was
conducted in two local municipalities of Limpopo Province, namely: Greater Giyani Local Municipality

individuals with specific characteristics or experiences that are essential for exploring the research
question in depth. This approach is particularly suitable for qualitative research, where the aim is to gain
rich, detailed insights rather than to g eneralise findings to a broader population ( Scârneci-Domnispru,
2024). The participants selection included the following characteristics: the respondents had to be
