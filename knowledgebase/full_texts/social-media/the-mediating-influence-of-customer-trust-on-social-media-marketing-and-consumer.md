Abstract - As visual products such as beauty and cosmetics are
being discovered and assessed in a digital -first way, the
marketplace has radically changed. Given the increased risks
involved in buying cosmetics online before actually seeing them
physically, this stud y focuses on the influence of soc ial media
marketing activities (SMMA) that affect the purchase intention
(PI), namely entertainment  (ET), interaction, trendiness, and
customization, with the mediating role of customer trust  (CT).
A structured questionnaire gathered primary insights from 350
online cosmetic buyers in Kerala's Ernakulam district via
snowball sampling. Furthermore, this methodological approach
allowed for capturing a diverse demographic spectrum of digital
shoppers within the region,  providing granular insights into
evolving e -commerce adoption patterns. The quantitative
evaluation was conducted through the use of multiple linear
regression, internal reliability checks, and mediation analysis.
The results suggest that all four promotional dimensions are
significant factors in CT (R2 = 0.618), with content trendiness
being the top predictor (β = 0.488). In addition, CT has a direct
positive relationship with PI (R2 = 0.577) and indirectly affects
the PI through marketing activities (Effect = 0.5338). The study
proves that, although creative marketing can spark initial
consumer interest, brand trust can be built by making it
contemporary, relevant to the trend and interactive, while the
online sales conversion can be achieved through their strategy.

I. INTRODUCTION
Digital technology has revolutionised the way businesses
communicate with customers. Social commerce is the
combination of social media and e -commerce features,
allowing users to find, assess and buy products in social
media contexts (Aloqool & Alsmairat, 2022 ; Hajli et al.,
2017). Brands are now frequently assessed and transactions
made on these platforms (Instagram, Facebook, YouTube)
that are becoming increasingly dynamic and are more than
just entertainment (Sokolova & Kefi, 2020) . This transition
to social commerce has been greatly accelerated with the
wide-spread adoption of smartphones, low cost of internet
connection and the popularity of social networks around the
world (Lăzăroiu et al., 2020).
Social media marketing has seen remarkable growth in recent
years, as consumers make more and more use of digital
platforms to get product tips, opinions from influencers, peer
reviews and enjoy interactive brand experiences (Singh &
Agrawal, 2024) . Social media is different from traditional
marketing in that it provides an interactive, real -time
environment with added value features like live -streaming,
user-generated content, and personalized ads (Cheung et al.,
2020). Hence, companies are spending more resources in
SMMA that are related to entertainment and electronic
word-of-mouth to influence consumers' perceptions and
behavior of purchasing (Kim & Ko, 2012; Yadav & Rahman,
2017).
The beauty and cosmetics industry are one of the most
dynamic and competitive sectors to benefit from social
commerce (Chuah et al., 2023) . With their visual and
experiential nature, cosmetic products are commonly
watched by users on tutorial videos, before -after shows, and
influencers' videos before users make purchasing decisions
(Sokolova & Kefi, 2020). But buying cosmetics online comes
with its fair share of risks, since they can't be touched in
person and can only be judged by the image provided online.
Trust is considered a key pillar for digital cosmetics brands
as there are concerns about product a uthenticity, misleading
information, quality, data privacy, and transaction security
(Hongsuchon et al., 2022).
There is a large body of research on social media marketing,
but few studies have explored the processes by which such
activities lead to purchase decisions within the mind of the
consumer. The majority of the published research focuses on
direct impacts o f SMMA on other metrics such as customer
engagement or brand awareness and neglects the mediation
of CT in the cosmetics industry (Alnaser et al., 2024). Visual
credibility and consumer trust are significant and key factors
in cosmetic purchases, and the mechanisms of these trust
building elements in SMMA are crucial to explaining digital
consumer behavior (Salhab et al., 2023).

II. RELATED WORKS
Social Media Marketing Activities (SMMA)
Previous studies at the foundational level had determined that
SMMA are multi -dimensional constructs that cons ist of
interaction, trendiness,  ET, customization and electronic
word-of-mouth, all of which affect customer equity drivers
and behavioral intention that affect downstream outcomes
(Kim & Ko, 2012 ; Yadav & Rahman, 2017; Yadav &
Rahman, 2018). Further research finds that these promotional
dimensions do not have the same effect in all sectors, but
rather that visual trendiness and real -time interaction are the
persistent drivers of interactions between consumers and
brands (Cheung et al., 2020; Chuah et al., 2023) . In the
personal care and cosmetics market, SMMAs positively
influence the perception of the product quality,
environmental attitudes, and consumer satisfaction, which
positively impacts both PI and the cognitive evaluation of the
product (An & Ngo, 2025; Begum & Kalpana, 2025; Nguyen
et al., 2024).
Customer Trust
Trust in social commerce is a vital cognitive connection
between outside marketing stimuli and consumer behavior
(Aloqool & Alsmairat, 2022 ; Hajli et al., 2017 ). Consumer
trust helps reduce perceived risk and transaction uncertainty
in online cosmetic purchases due to sensory risk factors
associated with the purchases, including not being able to
physically test texture or shades (Hongsuchon et al., 2022;
Lăzăroiu et al., 2020) . Research conducted on online
platforms shows that brand credibility and institutional trust
are the main drivers for conversion of promotional inputs into
PIs (Alrwashdeh, 2025; Salhab et al., 2023).
Purchase Intention
PI is considered the ultimate outcome variable in digital
commerce studies, which is largely affected by brand image,
platform interactivity and source credibility (Ali & Naushad,
2023; Sokolova & Kefi, 2020) . But synthesized evidence
points toward the existence of a long -standing
"intention-behavior gap", in which positive brand awareness
does not automatically mean that people will actually make
the purchase without going through other psychological
processes that help justify consumer choices such as trust
(Dwivedi et al., 2025) . Table I summarizes the empirical
studies reviewed across the three main constructs.

Research Gap
Existing studies have extensively examined the relationship
between promotional activities and overall brand equity and
engagement, but empirical studies that isolate CT as a
multi-dimensional mediator in the online beauty context are
still under-researched. The role of perceived value or brand
image as mediators has been the main subject of cosmetics
studies, with the path of trust under explored. Further, there
is limited empirical validation in localized emerging regional
markets like Kerala.
Theoretical Inferences and Hypotheses Development
Considering the synthesized literature, the dimension of
SMMA offer the informational and interactive cues that build
CT. The more trust that customers have in a company, the
less uncertainty they perceive, which leads to an increase in
PI, and so on. Therefore, CT acts as an explanatory mediator
between SMMA and ultimate PIs.
The following hypotheses are created to be tested
empirically:
H1: SMMA directly create CT among online cosmetics
buyers.
• H1a: Engaging ET content strengthens consumer
trust toward beauty brands
• H1b: Interactive communication enhances brand
reliability and CT.
• H1c: Perceived trendiness reinforces consumer trust
in online cosmetics.
• H1d: Personalized marketing content builds greater
buyer trust within digital beauty channels.
H2: Building consumer trust strongly motivates online
purchasing decisions
H3: Enhancing CT indirectly connects social media
marketing to purchase decisions
The multi-dimensional SMMA are hypothesized to influence
the PI (dependent variable) via the CT (mediator) as visual

III. RESEARCH METHODOLOGY
This section introduces the systematic methodology adopted
to explore the relationship between SMMA and PI while
examining the mediation role of CT. It provides details of the
research design, the sampling framework, the data collection
process, and analytical procedures used to analyse the
proposed model.
Research Design
This study is designed as a quantitative and descriptive
research. It is a design that can be used to empirically test the
statistical relationships among social media marketing
independent variable dimensions (ET, interaction, trendiness,
and customization), the mediating variable ( CT), and the
dependent variable (PI).
Target Population and Sampling Strategy
The audience is online beauty and cosmetics shoppers from
Ernakulam district in Kerala, India. A non -probability
sampling technique was used because the participants were
recruited by social media platforms that they have an
experience of buying cosmetics online. The total number of
valid respondents (N = 350) was used to collect primary data.

An online questionnaire consisting of three sections of
primary data was used. The first section is on demographic
characteristics. The second section assesses the aspects of the

Impact of CT on PI
A simple linear regression was used to test the direct effect
of CT on PI. The overall model was statistically significant
(R2 = 0.577, F = 475.197, p < 0.001), which means that 57.7%
of the total variance of the online purchasing decision can be
explained by the customer trust model. The effect of
customer trust was positive and significant on the purchase
intention (β = 0.760, t = 21.799, p < 0.001). Thus, the
hypothesis H2 is accepted.
Mediation Analysis
Hayes' PROCESS Macro (Model 4) was used to test the
hypothesis with 5,000 bootstrap resamples at a 95%
confidence interval. Social media marketing activities
(SMMA) had a direct effect on purchase intention which was
still statistically significant (Effect  = 0.1124, p = 0.0466).
Also, the indirect effect via customer trust was significant
(Effect = 0.5338, 95% CI [0.4439, 0.6271]). Customer trust
is a partial mediator as the confidence interval does not cross
zero and both direct and indirect pathways betwe en the
variables are significant. So, hypothesis H3 is accepted.

Discussion
The findings from the research show that social media
marketing can influence the purchasing of cosmetics online
as it directly builds consumer trust. Trendiness was the most
influential dimension in creating customer trust among the
four dimensions studied. This is a reflection of the visual and
fast-moving nature of the beauty market, where consumers
are looking for what is trending, viral and new product
formulations. In addition, interactive communication and
personalized marketing can reduce the online  purchasing
barriers, including shade matching concerns and concerns
about the authenticity of the product (Lăzăroiu et al., 2020) .
Importantly, the partial mediation effect reveals that creative
marketing is used to initially create consumer interest, only
to be successful in getting consumers to make a purchase
when it creates brand trust (Ali & Naushad, 2023 ; Salhab et
al., 2023).
As a digital marketer and cosmetics management, social
media campaigns need to focus on establishing trust before
making a sales pitch. It is important that brands have a regular
posting schedule, where they share trending beauty
techniques and new product releases to also stay relevant (An
& Ngo, 2025). Moreover, having faster response to customer
queries and open communication in public engagement
builds trust in the brand. When consumers are hesitant to
purchase a product, short -form video tutorials can be very
useful, combined with tailored product r ecommendations, to

V. CONCLUSION
The present study explores the structural relationships of
SMMA (comprising of ET, interaction, trendiness and
customization) with the outcome variable customer PI with
its mediation CT in Ernakulam district. The purpose of this
study is to link these cons tructs under an empirical model,
thus enabling a broader understanding of the psychological
motivations that underlie consumers' decisions in the digital
environment. The empirical results of the study with an
online cosmetic consumer’s sample of 350 (N = 350) reveals
that the marketing activities in SMM are strong predictors of
CT with the total variance accounted for by SMM activities
being 61.8% (R2 = 0.618). Individual dimensions showed that
trendiness is the most important dimension in consumer trust
β = 0.488), making it very important to ensure that the visuals
are fresh and timely, with current and up-to-date information
about the products to create a sense of credibility to the brand.
A significant direct predictor of PI was CT, explaining 57.7%
of the variance in PI (R 2 = 0.577). Moreover, mediation
analysis showed that the influence of marketing activities on
purchase decision was mediated by the customers' trust of the
brand (Effect = 0.5338), meaning that the marketing activities
have the highest commercial value if t hey are able to build
customers' trust in the brand. Despite its contributions, this
study has notable limitations that point to avenues for future
research. Specifically, the sampling method was
geographically restricted to the Ernakulam district using
non-probability techniques, which limits the generalizability
of the findings to broader regional or international cosmetics
markets. Future studies should incorporate multi -regional or
cross-national samples and employ probability sampling
methods to enha nce the empirical robustness and broader
applicability of the structural model.
