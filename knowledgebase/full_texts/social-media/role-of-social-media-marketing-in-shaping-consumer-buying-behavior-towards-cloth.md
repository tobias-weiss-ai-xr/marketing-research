clothingproducts arenotexplicitlydocumented.Thisstudyaimstoinvestigatethe effectofinfluencermarketing,socialproof,userreviews, trend-drivenFOMO,
personalization and credibility on consumer purchase of clothing. A cross-sectional quantitative research study was employed, with empirical data collected
through a questionnaire. The questionnaire consisted of demographic questions and twenty-five Likert scale questions assessing use of social media. Data
collection was done by convenience sampling on 4 October 2026. Descriptive statistics, Cronbach’s alpha reliability analysis, one-sample t-test, chi-square test,

ResearchMethodology
Research Design
The design of this study was quantitative, descriptive and cross-sectional study. Cross sectional method was preferred as it allows to study multiple
variableslikesocialmediamarketingelementsandconsumerbuyingbehaviourinonepointoftime.Thedatacollectionwasquantitativeandwasdone
through a questionnaire based on a structured online research methodology to help estimate the respondent’s perception and buying attitude toward
clothingproducts.
Sample and Data Collection
The questions employed a quantitative, cross-sectional empirical approach to assess the extent to which specific interventions of the platform impact
individualconsumerprocurementbehavioursintheapparel segment ofthemarket. Primarydatawas collected from aninternet surveylinkwhichwas
sent to a sample of internet and digital network users who regularly engage in the purchase of apparel and /or garment processes through the internet.
Theresearcherssuccessfullyusedanon-probabilityconveniencesamplingmethodtooptimiseresponsemetricswithinthedefinedwindowofoperation
that resulted in a final database of 30 effective data sources for this trial exploratory pilot. The measurement instrument consisted of 25 unique items
and was based on a 5-Point Likert Style ordinal scale, with data points scaled to positive absolute agreement (at 5) and absolute disagreement (at 1).
Themetricsinstrumentwasahybridcombinationofbaselineclassificatorydescriptivequestionsandbehaviouralevaluativescales.Theempiricalfield
datacollectionwascompletedatOctober2026withthecurateddatasetpreparingforinferentialstatisticalanalysis.
Instrument
Theprimarydatacollectioninstrumentwasastructuredquestionnaire,composedoftwoparts.
Section A (Q1-Q5) was a demographic and baseline behaviour profile of respondents online. In this section, we collected data on age, gender,
occupation,anddailysocialmediausagetimetoinvestigatethepossibleassociationbetweensocialmediausageandweeklyonlineshoppingfrequency.
Section B (Q6-Q25) had 20 items that measured the respondents’ perception on digital marketing aspects and purchase of apparel related to it.
Consumers’ responses were measured using a five-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (Strongly Disagree) to 5 (Strongly Agree). Six constructs were
adoptedfor measurement purposes based on the 20items: Influencer Impact, Reviews and Social Proof, Personalisation,Credibility andTrust, Trend-
DrivenFOMO,andPurchaseBehaviour.

Tools and Statistical Methods
The data obtained were tabulated, processed and analysed with the help of Microsoft Excel. Descriptive statistics (frequency, percentage, mean and
standard deviation) were employed to provide an overview of the demographic characteristics of the respondents and their responses to the
questionnaire items. The reliability of the questionnaire was measured by Cronbach’s alpha (α) in the evaluation of internal consistency of measuring
items. Correlation analysis was employed to determine the strength and direction of the relationship between the social media marketing dimensions
and consumer buying behaviour toward clothing products. The research employed multiple linear regression analysis to analyse the impact of five
social media marketing dimensions on consumer buying behaviour toward clothing products. The dimensions included Influencer Recommendations,
Customer Reviews and Social Proof, Perceived Credibility, Personalisation and Trending Content, and Fear of Missing Out (FOMO). These five
dimensions were analysed separately from the overall Social Media Marketing variable, which was not entered as an additional predictor in the same
regression model. Hypothesis testing was done at 5% level of significance, p<0.05 was considered statistically significant. The statistical techniques
were suitable for the quantitative nature of the study and were useful in testing the relationships and impact of social media marketing dimensions on
consumers’buyingbehaviourtowardclothingproducts.

Discussion
The result of this study has shown that there is a significant effect of social media marketing on consumer decision making of buying clothing. The
findinghas supported the previous finding of Bilal et al. (2014), Basit et al. (2021), and Rehman and Al-Ghazali (2022) which has also suggested the
positive impact of social media marketing on consumer decision making regarding their purchase of clothingproducts. The result of this study shown
thatthere is arelationshipbetween consumers' behaviourof buyingclothingproducts andsocialmediamarketing.When analyzingfor eachindividual
factor, fear of missing out(FOMO) was foundtobethe greatest predictorof buyingbehaviouramongconsumers. This would indicatethatconsumers
aremoreinclinedtopurchaseclothingproductsbasedonseeingproductsthataretrending, theconceptoftimesensitivity, etc.insocial mediacontent.
This result indicated thatcontent thatinduces FOMO influences the decision topurchase clothing items. Influencer Recommendationswas thesecond
most significant predictor for consumer buying behaviour. As a result of this, consumers might take into account the advice and recommendation of
product promotion by influencers when purchasing clothing. Fashion influencers could enhance product awareness and interest in a product by
showcasing clothing products, sharing experiences and recommending specific styles. This is in line with past research on social media influences on
purchase decision. Customer Reviews and Social Proof also proved to be significantly positively linked to consumer purchasing behaviour. This may
implythattheconsumermaybeusingtheexperienceofotherconsumersandtheirfeedbackoropinionaboutaproductbeforebuyingclothingproducts.
This mayhave been achieved throughpositive reviews, ratings, customerphotographs andsocial proof. In conclusion, there are differentsocial media
marketing factors that can influence the clothing buying behaviour of consumers. According to the regression analysis, FOMO and Influencer
Recommendations explained the highest percentage of variance in the regression model and had a significantly positive effect on clothing buying
behaviour whereas Customer Reviews and Social Proof, Perceived Credibility and Personalisation and Trending Content had a significant positive
relationship with clothing buying behaviour. Therefore, clothing brands can create blogs using real customer reviews, trending content, professional
influencer campaigns and credible influencers to interact with consumers and to stimulate shopping behaviour. Nevertheless, these findings must be
interpretedwithcautionduetothesmallsamplesizeof30respondentsandtheuseofconveniencesampling.
