# Social Media and Consumer Debt Accumulation Among the Lower Middle Class in Nairobi: A Focus on Instagram

Abstract: The rise in popularity of social media sites like
Instagram has revolutionized communication and consumer
behavior by promoting aspirational lifestyles, influencer
marketing, and easy-credit advertisement on the internet.
Although social media has improved  connections and commerce,
it can encourage overconsumption, especially for financially
challenged people. This study sought to determine the effect of
Instagram usage on consumer borrowing among the lower middle-
class population in Nairobi County, Kenya. Following the theories
of Consumer Socialization Theory and Social Cognitive Theory,
the study used mixed method research design comprising 399
participants out of a target population of 543,450 lower middle -
class people. Regression analysis was employed to analyze
quantitative data whereas qualitative data was analyzed through
thematic analysis. The results reveal that Instagram content,
influencer communication and time spent on the platform affects
borrowing behavior through increased consumption aspirations,
advertisement of easy credit products, and social comparison. The
study concluded that social media communication significantly
affects consumer debt accumulation and recommends improving
financial literacy programs and responsible digital lending an d
advertisement practices on social media.

Keywords: Instagram, social media communication, consumer
debt accumulation, financial behavior, lower middle class, Nairobi
County, Kenya.
1. Study Background
Social media has transformed the way people communicate,
get informed, and decide what to consume. Instagram, for
example, has ceased to be considered a platform which is used
to generate social interaction and has become an influential
marketing and comme rcial channel used by organizations,
banks, influencers, and content creators to influence consumer
behavior. By using visually appealing pictures, ads, influencer
endorsement, and recommendations generated by the
algorithm, Instagram constantly bombards consumers with
messages influencing their lifestyle and consumer behavior.
This phenomenon has create d new avenues of communication
and commerce; however, it raised some questions about the
effects of social media on financial decision -making of
financially constrained individuals.
Consumers' debts have increasingly become a

socioeconomic problem worldwide. This is because of
increased access to digital lending services and mobile lending
through concepts such as Buy Now, Pay Later (BNPL). Also,
social media has exposed people to various advertising
materials that promote cons umption now. From previous
research findings, it is clear that increased exposure to idealized
lifestyles, peer competition and social media influencer
marketing has led to materialistic values among people, which
causes impulsive purchases and borrowing a s a form of
financing for the purchase. Therefore, the issue of the influence
of social media on the behavior of individuals and leading to
consumer debts has been a growing problem.
In Kenya, internet connectivity and usage of smartphones
have contributed to increased use of social media among youth
and active people economically. Instagram is the most popular
visual communication platform in Kenya due to the presence of
lifestyle, en tertainment, business, and influencer marketing.
Similarly, Kenya has experienced significant growth in digital
lending services and allows consumers access to loans instantly
using mobile apps. While these technological innovations have
made financial inc lusion easier, they have also increased the
chances of borrowing money for discretionary spending,
especially amongst the lower middle class who struggle to
afford what they desire from the lifestyle images they see in
social media.
The lower middle class provides a key demographic to study
the correlation between social media communication and
consumer debt. This economic demographic usually has
enough buying power to engage with the virtual marketplaces
but not enough financial stability in case of any emergencies or
overborrowing. Consistent exposure to luxurious lifestyles and
products on Instagram may motivate people to overspend and
become more dependent on credit and borrowing through
digital channels.
While there are previous works that have investigated the
effects of social media use on consumer behavior, marketing
efficiency, and purchasing intentions, few studies have looked
into the connection between social media use and consumer
debt creation, especially in relation to the Kenyan context. The

A. Statement of the Problem
The wide use of Instagram by many people has brought about
major changes in product discovery, brand interactions, and
purchase processes of consumers. Influencer marketing
campaigns, advertisements, and lifestyle content has made this
platform an effective communication platform that can shape
consumer perceptions and behavior towards consuming
products. Although these changes have created increased
consumer involvement, they have also raised concerns about
their potential  impacts on overspending and borrowing,
especially for vulnerable groups of people. Unlike higher -
income earners, the lower -middle class is more vulnerable to
debt with a higher risk of falling back into poverty. According
to a 2020 survey conducted by the  United States International
University Africa (USIU- Africa) and the US embassy,
Instagram is one of the most widely used sites among Kenyan
middle-class citizens. Therefore, they are exposed to many
advertisements and influencers’ stories, which can trigger their
desires to purchase and spend money on credit.
In Kenya, fast growing smartphone adoption, Internet
connectivity, and digital lending services have made both social
media and consumer credit more available than before.
Consumers from lower middle classes are often subjected to
aspirational content, whi ch shows a lifestyle, products, and
experiences that can be above their financial abilities. In
addition, the availability of digital credit makes it easy for
consumers to borrow and consume. Despite these
developments, there is a lack of studies about the role played by
Instagram communication in contributing to debt building of
consumers in Kenya.
Prior research had investigated the effect of social media on
consumer purchasing behavior and financial literacy or digital
credit and over-indebtedness as two separate entities. As such,
there was a lack of information on how Instagram content and
usage as well as its users' engagements impact borrowing
behavior and consumer debt among low -to-middle income
consumers in Nairobi County. This information gap affected
efforts towards formulation of interventions geared towards
promoting good social media beha vior, financial literacy and
consumer protection. This paper investigated the effects of
Instagram use on consumer debt among the lower middle class
consumers in Nairobi County, Kenya. Through the
investigation of how Instagram content and usage affect
consumers, the study presents empirical evidence that enhances
the understanding of how social media communication affects
financial behavior in an ever-changing digital space.
B. Study Objectives
General Objective:
The main objective of this study was to investigate the role
of social media, specifically Instagram, in consumer debt
accumulation among the lower-middle-class in Kenya.
Specific Objectives:
• To analyze the influence of Instagram content on the
borrowing behaviors and consumer debt levels among
the lower middle-class population in Nairobi Kenya.
• To assess the role of Instagram content creators on the
borrowing patterns and consumer debt accumulation
among the lower middle-class in Nairobi Kenya.
• To examine the relationship between the time spent on
Instagram, and consumer debt levels among the lower
middle-class population in Nairobi Kenya.
C. Theoretical Framework
This research study was guided by the Consumer
Socialization Theory and the Social Cognitive Theory. Both
theories offer an excellent theoretical platform on how social
media communication affects the consumer attitudes, buying
behavior, and financial decision-making process. They
collectively describe how consumers acquire their
consumption-related knowledge from social interaction and
observations which may affect their borrowing behavior and
consumer debt creation.
The Consumer Socialization Theory is a theory by Ward
(1974). The theory suggests that an individual acquires their
consumption-related knowledge from social interactions with
different socializing agents such as the family members, peers,
mass media, and social media in the recent past. Social media
platforms like Instagram have played an additional role to the
traditional media in being one of the socialization agents by
offering the users various forms of marketing communication,
influencers' communication, social interactions, and
advertisement affecting their pr eferences and purchases. From
repeated exposure to such communications, the consumer
develops favorable attitudes towards certain products,
lifestyles, and even borrowing as a financing mechanism.
Social Cognitive Theory, formulated by Bandura (1986),
states that individuals learn behaviors through the observation
of others and the effects of their behaviors. In social media
platforms such as Instagram, the users are regularly exposed to
influencer, celebrities and peers showing behaviors that are
considered beneficial and socially desirable. This can result in
imitation of such behavior especially when there is social
reinforcement through likes, comments, and sharing. In

addition to that, according to Social Cognitive Theory, the

In combination, Consumer Socialization Theory and Social
Cognitive Theory offer a comprehensive framework for
understanding the ways through which Instagram
communication affects consumer debt creation. As explained
above, Consumer Socialization Theory provides insights into
the way social media is a vehicle for learning and financial
socialization while Social Cognitive Theory explains the role of

2. Literature Review
A. Social Media Content and Consumer Debt Accumulation
Social media has revolutionized modern marketing
communications by embedding marketing messages into
people's daily online activities. In contrast to traditional
advertising, platforms like Instagram feature a combination of
sponsored posts, influencer cam paigns, targeted
advertisements, and recommendation algorithms that influence
the formation of consumer attitudes and behavior in a
continuous manner (Taylor & Strutton, 2018; Pellegrino et al.,
2022). As a result, people get exposed to persuasive content
aimed at promotion of consumption in an environment where it
is not always clear whether the content is entertaining,
communicational or advertising.
An important kind of content on social media is digital credit
advertising. Social media platforms have become very popular
marketing tools for promoting mobile lending applications and
Buy Now, Pay Later services that promise quick and convenient
credits without many conditions (Bhalla & Kirui, 2023).
Although the development of mobile credit services has
improved people's access to credit opportunities, numerous
studies find that frequent exposure to messages about easy
credit may lead to normalization of  borrowing and impulsive
financial decisions especially for people who lack financial
literacy (Ahlström, 2020; Johnen et al., 2023). The same applies
to Buy Now, Pay Later products that can be promoted through
various social media platforms.
In addition to financial advertisements, social media posts
also promote consumerism via luxurious lifestyle imagery,
travel experiences, and images of possession of material goods.
Frequent exposure to such content leads to social comparison
and imitation of lifestyles of peers and influencers, which often
results in overspending by people whose finances do not permit
it (Taylor & Strutton, 2018). As noted by Credit Karma (2018),
peer-produced content and product recommendations may have
substantial power to influence people's purchases by making
consumption socially desirable and financially possible. In their
turn, Pellegrino et al. (2022) state that aggressive and well -
targeted social media marketing is what causes unhealthy
consumption patterns which can lead to adverse economic
effects on individual level and on societal level in general.
Moreover, persuasive character of social media is enhanced
by its design. Recommendation algorithms, personalised
advertising, one-click purchases, and limited-time offers reduce
the time people have to make conscious financial decisions and
increase the exposure to products and services relevant to their
preferences (Liu, 2023; Fallon, 2023). Such design features
stimulate spontaneous purchases which can cause indebtedness
in people who frequently use consumption -related content on
social media (Mondato, 2021; Muhammed, 2024).
B. Social Media Content Creators and Consumer Debt
Accumulation
In the current state of affairs, it can be asserted that social
media content creators are regarded as influential stakeholders
within the sphere of digital marketing. In general, the influence
consists in the ability to affect consumers' attitudes,
preferences, and behavior through direct engagement. Contrary
to classical celebrities, content creators engage in dialogue with
their followers through sharing their personal stories and
experiences that make them more convincing (Lou & Yuan,
2019; De Veirman e t al., 2017). Thus, today influencer
marketing becomes an inevitable part of brand communication
strategy because of its promotion of the product, service, and
lifestyle.
A great number of researchers claim that recommendations
of influencers, who are viewed as credible, competent, and
authentic, can be perceived more positively than traditional
advertising (Freberg et al., 2011; Lou & Yuan, 2019). To put it
briefly, the credibility of influencers increases the effectiveness
of influencer marketing because it enables imitating the buying
behavior and lifestyle as well as developing positive attitudes
towards promoted products (Djafarova & Rushworth, 2017).
Therefore, frequent contact with influencer content can increase
the likelihood of buying costly goods as the symbols of success
or status.
The lifestyle influencers have been seen to display idealistic
portrayals of fashion, travel, beautification, technology, and
lavish consumption as role models that can be followed by their
admirers. According to the theory of Social Comparison by
Festinger, people tend to make comparisons based on other
people whom they wish to emulate or admire. Such a tendency
might create inadequacies and FOMO, compelling them to
indulge in purchasing unnecessary items or borrow money to
keep up with their socially acceptable image (Przybylski et al.,
2013; Taylor & Strutton, 2018). The issue is quite pertinent in
visual social media like Instagram that displays idealized
images of life.

Accumulation
The amount of time people spend on social media has been
identified as one of the key factors which can reflect the level
of exposure to digital communications and marketing
messages. As people spend more time on social media such as
Instagram, they will g et more and more exposed to

advertisements, product promotions by influencers and peers,
and even recommendations of posts, which can change their
attitudes and preferences and affect financial decisions (Taylor

Based on the results of earlier researches, there is a positive
correlation between intensive social media usage and impulsive
buying, materialism, and decreased financial self -regulation.
Intensive interaction with consumer content normalizes
frequent shopping while making people believe that material

relevant to their previous behaviour. Personalized
communication context increases spending activities and
decreases chances for thoughtful financial decisions.
3. Research Design
This study employed a mixed -methods research design,
where the sequential explanatory approach was used to
investigate the effects of Instagram usage on consumer debt
acquisition in the lower -middle class of Nairobi County in
Kenya. With a mixed-methods research design, it was possible
to combine the qualitative and quantitative aspects of data
collection to have an enhanced understanding of the research
problem than what each method would yield on its own (Wasti
et al., 2019). First, quantitative data was gathered using
structured questionnaires. It was then followed by qualitative
data from interviews to gain additional information regarding
the experiences of the participants.
This study took place in Nairobi County in Kenya. Being the
capital city of the nation, and the most populated city in the
country, Nairobi is characterized by very high internet
connectivity and usage of social media platforms, such as
Instagram, thus, se rving as an ideal location for exploring the
link between Instagram usage and consumer debt acquisition.
Besides, Nairobi County features an extensive number of
people from the lower-middle class, who are active consumers
of digital financial services, lik e borrowing money online.
Purposeful selection of Nairobi County as the study location
was determined by the existence of proper conditions for
exploring the influence of Instagram content, its producers, and
time spent on this platform on consumer debt accumulation.
The target population was made up of lower middle class
inhabitants of Nairobi County, Kenya, which had an estimated
number of 543,450 people. The sample size was estimated
using the formula by Yamane (1967), producing a sample size
of 399 participants. Multistage sampling was used in selecting
participants for the quantitative part of the research. Data were
collected from 342 participants, among which only 316
questionnaires were complete and eligible for analysis. For the
qualitative part, 18 participants were pu rposively selected
through in-depth interviews.
4. Data Analysis and Presentation
The study aimed to achieve a response rate of 85% by
targeting 399 respondents. The initial response rate was 85.7%
(342/399). However, after screening, the final dataset was 316.
The respondents were predominantly young people, with
60.76% (n=192) of the respondents aged between 18 and 30
years, 25% (n=79) of the respondents aged between 31 and 40
years. Sixty percent of the respondents (n=191) were female,
while 36.08% (n=114) wer e male. Additionally, 53.16%
(n=168) of the respondents were single, while 85.44% (n=270)
were Christian. Forty -three point nine percent (n=139) of the
respondents were college -educated, while 36.71% (n=116)
were employed.
Almost half (47.78%) of the respondents (n=151) reported
using Instagram for more than two hours a day to follow
influencers. Regarding credit advertisements and aspirations,
69.94% of the respondents agreed that it was easy to come
across credit -related advertisements on Instagram.
Additionally, 69.9% of the respondents (n=221) agreed that
Instagram made them aspire to purchase the products they
promoted.
5. Summary and Conclusions
The study found that Instagram promotes credit -taking
among lower-middle class consumers by indirectly influencing
their decision-making process. Even though the social media
network does not overtly encourage people to take a loan, it
exerts pressure on potential borrowers to get a loan from lenders
that offer competitive rates and flexible repayment terms. In
addition, the study resolved to find out how users perceive the
financial credibility of influencers on Instagram. While many
users are skeptical of the financial credibility of influencers, the
content that influencers generate exerts considerable pressure
on social media users to consider taking loan products.
The conclusion that emerged from the study is that Instagram
has transformed from a social media platform to a commercial
space where financial habits and behaviors are dictated by the
actions of influencers. The study concluded that content
creators and m arketers exploit the aspirations and social
comparison mechanisms to influence borrowing decisions.
Therefore, social comparison and the sense of belongingness
that influencers generate have more impact on Instagram users’
borrowing behavior than their per ception of an influencer’s
financial credibility.
6. Study Recommendations
Based on the research conclusions, this study makes several
recommendations. The study recommends that policymakers
and regulatory bodies develop and enforce responsible
advertising policies for digital lenders on social media. These
policies should require the influencers to disclose any financial
affiliations and provide comprehensive details about the loan
products that they promote. The recommendations also include
promoting financial literacy among social media users,
encouraging influencers and brands  to operate responsibly by
disclosing financial partnerships, and advising digital banks to
implement responsible lending policies.
