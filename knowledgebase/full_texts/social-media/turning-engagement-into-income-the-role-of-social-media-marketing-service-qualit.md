ABSTRACT
Objective: This study aims to investigate the mechanisms that bridge digital
technology investment to corporate financial performance, specifically addressing the
“digital attention paradox” by examining the role of operational and cultural
capabilities in converting virtual engagement into tangible business benefits.
Research Design & Methods: Using an explanatory quantitative approach with a
causal survey design, data were collected via an online structured questionnaire. The
conceptual framework was empirically tested using Partial Least Squares Structural
Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM). This model operationalizes Social Media Marketing
(SMM) as an external trigger (antecedent trigger), along with Customer Orientation
(CO) and Service Quality (SQ) as dual -reflective mediating variables, leading to
Income (Inc) as the primary endogenous financial outcome.
Findings: Empirical results show that Social Media Marketing has no significant direct
effect on Income (Original Sample = 0.072, p = 0.059). Conversely, the impact of
SMM on financial performance is fully accounted for through a dual full -mediation
model. SMM significantly increases Service Quality (0.248) and Customer Orientation
(0.212). Furthermore, both Service Quality (0.566) and Customer Orientation (0.405)
exert a very strong direct influence on Income, accounting for 86% of the total
variance in that variable (R² = 0.860).
Implications: Theoretically, this study expands the Capability -Based View (CBV) by
demonstrating that static digital assets generate very minimal economic value unless
integrated with an organization’s human -driven internal capabilities. Practically, this
research provides a definitive roadmap for modern executives, demonstrating that the
success of digital transformation does not depend on the scale of social media
presence, but rather on aligning service delivery infrastructure with a customer-centric
culture to convert digital attention into tangible commercial transa ctions.
Novelty: The primary novelty of this study lies in its success in unpacking the “black
box” of digital conversion through the empirical validation of a dual-path full-mediation
structure. This study demonstrates that social media marketing lacks the independent
capacity to drive revenue; its financial sustainability depends entirely on the dual
mediating roles of Service Quality and Customer Orientation in transforming fleeting
digital engagement into sustainable business revenue.

INTRODUCTION
In the contemporary digital economy, the paradigm of market interaction has fundamentally shifted from
a physical-commodity-based economy to an attention -intensive digital ecosystem. The massive proliferation of
social media platforms such as Instagram, TikTok, and LinkedIn has redefined how organizations build relationships
with consumers (Alves et al., 2016). Human attention has now transformed into the most valuable digital currency.
For modern businesses, maintaining an aggressive digital presence is no longer an experimental option but a
survival strategy to preserve brand visibility and competitive advantage (Lamberton & Stephen, 2016). As a result,
companies allocate substantial budgets to produce creative content in pursuit of “vanity metrics” such as the
number of likes, shares, and followers. This approach is driven by a widely held linear assumption in the in dustry:
that high user engagement on social media will automatically and directly increase business revenue.
However, industry realities present a contradictory narrative that gives rise to the “digital attention
paradox.” Many companies record viral digit al interactions online, yet the actual revenue figures in their financial
reports remain stagnant or even decline (Saura et al., 2021). This real-world phenomenon reveals a broken link in
the digital conversion process. Social media, by its very nature, is merely an initial medium of interaction capable
of bringing potential customers to a business’s doorstep; however, these platforms lack the inherent ability to
ensure that this initial contact results in a sustainable financial transaction. The failure of these digital marketing

strategies often occurs because companies focus too much on  amplifying their “voice” externally while neglecting
the readiness of their internal organizational capacity (Quinn et al., 2016).
To resolve this paradox, academic and practical attention must shift from technological metrics toward
more human-centered internal aspects, one of which is Service Quality. When a Soci al Media Marketing (SMM)
strategy successfully builds high expectations through captivating visuals, those digital promises are immediately
put to the test when consumers interact with the company’s actual service system  (Kamboj et al., 2018). Service
quality is not merely about the speed of automated message responses, but rather about the empathy,
responsiveness, and human reliability that consumers experience when they step away from the virtual screen
(Truong & Chen, 2025). If the sweet promises on social media are shattered by the reality of poor service, consumer
trust will immediately evaporate. This disappointment not only cancels transactions but also triggers negative online
publicity that damages the company’s financial reputation (Tajvidi & Karami, 2021). Therefore, service quality acts
as an essential operational engine that transforms the flood of digital attention into valid transactions.
Successful digital conversion requires a strong philosophical foundation in organizational culture, namely
Customer Orientation. Companies with a strong customer orientation do not view social media users merely as
sales targets, but as whole human beings with their own needs and challenges  (Hsu & Hu, 2024) . When social
media marketing is conducted on the foundation of a customer-oriented culture, the content produced is no longer
manipulative in pursuit of fleeting virality but focuses on presenting genuin e solutions (Radebe et al., 2024). This
humanistic approach builds deep emotional bonds and trust, which ultimately breaks down purchasing resistance
as consumers move through the sales funnel. The integration of social media’s appeal, operational reliability of
service, and a culture of genuine care is, in theory, the complete formula for transforming digital engagement into
tangible financial contributions  (Hollebeek & Macky, 2019) . Although the importance of social media marketing,
service quality, and customer orientation has been widely discussed separately, the mechanisms by which these
three elements interact simultaneously to drive business revenue remain an uncharted “black box.” Most of the
existing literature remains trapped in simplistic and reductionist tests of direct effects, which often yield inconsistent
and contradictory findings. These studies overlook the fact that digital conversion pathways require a bridge of
internal organizational capabilities so that technology investments can yield results on financial statements.
This study aims to bridge this conceptual gap by rejecting the assumption of a direct linear relationship.
Using the Resource -Based View and Dynamic Capabilities Theory, we test a new structural model that positions
service quality and customer orientation as dual mediating variables. Therefore, the main novelty of this study lies
in its success in unraveling the “black box” of the digital conversion mechanism through the validation of a dual
full-mediation model, which demonstrates that social media marketing does not directly dictate financial revenue;
but rather depends entirely on the mediating roles of Service Quality and Customer Orientation in transforming
digital engagement in to sustainable business revenue a conceptual integration that has long been overlooked in
the global digital marketing management literature.
LITERATURE REVIEW
The development of the conceptual model in this study is theoretically grounded in the Capability -Based
View (CBV), a framework that emerged as an extension of the Resource-Based View (RBV). While the RBV argues
that a f irm’s competitive advantage stems from the ownership of valuable, rare, unique, and difficult -to-imitate
assets, the CBV goes a step further by asserting that digital assets or resources will not generate optimal economic
value if they exist in isolation (Tajvidi & Karami, 2021). In the context of digitalization, social media platforms are
categorized as static digital assets that competitors can easily purchase or imitate (Moro-Visconti & Cesaretti, 2023).
The true strategic value of these digital assets is realized only when a firm possesses the internal organizational
capabilities to integrate and deploy these technological resources to respond to market dynamics. Referring to this
concept, Social Media Marketing (SMM) acts as an external technological antecedent  (Eze et al., 2021) , Service
Quality and Customer Orientation act as internal organizational capabilities that serve as mediators in translating
these technology investments into tangible financial performance, namely business revenue.
The direct relationship between Social Media Marketing and Business Revenue is rooted in the premise of
digital marketing that interactive platforms facilitate two -way dialogue, build engagement, and reduce traditional
intermediary costs to drive sales  (Alves et al., 2016). Through SMM activities, organizations have a very powerful
tool for capturing market signals, monitoring shifts in user preferences, and collecting real -time interaction data
(Munoz et al., 2016). However, in line with contemporary empirical findings, the direct impact of SMM on financial
performance often yields inconsistent or weak results due to the existence of a cognitive gap between users’
recreational behavior in cyberspace and their actual financial commitment (Saura et al., 2021). Therefore, to ensure
that SMM strategies can be directly translated into revenue, companies need a stable conversion cha nnel where
the digital content produced isn’t merely chasing fleeting virality, but is designed to break down purchasing barriers
and directly trigger a flow of commercial transactions.
To address the uncertainty surrounding this direct impact, the first intermediary pathway links Social Media
Marketing to revenue through Service Quality. Service quality refers to users’ cognitive and affective evaluations of
the dimensions of reliability, responsiveness, empathy, and assurance provided by the firm (Truong & Chen, 2025).

When SMM channels are effectively used to address complaints, identify emerging trends, and provide responsive
feedback, they directly enhance the firm’s service management operational efficiency. SMM acts as an antecedent
that conditions and improves the quality of an organization’s service infrastructure  (Tajvidi & Karami, 2021) .
Furthermore, this superior service quality acts as a key driver of revenue by reducing the customer churn rate,
increasing the frequency of repeat purchases, and building strong loyalty. Through this pathway, service quality
functions as an operational mediator that ensures digital promises made on  social media are brilliantly fulfilled in
the physical world, thereby converting digital engagement into corporate cash flow (Tarnanidis et al., 2026).
The second mediating pathway in this model links Social Media Marketing to Revenue through the role of
Customer Orientation. Customer orientation is not merely a short -term marketing strategy, but a deep -rooted
business culture and philosophy that places customer interests, satisfaction, and value at the highest priority in
every firm’s decision (Hsu & Hu, 2024). Intensive digital interactions through SMM act as a catalyst that compels
organizations to listen closely to users’ voices, break down internal bureaucratic barriers, and shift the firm’s
orientation from a product -centric focus to a customer -centric one (Kamboj et al., 2018) . Once this customer -
oriented culture is firmly institutionalized, it becomes a key driver of revenue growth. This customer-centric culture
works to break down price barriers in users’ m inds, reduce the cost of acquiring new customers, and extend
customer lifetime value (Hsu & Hu, 2024). Thus, customer orientation functions as a cultural mediator that ensures
a firm’s digital marketing messages are supported by the organization’s sincerity in solving users’ real -world
problems (Wang, 2020) . This study rejects the simplistic direct -effect model by introducing a dual -path full -
mediation model. It is this synergistic combination of operational efficiency (Service Quality) and cultural sincerity
(Customer Orientation) that acts as a mechanistic processor to unravel the “black box” of digital marketing, while
simultaneously ensuring that every unit of attention captured on social media is successfully converted into business
revenue on the firm’s financial statements. Visually, the overall theoretical integration, hypothetical directions, and

METHODS
This study employs an explanatory quantitative approach with a causal survey design to test the structural
interactions among Social Media Marketing (SMM), Service Quality (SQ), Customer Orientation (CO), and Income
(Inc). The target population in this study includes organizational units or consumers who interact directly with the
company’s digital marketing ecosystem. The sampling procedure was conducted using a non -probability sampling
method through the online distribution of a structured questionnaire. The sample size was determined based on
methodological recommendations regarding the statistical power of p ath analysis, which requires a minimum
sample size to mitigate the risk of Type II errors in testing a multiple mediation model. The measurement scale
adopted in this research instrument uses a Likert scale to measure respondents’ perceptual responses to a ll
statement items of the proposed variables.
All latent variables in this research model were operationalized into reflective indicators adopted from
reputable literature on digital marketing and strategic management. Based on the model’s measurement
specifications, the Customer Orientation (CO) variable was measured using five reflective indicators (CO1 through
CO5). The Income (Inc) variable is evaluated using six measurement indicators (Inc1 through Inc6) that represent
the organization’s financial grow th and stability. Meanwhile, the Service Quality (SQ) variable is operationalized
using four main reflective indicators (SQ1 through SQ4). Finally, the Social Media Marketing (SMM) variable is

comprehensively measured through six external indicators (SMM1 to SMM6). The use of multiple reflective
indicators aims to ensure that all conceptual dimensions of each latent variable are adequately represented and
possess a strong level of content validity prior to empirical model testing.
The data analysis method u sed to test the hypotheses and conceptual framework of this study is Partial
Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM). The selection of PLS-SEM is based on its superior ability to
handle complex structural models with multiple mediating paths s imultaneously, without requiring strict
assumptions of data normality.  The data evaluation process consists of two main stages: measurement model
analysis (outer model evaluation) and structural model analysis (inner model evaluation). The outer model
evaluation stage focuses on testing whether the instrument meets validity and reliability criteria, which includes
testing convergent validity through factor loadings and Average Variance Extracted (AVE), testing discriminant
validity using the cross -loading matrix and the Heterotrait -Monotrait (HTM) ratios, and reliability testing using
Cronbach’s alpha, Composite Reliability (ᵖ_a), and Composite Reliability (ᵖ_c).
After the measurement model was found to meet all criteria for reliability and statistical validity, the
analysis proceeded to the second stage: the evaluation of the structural model (inner model). This stage was
designed to assess the model’s predictive quality and test the significance of the hypothesized relationships between
variables. Model fit criteria were evaluated using the Standardized Root Mean Square Residual (SRMR) and the
Normed Fit Index (NFI) to ensure that the empirical model was consistent with the field data. Furthermore, the
proportion of the endogenous variables’ variance explained by the exogenous variables was measured using the R-
squared (R2) and adjusted R -squared values. Significance tests for direct path coefficients and specific indirect
effects were conducted using non -parametric bootstrapping procedures. This algorit hmic approach ensures that
the strength and significance of the dual mediation effects of Service Quality and Customer Orientation can be
robustly tested without statistical bias.

Testing the role of Social Media Marketing (SMM) as an external technological driver revealed highly
intriguing structural findings. SMM was found to have a positive and significant direct effect on strengthening the
organization’s internal capabilities, both in operational and cultural aspects. Specifically, the direct effect of SMM

empirically demonstrate that the strategic use of social media marketing serves as a key stimulus capable of training
organizations to become more responsive in their service delivery and triggering a shift toward a work culture that
is more centered on customer needs.
The most crucial statistical finding was observed in the direct relationship between Social Media Marketing
and Revenue. The test results indicate that the direct effect of SMM on Revenue yielded a very weak path coefficient
of 0.072, with a T -statistic of 1.889 and a P -value of 0.059. Since the T -statistic is below 1.96 and the P -value is
above the critical threshold of 0.05, the hypothesis stating that social media marketing has a significant direct effect
on increasing business revenue is empirically rejected.
The rejection of this direct path provides very strong statistical confirmation of the existence of the “digital
attention paradox” in th e field, while also offering a fundamental initial empirical justification that investment in

DISCUSSION
This study was comprehensively designed to uncover the internal organizational mechanisms that bridge
digital technology investments to the achievement of sustainable financial excellence, while also addressing the
long-standing theoretical debate regarding the direct effectiveness of Social Media Marketing (SMM) expenditures
on income performance. A thorough evaluation of the structural model (inner model) in this study reveals a
statistical reality that is crucial and fundamental to the development of contemporary management theory, in which
the direct influence of Social Media Marketing on income is found to be statistically insignificant. This empirical
reality provides very strong scientific confirmation of t he actual existence of the “digital attention paradox”
phenomenon in the modern industrial sphere. This phenomenon demonstrates that high levels of casual digital
engagement metrics (vanity metrics) such as the number of likes, content shares, comments, followers, and even
viral video views across  various social media platformsin no way guarantee an instant transformation into a solid
stream of commercial revenue on corporate financial statements. These results firmly refute the simplistic linear
assumptions that have long been uncritically accepted by many industry practitioners and simultaneously reinforce
the findings of (Saura et al., 2021). The literature emphasizes that social media platforms are, in essence, merely
an initial medium of interaction with inherent structural limitations that prevent them from generating financial
conversions on their own if they operate in isolation from a company’s operational capabilities.
The theoretical insignificance of this direct influence pathway was brilliantly unraveled through the
validation of a dual -path full-mediation model involving Serv ice Quality and Customer Orientation as mediating
variables. The successful validation of this specific indirect effect makes a massive contribution to the development
and contextualization of the Capability -Based View (CBV) within the digital business lan dscape (Tajvidi & Karami,
2021). Based on the fundamental tenets of CBV, social media technologies and platforms are classified purely as
static digital assets that are very easy for competitors in the market to purchase, replicate, or simulate, and
therefore cannot serve as the sole sour ce of long-term competitive advantage. The true strategic value of these
digital assets is realized only when a firm possesses the internal organizational capabilities to configure, integrate,
and deploy these technological resources to respond agilely to the dynamics of market needs. In this research
model, SMM acts as an external driver (antecedent trigger) tasked with capturing public attention, exploiting market
information asymmetries, and gathering data on consumer expectations. When the digital inter action and
engagement data from SMM is effectively channeled into the organization, it serves as the primary fuel for activating
and configuring two key internal capabilities: the reliability of the value-delivery operational system (Service Quality)
and a  human-centered work culture philosophy (Customer Orientation).  It is this combination of integrated
interactions that, cumulatively, dramatically boosts the company’s ability to generate business revenue, as

The second mediation pathway, through the Customer Orientation variable, provides a cultural and
philosophical foundation that is crucial for strengthening and sustaining this digital conversion efficiency.  The
estimation results show that SMM has a positive and significant effect on Customer Orientation of 0.212, which in

channel is significant at 0.086, with a T -statistic of 2.138. From a strategic management perspective, the
transparent, equal, and intimate space for interaction on social media acts as a change agent that compels
organizations to dismantle the egos of internal, siloed bureaucracy and shift their business orientation from a
product-centric focus to a radically transformed, customer-centric one (Kamboj et al., 2018). Once this customer-
oriented culture has been deeply internalized into the very fabric of the organization, the company’s approach to
utilizing social media will undergo a cognitive evolution. The content, narratives, and interactive programs presented
on social media are no longer designed in a manipulative or self-serving manner to chase fleeting popularity metrics,
but are instead fully focused on delivering authentic value, education, and empathetic solutions that directly address
the real-life problems of their customer community. According to the postulate by Hsu & Hu (2024), This culture of
genuine and visible care builds psychological equity and deep consumer trust long before any formal transaction
begins. When these emotionally nurtured customers decide to make a purchase, they do so with a very high level
of brand commitment, which drastically reduces price resistance, lowers the cost of acquiring new customers, and
extends customer lifetime value significantly multiplying the company’s revenue.
It is this synergistic integration between the reliability of the physical service delivery system (Service
Quality) and the sincerity of the organization’s culture of care (Customer Orientation) that has fully and thoroughly
succeeded in unraveling the “ black box” of digital marketing a mystery that has long puzzled academics and
executives at global companies.  The scientific fact that the R -squared values for Service Quality (0.062) and

remarkable 0.860, constitutes a highly elegant theoretical finding in this study. This dynamic proves that Social
Media Marketing (SMM) functions purely as a concise external antecedent trigger. SMM is not designed to overhaul
or dominate the entirety of an organization’s internal structure; however, its modest leverage has been proven to
be absolutely essential for activating a company’s internal capabilities. With out the simultaneous activation of
service quality and a customer-oriented culture, the digital attention generated by social media will simply evaporate
into sunk costs that are detrimental to the corporation. Overall, the discovery of this dual full -mediation model
confirms a new paradigm shift in marketing theory: financial success in the digital economy is not determined by
how loudly, aesthetically, or virally a company voices itself on social media, but rather depends entirely on the
readiness, resilience, and alignment of the organization’s internal human capabilities in absorbing, managing, and
processing this abundance of digital attention into satisfying and human -centered real -world commercial
transactions. This study successfully resolves the dig ital attention paradox while offering a definitive strategic
roadmap for modern organizations to transform fleeting virtual interactions into permanent, stable, and sustainable
business revenue growth reflected in corporate financial statements.
