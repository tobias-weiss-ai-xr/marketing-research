Abstract: With the rapid growth of Internet technology and its related tools, the level of interest in and
engagement with the virtual space has increased, and, among other things, marketing communicatio n
has undergone significant changes. This space has provided a favorable opportunity for influencers to
produce and present the content they intend to use to influence decision -making and gain the trust of
audiences. However, the rapid advancement of market ing and content production has led to the
emergence of a new type of influencer in the Internet space, referred to in the literature of advertising
and communication as “virtual influencers” or human -like influencers. Their activities include
advertising, marketing, guidance, and entertainment in the Internet space, particularly on social
networks. Despite the global expansion of this type of influencer, there is a noticeable lack of activity
and research concerning them in Afghanistan. Therefore, the main question of this study is to address
the possibilities and challenges of creating and developing virtual influencers in the Afghan context.
Through in -depth interviews and semi -structured questions with seven journalists, marketing
professionals, and public relations specialists, the opportunities and challenges of creating and
developing this type of influencer in Afghanistan were examined. The findings identify the creation and
activities of virtual influencers at the intersection of four major themes within the context of Afghanistan
social networks: challenges, contexts, audiences, and professional ethics. According to the participants,
due to the lack of educational and academic resources in the fields of digital marketing and studies of
new technologies, there is still a considerab le gap between social networks and the development of
virtual influencers in accordance with global standards. However, if this type of influencer is established
and promoted, the priority of the people of Afghanistan will be the promotion of the values of dialogue,
participation, education, and tolerance.
Keywords :  Social Networks, Avatar, Technology, Communication, Censorship, Virtual Influencer.
INTRODUCTION
With the rapid growth of Internet technology and its related tools, the level of human interest in
and engagement with the virtual space has also increased, and our lives have been transformed by digital
technologies and artificial intelligence, to the extent that some of our actions are carried out under the
influence of advertising in the virtual space. Nowdays, “platforms make content more attractive, visually
appealing, and relevant to ensure that user engagement and participation remain consistently active ”
(Mesbah, 2026, p. 101). Previously, human influencers operated in traditional and later modern media
and social networks for marketing, advertising, and influencing consumers’ decision-making. However,
now, alongside the rapid development of the Internet and people’s dependence on the virtual space,

marketing communication in the virtual space has undergone transformation, and non -human
personalities, also referred to as virtual influencers, have taken on a significant share of activities related
to advertising, marketing, guidance, and/or entertainme nt in the Internet space, particularly on social
networks. These social media influencers (SMIs) have attracted large audiences by sharing their views
and opinions about their lives, experiences, brands, and products on social media platforms (Ju et al.,
2024, p. 1).
Now, with the advancement of virtual influencers, this type of marketing has experienced
significant growth on social media, with social media influencers at its core, sharing their views and
opinions about lifestyles, products, and personal stories (Audrezet et al., 2025).As non-human characters
such as virtual influencers (VIs), fictional characters, and avatars have become increasingly popular on
social media and are changing the dynamics of online interactions and marketing, understanding users’
interactions with non -human influencers is of great importance for both consumer researchers and
marketers (Ju et al., 2024, p. 21).
In academic studies, virtual influencers are referred to as “virtual counterparts of human
influencers” (Audrezet et al., 2025, p. 1), or digitally altered versions of real individuals, or digital
hybrids consisting of a head created with CGI and the body of a real person (Conti et al., 2022). Despite
their research importance, they have received less attention in research. Influencers are often known for
having expertise in specific topics and for creating educational, entertaining, or inspirational content that
provides value to consumers (Mouritzen et al., 2023). With the evolution of digital technology, virtual
influencers are becoming increasingly popular; however, they still largely face difficulties i n gaining
audience trust, as their levels of engagement are lower than those of human influencers, something that
may increase with the evolution of artificial intelligence (Hussain et al., 2026).
Among the advantages of these influencers compared with human influencers are greater
flexibility and adaptability to brand requirements, greater logistical control, and reduced public relations
risks (Audrezet et al., 2025). The emergence of virtual influencers marks the beginning of a new era in
marketing that integrates creativity with technology and redefines interactions between brands and
consumers. Understanding the impact of virtual influencers’ characteristics on audie nce responses and
engagement is highly important for fashion brands so that they can maximize social media engagement,
develop effective marketing strategies, and produce engaging content (Ju et al., 2024, p. 3).
Recent years have seen an explosion of studies on virtual influencers from a marketing,
communication, branding, and consumer psychology perspective (Hussain et al., 2026, P. 3) Despite the
studies conducted on consumer behavior, trust, and interactions in relation to virtual influencers, the
possibilities and challenges of adopting this new technology across different cultures and geographies
are another issue that needs to be ta ken into consideration. In this regard, the position and status of

virtual influencers on Afghan social networks is a particular subject for study that has not yet been
addressed. Afghanistan is a country where, despite security, political, and economic problems and the
prevalence of government censorship, people use the Internet and its services for cultural, social, and
commercial purposes. Although in August 2021, with the withdrawal of international forces and the
escape of the President, the government once again came under Taliban control, resulting in changes in
media and communications affair s (Mesbah, 2026). But despite economic problems and existence of
concerns, “currently, three new methods of marketing; LinkedIn, Google Ads, and artificial intelligence”
are being used in the country (Mirzaie, 2024).
The growth of some online marketing platforms such as “Azpag” and marketing on social
networks, including Instagram, TikTok, and Facebook, in traditional or animated forms, are among the
active marketing methods in the Afghan context. These platforms constitute part of the gradual changes
in the economic behavior of users in Afghanistan’s digital space, where, in some digital teams, girls play
roles in management, reviewing advertisements, and producing digital content (Sultani, 2026).
Nevertheless, at the end of 2025, 11.3 million people in Afghanistan were using the Internet, with an
Internet penetration rate of 25.5%; while there was a total o f 22.4 million active mobile phone
connections, with the difference being that some of these connections included only voice call and SMS
services.
The Taliban government’s action to utilize the Afghanistan Internet Exchange Center (NIXA)
project, which involves the installation of traffic monitoring systems and the management of Internet
network data, has raised concerns about strengthening the group’s surveillance and expanding Internet
censorship in Afghanistan (Rohani, 2026). However, given the rapid growth of artificial intelligence and
the formation of digital marketing, the possibilities for creating and developing virtual influencers in the
Internet space have still not emerged among activists in this field in Afghanistan. Therefore, the
important question that arises here is not a judgment about why these influencers do not exist or have
not been developed, but rather addressing the possibiliti es and challenges of creating and developing
virtual influencers in the Afghanistan context. In this regard, an effort is made to raise these challenges
by conducting in -depth interviews and posing semi -structured questions to journalists, marketing
professionals, and public relations specialists. This research is important in that, on the one hand, a new
scientific study is conducted in the field of marketing in Afghanistan’s virtual space and, on the other
hand, the possibilities and challenges of this industry are studied within the context this country. What
are the major cultural and communication challenges in the formation and development of virtual
influencers within the context of Afghanistan social networks? What contexts and possibilities can
create and strengthen the virtual influencer industry within the Internet context in Afghanistan?

The main objective of this study is to identify the most important cultural and communication
challenges involved in the creation and development of virtual influencers in Afghanistan. Undoubtedly,
this study will also identify the most important contexts and possibilities through discussions with
experts in this field.
Previous studies have primarily focused on human influencers in the digital environment, digital
literacy, consumer attitudes, and factors influencing online purchase intention.  Mirzaei (2025), in an
ethnographic study of Afghan migrant influencers on Instagram, identifies eight types of them. Most of
them are familiar with impression management techniques and, in order to attract followers, cross the
red lines of traditional Afghan culture and commodify and make their privacy public to a great extent.
They measure power and superiority by the number of followers and likes, and in their view, whoever
has attracted more followers possesses greater social capital and popularity. Hakimi et al. (2025) studied
the level of digital literacy among Afghans and found that there is a significant gap in higher-level digital
skills among young people. They also found that digital literacy is not uniformly distributed across
demographic groups based on age, gender, a nd geography. Osmani et al. (2022) showed that Afghan
women, when making decisions about online shopping, take four major antecedents into consideration:
perceived trust, perceived risk, perceived web quality, and electronic word -of-mouth. When these
factors provide benefits, they prefer online shopping over traditional shopping.
Haqmal et al. (2026), showed that perceived quality, perceived value and price, availability and
distribution, social influence, and consumer ethnocentrism have significant effects on consumer
awareness on east Afghanistan, whereas packaging and labeling, marketing and digital exposure, and
brand trust and reputation were not statistically significant.  Ghafourzay and Parıltı (2020) studied the
impact of social media marketing on customer inspiration and customer purchase intention in the
telecommunications sector of Afghanistan and found that social media marketing has a significant
positive impact on customer inspiration and customer purchase intention.
Shah and Jabarkhail (2022) examined the major factors affecting trust in online purchasing and
found that company reputation, service quality, security and privacy policy, and relationship
commitment predict online trust, which ultimately leads to repeat p urchase intention.  Hasnat et al.
(2023) identified the components that influence Afghan consumers’ attitudes toward advertising on
social media. They showed that information, entertainment, credibility, economy, and value corruption
are among the important components.
Overall, limited attention was paid to research on virtual influencers between 2016 and 2020;
however, after 2020, researchers increasingly examined this phenomenon in greater depth, particularly
in terms of authenticity and trust (Hussain et al., 2026). Looia and Kahlor (2024) examined the use of
positive and negative emotional sentiment in Instagram posts by human and virtual influencers. Their

findings showed that, compared with human influencers, virtual influencers were more likely to use
words with positive emotional sentiment in their Instagram posts.
This study, using a conceptual framework and a technology localization approach, examines the
contexts, challenges, and limitations of the formation and development of virtual influencers in
Afghanistan.   Localization is a process through which a technological or informational product is
adapted for use in a cultural or technical environment (Agbozo, 2022).  localization is typically defined
AS the linguistic, cultural and technical adaptation of digital content produced through modern ICTs so
that it can be suitably used by end-users in their own languages (Folaron, 2020, P. 209).
METHOD
This study has used a qualitative method and, through thematic analysis, stud ied the challenges
and opportunities of the formation of virtual influencers within the context of the Afghan Internet.
Thematic analysis (TA) is a method for identifying, analyzing and interpreting patterns of meaning
(‘themes’) within qualitative data  (Clarke & Braun, 2017). Through conducting this method, the most
important challenges and possibilities have been identified in the form of main and sub-themes through
discussions with experts in the field of marketing and public relations specialists. The participants in
this study were specialists in the fields of journalism, marketing, and public relations, who were
purposively selected based on their professional experience and higher education. The study has
included three public relations specialists, two journalists, and two specialists in the field of marketing,
as shown in Table 1. Among them, two were women and the other five were men. The semi-structured
questions were shared with the research participants through the WhatsApp social network, and their
responses were received in written form until reaching the stage of theoretical saturation. Then, the
researcher accurately and impartially studied the participants’ responses and, finall y, through coding
and the formation of main and sub -themes, prepared and arranged the table of themes  and a report
ultimately from their opinions.

RESULT AND DISCUSSIONS
The interviewees’ responses to the researcher’s questions were studied several times accurately
and impartially, and were then coded and consequently divided into main and sub-themes. Overall, the
interviewees’ responses to six major questions were divided into four main themes or axes. These four
major axes, as shown in Figure 2, were the challenges of creating virtual influencers in Afghanistan, the
contexts or opportunities for creating virtual i nfluencers in Afghanistan, the audiences of virtual
influencers in Afghanistan, and the issue of ethics in the Afghan virtual space. Table 2 shows that the
participants in this study considered the most important challenges to be technical challenges, the
absence or shortage of facilities, and/or censorship by the ruling system. As shown in Table 2, the most
important issues of concern identified as areas of activity for artificial intelligence influencers are
education, entertainment, and product marketing . Meanwhile, the issue of Afghan consumers or
audiences of these influencers was also identified as a theme. According to the participants, the majority
of the audiences in this country are illiterate or have low levels of literacy, and for this reason, they have
less trust in social media, while at the same time, they judge advertising content primarily from a cultural
and value -based perspective. The participants in this study also describe the issue of ethics and
governance in the virtual space as a very important topic.

The findings of this study define the creation and activities based on virtual influencers at the
intersection of four major themes or elements within the context of Afghan social networks: challenges,
contexts, audiences, and professional ethics. Accordin g to the interviewees of this study, marketing,
content production, and/or influencing the decisions of Afghan consumers in the digital space, above
all, require an in-depth pathology of the state of the audiences’ media literacy or knowledge, as well as
their trust in artificial intelligence. Overall, this finding is consistent with the study by Hakimi et al.
(2025), which emphasizes that digital literacy is not distributed according to age, gender, and geography.
The interviewee with code p6 says: “Creating content through virtual influencers on social
networks is not difficult, but gaining the trust of the audience, who views artificial intelligence with
skepticism and looks at it with a prior negative judgment, is an important issue. The Afghan consumer
accepts traditional commercial advertising more than online advertising or advertising based on artificial
intelligence. It takes time for people to become accustomed to such advertising.” Here, the findings of
Jabarkhail & Shah (2022) are indirectly taken into consideration; because in virtual influencers, the
consumer or audience not only pays attention to the service provider, but the identity of the content
produced is also placed under scrutiny.
Based on the findings of this research, verbal and non -verbal manners and communication in
content production and/or marketing should, in terms of cultural and social bias, be compatible with the
culture and values of Afghanistan; in particular, attention to the religious values of society is considered
a fundamental principle. The interviewee with code p3 says: “Many people see advertisements and

content that focus on sexual and religious issues. But they do not watch them with a mindset of
acceptance or approval. However, if we want to succeed, let us not forget continuity and value -
orientation.”
The findings of this research have identified the most important challenge in the formation of
virtual influencers as the absence of technical and service infrastructures. The interviewee with code p4
says: “Marketing is not simple, especially when you wan t to use artificial intelligence in the virtual
space. You need continuous and stable electricity. You need sufficient facilities and equipment. Even if
you do not have a standard sound recording device, you cannot produce a quality program. We do not
have 8 hours of stable electricity in 24 hours. For this reason, producing virtual content is difficult for
us. But despite that, we deal with it.”
On the other hand, the imposition of severe governmental restrictions on content production in
terms of religious and faith -related matters, censorship, as well as the harsh treatment of social media
activists by government officials, has created a kind of  fear and concern among influencers. Fear has
caused the virtual marketing industry in Afghanistan to remain a weak and marginalized industry.
Nevertheless, Afghanistan is a land that, from various dimensions, has the potential for producing
cultural and socio-political content in the virtual space. The findings of this research show that the most
important principle required in the agenda of inf luencers, whether virtual or human, is a focus on
education through various methods. The interviewee with code P1 says: “Due to being less biased than
human influencers, virtual influencers can take action in promoting and encouraging reading,
introducing cultural and educational works, and creating opportunities for education for girls deprived
of education. Today, the main discourse in Afghanistan is education, and this should be on the agenda
of influencers.”
Nevertheless, the participants in this study identify the most important concern and limitation as
the absence of ethical and legal frameworks for media activities in the virtual space. In their view, the
Afghan virtual space has now become a site of ethnic, linguistic, and regional conflicts. In addition, with
the rule of the Taliban, religious conflicts in this country have intensified; therefore, under such volatile
conditions, the possibility of neutrality is difficult, but deception and expediency are greater. The
interviewees with codes P4 and P5 believe that the Afghan virtual space is currently inflamed by
ethnocentrism and religious sectarianism. Virtual space influencers can gain greater and more long-term
importance if, instead of emphasizing polarization, they strive for empathy and convergence.

CONCLUSION
The conditions for the emergence of virtual influencers in Afghanistan remain limited and in need
of support due to the lack of technical infrastructure, the high cost of electricity and internet, as well as

Annusfy : Journal of Multidisciplinary Research| 19

poverty and unemployment in the country. Due to the lack of educational and academic platforms in the
field of digital marketing and studies of new technologies, there is still a considerable gap between social
networks and the emergence of virtual influen cers according to current global standards. However, in
the event of the formation and promotion of content production based on the functioning of social media
influencers and artificial intelligence, content production on education constitutes Afghanistan ’s most
important need. Although Afghanistan’s discursive space on social networks is influenced by ethnicity,
religion, and linguistic tensions, the priority of the people of Afghanistan is the promotion of the values
of dialogue, participation, education , and tolerance. With this approach, focusing on education,
particularly girls’ education, should be one of the most important agendas of virtual and human
influencers on social media. Although the level of media literacy among consumers of social networks
in Afghanistan is low, the issue of trust in artificial intelligence and new technology is also one of the
most important discussions in this country, and institutionalizing modern tools in traditional societies,
both because of technological bias and because of the lack of audience trust, is an important issue that
should be researched. Nevertheless, the factor behind the success of real or virtual influencers on social
networks is adherence to the values of professional ethics, maintaining neutrality, as well as respecting
privacy.
