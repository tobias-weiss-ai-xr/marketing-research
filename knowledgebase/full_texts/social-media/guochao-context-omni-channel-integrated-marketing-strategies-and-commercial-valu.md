Abstract. China's economic growth and rising incomes have reshaped consumption patterns.
Consumers now prioritize cultural meaning, emotional resonance, and social value in
products—fueling the "Guochao" (China-chic) trend, a core driver of domestic brand
growth. Guochao reflects cultural confidence translated into consumer behavior, pushing
brands to fuse traditional Chinese elements with modern design and lifestyles. Meanwhile,
internet technologies and mobile social media have overhauled brand-consumer
communication, replacing outdated one-way marketing with user-centric, omnichannel
strategies spanning online and offline spaces. This paper analyzes Mixue Bingcheng as a
typical Guochao case, using STP and 4P frameworks to examine its omnichannel strategy
across three dimensions: online-offline integration, social media content, and Guochao IP
development ("Xue Wang"/Snow King). Results show that centering its entire value chain
on this IP enabled Mixue to build a differentiated image of "affordable + cultural
resonance," achieving leapfrog growth in brand awareness and store network expansion. Yet
challenges remain: shallow cultural integration, repetitive content, and an underdeveloped
IP cultural narrative. In response, this paper proposes optimization recommendations
including deepening cultural connotation exploration, refining content stratification
strategies, and building integrated experiential frameworks. This study may serve as a
practical reference for Chinese catering brands engaging in Guochao marketing and enrich
relevant theoretical research.
Keywords: Guochao, Domestic Brands, Omni-Channel Integrated Marketing, Mixue
Bingcheng, Commercial Value
1. Introduction
In recent years, China's consumer market has exhibited a distinct "Guochao" phenomenon. An
increasing number of domestic brands have won the favor of young consumers through the creative
transformation and innovative development of traditional Chinese culture. Over 70% of young
consumers express a preference for domestic brands, believing that they embody cultural identity
and aesthetic individuality. Domestic brands are facing unprecedented opportunities for

development while simultaneously encountering intense homogeneous competition. How to stand
out among numerous brands and establish a unique brand image and emotional connection has
become a strategic imperative that every domestic brand must address.
Omni-channel integrated marketing offers an effective solution for brands to meet these
challenges. It emphasizes that brands should maintain a consistent brand image and coordinated
communication rhythm across all consumer touchpoints—whether online social media or offline
stores. It requires brands to possess robust content production capabilities and online-offline linkage
capabilities. Consumers' purchase decision paths have shifted from the traditional "Attention-
Interest-Desire-Memory-Action" model to "Interest-Search-Word-of-Mouth-Action-Share," and
brands must stimulate consumers' active participation at every stage.
With its unique brand positioning and marketing strategies, Mixue Bingcheng has become a
paradigm of omni-channel integrated marketing under the Guochao trend. Founded in 1997, Mixue
Bingcheng has grown into a beverage giant with over 25,000 stores worldwide. Its success is not
only attributable to its extremely cost-effective product strategy but also to its continuous innovation
in brand IPization, social media operations, and online-offline integration. However, has its Guochao
marketing truly achieved deep excavation of cultural connotations? As the brand expands, has its
marketing content become homogenized? Existing research provides limited systematic analysis on
this front. This paper takes Mixue Bingcheng as the research subject, sorts out its Guochao-oriented
omni-channel integrated marketing strategies, evaluates their effectiveness, identifies challenges,
and proposes optimization recommendations.
2. Theoretical foundations of Guochao and omni-channel integrated marketing
2.1. Definition of core concepts
Guochao is a consumer trend rooted in cultural identity, combining traditional Chinese cultural
elements with modern fashion design and contemporary lifestyles. It reflects the younger
generation's rediscovery and confident expression of their own cultural heritage. For domestic
brands, embracing Guochao means entering the realm of cultural value competition. Omni-channel
integrated marketing represents the latest development of integrated marketing in the digital age,
requiring the dismantling of barriers between online and offline, public and private domains, to
achieve seamless coverage across all consumer touchpoints. Its core asset is brand IP, its core driver
is quality content, and its core pathway is social viral dissemination.
2.2. Industry development trends under the Guochao consumption context
Currently, Guochao consumption has extended from apparel and cosmetics to diverse fields such as
catering and cultural tourism. According to iiMedia Research data, China's new-style tea beverage
market reached RMB 333.38 billion in 2023 and is expected to exceed RMB 400 billion by 2025.
Driven by Guochao, tea beverage brands are actively exploring traditional cultural elements, such as
product naming (e.g., "Misty Rain Oolong" and "Osmanthus Blossom"), packaging design (ink wash
painting and calligraphy), and spatial experiences (garden-style stores), giving rise to the "new-style
Chinese tea" segment. Meanwhile, social media has become the primary battleground for brand
competition, with "check-in" and "seeding" behaviors on platforms like Douyin and Xiaohongshu
directly influencing consumer decisions. Industry trends indicate that brand competition has shifted
from product taste and price to cultural connotations and emotional value, with digital full-link
marketing capabilities becoming the key to success.

2.3. Theoretical framework
This paper adopts STP theory and the 4P marketing theory as its core analytical framework. STP
theory includes market segmentation, target market selection, and market positioning, emphasizing
that enterprises should divide markets based on consumer demand differences, select the most
attractive segments, and establish unique brand positioning. The 4P theory encompasses Product,
Price, Place, and Promotion, representing a classic marketing mix tool. Applied to Mixue
Bingcheng: its market segmentation targets price-sensitive and fashion-conscious young students
and entry-level professionals, its target market selection focuses on third- and fourth-tier cities as
well as lower-tier segments within first- and second-tier cities, its market positioning establishes an
"affordable and approachable" image. Around this positioning, its products are primarily high-value
fruit teas and milk teas with prices mostly ranging from RMB 4 to 10, its channels build an
extensive "dense stores + mini-program + delivery platforms" network, its promotional activities
leverage the "Xue Wang" IP combined with Guochao festival-themed limited campaigns to achieve
brand rejuvenation. As scholars have noted, "Brand rejuvenation does not merely refer to innovative
communication measures in response to consumer youthfulness but rather seeks to find ways to
actively engage with target audiences" [1]. Currently, the tea beverage industry exhibits trends of
brand competition shifting toward cultural connotation transformation, with social media emerging
as the main battlefield.
3. Mixue Bingcheng's Guochao-oriented omni-channel integrated marketing practices
3.1. Brand development overview and motivations for Guochao transformation
Mixue Bingcheng started as a "Hanliu Shaved Ice" stall and entered the market with its "RMB 1 Ice
Cream," rapidly expanding through franchising. It had long been labeled by consumers as "cheap."
With intensifying competition, Mixue Bingcheng had to seek new points of differentiation. The rise
of Guochao came at an opportune time: its core customer base (young people aged 18–35)
constitutes the primary Guochao consumer group—CBNData data shows that 67% of this group is
willing to pay a premium for Guochao products, and tea beverages are among the categories with
the highest Guochao penetration rates, with "fruit tea consumption also highly concentrated in this
age group" [2]. This transformation was rooted in a profound insight into its own DNA and the
market environment.
3.2. Mixue Bingcheng's omni-channel integrated marketing practices
3.2.1. Online-offline channel integration
Mixue Bingcheng has built an online-offline integrated network of "10,000+ stores + mini-program
+ delivery platforms." Offline, it has launched Guochao-themed stores, such as the Postal Service
collaborative green-themed store and the Luoyang Hanfu-themed store, incorporating traditional
architectural elements and offering Chinese-style cultural creative merchandise, transforming
consumption spaces into check-in attractions and enhancing consumers' experiential engagement
with traditional Chinese culture. Online, it promotes Guochao promotional activities through its
mini-program and delivery platforms, directing traffic to offline locations.

3.2.2. Social media content dissemination
Mixue Bingcheng has established a communication matrix centered on Douyin, Weibo, Bilibili, and
Xiaohongshu, characterized by a distinct "personification" approach, engaging in frequent
interaction and meme-sharing with users. Its content strategy is driven by "emotionalization" and
"participatory engagement," encouraging user-generated content (UGC) [3]. In Guochao marketing,
Mixue Bingcheng launches festival-themed limited content, such as the "Jade Rabbit Xue Wang"
sticker series for the Mid-Autumn Festival, the "Xue Wang Dragon Boat Racing" short video for the
Dragon Boat Festival, and the "Guofeng Cosplay Challenge" encouraging users to decorate Hanfu
photos with Xue Wang stickers. This content retains the brand's entertainment DNA while subtly
incorporating traditional festival elements, bridging the cultural distance between the brand and its
users.
3.2.3. Xue Wang IP operations and cultural shaping
The core pillar of Mixue Bingcheng is the "Xue Wang" super IP. In 2018, in collaboration with Hua
& Hua, the new image was designed as a snowman wearing a crown and a red cape and holding an
ice cream scepter. "As a key strategy in the new consumption era, brand IPization aims to shape
brand images using IP thinking, endowing brands with personality" [4]. Xue Wang has been given a
complete persona, around which animated shorts, theme song MVs, and sticker sets have been
created. "Under the lens of meme theory, netizens, driven by their own emotional expression,
continuously integrate and remix memes with Xue Wang content to generate new cultural
meanings," and user co-creation has continuously iteratively evolved the Xue Wang IP [5]. In terms
of Guochao association, Mixue Bingcheng has dressed Xue Wang in Hanfu, transformed it into
"General Xue Wang" for participation in Guofeng markets, and collaborated with IPs such as the
Palace Museum Cultural Heritage. Users have also spontaneously created derivative works such as
"Xue Wang Time Travel," adding cultural symbolic meaning to the Xue Wang IP atop its
entertainment foundation, achieving continuous IP iteration.
4. Effectiveness and problem analysis of Mixue Bingcheng's omni-channel integrated
marketing
4.1. Brand communication effectiveness and market value growth
Mixue Bingcheng's omni-channel integrated marketing has achieved remarkable results. Brand
awareness has significantly increased, with the "Xue Wang" IP and theme song becoming deeply
ingrained cultural symbols. Wu Yaqing noted that brand entertainment communication in the new
media environment can achieve brand image rejuvenation by strengthening brand memory points
and enhancing consumer participation, Mixue Bingcheng, through its IP image, theme song, and
social media interactions, has shifted brand communication from one-way information delivery to
consumer participatory communication, thereby reinforcing brand cultural identity [6]. Compared
with premium brands, Mixue Bingcheng has successfully established a differentiated image of
"affordable but not cheap, approachable and humorous." In terms of market value, as of September
2024, its global store count exceeded 45,000, with 2023 revenue surpassing RMB 20 billion and net
profit growing over 30% year-on-year. Its total social media followers exceed 100 million, the
Douyin hashtag "Mixue Bingcheng" has garnered over 10 billion views, and the brand ranks among

4.2. Existing problems and challenges
First, there is insufficient depth in Guochao cultural integration. Marketing remains largely at the
level of borrowing visual symbols, lacking deep exploration of cultural connotations. "Marketing
strategies for internet-famous tea beverages must ensure the authenticity of promotional information
and avoid over-marketing" [7].
Second, social media content has become homogenized. The early "meme-play" style rapidly
attracted followers, but as numerous brands imitated this approach, users' aesthetic thresholds have
risen, and content has shown signs of formulaic patterning. "Current social media marketing by
new-style tea beverage brands suffers from shallow brand memory points and insufficient consumer
stickiness" [8].
Third, the brand IP image is disconnected from cultural construction. Xue Wang has strong
entertainment attributes but weak cultural connotation carrying capacity. "Brands need to truly
understand the connotations of traditional culture" [9]. Additionally, under the franchise model, food
safety incidents occasionally occur, posing threats to the brand image.
5. Optimization recommendations for Mixue Bingcheng's omni-channel integrated marketing
Deepen the Exploration of Guochao Cultural Connotations: From "Symbol Borrowing" to "Meaning
Co-Creation." Incorporate culturally storied elements into product R&D, such as 24-solar-term
limited-edition teas, collaborate with intangible cultural heritage inheritors to integrate traditional
craftsmanship elements into packaging and narrate their stories, produce documentaries telling
stories about raw material origins, endowing the Xue Wang IP with deeper social significance.
"Chayan Yuese adopts a direct-operation-only, no-franchise strategy, geographically limiting store
presence to preserve its unique characteristics"—Mixue Bingcheng can learn from its approach of
deeply cultivating local culture [10].
Optimize Social Media Content Strategies: Implement a "Layered Content" Approach. Retain
high-frequency, entertaining "fast content" while adding lower-frequency, high-quality "slow
content," such as Xue Wang IP animated series and user co-creation projects like "Xue Wang's
Travelogue." Improve negative word-of-mouth management online by promptly responding to
consumer complaints.
Build an Integrated Online-Offline Experience: Use mini-programs to record user preferences
and provide personalized recommendations through offline store check-in identification. Set up
"Xue Wang Check-in Points" to encourage photo sharing. Open themed flagship stores as experience
centers. Optimize queuing experiences by using pre-order systems to reduce waiting anxiety.
6. Conclusion
This paper examines Mixue Bingcheng's Guochao-driven omnichannel marketing, analyzing its
strategies, effectiveness, challenges, and optimization pathways. Key conclusions are as:
First, Mixue proves omnichannel integration works for mass-market domestic brands. By
Centering its ecosystem on the "Xue Wang" (Snow King) IP, it connects online-offline and public-
private channels, boosting brand momentum and user stickiness. Second, Mixue offers an
accessible, humorous take on Guochao—contrasting with Chayan Yuese's refined cultural aesthetics.

It blends traditional motifs with everyday levity and stress-relief themes, carving out a ditsinct
"afforable + relatable" identity. Yet overemphasizing entertainment risks diluting Guochao's core:
authentic cultural confidence. Third, its challenges—superficial cultural engagement, repetitive
content, and underdeveloped—are widespread among fast-growing domestic brands. This reflects a
"marketing-first, culture-lag" imbalance. Lasting success, not just virality, depends on aligning
marketing innovation with sustained cultural value creation.
This paper proposes three actionable recommendations: (1) shift from symbolic borrowing to co-
creating cultural meaning, (2) adopt a "fast+slow" content strategy, timely social posts paired with
deep, platform-specific narratives, and (3) design seamless online-offline experiences to strengthen
channel synergy. Limitations include reliance on public data and limited generalizability to premium
or non-foodservice brands. Future research should incorporate internal metrics and cross-brand
comparisons.
In summary, Mixue shows mass-market brands can build enduring equity through IP-led,
content-powered, social-first omnichannel strategies. Its experience underscores a vital truth:
Guochao is not a trend—it is a commitment. Only brands that embed genuine appreciation for
Chinese culture into their DNA will endure.
