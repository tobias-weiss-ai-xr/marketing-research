ABSTRACT
This study investigated the impact of social media marketing on the purchasing inten-
tions of young people in Durban, South Africa. The primary objective is to assess the
extent to which social media platforms affect consumer purchasing decisions, brand
preference, and loyalty, and to identify the factors that drive impulsive buying
behaviour. Employing a quantitative research approach with an exploratory design, data
were gathered from youth aged 18 and above who actively engage with social media.
Utilising a non-probability convenience sampling technique, the research targeted re-
spondents at two significant locations: the Workshop Market and the Early Morning
Market, resulting in a sample size of 120, which is suitable for descriptive statistical
analysis. Notably, the findings reveal that 85% of respondents spend over four hours
daily on social media, with 65% indicating that their purchasing decisions were influ-
enced by these platforms, thus affirming the considerable impact of social media on
consumer behaviour. Social networking sites emerged as the preferred marketing chan-
nels (90%), with entertainment cited as the primary reason for usage. Furthermore,
customer reviews proved to be the most significant factor in purchase decisions (35%),
followed closely by brand posts and influencer recommendations (25% each). Never-
theless, 55% of respondents expressed scepticism regarding influencer endorsements,
highlighting a demand for greater transparency. The study's implications suggest that
businesses should adopt authentic and engaging strategies to foster brand loyalty. Rec-
ommendations include leveraging credible influencers, promoting positive reviews, en-
suring secure payment systems, and providing educational content to encourage re-
sponsible consumption.

INTRODUCTION
The rapid evolution of digital technologies has transformed marketing dynamics glob-
ally, with social media emerging as a dominant force influencing consumer behaviour.
Platforms such as Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, and Twitter have redefined how busi-
nesses interact with consumers, offering real-time engagement, personalised advertis-
ing, and interactive features that strongly appeal to younger demographics. In Durban,
South Africa, youth constitute a significant portion of social media users, making them
a critical target for marketers seeking to enhance brand visibility and drive sales. Social
media marketing provides unparalleled opportunities for businesses to showcase prod-
ucts, share promotional content, and leverage influencer endorsements to shape con-
sumer perceptions. Unlike traditional advertising, which relies on one-way communica-
tion, social media fosters two-way interactions through likes, comments, and shares,
creating a sense of community and trust around brands. Research indicates that these
platforms influence purchasing decisions by amplifying peer recommendations, promot-
ing trends, and offering convenience through online shopping options. However, while
global studies affirm the effectiveness of social media marketing in driving consumer
engagement, there is limited empirical evidence on its impact within Durban’s unique
socio-economic and cultural context. This gap is significant because youth in Durban

LITERATURE REVIEW
The literature review examines both theoretical and empirical perspectives on social media marketing and its impact on
consumer behaviour, particularly among young people. It analyses key frameworks, such as Social Media Marketing The-
ory, Consumer Behavior Theory, and the Theory of Planned Behavior, to explain how digital platforms influence purchasing
choices. Previous research has highlighted the importance of personalised content, influencer endorsements, and interac-
tive engagement in fostering brand loyalty and encouraging impulsive purch ases. However, there are still gaps in under-
standing context-specific insights for Durban’s youth, where socio-economic and cultural factors may impact these dynam-
ics. This section combines existing research to build a foundation for the current study.
Theoretical Framework
This study is anchored in three interrelated theories: Social Media Marketing Theory, Consumer Behaviour Theory, and the
Theory of Planned Behaviour (TPB). Together, these frameworks provide a comprehensive understanding of how social
media marketing influences the purchasing behavior of young people in Durban. Social Media Marketing Theory emphasizes
the strategic utilization of digital platforms to create value, foster relationships, and shape consumer decisions. Rishi and
Bandyopadhyay (2018) assert that this theory transforms traditional one-way communication into interactive engagement,
enabling businesses to establish trust and loyalty through personalised content. Platforms such as Facebook, Instagram,
and TikTok allow brands to leverage the power of influencer endorsements, targeted advertisements, and user-generated
content to shape consumer perceptions and drive purchase intentions (Evans, 2012). This framework is particularly rele-
vant to the youth of Durban, who are highly active on social media and show a marked responsiveness to visually engaging
and trend-oriented content (Pandey, Sahu & Dash, 2018).
Consumer Behaviour Theory examines the psychological, social, and cultural factors that influence purchasing decisions.
Schiffman, Kanuk, and Kumar (2010) describe consumer behaviour as the intricate process individuals undergo when
selecting, purchasing, and evaluating products to satisfy their needs. Young consumers, in particular, are susceptible to
peer recommendations, socia l proof, and aspirational content prevalent on social media platforms (Ntobaki, Tlapana &
Matli, 2022). This theory identifies key determinants such as motivation, perception, and attitude, which are significantly
shaped by exposure to online influencer endorsements and promotional content. Impulsive buying behaviour, a recurring
theme in this study, can often be triggered by emotional appeals and the fear of missing out—both of which are heightened
by the immediacy and interactivity of social media (Karim et al., 2021). The Theory of Planned Behaviour (TPB), proposed
by Ajzen (1991), further enriches this analysis by demonstrating how attitudes, subjective norms, and perceived behav-
ioural control influence consumer intentions and actions. TPB suggests that individual purchasing decisions are affected
by their attitudes towards the behaviour (e.g., favourable perceptions of social media advertisements), the social pressures
from peers and current trends, and the perceived ease of engaging in the behaviour (e. g., the convenience of online
shopping). In the case of Durban’s youth, social media marketing cultivates positive attitudes through visually appealing
content, reinforces subjective norms through influencer endorsements, and enhances perceived control by facilitating
seamless purchasing experiences.

Social Media Marketing as a Strategic Tool
Social media marketing (SMM) has become a crucial element in shaping consumer behaviour, particularly among young
individuals who actively engage on platforms like Facebook, Instagram, and TikTo k. Scholars widely acknowledge that
social media serves a dual purpose, namely, it acts as both a promotional tool and a communication channel that encour-
ages interactive engagement between brands and consumers (Tlapana, Ntobaki, & Hawkins-Mofokeng, 2022; Karundeng,
Suyanto, & Yakup, 2025). This duality permits brands to not only showcase their products but also share supplier details
and establish emotional connections with their audiences through personalised content and influencer collaborations (Dol-
larhide, 2021). The active engagement of youths on these social media platforms offers businesses the prospect to signif-
icantly influence purchasing decisions by capitalizing on emerging trends and leveraging peer-driven endorsements (Ban-
dyopadhyay, 2025). Nonetheless, the effectiveness of SMM is often hampered by infrastructural challenges, such as un-
stable internet connectivity and specific limitations imposed by different platforms (Digital Marketing Institute, 2021).
These challenges signify missed opportun ities for businesses, underscoring the need for adaptive strategies that aim to
maintain consistent engagement across diverse digital environments. The strategic implication is unambiguous: companies
must prioritise both the selection of platforms and the optimization of content to align with the preferences of young
people, thereby overcoming technological obstacles.
Empirical evidence highlights the significant impact of SMM on the purchasing behaviour of young consumers. Research
indicates that key drive rs of purchase intentions include the relevance, personalization, and interactivity of marketing
content. Furthermore, strategies such as influencer marketing and user-generated content help enhance trust and authen-
ticity among consumers (Ismael, 2025; Aslam, Rashid, & Chaudhary, 2021). Young consumers exhibit a strong inclination
towards visually appealing and trend -oriented content, which often results in impulsive buying behaviours, sometimes
exacerbated by a fear of missing out (Karim et al., 2021). Not ably, there are gender and age variances that further
complicate this dynamic; for example, younger demographics (ages 18 -21) and female consumers tend to be more sus-
ceptible to social media influence than their older counterparts (Ismael, 2025). Despite t his body of knowledge, many
businesses resort to generic marketing strategies that fail to account for these demographic intricacies, resulting in de-
creased effectiveness of their campaigns. Additionally, an over -dependence on social media without acknowle dging risks
such as information overload and rising consumer scepticism has the potential to undermine trust and brand loyalty (Digital
Marketing Institute, 2021). A notable gap in the scholarly literature is the scarcity of context -specific research examining
the influence of SMM in Durban, which reflects a strategic oversight for marketers seeking to craft culturally relevant
campaigns that resonate with local youth.

Social Media Marketing and Youth Purchasing Behavior
Social media platforms have fundamentally transformed the consumer behaviour of youth by providing convenient access
to products without necessitating visits to physical stores (Huang et al., 2021). This shift empowers consumers to compare
prices, read reviews, and make informed purchasing decisions, thereby establishing SMM as a trusted and influential tool
(Jabeen et al., 2024). However, perceived affordability can often be undermined by hidden costs, such as shipping fees,
which can complicate the online purchasin g experience more than what is publicly advertised (Zhao, 2021). While SMM
enhances convenience and connectivity, issues of trust remain prevalent. Rapid technological advancements can exacer-
bate knowledge gaps, leading some consumers to feel uncertain about navigating digital platforms. Moreover, the rise of
online scams accentuates the tension between convenience and security, posing a challenge for marketers to effectively
balance engagement with consumer protection. These areas of concern highlight miss ed opportunities for businesses to
implement transparent pricing strategies and robust security measures that build trust and foster customer loyalty. Con-
sequently, it is imperative that businesses prioritise consumer education and secure transaction syste ms to mitigate risks
and bolster confidence in social media commerce.
Empirical evidence suggests that SMM has a significant influence on the purchasing behaviour of youth through tailored
content, interactive features, and facilitation of global connectiv ity (Malik, Asif & Wali, 2016; Ali & Budyastomo, 2021).
Research indicates that the majority of buying decisions are influenced by social media, largely driven by user interactions
such as sharing and messaging that transcend geographical boundaries (Ali &  Budyastomo, 2021; Efendioğlu, 2024).
These dynamic positions social media as a compelling marketing channel for expanding market access and fostering direct
communication between consumers and brands. Nevertheless, the continuous updates of these platforms, while sustaining
user engagement, also pose challenges such as information overload and decision fatigue, contrasting sharply with the
stability inherent in traditional marketing methods. The rapid expansion of online marketplaces further accelerates th e
shift towards digital purchasing (Muurnen, 2024), yet concerns regarding authenticity and reliability persist. These findings
underscore a strategic imperative: businesses must adopt adaptive content strategies, prioritise transparency, and leverage
influencer credibility to mitigate scepticism and enhance trust. Failure to address these challenges risks alienating consum-
ers and diminishing the effectiveness of social media marketing campaigns.
Synthesizing these insights reveals that SMM functions not me rely as a promotional tool but as a behavioural driver that
shapes patterns of youth consumption through convenience, personalization, and social proof. Despite global evidence
supporting SMM's impact, a notable lack of empirical research contextualised within Durban's socio-economic environment
exists, resulting in a critical knowledge gap. This absence of localised insights represents a missed opportunity for busi-
nesses to develop culturally relevant strategies that resonate with the youth of Durban. Immediate actions should include
the implementation of secure payment systems, the curation of authentic content, and the establishment of educational
campaigns to promote responsible consumption. By addressing these gaps, businesses can convert social media engage-
ment into sustainable brand loyalty and a competitive advantage within a rapidly evolving digital marketplace. The urgency
of this research lies in its capacity to bridge global trends with local realities, providing actionable insights for markete rs
and policymakers alike who seek to responsibly harness the transformative power of social media.

METHODS
This study employed a quantitative research approach, supported by an exploratory research design, to investigate t he
impact of social media marketing on the buying behaviour of youth consumers in Durban. Quantitative methods were
chosen for their ability to generate measurable data that can be statistically analysed, ensuring objectivity and reliability
(Albers, 2017). The exploratory design was appropriate because the phenomenon of social media’s influence on youth
purchasing decisions remains under-researched in the Durban context. Exploratory research facilitates the identification of
patterns and relationships, providing foundational insights for future studies (Thomas & Lawal, 2020). The target popula-
tion comprised youth aged 18 years and above residing in Durban who actively use social media platforms. This demo-
graphic was selected due to its high engagement with digital platforms and susceptibility to influencer -driven marketing
strategies (Willie, 2023). A non -probability convenience sampling technique was employed, allowing the researchers to
select participants who were easily accessible at two major locations: the Workshop Market and the Early Morning Market.
The study aimed for a sample size of 120 respondents, which is considered adequate for generating reliable descriptive
statistics and identifying trends in consumer behaviour. A larger sample enhances representativeness and reduces sampling
error, addressing limitations observed in prior studies with smaller samples (Albers, 2017). The sample size was determined
based on resource availability and the need to strike a balance between feasibility and statistical rigour.
Data collection relied on a structured questionnaire developed in alignment with the research objectives and problem
statement. The instrument included closed-ended questions, dichotomous items, multiple-choice options, and Likert-scale
statements to captu re respondents’ attitudes toward social media marketing, purchasing behaviour, and brand loyalty.
This design ensured clarity, minimised ambiguity, and facilitated quantitative analysis. Questions were organised themat-
ically to maintain logical flow and reduce respondent fatigue. Pre-testing was conducted to confirm clarity and relevance,
enhancing content validity (Heale & Twycross, 2015). Questionnaires were distributed manually to respondents at desig-
nated locations, ensuring direct engagement and high response rates. Completed questionnaires were reviewed for com-
pleteness before analysis. Data were processed using Microsoft Excel, which enabled the generation of frequency distri-
butions, percentages, and graphical representations such as bar charts and pie charts. These tools provided visual insights
into patterns of social media usage, purchasing motivators, and trust in influencer recommendations. The quantitative
approach enabled the systematic interpretation of numerical data, thereby supporting evidence-based conclusions (Sheard,
2018). Ensuring validity and reliability was central to the research design. Content validity was achieved by aligning ques-
tionnaire items with constructs identified in the literature on social media marketing and consumer behaviour. Face validity
was established through pilot testing with a subset of the target population, confirming that the instrument appeared
appropriate for measuring intended variables. Reliability was enhanced by using standardised questions and consisten t
administration procedures, which reduced measurement error and ensured stability across responses (Raharjanti et al.,
2022). The study adhered to strict ethical standards to protect participant rights and maintain research integrity. Respond-
ents were informed about the purpose of the study and assured of confidentiality and anonymity. Participation was volun-
tary, and informed consent was obtained prior to data collection. Participants were granted the right to withdraw at any
stage without penalty. Data were securely stored and used exclusively for academic purposes, complying with institutional
ethical guidelines (Braun et al., 2021).

The research findings reveal that social media marketing has a significant impact on the purchasing behaviour of youth in
Durban. An impressive 85% of respondents reported spending over four hours daily on platforms like Facebook, Instagram,
and TikTok, with 65% stating that their purchasing decisions are influenced by social media. The most preferred marketing
channels were social networking sites (90%), while entertainment was the primary reason for using these platforms,
highlighting the necessity for engaging and visually appealing content. Customer reviews emerged as the most influential
factor in purchase decisions, accounting for 35%, followed closely by brand posts and influencer recommendations, which
each garnered 25%. Nonetheless, 55% of respondents expressed scepticism towards influencer endorsements, indicating
a need for transparency. Price was identified as the leading purchase motivator, followed by quality and trendiness. Addi-
tionally, 70% of respondents sought reviews before making purchases, emphasizing the importance of trust in marketing
strategies. Despite high engagement levels, 40% had never made a purchase via social media, suggesting significant
untapped potential for conversion. These findings suggest that businesses should prioritise authentic content, utilise cred-
ible influencers, and develop affordability-driven campaigns to enhance trust and engagement, ultimately converting social
media interactions into lasting brand loyalty and a competitive edge in the rapidly evolving digital marketplace.
DISCUSSION
These results align with findings from three studies that collectively underscore the transformative influence of social media
on consumer behaviour in South Africa. Msosa et al. (2025) assert that social media marketing influences  purchasing
decisions by providing credible information, promoting engagement, and establishing trust through ads and reviews, while
also highlighting ethical concerns related to data privacy. Lutchman (2022) highlights that personalised social media ad-
vertising effectively drives online clothing purchase intentions, particularly through Instagram, which stands out for its
visual appeal and relevance. Meanwhile, Mlambo et al. (2025) find that university students' followership of influencers is
largely driven by entertainment, inspiration, and identity formation, with a notable preference for entertainment and life-
style influencers. While all studies emphasise engagement and authenticity as vital drivers, they differ in focus: Msosa et
al. highlight the necessity of ethical content strategies, Lutchman advocates for agile personalization, and Mlambo empha-
sises the psychosocial impact of influencers. Together, they suggest that businesses should integrate personalization,
transparency, and influencer partnerships to enhance consumer trust and loyalty.
In summary, the results of the present study and the three referenced studies align strongly in their emphasis on the
importance of engagement and authenticity in social media marketing. Each study acknowledges the significant influence
of social media on shaping purchasing behaviour, particularly among young people, while also highlighting the importance
of trust and ethical considerations in marketing strategies. This consistency highlights the crucial need for bu sinesses to
adopt comprehensive approaches that prioritise credible content, personalised advertising, and transparency to foster
enduring consumer relationships in the digital era.

CONCLUSIONS
Firstly, businesses should prioritise the development of authe ntic and engaging content on social media platforms, such
as Facebook, Instagram, and TikTok, which have been proven to significantly influence the purchasing decisions of young
consumers (RQ1). The study indicated that 65% of respondents made purchases mo tivated by social media, and 85%
reported spending over four hours daily on these platforms, thereby highlighting their substantial impact on buying be-
haviour. To leverage this influence, marketers should create visually appealing and interactive content that resonates with
users' entertainment-driven usage patterns, as entertainment remains the primary reason for social media engagement.
Incorporating collaborations with influencers and trend -based campaigns will help brands remain relevant and capture
attention in this highly active digital space.
Secondly, companies must enhance trust and brand loyalty through transparency and credibility, addressing RQ2 regarding
the influence of social media marketing on brand preference and loyalty. Findings revealed that customer reviews played
a significant role in influencing 35% of purchase decisions, with 70% of respondents actively seeking reviews before
making a purchase. However, a significant level of scepticism towards influencers remains, with 55% expressing distrust.
Consequently, businesses should prioritise the promotion of authentic user-generated content, positive reviews, and ethical
influencer partnerships to build trust. Ensuring clear disclosure of sponsored content and maintaining consistent engage-
ment with consumers will bolster credibility, ensuring that social media marketing cultivates long-term loyalty rather than
short-term gains.
Finally, marketers should aim to mitigate impulsive buying behaviour by advocating for affordability and responsible  con-
sumption, directly addressing RQ3, which examines factors contributing to impulsive purchases. The study found that price
was the foremost motivator, followed by quality and trendiness, while impulsive buying was often linked to fear of missing
out and entertainment-driven engagement. To counteract these tendencies, businesses should design campaigns that
emphasise affordability and integrate educational content that promotes financial responsibility. Furthermore, secure pay-
ment systems and personalised  advertising tailored to consumer preferences will enhance trust and reduce impulsive
spending, thus converting high levels of engagement into sustainable purchasing patterns and lasting brand loyalty.
Directions for Future Research
Future research should broaden the scope of this study by incorporating larger and more diverse samples from multiple
South African cities to enhance generalisability. Comparative analyses between urban and rural youth could yield insights
into how socio-economic and infrastructural disparities influence the effectiveness of social media marketing. Moreover,
employing mixed method approaches that combine quantitative surveys with qualitative interviews would allow for a
deeper exploration of the psychological and cultural factors  driving purchase intentions. Investigating platform -specific
impacts is also essential, as this study identifies social networking sites as predominant, while emerging platforms like
TikTok and Instagram may uniquely affect impulsive buying and brand loya lty. Additionally, researchers should consider
the long-term implications of social media marketing on consumer behaviour, notably in relation to sustainability and
ethical consumption patterns, thereby aligning with global objectives such as Sustainable Development Goal 12.
