Abstract: This study examines the formation of br and commitment through digital
experiences, focusing on the roles of perceived technical interactivity and social media content
among users of Online Travel Agent (OTA) applications for hotel bookings in Indonesia. The
rapid digitalization of the tourism s ector has positioned OTA platforms as primary channels
for hotel reservations, yet retaining users remains challe nging due to low switching costs and
intense competition. This research integrates Stimulus-Organism-Response (SOR) Theory and
Relationship Marketing Theory (RMT) to explain how perceived technical interactivity and
social media content are related to customer satisfaction and brand attachment, and how these
two organism states are related to brand commitment. A quantitative cross-sectional survey of
OTA hotel -booking users was conducted, and the data were analyzed using Partial Least
Squares Structural Equ ation Modeling (PLS -SEM). All six hypothesized direct relationships
were supported: perceived technical interactivity and social media content were positively
associated with customer satisfaction and brand attachment, which were in turn positively
associated with brand commitment. Customer satisfaction showed the largest direct path
coefficient to brand commitment (β = 0.457), followed by brand attachment (β = 0.306). The
model tested direct relationships only; indirect (mediating) effects were not tested,  and the
cross-sectional design supports associations rather than causal conclusions. The study
contributes to di gital marketing literature by providing an integrated test of stimulus –
organism–response relationships in the OTA hotel -booking context and off ers practical
implications for OTA companies in designing digital experiences and social media strategies
that foster long-term customer relationships.

Keywords: Brand attachment, brand commitment, customer satisfaction, online travel agent,
perceived technical interactivity, social media content.

commitment, and does not automatically guarantee the f ormation of long -term relationships

platform. The digital -based nature of the OTA industry means consumers have many

Although research on technological interactivity, social me dia marketing, customer
satisfaction, brand attachment, and brand commitment continues to develop, several research
gaps require attention. First, previous studies have established that perceived te chnical
interactivity is associated with its impact on use r satisfaction, purchase intention, flow
experience, or usage behavior in various digital contexts such as e-commerce, virtual shopping,
and metaverse (Sulam & Fasa, 2025) . What remains unresolved  is whether, in OTA hotel -

because the existing evidence comes from other digital contexts and other outcomes. This study
adds evidence on both relationships (H1 and H2) within a single OTA model. Second, previous
research has established that social media marketing activities are associated with brand equity,
brand trust, engagement, purchase intention, and loyalty (Al Amin et al., 2026; Vo Minh et al.,

customer satisfaction and engagement, what remains unresolved is how social media conten t
is related to customer satisfaction and brand attachment  at the same time in OTA application
contexts, where this has not been extensively studied. This study adds evidence on both
relationships (H3 and H4). Third, previous research has generally placed customer satisfaction
as the final outcome leading directl y to loyalty or reuse intention. However, recent research
shows that these relationships are not always direct because customer satisfaction must first
develop into commitment before producing loyal  behavior (Sumiyana & Komariyah, 2024) .
What remains unr esolved is how a cognitive -evaluative state (customer satisfaction) and an
affective state (brand attachment) are each related to brand commitment when both are
examined in the same model. This stud y adds an estimate of both relationships (H5 and H6)
and a descriptive comparison of their magnitudes. Fourth, previous research has established
brand attachment mainly in food delivery, mobile commerce, retail application, and restaurant
table booking application domains. What remains unresolved is whether these findings hold in
the OTA hotel-booking context, where purchase decisions have different involvement levels
and risks compared to other digital service categories. This study adds empirical evidence  on
brand attachment and its antecedents in this specific context.

The main theoretical foundation used to explain the mechanism of variable influence in
this research is the Stimulus-Organism-Response (SOR) Theory, integrated with Relationship
Marketing Theory (RMT). SOR Theory, first developed in environmental psychology, states
that external stimuli from the environment can influence individuals' internal emo tional and
cognitive states, which subsequently drive certain behavioral responses (Mehrabian & Russell,
1974). In its development in the digital era, the SOR paradigm has been widely adopted to
analyze how technical features, application interfaces, and  information quality on digital
platforms influence psychological evaluations and final user decisions (Ali et al., 2018; Liu et
al., 2022). The interactive characteristics of technology and atmospheric elements in a travel
application have been shown to function as strong stimuli that stimulate emotional engagement
and perceived usefulness in consumers (Camilleri et al., 2023; Sengupta & Cao, 2022).
 In this research's conceptual framework, Perceived Technical Interactivity and Social
Media Content are positioned as Stimulus (S). These two elements represent technological and
information stimuli faced by users when interacting with OTA applications (Ali et al., 2018).
These stimuli are then processed by users as Organism (O), triggering internal responses in the
form of cognitive evaluation represented by Customer Satisfaction, and deep emotional bonds
represented by Brand Attachment (Sengupta & Cao, 2022; Ye et al., 2026) . This organism's
internal evaluation subsequently produces Response (R) in the form of Brand Commitment, a
psychological commitment and user willingness to maintain long -term relationships with the
OTA application (Ye et al., 2026).
Customer Satisfaction and Brand Attachment are both classified as Organism states
because each is an internal state that forms after exposure to the stimuli  and before the
behavioral response, but they differ in nature. Customer Satisfaction is primarily a cognitive -
evaluative state: a judgment of whether the interaction with the application and the information
received met the user's expectations. Brand Atta chment is primarily an affective state: an
emotional bond and psychological closeness to the brand. Brand Commitment is classified as
the Response because it is the relationship -oriented outcome of these internal states, namely
the user's dedication and willingness to maintain the relationship with the OTA, rather than an
evaluation or feeling in itself. Although satisfaction and attachment are conceptually distinct,
they are expected to be correlated, which is why discriminant validity between them is assessed
in the Results.
To strengthen understanding of how these user internal responses transform into solid
loyalty, this research uses Relationship Marketing Theory (RMT) as a supporting theory. RMT
marks a marketing focus shift from short -term transaction -based marketing to the creation,
maintenance, and enhancement of strong, mutually beneficial relational relationships with
customers over time (Gilaninia et al., 2011). The core of relational marketing is articulated in
Commitment-Trust Theory, which confirms that relationship commitment and trust are crucial
mediating variables determining the success and sustainability of relational exchanges between
companies and consumers (Morgan & Hunt, 1994 ). Relational marketing emphasizes the
importance of customer retention because the cost of maintaining existing relationships is far
more efficient than attracting new customers in competitive market structures (Gilaninia et al.,
2011; Payne & Frow, 201 7). The application of RMT in digital platform contexts s uch as e-
travel agencies becomes increasingly relevant given intense competition due to low consumer
switching costs (Nusair et al., 2011). In this online domain, brand commitment does not appear
spontaneously but is developed through consistently percei ved satisfaction and emotional
bonds built during interaction processes (Nusair et al., 2011) . In the modern hospitality and
tourism industry, managing relationship marketing dimensions such as co mmitment and
conflict handling proves to be a primary dete rminant in strengthening customer engagement

Drawing from the growth of tourism sector digitalization in Indonesia, the increasing use
of Online Travel Agent applications as hotel booking means, and challenges in building long-
term user relationships amid high platform switching ease, this research is directed to
understand how digital experiences can shape Brand Commitment. Specifically examining
how Perceived Technical Interactivity and Social Medi a Content are related to Customer
Satisfaction and Brand A ttachment, and how these two constructs are related to Brand
Commitment among Indonesian OTA hotel booking application users, this research positions
Customer Satisfaction and Brand Attachment as or ganism states (cognitive -evaluative and
affective responses, respectively) that follow the digital stimuli from OTA platforms and are
hypothesized to be associated with brand commitment. The model is tested through six direct
hypothesized relationships; in direct (mediating) effects are not part of the objectives or
hypotheses of this study. Through this approach, the research is expected to provide theoretical
contributions to digital consumer behavior and brand relationship literature, while generating
practical implications for OTA managers in designing digital experiences capable of
strengthening user loyalty and commitment sustainably. Based on the research background, the
following research objectives are formulated: (1) to examine the relationship betw een
Perceived Technical Interactivity and Brand Attachment among OTA application users; (2) to
examine the relationship between Perceived Technical Interactivity and Customer Satisfaction
among OTA application users; (3) to examine the relationship between  Social Media Content
and Brand Attachment among OTA application users; (4) to examine the relationship between
Social Media Content and Customer Satisfaction among OTA application users; (5) to examine
the relationship between Brand Attachment and Brand C ommitment; and (6) to examine the
relationship between Customer Satisfaction and Brand Commitment.
Perceived technical interactivity refers to the extent to which users perceive control,
system responsiveness, and two-way communication in a digital platform. This concept reflects
individuals' perceptions of the s ystem's ability to provide real -time feedback, offer flexible
navigation, and enable personalization of user experiences (Yang & Shi, 2025) . Technical
interactivity is viewed as an important attribute in application-based environments as it shapes
perceptions of technology quality and dynamic usage experiences (Lee et al., 2021) . Beyond
functional aspects, perceived technical interactivity also creates personal and immersive
experiences. Interactions that allow users to actively engage in service explor ation processes
contribute to psychological closeness formation with the application brand. Technology-based
experience quality influences how consumers internalize brands in their cognitive and affective
structures (Malik & Depi, 2025). Responsive and participatory digital experiences strengthen
perceptions that the brand is dynamically present in meeting user needs. Interaction intensity
with the system increases opportunities for emotional relat ionship formation with the brand.
Empirical evidence shows  that interactive features in digital platforms are associated with
consumer affective bond formation with brands (Sanz-Blas et al., 2026) . Therefore, the first
hypothesis is proposed:
H1: Perceived Technical Interactivity positively influences Brand Attachment.

Furthermore, perceived technical interactivity reflects user perceptions of the system's
ability to provide quick responses, navigation control, and adaptive interaction experiences.
Responsive systems enabling active user participation create mor e meaningful usage
experiences aligned with individual expectations. Technical interaction quality in digital
applications is viewed as a determinant of positive evaluations of technology -based service
experiences (Ibrahim et al., 2025). Interactive application environments allow users to feel in

quality and user satisfaction has been supported in digital service and application -based
platform contexts (Lin et al., 2025). Thus, the second hypothesis is:
H2: Perceived Technical Interactivity positively influences Customer Satisfaction.

Social media content describes the form, quality, and characteristics of messages
conveyed by brands through social media platforms. This content can take the form of product
information, entertainment, educa tion, and interactive content designed to capture audience
attention and build meaningful digital experiences (Appel et al., 2020) . Social media content
quality is often measured through relevance aspects, mess age consistency, visual appeal, and
perceived informative value (Iswanto et al., 2025). Informative, authentic, and engaging social
media content can build symbolic experiences that strengthen consumer emotional closeness
to brands. Relevant content creates perceptions that the brand understands audience needs and
values. Exposure to consistent and meaningful content contributes to affective relationship
formation between consumers and brands (Atmaja & Wibowo, 2025). Consumer engagement
with social medi a content enables internalization of brand m eanings in individual identity.
Visual and narrative representations in digital content form deep emotional associations.
Research shows that marketing activities through social media play a role in building emotional
attachment to brands (AlFraihat et al., 2025). Therefore, the third hypothesis is:
H3: Social Media Content positively influences Brand Attachment.

Quality social media content characteristics provide clear, transparent, and easily
understood info rmation to consumers. Content that facilitates decision -making processes
enhances perceptions of benefit and convenience in service usage. Presentation of relevant and
valuable content shapes positive evaluations of overall brand experiences (Hollebeek &
Macky, 2019). Consumer interaction with social media content also contributes to perceptions
that the brand is responsive to audience needs. Exposure to educational and persuasive content
increases trust in the brand and strengthens experience evaluations. The effectiveness of digital
marketing activities through social media has been linked to increased consumer satisfaction
(Al Amin et al., 2026). Hence, the fourth hypothesis is:
H4: Social Media Content positively influences Customer Satisfaction.

Brand attachment reflects strong emotional closeness between consumers and brands,
forming the basis of long-term relationships. Deep affective bonds increase individuals' desire
to maintain relationships with specific brands. Strong emotional relationships play a role in
driving brand commitment in various consumption contexts (Ghorbanzadeh & Rahehagh,
2021). High attachment to brands strengthens relationship sustainability orientation and
reduces tendencies to switch to alternatives. Stable psychological bonds become a foundation
for consistent brand commitment formation. Empirical findings show that attachment is an
important determinant in brand commitment f ormation (Choi et al., 2024) . Thus, the fifth
hypothesis is:
H5: Brand Attachment positively influences Brand Commitment.

Customer satisfaction reflects positive evaluations of consumption experiences meeting
user expectations. High satisfaction levels  strengthen perceptions of value and relationship
quality between consumers and brands. Consistent experience evaluations form tendencies to
maintain relationships with brands in the long term (Mbango, 2018). Satisfaction serves as a
rational and emotion al foundation for building stable relationships with brands. Satisfied

H6: Customer Satisfaction positively influences Brand Commitment.

METHODS
This research employs a quantitative approach with explanatory research design aimed
at examining hypothesized relationships among variables based on formulated hypotheses. The
quantitative approach is selected because this research focuses on variable measurement using
structured questionnaire instruments and statistical data analysis to test the proposed conceptual
model. This research examines the hypothesized relationships of Perceived Technical
Interactivity and Social Media Content with Customer Satisfaction and Brand Attachment, and
of these two constructs with Brand Commitment. Thus, this research is classified as
explanatory research aimed at testing theoretically derived relationships among latent
constructs based on theory and previous empirical f indings (Hair et al., 2022) . The research
design employs a survey method with cross -sectional approach, where data collection is
conducted once within a specific period from respondents who have used Online Travel Agent
applications for hotel bookings. B ecause the design is cross -sectional and observational, the
results are interpreted as statistical associations consistent with the hypothesized relationships,
and causal inference is not claimed. Data are analyzed using Partial Least Squares Structural
Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) because the research model involves several latent constructs,
simultaneous structural relationships, and is oriented toward prediction and theory
development. According to Hair et al. (2022), PLS-SEM is an appropriate method for analyzing
complex research models, testing relationships among constructs simultaneously, and
providing reliable estimates even when data do not fully meet normal distribution assumptions.
This research utilizes two data sources: primary and secondary da ta. Primary data are
obtained directly from respondents through online questionnaire distribution to Online Travel
Agent application users who have used the application for hotel bookings. The collected data
measure respondent perceptions of Perceived Tech nical Interactivity, Social Media Content,
Customer Satisfaction, Brand Attachment, and Brand Commitment variables to answer
research objectives and test hypotheses. Secondary data are obtained through literature review
including reference books, scientific articles, national and international journals, proceedings,
and other relevant publications. Additionally, the researcher utilizes official websites and
credible online sources to obtain supporting data regarding OTA industry development,
consumer behavior, and concepts related to research variables. All secondary data serve as

The data collection technique employs a survey method with quest ionnaire instruments

respondents not meeting rese arch criteria to guarantee data quality for analysis (Hair et al.,

The population in this research is all OTA application users in Indonesia who have made
hotel bookings through digital platforms. Population is defined as the entire group of

research are individuals who have used OTA applications for hotel bookings, have experience
interacting with digital features on the se platforms, and have been exposed to social media
content related to the OTA platforms they use.

and abnormally fast responses, leaving 536 valid questionnaires (82.3% of those collected) for
the final analysis. The final sample of 536 respondents is well above the minimum required by
the power-based guidance and satisfies the sample-size requirement for the PLS-SEM model.
The sampling technique employed is purposive sampling, determining samples based on
specific criteria relevant to research objectives (Sekaran & Bougie, 2016). Purposive sampling
is chosen because this research requires respondents with specific experience and knowledge
regarding OTA application usage for hotel bookings, so not all individuals in the population
can be appropriate respondents (Malhotra, 2010) . Sample selec tion criteria include: (1)
respondents are active OTA application users who have made hotel bookings, (2) respondents
have experience interacting with interactive features on OTA applications, and (3) respondents
are exposed to social media content related  to OTA platforms. Purposive sampling usage is
also based on time and cost efficiency considerations, as well as the ability to reach respondents
matching target population characteristics through online questionnaire distribution (Hair et
al., 2022) . To  expand respondent reach, snowball sampling is also used complementarily,
where respondents who have completed questionnaires are asked to recommend or distribute
questionnaires to other OTA users meeting research criteria (Sekaran & Bougie, 2016) . The
combination of purposive and snowball sampling is expected to produce eligible and
appropriate samples for PLS -SEM data analysis requirements. However, both techniques are
non-probability methods and do not produce a statistically representative sample of a ll
Indonesian OTA hotel-booking users. The sample may be affected by selection and sampling
bias (for example, over-representation of active social media users and of respondents within
the researchers' and earlier respondents' networks), so the findings s hould be generalized
beyond the sample with caution.

instruments to measure respondent perceptions of each construct in the research model. The

Data analysis employs PLS -SEM with two -stage evaluation: outer m odel and inner
model assessment. Outer model evaluation ensures measurement model validity and reliability
through convergent validity, discriminant validity, and reliability testing. Convergent validity
is assessed thro ugh outer loading values (≥ 0.70) an d Average Variance Extracted (AVE ≥
0.50) (Hair et al., 2021; Henseler et al., 2009) . Discriminant validity is evaluated using the
Fornell-Larcker Criterion, where the square root of AVE for each construct should excee d its
correlations with other constr ucts, and using the heterotrait –monotrait ratio of correlations
(HTMT), where values below 0.85 (conservative threshold) or 0.90, together with bootstrapped
confidence intervals that do not include 1, indicate discriminant validity (Henseler et al., 2015).
HTMT is particularly important here because Brand Attachment and Brand Commitment are
conceptually close constructs. Reliability is assessed using Cronbach's Alpha and Composite
Reliability values (≥ 0.70) (Sarstedt et al., 2021). Inner model evaluation examines structural
relationships among constructs through R -Square (R²), Q-Square (Q²), and path coefficients.
R² values of 0.75 indicate strong predictive ability, 0.50 moderate, and 0.25 weak (Hair et al.,
2021). Predictive power was assessed with PL Spredict (Shmueli et al., 2019) using  10 folds
and 10 repetitions. Q²predict values greater than zero were examined together with the RMSE
and MAE of the endogenous indicators, which were compared with the linear model ( LM)
benchmark. Predictive power is c onsidered high when all indicators have lower PLS -SEM
prediction errors than the LM benchmark, medium when the majority do, and low when only
a minority or none do. The analysis was conducted using SmartPLS 4.1.1.8. Hypothesis testing
uses a bootstrapping procedure with 5,000 subsamples to obtain path coefficients, t -statistics,
p-values, and percentile bootstrap 95% confidence intervals, using two-tailed tests; hypotheses
are supported when the t-statistic > 1.96, the p-value < 0.05, and the 95% confidence  interval
does not include zero (Hair et al., 2021).

Discussion
The findings of this study provide comprehensive insights into the formation of brand
commitment through digital experiences among OTA hotel booking application users. All six
hypotheses were supported, providing empirical support fo r the proposed theoretical
relationships derived from the Stimulus -Organism-Response (SOR) and Relationship
Marketing Theory ( RMT) frameworks within the OTA context. Because the data are cross -
sectional and self-reported, these findings describe associations; they do not demonstrate causal
effects or statistically tested mediating mechanisms. Interpretations that go beyond the
measured constructs are explicitly labeled below as plausible managerial interpretations.
The results confirm that perceived technic al interactivity significantly influences both
brand attachment (β = 0.413) and customer satisfaction (β = 0.534). These findings align with
previous research demonstrating that interactive technical features in digital platforms play a
crucial role in shaping users' emotional connections and evaluative responses (Al-Shafei, 2025;
Zhao et al., 2025). The stronger effect on customer satisfaction compared to brand attachment
suggests that users primarily evaluate the functional benefits of technical interac tivity before
developing emotional bonds with the brand. This finding is consistent with Man & Hashim
(2025), who found that  system responsiveness and control flexibility are primary drivers of
satisfaction in digital platforms, as these features directl y address users' immediate needs for
efficiency and convenience during hotel searches and bookings. The significant relationsh ip
between perceived technical interactivity and brand attachment indicates that when users
perceive OTA applications as responsive, controllable, and personalized, they develop deeper
emotional connections with the brand. This finding extends the work of Anggraeni (2026), who
demonstrated that interactive experiences facilitate the internalization of brand meaning in
consumers' cognitive and affective structures. In the OTA context, features such as real -time
availability updates, personalized recommendations, and seamless navigation create immersive
experiences that transform transactional interactions into meaningful relationships. A plausible
managerial interpretation is that users who feel in control of their search and booking processes
may be more i nclined to see the OTA brand as more than a service provider; however, trust
and partner perceptions were not measured in this study.
Social media content demonstrated significant positive influences on both brand
attachment (β = 0.430) and customer satisfaction (β = 0.450). These findings support previous
research highlighting the importance of social media content in building consu mer-brand
relationships (Hollebeek & Macky, 2019) . The relatively balanced effects on both constructs
suggest that social me dia content serves dual functions: providing informative value that
enhances satisfaction and creating symbolic meaning that stren gthens emotional bonds. This
dual mechanism is consistent with Al Amin et al. (2026) , who found that social media
marketing activities simultaneously influence cognitive evaluations and affective responses.
The significant influence of social media content on brand attachment can be attributed to the
authentic and engaging nature of content that resonates with users' travel asp irations and
identity expression. Visual storytelling, user -generated content, and personalized travel

inspiration shared through OTA social media channels allow users to envision themselves
experiencing the destinations and accommodations featured. This p rocess facilitates the
internalization of brand values and creates emotional associations that extend beyond mere
service satisfaction, aligning with AlFraihat et al. (2025)  who demonstrated that well -crafted
social media content enables consumers to form deeper psychological connections with brands
through narrative and visual engagement. Furthermore, the influence of social media  content
on customer satisfaction underscores the importance of content quality in facilitating informed
decision-making. Info rmative and transparent content regarding hotel amenities, location
details, and user reviews helps reduce uncertainty and enhance s confidence in booking
decisions, consistent with Hollebeek & Macky (2019)  who argued that valuable content
supports consumer decision-making processes and enhances perceptions of brand competence
and reliability.
Brand attachment demonstrated a significant positive influence on brand commitment (β
= 0.306), aligning with previous research establishing emotional bonds as foundations for long-
term relationship maintenance (Choi et al., 2024; Ghorbanzadeh & Rahehagh, 2021) . This
finding indicates that us ers who develop deep emotional connections with OTA brands are
more likely to demonstrate dedication and willingness to mainta in relationships with those
platforms. The psychological proximity created through consistent positive experiences may
help transform transactional relationships into more meaningful ones, although loyalty and the
choice to stay despite competitive alternatives were not measured. The magnitude of this path
suggests that brand attachment is an important correlate of brand commitment; because indirect
effects were not tested, a mediating role cannot be concluded. Relationship marketing literature
suggests tha t users who feel that an OTA brand understands their preferences may be less
susceptible to competitive offers and more willing to  invest in the relationship (Nusair et al.,
2011).
Customer satisfaction showed the largest direct path coefficient to brand commitment (β
= 0.457) of the two predictors of brand commitment, consistent with satisfaction being an
important correlate of relationship sustainability in digital service contexts. This is a descriptive
comparison of coefficient magnitudes; the difference between β = 0.457 and β = 0.306 was not
formally tested. This finding aligns with research by Mbango (2018)  and Sumiyana &
Komariyah (2024), who demonstrated that satisfaction serves as both rational and emotional
foundation for brand commitment. T he stronger effect of satisfaction compared to brand
attachment suggests that users' commitment to OTA platforms may be primarily grounded in
the consistent fulfillment of functional expectations. The importance of satisfaction in driving
commitment can be attributed to the high-involvement nature of hotel booking decisions. Users
invest significant time and resources in travel planning, and positive experiences that meet or
exceed expectations reinforce the perception that the OTA platform is a reliable an d valuable
partner. This finding is particularly relevant given the competitive nature of the OTA industry,
where users have numerous alternatives with low switching costs. Satisfaction may create a
psychological barrier to switching by establishing a trac k record of positive experiences that
users are reluctant to risk abandoning, and satisfied users may also be more likely to engage in
positive word-of-mouth and repeat purchases. These behaviors, together with switching, were
not measured in this study an d are plausible managerial interpretations rather than
demonstrated effects.
The theoretical implications of this study are as follows. By integrating Stimulus -
Organism-Response (SOR) Theory and Relationship Marketing Theory (RMT), this research
provides a n integrated framework for examining the relationships among digital stimuli,
internal states, and brand commitment in digital contexts. The findings show that digital stimuli
(perceived technical interactivity and social media content) are positively asso ciated with
internal states (satisfaction and attachment), which are in turn positively associated with a

relational outcome (commitment). This is consistent with the complementary explanatory
value of both theoretical frameworks in the OTA context. The st udy also contributes to the
brand attachment literature by identifying perceived technical interactivity and social media
content as important antecedents of emotional bonds in digital service contexts, extending
understanding to application-based service platforms where emotional connections are formed
through mediated interactions. Furthermore, the research provides a descriptive indication of
the relative size of the satisfaction and attachment paths to brand commitment, suggesting that
functional fulfil lment may remain paramount even as emotional connections contribute
meaningfully to relationship sustainability.
From a managerial perspective, the findings offer several practical implications for OTA
companies seeking to strengthen user commitment and reduce switching behavior. The strong
influence of perceived technical interactivity on both satisfaction and attachment underscores
the importance of investing in responsive, intuitive, and personalized application features. OTA
companies should prioritize system responsiveness, navigation ease, and personalization
capabilities as these technical attributes significantly shape users' evaluative and emotional
responses. Features such as real -time availability updates, seamless booking processes, and
personalized recommendations should be continuously refined to enhance perceived
interactivity. The significant impact of social media content on satisfaction and attachment
highlights the strategic importance of content marketing in building brand relationships, where
OTA companies should develop comprehensive social media content strategies that balance
informative value with emotional resonance, providing practical information that facilitates
decision-making while also telling compelling stories that inspire travel aspirations and create
emotional connections. Finally, the stronger effect of satisfaction on commitment relative to
attachment suggests that OTA companies should prioritize functional excellence as the
foundation of relationship building, monitoring s atisfaction metrics closely and addressing
service failures promptly to maintain the functional trust that underpins commitment.
A prioritized managerial framework follows from the reported effect sizes (path
coefficients are compared descriptively, not th rough a formal test). Priority 1 – technical
interactivity: perceived technical interactivity has the strongest upstream path, to customer
satisfaction (β = 0.534), and also a meaningful path to brand attachment (β = 0.413), so
investment in responsiveness , navigation, and personalization should come first. Priority 2 –
satisfaction management: customer satisfaction has the largest direct path to brand commitment
(β = 0.457), so satisfaction monitoring and prompt resolution of service failures should be built
on top of the interactive features. Priority 3 – social media content: social media content has
balanced paths to brand attachment (β = 0.430) and customer satisfaction (β = 0.450), so
content should combine informative value (satisfaction) with emotion al storytelling
(attachment). Priority 4 – attachment building: brand attachment has a smaller but significant
path to brand commitment (β = 0.306), so personalization and relationship -oriented content
should support, rather than replace, functional excellence.
Despite these contributions, several limitations should be acknowledged. The cross -
sectional design limits causal inferences, as relationships were measured at a single point in
time, suggesting that future research could employ longitudinal designs to examine how digital
experiences and brand commitment evolve over time. The study focused on general OTA users
without distinguishing between specific platforms or types of users, so future research could
examine whether the relationships vary across different OTA platforms or user segments based
on travel frequency, purpose, or demographic characteristics. The reliance on self -reported
measures of perceptions and evaluations from the same respondents may be subject to common
method bias, which could infl ate or otherwise distort the estimated relationships (see the
common method bias assessment in the Results), indicating that future research could
incorporate objective behavioral data such as booking frequency, platform usage patterns, or

economic contexts, so cross-cultural research examining whether the relationships hold across

in digital consumer behavior.

CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS
Conclusion
This study examined the formation of brand commitment through digital experiences in
Online Travel Agent (OTA) hotel booking applications, fo cusing on the roles of perceived
technical interactivity and social media content. The findings provide empirical support for the
proposed theoretical relationships derived from the integrated Stimulus -Organism-Response
and Relationship Marketing Theory framework within the OTA context, with all six hypotheses
supported. Perceived technical interactivity and social med ia content were significantly and
positively associated with customer satisfaction and brand attachment, which were in turn
significantly and  positively associated with brand commitment. Furthermore, customer
satisfaction showed a numerically larger direct  path to brand commitment than brand
attachment (a descriptive comparison), suggesting that functional service performance plays
an important role in strengthening digital consumer relationships.
The findings contribute to digital marketing literature by providing evidence from an
integrated model of digital experiences, customer satisfaction, brand attachment, and brand
commitment in the OTA hotel booking context. The study extends understanding of the
antecedents of brand attachment in digital services and demonstrates the complementary roles
of customer satisfaction and brand attachment in developing brand commitment. From a
managerial persp ective, OTA companies should prioritize responsive and interactive
application features alongside relevant and engaging social media content to enhance customer
satisfaction and emotional connections. Given the larger path coefficient of customer
satisfaction to brand commitment, functional service quality should remain an important
foundation for relationship building , supported by personalized experiences, exclusive
offerings, continuous satisfaction monitoring, and prompt resolution of service failures.

Limitations
This study has several limitations. First, the research was conducted within the
Indonesian OTA hotel booking context and during a specific period, so the findings may not
be fully generalizable to other countries, digital service sectors, or  consumer contexts. In
addition, purposive and snowball sampling are non-probability techniques, so the sample is not
statistically representative and may be affected by sampling bias. Second, the study examined
a limited set of variables, consisting of perceived technical interactivity, social media content,
customer satisfaction, brand attachment, and brand commitmen t, while other technological,
relational, and psychological factors may also influence brand commitment. Third, the cross -
sectional design and reliance on self-reported data limit the ability to capture long-term changes
in consumer perceptions and relationships and may introduce potential common method bias,
which could inflate or distort the estimated relationships. The cross-sectional design also limits
causal interpretation, so the results should be read as associations. The questionnaire items
were adapted and translated into Indonesian by the authors without formal back-translation or
a pilot test, although their validity and reliability w ere evaluated in the main sample. Finally,
the model tested direct relationships only; indirect effects through cus tomer satisfaction and

brand attachment were not tested. The findings should therefore be interpreted within the
specific context, variables, sample, and research period examined in this study.

Future studies should broaden the scope of research objects by examining different OTA
platforms, digital service industries, regions, and cultural contexts, while using probability -
based or more diverse sampling where feasible and a larger sample to improve the robustness
and generalizability of the  findings. Researchers are also encouraged to use a longer
observation period or longitudinal design to capture changes in customer satisfact ion, brand
attachment, and brand commitment over time and to incorporate objective behavioral data
where possible. Additional technological, relational, and psychological variables may also be
included to develop a more comprehensive model of digital brand  commitment formation.
Extensions tied to the limitations of the current model include: (1) testing the specific indirect
effects of perceived technical interactivity and social media content on brand commitment
through customer satisfaction and brand atta chment using bootstrapped confidence intervals,
to answer whether these constructs mediate the relationships; (2) f ormally comparing path
coefficients (e.g., the satisfaction versus attachment path to brand commitment) and testing
differences across OTA pl atforms or user segments with multigroup analysis; (3) using
longitudinal or experimental designs to examine whethe r the hypothesized relationships hold
over time and to support stronger causal interpretation; and (4) assessing common method bias
with a de sign that separates the measurement of predictors and outcomes, or with objective
behavioral data.
