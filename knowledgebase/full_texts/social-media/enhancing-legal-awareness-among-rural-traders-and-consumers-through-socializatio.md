other rural communities.

I.  Introduction

The development of information technology has fundamentally transformed economic interactions.
Rapid digital transformation has accelerated the shift from conventional transactions to electronic commerce
(e-commerce), which is increasingly integrated with  social media platforms. This emerging business mod el,
known as social commerce, involves the buying and s elling of goods and services through social media
platforms that support marketing and transaction ac tivities (Abbas & Ramadani). Social commerce combin es
social communication with economic transactions, oﬀ ering convenience, low operating costs, and extensi ve
marketing reach. According to a 2023 report by Goog le, Temasek, and Bain & Company, Indonesia had the
largest digital economy in Southeast Asia, with dig ital transactions valued at USD 82 billion, of whic h 77%
came from the e-commerce sector. Furthermore, DataR eportal (2024) reported that 167 million Indonesian s
actively used social media, creating favorable cond itions for the growth of this interaction-based tra ding

II.  Literature Review and Hypothesis Development

2.1.  Legal Socialization and Legal Awareness

Legal socialization is the process of communicating legal information, values, and norms to the public
to help individuals understand, internalize, and ap ply legal provisions in their daily lives. Accordin g to
Friedman (1975), the eﬀectiveness of law in society  depends not only on the substance of the law and l aw
enforcement institutions but also on the community's legal culture. Legal socialization serves as an important
means of developing and strengthening this culture.  Soekanto (1983) identiﬁed four indicators of legal
awareness: legal knowledge, legal understanding, at titudes toward the law, and legal compliance behavi or.
These indicators can be strengthened through participatory and dialogical legal socialization that reﬂects the
community's social context. Widodo and Sulistiyono (2020) found that legal socialization increased pub lic
knowledge by up to 72% and improved legal compliance behavior by 58% among micro, small, and medium-
sized enterprises (MSMEs) following digital legal t raining. In contemporary legal practice, legal soci alization
extends beyond the dissemination of normative infor mation to include legal empowerment.  Golub (2010)
explained that legal empowerment enhances the publi c's ability to understand their rights, resolve dis putes,
and prevent legal harm. This approach is particular ly relevant to e-commerce, where individuals with l imited
legal knowledge may be more vulnerable to fraud and  digital manipulation. Several empirical studies in
Indonesia have also demonstrated the eﬀectiveness o f legal education in strengthening legal awareness
among rural communities and micro-business operators. Kurniawan (2019), for example, found that consumer

2.2.  Deﬁnition and Legal Aspects of E-Commerce

Electronic commerce (e-commerce) refers to the buyi ng and selling of goods and services through
electronic systems. Laudon and Traver (2022) deﬁne e-commerce as a commercial transaction process that
uses internet technology to facilitate the exchange  of goods, services, and information. In Indonesia,
Government Regulation No. 80 of 2019 concerning Trading Through Electronic Systems regulates commercial
transactions conducted through digital devices and internet networks. Law No. 1 of 2024, which amends Law
No. 11 of 2008 concerning Electronic Information an d Transactions, provides the legal framework for
electronic information, electronic documents, and e lectronic transactions. Electronic documents and
contracts may serve as legally recognized evidence when they satisfy the applicable legal requirements .
According to Susanto (2018), this development has t ransformed the evidentiary framework for transactio ns,
which previously relied more heavily on physical do cuments. Furthermore, Law No. 8 of 1999 concerning
Consumer Protection establishes consumers' rights t o receive accurate, clear, and honest information t hat is
not misleading. In digital transactions, these rights extend to product descriptions, prices, business operators'
identities, transaction terms, and return policies.  Asriati (2024) emphasized that limited understandi ng of
consumer rights can increase the public's vulnerabi lity to fraud in electronic transactions. Therefore , e-
commerce represents not merely a change in transaction methods but also a context in which comprehensive
legal understanding is essential to protecting cons umers from digital risks.

2.3.  Theory, Deﬁnition, and Characteristics of Social Co mmerce

Social commerce is a business model that integrates  social media interactions with commercial
transactions. Huang and Benyoucef (2013) describe s ocial commerce as an evolution of e-commerce that
relies on social interactions, user recommendations , and community relationships to inﬂuence purchasin g
decisions. Unlike conventional e-commerce, which primarily emphasizes online transactions, social commerce
places greater emphasis on the social interactions that shape consumer behavior. Several scholars have
proposed complementary deﬁnitions of social commerc e. Baghdadi (2013) describes it as the integration of
e-commerce and social media, enabling businesses to  use social networks to market and sell products.
Similarly, Liang and Turban (2011) characterize soc ial commerce as a form of community-based commerce
that incorporates user-generated contributions, inc luding reviews, recommendations, and discussions, i nto
the transaction process. In the Indonesian regulato ry context, Minister of Trade Regulation No. 31 of 2023
addresses social commerce as a form of social media service that facilitates the promotion or oﬀering of goods
and services. The regulation also establishes relev ant requirements concerning business activities con ducted
through electronic systems. Trust and risk theory, as discussed by Gefen (2000), highlights the import ance of
trust in online transactions, where buyers may have  limited opportunities to verify sellers and produc ts
directly. In social commerce, trust is often shaped  by seller reputation, user reviews, and social int eractions.
Brodie et al. (2011) also emphasized the role of us er engagement in shaping consumer behavior, suggest ing
that active interactions can inﬂuence purchasing de cisions. According to Zhang and Benyoucef (2016), s ocial
commerce has six distinctive characteristics:

a.  User-generated content: Photos, testimonials, and r eviews shared by users.
b.  Two-way communication: Direct interaction between s ellers and buyers.

when conducting transactions through social media.

III.  Research Method

The community service program employed a participat ory, educational, and practice-oriented
approach to enhance the understanding and legal awareness of residents in Gunung Silanu Village regarding
electronic commerce transactions conducted through social media. The program consisted of three main
stages: (1) preparation, (2) implementation, and (3 ) evaluation and follow-up. All stages were guided by the
principles of community-based empowerment and andragogy, which position community members as active
participants in the learning process. The program a dopted a mixed-methods approach that integrated leg al
education, practical digital transaction simulation s, and participatory evaluation through pre-tests a nd post-
tests. The activities comprised four main component s. First, interactive legal counseling was conducte d
through participatory presentations, discussions of  real-life cases, and question-and-answer sessions to
improve participants' legal knowledge. Second, simulations of safe digital transaction practices were provided
to train participants in the use of digital invoice s and transaction receipts, veriﬁcation of seller a nd buyer
identities, and appropriate complaint procedures. T hird, mentoring activities and the establishment of  a
village-based digital communication forum were impl emented through a WhatsApp group to facilitate
continued learning, information sharing, and legal consultation. Fourth, participants' legal understanding was
assessed using pre-tests and post-tests to evaluate  changes in their knowledge and determine the
eﬀectiveness of the program.

This section presents the results of the community service program conducted in Gunung Silanu

of digital law; (2) participants' ability to apply safe electronic transaction practices; and (3) the establishment
of a sustainable digital legal learning ecosystem a t the village level.

4.3.  Results of the Simulation of Safe Digital Transacti on Practices

The practical simulation required participants to u se their mobile phones to create digital invoices,
save transaction receipts, verify seller and buyer identities, and prepare simple terms and conditions for online
sales. The simulation results indicated that most p articipants could recognize and use basic digital i nvoices
after the training. Approximately 70% of participan ts were also able to verify seller identities by ex amining
account proﬁles, user reviews, and account activity . In addition, participants demonstrated a better
understanding of the importance of retaining transa ction receipts as potential legal evidence. These ﬁ ndings
suggest an improvement in participants' practical d igital transaction skills. The results are consiste nt with
Kolb's (1984) experiential learning theory, which e mphasizes learning through direct experience, reﬂec tion,
and practical application. Before the intervention,  only approximately 10–20% of participants demonstr ated
an understanding of the technical aspects of digital transactions. Following the training, the reporte d level of
proﬁciency increased to approximately 70–85%. This improvement suggests that hands-on training can be an
eﬀective approach to strengthening digital legal li teracy in rural communities. By combining theoretic al
explanations with practical exercises, the program enabled participants to apply the information they
received to real-world transaction scenarios.

Another important outcome of the program was the es tablishment of the Gunung Silanu Village
Digital Legal Awareness Communication Forum through  a WhatsApp group. The forum was designed to
connect community members with the community servic e team, facilitate digital legal consultations, sha re
information about online fraud, and support communi ty-level legal advocacy. Following the program, the
forum initially included 23 members. Its establishment represents an eﬀort to sustain community engagement
and provide continued access to digital legal infor mation beyond the formal training sessions. This in itiative
is consistent with Golub's (2010) concept of legal empowerment, which emphasizes that improved access to
legal knowledge and support networks can help commu nities reduce their vulnerability to legal harm and
participate more actively in addressing legal issue s.

V.  Conclusion

The community service program on the legal aspects of social commerce was successfully
implemented in Gunung Silanu Village, Jeneponto Reg ency, and contributed to improving the community's
digital legal literacy. The combination of legal ed ucation, safe digital transaction simulations, and the
establishment of a digital legal communication foru m helped strengthen participants' knowledge, practi cal
skills, and awareness of conducting transactions th rough social media. First, the program improved
participants' knowledge and understanding of digita l law. The pre-test and post-test results showed
substantial improvements in their understanding of consumer protection, the legal framework governing
electronic transactions, online fraud risks, and co mplaint procedures. These ﬁndings highlight the pot ential
eﬀectiveness of an andragogical approach that incor porates practical examples and case-based discussio ns
to improve legal awareness among rural communities.  Second, participants developed practical skills in
conducting safer digital transactions, including cr eating digital invoices, verifying seller and buyer  identities,
retaining electronic transaction records, and under standing the terms and conditions of online sales. The
practical training helped participants develop skills intended to reduce fraud risks and strengthen transaction
documentation. Third, the establishment of the Digi tal Legal Awareness Community Communication Forum
provided a foundation for sustaining the program's outcomes. The forum serves as a collaborative platf orm
for community members, village oﬃcials, and academi cs to facilitate legal consultations, share informa tion,
and strengthen digital legal awareness at the villa ge level.
To ensure the sustainability and broader impact of the program, several recommendations are
proposed. First, follow-up training should be provi ded to micro and small enterprises (MSEs) on busine ss
registration, including the Business Identiﬁcation Number (NIB), relevant business licensing requirements, and
product certiﬁcation, to strengthen their readiness to operate in the digital marketplace. Second, participants
should receive further guidance on secure payment m ethods, including electronic wallets and escrow
services, to improve their understanding of payment  security and consumer protection. Third, the progr am
should be replicated in other villages with similar characteristics to address the growing challenges associated
with social commerce transactions. Implementing the se recommendations could help foster a safer, more
