ABSTRACT
Background and Purpose : Social media marketing has become a crucial strategy for restaurants
seeking to attract and retain customers in a competitive environment. Among the various digital
marketing tools, influencer marketing has emerged as a powerful approach for shaping consumer
attitudes and driving purchase behaviour. However, despite its widespread adoption, there is limited
empirical understanding of how influencer characteristics affect consumer responses in the restaurant
context. Grounded in the Stimulus-Organism-Response (S-O-R) framework, this study aims to examine
how influencer trustworthiness, expertise, and homophily influence consumers’ brand attitude and
purchase intention in Saudi Arabia’s casual dining sector. It further explores the mediating role of brand
attitude in these relationships.

Methodology: Data were collected through an online survey targeting active social media followers of
casual dining restaurants in Saudi Arabia. A total of 343 valid responses were obtained using purposive
sampling. The data were analysed using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling (PLS-
SEM) with bootstrapping to test direct and mediating relationships among the study variables.

Findings: Results reveal that influencer trustworthiness and expertise significantly enhance brand
attitude and purchase intention, while homophily does not have a significant direct effect on purchase
intention. Brand attitude positively influences purchase intention and mediates the effects of all three
influencer attributes on purchase intention.

Contributions: This study contributes to Stimuli-Organism-Response (S-O-R) framework by
clarifying how specific influencer traits shape consumer decision-making in restaurant contexts.
Practically, it offers guidance for restaurant marketers in selecting influencers and fostering favourable
brand attitudes to optimize social media marketing outcomes.

Keywords: Social media influencers, brand attitude, purchase intention, casual dining restaurants.

1.0 INTRODUCTION
Social media has become an integral part of users’ daily lives, providing ubiquitous access to
vast amounts of information across the globe (Al-Muani et al., 2023; Hmoud et al., 2022). The
rapid growth in social media adoption has reshaped marketing trends and strategies, leading
firms to regard social platforms as critical channels for communicating and engaging with
customers  (Chetioui et al., 2020; Lee et al., 2023). Reflecting this shift, a recent report indicates
that approximately 73% of marketers believe social media can effectively support their
marketing campaigns. This confidence is closely linked to the continuous expansion of the
global social media user base. For instance, more than 4.26 billion individuals worldwide were
using social media in 2021, and this figure is projected to reach 6 billion by 2027 (González-
Serrano et al., 2024). In the current digital era, social media has thus emerged as the dominant
mode of communication worldwide. This pattern is also evident in Saudi Arabia, where social
media usage is exceptionally high, with more than 90% of the population reported to be active
on social networks (Mabkhot et al., 2022). In earlier years, businesses and companies primarily
relied on celebrities’ fame and social status to promote their brands. However, the evolution of
social media platforms has shifted attention toward social media influencers, whose
prominence as persuasive endorsers has grown substantially (Xu & Pratt, 2018).
The proliferation of social media users has catalysed the emergence of Social Media
Influencer Marketing as a distinct digital marketing paradigm (Shan et al., 2020; Zhou et al.,
2021). This contemporary marketing approach involves brands collaborating with prominent
social media personalities to endorse products and services across digital platforms
(Glucksman, 2017). Conceptually, influencer marketing encompasses the strategic utilisation
of endorsements, sponsorships, or product placements by influential individuals or
organisations as a social media marketing mechanism (Bu et al., 2022; Kay et al., 2020). More
specifically, it represents a strategic framework wherein marketers identify and engage
individuals who command substantial followings and social media prominence to
communicate brand messages to designated target audiences (Lou & Yuan, 2019).
The strategic significance of influencer marketing has been substantiated through
robust empirical evidence. Recognised as a sustainable long-term digital marketing strategy for
businesses (Ki et al., 2020), the global influencer marketing industry achieved a valuation of
USD148 million in 2019 representing an 8% year-over-year growth with projections exceeding
USD373 million within six years (Bu et al., 2022). Lou and Yuan. (2019), further documented
that 94% of marketers perceive influencer marketing campaigns as effective, while 89% report
that its return on investment equals or surpasses alternative marketing strategies (Martínez-
López et al., 2020). Notably, influencer marketing demonstrates an effectiveness rate 11 times
greater than traditional advertising methods (Lou & Yuan, 2019).
Saudi Arabia represents one of the Middle East's most extensive geographical
territories, with a total population of 35 million, the nation exhibits a notably youthful
demographic profile, wherein nearly half of residents are below 31 years of age (Alomair,
2022). The Saudi economy ranks among the world's top 20 largest economies and is
characterized as a rapidly developing market (Times, 2016). The restaurant sector in Saudi
Arabia has experienced substantial transformation, with investment exceeding 17 billion Saudi
Riyals (Alomair, 2022). This growth trajectory has positioned Saudi Arabia as one of the
world's leading consumer-oriented economies. The nation currently ranks as the fourth-highest
consuming country globally, with restaurants and hospitality establishments serving as primary
consumption destinations for Saudi consumers (Alomair, 2022).
Within the Saudi restaurant landscape, casual dining establishments constitute a
significant segment. These establishments are characterized by their hybrid service model,
combining elements of quick-service and fine dining formats while offering moderately priced
menu items within an informal ambiance (Bushara et al., 2023). The casual dining sector has

activity, whereas negative attitudes can discourage potential customers and compromise brand
reputation (Kudeshia & Kumar, 2017; Zhao et al., 2024). While existing research has
established that social media influencers significantly affect consumer purchase intentions in
developed markets, scholarly inquiry into influencer marketing within developing economy
like the Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) region remains limited (Aggad & Sh, 2021).
Furthermore, considerable knowledge gaps persist regarding the optimal utilization of
this emergent marketing phenomenon by practitioners (Campbell & Farrell, 2020). Given that
influencer marketing theory and practice remain in nascent stages of development (Chekima et
al., 2020), more comprehensive and rigorous investigation is essential to advance the literature
and inform marketing practice (Aggad & Sh, 2021; AlFarraj et al., 2021; Al-Muani et al., 2023;
Hmoud et al., 2022). Consequently, this study examines the influence of social media
influencer trustworthiness, expertise, and homophily on consumer purchase intention, with
particular emphasis on the mediating role of brand attitude.
2.0 LITERATURE REVIEW
2.1 Stimuli-Organism-Response Theory
Mehrabian and Russell (1974) originally proposed the stimulus–organism–response (S–O-R)
framework, which was subsequently refined by Jacoby (2002). The framework posits that
environmental cues function as stimuli (S) that shape individuals’ internal affective and
cognitive states (O), which in turn give rise to observable behavioural responses (R) (Ngah,
Thurasamy, Mohamad, et al., 2023). This perspective has been extensively adopted in
marketing research, including investigations of advertising effects and celebrity endorsement,
among other domains (Al Mamun et al., 2023; Lacroix et al., 2020; Ngah, Thurasamy,
Mohamad, et al., 2023; Zhu et al., 2020).
The S–O–R framework assumes that external stimuli do not exert a direct influence on
consumer behaviour; rather, they operate indirectly by first shaping consumers’ internal states,
which in turn determine behavioural outcomes (Hussain et al., 2023). In line with this view,
the theory is considered sufficiently flexible to be adapted to diverse research contexts,
provided that the core stimulus–organism–response logic is maintained (Ngah et al., 2021).
Within this framework, “stimuli” are conceptualised as external factors that impact an
individual’s internal state and subsequently behaviour (Min et al., 2019).
Given the focus of the present study, stimuli are operationalised as the influencer
characteristics. Specifically, influencer trustworthiness, expertise, and homophily are

2.2 Purchase Intention
Purchase intention denotes the strength of a consumer’s psychological commitment to
acquiring a particular product or service (Anas et al., 2023). It can be understood as the
perceived likelihood that a consumer will proceed with a purchase, shaped by perceived
benefits, emotional reactions, and prior experiences with the product or service (Morwitz,
2014). More broadly, intention represents an individual’s proximal inclination to perform a
specific behaviour and functions as a key indicator of motivational readiness to act.
Purchase intention can be understood as the joint expression of a consumer’s interest in
a specific product and the perceived likelihood of purchasing it, thereby closely mirroring the
consumer’s underlying preference for that brand or product (Al Mamun et al., 2023). In line
with (Rebelo, 2017), social media users’ purchase intentions may be reflected in several ways:
when users simulate or imagine purchasing the product, when they perceive a likelihood that
they will purchase it, or when they express a willingness to buy the product endorsed by
influencers on these platforms.
Within the restaurant sector, a strong association is frequently observed between
consumers’ behavioural intentions and their actual purchase behaviour. In essence, individuals
who exhibit a strong desire or plan to dine at a particular restaurant are more likely to translate
this intention into an actual visit and purchase. Accordingly, restaurant purchase intention can
be described as the extent to which prospective customers are motivated and willing to
patronise a restaurant and purchase its food and beverage offerings (Jani & Han, 2011).
2.3 Social Media Influencers
With escalating consumer demands and expectations, marketers continually innovate their
strategies to forge effective emotional connections with target audiences. This has propelled
the integration of influencer endorsements into advertising, as these approaches elicit
consumers' attitudinal and affective responses while shaping brand preferences and purchase
behaviours (Alomair, 2022; Min et al., 2019).
Social media influencers (SMIs) are individuals who have established substantial and
engaged followings, with content primarily reaching audiences who voluntarily choose to
interact (Aggad & Sh, 2021). Operating as third-party endorsers, they specialise in niche
domains such as beauty, food, fashion, or life stye and fitness, delivering recommendations,
product descriptions, and information across diverse social platforms to shape audience
attitudes (Alomair, 2022; Mabkhot et al., 2022; Putri & Tiarawati, 2021). Empirical evidence
confirms that digital influencers positively influence purchase intentions (Aggad & Sh, 2021;
Al-Muani et al., 2023; Bu et al., 2022; Ladhari et al., 2020), while prior studies demonstrate
that influencer marketing outperforms traditional advertising channels like radio or television
in both cost-efficiency and speed of audience persuasion, owing to followers' rapid
responsiveness to credible endorsers (Mabkhot et al., 2022).

2.4 Influencer Trustworthiness
Trustworthiness in influencer marketing refers to the extent to which consumers perceive an
influencer as honest, reliable, and dependable when sharing opinions, recommendations, or
endorsements of products and services (Wiedmann & Von Mettenheim, 2020). It represents a
foundational element of persuasive communication, as trust shapes how audiences interpret
influencer-generated messages and, ultimately, how they respond behaviourally to those
messages (Al-Muani et al., 2023; Casaló et al., 2020).
Early work in persuasion research highlights the importance of trust in the message
source, showing that perceived credibility significantly enhances the effectiveness of an
endorser and positively influences consumers’ evaluations of advertising content (Sternthal et
al., 1978). Building on this perspective, Lou and Yuan (2019) conceptualize influencer
trustworthiness as audience perceptions rooted in sincerity, and honesty (Lou & Yuan, 2019).
While more recent studies have expanded the construct by identifying additional dimensions
and contextual drivers of trust, scholars consistently emphasize that trustworthiness remains a
central determinant of influencer effectiveness.
In the context of social media, influencers often integrate brand endorsements into their
personal narratives, demonstrate product usage, and share first-hand experiences with their
followers. This practice contributes to perceptions of authenticity and relatability, which, in
turn, reinforce trust among audiences (Dhun & Dangi, 2023). As a result, consumers are more
likely to view influencers as credible sources of information rather than as traditional
advertisers.
Given this dynamic, trustworthiness is not merely a desirable attribute but a necessary
condition for persuasive influence. Influencers who successfully establish and maintain trust
are better positioned to shape attitudes and intentions, whereas those perceived as insincere or
opportunistic risk losing credibility and audience engagement. Accordingly, eliciting and
sustaining trustworthiness is essential for influencers seeking to exert meaningful persuasive
impact on their followers (Schouten et al., 2021).
Brand attitude has long been established as a central construct in marketing,
underscoring its critical role in shaping consumers’ intentions and behaviours (Al-Muani et al.,
2023; Azjen, 1980). Review of prior research reveals that influencer trustworthiness exerts a
significant influence on consumers' brand attitudes across diverse contexts. For instance,
Chekima et al. (2020) demonstrated that trustworthiness positively shapes product attitudes.
Similarly, Nafees et al. (2020) established its impact on product attitudes, while Hmoud et al.
(2022) reported comparable findings Instagram users in Jordan (Chekima et al., 2020; Hmoud
et al., 2022; Nafees et al., 2020). Despite the established effects of influencer trustworthiness
on brand attitudes, this relationship remains underexplored within developing markets like
Saudi Arabia and niche sectors such as casual dining restaurants. Addressing this gap through
further empirical investigation is essential, as it carries critical implications for marketers in
optimising endorser selection and crafting targeted strategies.
Trustworthiness characteristics are fundamentally predicated on fulfilling promises and
upholding honesty (Bonnevie et al., 2020). Recent empirical studies have established a robust
positive relationship between social media influencers' trustworthiness and purchase intention
across diverse contexts, including beauty products in France (Sokolova & Kefi, 2020),
Japanese cuisine in Thailand (Laohasukkasem et al., 2021), and skincare products in Malaysia
(Al Mamun et al., 2023). Thus, this study hypothesizes that:

H1: Influencers’ trustworthiness has a positive impact on brand attitude.
H2:  Influencers’ trustworthiness has a positive impact on purchase intention.

2.5 Influencer Expertise
Expertise constitutes a core dimension of source credibility alongside trustworthiness
(Ohanian, 1990), defined as the perceived extent of an influencers’ knowledge, skills, and
understanding in a given domain (Erdogan, 1999; Masuda et al., 2022; Rebelo, 2017). This
attribute functions similarly to a formal qualification, directly enhancing the endorser's capacity
to persuade consumers toward product or service adoption (Wang & Scheinbaum, 2018).
Scholars consistently recognise expertise as the paramount factor for successful endorsements
(Mabkhot et al., 2022), as it elevates the perceived validity of the communicator's assertions
and amplifies message persuasiveness, thereby increasing consumers' receptivity and
likelihood of behavioural compliance (Al-Muani et al., 2023).
The rapid expansion of social media influencers has significantly amplified this
marketing approach, facilitating unprecedented access to consumer cognition (Kim & Kim,
2021). Businesses can now strategically select influencers whose backgrounds and expertise
align closely with the products or services being promoted. Previous studies have demonstrated
that when consumers perceive influencers as domain experts, they exhibit heightened trust in
their recommendations and endorsements, which in turn elevates purchase intentions (AlFarraj
et al., 2021). Similarly, (Weismueller et al., 2020) identified a positive association in the
German cosmetics market. Collectively, these findings indicate that influencer expertise exerts
a consistent influence on purchase intention across diverse markets.
Expertise is a central dimension of source credibility and refers to the extent to which
an endorser is perceived as having the knowledge, competence, and experience to serve as a
dependable source of information (Erdogan, 1999). Endorsers or influencers regarded as
experts are expected to provide more reliable and authoritative information compared to those
who lack relevant background or contextual understanding. Consequently, audiences are more
likely to consider an influencer an expert only when they perceive the individual as skilful, and
knowledgeable within a specific domain (Hmoud et al., 2022; Schouten et al., 2021).
A high level of perceived expertise not only enhances the influencer’s credibility but
also strengthens the persuasive power of their messages. Endorsers recognized for their
expertise tend to elicit greater engagement the promoted brand (Ohanian, 1990). Empirical
research consistently demonstrates that expertise plays a pivotal role in shaping consumer
perceptions and attitudes toward brands. Specifically, when an influencer is viewed as an expert
in their field, the information they communicate is more readily accepted and internalized by
the audience, leading to a more favourable attitude toward the endorsed brand.
The robustness of this relationship has been validated across various industries. For
instance, (Chekima et al., 2020) found that influencer expertise significantly influences
consumers' product attitudes in the cosmetics industry. Similarly, Dhun and Dangi (2023)
provided empirical support for the positive effect of influencer expertise on brand attitude
formation among Instagram users. Thus, this study hypothesizes that:

H3: Influencers’ expertise has a positive impact on brand attitude.
H4: Influencers’ expertise has a positive impact on purchase intention.
2.6 Influencer Homophily
Homophily is commonly defined as the extent of perceived similarity between two individuals
who interact, particularly with respect to their beliefs, values, education, and social status (Ao
et al., 2023). In the context of online communication, (O’Reilly et al., 2016) describe
homophily as the user’s assessment of how similar an online source is to them in terms of
character, background, and expectations. In the influencer and endorsement literature, the term
similarity is frequently employed interchangeably with homophily (Dhun & Dangi, 2023),

In essence, homophily reflects how closely aligned the message source is with the
audience based on experiences, preferences, and values. This perceived similarity has
important implications for marketing communication effectiveness. Prior research suggests
that advertisements tend to be more persuasive when endorsers are perceived as similar to the
target consumers in terms of culture, beliefs, or demographic characteristics (Munnukka et al.,
2016). This notion resonates with the well-known adage, “birds of a feather flock together,”
emphasizing that individuals are more receptive to, and influenced by, sources they perceive
as similar to themselves.
Within the domain of social media influencer marketing, homophily emerges as a
particularly salient attribute. Social media environments are characterized by a high level of
interaction and engagement between influencers and their audiences, which elevates the
importance of perceived similarity (Arora et al., 2019; Masuda et al., 2022). Earlier studies
have demonstrated that similarity between influencers and followers significantly enhances the
influencer’s social appeal (Gonzales et al., 1983). Building on this, recent research has shown
that homophily influences individuals’ willingness to seek opinions, resonate with others, and
engage in social networking activities (Bu et al., 2022; Ladhari et al., 2020).
Perceived homophily can foster multiple relational and informational outcomes,
including greater inclination to seek information from influencers, the development of
interpersonal relationships with them, and increased willingness to share their content (Ladhari
et al., 2020). Consequently, contemporary scholars increasingly employ the concept of
homophily to explain followers’ reactions to the content produced by influencers, opinion
leaders, and celebrities in digital and social media contexts (Ladhari et al., 2020). Numerous
studies have examined homophily in diverse contexts and consistently demonstrated its
capacity to stimulate and sustain interaction. For instance, (Kim & Kim, 2021) found that
homophily exerts a positive influence on customer purchase intention. Complementing these
findings, a study conducted in the United States by (Lou & Yuan, 2019) reported that perceived
homophily between influencers and consumers positively enhances consumers’ purchase
intentions.

similarity is consistently associated with more favourable attitudes toward the advertised brand
and, in turn, greater advertising effectiveness(Ao et al., 2023; Munnukka et al., 2016). While
early work placed substantial emphasis on specific attributes like physical attractiveness,
subsequent empirical evidence suggests that attractiveness has a relatively weaker impact on
advertising outcomes compared to other credibility dimensions (Amos et al., 2008). In
response, scholars have increasingly highlighted the importance of congruence, or “fit,”
between the spokesperson’s characteristics and the brand that are salient to consumers (Ilicic
et al., 2015), implying that different endorser attributes may be differentially effective
depending on product category and endorsement context.
Within this broader perspective, homophily has emerged as a particularly influential
component of endorser credibility. Previous study (Schouten et al., 2021) demonstrate that key
credibility facets as similarity, and others collectively enhance favourable attitudes toward
endorsed products such as fitness, and fashion. Importantly, prior work suggests that similarity
between the message source and consumers can be more consequential for shaping brand
attitudes than other affective responses such as likeability as cited in (Munnukka et al., 2016).
Endorsers who resemble the target audience are generally regarded as more effective because
such similarity facilitates identification and allows consumers to relate more easily to the
endorser (Dhun & Dangi, 2023; Hughes et al., 2019). This, in turn, supports the development
of more positive brand attitudes. Thus, this study hypothesizes that:

2.7 Brand Attitude
Brand attitude denotes a consumer’s overall evaluative judgment toward a brand, shaped by
their cognitive and affective responses to brand-related stimuli (Al-Muani et al., 2023; Eagly,
1993; Hmoud et al., 2022). It reflects a relatively stable and enduring predisposition that guides
consumer behaviour over time (Kudeshia & Kumar, 2017; Mitchell & Olson, 1981). In
marketing, brand attitude is widely regarded as a key antecedent of consumer decision-making
and behavioural intentions toward a product or service ((Mitchell & Olson, 1981). For
businesses, cultivating a favourable brand attitude is strategically important, as it strengthens
brand image. Empirical evidence from the fast-food sector indicates that by evoking positive
emotional responses, restaurants can effectively shape brand attitude and, in turn, stimulate
purchase intention among consumers (Afandi & Marsasi, 2023; Pérez-Villarreal et al., 2019).
A robust body of theoretical and empirical literature affirms that brand attitude exerts a
significant influence on consumers’ purchase intention such as the theory of reasoned action
and the theory of planned behaviour(Ajzen, 1991; Fishbein & Ajzen, 1975). Within this
framework, MacKenzie and Spreng (1992) argue that favourable brand attitudes strengthen
purchase intention by enhancing consumer motivation (MacKenzie & Spreng, 1992). In the
domain of influencer marketing, (Al-Muani et al., 2023) emphasize that attitude toward the
brand plays a pivotal role in shaping consumers’ intention to buy, particularly in contexts where
influencers act as credible sources of brand information. These findings underscore the
importance of understanding consumers’ attitudes toward advertised brand, as such attitudes
are critical in determining the effectiveness of marketing efforts across diverse industries and
cultural settings. Empirical studies across developed economies consistently report a strong
and significant relationship between brand attitude and purchase intention (Chetioui et al.,
2020; Gomes et al., 2022; Kudeshia & Kumar, 2017). Building on this theoretical and empirical
foundation, the following hypothesis is proposed:

H7: Brand attitude has a positive impact on purchase intention.
2.8 Mediating Effect of Brand Attitude
Several prior studies have examined brand attitude as a mediating mechanism linking different
antecedents to purchase intention. Sallam and Wahid (2012) demonstrated that brand attitude
significantly mediates the relationship between attitude toward advertising and purchase
intention in the context of Yemeni consumers. Likewise, Al-Muani et al. (2023) provided
empirical support for the mediating role of brand attitude between influencer marketing and
purchase intention. Furthermore, Kunja et al. (2022) confirmed that brand attitude plays a
crucial mediating role in the relationship between electronic word-of-mouth (eWOM) and
purchase intention, specifically within smartphone brand fan pages on Facebook in India
(Kunja et al., 2022).
The positive influence of brand attitude on consumers’ purchase intention has been
extensively documented in both traditional marketing and e‑commerce contexts, where
favourable brand evaluations consistently predict stronger purchase intentions (Dall’Olmo
Riley et al., 2015; Zhao et al., 2024). In the present study, which investigates the impact of
influencer attributes on purchase intention in the under‑explored context of casual dining
restaurants in Saudi Arabia, brand attitude is positioned as a mediating construct within the
Stimulus–Organism–Response (SOR) framework. This theoretical configuration is both
logical and contextually relevant, as influencer marketing functions as the external stimulus,

3.0 METHODOLOGY
This study employed a quantitative research design with a cross-sectional survey approach to
gather empirical data. Cross-sectional surveys are extensively utilized in consumer research to
capture perceptions, knowledge, attitudes, intentions, and behavioural tendencies at a specific
point in time (Mabkhot et al., 2022). All measurement items were either adopted or adapted
from established scales validated in previous scholarly works. Specifically, influencer

5.0 DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION
This study tested ten hypotheses. The primary objective was to examine the effect of influencer
marketing on consumers’ purchase intentions in casual dining restaurants in Saudi Arabia and,
to investigate the mediating role of brand attitude in the relationships between influencer
attributes and purchase intention. By employing the SOR framework, this study examines the
key influencer attributes that shape consumers’ purchase intentions and brand attitude in this
context.
The results support H2 and H4, showing that influencer trustworthiness and expertise
have a positive, direct, and significant effect on purchase intention. This finding is consistent
with previous research demonstrating that trustworthy and expert influencers enhance
consumers’ intentions to purchase (Aggad & Sh, 2021; AlFarraj et al., 2021; Al-Muani et al.,
2023; Lefina & Hidayat, 2022; Park & Lin, 2020; Shan et al., 2018).
In addition, the findings confirm that trustworthiness, expertise, and homophily
positively, directly and substantially influence brand attitude (H1, H3, H5). These results
indicate that these specific influencer attributes are critical in shaping consumers’ attitudes
toward the promoted brands, which in turn affect their willingness to purchase the advertised
products or services. This is in line with prior studies that have reported positive effects of
influencer trustworthiness, expertise, and homophily on brand attitude (Abdulaziz et al., 2024;
Bu et al., 2022; Hmoud et al., 2022; Lin et al., 2021; Regina & Anindita, 2022), which confirm
that, for consumers in Saudi Arabia, influencer endorsements are a critical factor in fostering
positive attitudes toward casual dining restaurants. Thus, partnering with influencers who are
perceived as trustworthy, knowledgeable, and relatable is essential for this industry. Given that
dining experiences are often selected to satisfy social, hedonic, and experiential needs,
choosing influencers who resonate with the target audience’s lifestyle and preferences can
strengthen the impact of influencer endorsement on shaping positive brand attitudes and
enhancing the purchase intention among Saudi consumers in the casual dining segment.
In addition, the study revealed that brand attitude has a positive effect on purchase
intention, supporting H7. This finding is consistent with previous research showing that attitude
is positively associated with purchase intention (Chetioui et al., 2020; Kudeshia & Kumar,
2017; Sallam & Wahid, 2012). Consumers who hold favourable attitudes toward a restaurant
are more likely to visit that restaurant. A plausible explanation is that once customers form a
positive evaluation of a business, they develop a stronger intention to try its offerings and

purchase from it. In the restaurant context, consumers tend to be selective and risk-averse, as
trying a new restaurant can lead to dissatisfaction or even discomfort, particularly in terms of
digestive convenience. Consequently, customers are more inclined to form strong purchase
intentions toward restaurants for which they already hold favourable brand attitudes.
In order to further enhance the contribution of this study, brand attitude was examined
as a mediating variable. The analysis shows that brand attitude mediates the relationships
between influencer attributes and purchase intention, supporting H8, H9, and H10. In other
words, a positive brand attitude provides a mediating pathway through which influencer
attributes lead casual dining restaurant customers to develop stronger purchase intentions. This
finding is consistent with the SOR framework (Mehrabian & Russell, 1974), which posits that,
environmental stimuli (influencer attributes) influence cognitive and affective evaluations
(brand attitude), which in turn shape behavioural responses (purchase intention). Thus, casual
dining restaurants should not overlook the role of brand attitude and the other independent
variables if they wish to enhance the effectiveness of their marketing efforts and support long-
term sustainability and success.
6.0 THEORETICAL CONTRIBUTIONS
The present study advances the limited body of knowledge on purchase intention within the
casual dining restaurant sector in Saudi Arabia, a context that has received scant empirical
attention compared to developed economies and other industries while also responding to
recent systematic review calls (Kumar et al., 2024) for more empirical evidence from
developing economies. By conceptualising influencer attributes as stimuli, brand attitude as the
organism, and purchase intention as the response, this research proposes and validates a novel
SOR-based framework tailored to Saudi casual dining consumers. In doing so, it extends the
explanatory power of the SOR paradigm for predicting consumers’ behavioural responses and
purchase intentions toward casual dining restaurants in this underexplored market.
Furthermore, the study empirically examines the mediating role of brand attitude in the
relationship between influencer marketing and purchase intention. This directly addresses the
gap highlighted by (Joshi et al., 2025), who note that extant influencer marketing research tends
to focus on direct effects on consumer behaviour and call for greater attention to the underlying
psychological mechanisms. By demonstrating the attitudinal pathway through which influencer
characteristics shape purchase decisions, this study offers a more nuanced understanding of the
process by which influencer marketing drives consumer responses in the Saudi casual dining
context.
7.0 PRACTICAL IMPLICATIONS
This study offers several practical implications for marketers, businesses, and influencers
operating in the social media marketing ecosystem. As businesses continue to increase budgets
for influencer campaigns and seek more effective criteria for selecting suitable partners, they
face a persistent information deficit regarding which influencers are most appropriate for their
brands, products, or services (Eyal, 2018; Kim & Kim, 2021). Although social media platforms
provide basic user data and facilitate influencer search, the available information is typically
limited to demographic indicators and quantitative metrics (Kim & Kim, 2021), offering little
guidance on the psychological levers that drive consumer response.
The present study identified nuanced influencer attributes (expertise, trustworthiness,
and homophily) as critical criteria for influencer selection and campaign design. For marketers
and businesses, understanding how these characteristics shape brand attitude and purchase
intention can inform the development of more effective influencer marketing strategies. By
prioritising influencers who are perceived as knowledgeable, honest, and similar to their target

8.0 STUDY LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE STUDIES RECOMMENDATIONS
Although this study offers important contributions, it is not free from limitations. This study
has several limitations that open avenues for future research. First, the sample comprised
predominantly casual dining restaurant consumers in Saudi Arabia recruited using purposive
sampling, which constrains the generalisability of the findings to other national contexts and
market settings. In line with the view that consumer behaviour is shaped by national culture
(Lim et al., 2021), future research should replicate this study across different countries,
business types, and industries to generate deeper insights and generalisability of the results.
Second, the cross-sectional research design restricts the ability to observe changes in purchase
intention and preferred influencer attributes over time. The exclusive reliance on a quantitative
design from the user’s perspective may further narrow the interpretive depth of the findings.
Longitudinal research, complemented by alternative approaches such as qualitative or mixed-
method designs, could offer a richer and more nuanced understanding of the dynamics
underlying influencer marketing effects and consumer decision processes. Additionally, this
study examined social media in a general sense rather than focusing on specific platforms.
Future studies could investigate how influencer marketing on particular platforms (e.g., TikTok
or Instagram) shapes consumer behaviour, thereby unpacking platform-specific mechanisms
and strategies. In addition, the sample size and its composition limit the generalizability of the
findings and may introduce sampling error. The final sample comprises 343 consumers of
casual dining restaurants in Saudi Arabia, which may not be fully representative of consumers
in other regions, patrons of other types of restaurants, or customers in different industries.
Consequently, caution is warranted when generalising these findings beyond the specific
context examined. Future studies may encompass a wider and more diverse array of
participants across regions, restaurant formats, and sectors to yield more comprehensive and
generalisable insights.
