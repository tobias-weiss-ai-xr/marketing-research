Abstract. The offline cultural‑entertainment performance market has continued to experience robust growth.
Meanwhile, IP derivatives have leveraged social media to facilitate both dissemination and commercial
conversion, with emotional currency emerging as a critical analytical lens for interpreting contemporary
consumption behaviors in this sector. Taking the new‑media marketing of offline cultural‑entertainment
derivatives as its research subject, this paper employs literature review and case study analysis to
systematically delineate the relevant conceptual and theoretical frameworks, and further explores how
emotional currency is generated, disseminated, and ultimately achieves value appreciation within the
new‑media environment. This study reveals that offline cultural‑entertainment scenarios lay the foundation for
emotional currency, and User‑Generated Content communication within social circles drives its value
appreciation. Nevertheless, traffic‑dominated excessive marketing may result in the overdraft of emotional
currency. Value appreciation and overdraft are not entirely contradictory; instead, they are two sides of the
same coin with homologous symbiosis. While new‑media marketing creates emotional value, it inherently
carries risks of consuming audience sentiment.
Keywords: emotional currency, offline entertainment culture, new media marketing
1. Introduction
In the era of consumerism, emotional factors are increasingly incorporated into economic logic; offline
entertainment consumption continues to grow, and Intellectual Property (IP) derivatives rely on new media to
achieve niche communication and commercial monetization. This article introduces the concept of "emotional
currency", which refers to the emotional experience with exchange and circulation value within consumer
communities, and takes offline entertainment derivatives (peripherals, collectibles, co-branded products) as the
specific carrier. Most existing studies are conducted from a single perspective, such as fan economy, emotional
labor or symbolic consumption, while few explore the symbiotic relationship between emotional value
appreciation and overdraft risks from the perspective of emotional currency, which leaves a research gap. At
the same time, the current industry generally adopts traffic-oriented marketing to trivialize the audience's
emotions to achieve the purpose of promotion, resulting in the audience's expectation gap and the elimination

2. Related concepts and theoretical foundations of offline cultural and
entertainment derivatives and emotional currency marketing
2.1. Offline cultural and entertainment derivatives and new media marketing
Offline cultural and entertainment activities are cultural consumption forms characterized by the audience's
on-site participation. Concerts and music festivals are the most typical representatives of this format. Behr
believed that live in-person music differs from recorded music. Its experience is attached to a specific time and
physical space, and the audience's on-site participation is a necessary condition for constituting a complete
performance experience [1]. Offline cultural and entertainment derivatives are precisely derived from this
unique on-site emotional experience. They are not confined to the practical value of the product itself, but
carry more memories and emotional value that audiences acquire at the scene. For example, music festival IP
cultural and creative products and concert souvenirs, these products, as the carriers of fleeting and non-
retainable on-site experiences and feelings, have become media for audiences to extend their memories of the
performances.
New media has built a complete communication and consumption channel for cultural and entertainment
activities. Relying on the technical advantages of visuality, interactivity and immediacy, the social platform
breaks the time and space restrictions of offline performances, and the audience's spontaneous sharing
behavior continues to continue the emotions brought by the scene, promoting the continuous dissemination of
derivatives in the fan circle. From the perspective of consumption logic, network media has amplified the
appeal of cultural and entertainment content. Under the capital marketing model, brands will adopt various
marketing strategies such as limited-edition sales and bundled sales to stimulate the consumption desire of the
audience [2]. Such marketing models have a two-sided effect: On one hand, they help brand IPs realize the
monetization of emotional value and achieve the appreciation of emotional currency; on the other hand, high-
intensity marketing guidance can also easily give rise to irrational consumption behaviors, laying potential
risks for subsequent emotional value overdrafts.
2.2. Connotation of consumption and communication of emotional currency
Combined with the new media marketing scenarios of offline cultural and entertainment derivatives, this paper
defines "emotional currency" as the emotional experiences generated by the audience in offline cultural and
entertainment activities such as music festivals and concerts. This value can be spread and circulated within
the audience circle, and has the potential to be converted into actual consumption behaviors and commercial
benefits. The emotional economy theory explains the underlying logic of this value transformation: The

2.3. The core theories
Based on the definition of the connotation of emotional currency consumption and communication in the
previous text, the overall research framework of this paper mainly relies on two core theories: symbolic
consumption theory and new media marketing theory, to offer a theoretical foundation for the following
analysis of value‑added logic and overdraft risks for entertainment derivatives. Symbolic consumption theory
derives from the consumer‑society perspective. It holds that consumers' consumption behaviors no longer
focus only on the practical functions and material values of commodities, but instead pursue more of the
emotional symbols, memory values and identity meanings carried behind the commodities. The behavior of
audiences paying for commemorative products and limited cultural and entertainment derivatives is essentially
paying for emotional memories, contextual symbols and group identity. This theory can reasonably explain the
generative basis of emotional currency, and also the key internal motivation that enables cultural and
entertainment derivatives to achieve value-added.
New media marketing focuses on the transformation of marketing methods in the digital communication
environment. It refers to the method of marketing via new media platforms, which relies on channels such as
social platforms and short‑video to achieve brand promotion. In the new media environment, brands can
expand the emotional value of their products through diversified marketing methods such as topic operation
and content collaboration, promote the continuous accumulation of emotional currency, and achieve value
appreciation.
3. The appreciation of emotional currency: how new media transforms
emotion into a priced asset
3.1. New media marketing: the generation place and circulation platform of emotional currency
Offline cultural and entertainment activities are real scenes where the audience's emotions outbursts. But the
on-site emotions are short-lived and confined, making it hard to achieve continuous circulation on their own.
Relying on digital scenarios such as social networks and short videos, algorithms deliver targeted emotional
guidance for users. By tracking users' online behaviors with big data, they accurately capture the emotional
tendencies and demand changes of the audience [4]. Existing studies argue that consumer behavior is
essentially the reappearance of cultural capital. The symbolic system endows commodities with symbolic
connotations, thereby participating in the shaping process of individual identity and emotional expression [5].
Digital media shortens the time cycle of emotional expression, enabling individuals' immediate on-site
experiences to quickly be transmitted and spread within social circles. New media reconstructs the connection

3.2. Offline entertainment scenes as the basis for the generation of emotional currency
Marcel Halbwachs proposed that for groups that cannot experience the past firsthand, collective memory is the
core source of constructing group identity [6]. Offline entertainment venues rely on rituals and symbols to
construct memories, and the unique on-site experience gathers scattered personal emotions into collectively
shared feelings. Symbols serve as key media for evoking collective memory, covering diverse forms such as
audio cues, stage visuals, collective cheering behaviour, etc. Participants on site gain strong emotional
resonance through physical synchronization and consolidate the identity within the circle [7]. These immersive
on-site emotions precipitate into original emotional experiences, which constitute the indispensable practical
value of emotional currency and lay the foundation for the value-added in subsequent online dissemination.
3.3. UGC circle communication: driving the value addition of emotional currency
Offline cultural and entertainment scenarios lay a solid foundation for the generation of emotional currency
and begin a new round of dissemination by leveraging new media. Users share and repost content related to
concerts on social platforms, relying on the platforms' interactive and connection features to achieve
large‑scale diffusion of the content and gain rapid online popularity [8]. Within the fan community, the
audience converts the memories and emotions gained on site into diverse User-Generated Content (UGC)
including short videos, images, texts and edited clips. Through constant reposting, replication, and adaptation,
the original individual emotions break away from personal experiences and continuously accumulate value in
repeated dissemination. This user‑driven spontaneous diffusion process amplifies the influence of original
emotions and drives the offline‑generated emotional currency to achieve large‑scale value appreciation.
4. The overdraft of emotional currency: the consumption mechanism of
emotional value by the logic of new media marketing
4.1. Exhaustion of emotional currency caused by over-commercialized marketing
Emotions should exist independently as internal experiences, but when tied to commodities, they lose their
autonomy. In the process of the commodification of emotion, capital not only realizes the appreciation of
surplus value, but also extends its control scope from the production end to daily spiritual life through
continuous penetration into consumers' emotional sphere [4]. Thus, emotion is quantified into a kind of
realizable invisible capital, namely "emotional currency", which has the characteristics of being accumulable,
consumable, depletable and depreciable. Meanwhile, the essence of new media marketing is profit-driven
marketing. Under over-commercialization and marketing exploitation, digital capital, leveraging mechanisms
such as algorithmic recommendation and traffic monetization, continuously induces irrational consumption
and creates false demands, deeply permeating the material and spiritual worlds of Gen Z youth, and constantly
accelerating the consumption and depletion of emotional currency [2]. As a result, emotional consumption is
reduced to an appendage of capital appreciation.

production of affect and the private ownership of capital, and authentic affect will always conflict with the

logic of capital accumulation [9]. In the era of digital platforms, the traffic logic is the concrete manifestation
of this contradiction. Commercial platforms are underpinned by traffic and market expansion; when the traffic
logic becomes the core guiding principle of communication activities, other inherent values will be
systematically eroded [10]. Traffic algorithms convert users' emotional engagement into quantifiable data
indicators. The pure emotional connection between people is gradually replaced by commodity exchange
relationships. Thus, in the new media presentation of offline cultural and entertainment activities, algorithms
favor content with high viral potential, while the audience's real on-site feelings that cannot be converted into

authenticity of emotion, laying hidden dangers for the dissolution of the symbolic value of IP derivatives.
4.3. Emotional overdraft and the dissolution of symbolic value of IP derivatives
Against the backdrop of traffic‑driven marketing continuously squeezing and quantifying emotional value,
emotional experiences become commoditized and assigned monetary values, raising the risk that consumers
equate purchasing goods with satisfying emotional needs [11]. The originally vivid offline experience is
reduced to consumption records and commodities, thus obscuring the rich connotations of genuine emotion.
When this logic is applied to offline‑performance‑related IP derivatives, such alienation manifests as the
alienation of derivatives' emotional value. Derivatives carry the collective memories formed by music fans
on‑site. However, the intervention of new‑media marketing means IP‑derivative consumption is no longer
limited to commemorative significance; instead, it is systematically constructed as a necessary path for
emotional performance. Platforms amplify merchandise's symbolic value via algorithmic recommendations,
KOL‑led content marketing and flash sales, framing "purchasing merchandise" as an indispensable part of
ritual participation. Under the influence of this communication mechanism, the focus of some music fans'
emotional expression has shifted toward consumption behavior.
5. Value appreciation and overdraft: a reflection on the mechanism of new
media marketing
5.1. Symbiosis mechanism of value addition and overdraft
New media marketing, while driving the appreciation of emotional currency in music festivals, carries hidden
risks of emotional overdraft within its commercial and traffic‑driven logic. Both appreciation and overdraft
stem from the same communication‑commercial system and coexist inherently. By leveraging the advantages
of two‑way interaction and low‑cost dissemination enabled by new‑media platforms, marketing practices are
transformed into refined, operation‑oriented personalized services, which achieve the synchronous
improvement of IP communication effect and conversion efficiency [12]. Nevertheless, the traffic‑oriented
logic of new media marketing bears inherent flaws. Some marketing entities treat audience emotions merely as
tools for gaining traffic and fail to provide sustained emotional value. Marketing campaigns continuously
mobilize audience emotional perceptions and convert user emotions into resources for communication and
monetization. When commercialized emotional mobilization depletes music fans' emotional investment, and
on‑site experiences and content output fail to replenish new emotional value, the accumulated emotional
currency will keep diminishing and trigger the risk of emotional overdraft.

5.2. Homology of new media mechanisms
The new media marketing system shows obvious mechanism homology. The appreciation and overdraft of
emotional currency in offline cultural and entertainment scenarios do not stem from two unrelated
communication paths, but from the symbiotic relationship formed by relying on the same set of operating logic
of new media. Algorithm-driven distribution, niche diffusion, user UGC participation and emotional resonance
as communication mechanisms can help offline cultural and entertainment activities achieve atmosphere
spread and fan group gathering, thereby completing the sedimentation of emotional value and promoting the
continuous accumulation of emotional currency. But this mechanism itself highly relies on emotional arousal,
high-frequency exposure and traffic conversion. When operations move toward excessive commercialization,
the same communication logic will give rise to a large amount of homogenized content, which continuously
consumes the audience's emotional resources, treating fans' affection and empathy as tools for traffic
monetization, thereby triggering audience emotional burnout and emotional currency depletion. Value
increment and overdraft originate from the same root and accompany each other, constituting an inherent
structural contradiction in new media entertainment marketing.
6. Conclusion
This paper conducts research on the new media marketing of offline cultural and entertainment derivatives,
and discusses the value appreciation and overdraft risks of emotional currency in this context. Studies show
that the appreciation and overdraft of emotional currency come from the same set of operating logic of new
media, and the two are in a symbiotic relationship. UGC content creation and communication can accumulate
emotional currency, but after excessive commercialization, the audience's emotional resources will also be
continuously consumed, and the symbolic value of IP derivatives. This study has certain limitations and areas
for improvement. First, this article mainly conducts discussions based on literature review and phenomenon
analysis, with qualitative research as the main method, lacking empirical data support. Future research could
incorporate quantitative methods such as questionnaire surveys to measure the cycle of change in emotional
currency. Secondly, the concept of offline cultural entertainment is relatively broad, and actually includes
forms such as concerts, stage plays, and art exhibitions. This article does not make specific subdivisions, nor
does it conduct horizontal comparisons between projects of different categories, which leads to certain
limitations in the general applicability of the research results. Future research may expand scenarios and refine
the application of emotional currency in new media marketing.
