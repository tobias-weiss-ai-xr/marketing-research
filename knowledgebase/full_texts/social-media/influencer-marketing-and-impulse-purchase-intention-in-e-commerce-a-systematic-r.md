Abstrak

Influencer media sosial kini menjadi aktor penting dalam e -commerce dan social commerce,
tetapi bukti tentang cara mereka memicu pembelian impulsif tersebar di berbagai platform,
konstruk, dan teori. Tinjauan sistematis terbaru membahas pembelian impulsif pada e -
commerce live streaming, sehingga pembelian impulsif akibat influencer di luar live streaming,
serta studi yang terbit setelah pertengahan 2024, belum terangkum. Penelitian ini bertujuan
memetakan kajian tentang influencer media sosial serta perilak u dan niat pembelian impulsif
dalam e -commerce dan social commerce, sekaligus merumuskan agenda penelitian
mendatang. Mengikuti pedoman PRISMA 2020, pencarian dilakukan pada ScienceDirect,
Scopus, ProQuest, dan EBSCOhost untuk artikel jurnal berbahasa Inggris yang telah ditelaah
sejawat dan terbit daring pertama kali pada 2020 –2025. Kualitas metodologis dinilai dengan
Mixed Methods Appraisal Tool. Sebanyak 28 studi dalam korpus kerja dikodekan secara
deskriptif. Publikasi mencapai puncak pada 2025. Live streaming menjadi konteks yang paling
sering diteliti (11 studi), sedangkan 15 studi meneliti konteks lain; Instagram, YouTube, TikTok
Shop, dan Facebook commerce masing-masing hanya diwakili satu studi. Kerangka stimulus–
organisme–respons merupakan lensa teo ri yang paling umum (sedikitnya tujuh studi).
Pembacaan tematik awal menunjukkan bahwa penelitian berfokus pada atribut dan kredibilitas
influencer atau streamer, mekanisme relasional dan emosional seperti kepercayaan, interaksi
parasosial, dan penularan emosi, serta fitur konten atau konteks. Bidang ini terpusat pada live
streaming dan satu teori dominan. Penelitian mendatang perlu menelaah platform non -live,
definisi luaran, integrasi teori, dan dampak lanjutan pembelian impulsif. Temuan masih bersifat
sementara sampai pengodean teks lengkap selesai.

Abstract

Social media influencers have become prominent agents in e -commerce and social
commerce, yet evidence on how they trigger impulse buying is dispersed across platforms,
constructs, and theories. A recent review addressed impulse buying in live streaming e -

commerce, leaving influencer -driven impulse buying beyond live streaming, and studies
published after mid -2024, largely unsynthesised. This systematic literature review therefore
maps research on social media influencers and impulse buying behaviour and in tention in e-

2020, ScienceDirect, Scopus, ProQuest, and EBSCOhost were searched for peer -reviewed,

attributes and credibility, relational and emotional mechanisms such as trust, parasocial
interaction, and emotional contagion, and content or context features. The field is concentrated
on live streaming and one dominant theory. Future research should address non-live platforms,
outcome definitions, theoretical integration, and the downstream consequences of impulse
buying. Findings remain provisional pending full-text coding.

LATAR BELAKANG
Social commerce brings together the interactive, community-oriented features of social
media and the transactional capabilities of e -commerce, embedding user-generated content,
peer recommendations, and real-time engagement into the purchasing process (Dwivedi et al.,
2023; Jadil et al., 2023). Such socially enriched interactions foster consumer trust and, in turn,
the intention to buy (Hajli, 2015), while e-commerce more broadly allows firms to communicate,
promote, and transact with consumers online (Išoraitė & Miniotienė, 2018) . Within this
environment, social media influencers have become prominent marketing agents. They are
individuals who use social media to shape how communities engage with products, ideas, and
behaviours, and they may be sponsored by firms or self-identified (Hahn, 2024) [to be verified:
definition against the full text of Hahn (2024)]. Influencer marketing is a rapidly growing
industry, even though the literature still lacks consensus on how influencers should be
conceptualised (Malik et al., 2023; Sim et al., 2023) (Malik et al., 2023; Sim et al., 2023).
This development matters because influencer content and live interaction expose
consumers to stimuli that can prompt unplanned purchases. Impulse buying is a sudden
purchase made without prior planning or careful consideration (Karbasivar & Yarahmadi, 2011;
Stern, 1962); it is typically triggered by exposure to stimuli and is regarded as a predominantly
hedonic behaviour that departs from the normal shopping process (Parboteeah et al., 2009).
When the stimulus comes from a trusted and engaging influencer, the path from exposure to
purchase can be short, which raises commercial as well as consumer -welfare questions,
including overconsumption (D. Lee & Wan, 2023). The central academic problem is therefore

to explain how, and under what conditions, the influencer as a person, rather than the shopping
channel alone, converts exposure into unplanned buying.
Research on this problem has grown rapidly but along fragmented lines. Studies have
examined influencer or streamer characteristics and persuasiveness (Nguyen et al., 2025;
Tran et al., 2025), message and media credibility and trust (Shamim et al., 2024; Shamim &
Islam, 2022) , and relational and emotional mechanisms such as parasocial interaction
(Vazquez et al., 2020)  and emotional contagion (L. Li et al., 2024) . Many studies adopt the
stimulus–organism–response (S-O-R) framework(Ahmad & Lilani, 2025; Lin et al., 2023), while
others draw on signalling theory (Shamim & Islam, 2022), the heuristic–systematic model (Xin
et al., 2024), and match-up perspectives (Zhang & Zhang, 2024). The influencer is labelled as
an influencer, streamer, e-commerce anchor, key opinion leader, or internet celebrity, and the
outcome is framed variously as impulse buying behaviour, intention, or urge to buy, which
makes findings difficult to accumulate.
Prior synthesis has not resolved this fragmentation. The most recent systematic review,
by (Y. Li et al., 2025), synthesised 40 empirical, quantitative studies of impulse buying in live
streaming e-commerce published in journals indexed in Web of Science or Scopus, using the
S-O-R model as its organising framework. Studies set in other e -commerce or social
commerce contexts fell outside its scope, and its corpus extends only to studies published in
the first seven months of 2024. Within that corpus, two -thirds of the studies adopted S -O-R
and 31 of the 40 focused on China (Y. Li et al., 2025). Influencer-driven impulse buying outside
live streaming, as well as the most recent work, therefore remains unsynthesised. In the
working corpus of the present review, 15 of the 28 studies do not identify live streaming as
their research context (Table 2), 12 are cited as 2025 or 2026 journal issues, and only five (C.-
H. Lee & Chen, 2021; L. Li et al., 2024; Lin et al., 2023; Shao, 2024; Yan et al., 2023) are also
cited in the synthesis by Li et al. (2025).
A systematic literature review is therefore warranted to consolidate this dispersed
evidence from the perspective of the influencer rather than the channel. The novelty of the
present review lies in taking the social media influencer or streamer as its foc al construct
across live and non -live e-commerce and social commerce settings, regardless of research
design, in covering studies first published online up to 31 December 2025, and in drawing on
ScienceDirect, Scopus, ProQuest, and EBSCOhost. Its objective is to map research on social
media influencers and impulse buying behaviour and intention and to propose a future
research agenda. It is guided by three research questions. RQ1: What is the profile of research
on influencer -driven impulse buying in e -commerce and social commerce in terms of
publication trend, commerce context, and theoretical lens? RQ2: Which determinants and
mechanisms of impulse buying does this literature examine? RQ3: What gaps and directions
for future research emerge? The review contributes in three ways: it extends the evidence
base beyond live streaming and beyond mid -2024, it maps how influencers, outcomes,
contexts, and theories are conceptualised across studies, and it proposes a research agenda
that responds to the gaps identified.

METODE
 This study adopted a systematic literature review (SLR) approach guided by the
Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta -Analyses (PRISMA) 2020
statement. PRISMA 2020 was selected for its clear, systematic, and transparent framework for
identifying, screening, and synthesising relevant literature, thereby ensuring methodological
rigour and reproducibility (Sohrabi et al., 2021). It was guided by the three research questions
(RQ1–RQ3) set out in the Introduction.
Information Sources and Search Strategy
 ScienceDirect was used as the primary database owing to its extensive coverage of
peer-reviewed research. To reduce the likelihood of omitting pertinent studies, secondary
searches were conducted in Scopus, ProQuest, and EBSCOhost. All searches were
performed on.
 The following search string was applied to titles, abstracts, and keywords: ("influencer"
OR "social media influencer" OR "streamer") AND ("social commerce" OR "e-commerce") AND
("impulse buying" OR "impulse purchase" OR "impulse buying intention"). To bro aden
coverage, supplementary searches combined additional terms representing each core
construct. For the influencer construct, these terms were key opinion leader (KOL), digital
celebrities, streamer characteristics, livestreamer, and social media influen cer. For the
commerce construct, they were live commerce, livestreaming commerce, TikTok Shop, social
media, digital commerce, digital marketing, and social media e -commerce platforms. For the
impulse buying construct, they were impulsive behaviour, impulsive purchase behaviour, buy
impulsively, and impulse buying behaviour. Records retrieved through these supplementary
searches were.
Eligibility Criteria
 Studies were assessed against predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria covering
document type, language, timeframe, influencer construct, outcome construct, commerce
context, and determinant scope (Table 1). Eligibility with respect to timeframe was determined
by the date of first online publication, whereas studies are cited by their journal issue year in
accordance with APA style. Articles first published online in 2025 and subsequently assigned
to a 2026 journal issue were therefore eligible, whereas articles first published online after 31
December 2025 were excluded.

Selection Process
 The selection process followed the PRISMA 2020 flow structure of identification,
screening, eligibility assessment, and inclusion (Figure 1). Searches in ScienceDirect, Scopus,
ProQuest, and EBSCOhost yielded records, from which duplicates were removed. A total of
records were screened by title and abstract, reports were assessed in full text, and 28 studies
were included in the final analysis . The numbers of records excluded and the reasons for
exclusion at each stage are reported in Figure 1.
 All stages of the review, including screening, eligibility assessment, data extraction,
and quality appraisal, were conducted by a single reviewer. The use of a single reviewer
increases the risk of selection and extraction error and is acknowledged as a limitation of this
review.

Thematic Overview (Preliminary)
 A preliminary reading of titles and abstracts suggests three clusters. The first concerns
influencer or streamer attributes and credibility, including streamer characteristics (Nguyen et
al., 2025), the persuasiveness of key opinion leaders (Tran et al., 2025), e-commerce anchors’
characteristics (Lin et al., 2023), and message and media credibility (Shamim & Islam, 2022).
The second concerns the relational and psychological mechanisms through which exposure
becomes buying, such as trust (Shamim & Azam, 2025)  parasocial interaction and narrative
involvement (Vazquez et al., 2020), attachment and congruence (Shao, 2024), flow state and
perceived informativeness (Ullah et al., 2023) , customer inspiration (Yang et al., 2024), and
emotional versus rational pathways (C. Chen et al., 2026) ). The third concerns content and
context features, including live product display versus social interaction (Xin et al., 2024),
influencer–product match-ups (Zhang & Zhang, 2024), and negative bullet comments (Zheng
et al., 2026). This grouping rests on titles and abstracts only and must be confirmed through
full-text coding.

DISCUSSION
Interpretation of Findings and Answers to the Research Questions
 Regarding RQ1, the corpus is concentrated in live streaming and in a single theoretical
lens: live streaming is explicit in 11 studies and S -O-R in at least seven, whereas Instagram,
YouTube, TikTok Shop, and Facebook commerce are represented by one study each.
Knowledge about influencer -driven impulse buying is therefore shaped largely by the live
streaming context. Regarding RQ2, the preliminary thematic reading indicates that research
explains impulse buying through who the influencer is (attributes and credibility), how
consumers relate to the influencer (trust, parasocial interaction, attachment, emotional
contagion), and what the shopping environment offers (product display, interaction, comments,
and fit). RQ3 is addressed in the final subsection. Bec ause method, geography, and theory
remain to be verified from full texts, these answers are provisional.
Comparison with Previous Literature
 The prominence of S -O-R in the present corpus mirrors the findings of (Y. Li et al.,
2025), in whose live streaming corpus two -thirds of the studies adopted that framework,
suggesting that S-O-R has become a default lens in this area. The two reviews nevertheless
differ in scope and evidence base. (Y. Li et al., 2025) restricted their synthesis to quantitative
studies of live streaming e-commerce indexed in Web of Science or Scopus and published up

Why Findings May Differ Across Studies
 Several features of the corpus may explain why studies are difficult to compare.
Contexts range from live streaming to Instagram, YouTube, and Facebook commerce, and
product categories from beauty and food to luxury fashion and tourism. The influencer is
variously a streamer, anchor, key opinion leader, micro - or macro -influencer, or internet
celebrity, roles that differ in reach and in the relationship  they build with followers. Outcomes
also differ, from impulse buying behaviour to intention and urge to buy, and studies rely on
different theories and, probably, on different designs and national settings. Until these
dimensions are coded from full texts , apparent inconsistencies cannot be attributed to any
single source.
Theoretical Implications
 First, the dominance of S-O-R suggests that the field relies largely on a stimulus-and-
response logic. Combining it with dual -process or other complementary theories, as some
studies already do (C. Chen et al., 2026; Xin et al., 2024), could clarify when influencer stimuli
lead to affective rather than deliberative routes to purchase. Second, the many labels for the
influencer and for the outcome point to a need for clearer construct definitions, in line with the
observation that the c onceptualisation of influencers lacks consensus (Malik et al., 2023) .
Third, treating impulse buying as an outcome of the influencer rather than of the channel invites
theories that locate the source of the stimulus in the person.
Practical Implications
 For brands, the corpus suggests that selecting an influencer involves more than reach,
since credibility, perceived fit with the product, and the quality of interaction recur as themes
(Shamim & Islam, 2022; Xin et al., 2024; Zhang & Zhang, 2024) . Brands working outside live
streaming should not assume that findings from live commerce transfer directly to Instagram,
YouTube, or Facebook commerce. For platforms and policymakers, evidence of
overconsumption in some contexts(D. Lee & Wan, 2023) may support transparent commercial
disclosure and design choices that help consumers pause before buying. These implications
are tentative given the title- and abstract-based coding.
Contribution, Limitations, and Future Research Agenda
 This review contributes a broader, influencer -centred map of the literature than live -
streaming-specific syntheses, together with a structured set of directions for further work.
Future research could compare influencer -driven impulse buying across live and non -live
platforms; harmonise how the influencer an d the impulse buying outcome are defined and
measured; combine S -O-R with other theories to test boundary conditions; examine
downstream consequences such as overconsumption, which only one title in the corpus
addresses (D. Lee & Wan, 2023) ; and report research design and geographic context more
explicitly so that studies can be compared. The review has limitations. Screening, extraction,

SIMPULAN
Research on social media influencers and impulse buying is growing but concentrated
in live streaming and the stimulus –organism–response framework, with influencer attributes,
trust-related mechanisms, and content features as the main explanations. Non-live platforms,
inconsistent construct definitions, and downstream consequences remain under -studied.
Brands should therefore choose influencers for credibility and product fit rather than reach
alone, and should not assume that live-commerce findings transfer to other platforms. Future
studies should compare platforms, harmonise measures, integrate complementary theories,
and examine overconsumption. Findings remain provisional until full-text coding is complete.
