Abstract
To address the intense competition within the Shopee Indonesia skincare market, this study
aims to apply the Stimulus-Organism-Response (S-O-R) theory to examine how social media
marketing, affiliate marketing, and online reviews collectively affect overal l marketing
performance. Utilizing a quantitative approach, data were gathered through an online survey
of 127 purposively sampled Shopee skincare consumers and analyzed using Partial Least
Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM). The results reveal  that while affiliate
marketing directly and positively enhances both online reviews and marketing performance,
social media marketing significantly influences only online reviews. Furthermore, online
reviews significantly drive overall marketing performance and act as a crucial partial mediator
between social media marketing and marketing outcomes, though they do not significantly
mediate the impact of affiliate marketing. Theoretically, this research validates the
applicability of the S -O-R framework with in digital skincare marketing. Practically, the
findings imply that to translate digital engagement into tangible marketing success, businesses
must strategically deploy affiliate partnerships while actively managing and optimizing
customer online reviews as a vital bridge between social media efforts and final performance.

1. INTRODUCTION
Major shifts have occurred in marketing as a result of the expansion of the digital economy on a global scale. The
proliferation of mobile internet access and internet -connected devices has spurred a move away from analog marketing
strategies toward digita l ones Dwivedi et al., (2021) , who contend that widespread internet access and social media have
revolutionized business practices and customer habits, opening up enormous possibilities for cutting costs, raising brand
recognition, and boosting revenue. Nowhere is this shift more visi ble than in Indonesia’s e -commerce sector, where Shopee
has become the dominant platform for skincare products, commanding an 80.96% share of the facial moisturizer category and
80.71% of the serum and essence category in the third quarter of 2024 Magpie, (2024). Among the digital marketing tools
driving this growth, social media marketing (SMM) and affiliate marketing have proven to be key factors in marketing
performance, particularly for e -commerce businesses Tatik & Setiawan, (2024) ; Liadeli et al., (2022) ; by 2031 the global
affiliate marketing industry alone is projected to exceed USD 31 billion, with the fastest growth expected in the Asia -Pacific
region.
Despite this growth, the effectiveness of SMM and affiliate marketing in tangibly improving marketing performance as
opposed to simply purchase intention still requires empirical validation, since existing evidence remains incomplete and does
not yet expla in the underlying mechanism in a measurable way. This gap is especially consequential in Shopee’s skincare
industry, where competition is intense and no brand holds a permanent position: understanding what actually converts digital
marketing activity into durable marketing performance is therefore a critical, unresolved question for skincare businesses
operating on the platform.
The scale of the problem is evident in the numbers. Indonesia has more than 221 million internet users and is one of
Southeast Asia’s leading e-commerce markets, valued at an estimated USD 125 billion by 2027. Its skincare market alone was
valued at approximately USD 3.21 billion in 2024, projected to grow at a CAGR of 9.4% Meiyume, (2024). Yet brand rankings
are highly volatile: in the facial skincare Top Brand Index (TBI), Garnier led with 12.10% and Wardah with 11.80%, while
Ponds, Glow and Lovely, Citra, and Skintific each held 5.80% (Frontier Research, 2024). In e-commerce market share for facial
moisturizers (January–July 2024), The Originote led with 7.8%, followed by Skintific (6.1%), Glad2Glow (5.1%), Ms Glow
(3.6%), and Wardah (3.5%) (Databoks, 2024). In serums and essences, Skintific led with 10.8%, followed by Elformula (7%),
Y.O.U (4.2%), The Originote (4.2%), and Skin1004 (3.6%) in the third quarter of 2024 Magpie, (2024) . Interestingly,
Skintific’s share declined over the quarter, opening space for Glad2Glow, Somethinc, and Y.O.U to strengthen their positions.
This constant reshuffling confirms that no brand enjoys permanent dominance in Shopee Indonesia’s skincare industry, making
the effectiveness of digital marketing strategy a decisive factor separating high performers from their competitors.
Online reviews sit at the center of this dynamic. As a form of eWOM, online reviews significantly affect consumer
choices in e -commerce Feng et al., (2023) , and the credibility of information on social media including eWOM positively
affects purchase intention for skincare products, with trust in the online community acting as an important intermediary Ranjith
et al., (2025). Because skincare consumers rely heavily on others’ experiences before buying, online reviews function as a risk-
reduction mechanism that links digital marketing activity to final purchasing choices, and by extension, to marketing
performance.
This phenomenon can be explained through Uses and Gratifications Theory (UGT), which describes how users turn to
digital media to obtain relevant information, and the S -O-R Theory, which explains how digital marketing stimuli shape
consumer reactions through an evaluation process. Under the S-O-R model, marketing performance is the end result of a chain
reaction that begins with social media marketing and affiliate marketing as stimuli, continues through online reviews as the
organism representing the consumer’s internal evaluation including the formation of trust and risk perceptions and culminates
in marketing performance as the response. These three factors interact with one another to determine the efficacy of digital
marketing; UGT and S-O-R theories, combined, provide a theoretical framework for understanding this relationship.
Several independent studies support parts of this chain. Gunawan et al., (2024)  found that on the Shopee Indonesia

performance encompassing sales growth, market share, and brand value remains insufficiently analyzed for skincare e -

in relation to marketing performance has received limited empirical attention, even though, in theory, online reviews serve a s
a link between digital m arketing activities and market outcomes. Third, no study has comprehensively integrated these three
concepts into a single mediation model focused on the skincare industry on the Shopee platform. Underlying all three is a more
fundamental theoretical gap: previous studies have mostly treated online reviews as a standalone independent or moderating
variable, and have therefore not fully explained the transmission process of influence from digital marketing stimuli, throug h
consumer evaluation in the form of online reviews, to the formation of marketing performance as the final response. It is this
gap that underscores the need for an empirical test of this mediation mechanism.
Despite these caveats, this study is novel in that it fills a gap in the literature by investigating the role of online reviews
as a mediator between affiliate marketing, social media marketing, and the success of skincare brands’ Shopee Indonesia
campaigns. Theoretically, it positions online reviews as an organism that mediates the correlations among the stimuli of social
media marketing and affiliate marketing, thereby extending the S -O-R Theory, and offers an integrated model linking social
media marketi ng, affiliate marketing, online reviews, and marketing performance within the skincare business on Shopee
Indonesia a combination that has not previously been tested together in this context.
Accordingly, this study aims to provide digital skincare businesses with theoretical insight and evidence-based strategic
advice by examining the effect of social media marketing, affiliate marketing, and online reviews on marketing performance
on the Shopee Indonesia platform. More specifically, it seeks to explain how this process occurs namely, through consumers’
evaluation of online reviews rather than merely testing whether such an effect exists.

2. LITERATURE REVIEW
2.1 Stimulus-Organism-Response (S-O-R) Theory
This research is grounded in the Stimulus -Organism-Response (S-O-R) Theory, first proposed by (Mehrabian & Russell,
1974) and since extensively developed in the fields of consumer behavior and digital marketing. The theory explains that stimuli
from the ex ternal environment are received and processed by an organism in this case, the consumer thereby generating a
specific behavioral response. Unlike purely stimulus-response frameworks, S-O-R explicitly inserts an internal evaluative stage

between exposure and outcome, which makes it particularly suited to situations where consumers do not react to marketing
stimuli automatically, but instead form judgments before responding.
S-O-R theory is deliberately chosen for this study over simpler behavioral models for three reasons. First, digital marketing
on Shopee through social media content and affiliate recommendations rarely converts directly into purchase or brand
performance; consumers typically pass through an evaluative step in which they weigh the credibility of the information they
receive, a process S-O-R is specifically designed to capture. Second, S-O-R has already been validated in closely related digital
and e-commerce marketing contexts, including social media marketing activities in e -commerce Sohaib et al., (2022) , live-
streaming shopping Rahmadini  Yolanda, (2024), and consumer response to online reviews and ratings Rachmiani et al., (2024),
which supports its applicability to the present skincare -on-Shopee setting. Third, because this study's central concern is not
merely whether social media marketing and affiliate marketing affect marketing performance, but how that effect occurs, a
framework that models an intermediate organism state is more appropriate than a direct stimulus -response model that would
treat the relationship as a single, unmediated link.
Within the S -O-R model of this research, “stimulus” (S) refers to inputs from the digital marketing environment in the
form of social media marketing and affiliate marketing. Social media marketing activities carried out by skincare brands on
Shopee such as creating informative content, collaborating with beauty content creators, interacting with consumers through
live commerce, and providing transparent product information serve as communication stimuli for consumers. Affiliate
marketing, meanwhile, functi ons as a stimulus grounded in interpersonal trust, delivered through recommendations from
affiliates across various digital platforms.
The “organism” (O) in S-O-R theory refers to an individual's internal state that mediates the relationship between stimulus
and response, encompassing cognitive, affective, and evaluative dimensions. This study positions online reviews as that
organism, and this positioning requires explicit justification rather than assumption. Online reviews differ fundamentally from
social media marketing and affiliate marketing in origin and function: whereas SMM and affiliate marketing are stimuli that
originate externally from the brand or its affiliates, online reviews are consumer-generated evaluative content that a prospective
buyer must actively read, judge for credibility, and weigh against their own risk perceptions before it can influence behavio r
Cheung & Thadani, (2012) . This act of interpreting and synthesizing other users' experiences is itself an internal cognitive -
affective process precisely the kind of processing the S-O-R model assigns to the organism stage rather than a further external
stimulus or a final outcome . When a brand demonstrates credible and consistent digital marketing activity, consumers do not
respond to that activity directly; instead, they process it alongside reviews from fellow users, forming a collective judgment of
the brand's quality, trustworthiness, and risk that only then translates into a behavioral response Rachmiani et al., (2024). It is
this internal evaluative state not the reviews as a standalone communication channel that functions as the bridge between digital
marketing stimuli and consumer responses, and it is this mechanism that justifies testing online reviews as a mediator  rather
than as a second independent stimulus.
The “response” (R) is the reaction that arises from the organism's processing of a stimulus. In this study, that response is
the marketing performance of skincare brands on Shopee Indonesia, encompassing increases in sales volume (GMV), market
share, and brand position in the Top Brand Index (TBI). Marketing performance is thus modeled as the end product of the S -
O-R process: the measurable outcome of digital marketing stimuli social media marketing and affiliate marketing being
processed by the organism, o nline reviews, before manifesting as a market response. Prior applications of S -O-R theory in
digital marketing research have shown that this chain is capable of explaining how e-commerce platform elements function as
stimuli that increase consumer engagement and ultimately generate a marketing performance response Sohaib et al., (2022).

attainment of its marketing goals. These goals take many forms, but the four most common are sales, market share, the
acquisition of new custo mers, and brand equity Morgan et al., (2002) . In the skincare e -commerce industry on Shopee

the measurable outcome of a process in which digital marketing stimuli (social media marketing and affiliate marketing) are
processed by the organism (online reviews) to produce a tangible effect on a brand's competitive position in the market. Nursal
et al., (2022)  concluded that social media marketing and affiliate marketing together are key determinants of marketing
performance because they enhance promotional effectiveness, market reach, and sales.
2.3 Social Media Marketing
Theoretically, social media marketing (SMM) is defined as a company's use of social media to advertise its products, foster
customer relationships, and accomplish other marketing objectives Tuten & Solomon, (2018). It allows skincare firms to reach
a large audience efficiently and measurably, particularly on platforms like Shopee, through activities such as educational
content on active ingredients, collaborations with beauty creators, live commerce broadcasts, and  community management on
Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube. Within the S -O-R model established in Section 2.1, SMM functions as a stimulus (S) that
consumers receive through these social media channels.
Conceptually, SMM is expected to relate to both marketing performance and online reviews, but through two different
mechanisms. As a stimulus, consistent and informative SMM can build direct communication between brand and consumer
that translates into engagement, purchase, and ultimately marketing performance. At the same time, SMM can also shape the
organism stage of the S-O-R chain: when a brand's content is credible, consistent, and interactive, consumers are more inclined
to try the product and document their experience in the form of an online review, meaning SMM does not only act directly on
performance but also feeds into the evaluative process that online reviews represent.
Both mechanisms are supported by prior research. Tatik & Setiawan, (2024) found that Indonesian MSMEs experienced a
notable improvement in performance after implementing SMM, and a meta-analysis by Liadeli et al., (2022) demonstrated that
brand-owned social media communication increases sales.  Cao & Weerawardena, (2023) further showed that the strategic use
of social media strengthens marketing performance by building brand management capability. On the review -generation side,
Dwivedi et al., (2021) note that social and digital marketing activity shapes the volume and nature of electronic word-of-mouth
that consumers subsequently generate, indicating that SMM's influence extends into the evaluative content consumers produce,
not only into direct sales outcomes.

2.4 Affiliate Marketing
Theoretically, affiliate marketing is a form of performance -based marketing in which a brand partners with another party
(the affiliate) to promote its products or services in exchange for a share of the resulting sales Frost & Strauss, (2016) . Within
Shopee Indonesia's ecosystem, affiliate marketing operates chiefly through the Shopee Affiliate Program, which allows content
creators, beauty bloggers, and micro -influencers to share product links; globally, the affiliate marketing industry is p rojected
to exceed USD 31 billion by 2031, with the Asia-Pacific region expected to grow fastest. In the S-O-R model, affiliate marketing
functions as a stimulus (S) grounded in interpersonal trust, delivered through recommendations on various digital platforms.
As with SMM, affiliate marketing is conceptually linked to both marketing performance and online reviews. Because
affiliate recommendations carry interpersonal trust that consumers extend to the affiliate rather than to the brand directly, they

2.5 Online Review
Theoretically, an online review is an experience -based evaluation that consumers voluntarily share via digital platforms
regarding products or services they have used Cheung & Thadani, (2012) . As established in Section 2.1, online reviews are
positioned in this study as the organism (O) in the S-O-R model the internal, evaluative state that consumers construct from the
stimuli of social media marketing and affiliate marketing before arriving a t a behavioral response. This positioning is central
to the study's contribution: rather than treating online reviews as a standalone independent variable, this study tests them as the
mechanism through which digital marketing stimuli are converted into marketing performance.
Conceptually, online reviews are expected to relate to marketing performance in two ways. Directly, accumulated reviews
function as a trust and quality signal that lowers perceived risk for prospective buyers and thereby supports sales, market share,
and brand position independent of any single marketing campaign. Indirectly, because online reviews sit at the organism stage
of the S-O-R chain, they are expected to mediate the effect of both social media marketing and affiliate marketing on marketing
performance: the stimuli alone are only converted into a market response once they have been processed into consumer -
generated evaluative content that other prospective buyers can trust.
This dual role is supported by both market evidence and prior studies. Brands that have successfully dominated the market,
such as Skintific (10.8% share for serums) and The Originote (7.8% share for moisturizers), are known to have accumulated a
large number of positive reviews with an average rating above four stars (Muhamad, 2024; Intelligence, 2024), while brands
with less favorable reviews tend to experience market share erosion despite comparable levels of SMM and affiliate activity.
At the empirical level, Feng et al., (2023) show that online reviews, as a form of eWOM, significantly affect consumer decisions
in e-commerce; Liu et al., (2020)  found that responding to online reviews positively affects e -commerce sales performance;
and Ranjith et al., (2025) show that the credibility of eWOM on social media increases purchase intention for skincare products
specifically, with trust in the online community acting as an intermediary. Taken together, these findings support both a direct
effect of online reviews on marketing performance and a mediating role between digital marketing stimuli and marketing
performance.
H5: Online reviews have a positive effect on marketing performance.
H6: Online reviews mediate the effect of social media marketing on marketing performance.
H7: Online reviews mediate the effect of affiliate marketing on marketing performance.

3. RESEARCH METHOD
3.1 Research Sample
All Indonesians who have ever bought skincare goods on Shopee Indonesia are considered part of the study's population.
This research made use of purposive sampling, a non -probability sampling technique. It involves picking a sample based on
specified criteria that are relevant to the study's objectives, since the population is infinite and cannot be accurately identified.
This method is in line with other research that has used purposive sampling to examine the S -O-R model in the context of e -
commerce and digital marketing Rahmadini  Yolanda, (2024); Rachmiani et al., (2024).
The respondent criteria established in this study include: (1) being at least 17 years old and having the authority to conduct
online transactions independently; (2) having purchased at least one skincare product on Shopee Indonesia within the last six
months; (3) having been exposed to social media marketing activities by skincare brands, whether through Instagram, TikTok,
or YouTube, and/or having made a purchase via an affiliate marketing link; and (4) having read online reviews of a product
before deciding to buy. The researchers made sure that the people who filled out the surveys had first -hand knowledge of the
organisms, stimuli, and response variables that were part of this study by establishing these criteria.
According to the standard procedure for SEM-PLS, the sample size was determined to be ten times the number of indicators
that make up a single latent variable. Hair et al., (2022). The sample size for this study was determined using the 10 times rule
guideline in Partial Least Squares -Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM). According to Hair et al., (2022) , the
recommended minimum sample size is 10 times the largest number of formative indicators, or 10 times the largest number of
structural paths leading to a single latent construct. In practice, however, the most commonly used approach is 10 times the
number of initial indicators before screening based on outer loading values.
Based on the initial measurement model, this study used a total of 13 indicators/items. Accordingly, the minimum required
sample size was 10 × 13 = 130 respondents. Given the limitations in access to and availability of respondents during data
collection, the number of samples successfully collected in this study was 127 respondents. This number is close to the
minimum requirement and is considered sufficiently representative for data analysis using PLS -SEM Hair et al., (2022) .The
data gathering phase lasted around one month, and it involved administering an online questionnaire (GForm) to user groups
on Shopee and social media regarding skincare.

each indication, all variables were measured using a “five -point Likert scale, with 1 representing strongly disagree and 5
strongly agree”.
Social media marketing was measured based on the definition by ElSayad & Md Saad, (2024) , by adapting the five
dimensions of Social Media Marketing Activities that have been widely used in previous S-O-R theory-based research Yuniarti

3.3 Data Analysis Techniques
Using SmartPLS software, the acquired data were analyzed using SEM -PLS. This method was selected for this research
due to its applicability to both exploratory and predictive purposes, its capability to manage complicated structural models with
mediating v ariables, and its adaptability to data that does not necessarily adhere to a normal distribution assumption and
relatively moderate sample sizes Hair et al., (2022).
Both the outer (measuring) and inner (structural) models were evaluated during the model evaluation process. Tests for
convergent validity were conducted using outer loadings (>0.7) and AVE (>0.5) in the evaluation of the outer model. Tests for
discriminant validity were conducted using the Fornell -Larcker criterion and the Heterotrait -Monotrait Ratio (HTMT <0.90).
Construct reliability was checked using Cronbach's Alpha and Composite Reliability. A threshold of 0.7 or more is considered
appropriate Hair et al., (2022).
A number of metrics were used to evaluate the internal model, including the coefficient of determination (R²), predictive
relevance (Q²), and effect size (f²), which measure the relative contribution of each exogenous variable. These metrics were
used to g auge the extent to which the exogenous variables could explain the endogenous variables. Hypothesis testing was

mediation, in accordance with standard procedures used in SEM-PLS analysis Hair et al., (2022).

4.2 DISCUSSION
Taken together, the seven hypothesis tests reveal a consistent pattern: the two stimuli in this study, Social Media Marketing
and Affiliate Marketing, do not translate into Marketing Performance through the same mechanism. This pattern is discussed
hypothesis by hypothesis below, before turning to what it means for the mediating role of Online Reviews.
H1 (Social Media Marketing → Marketing Performance) was rejected (β = 0.165; t = 1.406; p = 0.160). Read through the
S-O-R lens adopted in this study, this outcome is consistent with the central argument advanced in Section 2.1: brand-generated
content such as educational posts, creator collaborations, and live-commerce interactions is a stimulus that consumers must still
evaluate before it shapes a market outcome, so a simple, unmediated path from stimulus to response is theoretically unlikely to
be strong. This finding diverges from Tatik & Setiawan, (2024) and the meta-analytic evidence in Liadeli et al., (2023), both
of which report a significant direct effect of social media activity on performance or sales. The divergence is plausibly explained
by differences in model specification and unit of analysis: those studies estimate SMM's effect without a mediating o rganism
variable, so the influence that actually passes through consumer evaluation processes may be absorbed into what appears to be
a direct effect. In a model that explicitly separates the evaluative stage — as this one does — that same influence is reassigned
to the indirect path, which is precisely what H6 confirms below. The rejection of H1 is therefore better read as evidence tha t
the direct-effect models used in prior research may overstate SMM's unmediated influence, rather than as evidence that SM M
is ineffective; it is consistent, however, with Cao & Weerawardena, (2023), who likewise found that social media strengthens
marketing performance only through an intervening capability rather than on its own.
H2 (Social Media Marketing → Online Reviews) was the strongest relationship in the model (β = 0.389; t = 3.757; p <
0.001; f² = 0.174, moderate). This supports the theoretical role assigned to SMM in Section 2.3: consistent, credible, and
interactive brand content encourages consumers to try the product and subsequently document their experience as a review.
The result is congruent with Dwivedi et al., (2021), who found that digital marketing activity shapes the volume and character
of consumer-generated eWOM. Unlike H1, this path did not surface clearly contradicting evidence in the literature reviewed
for this study; the studies examined consistently treat b rand-driven digital content as an antecedent rather than a substitute for
consumer-generated reviews, which strengthens confidence that H2 reflects a genuine mechanism rather than a sample-specific
artifact. Combined with the non-significant H1, this indicates that within this sample, SMM's influence on skincare marketing
performance operates almost entirely by first shaping consumer evaluation, not by acting on performance directly.
H3 (Affiliate Marketing → Marketing Performance) was supported (β = 0.278; t = 2.959; p = 0.003), in contrast to H1.
This asymmetry is theoretically meaningful: as defined in Section 2.4, affiliate marketing is a stimulus grounded in interpersonal
trust in the affiliate or content creator, which functions as a more immediate persuasion cue than brand-owned content and can
move a buyer toward a purchase without requiring the same extent of evaluative processing that brand content does. The result
aligns with Nursal et al., (2022), Gunawan et al., (2024), and Ash Shiddiqy & Arifiona Faradila, (2024), all of which report a
significant, relatively direct effect of affiliate marketing on purchase-related or performance outcomes on Shopee. Not all recent
evidence points the same way, however: in a skincare setting closely comparable to the present study,  Kholida & Nuvriasari,
(2026) found that affiliate marketing did not have a significant direct effect on purchasing decisions for skincare products on

stimuli becomes most informative. For H6 (SMM → OR → MP), the indirect effect was significant (β = 0.105; t = 2.139; p =

because the corresponding direct effect (H1, SMM → MP) was not significant, a stricter and more contemporary reading of

any SMM effect reaches Marketing Performance at all in this sample. This indirect -only pattern is consistent with  Kashif et

through an intervening evaluative construct (customer trust) rather than acting directly. Not every study reports such a clea n
mediation pattern, however: Salhab et al., (2023) found that brand trust did not significantly mediate the relationship between
social media marketing and purchase intention, illustrating that an evaluative organism does not automatically transmit a
stimulus's effect in every digital marketing context. The mediation observed for H6 in this study is therefore better understood
as evidence that online reviews specifically, rather than consumer evaluation in general, are the operative mechanism for
skincare SMM on Shopee Indonesia.

partial mediation. Because H3 (AM → MP) is significant while this indirect path is only marginal, the same stricter mediation

These findings carry both theoretical and practical implications. Theoretically, the study contributes to the development of
S-O-R Theory by providing empirical confirmation that Online Reviews function as the organism that mediates the relationship
between digital marketing stimuli and marketing performance, rather than as a standalone independent or moderating variable,
as online reviews have often been treated in prior research. This directly addresses the theoretical gap identified in Sectio n 1:
by expl icitly modeling Social Media Marketing and Affiliate Marketing as stimuli, Online Reviews as the organism, and
Marketing Performance as the response within a single integrated model, the study demonstrates that the S-O-R framework can
meaningfully explain not just whether digital marketing activities affect performance, but the evaluative mechanism through

H6 and H7 tests: rather than showing that Online Reviews mediate all digital marketing stimuli uniformly, the findings indicate
that the strength of this mediating role depends on whether a stimulus already carries an alternative, trust -based persuasion
mechanism. Social Media Marketing, which lacks this interpersonal trust component, is transmitted to Marketing Performance
almost entirely through Online Reviews, whereas Affiliate Marketing, which is inherently grounded in interpersonal trust,
reaches Marketing Performance largely through a direct channel that does not require the organism stage. This conditional view
of the organism's mediating strength refines the application of S -O-R theory in digital marketing research and offers a
theoretical explanation, rooted in the trust-basis of the stimulus, for why some digital marketing channels depend more heavily
on consumer evaluation than others. In addition, by integrating Social Media Marketing, Affiliate Marketing, Online Reviews,
and Marketing Performance into a single comprehensi ve model and combining Uses and Gratifications Theory with S -O-R
Theory, this study offers a context -specific theoretical framework for the skincare e -commerce industry on Shopee Indonesia
that had not previously been tested in this combination.

5. CONCLUSION
This research aims to analyze the effect of social media marketing and affiliate marketing on marketing performance,
with online reviews as a mediating variable in the skincare industry on the Shopee Indonesia platform. Based on the SEM-PLS
analysis, it was found that affiliate marketing has a positive and significant effect both directly on marketing performance and
on online reviews, whereas social media marketing only has a significant effect on online reviews and has not yet been proven
to directly affe ct marketing performance. Online reviews are proven to play an important role as an organism in the S -O-R
model because they have a positive significant effect on marketing performance and partially mediate the effect of social media
marketing on marketing performance, while their mediating role in the affiliate marketing path is not statistically significa nt.
These findings reinforce that the effectiveness of digital marketing in the skincare industry on Shopee Indonesia depends on
how marketing stimuli, whether through social media content or affiliate recommendations, can shape positive consumer
evaluations in the form of online reviews before impacting marketing performance. So, skincare business owners are advised
not to just focus on the intensity of social media marketing activities, but also actively encourage and manage customer reviews
as a strategic bridge to boosting sales, market share, and brand equity.

6. LIMITATION AND IMPLICATION
While this study significantly advances the Stimulus -Organism-Response (S-O-R) framework by proving that online
reviews act as a crucial evaluative bridge  especially for social media marketing, which requires this step to drive actual
performance its findings are inherently bounded by specific contextual limitations. Because the research focuses exclusively on
the high-perceived-risk skincare market within Shopee Indonesia, the observed asymmetric dynamics where trust-based affiliate
marketing bypasses the need for reviews to directly boost sales  may not universally apply to other product categories or
alternative platforms, such as TikTok Shop, where audience behaviors and content consumption differ. Nevertheless, the
implications provide a powerful, action able roadmap for e -commerce brands: producing engaging social media content is
fundamentally insufficient on its own. To translate digital marketing stimuli into tangible market share and brand equity,
businesses must proactively incentivize and manage cus tomer reviews to validate their social media efforts, while
simultaneously leveraging credible affiliate partnerships to secure more immediate, trust-driven sales conversions.
