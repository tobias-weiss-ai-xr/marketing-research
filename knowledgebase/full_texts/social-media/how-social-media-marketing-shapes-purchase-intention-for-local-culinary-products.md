	Background:	Local	 culinary	 products	 such	 as	pecel	and	brem	from
Madiun	are	 cultural	 assets	 whose	 commercialization	 increasingly
depends	on	digital	exposure,	yet	little	is	known	about	how	social	media
marketing	 shapes	 purchase-intention	 formation	 within	 this	 micro-
enterprise	setting.
Objective:	This	 study	 explores	 the	 mechanisms	 through	 which	 social
media	marketing	practices	shape	consumer	purchase-intention	formation
toward	Madiun's	pecel	and	brem.
Methods:	A	 qualitative	 multiple	 case	 study	 was	 conducted	 with	 thirty-
two	 sellers	 and	 twenty-four	 consumers	 through	 in-depth	 interviews,
participant	 observation,	 digital	 content	 archiving,	 focus	 group
discussions,	 and	 documentation.	 Data	 were	 analyzed	 thematically	 and
credibility	 was	 supported	 through	 source,	 method,	 and	 investigator
triangulation.
Results:	Seven	content	dimensions	emerged,	led	by	visual	authenticity	of
the	 craft	 process,	 interactive	 responsiveness,	 and	 origin	 narrative.
Exposure	did	not	operate	as	a	direct	causal	reported	factor	of	intention;
participants	 described	 perceived	 product	 authenticity,	 digital	 trust,	 and
parasocial	 familiarity	 as	 interpretive	 mechanisms,	 while	 perceived
spoilage	and	logistics	riskss	attenuated	intention	formation.
Conclusion:	Sellers	displaying	unedited	production	routines	and	timely
responses	 generated	 stronger	 reported	 intention	 signals	 than	 those
relying	primarily	on	polished	promotional	posts.	The	study	proposes	an
Authenticity-Trust-Familiarity	 framework	 for	 local	 culinary	micro-
enterprises.

INTRODUCTION
Local	culinary	products	occupy	a	distinctive	position	in	the	economy	and	cultural	identity
of	 Indonesian	 secondary	 cities.	 They	 are	 simultaneously	 everyday	 commodities,	 markers	 of
collective	memory,	and	instruments	of	place	branding.	Madiun,	a	mid-sized	city	in	eastern	Java,	is
nationally	recognized	for	two	heritage	foods	of	precisely	this	character.	The	first	is	nasi	pecel,	a
dish	of	steamed	rice	served	with	blanched	vegetables	and	a	spiced	peanut	sauce,	sold	by	hundreds
of	small	warung	and	household	kitchens	operating	on	thin	margins	and	daily	turnover.	The	second

is	brem,	a	fermented	glutinous-rice	confection	produced	almost	exclusively	in	a	cluster	of	villages
on	the	outskirts	of	the	city	and	consumed	largely	as	an	edible	souvenir.	Both	are	embedded	in
dense	social	and	inter-ethnic	networks	that	have	shaped	the	trading	practices	of	Mataraman	cities
for	generations,	so	that	the	everyday	routines	of	selling	them	constitute	social	infrastructure	as
much	as	commerce	(Yulianto	et	al.,	2024).	The	attributes	that	consumers	attach	to	such	products,
including	provenance,	freshness,	and	the	perceived	integrity	of	the	maker,	have	been	shown	to
drive	purchasing	behavior	more	strongly	than	price	alone	(Charton-Vachet	et	al.,	2024;	Kovacs	et
al.,	2022).
The	 commercial	 environment	 surrounding	 these	 products	 has	 changed	 considerably.
Micro-enterprises	that	once	relied	on	physical	location,	word	of	mouth,	and	passing	traffic	now
operate	where	visibility	is	interpreted	through	algorithmic	feeds	and	short-form	video.	Empirical
work	across	emerging	economies	indicates	that	digital	transformation	improves	the	marketing
performance	 of	 small	 and	 medium	 enterprises,	 particularly	 when	 social	 media	 is	 used	 as	 an
integrative	channel	rather	than	a	supplementary	billboard	(M.	Amin	et	al.,	2025).	Technological
readiness,	relative	advantage,	and	perceived	compatibility	determine	whether	small	firms	adopt
social	 media	 marketing	 at	 all	Ahmed	 et	 al.	 (2025),	while	 relational	 capability	 moderates	 the
translation	 of	 adoption	 into	 business	 performance	(Hafizni	 et	 al.,	 2025).	Web-based	 social
networks	 likewise	 enable	 sustainable	 growth	 by	 lowering	 the	 cost	 of	 market	 intelligence	 and
customer	acquisition	(Alkhasoneh	et	al.,	2025).	More	broadly,	social	media	and	digital	marketing
have	 become	 the	 principal	 architecture	 through	 which	 consumer	behavior	is	 now	organized
across	sectors	as	varied	as	retail,	sport,	and	hospitality	(Gashi,	2025;	Taraku	&	Taraku,	2025).	Yet
these	 findings	 are	 drawn	 largely	 from	 firms	 with	 formal	 marketing	 functions,	 and	 their
transferability	to	household-scale	culinary	producers	with	limited	digital	literacy	remains	an	open
question.
Purchase	 intention	 has	 long	 served	 as	 the	 proximate	behavioral	indicator	 in	 food
marketing	research	because	it	predicts	actual	buying	with	reasonable	fidelity.	Studies	of	local	and
traditional	food	demonstrate	that	intention	is	constructed	from	a	bundle	of	functional,	emotional,
and	symbolic	values	rather	than	from	utilitarian	evaluation	alone.	Nostalgic	emotion	attached	to
traditional	 preparations	 has	 been	 found	 to	 exert	 a	 substantial	 influence	 on	 the	 purchase
intentions	of	younger	consumers,	who	approach	heritage	food	as	a	vehicle	for	cultural	connection
rather	than	mere	sustenance	(Chen	&	Lin,	2024).	Consumption	value	in	food	tourism	shapes	both
purchase	 involvement	 and	 post-consumption	behavior,	 indicating	 that	 intention	 formation
extends	well	beyond	the	transaction	moment	(Stone	&	Zou,	2025).	The	manner	in	which	a	product
is	presented	at	the	point	of	sale	matters	as	well;	direct	selling	and	visible	on-the-spot	preparation
have	been	shown	to	raise	intentions	for	food	souvenirs	by	making	the	production	process	legible
to	the	buyer	(Wang	et	al.,	2025).	Similar	dynamics	appear	for	unfamiliar	agricultural	products,
where	consumers	rely	on	cues	of	origin	and	cultivation	practice	to	resolve	uncertainty	(Sgroi	et
al.,	2023).
A	 parallel	 body	 of	 scholarship	 examines	 how	 social	 media	 marketing	 acts	 upon	 these
intention-forming	 processes.	Short-video	 content	 raises	 purchase	 intentions	 primarily	 by
building	trust,	which	helps	explain	the	relationship	between	exposure	and	behavioral	response
Luo	et	al.	(2025),	and	content	marketing	on	short-form	platforms	exerts	comparable	effects	when
informational	 and	 entertainment	 value	 are	 combined	(Wei,	 2025).	Storytelling,	humor,	 and
promotional	 framing	 operate	 through	 distinct	 psychological	 routes	Cai	 et	 al.	 (2025),	while
sustained	marketing	activity	generates	brand	love	and	engagement	that	persist	beyond	individual
campaigns	(Sohaib	 et	 al.,	 2025).	For	 influencer-mediated	 communication,	 source	 credibility,
perceived	expertise,	and	perceived	value	jointly	determine	whether	endorsement	converts	into
action	(Sharma	et	al.,	2025;	Tian	et	al.,	2025).	Within	the	food	domain,	foodstagramming	reshapes
culinary	production	itself	Fathy	et	al.	(2025),	cinematic	techniques	alter	responses	to	food	video
marketing	Huang	 et	 al.	 (2025),	and	 food-related	 advertising	 influences	 dietary	 choice	 among
digitally	native	cohorts	(Nandwani	et	al.,	2025;	Sajjad	&	Pasha,	2025).	Social	media	has	also	been
used	as	a	culinary	intervention	channel	Jurado-Gonzalez	et	al.	(2025),	and	algorithmic	curation	is
increasingly	implicated	in	unplanned	food	purchasing	(A.	Amin,	2025).
Despite	 this	 accumulation	 of	 evidence,	 several	 limitations	 constrain	 its	 usefulness	 here.

The	 novelty	 of	 this	 study	 and	 its	 position	 relative	 to	 the	 state	 of	 the	 art	rest	 on	 three
interlocking	 contributions.	 The	 first	 is	 contextual:	 whereas	 prior	 research	 has	 concentrated	 on
branded	 consumer	 goods,	 tourism	 destinations,	 and	 formally	 organized	enterprises	 in
metropolitan	settings,	this	study	examines	two	heritage	culinary	products	with	radically	different
perishability	 profiles,	pecel	as	 a	 same-day	 prepared	 dish	 and	brem	as	 a	 shelf-stable	 souvenir,
produced	by	micro-enterprises	in	a	single	Indonesian	secondary	city.	This	paired	design	permits
comparison	of	how	social	media	marketing	operates	when	the	product	can	and	cannot	travel.	The
second	is	methodological:	the	dominant	literature	is	quantitative	and	variance-oriented,	testing
whether	constructs	covary	but	leaving	the	interpretive	pathway	opaque.	By	combining	in-depth
interviews,	participant	observation,	systematic	archiving	of	the	sellers'	own	digital	content,	and
focus	group	discussions,	this	study	reconstructs	the	meaning-making	process	by	which	exposure
becomes	 intention	 from	 the	 perspectives	 of	 both	 producer	 and	 buyer.	 The	 third	 is	 conceptual:
rather	than	treating	social	media	marketing	as	a	monolithic	stimulus,	the	study	disaggregates	it
into	content	dimensions	grounded	in	the	sellers'	own	practice	and	identifies	three	interpretive
mechanisms,	 namely	 perceived	 product	 authenticity,	 digital	 trust,	 and	 parasocial	 familiarity,
together	with	one	attenuating	condition	in	the	form	of	perceived	logistic	and	spoilage	risk.	These
are	synthesized	into	 an	 Authenticity-Trust-Familiarity	 framework	 that	 extends	 existing	 trust-
mediation	 accounts	Luo	 et	 al.,	 (2025)	and	Larfi	 et	 al.	 (2025)	by	 specifying	 where	 authenticity
claims	 originate,	 how	 they	 are	 verified	 by	 consumers,	 and	 under	 what	 conditions	 they	 fail	 to
convert	 into	 intention.	 It	 thereby	 connects	 work	 on	 influencer	 credibility	 and	 market-shaping
narratives	Cayla	 et	 al.	 (2025)	and	Zhang	and	Bryson,	 (2025)	to	 a	 class	 of	 enterprise	 largely
invisible	in	that	literature.
Proceeding	from	the	identified	gaps,	the	study	has	four	objectives:	(i)	describe	the	social
media	 marketing	 practices	 used	 by	pecel	and	brem	micro-enterprises;	 (ii)	 identify	 the	 content
dimensions	 consumers	 report	 using	 when	 forming	 purchase	 intentions;	 (iii)	 explain	 the
interpretive	 roles	 of	 perceived	 authenticity,	 digital	 trust,	 parasocial	 familiarity,	 and
logistics/spoilage	 risk;	 and	 (iv)	 develop	 a	 contextually	 grounded	 framework	 and	 practical
recommendations	for	heritage-food	micro-enterprises	and	local	support	institutions.

METHOD
This	research	adopted	a	qualitative	design	grounded	in	the	interpretive	paradigm,	on	the
premise	 that	 purchase	 intention	 toward	 heritage	 food	 is	 not	 a	 fixed	 attribute	 waiting	 to	 be
measured	 but	 a	 meaning	 constructed	 by	 consumers	 as	 they	 interpret	 what	 they	 see,	 read,	 and
experience	in	digital	spaces.	The	interpretive	stance	is	appropriate	because	the	research	question
concerns	how	and	why	social	media	marketing	influences	intention	rather	than	how	much,	and
because	 the	 sellers	 under	 study	 operate	 without	 formal	 marketing	 documentation,	 so	 their
practices	can	only	be	recovered	through	direct	engagement.	A	multiple	case	study	strategy	was
selected,	 treating	 each	 of	 the	 two	 product	 categories	 as	 a	 bounded	 case	 and	 each	 participating
enterprise	as	an	embedded	unit	of	analysis.	This	structure	allows	within-case	depth	and	cross-
case	 comparison	 between	 a	 perishable	 daily-consumption	 product	 and	 a	 shelf-stable	 souvenir
product,	which	is	central	to	the	study's	contribution.
The	research	was	conducted	in	Madiun	City	and	the	adjacent	brem-producing	villages	of
Kaliabu	and	Bancong	in	Madiun	Regency,	East	Java,	over	a	period	of	eight	months	from	October
2025	 to	 May	 2026.	 Two	 participant	 groups	 were	 recruited	 through	 purposive	 sampling	 with	 a
maximum	variation	logic.	The	seller	group	comprised	thirty-two	owner-operators,	sixteen	selling

Two	 contrasts	 are	 analytically	 consequential.	 The	 first	 concerns	 who	 produces	 the
content.	Among	pecel	sellers,	the	person	responsible	for	social	media	is	in	eleven	of	sixteen	cases
a	son	or	daughter	of	the	owner	rather	than	the	owner	personally,	whereas	among	brem	producers

both	 the	 age	 profile	 of	 the	 operators	 and	 the	 perceived	 seriousness	 of	 the	 channel;	brem
producers,	whose	product	is	bought	as	a	gift	and	shipped	beyond	the	city,	treat	digital	presence
as	a	core	business	function,	while	pecel	sellers	more	often	describe	it	as	something	the	younger
generation	handles.	The	second	contrast	concerns	revenue	dependence.	Pecel	enterprises	derive
between	 seventy	 and	 ninety	 per	 cent	 of	 revenue	 from	 walk-in	 customers,	 so	 digital	 activity
functions	as	a	supplement	to	physical	visibility.	Brem	producers,	by	contrast,	report	that	between

asymmetry	conditions	every	finding	that	follows	and	echoes	evidence	that	the	marginal	value	of
social	media	adoption	depends	on	how	far	the	firm's	market	extends	beyond	its	immediate	locality
(Alkhasoneh	et	al.,	2025;	Amin	et	al.,	2025).

Mechanisms,	and	Purchase	Intention
Perceived	product	authenticity	is	the	first	mechanism	and	the	most	frequently	invoked.
Content	dimensions	relating	to	craft	process	and	origin	narrative	are	converted	into	a	judgement
about	 whether	 the	 product	 is	 what	 it	 claims	 to	 be,	 which	 in	 the	 heritage	food	 context	 means
whether	it	was	made	in	the	traditional	manner	by	the	party	claiming	to	have	made	it.	Authenticity
in	 this	 sense	 is	 inferential	 rather	 than	 certified;	 consumers	 have	 no	 access	 to	 verification	 and
therefore	reason	from	visual	evidence	to	conclusions	about	production.	The	second	mechanism	is
digital	trust,	understood	as	confidence	that	the	transaction	will	proceed	as	represented,	that	the
product	will	arrive	in	the	described	condition,	and	that	the	seller	will	respond	if	it	does	not.	Trust
is	 built	 primarily	 through	 interactive	 responsiveness	 and	 price	 transparency,	 and	 it	 is	 the
mechanism	 through	 which	 authenticity	 becomes	 actionable.	 Several	 consumers	 described
believing	that	a	product	was	genuine	yet	declining	to	order	because	they	were	uncertain	the	seller
would	 handle	 a	 problem.	 The	 third	 mechanism	 is	 parasocial	 familiarity,	 which	 accumulates
through	repeated	exposure	to	the	same	face,	voice,	and	setting	until	the	consumer	experiences	a
sense	of	acquaintance	with	the	seller.	Participants	who	followed	an	account	for	several	months
without	 purchasing	 described	 eventually	 buying	 because	 it	 felt	 like	 supporting	 someone	 they
knew.	This	mechanism	is	well	documented	in	influencer	research	Tian	et	al.	(2025)	and	Ali	et	al.
(2025)	but	is	shown	here	to	operate	for	ordinary	micro-entrepreneurs	with	audiences	numbering
in	 the	 hundreds	 rather	 than	 the	 millions,	 which	 suggests	 that	 parasocial	 effects	 depend	 on
consistency	of	self-presentation	rather	than	on	audience	scale.
The	attenuating	condition	is	perceived	logistic	and	spoilage	risk,	and	it	explains	most	of
the	observed	cases	in	which	strong	authenticity	and	trust	judgements	failed	to	produce	purchase.

makes	it	desirable;	consumers	outside	the	city	therefore	convert	their	intention	into	a	plan	for	a
future	visit	rather	than	a	purchase.	For	brem	the	risk	is	moderate	and	manageable,	concerning
packaging	integrity	and	transit	time	rather	than	spoilage	as	such.	This	asymmetry	is	the	principal
source	of	divergence	between	the	two	cases	and	constitutes	a	boundary	condition	that	the	existing
literature,	developed	largely	around	durable	or	digital	goods,	has	not	had	occasion	to	specify.

Discussion
The	 findings	 of	 this	 study	 both	 confirm	 and	 qualify	 the	 established	 literature.	 The
centrality	of	trust	as	a	mediating	mechanism	corroborates	evidence	from	short-video	commerce
that	exposure	influences	intention	through	trust	rather	than	directly	Luo	et	al.	(2025)	and	from
social	 commerce	 research	 showing	 that	 information	 sharing	 affects	 intention	 through
relationship	quality	while	perceived	risk	operates	in	the	opposite	direction	(Larfi	et	al.,	2025).	The
present	study	specifies	the	content	antecedents	of	that	trust	in	a	setting	where	the	seller	has	no
brand	equity,	no	certification,	and	no	advertising	budget,	and	it	identifies	responsiveness	latency
as	 an	 observable	 and	 controllable	 antecedent.	 The	 salience	 of	 parasocial	 familiarity	 extends
influencer	 research	 to	 micro-scale	 accounts,	 indicating	 that	 the	 mechanism	 is	 not	 a	 function	 of
celebrity	but	of	consistent	personal	presence	(Ali	et	al.	2025;	Sharma	et	al.,	2025;	Tian	et	al.,	2025).
The	most	substantial	departure	from	prior	work	concerns	production	quality.	A	significant
strand	 of	 research	 demonstrates	 that	 sophisticated	 content	 technique	 enhances	 marketing
outcomes,	whether	through	cinematic	manipulation	of	food	video	Huang	et	al.	(2025),	multimodal
optimization	of	 influencer	 content	Zhang	and	Bryson	 (2025),	or	 interface	 design	 that	 manages
consumer	impatience	(Yang	et	al.,	2025).	In	the	present	context	the	relationship	is	non-monotonic.
Content	that	displayed	technical	polish	beyond	a	moderate	threshold	was	interpreted	as	evidence
of	 industrial	 production	 and	 reduced	 rather	 than	 enhanced	 intention.	 This	 suggests	 that	 the
persuasive	 function	 of	 production	 quality	 is	 category-dependent,	 and	 that	 for	 products	 whose
value	proposition	rests	on	artisanal	provenance,	visible	imperfection	carries	evidentiary	weight.
The	implication	for	practice	runs	directly	counter	to	the	standard	advice	given	in	digital	marketing
training	 programs,	 which	 typically	emphasizes	image	 quality,	 consistent	 visual	 identity,	 and
professional	editing.
The	results	also	speak	to	the	literature	on	social	media	marketing	and	SME	performance.
Studies	establishing	that	digital	transformation	improves	marketing	performance	M.	Amin	et	al.
(2025),	that	 technological	 determinants	 condition	 adoption	Ahmed	 et	 al.	 (2025),	and	 that
relational	 capability	 mediates	 the	 performance	 effect	Hafizni	 et	 al.	 (2025)	are	 supported	 in
general	direction.	The	present	findings	add	that	for	micro-enterprises	the	binding	constraint	is
rarely	adoption	itself,	since	all	thirty-two	enterprises	had	already	adopted	at	least	two	platforms,
but	 rather	 the	 alignment	 between	 platform	affordance	 and	 product	 logistics.	Pecel	sellers	 who
invested	effort	in	platforms	optimized	for	national	reach	obtained	engagement	without	revenue,
a	pattern	of	misalignment	that	adoption-focused	frameworks	do	not	capture.	This	observation	is
consistent	with	the	emphasis	on	business	communication	strategy	as	an	internal	sustainability
mechanism	in	small	firms	Kalogiannidis	et	al.	(2025),	with	evidence	on	how	social	media	branding
operates	for	place-bound	offerings	in	emerging	economies	Alaflak	et	al.	(2025),	and	with	findings
on	how	information	and	communication	technologies	contribute	to	the	resilience	of	place-bound

CONCLUSION
This	study	set	out	to	explain	how	social	media	marketing	shapes	purchase	intention	for
local	culinary	products,	using	the	pecel	sellers	and	brem	producers	of	Madiun	as	paired	cases.	The
evidence	gathered	from	thirty-two	sellers,	twenty-four	consumers,	one	hundred	and	forty	hours
of	 observation,	 and	 a	 six-month	 archive	 of	 digital	 content	 supports	 four	 principal	 conclusions.
First,	social	media	marketing	practice	among	these	enterprises	is	pragmatic	and	incidental	rather
than	strategic,	with	content	produced	during	the	work	process	itself	and	platform	selection	driven
by	whether	the	product	can	be	shipped	rather	than	by	audience	analysis.	Second,	consumers	form
purchase	intentions	based	 on	seven	 content	 dimensions,	 of	 which	 visual	 evidence	 of	 the	 craft
process	 and	 interactive	 responsiveness	 carry	 the	 greatest	 weight,	 and	 technical	 production
quality	 carries	 none	and	attenuating	 condition	 beyond	 a	 moderate	 threshold.	 Third,	 the
relationship	between	marketing	exposure	and	intention	is	not	direct	but	is	interpreted	through
perceived	 product	 authenticity,	 digital	 trust,	 and	 parasocial	 familiarity,	 and	 is	 attenuated	 by
perceived	logistic	and	spoilage	risk,	which	for	non-shippable	products	is	severe	enough	to	sever
the	pathway	entirely.	Fourth,	the	two	product	categories	diverge	systematically	along	the	mobility
dimension,	 so	 that	 heritage	 narrative	 and	 national-reach	 platforms	 generate	 returns	 for	brem
while	producing	engagement	without	revenue	for	pecel.
The	theoretical	contribution	lies	in	the	Authenticity-Trust-Familiarity	framework,	which
specifies	 the	 interpretive	 pathway	 between	 social	 media	 content	 and	 purchase	 intention	 for
heritage	food	produced	by	micro-enterprises,	and	which	identifies	product	mobility	as	a	boundary
condition	 determining	 which	 content	 dimensions	 repay	 investment.	 Practically,	 the	 findings
suggest	 that	 capacity-building	 programs	 for	 heritage	 culinary	 producers	 should	 reverse	 two
elements	 of	 conventional	 digital	 marketing	 training.	Rather	 than	 teaching	 image	 polish,	 such
programs	 should	 teach	 producers	 to	 document	 process;	 rather	 than	 encouraging	 presence	 on
every	 available	 platform,	 they	 should	 help	 producers	 match	 platform	 reach	 to	 the	 geographic
range	within	which	their	product	remains	viable.	The	single	highest-return	intervention	identified
in	 this	 study	 requires	 no	 financial	 investment	 at	 all,	 consisting	 simply	 of	 reducing	 the	 interval
between	customer	enquiry	and	reply.
Several	limitations	qualify	these	conclusions.	The	study	is	bounded	to	one	city	and	two
products,	 and	 its	 findings	 are	 offered	 as	 analytically	 rather	 than	 statistically	generalizable.
Purchase	intention	was	reconstructed	from	participant	accounts	rather	than	observed	through	to
completed	transactions,	so	the	intention-behavior	gap	remains	unexamined.	The	archived	content
corpus	covers	six	months	and	cannot	capture	seasonal	variation	associated	with	holiday	gifting
cycles,	 which	 are	 consequential	 for	brem.	 Future	 research	 could	 test	 the	 proposed	 framework
quantitatively	across	a	larger	sample	of	heritage	food	clusters,	extend	the	design	longitudinally	to
trace	 intention	 through	 to	 purchase	 and	 repurchase,	 and	 examine	 whether	 the	 negative
relationship	between	production	polish	and	perceived	authenticity	holds	across	other	categories
of	artisanal	product.
