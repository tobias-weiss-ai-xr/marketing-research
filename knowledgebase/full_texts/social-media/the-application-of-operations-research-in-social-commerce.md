ABSTRACT: This paper adopts a quantitative, data -driven approach, consistent with the Operations Research (OR)
emphasis on evidence -based decision-making, to examine how scholarly research on social commerce analytics has
developed. Social commerce, the integration of social media interaction with commercial transactions, increasingly
depends on analytical techniques to interpret consumer behaviour and to support managerial decisions. A systematic
literature review and bibliometric analysis were conducted on records retrieved from Scopus using the search string
"Social Commerce" AND "Analytics". Sequential screening by time span, document type, language and source type
reduced an initial 1,455 records to a final dataset of 416, of which 415 documents published between  2016 and 2026
were retained for the main analysis. Using R Studio and Biblioshiny, the study applied performance analysis and science
mapping, including annual scientific production, source and country productivity, three -field plots and thematic
evolution. The dataset records an annual growth rate of 14.53%, 1,337 contributing authors, 33.98% international co -
authorship and an average of 46.46 citations per document. Annual output rose from 17 publications in 2016 to 66 in
2026 (R² = 0.7954), with India, China and the USA emerging as leading contributors and IEEE Access as the most
productive source. Thematic evolution shows a shift from foundational social media and e -commerce themes before
2020, towards data analytics and machine learning during the COVI D-19 period, and towards predictive analytics,
artificial intelligence and strategic decision -making after 2022. The findings provide a structured knowledge map for
scholars and practical direction for organisations adopting analytics-led social commerce strategies. The findings offer
an organized knowledge map for scholars and guidance for organizations using analytics -driven social commerce
strategies.

1. INTRODUCTION
Social commerce has emerged as one of the most significant opportunities in digital retail, converting social interaction
into measurable commercial value. Mordor Intelligence (2026) valued the global social commerce market at USD 1.63
trillion in 2025 and  projected it to reach USD 7.55 trillion by 2031, a compound annual growth rate of 29.12%. This
expansion generates large volumes of behavioural, textual and transactional data, which presents both an opportunity
and a problem: such data must be converted systematically into reliable decisions. Operations Research employs
analytical and mathematical techniques that support decision -making in an environment of limited resources and
uncertainties. The techniques employed in OR include mathematical modeling, o ptimization, forecasting and decision
making based on data analysis. Operations Research techniques also play a role in the research process since, before
conducting any kind of analytics, the body of knowledge should be mapped to determine what techniques  have been
used in research, what evidence is available and the gaps that remain unfilled. Bibliometric analysis offers a systematic,
quantitative and reproducible procedure for this purpose (Donthu et al., 2021; Zupic & Čater, 2015). Therefore, in this
paper, scientific literature is treated as data. The study employs a systematic literature review combined with a
bibliometric approach. Records were retrieved from Scopus using the search string "Social Commerce" AND
"Analytics", and a PRISMA-style screening process was applied sequentially. The time-span filter (2016–2026) reduced

2. LITERATURE REVIEW
The intersection between e-commerce and social media gives birth to social commerce -commercial processes supported
by social interactions among consumers (Han et al., 2018). Initially, e-commerce was mostly a transaction process, while
social networks, user-generated content, and online communities made a consumer speak his mind and attract attention.
According to Goyal et al. (2021), the paradigm of consumption changed from individual and isolated purchases to
collaboration and interactivity of shopping. The field can be viewed through multiple lenses including psychology,
sociology, computer science, and marketing, which makes the definition of the field still unclear (Han et al., 2018). As
Lin et al. (2017) prove, the first research in the field emphasiz ed the organization, advertising, and word -of-mouth
instead of the concept definition.
Recurring reviews reveal three main topics: consumer behavior, social interaction, and marketing/engagement. Trust,
risk perception, purchase intention, and sharing intention are the core constructs used. Lin et al. (2017) state that online
reviews, trust, and electronic word-of-mouth became the focus of the researchers while two out of four themes revealed
by Goyal et al. (2021) are related to purchase and sharing intention. Zhao et al. (2023) analyzed 56 articles through the
lens of Stimulus–Organism–Response framework. Thus, the field moved from description of the platform to analysis of
the mechanisms behind consumers' behavior. The role of social interaction through reviews, communities, and reputation
is to characterize the platform itself and decrease buyers' uncertainty. The marketing perspective covers the approach to
social commerce from the viewpoint of the firm.
The technological environment has also changed. Herzallah et al. (2025) compared 2008 –2018 with 2019–2022 and
found a shift from perceived sociability and mobile commerce towards UTAUT, small and medium -sized enterprises,
perceived risk and live streaming.  They also pointed to artificial intelligence, augmented reality and blockchain as
possible future directions. These are emerging directions rather than established research areas, and it is still open how
far they have entered the published literature.

The reviews have moved from descriptive and definitional work towards periodised, evolution-oriented analysis. They
use different databases, time windows and techniques, so their results are not directly interchangeable. Where review

themes such as live commerce, AI and blockchain have moved from the margins to the centre of the field is not clearly
established in these studies. This does not mean social commerce has been neglected. Because the field evolves quickly,
its thematic structure needs to be reassessed periodically, and an updated analysis covering a recent period is therefore
justified.

Article Selection, Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria
Database search was performed in Scopus using the search string “Social Commerce” AND “Analytics” where the
keywords identified publications related to the topics under study by means of title, abstract, and keywords. The search
and selection process progressively reduced the initial number of publications depending on the inclusion and exclusion
criteria established by the researcher. Overall process was built in accordance with the PRISMA-style screening method
in order to present how initial search resul ts were reduced to the final bibliometric database.  Initial search in Scopus
resulted in 1,455 publications. The following four filters were then applied successively:
1. Time span (2016 -2026): reduced the initial number of publications from 1,455 to 1,324 by eliminating those
publications which do not belong to the chosen ten-year time frame.
2. Document type (Article and Review): reduced the number of publications from 1,324 to 420. The final number of
publications at this step consisted of 394 Articles and 26 Reviews.
3. Language (English): reduced the number of publications from 420 to 418 by excluding two publications which failed
to satisfy the English language requirement.
4. Source type (Journal and Trade Journal): reduced the number of publications from 418 to 416 which made up the
final.
The final dataset used for the bibliometric analysis therefore consisted of 416 papers, drawn from an initial pool of 1,455
Scopus records. The filtering sequence was applied consistently so that each stage established a more focused dataset
for the subsequent analysis. The PRISMA flow diagram presented separately in Figure 1 provides a visual representation
of this screening process.

Comments on the filtering process
Filtering exemplifies the necessity of setting definite boundaries before making the bibliometric analysis. To begin with,
the first search yielded 1,455 records in total. Filtering by “2016 -2026-time restriction” brought down the number of
records to 1,32 4 while making a consistent time period of ten years for analyzing contemporary activity in Social
Commerce Analytics. Filtering by the document type reduced the dataset from 1,324 to 420 records, which included 394
Articles and 26 Reviews. It makes it possible to analyze the consistent academic literature while ignoring other document
types present in the broad set of Scopus results.
Filtering by language made a relatively small influence on the number of records left in the dataset. Specifically, 420
records became 418 records when filtered by the English language. This means that only two records were left out of
the process. Limiting the final dataset to the English language makes it consistent for further bibliometric analysis.
The third source-type filter further decreased the dataset size from 418 records to 416 records. The selected source types
were Journal and Trade Journal. Thus, the resulting 416 papers can be considered the final database, which was obtained
after applying all the mentioned Scopus filtering criteria. This sequence of numbers of records (from 1,455 to 416)
represents a transparent way for the further analysis to be conducted.
It is necessary to draw the line between the sequential Scopus filters and the stage of eliminating duplicates. Considering
the filtering data used in this research, 416 records can be considered the final number, considering the time span,
document type, language, and source type filters. There was no specified step of numerical removal of duplicates, so
416 records are reported as the final filtered database.

Bibliometric Analysis
It is necessary to conduct a bibliometric analysis since this will allow for the creation of a quantitative and systematic
picture of the research field. Bibliometrics are especially helpful when there is a large volume of publications within a
review, and the aim is to find some hidden patterns. In the current case, the final dataset consisting of 416 papers serves
as a good basis for studying the evolution and structure of the field of Social Commerce Analytics using the quantitative
bibliometric approach
Bibliometric analysis makes it possible to investigate publication trends, keyword trends, theme evolution, and major
research areas related to Social Commerce Analytics. Such indicators allow creating an evidence -based picture of the
development of the field during the chosen period and finding connections between concepts that appear repeatedly in
the literature.
The other advantage of the bibliometric analysis is that it enables the study of changes in research priorities throughout
the years. Year-to-year publication trends can indicate how the research activities have been developing between 2016
and 2026. Keyword trends are used to see which concepts have gained popularity; keyword co-occurrence helps to see
which concepts are often studied together.
A systematic review should be sufficiently transparent for another researcher to understand and reproduce its major
stages. The present methodology records the search terms, database, publication period, document -type criteria,

Donthu et al. (2021) propose a structured process for conducting bibliometric reviews. This is how the present review
has followed the process:
Step 1- Define the aims and scope of the bibliometric study
The first phase involved setting the research area as Social Commerce Analytics and the scope of the literature review.
This work will only include articles based on the search terms “Social Commerce” AND “Analytics”, dated between
2016 and 2026. Ultimately, the aim of the research is to conduct a quantitative analysis of the research area and highlight
the key patterns of publications, keywords, and themes within it. The final set of 416 articles serves as the basis for this
work.
Step 2- Choose the techniques
Based on the study objectives, we selected appropriate bibliometric and science -mapping techniques. These include
publication trend analysis, keyword analysis, keyword co -occurrence analysis, and thematic mapping. Publication
analysis provides an overview of research activity over time; keyword analysis identifies frequently occurring concepts;
co-occurrence analysis examines relationships between concepts; and thematic mapping helps organize the literature
into broader research themes. Together, these techniques provide a quantitative view of the structure and development
of Social Commerce Analytics.
Step 3- Collect the data
The data collection stage involved retrieving the literature from Scopus using the search string “Social Commerce” AND
“Analytics”. The initial search generated 1,455 records. The predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria were then
applied sequentially: the 2016–2026-time span produced 1,324 records; Article and Review document types produced
420 records; English-language filtering produced 418 records; and Journal and Trade Journal source types produced the
final 416 records. These 416 records constitute the database used for the bibliometric analysis.
Step 4- Run the analysis and report the findings
The final stage involves applying the selected bibliometric techniques to the 416-paper dataset and reporting the findings
through quantitative results and visualisations. Performance analysis and science mapping complement each other:
publication activity indicates development of the field, while science mapping shows relationships among keywords and
themes. The results can therefore describe the research trajectory of Social Commerce Analytics and identify areas for
future research.
Lastly, the following software and resources were used for the bibliometric analysis:
1. R Studio - this software enables access to “Biblioshiny,” which can be used for bibliometric analysis and
visualisation of the research dataset.
2. Biblioshiny- a free and open -source, web-based graphical interface for the Bibliometrix R package, allowing
bibliometric and science-mapping analysis to be performed through a graphical interface rather than requiring extensive
coding
3. Mendeley Desktop- used as the reference manager for this review. All ".ris" files exported from Scopus were
imported into Mendeley for ease of management and citing.
4. Scopus- the database used to identify and retrieve the literature forming the dataset for this bibliometric review.
The final database consisted of 416 papers after the specified screening criteria were applied.

          4. DATA ANALYSIS
Main Overview
The dataset consists of 415 documents published between 2016 and 2026 across 257 sources with an annual growth rate
of 14.53%, which indicates an overall expansion of research interest in social commerce during the selected period.
Initially, the dataset i ncluded documents between 2016 and 2027, with 416 documents which resulted in a declining
growth rate of -22.71% annually. Since 2027 is an incomplete year, its inclusion resulted in an artificial decline in the
calculated growth rate. Therefore, the 2016-2026 dataset is used for the main analysis.
The dataset has an average document age of 3.86 years and an average of 46.46 citations per document, indicating that
the publications have received considerable scholarly attention. A total of 1337 authors contributed to the dataset with
an average of 3.56 co-authors per document. This indicates that the research in social commerce is dominated by single-
author studies rather than a large group of collaborative authors. The 33.98% international co -authorship rate indicates
the presence of cross-country collaboration.
The dataset contains 1644 author keywords and 2791 keyword plus terms, highlighting the wide range of concepts and
research areas associated with social commerce. It consists of 390 document-type articles, and 25 publications are review
papers showing that the research is majorly based on original research.
Annual Scientific Production
The graph in Figure 2 shows the annual publication trend in social commerce research from 2016 to 2026. Overall, the
trend is upward, although there are fluctuations across individual years. Publications increased from 17 in 2016 to 28 in
2018, followed by a decline to 22 in 2019. Research output then increased sharply to 32 articles in 2020 and 50 in 2021.
A significant decline is observed in 2022, when publications fell to 32, but the trend recovered in 2023 and remained
relatively stable in 2024. The strongest growth occurs in 2025 and 2026 with publications rising to 61 and 66
respectively, the highest levels recorded during the study period.
The upward trendline, represented by y= 4.209x + 11.982, indicates an average increase of approximately 4.29
publications per year. The R 2 value of 0.7954 shows that around 79.54% of the variation in publication output is
explained by the overall linear growth trend. Thus, despite temporary declines, the graph demonstrates a clear and
sustained increase in scholarly interest in social commerce.

   5. FUTURE RESEARCH AGENDA
This analysis set out to trace how research on social co mmerce has developed between 2016 and 2026. The 415
documents examined (390 articles and 25 reviews) appear to show a field that is expanding steadily, with an annual
growth rate of 14.53% and a linear trend (y = 4.209x + 11.982, R² = 0.7954) that would su ggest sustained, if uneven,
growth in output. At the same time, the thematic evolution maps indicate a gradual movement from social media and
basic e-commerce toward machine learning, predictive analytics and strategic decision -making. The agenda below is
organised into six areas, moving from the methodological to the substantive, and each should be read as a tentative
direction rather than a definitive gap.
1. Clarifying the scope and vocabulary of the field
The dataset contains 1,644 author keywords and 2,791 keyword plus terms, which may point to a wide and somewhat
fragmented vocabulary. Terms such as commerce, electronic commerce and social commerce appear to overlap in the
thematic maps, so it is not entirely clear whether they describe one domain or several. Future studies could publish the
exact search query, merge near-identical terms (for example, social media and social networking) and remove generic
words before repeating the keyword and thematic ana lyses. Doing so might make it easier to judge whether social
commerce is emerging as a distinct domain or remains a sub-theme of e-commerce research (Singh & Singh, 2018; Tian
& Liu, 2017).
2. Testing analytical techniques with real-world data
Machine learning, deep learning, sentiment analysis and big data analytics become more prominent from the COVID
period onward, and journals such as IEEE Access, Expert Systems with Applications and Journal of Big Data show
rising output in the later years.  Many of the studies behind these themes seem to concentrate on proposing or
demonstrating models (Arasu et al., 2020; Sharma & Shafiq, 2022; Zhang et al., 2022), although this impression would
need to be confirmed through closer content analysis. Research ers could examine whether such techniques improve
outcomes such as purchase intention, user -intention assessment or business decision -making when applied to live
platform data, across different platforms and over longer periods. Tian and Liu (2017) raise the question of whether big
data necessarily leads to greater knowledge, and this question may deserve renewed attention as analytical approaches
become more complex.

3. Bringing in consumer behaviour and engagement
Although the three-fields plot links keywords such as sentiment analysis and decision making with leading authors and
countries, consumer -focused themes appear less central than computational ones. Earlier studies have examined
engagement metrics and user behaviour on social platforms (Liao et al., 2022; Rahman et al., 2017), yet it is possible
that trust, perceived risk and long -term loyalty have received comparatively limited attention. Future work could
investigate how consumers respond to live -streaming, social media marketing and mobile commerce, and whether
findings from one context are likely to hold in others.
4. Connecting social commerce with sustainability and supply chains
In the post-COVID period, themes related to sustainability, supply chains and strategic decision-making begin to emerge,
which may indicate that the field is widening beyond marketing and platform use. However, these themes appear
relatively peripheral at present (Papagiannidis et al., 2019; Zhang & Song, 2022). Future research could explore how
social media shapes logistics, returns and responsible consumption, and whether social commerce contributes to, or
detracts from, sustainable outcomes. Such studies  would probably benefit from combining social media data with
operational or environmental measures.
5. Cross-country comparison and international collaboration
India, China and the USA show the strongest growth in output, with India rising from 3 publications in 2016 to 197 in
2026 and the steepest trend line (y = 19.7455x − 32.8364). The UK and Australia grow more gradually, and the
international co-authorship rate of 33.98% suggests that cross -country collaboration exists but may still be limited.
Comparative studies across these countries could help to establish whether consumer behaviour, platform adoption and
regulation differ in ways that matter for theory, as Li et al. (2020) have proposed for the wider digital economy in Asia.
Partnerships with regions that appear less often in the dataset may also be worthwhile.
6. Widening the method and the time window
The average citations per year peak at around 25 for articles published in 2021 and fall sharply for 2025 and 2026. This
decline is likely to reflect the shorter time available for recent papers to be cited rather than a drop in quality or relevance,
and it should therefore be interpreted with caution. Later studies could repeat the analysis once 2026 is a complete year,
extend the number of time slices in the thematic evolution analysis (Cobo et al., 2011), and draw on additional databases,
document types and languages. Complementing bibliometric mapping with systematic content analysis could also help
to verify the interpretations offered here (Aria & Cuccurullo, 2017; Zupic & Čater, 2015)
6. CONCLUSION
This study mapped the development of social commerce analytics research through a systematic and bibliometric review
of 415 documents published between 2016 and 2026. In line with the research objectives, annual output grew from 17
publications in 2016 to 66 in 2026, at an annual growth rate of 14.53% (R² = 0.7954). IEEE Access, India, China and
the USA emerged as the leading source and contributing countries, supported by a 33.98% rate of international co -
authorship, while the conceptual structure linked s ocial media and commerce with big data, machine learning and
predictive analysis. Thematic evolution moved from social media and e -commerce foundations before 2020, to data
analytics and machine learning during the COVID -19 period, and to predictive analyt ics, artificial intelligence and
strategic decision-making after 2022.
Academically, the study provides a reproducible map of the field and shows a shift from descriptive to predictive,
decision-oriented analytics, which offers a base for applying OR techniques to social commerce problems. Managerially,
e-commerce platforms, retailers, marketers and small businesses selling through social channels can use these findings
to prioritise sentiment analysis, machine learning and predictive analytics. Socially, the growing contribution of
emerging economies and the widening focus on  customer engagement and sustainability point to benefits beyond
individual firms, provided that data is used responsibly. The study was limited by time and data availability, as it relied
on a single database (Scopus), English-language publications and one search string, and it does not assess the quality of
individual studies. These gaps can be addressed in future research by extending the dataset to additional databases and
applying OR techniques, such as forecasting and optimisation, to specific social commerce decisions.
