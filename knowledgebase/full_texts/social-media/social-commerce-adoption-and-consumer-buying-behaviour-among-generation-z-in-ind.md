Digital technology has transformed how people communicate and interact with each other, bringing consumers and
retailers closer together. Social media enables the flow of information, experiences, and feelings within online communities
where user-generated content, peer reviews, and recommendations are shared. This has driven a marked shift toward social
commerce — the selling of merchandise directly through social media platforms — which has been widely adopted across
countries including India, particularly after the pandemic made physical shopping less convenient. India’s social commerce
market continues to grow, supported by rising smartphone penetration, affordable internet access, and increasingly
reliable digital payment systems. As technology continues to evolve, marketers are increasingly targeting Generation Z
(born 1997–2012), a digitally native and digitally-savvy cohort. This study measures the extent to which social commerce
influences the buying behaviour of Indian Generation Z consumers, with findings intended to inform marketing strategy.
A structured questionnaire was administered to 400 respondents aged 18–27 who had purchased a product after being
influenced by social media, such as peer reviews from friends or influencer recommendations. Data were collected on a five-
point Likert scale, validated, and analysed using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM). The
analysis covered descriptive statistics, reliability assessment, validity testing, and structural model evaluation. Findings
confirm that social commerce has a significant, positive effect on consumer buying behaviour, and that the relationship
between the two constructs is statistically meaningful. Social media assists young consumers through convenience,
personalised recommendations, interactive content, and customer reviews — features that strengthen engagement, build
brand loyalty, and enhance purchase intention. As Indian youth constitute one of the largest consumer markets globally
after China, this evidence carries both theoretical and practical significance for marketers. Suggested variables for future
research include trust, perceived risk, electronic word-of-mouth, and social influence.

Introduction
Although e-commerce has existed for some time, the
practice of purchasing items online is no longer novel —
it is sometimes described as a “punch card” activity that
simply digitises conventional buying and selling. Social
commerce, a branch of e-commerce rooted in social
media, has reshaped this landscape by enabling users to
shop directly through their preferred social platforms.
Social commerce is often described as the next stage
of e-commerce, made possible by digital technology
and used by businesses to engage customers more
authentically. The evolution of the internet from Web 1.0
to Web 2.0 enabled real-time, two-way communication
— a feature demanded by platforms such as Facebook,
YouTube, Twitter, Instagram, and TikTok — which in
turn opened the door to social commerce through real-
time responsiveness and consumer engagement.
India’s market is witnessing rising smartphone
ownership and growing adoption of digital payments.
Generation Z is increasingly shaping its own narrative
through advocacy and online expression, and more

The proliferation of smartphones among young people,
who spend considerable time on their devices, has been
central to enabling social commerce.
Users build connections, engage, and exchange peer

choices. Influencer content affects and alters buying
decisions at every stage of the customer journey, and as
businesses map out the social commerce path, influencer
marketing has reached its peak importance.
Growth of Social Commerce in India
Social commerce in India has grown exponentially in
recent years. The pandemic, combined with cheaper
data and wider smartphone access, pushed consumers
toward digital channels and online shopping. Social
media platforms — Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp,
YouTube, Pinterest, and others — have integrated smart
retail features to streamline the purchase journey and
reduce drop-off, reinforcing social commerce as a leading
shopping channel for a large consumer base.
This growth has narrowed the gap between the digital
and physical retail worlds. Even in remote areas, Tier-II
and Tier-III cities are rapidly adopting social commerce,
with businesses selling directly through chat and video
calls on social platforms. The integration of digital
payment methods such as UPI and mobile wallets has
further extended access to customers with limited
financial inclusion, building a substantial ecosystem for
social commerce nationwide.
India’s socio-economic conditions and the rise of
Generation Z are key drivers of this growth. Today’s
generation, already immersed in technology, values
new experiences such as AI-driven personalisation and
augmented-reality try-ons. Combining online shopping
with social media creates customer experiences that
genuinely resonate with this segment, and the trend
is expected to continue evolving as social commerce
becomes a streamlined feature of digital retail.

Review of Literature
The existing literature identifies social commerce as
an important area of research due to the rapid growth
of social media and its influence on online consumer
behaviour. Prior work has examined the roles of
trust, social interaction, user-generated content, and
technological readiness in shaping purchase decisions.
Liang and Turban (2011) introduced a comprehensive
framework for social commerce, explaining that it
integrates social media technologies with e-commerce
to facilitate transactions through social interaction,
recommendations, and user-generated content, thereby
enhancing engagement and creating value for both
consumers and businesses.
Hajli (2015) examined determinants of purchase intention
in social commerce and found that social support, trust,
and online communities significantly influence buying
intentions, concluding that consumer trust plays a vital
role in encouraging purchases through social platforms.
Kim and Park (2013) investigated how social commerce
characteristics affect consumer trust and purchasing
behaviour, finding that information quality, transaction
safety, and effective communication positively influence
trust, which in turn enhances purchase intention.
Yadav, de Valck, Hennig-Thurau, Hoffman, and Spann
(2013) proposed a conceptual framework describing how
social interaction, consumer participation, and digital
engagement contribute to successful social commerce
strategies, noting that participation builds long-term
value and strengthens brand loyalty.
Abed, Dwivedi, and Williams (2017) reviewed social
commerce adoption literature and identified trust,
perceived usefulness, social influence, and technological
readiness as major determinants of adoption behaviour.
Hair, Hult, Ringle, and Sarstedt (2022) emphasised the
application of PLS-SEM in marketing and consumer
behaviour research, providing methodological guidance
for assessing reliability, validity, and structural
relationships in behavioural studies.

consumers aged 18 to 27 who had actually purchased
through social commerce platforms. Respondents were
selected via convenience sampling and were required to
be frequent users of social media sites such as Facebook,
Instagram, WhatsApp, or YouTube for finding or
purchasing products. This sample size and profile is
sufficient for the PLS-SEM analysis used in this study.

Data Gathering
Primary data were collected via a structured
questionnaire in two parts. The first section captured
demographic information — age, gender, education,
occupation, and frequency of social commerce shopping.
The second section measured the two core constructs,
Social Commerce Adoption and Consumer Buying
Behaviour, on a five-point Likert scale (1 = Strongly
Disagree to 5 = Strongly Agree).
Respondents were Generation Z consumers in India
aged 18–27 with prior experience using platforms such
as Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp, or YouTube for
shopping. Responses were gathered mainly through
Google Forms, supplemented where possible by offline
questionnaires distributed at colleges, universities, and
workplaces.
All valid responses (n = 400) were collected through
convenience sampling. Data were coded and processed
in SPSS for descriptive statistics and reliability checks,
and AMOS/SmartPLS or regression analysis was used
for hypothesis testing.

Overall, the SmartPLS results show that Social Commerce
Adoption exerts a statistically significant, positive effect
on Consumer Buying Behaviour among Generation Z in
India (β = 0.73, p < 0.001). The model accounts for 53%
of the variance in buying behaviour (R² = 0.53), which
counts as moderate explanatory power, while the large
effect size (f² = 0.41) and positive predictive relevance (Q²
= 0.35) confirm that the relationship is not only significant
on paper but also substantively meaningful.
The collected data were analysed systematically using
IBM SPSS Statistics and SmartPLS 4 to examine the
relationship between Social Commerce Adoption and
Consumer Buying Behaviour among Generation Z
consumers in India. The analysis proceeded through a
series of sequential steps to ensure reliability, validity,
and robustness of the findings.
Initially, questionnaires were screened for completeness,
consistency, and missing values, and only valid
responses were retained. Descriptive statistics —
frequency, percentage, mean, and standard deviation —
were computed in SPSS to summarise the demographic
profile and describe the study variables.
The measurement model was then evaluated in SmartPLS.
Reliability was assessed using Cronbach’s Alpha
and Composite Reliability, while convergent validity
was examined through Average Variance Extracted
(AVE) and factor loadings. Discriminant validity was
established using the Fornell–Larcker Criterion and the
Heterotrait–Monotrait (HTMT) ratio, confirming that the
constructs were both reliable and distinct.
After confirming the adequacy of the measurement
model, the structural model was assessed to test the
hypothesis. Significance of the path coefficient was
determined through bootstrapping with 5,000 resamples.
Explanatory power was evaluated using R², effect size
using f², and predictive capability using Q².
Finally, the structural model results were interpreted
to determine whether Social Commerce Adoption
has a significant positive impact on Consumer Buying
Behaviour among Generation Z consumers in India,
and findings were presented using tables and figures
alongside discussion aligned with the research objectives
and hypothesis.
Conclusions
This study investigated the effect of Social Commerce
Adoption on Consumer Buying Behaviour among
Generation Z in India. Rapid advancements in digital
technology, rising internet penetration, widespread
smartphone use, and growing acceptance of social
media have drastically altered how consumers interact
with brands and make purchasing decisions. Social
commerce represents a significant evolution of traditional
e-commerce, merging shopping with social networking
so that consumers can discover products, receive
recommendations, interact with sellers, and purchase
directly on social platforms. As one of the most digitally
connected and socially active consumer segments,
Generation Z’s buying behaviour in this context is of
growing importance to businesses and marketers.
The study adopted a descriptive, quantitative design and
gathered primary data from 400 Generation Z participants
in India using a structured questionnaire, analysed via
SmartPLS (PLS-SEM). Results confirmed that both the
measurement model and structural model were reliable
and valid, and revealed a positive relationship between
Social Commerce Adoption and Consumer Buying
Behaviour — supporting the alternative hypothesis.
The analysis shows that Generation Z’s adoption of social
commerce influences their buying behaviour. Shoppable
media, targeted suggestions and recommendations,
influencer marketing, customer reviews and ratings,
social interactions, and hassle-free purchasing processes
all support and trigger Generation Z’s purchase
decisions.
Interactive content, targeted product recommendations,
influencer marketing, customer feedback, ratings,
social interactions, and convenient purchasing options
facilitate consumer choice and informed decision-
making. Generation Z relies heavily on social media as
an information source and typically conducts research
online before buying, making it important for companies
to engage in social commerce to build client relationships
and enhance satisfaction.
The findings underscore the importance of establishing
customer trust and making the shopping experience
as seamless as possible. Businesses should invest
adequately in security, communication, customer
support, and authentic influencer partnerships to
maximise their appeal to Generation Z, while actively
creating opportunities for customisation and user-
generated content to strengthen purchasing intention
and brand loyalty.
These conclusions are of considerable relevance
to marketers, online sellers, entrepreneurs, and
policymakers, who must continually rethink their
approaches to social commerce and update marketing
strategies. Effective engagement with Generation Z
increasingly involves technologies such as artificial
intelligence for better recommendations, live commerce,
and immersive shopping experiences.
In conclusion, this study offers empirical validation for
the claim that Social Commerce Adoption influences
Consumer Buying Behaviour among Generation Z in

Recommendations
Strategies that companies can utilise to understand and
target Generation Z in social commerce:
• Build consumer trust around social commerce
platforms. Lack of trust may be a major hindrance to
purchasing; flexible money-transfer options, flexible
return policies, and excellent customer service can
boost trust and improve conversion rates.
• Use influencer marketing to reach Generation Z
consumers where they already spend their time,
leveraging influencers’ credibility and audience
reach.
• Encourage word-of-mouth through incentives and
rewards for shoppers who share product reviews on
their own social media profiles, helping build trust
among new customers.
• Invest in personalisation by tracking consumer
preferences and recommending relevant products
— Generation Z expects a personalised shopping
experience, which strengthens purchase decisions.
• Stay current with emerging social commerce trends,
such as video-led product showcases, to enhance the
customer purchase experience.
• Pursue further research on trust, risk perception,
social influence, influencer credibility, electronic
word-of-mouth, and satisfaction to better understand
Generation Z’s engagement with social commerce.
• Extend the study to other demographic segments
such as Millennials and Generation Alpha.
Limitations of the Study
The study is limited to Generation Z in India, a segment
in which social-media-driven online shopping is
rapidly growing and which offers a sizeable base for
behavioural analysis. It relies on convenience sampling
within a defined geographical area, which can introduce
shortcomings for generalising the findings.
The cross-sectional design limits conclusions about how
social commerce adoption may influence consumer
buying behaviour over time. The study also focuses
on social commerce adoption without accounting for
variables such as trust, social influence, risk perception,
and positive electronic word-of-mouth, which could
also shape Generation Z’s social commerce buying
behaviour. Finally, reliance on self-reported data may
introduce survey bias among respondents.
Future Scope of the Study
Social commerce remains a growing topic of interest
among researchers and academicians. Future research
can extend this study to cover Millennials and Generation
Alpha, and incorporate additional variables such as social
influence, trust, risk perception, influencer credibility,
electronic word-of-mouth, and customer satisfaction.
A mixed-methods approach — combining qualitative
interviews with quantitative analysis — could deepen
understanding, while a longitudinal design could track
adoption trends over time and reveal how the dynamic,
unpredictable nature of technology continues to shape
consumer behaviour.
