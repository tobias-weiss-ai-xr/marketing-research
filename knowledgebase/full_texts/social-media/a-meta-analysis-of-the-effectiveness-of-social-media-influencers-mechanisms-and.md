Abstract
The use of social media influencers as persuasive marketing agents has become ubiquitous. However, a comprehensive
understanding of their effectiveness, mechanisms, and moderation is still lacking. To address this gap, we conducted a
meta-analysis of 71 papers, yielding 135 experimental studies and 571 effect sizes related to the impact of social media
influencers compared to other forms of brand endorsements. Our results reveal that social media influencers significantly
impact both consumer engagement and purchase intention, and they are relatively more effective than brand posts, virtual
influencers, and celebrities. A meta-analytic structural equation model analyzing the influencing mechanisms suggests
that social media influencers enhance consumer responses indirectly through their credibility and attractiveness. A meta-
regression analysis further shows that various factors—including characteristics of the influencers, message, products,
social media platforms, and followers, as well as their interaction with influencer size—moderate the effectiveness of
social media influencers. Notably, our results indicate that influencer size can address some inconsistencies in previous
research. For instance, small and medium-sized influencers are more effective in driving engagement, while larger influ -
encers have greater impact on purchase intention. Our research provides novel, rich, and nuanced insights that can help
managers with decisions such as: (a) when to choose influencers over alternatives, and (b) how to optimize their use.

Third, various theories try to explain the mechanisms
underlying social media influencer effects. One approach
emphasizes the direct impact of social media influenc -
ers on consumer response, highlighting their ability to
shape attitudes and behaviors directly (Liu & Lee, 2022).
Other theories adopt a process-oriented view and try and
uncover the mechanisms by which social media influenc -
ers impact responses. For instance, it has been opined that
the perceived credibility and attractiveness of social media
influencers help in enhancing attitudes and purchase inten -
tion (Schouten et al., 2020). Additionally, the hierarchy of
effects model (Lavidge & Steiner, 1961) has helped theo -
rize how social media influencers’ perceived credibility and
attractiveness, as conveyed through advertising and brand
attitude, can influence engagement and purchase intention.
Empirical support for various partial models exists but a
yet-to-be-developed-and-tested model that integrates these
theories and mechanisms might provide greater explanatory
power in describing how social media influencers impact
consumer responses.
Fourth, previous research has attempted to address the
varying impact of social media influencers by investigating
moderators such as their characteristics (e.g., size), mes -
sage characteristics (e.g., types of message appeal), type of
promoted product and associated benefits, and followers’
characteristics (e.g., age and gender). The results, however,
remain ambiguous. To illustrate, specific to influencer char-
acteristics, some research has indicated a positive impact of
social media influencers’ number of followers on purchase
intention (Giuffredi-Kähr et al., 2022; Leung et al., 2022a),
while other studies have found the opposite (Park et al.,
2021). In addition, certain areas, such as the role of social
media influencer age in moderating consumer responses,
has not received attention in previous research.
Social media influencers’ impact has been investigated
in previous meta-analyses (Ao et al., 2023; Han & Bala -
banis, 2024) and a conceptual paper (Leung et al., 2022).
A review paper and meta-analyses dealing with celebrity
endorsement (Elmi, 2022; Knoll & Matthes, 2017; Packer
et al., 2022; Schimmelpfennig & Hunt, 2020) provide rel -
evant information regarding the influence of endorsers as
well (see Web Appendix A for an overview of these papers).
However, there are some key differences between our and
earlier meta-analyses and review papers. In addition to
addressing the gaps identified in earlier research, we out -
line these differences below, representing some of our novel
contributions.
First, our meta-analysis comprising of a sample of 135
experimental studies allows us to examine the impact of
social media influencers’ characteristics as well as their
interaction with other factors on a wide range of consumer
responses. We find that social media influencers have a sig-
nificant positive impact on credibility, ad and brand atti -
tudes, engagement, and purchase intention. These findings
enable a more nuanced understanding of influencer market-
ing’s effectiveness, particularly in driving engagement and
purchase intention, which are crucial marketing objectives.
Second, following Knoll and Matthes’ (2017) evaluation
of the comparative effectiveness of traditional celebrities
with other forms of endorsement, this is the first meta-anal-
ysis to provide a comprehensive overview of social media
influencers’ comparative effectiveness—against not only
celebrities but also virtual influencers, brand posts on social
media, and no endorsement—on customer engagement and
purchase intention. The analysis indicates that social media
influencers are more effective than social media brand posts
and no endorsement for both, engagement and purchase
intention. They are also more effective than virtual influenc-
ers in triggering purchase intention, and more effective than
celebrities in driving engagement. Compared to celebrities
(Knoll & Matthes, 2017), which are more effective only vis-
à-vis no endorsement, influencers are more effective than
other endorsers, particularly in driving engagement and pur-
chase intention. While social media influencers are only one
of several product promotional options available to firms,
our findings provide valuable scientific insights into when
they should be prioritized over other options.
A significant distinguishing and novel feature of our
analysis is evidence of mediation providing deeper insights
into the mechanisms underlying the outcomes associated
with social media influencers. This model provides empiri -
cal support for Schimmelpfennig and Hunt ( 2020)’s lit -
erature review of mediation in celebrity endorsement and
Leung, Gu, Li, et al.‘s (2022) conceptual paper outlining
propositions in influencer marketing. Using a meta-analytic
structural equation modeling approach, we test three inde -
pendent models as well as an integrated model that com -
bines various mechanisms and theories explaining how
social media influencers drive customer responses. We find
that social media influencers indirectly enhance engagement

larger and smaller social media influencers. While larger
size is positively associated with fame, it often leads to a
loss of credibility, emphasizing the importance of align -
ing influencer size with the type of consumer response,
message, and product characteristics. In particular, larger
influencers drive purchase intention through their exten -
sive reach and benefit from evidence-based messages, such
as rational and usage-based endorsements, which enhance
their credibility, particularly for familiar and search prod -
ucts. In contrast, smaller influencers excel at engagement
by leveraging personal connections and thrive on abstract
and recommendation-based messages that feel more authen-
tic. These findings point to the conditional effectiveness of
social media influencers and can assist firms in identifying
settings that maximize such effectiveness.
Influencer marketing
Social media influencers build content-driven following on
platforms such as Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, LinkedIn, or
their own website to shape consumer perceptions, attitudes,
and behaviors. They achieve these goals by consistently
creating relatable, engaging content and directly interacting
with their audiences, which helps build trust and authentic -
ity (Leung et al., 2022). Such influencers are seen as social
media opinion leaders who integrate personal experiences
with product/service endorsements to maintain credibility
and relatability, often weaving promotions into their narra -
tives (Goldenberg et al., 2024; Hughes et al., 2019).
Social media influencer marketing leverages influenc -
ers’ reach and credibility, incentivizing them to showcase
offerings in a way that feels authentic and aligns with their
personal brand (Leung et al., 2022a; Pan & Misra, 2021).
This strategy capitalizes on the trust that influencers have
built with their audience, making endorsements more per -
suasive than traditional advertisements. As consumers grow
increasingly skeptical of advertising, influencer marketing
has become a cornerstone of modern digital strategies, driv-
ing awareness, engagement, and purchase intention across
industries (Beichert et al., 2024; Hughes et al., 2019).
The landscape of influencer marketing has changed sig -
nificantly, transitioning from traditional influencers and
celebrities to social media influencers and, more recently,
virtual influencers (see Table 1). Traditional influencers
rely on expertise and personal networks to influence niche
communities. They are similar to social media influencers,
with the exception that their network is limited to immedi -
ate family and friends. The internet, especially social media,
enables these individuals to develop their own content and
reach wider audiences within their areas of expertise, allow-
ing them to potentially become social media influencers.

their fame from fields such as acting, sports, entertainment,

The mechanisms underlying social media influencer
effectiveness are explored through several theoretical mod -
els. A direct effects model highlights how social media
influencers drive consumer responses without mediating
variables (Leung et al., 2022a, b). Social influence theory
further explains that social media influencers’ perceived
attractiveness and credibility mediate their impact, rein -
forcing consumer attitudes and behaviors (Schouten et al.,
2020). The hierarchy of effects model additionally suggests
that ad attitude and brand attitude mediate the impact of
social media influencers characteristics (e.g., credibility,
attractiveness) on behaviors like engagement and purchase
intention (Ozdemir et al., 2023; Qu & Baek, 2024). These
mechanisms underscore how social media influencers’ cred-
ibility and attractiveness as their main sources of persuasion
may drive downstream consumer responses. Despite these
useful findings, these partial models cannot fully explicate
mediation leading to the possibility of testing other combi -
natorial models.

Literature review
We now provide an overview of prior research, focusing on
three key areas: (a) social media influencer effectiveness,
including effects as compared to other forms of endorse -
ments; (b) mediation explored through a direct effects
model, a model based on social influence theory, or the hier-
archy of effects model; and (c) moderator analysis, includ -
ing social media influencer characteristics, message and
product types, as well as the influence of different social
media platforms and follower demographics (see Web

Extant research has explored social media influencers’
ability to engage audiences and drive purchase intention.
Despite some conflicting findings, primary studies reveal
that social media influencers are often effective in promot -
ing products and brands, leveraging their credibility and
attractiveness to positively influence consumer responses
(Cascio Rizzo et al., 2024). Research also indicates that
other endorsers, such as celebrities, are not always effective
in triggering all types of consumer responses. For instance,
Knoll and Matthes (2017) found that celebrity endorsements
do not significantly impact consumer attitudes or behavioral
intentions.

Conceptual background
We now delve into the conceptual background, structuring
it to help address the main contributions of the investigation
as delineated above, including: (1) evaluating the effective-
ness of social media influencers, (2) explaining the mecha -
nisms by which social media influencers drive consumer
responses, and (3) identifying boundary conditions of social
media influencer effectiveness.
Social media influencers effectiveness
To assess the effectiveness of social media influencers, we
categorize consumer responses into credibility, attractive -
ness, ad attitude, brand attitude, engagement, and purchase
intention (Table 2). The percentages in Table 2 refer to the
ratio of studies in our dataset that provide effects specific to
a response variable observed most frequently. While other
outcome variables, such as ad and brand recall, recognition,
and attention, have been studied earlier (e.g., Eisend and
Tarrahi (2016); Rosengren et al. ( 2020), these have been
explored in only two experimental studies and thus were not
considered in this meta-analysis.
A review of prior research highlights several recurring
issues, such as fragmented measurement approaches, narrow
behavioral outcomes, limited platform diversity, and chal -
lenges in replicating real-world dynamics and relationships.
One of the most notable limitations in prior research is the
inconsistency in measurement scales of critical constructs
(see Web Appendix C for a detailed review of these incon -
sistencies). For instance, credibility, a fundamental construct
in social media influencer scholarship, has been measured
as a standalone concept as well as through varied proxies
such as expertise (Lee et al., 2021), authenticity (Kapitan
evidence that smaller influencers are more engaging than
larger ones (Cascio Rizzo et al., 2024), there is not enough
support for other responses such as purchase intention.
Additionally, findings conflict regarding the effectiveness
of message and product types. For instance, while some
research highlights the superiority of emotional appeals over
rational appeals (Ozdemir et al., 2023), other studies report
contradictory results (Zhu et al., 2022). Similarly, there is
no clear consensus on whether social media influencers are
more effective in promoting hedonic versus utilitarian prod-
ucts (Belanche et al., 2024; Cho et al., 2024), new versus
familiar products (Wies et al., 2023; Zhu et al., 2022), or
search versus experience products (Hill et al., 2020; Xie et
al., 2022).
In addition to the abovementioned ambiguities, several
moderators remain underexplored. In particular, character -
istics like influencers’ and followers’ age and gender have
received little attention, with limited findings indicating that
social media influencers may be more effective with female
audiences (Han & Balabanis, 2024). Similarly, message-
related factors such as abstractness or endorsement type
(e.g., usage-based versus recommendation-based) are yet
to be systematically studied. Moreover, platform-specific
nuances such as differences in social media influencer effec-
tiveness on TikTok vs. Instagram, have not been addressed
adequately (Han & Balabanis, 2024). These gaps highlight
the need to examine novel and insufficiently addressed mod-
erators for a more comprehensive understanding of social
media influencer effectiveness across diverse contexts.

and attractiveness, influencers play a crucial role in influenc-

the hierarchy of effects, consumers are expected to initially

influence engagement and purchase intention (Barry, 2002),
aligning with the affect transfer model (MacKenzie et al.,

Effect size preparation and integration
Effect sizes were transformed into correlations which were
adjusted for measurement errors in the dependent variables.
When reliability data was unavailable in a paper or when a
single-item measure was used, we utilized the mean reli -
ability for that construct across all studies. Additionally, a
random-effects model and variance weights were used to
integrate effect sizes. To evaluate the homogeneity of effect
sizes, we employed the Q-statistic which assesses whether
the observed heterogeneity is greater than that expected by
chance alone (Higgins & Thompson, 2002).
To mitigate the impact of publication bias, we employed
several techniques. We calculated Rosenthal’s fail-safe
N (1991) to determine the number of zero-effect studies
required to reduce a significant effect to non-significant
(Rosenberg, 2005). The results of this analysis (Table 4)
indicate that a substantial number of zero-effect studies are
required to render the overall influencer effects non-signifi -
cant. Also, contour-enhanced funnel plots indicate the pres-
ence of some missing values in the plot for the effect sizes
referring to attractiveness, which could be an indicator of
publication bias for this variable. However, the results of
Egger’s test indicated symmetry of all funnel plots, suggest-
ing that publication bias was not severe (see Web Appendix
G).
Employing the criterion of standardized residuals greater
than 3 indicated three outliers in the effect sizes for the

Data collection
We identified all published and unpublished papers related
to social media influencer effects by searching popular data-
bases (Business Source Complete, Scopus, SpringerLink,
Emerald, JSTOR, Sage, ProQuest Digital Dissertations,
SSRN, and Google Scholar), using search terms includ -
ing “social media influencer” or “influencer marketing” or
“social media influencer marketing” or “online influencer
marketing” or “online influencer” or “digital influencer”
or “influencer endorsement” or “influencer advertising” or
“instafamous”, in the title, abstract, and keywords of these
databases. Additionally, we conducted a snowball search by
checking the reference lists of studies we identified, espe -
cially those with high citations on Google Scholar, and
integrated them into our database. The search process was
completed in July of 2024 and yielded 2,075 papers.
Several inclusion criteria were applied to develop the final
database. We removed all non-quantitative studies, includ -
ing qualitative review papers, as well as research based
on methods such as surveys, and focused on experimental
studies that tested causal relationships. For an experimental
study to be included, the investigation required comparing
the experimental “social media influencer” endorsement
condition with a control condition that could be a mes -
sage featuring a celebrity, virtual influencer, brand post on
social media, or no endorsement. Consequently, we elimi -
nated studies comparing different types of “social media
influencer” influencers, such as small versus large, as these
experiments lacked guidance about the general effectiveness
of “social media influencer” influencers. They did provide
insights about the comparative effects of different types of
“social media influencer” influencers, an aspect we address
in our meta-analyses by moderator variables. Finally, papers
were included in the database only if the information pro -

Results
Table 4 presents the average correlations between social
media influencers and consumer response, showing that
social media influencers have positive and statistically sig -
nificant effects on all responses, except for attractiveness.
While attractiveness is considered a key source of a social
media influencer’s persuasiveness, the results suggest that
influencers are perceived as equally attractive as other
endorsers, such as celebrities or virtual influencers ruling
out attractiveness as a unique source of influence for social
media influencers. In contrast, credibility emerges as the
strongest consumer response, highlighting it as a distinctive
and exclusive dimension for social media influencers. Fur -
thermore, the Q-statistic test results indicate the presence of
heterogeneity. We conducted a robustness check by exclud-
ing studies involving virtual influencers (see Web Appen -
dix L). The findings are largely consistent with the ones in
Table 4, but the effect on brand attitudes drops below the
significance threshold.
Table 7 presents the integration results separately for
the four control groups—celebrity, virtual influencer,
brand posts on social media, and no endorsement—exam -
ining their effects on engagement and purchase intention.
The results indicate that social media influencers are more
effective than celebrities in driving engagement but equally
effective in influencing purchase intention. Building on pre-
vious findings regarding the overall impact of social media
influencers, we posit that their credibility plays a crucial
role in engaging consumers with endorsed products, while
celebrity fame enhances persuasion, making celebrities as
effective as social media influencers in driving purchase
intention.
Additionally, social media influencers outperform virtual
influencers in driving purchase intention but not engage -
ment. These findings suggest that social media influencers’
authenticity gives them an advantage in encouraging pur -
chases, whereas both, virtual and social media influencers
are equally effective in engaging consumers. Furthermore,
social media influencers are more effective than both, brand
posts on social media and no endorsement strategies in
terms of both, engagement and purchase intention. These
findings support our prediction that social media influencers
serve as a more credible source compared to brand-owned
or brand-initiated advertisements, where consumer skepti -
cism remains a significant barrier to effectiveness.

Other forms of endorsements should be the search canvas
for these generations.
Adapt message strategy based on influencer size  The
effectiveness of social media influencers’ endorsements
depends on both their size and the type of messaging. For
larger influencers, a more concrete and evidence-based
message enhances credibility. A well-supported, logical
endorsement strategy—backed by facts, demonstrations, or
testimonials—resonates better with their audience. In con -
trast, for smaller social media influencers, more abstract and
recommendation-driven messages work better. Their audi -
ence responds more positively to broad, narrative-driven
intention, celebrity and social media influencers are supe -
rior. Celebrities can drive stronger brand credibility and
aspiration-based purchases, especially for high-status or
luxury products. Social media influencers offer a cost-effec-
tive alternative, particularly when they have niche expertise
and established trust within their communities.
Align influencer size with marketing objectives Social
media influencer size should be strategically selected
based on the campaign’s primary objective. For purchase
conversion, larger influencers are more persuasive due to
their broad reach, strong brand associations, and perceived
authority. They work well for widely known or mass-mar -
ket products where awareness and credibility drive conver -
sions. For brand engagement, smaller influencers tend to be
more effective, as they evoke greater audience trust, stron -
ger interaction rates, and greater perceived authenticity.
They are particularly useful for niche brands, new product
launches, and brands seeking long-term consumer relation -
ships rather than one-time conversions.

Influencers and sales
Much of the existing literature centres on intention-based
outcomes—such as purchase intention or willingness to
buy. Future research should move beyond such proxies and
incorporate observable behavioural indicators, including
transactional data, click-through rates, redemption metrics,
and conversion rates which offer assessment of influenc -
ers’ behavioral impact. Furthermore, comparative analyses
examining how different types of influencers (e.g., large vs.
small) influence behaviours—such as purchasing, or pay -
ing a price premium—can yield valuable insights. Such evi-
dence would help marketers make more informed decisions
about influencer selection and campaign design based on
performance-driven metrics.
Influencer marketing versus traditional advertising
While social media has emerged as a key component of mod-
ern marketing, influencer marketing research tends to com -
pare influencers only to other social media-based sources,
such as celebrities, virtual influencers, or brand-generated
posts. However, such a comparison overlooks broader stra-
tegic decisions marketers face, particularly when allocating
resources between influencer marketing and advertising in
traditional media (e.g., TV , radio). Future research should
address this gap by examining the persuasive effectiveness
of social media influencers vis-à-vis more traditional forms
of advertising. Such comparisons would provide a more
meaningful benchmark for assessing the relative perfor -
mance of influencer marketing and offer insights into when,
how, and why brands should favor influencer partnerships
over conventional media channels.
Message persuasiveness
Although message-level characteristics such as emotional -
ity or abstraction have been frequently examined, little is
known about how the strength and persuasiveness of the
product message itself shape consumer responses. Influ -
encer marketing research has yet to adequately address how
features such as argument quality, clarity of claims, benefit
salience, and product differentiation impact the effective -
ness of endorsements. These elements, central to advertis -
ing effectiveness, warrant greater attention in the context of
influencer campaigns. Future studies should explore how
persuasive message content interacts with influencer char -
acteristics to influence brand attitudes, purchase intentions,
and willingness to pay, offering more nuanced guidance for
message strategy design.

Match product strategy with influencer type and
size Marketing managers must align social media influ -
encer selection with product characteristics, particularly
in terms of product type and brand familiarity. For search
products—those that consumers can evaluate before pur -
chase (Jain et al., 2000; Jain & Posavac, 2001)—larger
social media influencers tend to be more effective. These
influencers can provide detailed information, comparisons,
and credibility-building content that aids decision-making.
For experience products—those that can be fully evalu -
ated only after use—smaller social media influencers are
more effective, as they can leverage personal narratives and
lifestyle integration to recommend these kinds of products
more persuasively. Additionally, for well-known brands and
established products, larger social media influencers help
reinforce credibility and scale messaging, making them
useful for mass-market promotions. However, for newer or
niche brands, smaller social media influencers may be more
effective.
Limitation and future research direction
Our meta-analysis is constrained by the currently available
empirical research. While some additional variables have
been mentioned in existing scholarship, there were insuffi -

sis. Moreover, prior research exhibits certain limitations and
issues, as discussed in the literature review. To address these
gaps, Table 3 outlines key areas for future research along

Measurement standardization
A key challenge for future research is the development of
reliable and valid scales for measuring critical constructs
such as influencer credibility, engagement, brand attitude,
and attitude toward the ad. Current studies often use incon -
sistent operational definitions hindering comparability and
limiting the ability to build cumulative knowledge. Future
work should prioritize conceptual clarity and develop stan -
dardized measurement frameworks that can be applied
across different platforms and influencer types. Doing so
would facilitate more robust comparisons, meta-analytic
synthesis, and theoretical advancement in influencer mar -
keting research.

Crisis management and influencer communication
When brands are perceived to have transgressed resulting in
a crisis (Jain & Jain, 2024), they may turn to social media
influencers to manage potential reputational damage (Singh
et al., 2020). However, the effectiveness of this strategy is
under-researched. Social media influencers may play a piv-
otal role in shaping public perception during product recalls
or data breaches, making it crucial to understand their role
in managing brand transgressions. Moreover, examining
crisis scenarios’ influence on the dynamics between influ -
encers and their followers can shed light on how crisis com-
munications impact overall brand perception and recovery.
Influencers and social marketing
Owing to their relationship with their followers, social
media influencers are increasingly assuming advocacy for
social causes, particularly those relevant to the younger
generations (White et al., 2019). Research into how effec -
tively social media influencers leverage their influence to
promote these causes and inspire pro-social behavior is
central to understanding their impact within the domain of
social marketing and corporate activism. Given that authen-
ticity is key to social media influencers’ success, examin -
ing how their personal values align with the causes they
associate themselves with will provide important insights
into fostering trust with their audience. Moreover, exploring
social media influencers’ collaborations with non-profits to
enhance engagement can contribute to the development of
more impactful social marketing strategies.
Virtual and AI-Based influencers
Virtual influencers (computer generated character) and
the more recent AI-driven virtual influencers (AI enabled
characters) represent a new frontier in marketing, offering
brands novel ways to engage with consumers. While our
meta-analysis suggests that virtual influencers are compa -
rable to social media influencers in fostering engagement, a
more granular comparison between different types of virtual
influencers such as those varying in the extent to which they
appear human (e.g., more human: @lilmiquela; less human:
@noonoouri) as well as characters (@nobodysausage)
offers an exciting avenue for investigation. Finally, collab -
orative endorsements between human influencers and AI-
based influencers need to be explicated as they may serve as
a potential future persuasion strategy.

Long-term effects of social media influencer marketing
Most research on social media influencer marketing has
focused on short-term outcomes like immediate engage -
ment and brand awareness. However, brands often pursue
sustained, long-term effects of social media influencer cam-
paigns. Do/can social media influencers foster lasting brand
loyalty, or does their impact wane over time? Investigating
whether the long-term impact of social media influencers
follows a specific pattern will help brands manage ongoing
campaigns. Brands need to understand how to maintain the
impact of social media influencers beyond the initial buzz,
ensuring that their marketing efforts yield lasting benefits.
Furthermore, the speculation that influencers’ impact is
mostly short-term could spur research to explore ways and
means to prolong this impact temporally.
Enhancing external validity
Future research should supplement the current experimental
and survey research with field studies incorporating which
can enhance external validity by simulating real-world
engagement scenarios, providing more generalizable and
actionable insights.
Platform-specific influencer strategies
Social media influencer marketing effectiveness is inter -
twined with the characteristics of each social media plat -
form. In the current meta-analysis and based on available
data, we operationalized social media platforms as Insta -
gram versus other social media platforms such as TikTok
and YouTube. The next step is to dive more deeply into
micro-level differences between these and various other
social media platforms. Such inquiries can provide specific
insights regarding social media influencers’ platform-spe -
cific strategies in light of differences across platforms (e.g.,
algorithmic variations) that influence persuasion.
Cultural sensitivity and global influencer campaigns
Social media influencer marketing is now global making
cultural sensitivity an important consideration in campaign
success. Brands and influencers must consider cultural
nuances in values, communication styles, thinking, and
behavior to engage audiences across different markets. Such
an understanding may lead to the need to adapt messages
and strategies to align with local cultures’ sensibilities,
ensuring that influencers as well as their content is relevant
and authentic across cultural contexts.
