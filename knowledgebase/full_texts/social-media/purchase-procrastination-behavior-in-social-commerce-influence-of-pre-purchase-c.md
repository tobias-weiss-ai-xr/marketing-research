ABSTRACT
The growth of social media has changed consumer purchasing behavior, with Generation Z often
delaying purchase decisions despite having easy access to product information, reviews, and
recommendations through social commerce. This study aims to examine the factors that influence
purchase delay behavior among Generation Z in the context of social commerce. This study employs
a quantitative causal-associative design, collecting data through surveys and analyzing them using
Structural Equation Modeling–Partial Least Squares (SEM-PLS). The findings demonstrate that
exposure to live streams on TikTok Shop plays a crucial role in triggering purchase delay behavior
among Generation Z consumers in Bali Province. The higher the intensity of live stream exposure,
the greater consumers’ tendency to postpone purchase decisions as they conduct a more in-depth
product evaluation. Live stream exposure also increases product comparison behavior and
strengthens the influence of the social environment in the decision-making process. Additionally,
product comparison behavior and the influence of the social environment were found to increase the
tendency toward purchase postponement. Consumers tend to compare various product alternatives
and consider reviews, comments, and recommendations from their social environment before deciding
to buy. These two variables were also found to mediate the influence of live stream exposure on
purchase postponement behavior.

INTRODUCTION
Advances in information and communication technology have transformed consumer
purchasing behavior, with social media evolving beyond a communication tool into a
social commerce platform that integrates social interaction and economic transactions
within a single digital environment (Zulfan & Hermawan, 2025). In Indonesia, this trend
continues to grow, as 65% of respondents identify social commerce as buying and selling
activities conducted through social media (Populix, 2022). Within this ecosystem,
consumers can simultaneously engage in social interactions, explore product alternatives,
access reviews, and receive recommendations. Social media has also become a major
source of product discovery and an important factor in consumer decision -making,
making the retail shopping experience increasingly interactive, real-time, data-driven, and
persuasive (Putri et al., 2024).

As illustrated in Figure 1, the social commerce environment has become increasingly
interactive, yet greater interactivity and information availability do not necessarily
accelerate purchase decisions. Consumers tend to engage in more extensive evaluation
processes before making a purchase, as easily accessible information encourages deeper
product assessment (Zhang & Xu, 2024). This finding is supported by global data from
the Capgemini Research Institute in 2025, which shows that 74% of consumers switch
brands when they find lower prices, while 71% actively seek information about product
quality (Capgemini, 2026). In addition, consumers consider promotional offers, service
convenience, and customer reviews, indicating that purchase decisions increasingly
involve repeated information search and evaluation processes, which ultimately
contribute to purchase delay behavior.
Purchase delay behavior refers to a situation in which consumers postpone purchasing
decisions despite already showing interest in a product. In social commerce, the
abundance of information, user reviews, and social recommendations encourages
consumers to continue evaluating alternatives before making a final decision (Ma et al.,
2022). One of the fastest-growing social commerce platforms is TikTok Shop, which
utilizes digital marketing tools such as short videos, influencers, affiliate programs, and
particularly live streaming to enhance consumer engagement (Ying et al., 2025). Live
streaming enables dynamic interactions between sellers and consumers, making it a key
source of product information and persuasion (Kang et al., 2021; Du et al., 2024).
Alfarisi and Sukaris (2024) stated that live streaming influences consumer decision-
making by providing rich and interactive information. Consumers commonly seek
additional information, compare prices and product quality, and evaluate other
consumers’ experiences before making purchase decisions (Armagan & Sevtap, 2022).
The richness of information delivered through live streams also encourages more
comprehensive cognitive evaluations (Liu et al., 2023). This behavior is particularly
evident among Generation Z, who are active in social commerce but remain selective by
evaluating information credibility, comparing alternatives, and considering social
feedback before purchasing.
According to consumer decision-making theory, information search and alternative
evaluation are essential stages of pre-purchase behavior (Petcharat & Leelasantitham,
2021). In the TikTok Shop context, live-stream product presentations and interactions
serve as important informational cues, while consumers also evaluate product risks,
benefits, suitability, purchase volume, and user reviews before deciding to buy (Lo et al.,
2022; Wang et al., 2024). Furthermore, technological convenience does not necessarily
lead to immediate purchases, as Generation Z tends to engage in information seeking,
source evaluation, and product comparison before forming purchase intentions (Said et

The study adopts the Stimulus–Organism–Response (SOR) framework to explain how

environmental influence represent the organism, and purchase delay behavior constitutes
the response. While prior research has predominantly emphasized the role of live
streaming in accelerating purchase decisions through information richness and direct
interaction, limited attention has been given to its potential to intensify cognitive
evaluation and consequently delay purchases among Generation Z consumers. This study
aims to analyze the influence of live stream exposure, product comparison behavior, and
social environmental influence on purchase delay behavior among Generation Z
consumers using TikTok Shop. The study contributes to the social commerce literature
by extending the SOR perspective and identifying pre-purchase evaluation mechanisms
that explain how live stream exposure may lead not only to purchase acceleration but also
to purchase delay.

LITERATURE REVIEW & HYPOTHESIS DEVELOPMENT
The Effect of Live Broadcast Exposure
Live streaming represents a digital marketing strategy that creates a high -intensity
stimulus environment in the consumer decision-making process (Qu et al., 2023).
Through live streaming, consumers are exposed to product presentations, limited-time
promotions, and real-time social interactions within a digital platform. The integration of
entertainment, communication, and commercial activities simultaneously makes live
streaming a unique marketing approach compared to conventional methods (Rehman et
al., 2025). Unlike traditional product promotion, live streaming enables consumers to
actively participate by asking questions, providing comments, and receiving immediate
responses from sellers, thereby creating a more interactive shopping experience (Xiao et
al., 2026).
In addition, live streaming helps consumers obtain product information through
demonstrations, direct communication, and real-time promotional activities (Sun et al.,
2019). From the perspective of the Stimulus–Organism–Response (SOR) framework, live
streaming functions as an external stimulus that can affect consumers’ internal cognitive
processes, evaluations, and subsequent behavioral responses  (Woodworth, 1929;
Mehrabian & Russell, 1974). The information richness and interactive nature of live
streaming may influence how consumers perceive products and make purchasing
decisions. Oktaviani et al. (2024) found that live streaming can strengthen purchase
decisions by increasing consumer confidence and reducing uncertainty. Zhang et al.
(2024) stated that greater exposure to information may encourage consumers to conduct
deeper evaluations before purchasing, potentially contributing to purchase delay
behavior.

The Effect on Purchase Delay Behavior
Product comparison behavior refers to the cognitive and behavioral activities
performed by consumers when evaluating two or more product alternatives before making
a purchase decision. This behavior occurs during the alternative evaluation stage of the
consumer decision-making process, where individuals compare product features, benefits,
prices, quality, and suitability based on the information they obtain (Engel et al., 1968).
In the context of social commerce, this behavior becomes increasingly important because
consumers are exposed to various product choices and information sources

Zafar et al. (2021) state that social environmental influence refers to the extent to which
consumer decisions are affected by information, opinions, recommendations, and
behaviors from other individuals. The social environment acts as an external source of
information that shapes consumer perceptions, attitudes, and evaluations toward
products. Product comparison behavior and social environmental influence are important
components of the pre-purchase evaluation process because consumers attempt to reduce
uncertainty and increase confidence before making a decision. The availability of multiple
product alternatives and social information can lead consumers to engage in more
extensive evaluations. As consumers compare more options and consider more external
opinions, the decision-making process may become longer. This condition can increase
purchase delay behavior, as consumers postpone transactions to obtain sufficient
information and select the most suitable option (Khan et al., 2022).

H4: Product comparison behavior has a positive effect on purchase delay behavior.
H5: Social environment influence has a positive effect on purchase delay behavior.

The Effect of Purchase Delay Behavior as a Mediator
Based on SOR theory, the influence of live broadcast exposure on consumer behavior
does not always occur directly. However, it can occur through internal processes such as
product comparison behavior and the influence of the social environment. These two
variables serve as evaluation mechanisms that consumers employ before making a
purchase decision (Zhao et al., 2020). Live broadcast exposure provides consumers with
product information, reviews, and social interactions that encourage them to evaluate
alternatives and consider other opinions before purchasing. Therefore, product
comparison behavior and the influence of the social environment can explain how live
broadcast exposure affects purchase delay behavior through consumers’ decision-making
processes.
Live broadcast exposure can influence consumer purchase delay behavior through
both cognitive evaluation and social mechanisms. In live streaming e-commerce, real-
time product information, demonstrations, and host interactions encourage consumers to
evaluate and compare available alternatives before making purchase decisions (Chen et
al., 2023b). Xiao et al. (2025) found that dynamic information cues in live streaming
enhance consumers’ information processing and product evaluation activities, suggesting
that product comparison behavior can mediate the relationship between live broadcast
exposure and purchase delay behavior. Moreover, social factors within live streaming
environments, such as streamer interaction, user comments, and community influence,
also shape consumer decision-making. Huo et al. (2023) demonstrated that social
interaction and environmental cues in live streaming affect consumer perceptions and
behaviors, leading consumers to seek social validation before purchasing. Therefore,
product comparison behavior and social environment influence may serve as mediating
mechanisms that explain how live broadcast exposure affects purchase delay behavior.

H6: Product comparison behavior mediates the effect of live broadcast exposure on
purchase delay behavior.
H7: Social environment mediates the effect of live broadcast exposure on purchase delay
behavior.

RESEARCH METHODS
The study employs a quantitative approach and utilizes a causal-associative design,
which aims to identify correlations and the influence between two or more variables.
Quantitative research involves sampling and data collection using research instruments
to test predetermined hypotheses (Sugiyono, 2018). This study was conducted in the
Province of Bali, which is one of Indonesia’s key economic hubs and premier tourism
destinations with a high level of digital penetration. Bali was selected as the research site
because its population is adaptable to technological advancements, particularly in e -
commerce activities.
All TikTok Shop customers in Bali who belong to Generation Z make up the
population for this study. This generation, born between 1997 and 2012, has previously
used the TikTok Shop platform to research  products. To select the study sample,
respondents representative of the study population’s characteristics were sought. This
study uses a non-probability sampling technique with a purposive sampling approach due
to the huge and ill-defined population. This approach was selected to ensure that the
sample is consistent with the research topic and accurately reflects the features of the
population being studied.
Purposeful sampling was used because not all consumers have experiences and
characteristics that align with the research focus. The developed criteria for respondents
were in line with the goals of the study. Hair et al. (2019) guidelines, which suggest that
the sample size for multivariate quantitative research should be at least 5 to 10 times the
total number of parameters in the research instrument, were used to determine the sample
size in this work.
This study uses quantitative data as the basis for analysis. Primary data are used as the
data source. This study uses primary data collected from informants through  a
questionnaire that satisfies the requirements for the variables of live stream exposure,
product comparison behavior, social environment influence, and purchase delay behavior
among Generation Z users of TikTok Shop. This study’s data gathering approach is a
survey, which involves sending out a closed-ended online questionnaire. Google Forms
is used to distribute the questionnaire.

The data analysis technique used in this study is SEM-PLS. The SmartPLS software
is used to analyze the data. The analysis process comprised Average Variance Extracted
(AVE), Composite Reliability, Cronbach’s Alpha, and Heterotrait -Monotrait Ratio
(HTMT) to evaluate validity and reliability; measurement model evaluation through
outer loadings; and descriptive analysis to characterize respondents’ traits and research
variables. Additionally, the effect size (f²), predictive relevance (Q²), and coefficient of
determination (R²) were used to assess the structural model. Hypothesis testing was
conducted to determine the importance and direction of the correlations between the
research variables.

on TikTok Shop and actively engages in pre -purchase evaluation through product
comparison and the use of information from their social environment. This high level of
evaluation activity is also accompanied by a strong tendency among respondents to
postpone purchase decisions. These findings indicate that before making a purchase,
respondents tend to consider various product alternatives, promotions, reviews, and other
social information to reduce uncertainty and ensure the product’s suitability for their
needs.

DISCUSSION
The findings of this study collectively demonstrate that exposure to live streaming on
TikTok Shop has a positive and significant effect on purchase delay behavior, both
directly and through the mediating roles of product comparison behavior and social
environmental influence. Kang et al. (2021) and Hameed et al. (2025) stated that these
findings are consistent with the Stimulus–Organism–Response (SOR) framework, in
which live streaming exposure functions as an external stimulus that triggers consumers’
internal evaluation processes before generating specific behavioral responses .
Furthermore, Consumer Decision Making Theory explains that consumers tend to seek
out and evaluate various alternatives prior to making a purchase decision, meaning that
the greater the volume of information received, the longer the evaluation process becomes
(Said et al., 2023).
Live streaming exposure was found to actively encourage consumers to compare
products across multiple sellers, encompassing aspects such as price, quality,
specifications, brand reputation, and promotional offers. The more frequently consumers
are exposed to live broadcasts, the greater their tendency to compare product alternatives
before purchasing. This reinforces Zhang et al. (2023)’s argument that high exposure to
digital information broadens consumers’ reference sets and encourages more in-depth
evaluation, ultimately prolonging the decision-making process. Chen et al. (2023a) affirm
that alternative evaluation and product comparison behavior significantly increase
consumers’ tendency to delay purchase decisions.
Live streaming also strengthens the influence of the social environment in consumer
decision-making. Generation Z consumers tend to pay close attention to the number of
products sold, consumer reviews, recommendations from their social circles, and the
opinions of influencers or hosts during broadcasts. Yin et al. (2025) explain that various
social cues emerging during live streaming, such as user comments and influencer
recommendations, reinforce social influence in the product evaluation process. This
aligns with Petcharat and Leelasantitham (2021), who assert that live streaming amplifies
social influence in product evaluation. Additionally, Zafar et al. (2021) confirm that
interactive features in social commerce enhance social influence throughout the process
of evaluating and making online purchase decisions.

The growing influence of the social environment subsequently encourages consumers
to adopt a more cautious stance and postpone purchasing decisions until they obtain
greater confidence (Ahn & Lee, 2024). This is supported by Ridwan et al. (2025), who
explain that social influence constitutes part of consumers’ internal evaluation process
before a behavioral response is generated. The higher the consumer’s engagement with
the social information received, the greater their tendency to postpone the purchase
decision until they gain stronger confidence in the selected product  (Faronsyah &
Hidayah, 2025). Forghani et al. (2022) further state that social influence in digital

postponement of purchases until sufficient information and social validation are obtained.
These findings confirm that product comparison behavior and social environmental
influence serve as significant mediators in explaining the relationship between live

leverage information obtained from live broadcasts to evaluate product alternatives more
thoroughly, thereby extending the decision-making process. These findings advance the
understanding of digital consumer behavior dynamics, particularly among Generation Z
users of TikTok Shop, and carry important implications for live-streaming-based
marketing strategies.

CONCLUSION
Based on the research findings, it can be concluded that live stream exposure on
TikTok Shop significantly influences purchase delay behavior among Generation Z
consumers in Bali Province. Greater exposure to live streams encourages consumers to
postpone purchasing decisions by providing extensive product information that promotes
more careful evaluation. Live streaming functions not only as a promotional tool but also
as an information environment that shapes consumer decision-making through product
demonstrations, interactions, reviews, and recommendations. Additionally, product
comparison behavior and social environment influence significantly increase purchase
delay behavior. Consumers who compare alternatives and rely on social information,
such as reviews, comments, recommendations, and influencer opinions, tend to take
longer before purchasing. These findings confirm that both variables serve as important
mechanisms linking live stream exposure to purchase delay behavior.
This study contributes to the development of the SOR framework in social commerce
by demonstrating that digital stimuli do not always accelerate purchase decisions but can
trigger cognitive evaluation processes that lead to postponement behavior. Sellers and
marketers should recognize that increasing engagement through live streaming needs to
be balanced with strategies that reduce consumer uncertainty and simplify decision -
making. This study is limited to Generation Z TikTok Shop users in Bali Province, which
may limit the generalizability of the findings. Future research is suggested to examine
other consumer groups, different social commerce platforms, and additional factors such
as perceived risk, trust, or impulsive buying tendencies to provide broader insights into
purchase delay behavior.
