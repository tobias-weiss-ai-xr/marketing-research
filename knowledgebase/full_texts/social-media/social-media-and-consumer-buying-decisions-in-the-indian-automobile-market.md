Abstract. The evolving dynamics of the Indian automobile market
have intensified the consumerization of the industry and created new
challenges for car manufacturers. Today, consumers no longer
demand only a car as a product, but expect a comprehensive
experience pa ckage that includes information, interaction, trust,
service quality, and post -purchase engagement. In this context,
social media has become a powerful platform not only for corporate
marketing and promotion, but also for consumer research,
comparison, opinion formation, and decision -making. The internet
has become a dominant source of consumer -driven communication,
where information, feedback, opinions, reviews, and sentiments
spread rapidly and often influence consumers more effectively than
traditional c orporate messages. This paper examines the role of
social media in consumer buying decisions in the Indian automobile
market, with particular attention to passenger cars. The study is
based on an exploratory empirical survey of 300 young adults who
are educated, technologically active, smartphone users, and active
participants in social media platforms. The findings show that social
media influences consumer awareness, information search,
evaluation of alternatives, attitude formation, and post -purchase
communication. However, the final automobile purchase decision is
still more likely to occur offline due to the high -involvement nature
of automobile products. The study also identifies gender -based
differences in internet use, trust in social media, and info rmation-
seeking behaviour. The article concludes that automobile companies
in India should integrate traditional and digital media strategies,
strengthen brand communities, improve online information quality,
and use social media not only as a promotional channel but also as
a consumer engagement and relationship-building tool.
Keywords: competitive differentiation; social media;
consumerization; Indian automobile industry; passenger cars;
digitization; consumer buying decision; online word -of-mouth;
automobile marketing; consumer engagement; brand reputation;
social media marketing.

Introduction. Marketing has long been a cornerstone of business
success, with many well -established companies achieving prominence
through effective marketing strategies. An integrated communication
strategy has played a crucial role in shaping these successes. In the p ast,
traditional mass communication methods, such as television, print media,
newspapers, magazines, and direct mail, were the primary ways to reach
audiences. These media channels were used to deliver messages aimed at
shaping consumer awareness, attitudes, and perceptions toward brands.
However, as the business environment has evolved and technology has
advanced, marketing messages have shifted to engage consumers on a deeper
level, whether physically or emotionally. A major shift in this evolution is
the rise of social media, which has been embraced by companies to connect
with consumers in innovative ways. Social media refers to web -based and
mobile platforms that facilitate two -way communication, transforming the
interaction between companies and consumers into an engaging dialogue
(Baruah, 2012).
Social media takes many forms, including blogs, wikis, podcasts, video
content, social networking platforms, microblogging platforms, and other
online communication tools. Unlike traditional media, which was largely
one-way communication from producer to a udience, social media enables
customers to actively participate in the creation and dissemination of content.
This has changed the nature of marketing because consumers are no longer
passive receivers of brand messages. They are active participants in the
communication process.
The concept of social media has revolutionized marketing by enabling
brands to engage directly with consumers and encourage interaction. The
growth of digital media has been particularly noteworthy, with digital
advertising increasing by 44.5% in 2014 compared to 2013 (FICCI-KPMG,
2015). This reflects the growing impact of social media on consumer
behaviour and decision-making.
Consumer-generated media now plays a crucial role in shaping buying
decisions, influencing everything from brand awareness and information
gathering to post-purchase evaluations. Social media platforms have become
increasingly trusted sources of information, often competing with traditional
corporate communication in credibility. Today, social media not only
informs consumers but also influences their purchasing decisions, making it
an essential tool for brands.
For businesses, social media represents a low-cost promotional avenue
that offers vast reach, connects with large audiences, and generates
substantial traffic. It provides a platform for companies to showcase their
products and services, engage with custom ers, and promote brand
awareness. In addition to marketing, social media offers benefits in
marketing research, public relations, customer engagement, and product
development. Marketers are increasingly focusing on social media -driven

The Indian automobile industry is a key driver of economic growth,
combining innovation, technology, and creativity. In 2020 -2021, the sector
produced 26.3 million vehicles, including passenger vehicles, commercial
vehicles, two -wheelers, and three -wheelers, marking a 16.2% increase
compared to the previous year (SIAM, 2021). The industry accounts for
7.1% of India’s GDP and employs more than 37 million people. India is the
fifth-largest vehicle producer globally and is projected to become the third -
largest automobile market by 2025, with 6.5% of global vehicle sales (Make
in India, 2021).
As internet usage and social media grow, the automobile sector has a
unique opportunity to connect with consumers through digital platforms. In
2021, more than 43% of traffic to automotive websites came from search
engines, while social media also became a n important source of consumer
engagement (Statista, 2021). Consumers use automotive websites and social
media platforms to compare features, check prices, research companies, read
reviews, follow new launches, and engage with brands.
The Indian automobile industry concluded FY 2021 with a 7.2% growth
in unit sales (ICRA Research Services, 2021). It is one of the largest
automobile markets globally, with annual production of 26.3 million
vehicles in FY 2020 -2021, reflecting a 16.2% incr ease from the previous
year. The industry contributes about 7.1% to India’s GDP (India Brand
Equity Foundation, 2021). India is currently the fifth -largest vehicle
producer in the world and is projected to become the third-largest automobile
market by 2025 (Make in India, 2021).
A study by Capgemini revealed that 78% of Indian consumers would
be likely to purchase a vehicle from a specific car manufacturer or dealer if
positive comments and reviews were found on social media (Economic
Times, 2021). The increasing role of online re search and web usage in
vehicle purchases is evident, with 95% of consumers in India researching
vehicles online in 2021, up from 86% in 2010. Given this trend, it is crucial
for automobile companies to understand how to engage with social media
effectively and connect with potential customers. Social media platforms
also give existing customers a voice, allowing brands to gain valuable
feedback and strengthen relationships with loyal consumers.
For automobile companies, a strong digital presence and effective
social media optimization are essential to staying competitive and
connecting with today’s technologically active consumers. Therefore, this
paper explores the role of social media at variou s stages of the consumer
decision-making process and proposes strategies that can help automobile
companies design comprehensive social media campaigns. These strategies
can assist not only in attracting potential customers, but also in retaining and
nurturing customer loyalty over the long term.

Literature Review. Social media has become a top priority for many
executives at leading global companies. Decision -makers are increasingly
looking for ways to use platforms such as Facebook, Twitter, LinkedIn,
YouTube, and other social networking sites for profitable business purposes.
Social media serves as an open platform for interactive communication,
enabling individuals to become content creators and share information with
others through dynamic engagement.
Stokes defines social media as content created, published, and shared
by individuals online, including blogs, images, videos, and other digital
formats (Sharma, 2013). Strauss and Frost also describe social media as
online tools and platforms that enable i nternet users to collaborate on
content, share insights and experiences, and connect for both business and
personal purposes (Sharma, 2013). Since its emergence, social media has
become a preferred mode of communication, and companies have
recognized it as a strategic marketing tool.
Several factors have contributed to the rapid growth of social media as
a marketing tool. These include technological advancements, such as
expanded broadband access, improved software, and the increased
availability of computers and mobile phones. Social factors, such as the rapid
adoption of social media by younger age groups, and economic factors, such
as the growing affordability of digital devices, have also played an important
role.
Social media plays a crucial role in delivering effective customer
support (Sharma, 2013). It serves as an interactive platform where customers
can share opinions about products or services. Social media is often
perceived as more trustworthy than traditio nal media channels.
Consequently, a company’s reputation is no longer controlled only by its
corporate communications team, but is also shaped by technologically active
customers who can quickly share positive or negative experiences.
This creates a major challenge for organizations. Companies need to
monitor social media actively in order to understand feedback, ideas,
complaints, and opinions related to their products or services. Such
monitoring allows companies to understand the rol e of social media in
influencing consumer decision -making and to respond effectively to
consumer expectations.
The evolution of social media can be understood as part of the broader
development of communication systems. Early long -distance
communication methods included audio and visual forms of messaging. The
postal system in ancient Persia, telegraph, telephone, and radio all
contributed to the development of long -distance communication. In the
twentieth century, the internet created a new era of digital communication.
Early tools such as email, ARPANET, USENET, Bulletin Board Systems,
IRC, and Napster paved the w ay for online interaction. In the twenty -first
century, platforms such as Friendster, Flickr, Orkut, Facebook, Twitter, and

YouTube transformed online communication. Today, platforms such as

Social media has become an integral part of online life and has
transformed how consumers obtain information and express opinions.
Consumers are part of, or aspire to belong to, various online groups that may
significantly influence their purchasing behavi our (Solomon, 2010).
Traditionally, purchasing decisions were based largely on information from
mass media. However, online social networks now play an important role in
shaping customer buying behaviour (East et al., 2013).
Several social networking groups influence consumer behaviour.
Primary groups include people with close relationships, such as family
members or close friends. Secondary groups are broader and may include
several primary groups. Informal groups include ind ividuals with common
interests or cultural backgrounds. Formal groups are more structured and
organized. Virtual groups include online communities, blogs, and forums
where people connect digitally.
Reference groups are particularly important because their opinions and
experiences influence consumer purchase decisions. Reference groups
influence consumers in three major ways. First, informational influence
occurs when consumers seek information about different brands from others.
Second, utilitarian influence occurs when consumers make decisions based
on the satisfaction or expectations of others in their social circle. Third,
value-expressive influence occurs when consumers choose brands that
reflect their personal values or social identity (Solomon, 2010).
Online word-of-mouth communication refers to the sharing of positive
or negative experiences by consumers. It can result from satisfaction or
dissatisfaction with a product or service. Word -of-mouth is a form of
personal communication that influences consu mer decisions regarding
brands, products, services, companies, or organizations (Evans et al., 2009).
Its influence is shaped by factors such as tie strength, homophily, and source
credibility (Brown, 2007). Tie strength refers to the strength of interpersonal
relationships within social networks. Homophily refers to similarity between
group members. Source credibility refers to the perceived expertise and
objectivity of the person providing information.
Online word-of-mouth occurs through social networking sites, blogs,
online forums, virtual communities, and review or rating websites
(Goldsmith & Horowitz, 2006). One of its main advantages is that it allows
consumers to gather insights from a broad range  of people, not only from
individuals they personally know. Information search is a crucial step in
decision-making because consumers seek information to reduce uncertainty
and perceived risk (Peterson & Marino, 2003). Many consumers rely on
online reviews  and recommendations before making purchase decisions,
particularly when considering new products (Kim & Srivastava, 2007).

Thurau and Walsh (2003) identified five motivations that drive
consumers to engage with online opinions and allow them to influence
purchase decisions. These motivations include seeking purchase -related
information, social orientation through information, community
membership, incentives and rewards, and learning about new products.
Webster (2011) found that many American social media users maintained at
least one social media profile, while a significant proportion followed
favorite brands, products, or co mpanies. These findings show that online
social networks have become important sources of product -related
information for consumers.
In India, social media has become a major marketing channel. As of
2015, 75% of India’s online population could be classified as digital
consumers, meaning they engaged with digital media in some form during
the purchasing journey (Ernst & Young, 2015). The number of internet users
in India reached 302 million by December 2014, while active social media
users surpassed 106 million (Ernst & Young, 2015). India was among the
top three countries globally in terms of Facebook users, with more than 100
million active users on the platform (Ernst & Young, 2015).
With this large user base, social media has moved from being a
supplementary marketing tool to becoming a primary communication
channel. Facebook has been the dominant platform, followed by Twitter and
YouTube. These platforms have become important for bus inesses that seek
to build a strong online presence. The three main goals that social media
helps companies achieve are building brand awareness, disseminating brand
news, and fostering community and customer engagement.
In the Indian automobile market, these developments are highly
relevant. Automobile purchases are high-involvement decisions that require
extensive information search, comparison, and evaluation. Consumers
increasingly use automotive websites and social media platforms to compare
features, check prices, evaluate brand reputation, read reviews, and interact
with companies. Therefore, the role of social media in automobile buying
decisions requires systematic analysis.
The influence of social media on automobile buying behaviour can be
understood through the consumer decision -making process. Social media
affects not only the initial awareness of automobile brands, but also
information search, evaluation of alternatives, purchase intention, and post-
purchase communication. Since automobiles are high-involvement products,
consumers usually combine online information with offline experience,
particularly dealer visits and test drives. Therefore, social media should be
considered not as a replacement for traditional sales channels, but as an
important supporting mechanism that strengthens consumer awareness, trust,
comparison, and engagement.

Aims. The aim of this study is to examine the role of social media in
consumer buying decisions in the Indian automobile market, with particular
attention to the way social media influences awareness, information search,
evaluation of alternatives, attitude form ation, purchase intention, and post -
purchase communication.
The objectives of the study are to understand the concept of social
media marketing and its applications in the Indian automobile industry; to
identify the influence of social media on the consumer decision -making
process; and to suggest social media strategies for automobile companies at
different stages of consumer decision-making.
Methodology. An exploratory empirical study was conducted to
examine the behaviour of young adult students in relation to the automobile
market. The study focused on information search, brand perception, social
media use, and decision -making processes. The main purpose  was to
understand the role and significance of social media in brand building and
automobile marketing.
The sample consisted of 300 young adults. The respondents were
evenly split between genders, educated, technologically active, smartphone
users, and active users of social media for various purposes. They were
enrolled in BE, BBA, and BCA programs. The res pondents came from
families with substantial financial means and owned at least one bike, while
a few also owned cars. Although the respondents were students with no

continues to play a strong role in shaping consumer perceptions, while actual
automobile purchases still tend to occur offline.

Discussion. The results of the study confirm that social media has
become an important part of the consumer decision -making process in the
Indian automobile market. However, its role is not uniform across all stages
of decision-making. Social media is especially influ ential in the early and
middle stages of the process, including awareness, information search,
opinion formation, comparison of alternatives, and evaluation of brands. It
also plays an important role after the purchase because consumers use social
media to  share feedback, post reviews, and communicate with other
consumers and companies.
The findings show that consumers increasingly rely on social media for
information about automobile products. A large share of respondents agreed
that social media is an essential tool for actively seeking information about
automobile products. Reviews and comments on social media also influence
attitudes toward automobile brands and encourage consumers to try new
brands. These findings support the literature on online word -of-mouth,
which argues that consumer-generated content influences purchase decisions
by reducing uncertainty and providing social proof (Evans et al., 2009;
Goldsmith & Horowitz, 2006; Kim & Srivastava, 2007).
At the same time, the results indicate that social media does not fully
replace traditional media or offline purchasing. Television remains an
influential medium for learning about automobile brands. This is particularly
important in the Indian automobile market, where automobile purchases are
high-involvement decisions and consumers still value traditional brand
communication. The actual purchase decision also tends to happen offline,
even when consumers use social media and websites for research and
comparison. This confirms that for high-value products such as automobiles,
social media functions primarily as an information, engagement, and trust -
building channel rather than as a complete substitute for physical
dealerships.
Gender differences in the findings are also important. Boys were more
likely than girls to use the internet to research automobile products, to trust
social media when uncertain about products, and to consider social media
more effective in capturing customer attention. Girls, however, placed higher
importance on brand reputation, while boys prioritized product
specifications. These findings suggest that automobile companies should not
use identical social media messages for all consumer groups. Instead, th ey
should develop segmented communication strategies. For example,
campaigns aimed at male consumers may emphasize technical

The finding that only 42% of respondents expressed interest in online

The study also shows that the quality of information is the most
important factor influencing online automobile purchase intention.
Consumers expect accurate, complete, transparent, and easily accessible
information about vehicle features, prices, comparis ons, financing options,
safety, and service support. If online information is incomplete or difficult
to navigate, consumers may lose trust and return to traditional information
channels. Therefore, automobile companies should improve website
usability, social media content quality, and digital customer support.
Online advertising was found to be the leading factor encouraging
consumers to revisit automobile brand social media pages. However, online
advertising should not be limited to promotional messages. It should be
interactive, informative, visually appealing , and connected with consumer
needs. Rewards and loyalty programs can also support engagement, but the
study shows that their influence is weaker than online advertising and other
miscellaneous factors. This means that consumers are attracted not only by
incentives but also by relevant content, useful information, community
interaction, and brand experience.
Overall, the results demonstrate that social media is becoming a
powerful tool in the Indian automobile market, but its effectiveness depends
on how strategically companies use it. Social media should be used to build
brand awareness, provide reliable info rmation, encourage peer interaction,
respond to customer concerns, support online word -of-mouth, and maintain
long-term customer relationships. The strongest approach is not to replace
traditional media or dealerships, but to combine traditional media, dig ital
platforms, social media communities, and offline experiences into one
integrated consumer journey.

role in shaping consumer buying decisions in the Indian automobile market.
While the amount of time consumers spend online is steadily increasing,
traditional media, particularly television, continues to hold an influential role
in shaping consumer behaviour. Consumers actively use online platforms to
search for information, compare alternatives, read reviews, share feedback,
and communicate with brands. However, the actual automobile purchase
decision tends to happen offline.

In the consumer decision -making process, social media primarily
influences consumers up to the evaluation of alternatives stage. Even at this

turn to online platforms to share feedback and experiences. This strengthens

The study also concludes that online shopping remains a secondary
motivation for many consumers. Most consumers prefer to conduct online
research, evaluate alternatives, and then make their purchase in-store. When
evaluating automobile brands, brand reputa tion and product specifications
are the most influential factors. Authenticity and quality of information
provided online are key drivers for consumers who consider online
purchases, followed by ease of navigation and recommendations from
others.
Online advertising is the leading factor encouraging consumers to
revisit automobile brand social media pages, while rewards and loyalty
programs also play a supporting role. Younger consumers spend significant
time on social media platforms to gather and validate information about
products and brands, but they remain hesitant about purchasing high -value
products such as automobiles online.
For automobile companies, this shift signals the need for innovative
strategies that make social media a more effective consumer engagement
channel. Companies should recognize differences in how males and females
use social media because these variations m ay influence engagement and
decision-making processes. Social media has the potential to transform how
automobiles are marketed and sold if companies use these platforms
strategically.
Automobile companies should create compelling online rewards and
loyalty programs, design eye -catching advertising campaigns, emphasize
brand reputation and vehicle specifications, and offer incentives for online
engagement. They should also simplify website navigation and improve the
online customer experience. Since automobiles are high -involvement
products, companies should connect digital engagement with physical
experience by offering test drives at dealerships and exclusive online
booking benefits.
Building strong brand communities is also important. Such
communities can provide a space for customers to share experiences, post
reviews, interact with companies, and support brand loyalty. By integrating
traditional and digital media, automobile compani es can create a more
