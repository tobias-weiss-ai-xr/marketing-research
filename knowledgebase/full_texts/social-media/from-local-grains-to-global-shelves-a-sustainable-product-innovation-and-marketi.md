Abstract

Nutsafir Cookies is a processed food MSME from Lombok that has been operating since 2012 and
has successfully expanded into export markets through the Shopee Export Program and Shopee
FLEXI. Amid sales growth reaching hundreds of percent annually through digital channels, the owner
faces increasingly intense competition from similar souvenir MSMEs in Lombok. This community
service article aims to formulate a sustainability -oriented product innovation and marketing
operations strategy to help Nutsafir Cookies maintain its competitiveness. The approach used is
descriptive qualitative research based on document analysis and a single case study, drawing on
secondary data from official publications, mass media, and the partner's social media accounts,
which is then triangulated with findings from prior studies on similar MSMEs in Indonesia. The
analysis maps the partner's condition onto the Dynamic Capability Theory and Triple Bottom Line
frameworks. The findings indicate that the product innovation strategy can b e directed toward
diversifying local grain-based variants and adopting more environmentally friendly packaging, while
the marketing operations strategy needs to integrate production efficiency with a social
sustainability narrative, namely the empowerment of local women, as a point of differentiation in
the souvenir market. This pattern is consistent with findings on batik MSMEs, culinary MSMEs, and
other women-led MSMEs in Indonesia that have applied similar strategies. This article is based on
secondary data, so its conclusions are analytical-strategic in nature and have not been empirically
tested directly at the partner's site.

Keywords: product innovation, marketing operations, sustainability, competitiveness, MSME

INTRODUCTION
The processed food MSME sector is one of the pilla rs of the local economy in tourism
destinations such as Lombok, particularly in the regional souvenir segment. Nationally, MSMEs
contribute up to 61 percent of Indonesia's Gross Domestic Product and absorb around 97 percent
of the national workforce (Coordinating Ministry for Economic Affairs of the Republic of Indonesia,
2023), while at the destination level, local food products also help sustain regional identity and the
visitor experience, as shown by Everett & Aitchison (2008) in their study of culinary  tourism in
Cornwall, England. One long-standing player in the Lombok souvenir segment is Nutsafir Cookies, a
home-based business founded by Sayuk Wibawati in Mataram in September 2012 (Inside Lombok,
2026). Its main products are cookies made from local West Nusa Tenggara grains, such as red beans,
corn, cashews, soybeans, and lebui, a black bean variety native to Lombok that has traditionally
been known as a vegetable ingredient, processed without preservatives, artificial coloring, or
artificial sweeteners.
Nutsafir Cookies recorded sales growth of up to 20 percent after joining e -commerce
platforms, with annual increases of up to hundreds of percent through Shopee, and has been
included in the Shopee Export Program and Shopee FLEXI as part of its internat ional market
expansion (Inside Lombok, 2026). Behind this achievement, the owner has openly stated that the
business is under pressure due to the growing number of competitors, requiring resilience,

This statement points to two interrelated issues. First, on the product side, the current range,
ten nut-based flavor variants, risks losing its differentiating appeal if not regularly refreshed, while
similar competitors in Lombok continue to emerge offering relatively comparable products. Second,
on the marketing operations side, Nutsafir Cookies' presence across many channels at once, from
Shopee, Tokopedia, WhatsApp Business, TikTok, and Instagram to its official website, is not
necessarily matched by adequate production capacity and local raw material supply chain
management to sustain seasonal or export demand surges.
This operational challenge cannot be separated from the issue of sustainability. Nutsafir
Cookies' raw materials depend on grain supplies from local NTB farmers, while its production
process involves local women's groups. Both aspects represent social and economic sustainability
capital that, if managed strategically, can become a source of competitive advantage difficult for
competitors to replicate, while also risking becoming a weak point if the supply chain and
production capacity are not managed according to sustainable operational principles.
This community service article aims to formulate a sustainability-based product innovation
and marketing operations strategy for Nutsafir Cookies, linking the Dynamic Capability Theory and
Triple Bottom Line frameworks to the context of a home-scale local food MSME. Theoretically, this
article enriches the application of both frameworks to regional souvenir sector MSMEs, a context
relatively rarely discussed compared to their application in large corporations. Practically, this
strategic framework is designed as a reference for the partner and similar processed food MSMEs
in Lombok facing similar competitive pressure. The novelty of this article lies in its attempt to
explicitly connect the dimensions of product innovation and marketing operations with the three
pillars of sustainability, namely economic, social, and environmental, while triangulating the
formulated strategy with empirical evidence from similar MSMEs across various regions of
Indonesia.

LITERATURE REVIEW
Dynamic Capability Theory explains an organiza tion's ability to integrate, build, and
reconfigure internal and external competencies to respond to rapidly changing business
environments, through three main processes: sensing, seizing, and transforming (Teece, 2007). In
the context of Nutsafir Cookies,  sensing capability is reflected in its response to export
opportunities through the Shopee Export Program and Shopee FLEXI, while seizing and
transforming capabilities still need strengthening on the production and product development side,
so that digital sales growth does not rest solely on promotional strategy but also on adaptive
internal capacity.
This finding aligns with empirical evidence from Indonesian MSMEs. Prabowo et al. (2021)
found that dynamic capability positively influences sustainable competitive advantage among small
businesses in the food-and-beverage and fashion sectors in Palembang, with entrepreneurial
marketing acting as a mediator. This finding reinforces the relevance of the Dynamic Capability
framework for analyzing food MSMEs such as Nutsafir Cookies, given the comparable sector and
business scale.
The Resource-Based View states that sustainable competitive advantage stems from
resources that are valuable, rare, inimitable, and non-substitutable, or VRIN (Barney, 1991). NTB's
distinctive grain-based raw materials, particularly lebui, which is rarely processed into snacks, along
with the involvement of local women's groups in production, meet these VRIN characteristics, as

Product innovation in food MSMEs generally proceeds through stages of opportunity
identification, concept development, testing, and new product launch (Cooper, 1990). At the home-
industry scale, these stages are typically carried out more simply and quickly than in large
corporations, yet still require clear direction so that new product variants genuinely respond to
market preferences rather than merely adding to the number of variants.
Sustainable Operations Management emphasizes the importance of integrating
environmental and social dimensions into everyday operational decisions, from raw material
procurement and production processes to packaging and distribution (Kleindorfer et al., 2005). This
framework aligns with the Triple Bottom Line concept, which assesses business performance not
only in terms of profit but also people and planet (Elkington & Rowlands, 1999). Simpson et al.
(2004) showed that environmental responsibility among SMEs, including in raw material
management and production processes, can be a source of competitive advantage rather than
merely a compliance burden. For an MSME such as Nutsafir Cookies, the profit dimension is
reflected in digital sales growth, the people dimension in the empowerment of local women in the
production process, while the planet dimension can be strengthened through its existing practice
of production without preservatives and artificial coloring, as well as the potential development of
more environmentally friendly packaging.
On the packaging dimension specifically, Marvinita et al. (2026) found that the adoption of
environmentally friendly packaging among culinary MSMEs in Bandar Lampung is influenced by
perceived environmental value, which shapes a positive attitude toward green innovation before
ultimately driving adoption intention. This stimulus-organism-response pattern indicates that
packaging changes in food MSMEs cannot simply be introduced as a technical decision, but need to
be communicated as part of the environmental values the business upholds.
On the people dimension, Veranita et al. (2023) showed that digital marketing training for
women-led MSME actors contributes to strengthening family economic resilience, while also
building women's confidence and social role within the business. This finding is relevant to the
position of local women's groups in Nutsafir Cookies' production process, which could potentially
be strengthened through a similar approach.

METHODS
This article uses a descriptive qualitative approach with a document analysis design and a
single case study of the Nutsafir Cookies MSME in Mataram, Lombok (Yin & Campbell, 2017). This
design was chosen because field conditions at the time of writing did not allow for interviews or
direct observation with the partner, so the prima ry data sources are public documents and
verifiable secondary literature.
The data used comprise three groups. First, public documents on Nutsafir Cookies, namely
mass media publications, the official website, and the business's social media accounts. Second,
verified academic literature on dynamic capability, operational sustai nability, and marketing
strategy among similar MSMEs in Indonesia, used as a comparison and basis for triangulating
findings. Third, policy documents relevant to Indonesia's processed food MSME sector.
The preparation of this article was carried out in four stages. The first stage was collecting
public documents on Nutsafir Cookies' condition. The second stage was identifying operational and

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Partner's Operational-Marketing Conditions and Challenges
Nutsafir Cookies currently operates across six sales and promotion channels at once: Shopee,
Tokopedia, WhatsApp Business, TikTok, Instagram, and its official website, nutsafirlombok.com, in
addition to two physical outlets on Jalan Angsoka in Mataram and at Lombok International Airport.
This multichannel presence supports digital sales growth reaching hundreds of percent annually,
yet according to the owner's own statement, this growth has occurred alongside increasingly
intense competitive pressure from similar souvenir MSMEs in Lombok. This condition suggests that
downstream strength, i.e., sales channels, is not necessarily matched by upstream strength, i.e.,
production capacity and local raw material supply chain management.
Sustainability-Based Product Innovation Strategy
The proposed product innovation is directed at two targets. First, strengthening
differentiation of local-ingredient-based variants, which have long been Nutsafir Cookies' strength,
for instance by developing seasonal variants from local grains not yet widely processed by
competitors, building on the lebui or Lombok black bean base that has become part of the product's
identity. Second, developing more environmentally friendly packaging, for example by reducing
single-use plastic layers in souvenir packaging, in response to growing consumer attention to the
environmental aspects of the products they purchase. This pattern is consistent with Marvinita et
al.’s (2026) finding that the adoption of eco-friendly packaging among culinary MSMEs is more
effective when communicated as an environmental value the business holds, rather than merely as
a material change to the packaging.
Sustainable Marketing Operations Strategy
On the marketing operations side, the proposed strategy is not to add new channels but to
integrate existing channels through a shared content calendar and a consistent sustainability
narrative across each channel. The story of local women's empowerment in the production process
can be featured as marketing content that differentiates Nutsafir Cookies from competitors, while
also strengthening the people dimension within the Triple Bottom Line framework. This approach
aligns with Veranita et al. (2023) finding that digital marketing training for women-led MSMEs not
only increases income but also strengthens women's confidence and social role in the business, a
value worth featuring in Nutsafir Cookies' marketing narrative. On the production operations side,
strengthening partnerships with local grain suppliers needs to be planned on a more structured

Comparison with Studies on Similar MSMEs in Indonesia
The strategies formulated in the two preceding sections can be triangulated with findings
from prior research on similar MSMEs in Indonesia. Among batik MSMEs in Central Java, Nuryakin
(2018) found that competitive advantage and product innovation significantly affect marketing
performance, a pattern supporting the argument that local-ingredient-based variant diversification
at Nutsafir Cookies could yield similar results. Among small food-and-beverage businesses in
Palembang, Prabowo et al. (2021) found that dynamic capability affects sustainable competitive
advantage, reinforcing the argument that the sensing and seizing capabilities Nutsafir Cookies has
already shown through its participation in the Shopee Export Program need to be followed by
transforming capability on the operational side. Among culinary MSMEs in Bandar Lampung,
Marvinita et al. (2026) found that eco-friendly packaging adoption is influenced by consumers'
perceived environmental value, supporting the argument that Nutsafir Cookies' packaging changes
need to be accompanied by a clear environmental narrative. Among women-led MSMEs in West
Bandung, (Veranita et al., 2023) found that digital marketing training strengthens economic
resilience as well as women's social role, supporting the argument that the story of local women's
empowerment at Nutsafir Cookies could be reinforced through a similar training approach. The
consistency of patterns across these four studies indicates that the strategy formulated for Nutsafir
Cookies aligns with published empirical evidence on similar MSMEs across various regions of
Indonesia, even though each business's context remains distinct.

Discussion
The three strategies above are interconnected within the Dynamic Capability framework.
Sensing capability is reflected in reading the competitive pressure reported by the owner, seizing
capability in formulating sustainability-based product and packaging innovation, while transforming
capability is reflected in the effort to integrate production operations and multichannel marketing
so that they work in concert rather than in isolation. From a Triple Bottom Line perspective, this
strategy balances the profit dimension through sales and export growth, the people dimension
through local women's empowerment, and the planet dimension through preservative -free
production and the potential for more environmentally friendly packaging. These three dimensions
need to be managed together, since strengthening one dimension while neglecting the others risks
creating imbalance, for example rapid sales growth without adequate production capacity and raw
material supply.
CONCLUSION
A sustainability-based product innovation and marketing operations strategy has the
potential to help Nutsafir Cookies maintain its competitiveness amid the growing number of
competitors in Lombok's regional souvenir segment. In practical terms, the partner can begin with
the steps most feasible given current resources, such as developing a shared content calendar that
features a sustainability narrative, before moving toward larger changes such as developing eco-
friendly packaging. Triangulation with research on batik MSMEs, culinary MSMEs, and women-led
MSMEs across various regions of Indonesia shows that this strategic pattern is consistent with
published empirical evidence, even though each MSME operates in a different context. This article's
limitation lies in its data sources, which consist entirely of secondary data and comparative
literature, without direct testing at the partner's site, so the resulting conclusions are analytical-
strategic in nature and have not measured implementation impact empirically. Further research or
community service work on similar MSMEs is recommended to measure the impact of
implementing this strategy more concretely, for instance through changes in sales volume,
production cost efficiency, or the level of local women's group involvement.
