ABSTRACT: This research aims to analyze the influence of social media
marketing and online customer reviews on purchase decisions via
Instagram Reels, with brand awareness as a mediating variable. The
research adopts a quantitative approach with a population of Pink flash
Instagram followers who have previously purchased its products. The
number of samples used in this research was 100 respondents using
techniques purposive sampling. Data was collected using an online
questionnaire. The data analysis method used is a range  of scales and
Structural Equation Model (SEM) with software SmartPLS 4.0. The results
indicate that brand awareness significantly influences purchase decisions.
Online customer reviews have a significant positive influence on brand
awareness but do not directly impact purchase decisions. Social media
marketing positively i nfluences both brand awareness and purchase
decisions. Furthermore, both online customer reviews and social media
marketing have indirect positive effects on purchase decisions through
brand awareness.
Keyword: social media marketing, online customer review, purchase
decision, brand awareness

INTRODUCTION
In the digital era, purchasing decisions are increasingly shaped by consumers' online interactions,
particularly on social media platforms. These decisions are not merely influenced by traditional marketing
efforts but are also driven by peer -generated content such as online reviews. Social media marketing and
online customer reviews, therefore, have emerged as crucial components of the consumer decision-making
process. According to Ul Huda et al. (2021) , purchasing decisions are a reflection of consumer awareness,
shaped by both internal desires and external stimuli, including brand visibility and information availability.
The rise of social media has redefined marketing strategies across industries. Social media marketing
leverages platforms like Instagram to engage customers, promote products, and enhance brand visibility
(Pamungkas and Pramesti, 2021). Simultaneously, online customer reviews, considered a form of electronic
word of mouth, provide prospective buyers with authentic insights, shaping their perception of product
quality and credibility (Almana and Mirza, 2013; Rahmayanti and Dermawan, 2023). While both social media
marketing and online customer reviews are known to influence consumer behavior, their interrelationship
with brand awareness, defined as a consumer’s ability to recognize and recall a brand, remains an area of
continued investigation (Purwaningsih and Susanto, 2020).

In Indonesia, the rapid growth of internet penetration has accelerated digital engagement. As of
January 2023, 213 million Indonesians, approximately 77 percent of the population, are internet users (BPS,
2023). Instagram stands out as one of the most popular platforms, ranking fourth globally with over 89
million users in the country. Features like Instagram Reels allow brands to create short, engaging videos,
which can significantly impact both brand awareness and purchase intentions (Elvina et al., 2024).
This study focuses on the case of Pinkflash , a Chinese beauty brand that has successfully leveraged
Instagram Reels for marketing its products in Indonesia. Launched in 2020 and certified by BPOM,
Pinkflash has rapidly gained traction among Indonesian consumers, reaching IDR 1.5 billion in lipstick sales
within a two-week period. By analyzing how social media marketing and online customer reviews influence
purchasing decisions through brand awareness, this study provides insights into how brands can optimize
their digital strategies.
Although the importance of digital platforms in marketing continues to grow, limited empirical
research has explored the indirect effects of online engagement, especially through Instagram Reels, on
brand awareness and purchase decisions. Therefore, this r esearch aims to fill that gap by examining the
direct and mediating effects of social media marketing and online customer reviews on consumer behavior,
using Pinkflash as the case context.

LITERATURE REVIEW AND HYPOTHESIS DEVELOPMENT
Brand Awareness and Purchase Decision
Brand awareness refers to the degree to which consumers are able to recognize or recall a brand as
part of a particular product category (Keller, 1993) . It encompasses various levels, including brand
recognition (the ability to confirm prior exposure to the brand), brand recall (the ability to retrieve the brand
from memory when prompted by a product category), and top-of-mind awareness (the first brand recalled
when a product class is mentioned) (Aaker, 1996). These components form the cognitive foundation upon
which consumers build their evaluation and decision-making processes.
In marketing literature, brand awareness is acknowledged as a key factor in reducing perceived risk
and simplifying purchase decisions, especially in low -involvement or first -time buying contexts. When
consumers are aware of a brand, they are more likely to associate it with previously held positive experiences
or social proof, thereby decreasing the uncertainty typically associated with trying a new product (Esch et
al., 2006). This is particularly relevant in the context of online purchases, where physical inspection is limited,
and consumers rely on brand cues for assurance.
From a theoretical perspective, the Hierarchy of Effects Model and the AIDA Model both support
the centrality of brand awareness in driving purchasing behavior. The Hierarchy of Effects Model suggests
that consumer response to marketing follows a sequence from cognitive (awareness and knowledge) to
affective (liking and preference) to conative (conviction and purchase) stages (Lavidge & Steiner, 1961) .
Similarly, the AIDA Model (Attention, Interest, Desire, and Action) places brand awareness as the initial
step in capturing consumer attention before higher-order processes like interest and desire take place (Strong,
1925). These models underscore that without awareness; subsequent consumer responses are unlikely to
occur.
Empirical research consistently supports the notion that higher levels of brand awareness are
associated with greater purchase intention and decision outcomes. For instance, Hutter et al. (2013) found
that brand awareness significantly predicted consumer engagement and purchase intention in social media
contexts. Likewise, Huang and Sarigöllü (2012) , through a cross -national study, demonstrated that brand
awareness positively affects brand choice likelihood across product categories and cultural settings. In
another study, Kwon and Lennon (2009)  found that brand awareness increased consumers' perception of
product quality, which in turn influenced their decision to purchase fashion items online.
Given conceptual clarity and empirical support, it is proposed that:

H1: Brand awareness positively influences purchase decisions.

Online Customer Review and Brand Awareness
Online customer reviews (OCRs) refer to user -generated evaluations, ratings, or written feedback
posted on digital platforms regarding products or services that consumers have used or purchased (Chevalier
& Mayzlin, 2006) . These reviews are a subset of electronic word of mouth (e -WOM), but unlike private

recommendations, OCRs are publicly accessible and persist over time. Their asynchronous and scalable
nature allows them to reach a wider audience, thereby influencing perceptions even before direct brand
engagement occurs (Duan et al., 2008).
OCRs play a pivotal role in shaping brand awareness, particularly in online shopping contexts where
consumers cannot physically evaluate products. When potential consumers encounter product reviews
during their browsing journey, those reviews often serve a s early touchpoints with the brand. Positive and
consistent reviews contribute to higher exposure and recognition, while also providing informational value
that strengthens recall and understanding of the brand (Baek et al., 2012) . Thus, OCRs act as a de facto
advertising mechanism that raises brand visibility in digital spaces.
The influence of OCRs on brand awareness is supported by the Information Adoption Model
(Sussman & Siegal, 2003), which posits that information quality and source credibility affect an individual’s
likelihood of internalizing that information. Reviews perceived as useful and credible enhance cognitive
processing and brand learning, leading to increased familiarity with the brand. Moreover, the Two-Process
Model of Brand Awareness suggests that consumer familiarity is reinforced through repeated exposures to
relevant cues, such as product mentions, logos, or user experiences in reviews (MacInnis et al., 1991).
Empirical research supports the link between OCRs and brand awareness. For example, Bruhn et al.
(2012) found that user-generated content significantly impacts brand perception and brand awareness more
than firm -generated content. Similarly, Akbar et al. (2023)  demonstrated that online customer reviews
contribute positively to brand awareness and subsequently influence online purchase intention. In the
cosmetics industry specifically, Park and Lee (2009)  observed that product reviews enhanced brand
recognition and recall among young consumers. These studies suggest that the visibility and trust associated
with OCRs are instrumental in enhancing consumer familiarity with a brand, especially in crowded or
competitive markets.
Based on this theoretical and empirical foundation, the following hypothesis is proposed:

H2: Online customer reviews positively influence brand awareness.

Online Customer Review and Purchase Decision
Online customer reviews (OCRs) have emerged as a central factor influencing consumer purchase
decisions in digital marketplaces. Defined as publicly accessible evaluations or feedback provided by
previous buyers, OCRs serve as a form of electronic word of mouth that influences prospective consumers’
evaluations of product quality, credibility, and risk (Mudambi & Schuff, 2010). These reviews provide social
proof and experiential insights that substitute for the lack of physical interaction with products in online
settings.
The decision-making process in online purchases is typically more information -driven, especially
given the uncertainty and perceived risk associated with buying intangible or unfamiliar goods. OCRs
function as risk-reducing tools by offering third -party validation and detailed product experiences, which
consumers use to form judgments about the likelihood of satisfaction (Park & Lee, 2009). This mechanism
is particularly influential in high -involvement categories such as beauty, fashion, and electronics, where
perceived performance and satisfaction are heavily dependent on social consensus.
The Elaboration Likelihood Model (ELM) provides a theoretical foundation to understand the
differential impact of OCRs on consumer behavior. According to this model, consumers process persuasive
information through either a central route (careful consideration of message content) or a peripheral route
(reliance on heuristics like source credibility or volume of reviews) (Petty & Cacioppo, 1986). In the context
of OCRs, detailed and credible reviews processed through the central route may influence actual purchase
intentions, whereas brief or inconsistent reviews may be processed peripherally and fail to generate a
meaningful behavioral impact.
Despite these theoretical foundations, empirical findings on the direct effect of OCRs on purchase
decisions remain mixed. Some studies, such as those by Senecal and Nantel (2004) , report that OCRs
significantly influence product choice, especially when consumers are undecided. However, other research
notes that the direct effect may diminish when reviews are perceived as excessive, contradictory, or lacking
relevance to the consum er’s personal needs (Lee et al., 2008) . Additionally, when mediated by brand
awareness or product involvement, the direct path from OCRs to purchase behavior may become
statistically insignificant (Zhang et al., 2014).

In contexts where consumers already have established brand familiarity or other dominant cues, the
influence of OCRs on purchase decisions may be indirect or even negligible. This suggests that OCRs may
not always independently drive consumer behavior unless accompanied by reinforcing factors such as strong
brand recognition or emotional connection with the reviewer content.
Based on this nuanced understanding, the following hypothesis is formulated:

H3: Online customer reviews do not significantly influence purchase decisions.

Social Media Marketing and Brand Awareness
Social media marketing (SMM) refers to the strategic use of social media platforms to promote
products or services through content creation, user engagement, and brand interaction (Godey et al., 2016).
It incorporates activities such as posting visual content, engaging with audiences through comments or
direct messaging, running targeted advertising campaigns, and utilising platform -specific features like
Instagram Reels, Stories, and Live videos. As d igital environments evolve, SMM has become a central
avenue for building brand presence and equity.
Brand awareness, defined as the ability of consumers to recognize or recall a brand under different
conditions (Keller, 1993), is one of the primary objectives of SMM. Social media platforms provide repeated
exposure to branded content, which enhances memory encoding and recall. This mechanism is reinforced
through interactive elements such as likes, shares, tags, and comments t hat allow consumers to actively
participate in brand conversations, increasing both visibility and familiarity (Ashley & Tuten, 2015) . As a
result, brand awareness grows not only through passive observation but also through social validation and
peer reinforcement.
The theoretical basis for this relationship is grounded in the Mere Exposure Effect (Zajonc, 1968),
which posits that repeated exposure to a stimulus increases an individual’s preference for it. In the context
of social media, the frequent display of brand logos, hashtags, and influencer endorsements can lead to
increased cognitive accessibility of the brand. Additionally, the Integrated Marketing Communication (IMC)
framework supports the idea that consistent and engaging messages across platforms enhance brand recall
and recognition (Kliatchko, 2005) . Social media, as a multi -modal and interactive IMC tool, is especially
effective in achieving this outcome.
Empirical studies support the positive impact of SMM on brand awareness. Hutter et al. (2013) found
that user engagement with brand content on Facebook significantly improved brand awareness and
purchase intention. Similarly, Bilgin (2018) reported that SMM activities on Instagram and Facebook had a
statistically significant effect on brand awareness and customer loyalty. In the cosmetics sector, Jing (2024)
demonstrated that visual storytelling and influencer -based SMM practices strengthened brand awareness
and perceived brand uniqueness among Gen Z consumers.
Furthermore, platform-specific features such as Instagram Reels enable brands to create dynamic,
short-form video content that captures attention quickly and promotes brand memorability. These features
are particularly suited for younger audiences, who ten d to consume content in fast -paced formats and rely
on visual branding cues (Sheldon & Bryant, 2016).
Given conceptual and empirical evidence, this study posits the following hypothesis:

H4: Social media marketing positively influences brand awareness.

Social Media Marketing and Purchase Decision
Social media marketing (SMM) plays a critical role in influencing consumer purchase decisions by
providing interactive, engaging, and visually appealing content that guides customers through the decision-
making process. Through platforms like Instagram, brands are able to communicate directly with consumers,
deliver product information, and build emotional connections that contribute to purchasing behavior
(Godey et al., 2016).
The consumer decision process in digital settings often begins with exposure to brand content. SMM
allows for real-time engagement, enabling consumers to access product reviews, influencer endorsements,
and branded storytelling that enhance trust and stimu late buying interest. According to the Technology
Acceptance Model, perceived usefulness and perceived ease of access to information can drive behavioral

244

intentions, including purchase decisions (Davis, 1989) . SMM contributes to this by reducing information
search costs and increasing convenience, particularly through features like Instagram Reels that deliver
short-form content quickly and accessibly.
Empirical studies affirm the positive relationship between SMM and purchase decisions. For instance,
Yadav and Rahman (2018)  found that SMM activities significantly impact purchase intention, especially
when content is perceived as authentic and visually engaging. Similarly, Kim and Ko (2012)  showed that
SMM enhances not only brand equity but also leads to actual buying behavior, particularly in lifestyle and
fashion segments.
Given the literature and theoretical grounding, the following hypothesis is proposed:

H5: Social media marketing positively influences purchase decisions.

Online Customer Review, Brand Awareness, and Purchase Decision
The influence of online customer reviews (OCRs) on purchase decisions often occurs indirectly
through the development of brand awareness. While some OCRs may not immediately drive purchase
behavior, they play a crucial role in building familiarity, recogni tion, and perceived credibility of a brand.
This cognitive foundation, in turn, supports later stages of the consumer decision-making process (Park &
Lee, 2009).
The Hierarchical Model of Advertising Effects provides a framework for understanding this mediated
relationship. The model posits that cognitive responses such as awareness precede affective responses and
conative actions like purchasing (Lavidge & Steiner, 1961).  OCRs contribute to the cognitive stage by
offering product information and user experiences that help form brand knowledge. As consumers
repeatedly encounter consistent and positive reviews, their ability to recall and recognize the brand increases,
establishing brand awareness (Baek et al., 2012).
Once brand awareness is developed, it reduces uncertainty and builds trust, which are essential for
purchase decisions, especially in digital retail environments where physical inspection is not possible.
Empirical findings support this pathway. For exampl e, Akbar et al. (2023)  found that OCRs significantly
enhanced brand awareness, which subsequently led to stronger online purchase intentions. Similarly, El-
Said (2020) demonstrated that review-based brand knowledge served as a mediating factor between online
information and hotel booking intentions.
Based on literature and theoretical support, the following hypothesis is proposed:

H6: Online customer reviews positively influence purchase decision through brand awareness.

Social Media Marketing, Brand Awareness, and Purchase Decision
Social media marketing (SMM) influences purchase decisions not only through direct engagement
but also indirectly through the development of brand awareness. In the digital environment, repeated and
meaningful exposure to brand content on platforms such as  Instagram, Facebook, or TikTok increases
brand salience and cognitive accessibility. Once brand awareness is established, it serves as a crucial
psychological step toward shaping purchase intention (Keller, 1993).
This mediated relationship is supported by the Hierarchy of Effects Model, which outlines a
progression from awareness to knowledge, liking, preference, conviction, and finally to purchase (Lavidge
& Steiner, 1961). SMM operates at the initial levels of this hierarchy by attracting attention and generating
brand knowledge through engaging visual and interactive content. When brand awareness is successfully
built, it reduces uncertainty and facilitates easier decisio n-making, particularly in competitive product
categories.
Empirical studies confirm the indirect role of brand awareness in linking SMM to purchasing
behavior. Godey et al. (2016) showed that social media marketing efforts significantly improved brand equity,
and this enhancement positively affected consumer behavior. Similarly, Bilgin (2018) demonstrated that
brand awareness acts as a significant mediator between SMM activities and consumer loyalty and purchase
outcomes. These findings suggest that SMM can be more effective when it builds strong brand recognition,
which in turn influences final purchasing decisions.
In light of this evidence, the following hypothesis is proposed:

H7: Social media marketing positively influences purchase decision through brand awareness.

METHODS
This study employs a quantitative explanatory research design to investigate the relationships among
social media marketing, online customer reviews, brand awareness, and purchase decisions. Explanatory
research is appropriate for testing hypotheses and un derstanding causal relationships between variables
using statistical methods (Creswell, 2014) . The research aims to determine both the direct and indirect
effects of social media marketing and online customer reviews on purchase decisions, with brand awareness
as a mediating variable.
The population of this study includes Instagram users who follow the official account of
@Pinkflashcosmetics and have previously purchased Pinkflash  products. To obtain data from relevant
respondents, the study applied a non-probability sampling technique using purposive sampling. This method
ensured that only individuals who had both viewed Pinkflash content through Instagram Reels and made a
purchasing decision were included. A total of 100 valid responses were collected and analysed.
Primary data were obtained through a self-administered online questionnaire distributed via Google
Forms. Respondents were asked to confirm that they had watched Pinkflash Instagram Reels and purchased
a product from the brand. Secondary data related to the brand profile and market engagement were gathered
from the official Pinkflash Instagram page and relevant marketing reports.
All variables in the study were measured using multiple items adapted from established scales in
previous research, with minor modifications to suit the context of cosmetic product marketing on Instagram.
A five -point Likert scale was used for all items, w here 1 indicated “strongly disagree” and 5 indicated
“strongly agree.” Social media marketing was measured using indicators adapted from Godey et al. (2016),
while online customer review items were adapted from Park and Lee (2009). Brand awareness was measured
based on Keller’s (1993) brand equity framework, and purchase decision items were drawn from Kotler and
Keller (2012).
Data analysis was conducted using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM)
through SmartPLS version 4.0. This method was selected due to its suitability for models with multiple
constructs, small to medium sample sizes, and predictive analysis (Hair et al., 2019). The analysis involved
two main stages. First, the measurement model was assessed by evaluating indicator reliability, internal
consistency reliability, convergent validity, and discriminant validity. Second, the structural model was tested
to examine the  significance of hypothesised  relationships using path coefficients, R -square values, and
bootstrapping to assess the significance of both direct and indirect effects.

Discussion
This study examined how social media marketing and online customer reviews influence consumer
purchase decisions in the cosmetics industry, with brand awareness serving as a mediating variable. The
findings contribute to a deeper understanding of the mecha nisms through which digital engagement
strategies affect consumer behavior, particularly in the context of Instagram Reels and the Pinkflash
cosmetics brand.
The results affirm that brand awareness plays a crucial role in shaping consumer purchasing decisions.
This finding aligns with Keller's (1993)  conceptualization of brand awareness as a key dimension of
customer-based brand equity. Consumers who are aware of a brand are more likely to consider it during the
decision-making process. This is especially important in product categories such as cosmet ics, where
perceived familiarity and trust are critical to purchase intent. Supporting this, Huang and Sarigöllü (2012)
found that brand awareness significantly influences consumer preference and brand choice across diverse
markets.
Online customer reviews were found to enhance brand awareness, consistent with previous research
that emphasizes the role of electronic word of mouth in brand perception. Studies by Bruhn, Schoenmueller,
and Schäfer (2012) and Baek, Ahn, and Choi (2012) suggest that customer-generated content contributes to
brand recognition by increasing visibility and offering social proof. However, the direct effect of online
customer reviews on purchase decisions was not supported. This contrasts with findings by Park and Lee
(2009) and Senecal and Nantel (2004), who found that reviews have a direct influence on consumer decisions.
The discrepancy may be attributed to the context of cosmetics, where other factors such as brand familiarity,

250

aesthetic presentation, and influencer endorsements may play a more dominant role in driving action than
textual reviews alone.
On the other hand, social media marketing has shown to have a significant influence on both brand
awareness and purchase decision. These findings support prior research by Godey et al. (2016) and Bilgin
(2018), who demonstrated that strategic use of social media platforms enhances consumer engagement and
positively affects brand outcomes. In particular, Instagram Reels appears to be an effective tool for engaging
consumers with visually rich and emotionally res onant content. This is particularly relevant in the beauty
and fashion industries, where visual storytelling is central to consumer engagement. Jing (2024) highlighted
that platforms offering immersive content help brands build a unique identity that enhances both
recognition and desire.
Furthermore, the mediating role of brand awareness was confirmed in the relationships between
online customer reviews and purchase decision, as well as between social media marketing and purchase
decision. This is consistent with the Hierarchy of Effects M odel proposed by Lavidge and Steiner (1961) ,
which asserts that awareness is a prerequisite for subsequent stages in the consumer journey, including
preference and action. Similar mediation effects have been identified in studies such as Akbar et al. (2023)
and Bilgin (2018) , which showed that brand awareness is a critical intermediate variable that translates
exposure to digital content into actual purchase intention.
Overall, this study contributes to the literature by clarifying how both user -generated and firm -
generated digital content influence consumer behavior. The findings suggest that while customer reviews
are valuable for building brand familiarity, social med ia marketing is more consistently effective in shaping
both awareness and behavior. For marketers, the implications are clear. Brands should integrate social media
strategies that not only deliver engaging content but also amplify positive customer feedbac k in ways that
enhance brand cognition. Such integrated approaches are likely to produce stronger consumer responses
and lead to more effective digital campaigns.

Implications
The findings of this study offer meaningful implications for both theory and practice. From a
theoretical perspective, the study contributes to the growing body of knowledge on digital consumer
behavior by confirming the role of brand awareness as a critic al mediating construct in the relationship
between social media marketing, online customer reviews, and purchase decision. This supports and extends
the Hierarchy of Effects Model, suggesting that consumer exposure to marketing stimuli, whether brand -
generated or user -generated, must first translate into cognitive awareness before affecting behavioral
outcomes. The results also nuance the understanding of electronic word of mouth by demonstrating that
online customer reviews do not always lead directly to a ction, particularly in categories such as cosmetics
where visual and emotional brand associations are more influential. This highlights the need for future
theoretical models to incorporate the contextual effectiveness of different digital stimuli across i ndustries
and product types.
From a practical standpoint, the study offers actionable insights for marketers and brand managers
in the beauty and lifestyle sectors. The significant impact of social media marketing on both brand awareness
and purchase decision underlines the importance of investing in creative and consistent content strategies,
especially on platforms like Instagram that are visually driven and widely used by younger audiences.
Features such as Instagram Reels, which offer dynamic short-form content, should be leveraged to showcase
product use, customer testimonials, and influencer collaborations. Additionally, while online customer
reviews may not independently drive purchases, they should not be neglected, as they contribute
meaningfully to brand exposure and credibili ty. Brands should actively encourage satisfied customers to
share their experiences and consider integrating reviews into marketing content to reinforce brand messages.
By strategically combining user -generated content with professional marketing efforts, companies can
strengthen brand presence and guide consumers more effectively through the decision-making process.

CONCLUSION
This study provides empirical evidence on how social media marketing and online customer reviews
influence purchase decisions in the cosmetics sector, specifically through the mediating role of brand
awareness. The results highlight that brand awareness is a critical factor that significantly affects consumer
purchase behavior. Social media marketing demonstrated a strong positive effect on both brand awareness

251

and purchase decisions, confirming the strategic importance of visually engaging platforms such as
Instagram Reels in shaping consumer perceptions and driving sales. While online customer reviews did not
directly influence purchase decisions, they signific antly contributed to brand awareness, which in turn
positively impacted purchasing behavior. These findings reinforce the notion that digital consumer behavior
is shaped more effectively through integrated approaches where brand -generated and user -generated
content work synergistically. The mediating role of brand awareness aligns with classical advertising theories
like the Hierarchy of Effects Model, emphasizing that awareness is a foundational step toward action. For
practitioners, this implies that inve stments in creative content and customer engagement on social media
are essential, especially when supported by authentic customer testimonials. Future research may explore
other mediators such as trust or influencer credibility to further understand the d ynamics of digital
purchasing decisions in competitive consumer markets.
