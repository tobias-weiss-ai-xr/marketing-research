Abstract

Unscripted dialogic campaign programs remain underexplored as integrated
political marketing strategies for candidate positioning and youth engagement.
This study analyzes the political marketing mix of “Desak Anies” during the
2024 Indonesian presidential  election, examining its role in candidate image
construction and young voter mobilization. A descriptive qualitative approach
employed an embedded single -case study design. Purposively selected sources
included key informants comprising Ubah Bareng moveme nt initiators and
campaign strategists, livestream event recordings, media monitoring reports, and
digital interaction metrics. Data were collected through systematic
documentation and analyzed using Miles, Huberman, and Saldaña’s interactive
model, with coding organized around Kotler and Kotler’s 4P political marketing
framework. The analysis characterizes the program as a platform for two -way
deliberative dialogue and unscripted public questioning. It identifies the
integration of face -to-face “retail pol itics” with “wholesale politics” through
social media livestreaming and micro -content amplification as central to the
campaign’s communication strategy. This integration positioned the candidate
as an authentic, responsive technocrat willing to undergo pub lic scrutiny. The
findings suggest that authenticity, interactive dialogue, and media convergence
offer potential mechanisms for building political trust and encouraging voluntary
youth engagement. The study contributes a contextual account of how dialogic
campaigning connects candidate positioning, public participation, and digital
amplification within an integrated political marketing mix. Further quantitative

INTRODUCTION
The political campaign system in the 2024 Indonesian Presidential Election
experienced a fundamental paradigm shift. Changes in voter demographics positioned the
youth cohort, comprising Millennials and Generation Z, as the largest constituent segment,
playing a crucial role in determining national political outcomes. However, the psychographic
characteristics and political behavior of this new generation demonstrate a clear disruption
compared to previous election cycles. Contemporary young voters can no longer be
effectively influenced by conventional, top -down campaign instruments such as billboards,
street banners, commercial posters, or rigid and patronizing poetic slogans. Conversely, this
digital-native generation possesses high information literacy, a critical stance toward elite
promises, and an urgent demand for inclusive, rational, and egalitarian spaces for public
dialogue.
A primary issue shadowing the Indonesian democratic landscape is the dominance
of a one -way political communication model, distorted by superficial visual imaging or
shallow populist gimmicks. This campaign approach creates a wide gap between young
voters' expectations for substantive debate and the political offerings tendered by candidates.
Consequently, many rational and first -time voters feel alienated, subsumed by commercial
political propaganda, or experience growing distrust toward democratic
institutions(O’Shaughnessy & Henneberg, 2002).
This issue of gimmick -based campaigns and youth alienation warrants a firm
academic and conceptual response. This study argues that the dominance of shallow
symbolic campaigns must be counterbalanced, or even transformed, through the adoption
of a political marketing strategy anchored in authentic value  propositions and two -way
deliberative dialogue. Drawing upon the political marketing frameworks of Philip Kotler and
Neil Kotler (Kotler dkk., 2021), campaign activities should not be treated merely as forced
hard-selling or the passive dissemination of doctrine. Instead, modern political marketing
must be rooted in deep social intelligence to understand constituent preferences and

Qualitatively, this study is situated within the broader body of academic literature on
political communication and marketing. Previous research has documented the vital role of
political marketing and social media in shaping voter behavior. For instance, Lees -
Marshment emphasizes the integration of marketing concepts within political
organizations(Lees-Marshment, 2001) , while Strömbäck and Scammell highlight the
commodification of image and authenticity in the era of modern political mediatization
(Scammell, 2014). In the Indonesian context, studies by Firmanzah and Sukma et al. examine
the application of the marketing mix in national elections, though they predominantly focus
on traditional mass media and commercial political advertising (Sukma dkk., 2023). On the
other hand, recent literature evaluating the 2024 Election (Malindir dkk., 2024a), has begun
mapping youth voter participation. Nevertheless, most previous studies view youth digital
participation as a passive response to social media content or merely compare the visual
popularity of instruments across candidates, without holistically examining how micro-scale
face-to-face interactions (retail politics) exponentially converge into amplified macro -scale
mass movements (wholesale politics). A fundamental research gap persists: the lack of a
comprehensive analysis elaborating on the mechanics of unscripted dialogic campaign
programs as an integrated political marketing mix capable of dismantling top -down
communication and mobilizing young voters organically(Maspul, 2024).
To address this academic gap, this study offers significant theoretical novelty and
contribution. The study's novelty lies in analyzing "Desak Anies" an unscripted, open
dialogue forum initiated by the youth volunteer movement Ubah Bareng  during the 2024
Presidential Election as its primary unit of analysis. Specifically, this research highlights the
unique synthesis between retail politics (which prioritizes physical encounter, transparency,
and audience freedom to interrupt or critique the candidate) and wholesale politics (which
relies on live streaming transmissions and micro -content differentiation across TikTok, X,

responsive, authentic figure open to public scrutiny, and evaluate the impact of this
interactive marketing mix in driving organic political mobilization among first -time and
young voters in Indonesia (Ormrod, 2005).

METHODS
1. Type of Research
This study adopted a qualitative research approach to examine the execution and
multi-dimensional impacts of the "Desak Anies" political campaign during the 2024
Indonesian Presidential Election. The qualitative paradigm was selected because political
marketing strategies and candidate image construction involve complex social interac tions,
symbolic meanings, and strategic communication processes that cannot be fully captured
through purely quantitative or numerical measurements(Huyler & McGill, 2019). Specifically,
this study focuses on understanding how Philip Kotler and Neil Kotler's political marketing
framework comprising market research, segmentation, product positioning, and the political
marketing mix  was operationalized in a real -world election context to reshape candidate
image and mobilize youth constituents (Kotler dkk., 2021).

2. Research Design
To systematically address the research objectives, an embedded single -case study
design was implemented (Yin, 2018). The primary unit of analysis was the "Desak Anies"
campaign movement initiated by the youth volunteer organization Ubah Bareng during the
2024 Presidential Election(Fajar Satriyawan Wahyudi & Muhammad Irham Firdaus, 2024) .
The case study framework is particularly appropriate for analyzing contemporary, real -life
political phenomena where the boundaries between phenomenon and context are not clearly
evident. The embedded sub -units of analysis included: (1) the structural fo rmulation of
candidate positioning and marketing mix strategies (Scammell, 2014) ; (2) the physical
execution across regional dialogue editions (retail politics)(Malindir dkk., 2024a); and (3) digital
media amplification and viewer engagement across social media platforms ( wholesale politics).
This design aligns directly with the research objective of mapping the multi -level synthesis
between micro-scale direct dialogues and macro-scale digital virality(Newman, 1999).
3. Participants and Sampling Technique
Informants, data sources, and empirical materials were selected using a non -
probability purposive sampling. This strategy ensured that selected data units possessed high
information richness and direct authority regarding the campaign's conceptualization a nd
operational execution(Ramaniya dkk., 2025).
a. Key Informants: Primary qualitative insights were derived from campaign strategists and
core movement initiators, specifically key figures from Ubah Bareng  (e.g., Usamah Abdul
Aziz/Sami Usamah as co -founder) who held direct responsibility for designing the
unscripted dialogue format(Fajar Satriyawan Wahyudi & Muhammad Irham Firdaus, 2024).
b. Event Editions and Digital Materials: The dataset encompassed live-stream recordings and
event documentation from 22 to 24 major regional "Desak Anies" editions spanning Java,
Sumatra, Kalimantan, Sulawesi, Nusa Tenggara, and Maluku (e.g., Jakarta, Surabay a,
Yogyakarta, Bandung, Medan, Makassar) (Amelia Rahima Sari, 2024). Furthermore, sampling
included thematic editions targeting specific occupational and interest groups (e.g., Labor ,
Healthcare, Education, Teacher r, Lecturers, Youth , Sports, Fishermen , Farmers)(Anies
Baswedan, 2024).
c. Constituent Demographic Focus: The target constituent segment represented in the
analyzed interactions comprised university students, first -time voters, young professionals,

4. Instruments and Data Collection
The primary instrument for data collection was the researcher, guided by structured
digital observation matrices and document analysis protocols. Data were gathered through a
systematic documentation procedure categorized into primary and secondary sources:
a. Primary Data: Exclusive interview transcripts with the movement initiators and key
strategists as documented in primary campaign records and verified in -depth media
interviews (such as the Tempo documentary archive titled "Desak Anies: Jejak Kesadaran
Politik 2024") (TV TEMPO, 2026).
b. Secondary Data: Full -length live-stream video archives from official YouTube channels
(e.g., Anies Baswedan official channel), digital metric logs from TikTok Live, short-form
micro-content clips across TikTok, X (Twitter), Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts under
the hashtag #DesakAnies, media monitoring reports (e.g., Drone Emprit news and social
media mention trends comparing "Desak Anies" vs. "Gemoy"), and releva nt academic
literature.
c. Data Trustworthiness: To ensure data rigor, trustworthiness, and credibility, source
triangulation was applied by cross-referencing statement accounts from movement initiators,
factual video archives of live event dialogues, and independent external media reports (Drone
Emprit and Tempo).(Satria & Rahmawati, 2025)  Additionally, theoretical triangulation was
maintained by mapping empirical findings against established frameworks of political
marketing and deliberative political communication(Dewansyah, 2015).
5. Data Analysis Technique
Qualitative data were analyzed systematically following the interactive model
proposed by Miles, Huberman, and Saldaña (2014), executed through thematic qualitative
coding (Dewansyah, 2015). The analysis proceeded through three main interconnected
stages:
a. Data Reduction: The extensive corpus of raw text, video transcripts, digital interaction
metrics, and interview quotes was filtered, condensed, and categorized. Coding categories
were operationalized based on Kotler's 4P political marketing mix framework: Product
(candidate track record, public policy thoughts, technocratic capabil ity); Price (constituent

Primary Qualitative Findings: Authenticity Construction and Primary Statements
The most fundamental differentiating factor that rendered " Desak Anies" a robust
political marketing strategy was the courage to eliminate scripted events (unscripted events) and
break down the distance between political elites and the citizenry (Strömbäck, 2007, hlm. 52).
Based on media coverage research and exclusive Tempo interviews, the initial concept for
establishing this open dialogue forum originated from the thoughts of Usamah Abdul Aziz
(Sami Usamah) as the co -founder/initiator of Ubah Bareng  alongside his network of
supporting youth activists.
Usamah Abdul Aziz and the Ubah Bareng  team (Fajar Satriyawan Wahyudi &

authenticity, wherein the presidential candidate was challenged to demonstrate intellectual
quality spontaneously before the public without filters. In an in -depth interview featured in

perspective regarding the primary rationale for designing this interactive campaign format:
"We initially designed this event without any limitations whatsoever. There is no pre -submitted list
of questions, and anyone in attendance is permitted to interrupt the discussion if the answers provided are
deemed normative. We wanted to test whether this potential leader is genuinely resilient and respects the
intelligence of young voters. Young people do not need dancing responses or visual gimmicks; we need ideas
and the courage to be directly pressed. " (Tempo Interview, Desak Anies: The Trace of Political
Consciousness 2024).

DISCUSSION
The empirical findings of this study demonstrate that the operationalization of Kotler
and Kotler's political marketing mix within the "Desak Anies" program fundamentally
transformed the 2024 Indonesian Presidential Election campaign paradigm from top -down
political persuasion into a two-way deliberative dialogue. Structured around two key thematic
sub-sections, this discussion critically interprets these findings in relation to the primary
research problem, theoretical frameworks, and existing literature, while highlighting the
theoretical and practical implications, limitations, and future directions.
Paradigm Transformation: From Symbolic Gimmicks to Substantive Authenticity
The finding that "Desak Anies" successfully constructed the candidate's image as an
accessible, data -driven, and scrutiny -ready technocrat indicates that unscripted public
dialogue plays a vital role in building political trust among young constituents. By providing
neutral physical venues and removing question pre -screening, the campaign directly
addressed the growing alienation of Gen Z and Millennial voters who reject one -way, top-
down propaganda(O’Shaughnessy & Henneberg, 2002) . This finding is consistent with the
primary objective of this study, which sought to explain how interactive marketing mix
strategies redefine political candidate image and overcome voter skepticism in a developing
democracy. As observed in the Drone Emp rit tracking data, media coverage transitioned
from initial dominance by visual character gimmicks ("Gemoy" ) toward policy -focused
deliberative forums ("Desak Anies") once regional editions commenced(Maspul, 2024).
This shift aligns with previous research on political marketing orientation and
mediated authenticity. Specifically, Lees -Marshment asserts that market -oriented political
organizations must construct authentic value propositions rooted in constituent needs rather
than forced salesmanship. Furthermore, Enli posits that digital platforms enable politicians
to exhibit spontaneity and vulnerability, which enhances perceived authenticity compared to
tightly scripted mass media appearances (Enli, 2015). However, our findings contrast with
traditional political marketing studies in developing nations (Firmanzah, 2008) , which
emphasized the supremacy of commercial mass media and visual billboard advertising in

Retail-Wholesale Convergence, Implications, and Research Limitations
The empirical evidence showing that 20 physical event editions accommodating
66,789 direct attendees generated over 120 million digital impressions via user -generated
micro-content underscores the trategic power of retail -wholesale convergence .(Newman,
1999) Small-scale, highly authentic face -to-face interactions (retail politics) produced high -
emotion raw material that spontaneously fueled organic digital word-of-mouth dissemination
across TikTok, YouTube, and X (wholesale politics). This finding reinforces Strömbäck and
Kiousis’s model of strategic political communication (Strömbäck & Kiousis, 2014) ,
illustrating that real-time audience engagement yields significantly higher organic reach and
engagement rates than paid one-way advertising(Kruikemeier dkk., 2013).
Theoretically, these findings extend Kotler and Kotler's political marketing mix
(Kotler dkk., 2021) and deliberative communication models to youth cohorts in emerging
democracies, demonstrating that substantive branding can successfully supplant visual
gimmicks. Methodologically, this study offers an analytical framework for evaluating hybrid
campaign models that combine physical micro-events with macro digital virality. Practically,
the results provide a cost -effective blueprint for political candidates, campaign strategists,
and democratic institutions seeking to foster political efficacy and organic voter mobilization
without relying on massive b illboard logistics or commercial ad buys (Coleman & Freelon,
2015).
This study is limited by its qualitative single -case study design and non -probability
purposive sampling, which restricts the statistical generalizability of the findings across all
voter cohorts. Furthermore, this research did not econometrically measure the direct
conversion rate between digital earned media metrics (e.g., live-stream views and hashtag
impressions) and actual vote shares at polling stations (TPS). To address these limitations,
future research should incorporate quantitative -correlational approaches or electoral

CONCLUSION
This study demonstrates that the political marketing strategy implemented through
the "Desak Anies" program during the 2024 Indonesian Presidential Election successfully
transformed the political campaign paradigm from top-down persuasion into an interactive,
deliberative dialogue. By operationalizing Philip Kotler and Neil Kotler's political marketing
mix framework, the campaign team addressed young voters' saturation with superficial visual
gimmicks and one -way political advertising. The deliberate positioning of the candidate as
an accessible, authentic, and technocratic leader who is willing to be scrutinized unscripted
served as the core value proposition. Crucially, the campaign achieved a strategic synthesis
between f ace-to-face "retail politics" in inclusive public venues and digital "wholesale
politics" through real -time live streaming and user -generated micro-content amplification.
This dual mechanism effectively suppressed voter skepticism, mitigated public distrust, and
catalyzed voluntary, organic mobilization among Gen Z and Millennial constituents across
Indonesia.
Building upon these empirical findings, this study offers distinct scholarly
contributions across theoretical, methodological, and practical dimensions within the fields
of political marketing and deliberative communication. Theoretically, it extends Kotle r's
political marketing mix and deliberative democracy frameworks to digital -native youth
cohorts in emerging democracies, proving that transitioning from symbolic branding to
substantive branding can redefine the norms of civic engagement. Methodologically, it
provides a qualitative analytical framework for evaluating modern hybrid political campaigns
by illustrating how micro -level retail interactions generate organic raw material for macro -
level wholesale digital distribution. Practically, it offers a pragmatic, cost -effective, and anti-
gimmick campaign blueprint for political candidates, campaign strategists, and electoral
institutions, proving that unscripted, policy -focused dialogue forums offer a high -
engagement alternative to traditional, high-budget visual advertising.
To advance scholarly inquiry beyond the qualitative boundaries of this study, several
directions for future research are recommended. Future studies should integrate quantitative-
correlational approaches or electoral econometric modeling to measure the exa ct direct
