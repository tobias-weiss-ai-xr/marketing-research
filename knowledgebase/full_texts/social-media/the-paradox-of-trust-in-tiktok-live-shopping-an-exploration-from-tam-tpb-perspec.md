Behavior (TPB) and the Technology Acceptance
Model (TAM) to investigate factors influencing
young Indonesian customers' purchasing interest

functional utility. Gender does not moderate the
trust-attitude relationship, indicating a univers al

1 INTRODUCTION

In Indonesia's ever -changing digital economy landscape, social commerce,
particularly TikTok Shop, has redefined consumer behaviour among the younger
generation. While traditional e-commerce relies heavily on static reviews, the integration
of live streami ng on TikTok creates a unique dynamic where real -time interaction and
perceived trust are the main drivers of transactions. In this context, trust is no longer just
a foundation of business as usual, but a critical mechanism for reducing vulnerability and
uncertainty in a digital environment where the quality of information and direct
interaction with sellers play a crucial role (Ennew & Sekhon, 2007) ,(D. J. Kim et al.,
2008). However, there is a significant theoretical gap: most previous studies have used
the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) in stable market environments, making them
less capable of e xplaining the high -risk and volatile nature of social commerce in
developing markets such as Indonesia.
This study aims to fill this gap by integrating the Theory of Planned Behaviour
(TPB) and TAM to explore how trust functions not merely as a facilitator but as a critical
mediator for Indonesian youth. Furthermore, despite the demographic dominance of
Indonesia's younger generation, empirical evidence on how gender moderates the
relationship between trust and purchase intention in the context of live -streaming still
shows inconsistent results. By testing these variables, this study contributes to a deeper
understanding of digital consumer psychology in one of the world's most active social
media markets.

The urgency of this research is further emphasised by the massive growth of
internet users in Indonesia, which reached more than 212 million in 2023. This growth is
concentrated in social media activities, where platforms such as TikTok have evolved
from mere entertainment tools to major shopping destinations for the younger generation.
Despite this trend, many young consumers still express concerns about data privacy and
the authenticity of products sold during live sessions. Therefore, this study aims to
provide a comprehensive framework for understanding how trust and technology interact
in shaping the shopping motivations of Indonesian youth, as well as to provide theoretical
insights and practical recommendations for social commerce practitioners in the region.

than 200 million users, shows significant digital market potential. However, data from

it in seventh place, even one level below Japan, which has 104.4 million internet users
(Datareportal,2025). This comparison demonstrates that there are still prospects in the

2.2 Theory of Planned Behaviour (TPB)

The Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) is one of numerous theoretical
frameworks that can be used to comprehend social commerce and purchase intentions.
The importance of consumer sentiments as a mediating variable is shown by this
approach. According to (Ajzen, 1991a), TPB is a logical selection model that uses the
concepts of attitude, subjective norm, and perceiv ed behavioral control to explain
behavioral intention as the main psychological antecedent of behavior. According to this
idea, customers' sentiments regarding social commerce have a significant impact on their
purchasing intentions. In this regard, TPB is one of the key theories that explain consumer
behavior motivation, including purchase intention (Mou & Benyoucef, 2021) ,(Smith et
al., 2013) . Additionally, according to Ajzen,, TPB can be expanded by including
additional predictive indicators in addition to intention or behavior.

2.5 Perceived usefulness & attitude towards using s -commerce on social media

PU stands for the positive results that people anticipate from a technology's

PU is also favorably correlated with Attitude Toward Technology. Thus, the following
theory is put forth:

positively correlated with PU.

2.7 Mediating effect of attitude towards using s-commerce on social media platforms

A good attitude plays a significant mediating effect in consumer purchasing
intention and boosts an individual's motivation to act (Nguyen, 2023). Due to thoughts of
convenience and advantages, a good attitude toward social media raises transaction intent.
Attitude is influenced by elements includin g perceived usefulness (PU) and perceived
ease of use (PEU), which raises purchase intent (Yuan et al., 2021). Influencer credibility
and trust (PT) are examples of external influences that reinforce attitude and impact
purchasing decisions (Ing & Ming, 2018). As a result, attitude serves as a crucial mediator
in the impact of other aspects on purchasing decisions in addition to enhancing consumer
involvement.
Given these results, we postulate that the impacts of PEU, PU, and PT on platform
use are mediated by Attitude Toward Using S -Commerce (ASC) on social media
platforms (H5 for PEU, H6 for PU, and H7 for PT).

3 METHODOLOGY

3.1 Research design

To understand the characteristics of the research variables, this study adopted a
descriptive quantitative design that included a literature review, survey, and data analysis
(Edgar & Manz, 2017) . The research focuses on participants' participation in social
commerce, particularly their views and opinions on consumer purchasing behaviour on
social media (Aldhahery et al., 2018), as well as factors from the TAM model and trust.
The analytical model was tested using Partial Leas t Squares Structural Equation
Modelling (PLS-SEM), which was chosen for its ability to manage complex relationships
between latent variables and data with abnormal distributions, even with limited sample
sizes.

3.2 Sample

The target population for this study is individuals aged 18 -36 years in Indonesia
who have experience or intention to purchase directly through social commerce on major
social media platforms at least once in the last month. This selection is based on high
digital literacy and consistent internet access, making it ideal for studying digital
commerce behaviour. The minimum sample size is 100 respondents, based on the ‘10
times rule’ guideline in PLS-SEM, which suggests a sample size at least ten times larger
than the number of structural paths to each construct (Hair Jr et al., 2021).

3.3 Data gathering

Purposive sampling and quota sampling were used in tandem to gather res earch
data. Adequate demographic representation on factors like age, gender, and frequency of
s-commerce use was guaranteed via quota sampling. Purposive sampling, on the other
hand, sought out people who had appropriate expertise and experience with socia l
commerce. The Surveymars website was used to recruit respondents, who were then
reposted on well -known social media platforms. The primary tool was a structured
questionnaire with a seven -point Likert scale (from "Strongly Disagree" to "Strongly
Agree"), which was used because it might better capture respondents' in -depth
assessments and provide greater correlations (Taherdoost, 2019).
The questionnaire was developed through several important stages. The indicators
were adapted from previously validated instruments to ensure content validity. Semantic
consistency was maintained through back-translation between the English and Indonesian
versions, validated by bilingual experts. A trial involving 30 respondents assessed clarity
and interpretability, which then resulted in minor adjustments to the questionnaire to
improve the quality of the instrument.
This final research instrument consists of 28 items m easuring 5 latent variables:
Perceived Ease of Use (PEU), Perceived Usefulness (PU) (Venkatesh & Davis, 1996) ,
Perceived Trust (PT) (Corritore et al., 2005), Attitude Towards Using Social Commerce
(ASC) (Mumuni et al., 2019), and Purchase Intention (PI) (Venkatesh & Davis, 1996) .
Prior to distribution, the instrument was validated with a minimum of 30 respondents.

3.4 Data processing and analysis

The Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM) approach
was used in this work using SmartPLS 4.0 software, which was chosen due to its capacity
to manage complex models and non -normally distributed data. This study, which
included 346 respondents, adhered to the SEM protocols suggested by (Hair et al., 2019).
These pro cesses included assessing the measurement model for variable validity and
reliability as well as evaluating the structural model to determine the relevance of factors
impacting purchase intention.
Smart PLS is effective for small sample sizes and model ana lysis with formative
constructs (Hair et al., 2019) . PLS 4.0 excels over regressi on in evaluating mediation
effects (Henseler et al., 2015). PLS-SEM is ideal for complex models with unmet sample
conditions. The initial assessme nt focuses on the representation of the measurement
model, which is important for structural model estimation. After the measurement model
assessment, the study proceeds with SEM and tests model invariance through MICOM
(Cheah et al., 2020), concluding with a permutation-based multigroup analysis to detect
gender differences.

4.1 Demographics of respondents

The research respondents consisted of 155 men (44.80%) and 191 women
(55.20%). Most respondents were aged between 26 and 30 years (30.64%), followed by

dominated by bachelor's degree graduates (39.02%) and diploma graduates (26.88%).
They are likely to be individuals who are active users of social commerce, are digitally
connected, and are interested in the latest trends.

5 DISCUSSION

5.1 The role of technology acceptance (H1 & H2)

The findings demonstrate a strong positive correlation between Attitude (ASC)
and Perceived Ease of Use (PEU) (H1). This result is consistent with (Davis & Davis,
1989) fundamental TAM theory as well as current research by (Abed, 2018) , which
claims that users' psychological comfort is greatly increased by social commerce
platforms' ease of accessible. This result also demonstrates that a number of  earlier
empirical studies have demonstrated the significance of technology usability in forming
and evolving consumer attitudes (Wasudawan et al., 2025), (Allam et al., 2024), (Yuan
et al., 2021), (Vahdat et al., 2020) and in fostering the development of trust in the use of
social media, which is currently taking place. TikTok's user -friendly interface lowers
technological obstacles for young Indonesians, which in turn encourages them to keep
using the platform.
However, the findings on Perceived Usefulness (PU) towards Attitude (H2) show
the weakest influence. This is interesting because it contrasts with research (Gavino et
al., 2019),(Chatterjee & Kumar Kar, 2020), (H. M. Kim & Ryu, 2021) that places PU as
the main driver in a traditional bus iness context. Here we see a shift; for the younger
generation in Indonesia, functional usefulness is beginning to be replaced by the aspect
of shoppertainment. As stated by (M. Wang et al., 2021), emotional interactions in social
commerce often determine attitudes more than mere transactional efficiency.

Trust as the Primary Driver and Gender Nuances (H3 & H3a)
The strongest positive connection between Attitude and Perceived Trust (PT) was
discovered (H3). These findings bolster the claim (Hajli, 2014) that the main "currency"
in the social media ecosystem is trust, which is developed by community engagement and
visual openness. The main "currency" in an unpredictable digital world is trust. Young
consumers feel more confident about the legitimacy of products thanks to TikTok's ability
to show live product visualizations. Additionally, this survey demonstrates that
consumers use social commerce platforms with confidence and trust to fulfill their
purchase needs. They see social media as a secure setting that can boost contentment and
add value (like special deals or instant discounts). These findings are in line with earlier
research that emphasizes the importance of trust in digital transactions ( (Elshaer et al.,
2023), (Lăzăroiu et al., 2020), (Junaedi et al., 2022)).
Contrary to expectations ( (Feng & Ivanov, 2023) , (Gong et al., 2021), (Nord et
al., 2016) ) positing socio -emotional differences), multigroup analysis reveals no
significant moderation (p_diff=0.92; Table VII), rejecting H3a. This suggests trust -
attitude linkage is invariant across genders in Indonesia's TikTok context, possibly due to
platform's uniform visual appeal overriding processing variances.
From Attitude to Purchase Intention (H4)
The analysis's findings confirm that Attitude (ASC) significantly affects Purchase
Intention (H4). This result supports the Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB) and is in line
with research by (Yuan et al., 2021), which asserts that a positive attitude is a necessary
precondition for the emergence of behavioral intention (purchase), as well as research by
and (D. Zhang et al., 2023). Consumers see the site as an engaging information source,
useful for making decisions, an d successful at promoting goods through
recommendations from friends or influencers. This optimistic outlook on TikTok Shop
results from a mix of security (trust) and pleasure (hedonism).

This study demonstrates that the effects of PEU (H5), PU (H6), and PT (H7) on
purchase intention are mediated by attitude. This is an important discovery that clarifies
consumers' internal mechanisms. According to the framework (Venkatesh, 2022) ,
technological considerations (PEU & PU) do not immediately result in action without
first undergoing internal a ssessment (Attitude). This result is consistent with ( (Yuan et

5.2 Theoretical implications

In theory, this study makes the following contributions to the body of knowledge
on marketing management and customer behavior: 1) Extension of the TAM-TPB Model:
This study demonstrates that when it comes to entertainment platforms, the traditional
TAM model must be modified. Future social commerce research may need to substitute
hedonistic variables with traditional utilitarian ones, as indicated by the result that PU has
a limited influence. 2) Contextualization of Trust: This research expands our knowledge
of soci al trust, which is now dynamic and based on real -time interaction and visual
transparency. 3) Gender Invariance Insight: Unsupported H3a suggests that
shoppertainment and other environmental factors homogenize gender effects in emerging
economies, challenging schema theory extensions.

5.3 Managerial implications

For business practitioners, sellers, and social commerce platforms, this study
offers several strategic recommendations: 1) Prioritise Authenticity over Aesthetics:
Given that trust is a key factor, sellers are advised to focus on honest and unfiltered (raw)
live content rather than overly polished advertisements, in order to minimise negative
consumer perceptions. 2) Universal Strategies Across Demographics. Gender invariance
(H3a unsupported) implies no need for segmented designs; uniform features like visual
transparency and community testimonials suffice for both males and females, simplifying
scaling while addressing privacy concerns prevalent among youth. 3) Shoppertainment

5.4 Limitations and future studies

This study has limitations even if it adds imp ortant knowledge. The results may
not apply to later age groups because the sample was drawn from young individuals in
the Indonesian community. Furthermore, this study did not address how local culture
influences trust. More focused factors (such perceived risk or social presence) should be
considered in future studies. Additionally, this study does not take into consideration
external factors that could affect trust, such as shifts in political legislation, data security
incidents, and influencer marketing trends. Additionally, this study did not look at other
moderating factors including digital culture, social media usage weight, income, and
online buying experience. When the geographic coverage may be expanded to look for
further regional collectivism culture patterns, more research is advised to examine these
variables and see whether trust impacts may differ in various segments.

6 CONCLUSION

The empirical patterns from this study confirm that within Indonesia’s social
commerce ecosystem particularly on entertainment driven platforms like TikTok Shop
attitude towards using social commerce (ASC) serves as the dominant psychological
bridge. While perceived ease of use (PEU) and perceived trust (PT) both exert considerable
influence on attitude, the functional aspect of technology (perceived usefulness, PU) plays
a notably minor role. This qualitative shift marks a genuine departure from traditional
technology acceptance models: for young Indonesians, the sheer utility of an app no longer
drives engagement . Instead, what truly shapes attitude is the seamless, low  effort
experience (ease of use) combined with a deep sense of transactional security (trust). The
data show that trust, in particular, channels strongly through attitude to shape purchase
intention, underscoring that shoppertainment succeeds not because of feature-richness, but
because users feel both entertained and protected.

usefulness and more about whether the platform feels instinctively right, fluid, and
trustworthy.
Regarding gender, the study finds no meaningful difference in how men and
women process the relationship between trust and attitude. Both groups show a similarly
strong pathway, indicating that the psychological mechanism linking digital trust to a
favourable shopping attitude is invariant across genders. Nevertheless, the overall
conclusion stands: attitude functions as the universal catalyst that turns perceptions of ease
and security into tangible purchase actions. In the evolving social commerce landscape, it
is not the sophistication of technology but the quality of engagement and the reassurance
of trust that ultimately drive young Indonesian consumers forward.
