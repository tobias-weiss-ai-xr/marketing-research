Abstract: This study aims to analyze the influence of hedonic shopping motivation, shopping
lifestyle, and positive emotion on impulse buying of beauty products on TikTok Shop. The
study employs a quantitative approach using a descriptive method. Primary data were obtained
through an online questionnaire distributed to 200 consumers of beauty products on TikTok
Shop who had made a purchase within the last three months, selected using purposive sampling
with the Lemeshow formula. Data analysis was conducted using the Partial Least Squares–
Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) method with the aid of SmartPLS 4.0 software,
covering evaluation of the outer model (convergent validity, discriminant validity, and
reliability) and the inner model (R-square and path coefficients). The results indicate that
hedonic shopping motivation, shopping lifestyle, and positive emotion each have a positive
and significant influence on impulse buying of beauty products on TikTok Shop, with the three
variables jointly explaining 81.5% of the variance in impulse buying. These findings suggest
that psychological and lifestyle-related factors play a central role in driving unplanned
purchases on social-commerce platforms, offering practical implications for beauty brands
designing marketing strategies on TikTok Shop.

Keyword: Hedonic Shopping Motivation, Shopping Lifestyle, Positive Emotion, Impulse
Buying.

INTRODUCTION
Digital transformation has substantially reshaped consumer behavior, shifting shopping
activity from conventional channels toward fully digital experiences. Consumers no longer rely
on digital platforms solely to search for information; they increasingly use them to complete
purchases directly, making shopping faster, more practical, and more deeply embedded in
everyday life.
Among the platforms driving this shift, TikTok stands out for its exceptionally high
engagement. According to We Are Social (2025), TikTok recorded the highest average usage

time in Indonesia among social media platforms, reaching 44 hours and 54 minutes per month,
surpassing YouTube, WhatsApp, and Instagram. Indonesia also ranks first globally in the
number of TikTok users, with more than 190 million users in 2025, underscoring the platform's
substantial reach and its potential to shape consumer behavior.
TikTok's evolution from an entertainment platform into a social-commerce ecosystem
through TikTok Shop allows consumers to browse and purchase within a single application.
Supported by a large user base and a highly personalized recommendation algorithm, TikTok
Shop can surface content aligned with individual preferences, making it easier for consumers
to discover products while simultaneously increasing engagement (Productsup, 2023).
This trend aligns with the growing preference of Indonesian consumers for online
shopping. Based on the Indonesia Consumer Report 2023 by Standard Insights, published by
GoodStats, fashion is the most purchased category online (70.13%), followed by beauty and
personal-care products (49.7%), and food and beverages (40.8%). Within TikTok Shop
specifically, beauty and personal-care products contribute the largest share of transactions,
reaching 69% based on compas.co.id (2025) data, a proportion notably higher than on other e-
commerce platforms. This dominance suggests that beauty products possess a strong appeal
capable of prompting rapid, and often unplanned, purchase decisions.
The high demand for beauty products reflects not only functional needs but also lifestyle,
trends, and individual preferences that collectively contribute to impulse buying (Fitria &
Andarini, 2025). However, prior studies examining the psychological drive rs of impulse
buying report inconsistent findings. Regarding hedonic shopping motivation, Fitria and
Andarini (2025) and Utama et al. (2025) found a significant positive effect on impulse buying,
whereas Dewi and Adi (2023) found no significant effect. Regarding shopping lifestyle, Fitria
and Andarini (2025) reported a positive and substantial partial effect, whi le Solikha and
Putranto (2025) found a significant negative effect. Regarding positive emotion, Cahyani and
Purwanto (2022) and Azid et al. (2024) found a significant positive effect, while Trigina and
Utami (2024) found no significant effect.
These inconsistencies indicate a research gap, particularly because studies examining
hedonic shopping motivation, shopping lifestyle, and positive emotion simultaneously within
a single model remain limited, especially in the context of beauty products on TikTok Shop. In
practice, impulse buying rarely results from a single factor; rather, it emerges from the
interaction of internal drivers such as hedonic motivation, shopping -related lifestyle, and
consumers' emotional states during the purchasing process. This study therefore analyzes, in a
more comprehensive manner, the influence of hedonic shopping motivation, shopping lifestyle,
and positive emotion on impulse buying of beauty products on TikTok Shop.
Impulse Buying. Solomon (2020) defines impulse buying as a purchase that occurs
spontaneously, driven by a sudden and powerful urge to buy immediately, without prior
information search or brand consideration. Pandowo et al. (2024) similarly describe it as an
unplanned purchase driven by emotional responses to internal or external stimuli, characterized
by spontaneity, difficulty of control, and strong emotional involvement.
Hedonic Shopping Motivation. Sukma Irdiana et al. (2024) define hedonic shopping
motivation as the drive to shop in order to obtain pleasure, entertainment, and enjoyable
experiences, rather than to fulfill a specific need. Consumers with strong hedonic motivation
tend to prioritize the shopping experience itself, which increases their tendency toward
spontaneous purchasing.
Shopping Lifestyle. Fatmawatie (2022) defines shopping lifestyle as a consumption
concept reflecting how individuals choose to spend their time and money, mirroring their
values and tastes. When shopping becomes a recreational activity rather than merely a means
of fulfilling needs, individuals become more susceptible to impulse buying (Pandowo et al.,
2024).

Positive Emotion. Solomon (2020) explains that emotion is a feeling or mood
experienced by consumers that shapes their responses to products and their purchase decisions.
Positive emotions such as happiness, joy, and enthusiasm often make consumers more
susceptible to impulse buying, since purchasing is perceived as a form of self-reward (Sukma
Irdiana et al., 2024).
Based on this theoretical foundation and prior empirical findings, this study proposes the
following hypotheses: (H1) Hedonic Shopping Motivation has a positive and significant
influence on Impulse Buying; (H2) Shopping Lifestyle has a positive and significant influence
on Impulse Buying; and (H3) Positive Emotion has a positive and significant influence on
Impulse Buying. The objective of this study is to examine whether hedonic shopping
motivation, shopping lifestyle, and positive emotion influence impuls e buying of beauty
products on TikTok Shop, both individually and as a set of interrelated psychological drivers.

METHOD
This study adopts a quantitative, descriptive research design (Sugiyono, 2019), grounded
in positivist philosophy and intended to test the hypothesized relationships among variables
using numerical data. The data source is primary data, obtained through a closed-ended
questionnaire distributed to respondents who reported their perceptions and experiences
directly (Sugiyono, 2018). Four variables are examined: Hedonic Shopping Motivation (X1),
Shopping Lifestyle (X2), and Positive Emotion (X3) as exogenous variables, and Impulse
Buying (Y) as the endogenous variable, each measured with a five-point Likert scale.
The population consists of TikTok Shop users in Jakarta. A non-probability, purposive
sampling technique was applied, with respondents selected based on the following criteria: (1)
aged 17 years and above; (2) having an interest in beauty products; and (3) having purchased
a beauty product through TikTok Shop within the last three months. Because the population
size was unknown, the minimum sample size was determined using the Lemeshow formula,
with Zα = 1.96 (95% confidence level), P = Q = 0.5, and a precision level (L) of 10%, yielding
a minimum requirement of approximately 96 respondents. This study used 200 respondents,
exceeding the minimum requirement. Data were collected through an online questionnaire
distributed via Google Forms.
Hypothesis testing was conducted using Partial Least Squares –Structural Equation
Modeling (PLS-SEM) with SmartPLS 4.0. The analysis comprised two stages: (1) evaluation
of the outer (measurement) model, including convergent validity (outer loadings > 0.7 and
AVE > 0.5), discriminant validity (cross -loadings), and internal-consistency reliability
(Cronbach's alpha and composite reliability > 0.6), following Hair et al. (2017) and Ghozali
and Kusumadewi (2023); and (2) evaluation of the inner (structural) model, including the
coefficient of determination (R-square) and path-coefficient significance testing. Hypothesis
testing employed bootstrapping with 5,000 resamples at a 95% confidence level; a hypothesis
was supported when the t-statistic exceeded the critical t-table value of 1.972 and the p-value
was below 0.05.

RESULT AND DISCUSSION

respondents (72%) were between 17 and 23 years old, followed by 19% aged 24–29, 5% aged
30–45, and 4% above 46 years old. By domicile, the majority resided in South Jakarta (67%),
followed by East Jakarta (17%), West Jakarta (9%), Central Jakarta (4%), and North Jakarta
(3%). All 200 respondents (100%) confirmed having an interest in beauty products and having
purchased a beauty product on TikTok Shop within the preceding three months, satisfying the
study's sampling criteria, as summarized in Table 1.

responses. For Hedonic Shopping Motivation, 57.2% of responses were "Agree" and 35.2%

recognition. For Shopping Lifestyle, 49.0% were "Agree" and 41.3% "Strongly Agree"

product information. For Positive Emotion, respondents reported feeling comfortable, happy,
inspired, and enthusiastic while shopping. For Impulse Buying, res pondents indicated a

All three hypotheses are supported. Hedonic Shopping Motivation shows the strongest
effect on Impulse Buying (β = 0.434; t = 6.164; p < 0.001), followed by Shopping Lifestyle (β
= 0.261; t = 3.610; p < 0.001) and Positive Emotion (β = 0.242; t = 2.803; p = 0.005).
Effect of Hedonic Shopping Motivation on Impulse Buying. The finding that
hedonic shopping motivation positively and significantly influences impulse buying indicates
that consumers who shop for pleasure, entertainment, and emotional satisfaction rather than
functional necessity are more prone to unplanned purch ases. Descriptively, respondents
reported enjoying the discovery of new beauty products, appreciating recognition from others
after a purchase, and using TikTok Shop as an outlet for boredom or stress relief. This result is
consistent with Sukma Irdiana et al. (2024) and empirically supports Barokah et al. (2021) and
Fitria and Andarini (2025), who likewise found a significant positive effect of hedonic
shopping motivation on impulse buying.
Effect of Shopping Lifestyle on Impulse Buying. Shopping lifestyle was also found
to positively and significantly influence impulse buying, suggesting that consumers who devote
considerable time and attention to beauty-related content and shopping activities are more
likely to buy spontaneously when they encounter an appealing product. This finding aligns
with Rizal et al. (2025) and Dewi and Adi (2023), though it contrasts with Solikha and Putranto
(2025), who found a negative relationship in a different sample, underscoring that the direction
of this effect may be context-dependent.
Effect of Positive Emotion on Impulse Buying. Positive emotion was found to
positively and significantly influence impulse buying, indicating that consumers who feel
comfortable, happy, inspired, and enthusiastic while browsing TikTok Shop tend to make
faster, less deliberated purchase decisions. This is consistent with Sukma Irdiana et al. (2024)
and supports Azid et al. (2024) and Solikha and Putranto (2025), although it differs from
Trigina and Utami (2024), who found no significant effect in the context of Shopee. This
divergence suggests that the emotional pathway to impulse buying may be more pronounced
on platforms such as TikTok Shop, where entertainment content and shopping are tightly
integrated.
Taken together, these findings confirm that impulse buying of beauty products on
TikTok Shop is shaped jointly by psychological motivation, lifestyle orientation, and emotional
state, reinforcing the view that social-commerce environments amplify unplanned purchasing
by blending entertainment with transactional opportunity.

CONCLUSION
This study concludes that Hedonic Shopping Motivation, Shopping Lifestyle, and
Positive Emotion each have a positive and significant influence on Impulse Buying of beauty
products on TikTok Shop, jointly explaining 81.5% of its variance, with hedonic shopping
motivation exerting the strongest influence, followed by shopping lifestyle and positive
emotion. For practitioners, beauty brands operating on TikTok Shop may strengthen hedonic
motivation by reinforcing social validation, for instance through user-generated review content
and influencer collaborations; align marketing strategies with consumers' shopping lifestyle by
offering trend-driven content such as product demonstrations and live streaming; and enhance
positive emotion through consistent, engaging promotions such as flash sales and time-limited
vouchers. For future research, incorporating additional variables such as Fear of Missing Out
(FOMO), sales promotion, price discounts, and electronic word of mouth (e-WOM), as well as
broadening the sample across different age groups and regions, is recommended to improve
the generalizability of the findings.
