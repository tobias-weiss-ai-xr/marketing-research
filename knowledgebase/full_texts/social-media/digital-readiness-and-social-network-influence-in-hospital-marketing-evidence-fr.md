2. Materials and Methods
The cross sectional study was conducted in Vadodara district
ofGujarat. TheselectionofVadodara,agrowingurbancenter
in Gujarat serving the healthcare needs of diverse population
thatincludesamixofurban,per-urbanandruralcommunities.
Thissocio-demographicdiversityprovidesarichcontexttothe
private and public healthcare organizations to leverage digital
platformsforpatientengagement. Additionally,beingreferred
to as Education Hub of Gujarat with significant number of IT
professionals and tech-savvy population, Vadodara is an ideal
setting for assessing how hospitals integrate technology and
digitalmarketing. Thereislimitedavailabilityofliteraturethat
focuses on the digital presence of hospitals with a particular
localcontext. Thisgaphighlightstheneedforlocalizedstudies
todelvedeeperintothelandscapeanalysisofhowhospitalsin
tier-2 cities adapt to technological advancements to meet the
evolvinghealthcareneedsoftheirpatients.
A non-probability purposive sampling approach was used
to include hospitals that met the predefined eligibility criteria
and had sufficient publicly accessible digital information for
assessment. Hospitals were eligible for inclusion if they: 1)
werelocatedinVadodaradistrict;2)werecategorizedaseither
public or private hospitals; 3) had an official website and/or
identifiable official digital or social media presence; 4) were
classified as medium-sized or large hospitals based on bed
strength as per book Hospital Administration by DC Joshi
and Mamta Joshi, ISBN 978-81-8448-676-6; and 5) agreed
to participate in the study. Hospitals were excluded if they
did not have an identifiable official digital presence, and if
sufficientinformationwasunavailableforassessmentusingthe
structuredchecklist.
A total of 15 hospitals met the eligibility criteria and
were included in the final assessment. The sample comprised
10 private hospitals and 5 public hospitals. The unequal
distribution reflects the number and availability of eligible
hospitals that were included under the study selection criteria,
givenconsent,andshouldnotbeinterpretedasaproportionate
representationofallhospitalsinVadodaradistrict.
The study involved assessing the content of the website
of the hospitals in Vadodara district who consented to be
a part of the study. The content on the hospital’s website
was evaluated through a structured checklist which involved
content validation by marketing experts. This study utilized
secondary data obtained from the hospital’s website, hence
ethical approval was not applicable. The checklist covered
the name of the hospital, the size of the hospital (large and
medium), category it belongs to (public or private), and the
year of establishment. Different statements pertaining to
use of digital media (Website and respective social media
handles used by Hospitals) for transferring information to the
patientswasevaluatedusingthe3pointlikertscalelikeAlways,
Sometimes and Rare. In this, always means the hospital
updated information on their social media accounts on daily
basis, sometimes implies that the hospital shows an erratic
frequencybut,updates2or3timesperweekandrarelyshows
thattheupdatesaresoinfrequentandoncefor15daysorlong
ago.
A total of fifteen hospitals consented to be a part of
the study. These hospitals encompassed public and private
hospitals and also, medium and large sized hospital. The
selected hospital’s website and social media account was
monitored at regular interval, that is once a day through a
structuredchecklist.
Through t-distribution the frequencies of different

3.1. Social media activity
Two hospitals updated their content on a daily basis
and were classified as active participants on social media.
These hospitals actively engage with users by responding to
comments, posting educational health content, and promoting
their services through targeted campaigns. Most of private
hospitals maintain their social media accounts by posting
content two to three times per week. Fourteen of the
15 hospitals have a homepage that is easy to navigate and
understand. Twelve hospitals out of fifteen hospitals provide
linkstotheirsocialmediaaccountsontheirwebsites.
3.2. Website features
Nearlyallthehospitalsincludecautions,terms,andconditions
on their webpages. This practice enhances patient trust and
promotes transparency by clearly outlining the hospital’s
policies, procedures, and user responsibilities. It also
encourages patient compliance with guidelines, thereby
supporting safer and more informed interactions with
healthcare services. All private hospitals provide information
on discounts and free medical checkups, whereas public
hospitalsdonotmentionsuchoffersontheirwebsites. Eleven
hospitals provide links or icons directing page visitors to their
YouTube channels. Eleven hospitals rarely offer videos or
blogsondifferentailmentscreatedbytheirstaff. Threepublic
hospitals provide options for funds and donations on their
websites. Nine hospitals have mobile applications, but none
of these hospitals provide information about the app on their
websites. Sixhospitals, mostof which arepublic, donot have
amobileapp.
3.3. Patient engagement and information sharing
Testimonials from patients are regularly updated on most
hospital webpages. However, online doctor availability
is rarely mentioned on hospital websites. Ten hospitals
rarely provide information on health issues or crises. Eight
hospitalsconsistentlyanswercommonhealth-relatedquestions.
However, a mix of public and private hospitals have not
focusedmuchonansweringquestionsfrompagevisitors. The
social media accounts of fourteen hospitals are used to create
health awareness, while one hospital’s social media activity in
thisregardiscategorizedasrare.
3.4. Comparative analysis of private and public hospitals
A Chi-square test was performed to analyze the association
between hospital type (public or private) and the observed
parameters. This test was chosen because it effectively
evaluatestherelationshipbetweencategoricalvariables. Inthis
case,itwasusedtodetermineifthetypeofhospital(publicor
private)wassignificantlyassociatedwithfactorssuchassocial
mediaactivity,homepagevibrancy,andthepresenceofpatient
engagementfeatures. Theresultswerefoundtobestatistically
insignificant, indicating no significant association between the
parameters and the hospital category. Despite the lack of
statistical significance, some differences between private and
public hospitals were noted. Private hospitals, regardless of
their year of establishment, are more active participants on
social media compared to public hospitals. Private hospitals
also have more vibrant and attractive homepages and offer
pop-up chatbot facilities, which are not as common in public
hospitals. Both private and public hospitals maintain an equal
intensity in linking their visitors to social media accounts,
mentioning cautions and terms and conditions, and providing
linkstoYouTubechannels.
Private hospitals are unique in offering information on
discounts and free medical checkups, a feature not found
on public hospital websites. Both sectors are lacking in the
provision of videos or blogs related to different ailments by
their staff. Public hospitals do not actively provide breaking
news on health issues or crises, while private hospitals are
moreproactiveinaddressingcommonhealth-relatedquestions.
Both public and private hospitals use their social media
accountstospreadhealthawareness.
4. Discussion
Thepresentstudyhighlightstheincreasinguseofdigitaltools
like websites and social media platforms, to improve patient
engagement, thus leading to improvement in service delivery.
Khan, S., et al., in his study emphasis that social media
campaigns are a great way to reach and engage with potential
influencers and the perfect platform to share the services of
private healthcare institutions with customers.9 The private
sector hospitals demonstrated a stronger online presence
across different parameters like social media participation,
website aesthetics and providing patient testimonials. These
findings also resonate with previous research by Ventola,
Korda and Itani, which also showcased the heavy investment
of private sector to build their brand identity and enhance
patient trust.1,6 Additionally, Stewart emphasized that private
healthcare organizations are more likely to adopt advanced
digital marketing techniques, such as maintaining vibrant
websites and leveraging social media platforms like Facebook
andInstagram,toimprovepatientoutreach. 10
Differenceinstrategicfocusbyprivateandpublichospitals
can be seen, in the present study as (p = 0.004) indicating
that private hospitals engage though different social media
handles like LinkedIn, Facebook and Instagram to engage
with their patients. A study by Southwell and Thorson
reported that social media is a vital tool for interaction with
patients and combating misinformation.4 Similar to present
study, a previous study also showed that Public hospitals

While the study showcases the utilization of digital and

Comparative studies across regions or countries could further
