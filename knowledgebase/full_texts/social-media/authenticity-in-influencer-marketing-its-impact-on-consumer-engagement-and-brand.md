ABSTRACT
Influencer marketing has emerged as an important digital marketing strategy through which brands communicate
with consumers in an increasingly social media-driven environment. However, the effectiveness of influencer
marketing depends not only on the reach or popularity of influencers but also on how authentic they are perceived
to be by their followers. The present study examines the influence of influencer authenticity on consumer
engagement and brand advocacy among social media users in Chennai. The study adopted a descriptive research
design and collected primary data from 125 respondents using a structured questionnaire and a five-point Likert
scale. Convenience sampling was used for selecting the respondents. The data were analysed using percentage
analysis, descriptive statistics, reliability analysis, correlation and regression analysis. The findings indicate that
influencer authenticity has a significant positive influence on consumer engagement. Consumer engagement also
has a significant positive influence on brand advocacy. Further, influencer authenticity has a significant direct
influence on brand advocacy. The multiple regression analysis indicates that influencer authenticity and consumer
engagement together explain 22.8% of the variation in brand advocacy. The findings suggest that brands should
focus on genuine, transparent and relatable influencers rather than relying solely on follower numbers and reach.
Building authentic influencer–consumer relationships can help brands generate meaningful engagement and
encourage consumers to recommend and support the promoted brands. The study concludes that authenticity
represents an important factor in strengthening the effectiveness of influencer marketing and developing positive
consumer responses.

INTRODUCTION
Social media has transformed the way consumers
interact with brands and make purchasing decisions.
Platforms such as Instagram, YouTube, Facebook,
and other digital channels have created opportunities
for individuals to share opinions, experiences, and
recommendations with large audiences. In this
changing digital environment, influencer marketing
has emerged as an important promotional strategy
for brands seeking to connect with consumers in a
more personal and interactive manner. Influencers
can shape consumer perceptions by creating content
that combines product information with their own
experiences, opinions, and lifestyles. Unlike
traditional advertising, influencer marketing often
appears more personal and relatable, which can
contribute to greater consumer attention and
interaction.
The effectiveness of influencer marketing, however,
depends considerably on how authentic the
influencer is perceived to be. Authenticity refers to
the extent to which an influencer is perceived as
genuine, honest, transparent, consistent, and true to
their own identity. Consumers are increasingly
exposed to sponsored posts, paid collaborations,
product placements, and promotional content. As a
result, they may distinguish between content that
appears genuinely created by an influencer and
content that is perceived as purely commercial.
When followers believe that influencers
communicate honestly and maintain consistency
between their opinions and promotional activities,
they may develop greater trust and willingness to
interact with the content. Authenticity can therefore
become an important factor in determining the
effectiveness of influencer marketing.
Consumer engagement represents the level of
involvement and interaction that consumers have
with influencer-generated content and the brands
associated with it. Engagement may involve
cognitive responses, emotional reactions, and
behavioural activities such as liking, commenting,
sharing, saving, or discussing content. Influencers
who are perceived as authentic may be more
successful in generating such engagement because

their content can create a stronger sense of

social media and influencer marketing has
highlighted the importance of credibility, trust,
authenticity, and perceived relational value in
influencing consumer responses (Audrezet et al.,
2020; Lou & Yuan, 2019).
Beyond engagement, influencer marketing can also
contribute to brand advocacy. Brand advocacy refers
to consumers' willingness to voluntarily
recommend, support, and speak positively about a
brand. Engaged consumers may be more likely to
share brand-related content, recommend products to
others, provide positive word-of-mouth, and
encourage their social networks to consider a

serve as an important behavioural outcome of
effective influencer communication and may
contribute to stronger relationships between
consumers and brands.
Authenticity is particularly relevant because
consumers can easily encounter a large volume of
influencer-generated promotional content.
Excessive commercialisation may reduce the
perceived genuineness of an influencer and may
negatively affect followers' responses. On the other
hand, transparent sponsorship disclosures, genuine
product experiences, relatable communication, and
consistency between an influencer's identity and the
products promoted may strengthen consumer
confidence. Therefore, understanding the role of
authenticity is important for marketers who use
influencers as part of their digital marketing
strategies.
In the Indian context, the increasing popularity of
social media has created substantial opportunities
for influencer-based marketing. Consumers,
particularly active social media users, regularly
encounter influencers promoting fashion, beauty,
food, technology, lifestyle, and other products and
services. Chennai represents an important urban
market in which consumers are exposed to diverse
forms of digital and influencer marketing. However,
the effectiveness of such marketing cannot be
assumed solely from the popularity or reach of an
influencer. The perceived authenticity of the
influencer and the resulting consumer response are
important considerations.
Against this background, the present study examines
the impact of authenticity in influencer marketing on
consumer engagement and brand advocacy among
consumers in Chennai. The study focuses on three
aspects: the influence of influencer authenticity on
consumer engagement, the relationship between
consumer engagement and brand advocacy, and the
influence of influencer authenticity on brand
advocacy. By examining these relationships, the
study seeks to provide a better understanding of how
perceived authenticity can contribute to meaningful
consumer responses and stronger brand-related
advocacy in the social media environment.
REVIEW OF LITERATURE
Influencer Authenticity
Influencer authenticity has become an important
factor in understanding the effectiveness of
influencer marketing. Authenticity generally refers
to the perception that an influencer is genuine,
honest, consistent, and transparent in
communication with followers. Audrezet et al.
(2020) observed that influencers can strengthen their
relationships with followers when they demonstrate
genuine passion and maintain consistency between
their personal identity and the content they create.
Their study also highlighted that excessive
commercialisation may weaken perceptions of
authenticity.
Lou and Y uan (2019) found that the value and
credibility of influencer-generated content can
positively affect consumers' trust in branded content
and their purchase-related responses. The study
indicates that the quality of information provided by
influencers and the trustworthiness of the influencer
are important factors in shaping consumer
responses. Similarly, Djafarova and Rushworth
(2017) found that social media personalities can
influence consumers because followers perceive
them as relatable and trustworthy sources of
information.
Authenticity is also closely associated with
transparency in sponsored content. When
influencers openly communicate their commercial
relationships with brands, consumers may perceive
the communication as more honest and credible.
Boerman (2020) noted that disclosure of sponsored
content can influence consumers' recognition and
evaluation of advertising. Thus, authenticity is not
limited to personal expression but also involves the
manner in which commercial relationships are
communicated.
Influencer Authenticity and Consumer
Engagement
Consumer engagement represents the degree to
which consumers cognitively, emotionally, and
behaviourally interact with social media content. In
influencer marketing, engagement can be reflected
through activities such as liking, commenting,
sharing, saving, and discussing influencer content.
When followers perceive an influencer as genuine
and relatable, they may be more willing to interact
with the influencer's content.
Lou and Kim (2019) found that characteristics of
social media influencers, including credibility and
attractiveness, can influence followers' attitudes and
behavioural responses. Similarly, Sokolova and Kefi
(2020) reported that credibility and parasocial
interaction with influencers can contribute to
consumers' purchase intentions. These findings
suggest that the relationship developed between

Audrezet et al. (2020) further suggested that

Followers may respond more positively when

interests. Therefore, authenticity can be expected to

Consumer Engagement and Brand Advocacy
Consumer engagement extends beyond simple
interaction with social media content and can
contribute to stronger consumer-brand relationships.
Highly engaged consumers may voluntarily
communicate their experiences and opinions to other
consumers. Such activities can take the form of
positive word-of-mouth, recommendations, social-
media sharing, and active support for a brand.
Hollebeek et al. (2014) conceptualised customer
brand engagement as a multidimensional construct
involving cognitive, emotional, and behavioural
dimensions. Their work suggests that consumers
who are highly engaged with a brand are more likely
to develop stronger relationships with the brand.
Vivek et al. (2012) similarly emphasised that
consumer engagement involves interactive
experiences that can strengthen relationships
between consumers and organisations.
In the context of social media, engaged consumers
can become valuable advocates because they
voluntarily communicate brand-related information
within their social networks. This makes consumer
engagement particularly relevant to brands using
influencer marketing, as engagement generated
through influencer content may extend into positive
recommendations and advocacy.
Influencer Authenticity and Brand Advocacy
Influencer authenticity can also have a direct
influence on consumers' willingness to support and
recommend brands. When consumers perceive an
influencer as genuine, they may place greater trust
in the influencer's product recommendations. This
trust can subsequently influence their willingness to
communicate positively about the promoted brand.
Freberg et al. (2011) identified social media
influencers as a new category of opinion leaders who
can shape audience perceptions through their
communication and personal influence. De Veirman
et al. (2017) demonstrated that influencer
characteristics can affect brand-related outcomes,
particularly in Instagram-based marketing. These
studies indicate that influencers can play an
important role in transferring their perceived
credibility and influence to the brands they promote.
However, the success of this transfer depends on the
perceived fit between the influencer, the promoted
product, and the consumer's expectations. When
promotional communication is perceived as genuine
and consistent with the influencer's identity,
consumers may be more willing to develop positive
attitudes towards the brand and communicate those
attitudes to others.
Research Perspective
The literature indicates that authenticity is an
important element of successful influencer
marketing. Existing studies have examined
influencer credibility, trust, parasocial interaction,
purchase intention, and engagement from different
perspectives. However, comparatively greater
attention can be given to the relationship between
perceived influencer authenticity, consumer
engagement, and brand advocacy within the context
of Indian consumers.
The present study therefore focuses specifically on
consumers in Chennai and examines whether
perceived influencer authenticity significantly
influences consumer engagement and brand
advocacy. It also examines whether consumer
engagement is significantly associated with brand
advocacy. The study provides an empirical
perspective on how authenticity can contribute to
stronger consumer responses to influencer
marketing.
RESEARCH GAP
Influencer marketing has received considerable
attention in recent marketing research, particularly
in relation to influencer credibility, trust,
attractiveness, purchase intention, and consumer
responses. Existing studies have established that
influencer characteristics can influence how
consumers perceive and respond to branded content.
However, the role of authenticity remains
particularly important as influencer marketing
becomes increasingly commercialised. While
previous studies have examined authenticity in
relation to trust and purchase-related outcomes,
fewer studies have simultaneously examined its
relationship with consumer engagement and brand
advocacy.
Further, much of the existing literature has focused
on international markets and specific social media
platforms, while empirical evidence from Indian
urban consumers remains comparatively limited.
Chennai provides an appropriate context for
examining this issue because consumers are
increasingly exposed to influencer-generated
content across different social media platforms.
Therefore, there is a need to examine whether
consumers' perceptions of influencer authenticity
translate into greater engagement and stronger
willingness to advocate for brands. The present
study addresses this gap by empirically examining
the influence of influencer authenticity on consumer
engagement and brand advocacy among social
media users in Chennai.

RESEARCH METHODOLOGY
The study adopted a descriptive research design to
examine the influence of influencer authenticity on
consumer engagement and brand advocacy. The
study was conducted among social media users in
Chennai who follow or interact with social media
influencers. Primary data were collected through a
structured questionnaire using a five-point Likert
scale, ranging from 1 = Strongly Disagree to 5 =
Strongly Agree. A total of 125 valid responses were
considered for the analysis. Respondents were
selected using convenience sampling, considering
the accessibility of social media users and the
practical scope of the study. The questionnaire
consisted of questions relating to respondents'
demographic characteristics and statements
measuring influencer authenticity, consumer
engagement, and brand advocacy. The collected data
were analysed using percentage analysis, descriptive
statistics, correlation analysis, and regression
analysis with the help of SPSS.

Measurement of Variables
The major constructs were measured using
statements based on the existing literature on
influencer marketing and consumer behaviour.
Respondents indicated their level of agreement with
each statement using a five-point Likert scale.
Influencer Authenticity was measured through
dimensions such as genuineness, honesty,
transparency, consistency, and relatability.
Consumer Engagement was measured through
respondents' cognitive, emotional, and behavioural
involvement with influencer-generated content.
Brand Advocacy was measured through
respondents' willingness to recommend, positively
discuss, share information about, and support brands
promoted by influencers.

statistics, correlation analysis and regression
analysis. The analysis was conducted to examine the
influence of influencer authenticity on consumer
engagement and brand advocacy among social
media users in Chennai.

Consumers who perceive influencers as genuine,

SUGGESTIONS AND MANAGERIAL
IMPLICATIONS
Based on the findings of the study, several
suggestions can be made for brands and marketing
professionals using influencer marketing.
Focus on Authentic Influencers

Brands should give greater importance to the
perceived authenticity of influencers rather than
selecting influencers solely on the basis of follower
count. Influencers who communicate naturally,
express genuine opinions and maintain consistency
with their established identity may generate stronger
consumer responses.
Ensure Influencer–Brand Compatibility
Brands should select influencers whose personality,
content style and audience are compatible with the
brand. When the products promoted by an influencer
are consistent with their usual content and interests,
the promotional message is more likely to appear
genuine to followers.
Encourage Genuine Product Experiences
Influencers should be encouraged to share their
actual experiences with products rather than relying
exclusively on scripted promotional messages.
Genuine demonstrations, reviews and personal
experiences can increase the perceived credibility of
influencer-generated content.
Maintain Transparency
Influencers and brands should clearly communicate
sponsored relationships. Transparency regarding
paid collaborations can help maintain trust and
reduce the possibility that consumers perceive
promotional content as misleading.
Encourage Two-Way Interaction
Brands should encourage influencers to interact with
their followers through comments, questions, polls,
live sessions and other interactive formats. Such
activities can increase consumer involvement and
strengthen engagement with the promoted brand.
Convert Engagement into Advocacy
Marketing campaigns should be designed not only
to generate likes and views but also to encourage
consumers to recommend and share brand-related
experiences. User-generated content, referral
activities and community-based campaigns can help
transform engagement into brand advocacy.
Avoid Excessive Commercialisation
Influencers should avoid promoting too many
unrelated brands within a short period. Excessive
sponsored content may reduce perceptions of
authenticity and make followers less responsive to
promotional messages.
Monitor Long-Term Relationships
Brands should consider developing long-term
relationships with suitable influencers rather than
relying exclusively on one-time promotional
campaigns. Consistent collaborations can create
greater familiarity and credibility among followers.
CONCLUSION
Influencer marketing has become an important
component of contemporary digital marketing, but
its effectiveness depends not merely on the reach or
popularity of influencers. The present study
highlights the importance of authenticity in shaping
consumer responses to influencer-generated content.
The findings indicate that consumers who perceive
influencers as genuine, transparent, consistent and
relatable are more likely to engage with their
content.
The study also demonstrates that consumer
engagement has a significant positive influence on
brand advocacy. Consumers who actively interact
with influencer content are more likely to
recommend, support and communicate positively
about the associated brands. In addition, influencer
authenticity has a direct positive influence on brand
advocacy, indicating that authenticity can contribute
to favourable brand-related behaviour beyond
generating engagement alone.
The findings therefore suggest that brands should
adopt a more relationship-oriented approach to
influencer marketing. Instead of focusing primarily
on follower numbers and short-term visibility,
marketers should identify influencers who have
genuine relationships with their audiences and
whose personal identity is compatible with the
brand. Authentic communication, transparent
sponsorship, meaningful interaction and relevant
brand partnerships can contribute to stronger
consumer engagement and advocacy.
Overall, the study establishes that authenticity is an
important element of effective influencer marketing.
For brands operating in the increasingly competitive
social media environment, building credible and
genuine influencer relationships can help transform
consumer attention into meaningful engagement and
positive brand advocacy.
LIMITATIONS OF THE STUDY
The study has certain limitations that should be
considered while interpreting the findings. First, the
study was restricted to 125 respondents from
Chennai, and therefore the findings may not be
generalised to consumers in other cities or regions.
Second, the study adopted convenience sampling,
which may limit the representativeness of the
sample. Third, the study focused on consumers'
perceptions of influencer authenticity, consumer
engagement and brand advocacy and did not
examine other factors such as influencer credibility,
attractiveness, expertise, parasocial interaction or
purchase intention. Finally, the study was cross-
sectional in nature and therefore reflects
respondents' perceptions at a particular point in time.
SCOPE FOR FUTURE RESEARCH
Future studies can extend the research by using a
larger sample covering different cities and
demographic groups. Comparative studies can also
examine whether perceptions of influencer
authenticity differ across age groups, gender, income
levels and social media platforms. Future
researchers may incorporate additional variables
such as trust, influencer credibility, parasocial
interaction, brand trust and purchase intention.
Further research can also examine whether
consumer engagement acts as a mediating variable
