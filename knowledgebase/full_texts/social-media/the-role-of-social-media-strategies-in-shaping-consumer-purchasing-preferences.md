# The Role of Social Media Strategies in Shaping Consumer Purchasing Preferences

083

The Role of Social Media Strategies in Shaping Consumer
Purchasing Preferences
Abstract Keywords

This research examines the contribution of social media marketing strategies to shaping the
buying preferences of consumers regarding Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, YouTube, and
Twitter/X (X, 2021). The research utilized quantitative research methods to coll ect the data
for this study from a selection of 385 individuals with differing demographics through the
use of a structured questionnaire. The questionnaire consisted of 4 dimensions: quality of
social media content, effectiveness of influencer marketing, impact of targeted advertising,
and impact of word -of-mouth marketing on buying behaviour. The data were analysed
using descriptive statistics, Pearson correlation, and multiple linear regression analysis to
test 4 research hypotheses. The results showed that there was a statistically significant and
positive predictive relationship among all 4 dimensions of social media strategy on
consumers' buying behaviour (R2 = 0.712, F = 95.43, p < 0.001). The strongest predictor was
found to be the effectiveness of i nfluencer marketing (β = 0.381), followed by the
effectiveness of word-of-mouth marketing (β = 0.274), targeted advertising (β = 0.198), and
quality of social media content (β = 0.152). Therefore, the findings provide empirical
evidence to support the hypo thesis that an effective social media strategy has a positive
effect on consumers' propensity to purchase goods/services. Finally, the implications of
these findings for managers and researchers, and suggestions for future research are
discussed .

1. Introduction
Today's digital marketing and consumer
behaviour have changed dramatically as a result of
the recent surge in the availability and use of
social networking sites. With approximately 4.9
billion active global social network users
projected for 2024 (DataReportal, 2024),
companies are taking advantage of social media as
their main means of communicating with
consumers, establishing their brands through
storytelling, and reaching out to existing and
potential customers.

Social Media Marketing consists of a
variety of methods and strategies, ranging from
creating and posting content, to influencer
marketing, to advertising on social media, and
community management. All these methods and
strategies have the objective of impacting
consumers' attitude and perception towards
products and services and changing th e way
consumers make purchasing decisions.
As of 2023, the worldwide market for
social media advertisements (the commercial
significance of social media) was estimated to be
valued at US$226.8 billion, with an estimated
growth rate will take this value up to US$358.9
billion, by the year 2028 (Statista, 2024). The fast
rate of growth of the social media advertising
market demonstrates how marketers are realising
that they provide an ideal method of connecting
with consumers in a targeted manner and thereby
achieving higher conversion rates than through
more traditional advertising methods. However,
academic research has not yet fully explored how
individual social media marketing strategies
convert into measurable changes in consumer
behaviour or purchase intent.
Many studies have shown that there is a
relationship between the use of social media and
how consumers make their decisions (Kaplan &
Heinlein, 2010; Mangold & Foulds, 2009).
However, there are not as many studies that look
at the different effects o f different types of
strategies (e.g., content quality, influencer
marketing, targeted advertising, and word -of-
mouth marketing) on buying preferences using an
empirical framework. This gap is particularly
large in studies that utilize both rigorous statis tical
analysis and primary data collection..
To fill in the gap in the literature, we
develop and test a conceptual model that will help
identify the relationships between four
components of a social media strategy and
consumers' purchasing preferences. Planned
behavior theory (Eisen, 1991) and the information
adoption model (Sussman and Segal, 2003)
provide the theoretical background for our
research. We collect data using a structured
questionnaire and analyze the data using multiple
regression analysis to identify strong empirical
relationships..
1.1 Research Objectives
The following are the objectives of the study: • To
analyse how the quality of social media content
affects consumers' decisions about what to buy.

• To analyse how influencer marketing  affects the
way consumers make buying decisions.

• To analyse how ads targeted to consumers on
social media will influence their purchasing
decisions.

• To evaluate how consumers' decisions to
purchase products or services will be impacted by
word-of-mouth (eWOM) advertising on social
media platforms.

2.  Literature Review
2.1 Social Media Marketing: Theoretical
Foundations
Social media marketing (SMM) can be
defined as utilizing various forms of social media
to help market and promote products/services to
consumers (Tuten & Solomon, 2017). Depending
on various theoretical frameworks, social media
marketing can be analyzed based on various
research studies conducted in regard to SMM &
social media usage. The Technology Acceptance
Model (Davis, 1989) shows th at perceived
usefulness and perceived ease of use are the two
main factors that affect whether or not people use
& adopt technologies, including the use of social
media for information when deciding on what
items to purchase. Ajzen's(1991) Theory of
Planned Behavior also indicates how consumers
make their buying decisions through how
consumers perceive situations, through subjective
norms, and by having perceived behavioral
control (based on social media content) over their
actions; therefore, based on how all these factors
have been affected by the way that peer influence
and/or social media content created & displayed.
The IAM, proposed by Sussman and
Siegal in 2003, is an important theoretical
framework for understanding influencer and word
of mouth marke ting. The Information Influence
Theory (IAM) explains why individual consumers
will adopt information shared electronically from
credible/ trustworthy sources as high quality. In
2014, He et al. extended the IAM to the context of
social commerce, finding b oth informational and
normative social influences have a significant
effect on purchasing intention, through the
perception of usefulness of online reviews..

2.2  Social Media Content Quality and
Consumer Behavior
Content quality is a key factor in the
effectiveness of social media marketing. Holbeck
et al. (2014) define social media brand
engagement as a positive cognitive, emotional,
and behavioral activity of the consumer, related to
the brand, during or after their interactions with
the brand. High -quality, relevant, and visually
appealing content enhances brand engagement
and, consequently, purchase intentions.
Kim and Ko (2012) conducted a
pioneering study examining the social media
marketing activities of luxury fashion brands.
They identified conten t quality —specifically,
entertainment, engagement, trendiness,
personalization, and word -of-mouth
recommendations—as influential factors in
customer brand value and purchase intentions.
There is supporting evidence from Judy et al.,
2016 who analyzed the s ocial media marketing
strategies for luxury brands located in 4 different
regions and found that the social media content
dimensions have a strong influence on both the
brand equity and the response of consumers. More
recent studies from Forfield et al. .. .  In a 2018
study it was determined that social media user
behavior - passive consumers versus actively
engaged - affect the relationship between the
quality of the content and the users' attitudes
toward the purchase. Passive consumption (e.g.
viewing a sponsored post) created greater brand
awareness while active engagement (e.g. comment
and share) created greater purchase intentions.
2.3  Influencer Marketing
One of the fastest -growing components of
digital marketing strategies today is influencer
marketing. Influencers are people who have
specialized knowledge or authority within their
respective fields or industries, and have
established a relationship with their audience that
allows them to connect through their unique

080

networks. Because of these connec tions,
influencers have the ability to impact consumers’
purchasing decisions based on their credibility or
through other means such as their reach and
relevance (Freberg, 2011). The global influencer
marketing industry was valued at $21.1 billion
(USD) in  2023, an increase over the previous
year's total value of $16.4 billion (USD)
(Influencer Marketing Hub, 2023).
According to Lu and Yuan (2019),
researchers examined how uses of social media
influence followers’ intentions to purchase from
brands by utili zing the trustworthiness of the
influencer and the credibility of the informational
content they create. Lu and Yuan found that the
quality of the information provided by the
influencer and the perception of the influencer’s
credibility both serve as media ting variables in
this relationship between trustworthiness and the
behavioral intentions of the follower.
Additionally, they noted the importance of
credibility on consumers’ intentions; specifically,
it was noted that consumers are more likely to be
influenced by creators who they perceive to be
truthful and transparent regarding their association
with a brand.
Danish and Dottler (2019) investigated
how to use the strategic management of
relationships between an influencer and their
audience to predict pu rchase intentions;
specifically, that the stronger the quality of
relationship between an influencer and their
audience—defined as trust, commitment,
satisfaction, and the amount of mutual
interaction—the greater likelihood there will be.
Their study revea led that smaller -sized influencers
(those who have 10,000 to 100,000 followers)
frequently have higher engagement and more
authentic consumer experiences than larger -sized
influencers, a result that provides valuable insights
into the strategic decisions made by brands..
The quasi -social interaction theory (Horton and
Wohl, 1956) provides a fundamental basis for
understanding the effectiveness of influencers: as
people develop a quasi -social relationship with a
media figure, they develop familiarity with an d
trust in the media person’s expertise, which in turn
allows for effective persuasion. Chung & Zhu
(2017) utilized this theoretical framework in an
examination of social media influencers and found
that quasi -social interaction has a positive effect
on consumer attitudes toward the brand and on the
intent to purchase, especially among younger
consumers.
2.4  Targeted Advertising via Social Media
Thanks to the computational power of
social media, advertising is more accurately
targeted than ever before. Businesses are now able
to use demographic information, behavioral
trends, interest profiles, and other patterns of
behavior to create advertisements that appeal to
very narrow consumer segments (Iyer et al.,
2005). The increasing accuracy with which
advertisements can be targeted will also increase
the relevance of advertising, and therefore the
receptivity of consumers to the commercial
messages received. Bleier and Eisenbeiss (2015)
found that ads which are personalized for their
audience have significan tly greater click -through
rates and intent to purchase than ads which are not
personalized. They also found that ad relevance is
the primary mediator variable between
personalized and non -personalized ads regarding
consumer responses.
Consumers can be infl uenced positively by a
personalized ad, but this is not always true
(Sharma et al.). According to a study done in 2022
on a phenomenon called the personalization
paradox, too much personalization of an ad can
cause fears regarding privacy violations and
subsequently cause a consumer to become less
favorable toward the ad in question. Researchers
concluded that either excessive personalization or
not enough personalization will result in
decreased effectiveness of the ad. Therefore, the
optimal level of pers onalization has been reached
when the ad is relevant to the individual consumer
and meets their expectations regarding how much
privacy they wish to have. Ethical considerations

083

should therefore be considered when developing
social media marketing strategies.

2.5  Word-of-Mouth Marketing (eWOM)
Word-of-mouth marketing encompasses
various types of comments provided by future,
past, or present customers about a product or
brand that have been shared on a large scale over
the internet (Hennig -Thurau et al., 2 004: 39).
Generally, eWOM is viewed as having greater
credibility and being less commercially driven
than content produced by marketers; therefore, it
possesses considerable persuasive abilities..
Cheung and Thadani (2012) performed an
organized examination of the existing literature on
electronic word -of-mouth (eWOM) and found
three kinds of characteristics to value; the
characteristics are message characteristics (e.g.,
quality, credibility, number) as well as the
characteristics of a source (e.g., expert ise,
trustworthiness), and the characteristics of the
receiver (e.g., the degree to which the receiver is
engaged, prior knowledge of the product) that are
considered the greatest predictors of eWOM
effectiveness. The findings showed that eWOM
marketing ha d a large impact on consumer
attitudes and purchase intentions; credibility of the
source was the most commonly cited predictor of
effectiveness among the three variables studied by
the researchers.
This framework was subsequently expanded into
social commerce environments by Ismagilova et
al. (2020), who found that social media word of
mouth advertising (i.e., product ratings, reviews
and recommendations) strongly predicts
consumers' decisions to purchase products in
general, and particularly, high -impact product
categories. This study also featured a quantitative
indicator of the relationship between review
volume and consumer decision making; that is, as
more reviews are posted consumers will perceive
the product to be more popular, which is an
independent variable that influences consumer
decision making.
3.  Research Methodology
3.1  Research Design
This study adopts a quantitative, positivist
research model, consistent with the objective of
testing hypothetical relationships between
theoretically derived concepts using statistical
inference. A cross -sectional survey design was
employed, given its sui tability for collecting data
from large samples simultaneously and its ability
to examine relationships between variables
through correlation and regression analysis
(Cresswell, 2014).
3.2.  Population and Sample
Social Media Users aged 18 and Over Who Hav e
Made One or More Online Purchases Within the
Last Six Months Was the Target Population for
the Research. The Research Team Used a Non -
probability Purposeful Sampling Method Because
There Was No Defined Sampling Frame to
Operate From. A Combination of Sno wball
Sampling to Increase the Size of the Sample Will
Also Be Used. Sample Size Will Be Calculated
Using the Formula Developed by Cochran (1977).
n = Z² × p × (1 - p) / e²
where Z = 1.96 (95% confidence level), p
= 0.5 (assuming maximum the minimum sample
size was determined as n=384 after rounding up to
n=385 to guarantee accuracy because the criteria
used were  >.99 for confidence level (variance),
0.05 for margin of error.
The number of questionnaires distributed was 420.
Of those, 397 were returned for a response rate of
94.5%. A final number of 385 valid returned
questionnaires were available after excluding
those that were incomplete or had conflicting
responses.

3.6  Data Analysis
Data analysis for this study included descriptive
statistics (e.g., means, standard deviations, etc.)
for all study variables and correlation analyses
(Pearson), to determine the direction and strength
of the relationships between all variables. Circular
regression analysis was used to test each of the
research hypotheses and to measure h ow much

5.1 Interpretation of Results
Further, as a result of our model's ability to
explain 71.2% of the variance in purchase

marketing having a significant effect on consumer
purchasing preference. Thus, the four overall
strategic dimensions can be viewed as a
framework that allows for comprehensive
evaluation of the impact of social media
marketing on consumer purchasing decisions.
Influencers have proven to be very
influential by creating quasi -social connections
between themselves and their fans/consumer base

influence the decision -making process of

5.2  Theoretical Implications
This research is a valuable addition to both
theoretical knowledge, about consumer behaviour
and social media marketing. It creates a broader
understanding of how social media marketing
affects consumers’ purchase intent, by integrating
the theory of planned behaviour (Eisen, 1991) and
the model of information adoption (Sussman and
Segal, 2003) within a single empirical framework.
Furthermore, the evidence of four different types
of strategy dimensions s upports the creation of
more precise frameworks for measuring the
effectiveness of social media marketing, rather
than relying on just one overall measure of social
media use.
The R² value of .712 indicates that this
four-element structure is predictive of much of the
impact of social media on consumer behaviour,
and strongly supports future research..

5.3  Managerial Implications
This research provides marketing
professionals with actionable insights. Brands
should strive to create authentic and meaningful
brand ambassador programs through micro -
influencers. These micro -influencers should have
an affinity with the brand’s ethos and goals for
their customer base. Additionally, brands should
allocate resources to encourage word of mouth by
developing review sites, providing social
engagement incentives, and creating a
community-building program. This will leverage
the predictive nature of word of mouth and will
align with the focus of the brand manager.
Advertisers must put people first by
creating t argeted advertisement plans that focus
on being transparent and protecting consumer
privacy such as by allowing an ―opt -in‖ feature,
providing a ―clear value proposition‖ that makes it
obvious to consumers how their personal
information is being used for p ersonalized ads.
Lastly, advertisers should continue to invest in
developing good quality & visually appealing &

to provide value to consumers as well as support

6.  Conclusion
The objective of this research was to
examine how social media marketing strategies
influence consumer purchasing preferences, via a
comprehensive empirical study that was
completed using quantitative data. The
investigators utilized previously validated
measures to record data from 385 survey
respondents, which were subsequently analyzed
using multiple regression analysis techniques. The
data revealed that all of the four hypothesized
independent variables (i.e., quality of social media
posts, capability o f social media publishers as
influencers, targeting strategies in social media
advertising campaigns and word of mouth) were
found to have a statistically significant positive
effect on consumer purchasing preferences.
The leading strategic component of
promoting products through social networks was
influencer marketing (β = .381); followed by word
of mouth marketing (β = .274); targeted
advertising (β = .198); and lastly content quality
(β = .152). Accordingly, the integrated model
accounted for 71.2% of t he variance in consumer
purchasing preferences, setting out the theoretical
and practical importance of the social media
marketing framework that was developed during
this research project.
There are some constraints associated with
the data in this study.  First, because a non -
probability sampling method was used, results
from the sample cannot be generalised to the
wider population of users from the culture/nation;
in addition to that, because a cross -sectional
design was used, no causal inference could be
made. Longitudinal designs would improve the
ability to establish a temporal relationship
between variables. Future studies should also
investigate the role of consumer engagement as a
moderator of the relationships identified in this
study. These variabl es may vary across product
categories, age groups and cultures.This study
contributes to the body of empirical research by
helping to develop theory in the areas of social
media marketing and consumer behaviour as well
as providing organizations with guide lines to help
them use social media marketing effectively.
