Abstract
This study explores the influence of web listening capability (WLC) and social media responsiveness
(SMR) on consumer purchase intention (CPI) in the growing social -commerce environment with
consumer trust (CT) as a mediator and privacy concerns (PC) as a moderator between trust and
intention. The survey results of the 350 online consumers were used to estimate a PLS -SEM. All direct,
indirect and moderating effects were found to be significant. Consumer trust becomes the most salient
proximal antecedent of purchase intention and privacy issues intensify the trust –intention relationship.
The model accounts for 54.0% of the variance in CPI, providing theoretical and managerial
understanding of the nature of trust in social commerce in emerging markets.
Keywords: Web Listening Capability, Social Media Responsiveness, Perceived Information Quality,
Consumer Trust, Privacy Concerns, Consumer Purchase Intention, PLS-SEM and Pakistan.
Introduction
The social -media landscape in Pakistan is rapidly evolving, and this has changed the
nature of brand–consumer engagement from a monologue to a dialogue; one where brands have
to listen, respond and adapt in real -time. In this landscape, web listening ability, social media
responsiveness and the quality of the information shared is a strategic factor in determining
digital credibility, according to consumers. However, the behavioural mechanisms linking such
capabilities to purchase intention and the pivotal role of privacy concerns haven't been
sufficiently studied when applied to emerging-market contexts (T. Bibi et al., 2025).
This study aims to fill that gap by modelling that WLC, SMR, and PIQ are parallel
antecedents of CPI, and that CT is a mediator between them and CPI and that PC is a contingent
moderator between CT and CPI. This study only includes Pakistani consumers who actively
interact with brands through social media, thereby providing a glimpse of the push-pull dynamics
between relational reassurance and data concerns in a mobile-first world (Khan et al., 2023).
Research Objectives
• To explore the direct effect of WLC, SMR and PIQ on consumer purchase intention.
• To understand how WLC, SMR and PIQ affect consumer trust in a social -commerce
environment.
• To evaluate the mediation effect of consumer trust in the relationship between these
antecedents and purchase intention.

Literature Review
Web listening capability is the systematic process a firm uses to listen to, interpret and
respond to consumer signals on the web. It's a dynamic capability to transform that unstructured
online chatter into actionable insight and, crucially, responsiveness that can be seen. Social
media responsiveness is the outward expression of that capability, that is, the ability to respond
quickly, personally and empathically to consumer questions and content on a public forum. Both
constructs are closely related conceptually and analytically different (Attonete et al., 2026).
Perceived information quality is the consumers' assessment of the accuracy, relevance,
timeliness and completeness of the informative message conveyed through the digital touch
points. In social-commerce, information quality is a credibility cue that decreases cognitive effort
and decreases perceived transaction risk. WLC, SMR and PIQ are the three pillars of
informational and relational elements which form the basis of digital consumer trust  (Senali et
al., 2024).
In emerging markets with uneven formal recourse mechanisms, consumer trust is the best
short-term predictor of purchase intentions. However, there is a contingent tension due to privacy
concerns: Consumers with a higher view of data -collection risks may reweight the utility of trust
in their behavioral intent decision. This moderation can be either negative or positive, and is
therefore theoretically unclear and thus should be assessed empirically in the present model
(Shava, 2025).

H1: Listening on the web is positively and significantly influencing consumer purchase
intention.

the consumer.

H4: The hypothesis of “Web listening capability has a significant positive effect on

H6: Perceived information quality has a significant positive effect on consumer trust.

Methodology
The study is quantitative, a survey, and is based on a post -positivist paradigm. Target
population are Pakistani consumers 18 years and older who follow, interact with and/or transact
with brands on key social-media platforms. Purposive and snowball sampling were used to reach
the respondents on mobile channels, resulting in responses of 350 respondents, which is
considered as a good sample size in PLS -SEM on the bases of the ten -times rule, due to the
complexity of the model.
All 6 constructs were measured reflectively through multi -items, 5 -point Likert scales
adapted from the social -commerce, trust and privacy literatures. Academic experts and pilot
sample were used for the pre -testing of the instrument. To address common -method concerns,
the measures were applied the following: Anonymity, item separation, and Harman's single -
factor screening.
Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling was used to analyse the data, which
is suitable for a predictive theory building with mediation and moderation. Reliability,
convergent validity and discriminant validity (HTMT) of the measurement model were
evaluated. Path coefficients, R² and adjusted R2, f2 effect sizes and bootstrapped (5,000
resamples) direct, indirect and interaction effects were used to evaluate the structural model.
Approximate model fit of SRMR was inspected (Afzal et al., 2024).

Discussion
The results support three theoretical arguments. Second, in a social -commerce context,
firm-side capabilities such as web -listening, responsiveness and information quality act as
capabilities that work as informational and relational levers that collectively serve to develop
consumers' trust and, in turn, purchase intention  (John et al., 2024) . Second, consumer trust is a
dominant proximal mechanism, influencing the formation of behavioural intent based on these
capabilities directly (β = 0.525) and with a large f² (0.404), confirming its structural centrality
.(Oyekunle et al., 2024) . Third, privacy is a moderator on the trust –intention pathway which
amplifies, rather than attenuates (β = 0.341). This counterintuitive finding indicates that in
Pakistan's budding digital economy consumers who are sensitive to privacy do not disengage:
they instead attribute more behavioural weight to the trust signal, which can be regarded as a
heuristic for credibility to compensate for the perceived sacrifice in having to share their data
(Rehman et al., 2019).
Limitations
A cross sectional design limits causal conclusions, while self -reports of perceptions may
be subject to recall and social-desirability bias. Sample was limited to digitally active consumers
and is not generalisable to other segments with low connectivity. The near -boundary HTMT
between constructs CT and CPI also indicates conceptual closeness which can be made more
specific by more differentiated operationalisation of the constructs in future research.
Future Research Directions
Future research might consider a longitudinal or experimental design to examine causal
sequencing, incorporate objective traces from the platform (e.g., consumer behaviour) and
incorporate other moderate boundaries (e.g., brand familiarity, platform reputation, consumer
digital literacy). Comparing emerging and mature social -commerce markets would further
elucidate the model's trust -centredness and privacy -moderation, and their portability across
cultures.

Conclusion
This study provides a theoretically integrated and empirically validated explanation of
how web listening capability, social media responsiveness, and perceived information quality
contribute to the consumer purchase intention as well as how perceived information quality
strengthens the trust –consumer purchase intention relationship with the mediation of consumer
trust and how the influence of privacy concerns is magnified on the trust –consumer purchase
intention relationship. In Pakistan's quickly digitized social -commerce landscape, brands must
invest in both aspects at once – the ability to listen, and the practice of transparent information –
as these both help create the sense of trust that privacy -conscious consumers most heavily invest
in when making purchasing decisions.
