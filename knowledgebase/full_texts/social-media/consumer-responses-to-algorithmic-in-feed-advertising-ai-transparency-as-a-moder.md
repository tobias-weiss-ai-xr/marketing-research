Abstract
Driven by the advancement and application of artificial intelligence (AI) technology, in-feed advertising has
become a critical driver of consumption growth in social commerce. This study explores how stimuli from
algorithmic in-feed advertising shape the psychological perceptions of young adults, thereby influencing their
purchase and sharing intentions. Based on the stimulus–organism–response (SOR) theory, this research con -
structs a theoretical relationship model. Online questionnaire data were collected from 609 Chinese young adult
respondents, and partial least squares structural equation modeling (PLS-SEM) was employed for empirical
analysis. The empirical results reveal five key findings: (1) entertainment, personalization, and incentives exert
positive effects on users’ affective attitude, whereas informativeness and credibility show no significant associa-
tion with affective attitude; (2) informativeness, personalization, incentives, and credibility are positively corre -
lated with cognitive attitude, while entertainment has no significant impact on cognitive attitude; (3) advertising
intrusiveness negatively predicts both affective and cognitive attitudes; (4) both affective and cognitive attitudes
significantly enhance users’ purchase and sharing intentions; (5) AI transparency significantly moderates the
linkage between cognitive attitude and purchase intention, yet it exerts no moderating effect on the relationship
between affective attitude and purchase intention. This study’s findings offer practical and theoretical insights
for social commerce platforms, advertising designers, and marketers to optimize algorithmic in-feed advertising
strategies and effectively stimulate young consumers’ social commerce engagement.

Introduction
In-feed advertising is a novel form of native advertising in which promotional content is embedded directly within
a social platform’s natural, organic content feed and presented in formats that resemble surrounding posts or short
videos1. Unlike traditional display advertising, which typically appears in clearly separated spaces, in-feed adver-
tising is embedded in a continuous stream of surrounding content and is therefore encountered under conditions
of incidental exposure, rapid scrolling, and low initial involvement. In recent years, AI-powered algorithmic

customization has been applied to increase perceived relevance by matching advertising content to users’ inferred
preferences2,3. In-feed ads are becoming an important commercial interface and the main traffic source for social
commerce to acquire customers efficiently. In 2024, in-feed ads spending on social media in the United States
reached $80.39 billion, accounting for 73.9% of total in-feed ads 4. China’s in-feed ads market is projected to
reach $34.56 billion in 2025, which is the fastest-growing category, with a year-on-year growth rate of 18.85%5.
Social commerce consumers encounter products through social interaction and platform-mediated pathways
rather than only through deliberate product search6,7. With global social commerce sales projected to reach $1.3
trillion by 2025, platform-native buying behavior is becoming a durable revenue channel8. Facebook and TikTok
have shifted from simply enabling commerce to actively building the infrastructure that powers it 9,10. Shipping
Shops, Marketplace, in-app checkouts, and shoppable ads allow consumers to discover, evaluate, and purchase
products without leaving the app, reducing friction while improving conversion. In social commerce, in-feed ads
are increasingly generated and delivered by AI-driven algorithmic recommendation. Such ads are automatically
selected, ranked, and displayed in users’ feeds by the platform algorithm based on their browsing, interaction,
and purchase behavior, e.g.,“recommended for you” or “you may also like” on Douyin, Xiaohongshu, or WeChat
Moments. Leveraging rich user data, AI algorithmic advertisements enable precise targeting and personalized rec-
ommendations, often shortening the path from discovery to purchase2,3. Algorithmic in-feed advertising in social
commerce offers seamless integration with consumer-generated content, which enhances engagement without
abruptly disrupting the browsing experience. However, the challenging problem that arises in this domain is to
better understand the orientations of consumers’ decision-making, which is influenced not only by the homogeni-
zation of in-feed ads but also by the trust and ethical concerns arising from AI algorithm biases. Recent studies
on in-feed ads suggest that users often process content through fast, cue-based scanning, while responses depend
heavily on how advertising-related signals are perceived within the surrounding feed context 11. In-feed ads can
improve click-through intention, but excessive optimization may also generate concealment or deception con -
cerns and eventually weaken perceived outcomes and responses 12,13. Currently, over 62.3% of the world’s total
population uses social media. Young social network users, particularly those in Generation Z, known as digital
natives, show their technological agility and spending power growth in social commerce platforms4,14. In China,
more than 60% of active users on content and social platforms 15, and the online shopping adoption rate of post-
90s youth reaches 95.1%16. The younger generation has translated into social online consumption habits because
of advertainment, interactions and recommendations. Consequently, there is a need for further research to explore
the psychological mechanism of the consumers’ decision-making and driving factors of algorithmic in-feed envi-
ronment where AI specific recommendation is typical.
The social transmission of native advertising has substantial marketing value in networked environments17,18.
There is significant attention on the research and more insights into the impacts of in-feed ads on consumer
behavior in social commerce. The study by Shao et al. reported that the informational content, reliability, enter -
tainment, and interactivity of in-feed ads can positively influence purchase intention, whereas interference can
weaken advertising effectiveness19. Consumers’ attitudes toward native in-feed ads have also been linked to their
intention to share advertising content, particularly when the advertising is perceived as nonintrusive and com -
patible with the platform experience 20. Furthermore, previous studies have indicated that the rise of AI-driven
ads promotes digital marketing, yet trust among consumers is waning because of concerns over authenticity and
transparency. Transparency disclosure has positive effects in enhancing consumers’ emotional and cognitive trust
in the advertisements and reinforces the positive impact of trust on purchase intention 21,22. Therefore, we focus
on understanding the impacts of in-feed ads on young adults’ behavior, which is vital for marketers, advertisers
and social commerce platforms in emerging digital economies such as China. Our central research questions are
as follows: RQ1. What are the main attributes of algorithmic in-feed advertising and how do they affect young
adults’ behavioral intention in social commerce? RQ2. To what extent do affective attitude and cognitive attitude
toward in-feed ads affect young adults’ behavioral intention? RQ3. What is the moderate impact of AI transpar-
ency on the nexus between psychological factors affective attitude, cognitive attitude, and purchase intention?

Despite the growing importance of in-feed ads, research specifically on the influences of in-feed ads on pur -
chase behavior remains limited. First, recent studies on social commerce and in-feed ads have often concentrated
on outcomes such as click-through intention and adoption of recommendation, while much less attention has
been given to purchase behavior as a parallel outcome of commercial importance in feed-based environments.
Second, while prior studies have examined behavioral responses through technology adoption model (TAM)
and theory of planned behavior (TPB) in social commerce and in-feed advertising 7,13, the factors identified may
not comprehensively explain how individual decisions are made. Third, AI-powered social commerce has been
shifting to an integrated tighter system, and there is an absence of insight into the optimization of algorithmic in-
feed ads and its impact on young adult groups, which are more likely to purchase and share on social commerce
platforms.
For this study, it was important to investigate the psychological processes through which the perceived features
of algorithmic in-feed ads shape young adults’ purchase intention and sharing intention in social commerce. A
conceptual model is developed on the basis of the stimulus–organism–response (S-O-R) framework, which delin-
eates the process through which external environmental stimuli are associated with an individual’s internal state,
ultimately resulting in behavioral or intentional responses23. Six perceived generic in-feed advertising attributes
are identified within an AI algorithmic recommendation context: entertainment, informativeness, personaliza -
tion, intrusiveness, incentives, and credibility. This study explores affective attitude and cognitive attitude as
two dimensions of internal evaluation and purchase intention and sharing intention as dual response outcomes.
AI transparency is considered a construct that buffers the relationship between affective attitude, cognitive atti -
tude, and purchase intention, respectively. Overall, the research examines the mechanisms through which these
perceived factors affect individuals, provides an empirically grounded account of how consumers’ behavior is
related to AI-powered in-feed ads and offers insights for both research and practice in social commerce.
Theoretical background and hypothesis development
Stimulus–organism–response (SOR) theory
Originating in environmental psychology, SOR describes a chain in which external cues influence internal states
and subsequent behavioral outcomes 23. SOR provides a theoretical foundation for analyzing user behavior
through which external stimuli (S) influence behavioral responses (R) via internal psychological states (O) 23,24.
SOR has been applied to assess users’ mental processes and underlying psychological mechanisms of online
commerce. In the domain of social commerce, the SOR framework is frequently used to map content char -
acteristics (stimuli) onto consumers’ psychological states (organism) and ultimately to purchase intention and
purchase behavior (responses)7. Therefore, perceived advertising features can be conceptualized as key stimuli in
the social commerce information environment that shape consumers’ internal evaluations and then translate into
purchase intention and sharing intention. Because of the profound applicability of SOR to our research questions,
this study intends to apply SOR to develop a causal chain and investigate how AI algorithmic recommended in-
feed ads affect the purchase intention and sharing intention of young adults. A conceptual model derived from the
preceding discussions and hypotheses is illustrated in Fig. 1.
Stimulus (S)
Entertainment (ENT)
Entertainment emphasizes the extent to which in-feed ads deliver hedonic appeal and engagement at the content
level. From the advertising value perspective, entertainment is a core source of perceived advertising value and is
associated with more favorable overall attitudes and effectiveness-related responses 25. In social media contexts,

advertising competes with entertainment content for attention and affective evaluations and increases receptiv -
ity to social network advertising. A review of social commerce research indicates that enjoyable and immersive
experiences are linked to approach-oriented responses and purchase-related intentions 26. With the development
of technologies, generative and automated creative tools can be applied to produce visually rich, dynamic, and
experiment-driven advertising content more efficiently, which may further strengthen the entertainment value of
in-feed advertising2,27,28. Therefore, we propose the following hypothesis:
H1a: Entertainment is positively associated with affective attitude toward in-feed advertising.
H1b: Entertainment is positively associated with cognitive attitude toward in-feed advertising.
Informativeness (INF)
Informativeness highlights the diagnostic value of in-feed ads for decision-making. Sufficient product informa -
tion, usage scenarios, and comparative cues reduce uncertainty. Advertising value research treats informativeness
as an important contributor to perceived advertising value and attitude25,29. In social commerce, consumers often
rely on platform content, others’ experiences, and information cues to make decisions, and the availability and
quality of such cues are repeatedly linked to purchase intention 19. In AI-enabled feed environments, advertis -
ers can increasingly tailor product information, recommendations, and decision-support cues to users’ inferred
needs and preferences, which may strengthen the diagnostic value of advertising content2. Thus, informativeness
should more directly enhance cognitive attitudes: when advertising is perceived as useful and clear, belief-based
evaluations of value and credibility are likely to improve, thereby strengthening purchase intention25. Therefore,
we propose the following hypothesis:
H2a: Informativeness is positively associated with affective attitude toward in-feed advertising.
H2b: Informativeness is positively associated with cognitive attitude toward in-feed advertising.
Personalization (PER)
Personalization refers to consumers’ subjective perception that an in-feed ad is matching interests, fitting needs,
or aligning with context. Experimental evidence in social network advertising suggests that perceived per -
sonalization can improve brand attitude and click intention via increased perceived relevance, implying that
personalization may first influence cognitive judgments about fit and then spill over into overall evaluation 30.
Recent meta-analytic evidence further indicates that, on average, personalized advertising outperforms nonper -
sonalized advertising in enhancing attitudes and behavioral intentions 31. It becomes especially important for AI

trigger privacy and trust concerns, leading some consumers to interpret targeting as surveillance or manipulation

H3a: Personalization is positively associated with affective attitude toward in-feed advertising.
H3b: Personalization is positively associated with cognitive attitude toward in-feed advertising.
Intrusiveness (INT)
Intrusiveness reflects the extent to which an in-feed advertisement is perceived as overly promotional, interrup -
tive, or strongly persuasion driven, thereby disrupting users’ browsing autonomy11,35. In feed browsing, intrusive
sales cues may violate expectations of authentic content, activate persuasion knowledge, and trigger psychologi-
cal reactance that typically generates irritation and resistance. Reactance theory suggests that when individuals
perceive that their freedom of choice is threatened, they become motivated to restore autonomy and evaluate the
message/source more negatively36. The Persuasion Knowledge Model further posits that once consumers recog-
nize persuasive intent, it decreases credibility and value judgments37. It may become more salient in AI algorith-
mic feed environments, where highly targeted and continuously optimized advertising can increase perceived
relevance but may also intensify consumers’ sense of surveillance, manipulation, or loss of autonomy 2. Recent
research has shown that personalized marketing systems exploit consumer vulnerabilities or create resistance
when users perceive the advertising process as overly intrusive or strategically manipulative 12. Therefore, we
propose the following.
H4a: Intrusiveness is negatively associated with affective attitude toward in-feed advertising.
H4b: Intrusiveness is negatively associated with cognitive attitude toward in-feed advertising.
Incentives (INC)
Incentives influence the extent to which in-feed ads offer consumers tangible or perceived benefits for attend -
ing to, engaging with, or acting on the ad, such as discounts, coupons, cashback, referral rewards, free shipping,
limited-time deals, or other economic advantages 38. In social commerce contexts, incentives function as extrin -
sic motivators that can increase the immediate utility of advertising exposure and lower the perceived sacrifice
associated with purchasing decisions39,40. Prior research on mobile and social advertising shows that promotional
rewards can stimulate stronger consumer responses, including consumption-related and sharing-related inten -
tions, because they enhance the practical attractiveness of the advertising message and make engagement feel
more worthwhile38,41. Research on referrals and social sharing further suggests that well-designed promotional
incentives can encourage users not only to purchase but also to share offers with others, especially when the
incentive structure supports social diffusion 17,39,41. Incentives of in-feed ads strengthen cognitive attitudes by
increasing perceived value, usefulness, and decision utility38,39. Incentives may also improve affective attitudes,
as consumers can experience excitement, satisfaction, or pleasure when they perceive that an ad provides a con-
crete benefit rather than merely persuasive pressure40. Therefore, we propose the following hypothesis:
H5a: Incentives are positively associated with affective attitude toward in-feed advertising.
H5b: Incentives are positively associated with cognitive attitude toward in-feed advertising.
Credibility (CRE)
As described by Eisend 42, credibility reflects the extent to which consumers perceive an advertisement and
its claims as believable, truthful, and trustworthy. In-feed ads are embedded within social streams and often
mimic editorial or user-generated content, making the commercial nature of the message less salient and the

trustworthiness of the message more consequential43,44. Consumers may rely heavily on credibility-related judg-
ments when deciding whether an advertising message is worth believing and considering because commerciality
cues can reduce perceived credibility and trigger fears of manipulation. Advertising credibility has a stronger
effect on advertising attitude and is a key factor in increasing consumers’ positive attitudes and willingness to
purchase45,46. However, opacity in AI systems can increase skepticism, whereas transparency can mitigate nega-
tive attitudes and strengthen trust in both AI systems and the organizations that use them47. When in-feed ads are
perceived as credible, consumers are more likely to form favorable cognitive attitudes because the ad is seen as
more dependable and worthy of consideration42,45. Therefore, we formulate the following hypotheses:
H6a: Credibility is positively associated with affective attitude toward in-feed advertising.
H6b: Credibility is positively associated with cognitive attitude toward in-feed advertising.
Organism (O)
Affective attitudes (AAs) and cognitive attitudes (CAs)
Attitude is a core antecedent of intention and behavior in classic behavioral intention theories, such as the theory
of reasoned action and the theory of planned behavior 48,49. Attitudes are typically treated as having distinguish-
able affective and cognitive properties. Affective attitudes reflect feeling-based evaluations, whereas cognitive
attitudes reflect belief-based assessments. Breckler50 provided an influential empirical validation that affect and
cognition can be meaningfully distinguished as components of attitude. Crites et al.51 further advanced this line of
research by developing and validating measures specifically designed to assess the affective and cognitive proper-
ties of attitudes. Recent research on online commerce has explicitly modeled environmental cues as antecedents
that operate through both affective and cognitive states to shape subsequent outcomes52.
In the context of AI algorithmic recommendation, affective attitude captures users’ immediate feeling-based
evaluation of the ad, such as liking, enjoyment, or irritation, whereas cognitive attitude captures more deliberate
belief-based judgments about usefulness, value, relevance, and credibility. Both affective attitude and cognitive
attitude are expected to influence purchase intention and sharing intention, potentially with different relative
strengths across the two outcomes. Purchase intention may be shaped by how appealing the ad feels as well as by
whether the advertised information appears useful and credible; sharing intention may likewise depend on both
emotional resonance and the perceived value or appropriateness of passing the content on to others. Therefore,
we propose the following hypothesis:
H7a: Affective attitude is positively associated with purchase intention.
H7b: Affective attitude is positively associated with sharing intention.
H8a: Cognitive attitude is positively associated with purchase intention.
H8b: Cognitive attitude is positively associated with sharing intention.
Responses (R)
Purchase intention (PI)
Purchase intention refers to consumers’ likelihood or willingness to purchase the advertised product after exposure
to advertising content19. It tends to emerge from internal cognitive and affective reactions52. In social commerce,
platform and content cues are also commonly theorized to influence purchase-related responses via mediators
such as flow, social support, or virtual experience26. Purchase intention is expected to depend strongly on cogni-
tive attitude because purchasing involves judgments of value, fit, and confidence. Prior social commerce research
suggests that consumers’ approach-related behavioral intentions can emerge from internal cognitive and affective
reactions to online commercial stimuli52,53.

Share intention (SI)
Sharing intention, while also influenced by cognition, refers to consumers’ willingness to forward, repost, rec -
ommend, or otherwise distribute the advertisement or product-related information through social channels. It is
especially sensitive to affective attitudes because sharing is often motivated by emotional resonance and social
expression18. If customers develop positive attitudes toward an in-feed advertisement of a product or brand, they
are also more prone to recommend it to others20. This distinction may be especially salient among young adults,
for whom sharing on social media is often tied not only to utility but also to self-presentation, social expression,
and peer connection.
Moderator (M)
AI transparency (AT)
AI transparency refers to the extent to which users understand why an AI-enabled advertising system recom -
mends particular advertisements, what information it uses, and how the recommendation process operates. The
lack of transparency may create uncertainty about whether an advertisement genuinely matches their needs or
results from undisclosed targeting practices. Disclosing transparency statements, algorithmic explanations, and
platform-generated alerts improves consumer trust and perceptions of advertisements 54,55. According to Persua-
sion Knowledge Theory, AI transparency and disclosure preserve the central relevance-based pathway and do not
change the advertisement itself, but they reinforce the transition of relevance into intention. Qadri, Park and Yoon
identified a significant moderating effect of AI algorithm transparency on the relationship between a general atti-
tude toward advertising and consumer sustainable purchase intention47,56. Based on this, the effect of an in-feed
ad on purchase intention should be moderated by whether the AI origin has been disclosed. AI transparency as a
moderator, rather than as an antecedent or mediator, is thus theoretically consistent with how a transparency cue
is expected. Accordingly, we propose:
H9a: AI transparency strengthens the positive relationship between affective attitude and purchase intention.
H9b: AI transparency strengthens the positive relationship between cognitive attitude and purchase intention.
Methodology
Measurement development
This study employed a quantitative approach in which a survey questionnaire was used to measure each con -
struct. The questionnaire was developed on the basis of established scales from the literature, and the detailed
measurement items are presented in Table 2. Survey questions used to gauge entertainment and informative -
ness variables were referred to and adapted from Ducoffe 29. The items measuring personalization are quoted
from De Keyzer et al. 30. Intrusiveness, incentives, affective attitude and cognitive attitude were referred to and
adapted from Edwards et al.35, Kim and Han39 and Varnali et al.41. Credibility is cited from Brackett and Carr57.
Questions measuring purchase intention and share intention were quoted and adapted from Lee et al. 20 and
Shao et al.19. AI transparency variables were referred and adapted from Park and Yoon47. To ensure content rel-
evance and clarity, the questionnaire consisted of three broad sections. In the first section, participants were told
that the survey examined their responses to ads recommended by platform algorithms in their social commerce
feeds. A definition and examples were provided to ensure a shared understanding of the target ad type. Only
those who have seen such ads in the past proceeded to the main survey. In the second section, the respondents’
background information, including sex, age, education level, occupational status, disposable income, and social
commerce platform use, was collected. The third section measured the core latent constructs in the proposed

model, including informativeness, entertainment, personalization, intrusiveness, incentives, credibility, affective
attitude, cognitive attitude, purchase intention, and sharing intention. All construct items were measured using a

Sample selection and data collection
In accordance with the Medium and Long-Term Youth Development Plan (2016–2025), the youth group in China
is defined as those aged 14 to 35 58. To ensure that the research subjects possess full civil capacity and indepen -
dent consumer decision-making ability, the target respondents of this study were young adults aged 18 to 35 who
had experience with in-feed advertising and online shopping in social commerce. The questionnaires were col -
lected through an online survey using a convenience sampling method. The survey was published online through
Wenjuanxing (www.wenjuanxin.com) and disseminated through social media platforms such as WeChat, Xiao -
hongshu, Weibo and QQ. Data were collected from June 2026 to July 2026. Overall, a total of 609 valid responses
were considered for this research. G-power 3.1 was used, with a power level of 0.95 and an effect size (f2) of 0.15,
taking into account 10 predictors. The minimum required sample size was 178. However, the prominent 10-fold
rule suggests that the minimum sample size should be 10 times the maximum number of arrowheads pointing at
a latent variable anywhere in the partial least squares path model 59. The sample of N = 609 also satisfies previ -
ous studies in which published SEM research typically incorporates 200–400 cases to fit models 60. The present
study retained 609 valid questionnaires, which exceeds this threshold and therefore indicates that the final sample
size was sufficient for the purposes of the statistical analysis. Our survey was conducted in accordance with the
Declaration of Helsinki and the relevant laws and regulations of the People’s Republic of China. This study
received ethical approval from the Ethics Committee of China Agricultural University (Approval No. CAUHR-
20260521). All participants provided informed consent for their involvement in the study and for the use of their
contributions in publications.

Discussion
On the basis of the S-O-R model, this study proposed 18 hypotheses, of which 14 were confirmed through empiri-
cal investigation. The subsequent parts provide a more detailed analysis of the findings (H1a-H6b), addressing
RQ1. The support for H1a, H3a and H5a indicates that entertainment, personalization and incentives have a
statistically significant positive effect on affective attitude toward algorithmic in-feed ads. The finding, however,
did not support H2a and H6a, which propose a positive relationship between informativeness, credibility and
affective attitude respectively. The study further found that the informativeness (H2b), along with personalization
(H3b), incentives (H5b)and credibility (H6b), contribute significantly to the cognitive attitude to in-feed ads. The
finding also indicated that entertainment (H1b) had no favorable impact on cognitive attitude.
These findings reinforce the theoretical distinction between affective and cognitive attitude components:
hedonic cues operate mainly on affective responses, whereas utility and credibility related cues dominate the
formation of cognitive attitude. For young adults whose product discovery often occurs during casual browsing
rather than deliberate search, entertainment makes advertising more compatible with platform use routines. The
results of this study are consistent with those of Herzallah and Hanaysha, that entertainment remains a central
driver of perceived emotional value and favorable user response in contemporary digital commerce settings 7,67.
Recent personalized advertising research suggests that relevance is among the main reasons that personalization
improves downstream outcomes such as brand attitude, click intention, and WOM-related responses 32,68. Simi-
larly, incentives produce positive feelings as well as utilitarian beliefs about advertisement-related benefits. This
suggests that concrete benefits such as discounts, rewards, or limited-time deals make young adults feel more
attractive and worthwhile to purchase and share. In feed-based social commerce, this immediate sense of gain

newer consumer protection work on AI-enabled and generative marketing has emphasized that highly individual-

ity12. AI-enabled recommendation may improve relevance, but if users experience the resulting ad as intrusive,
that advantage can be eroded.

mentary predictors of behavioral intentions. This interpretation is especially plausible for young adults because
sharing on social platforms is tied not only to private preference but also to self-presentation, peer visibility, and
relationship maintenance69. The finding suggests that sharing intention in social media in-feed settings is not only
associated with an emotional impulse, but also a utilitarian choice when they rationally perceive the ad content as
valuable and informative. Together, these results indicate that affective and cognitive attitudes function as parallel
mediators, translating distinct in-feed ad features into behavioral outcomes.

Conclusion
This study examined how the perceived features of algorithmic in-feed advertising affect young adults’ purchase
and sharing intentions in an AI-enabled social commerce context. The data analysis indicated that affective atti -
tude is primarily related to entertainment, personalization, intrusiveness and incentives, whereas cognitive atti -
tude is associated with informativeness, credibility, personalization, intrusiveness and incentives. Notably, both
affective and cognitive evaluations positively predict purchase intention and sharing intention, confirming their
parallel roles in translating advertising perceptions into behavioral tendencies. In addition, the study has shown
that AI transparency can significantly strengthen the impact of cognitive attitude on purchase intention. Although
AI systems support targeting and delivery, advertising effectiveness depends not on technical precision alone but
on users’ psychological evaluations. Thus, the movement from browsing to buying and sharing is mediated by
how different advertisement features activate distinct internal judgments. With respect to Chinese young adults,
our structural model underscores the complexity of the dynamic attitudes and behaviors of in-feed advertising in
social commerce. Overall, in AI-enabled feed environments, advertising effectiveness should be understood as a
psychologically mediated process in which technological features shape, but do not replace, consumer evaluation
mechanisms.
Theoretical and practical implications
This study provides a thorough model for examining the psychological mechanisms underpinning behavioral
intentions in social commerce. The findings offer significant theoretical ramifications for understanding the value
of algorithmic in-feed advertising, primarily in terms of consumers’ attitude and behavioral intention. Refining
the SOR framework, the results show an overall view of the antecedents and consequences of in-feed ads, which
helps to analyze the potential driver of consumers’ behavior in AI algorithmic recommendation context. We
divided consumer attitude into affective attitude and cognitive attitude, and defined two kinds of behavioral inten-
tion as purchase intention and share intention. It contributes to the social commerce literature through applying
a balanced cognitive-emotional perspective. AI transparency disclosure needs to be considered when designing
interventions to promote the acceptance of in-feed ads, especially among younger populations. These extend and
broaden the applicability of SOR theory prior in-feed advertising work and prove the significance in understand-
ing young adults’ behavior in social shopping environment where AI is typical. Although AI enhances targeting
and customization, its impact operates indirectly by influencing how consumers perceive advertising features
rather than directly determining behavioral outcomes.
On the basis of the findings of this study, several practical implications can be drawn for marketers, platform
managers and advertisers seeking to promote algorithmic in-feed ads in social commerce in China and elsewhere.
The results of our research suggest that entertainment and incentives for algorithmic in-feed ads foster affective
attitudes that drive young adults’ purchase intention. Advertisers should prioritize these attributes in ad design
rather than relying solely on AI-enabled exposure or targeting precision. Recognizing the impact of informative-
ness and personalization on sharing intention, the platform community and youth-led projects can be tailored
to encourage peer dissemination by ensuring that recommended ads are relevant, useful, and easy to share.

Limitations and future research
This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, although this study incorporates AI trans-
parency as a moderating variable, the empirical model primarily captures users’ perceived attributes of algorith-
mic in-feed advertisements rather than measuring AI-driven constructs directly. We call for empirical studies on
the investigation of the role of AI affordances in refinement of particular constructs and scales for conventional
in-feed advertising. Future research can also further explore AI-related factors, such as the adoption intensity of
AI personalization, user AI self-efficacy, and AI literacy, to enrich relevant theoretical frameworks. Second, the
research employs convenience sampling and restricts the sample to young adults aged 18 to 35, which introduces
selection bias and reduces the generalizability of the research findings to other demographic groups. Subsequent
studies can adopt stratified random sampling and expand the sampling scope by covering more diverse age and
consumer groups to improve the external validity of the proposed model. Third, intention has long been estab -
lished as the strongest proximal predictor of actual behavior in theories of reasoned action and planned behavior.
This study adopts cross-sectional data to measure consumers’ purchase and sharing intentions, rather than their
actual behavioral outcomes. To better clarify the causal psychological mechanisms among the research variables,
future studies can adopt experimental methods or longitudinal research designs for in-depth verification and
analysis. Finally, this study treats purchase intention and sharing intention as two parallel outcome variables and
fails to explore their potential sequential correlations and reciprocal relationships. Therefore, it is worthwhile for
future research to further investigate the interactive relationships between different consumer behavioral inten -
tions and their corresponding actual behaviors.
