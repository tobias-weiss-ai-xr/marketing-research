Abstract: Local food in the Mekong Delta is closely connected with the daily life of the region, and it reveals cultural identity
and stories of the region. Based on Cultural Heritage Theory and Uses and Gratifications Theory, this study examines
tourists’ responses to short -form culinary videos on social media.  Data collecte d from 358 tourists at agricultural and
ecological tourist attractions in the Mekong Delta were analysed using partial least squares structural equation modelling.  The
model includes three cultural features of the videos: culinary symbolism, dish uniquenes s, and community food value. It then
considers how content satisfaction and positive emotional response relate to content sharing and culinary tourism intention.
The findings link these cultural features to content satisfaction and positive emotional respo nse, which are associated with
content sharing and culinary tourism intention . Positive emotional response had a stronger association with content sharing
than content satisfaction did (β = 0.396 versus 0.314). The results demonstrate the significance of e motional appeal and
cultural engagement in marketing culinary tourism using short -form media.  Community media programs should focus on
developing content that creates “content enjoyment” and “emotional engagement” to improve shareability and positive
destination awareness. The analysis builds on the literature in the hospitality and tourism sector by theorizing culinary heritage
video to mean a cultural stimulus that influences engagement and destination intentions. Culturally based video storytelling and
satisfaction-based content design are effective ways for destination actors to increase interest in culinary tourism.

Keywords: culinary, Mekong Delta, agritourism, media engagement, emotional response, short video

INTRODUCTION
Cuisine is a way for people to access and visualize the culture, history and identity of different communities around the
world (Cook & Crang, 1996) . River cuisine is the expression of the interaction between human imagination and nature
(Jackson et al., 2022; Thomas et al., 2026). Culinary activities of the Mekong Delta are in direct connection with the local
ecosystem and depend on raw materials in the area (Le Minh & Anh, 2025) . These are fish, rice, tropical fruits, as well as
folk knowledge and traditional rituals (Tri et al., 2023) . These dishes not only have nutritional value but also tell the story
of the relationship between people and the living environment, thereby reflecting the cultur al identity of the local
community (Suhud et al., 2025). Although cuisine has become a popular topic of tourism and cultural research worldwide,
there is little literature on its use to attract viewers. More specifically, they have not anal yzed modern media sites like short
films and documentaries (Connell, 2012). Though most studies are devoted to material cultural heritage like monuments
and festivals, less attention has been paid to intangible dimensions, including cuisine (Zhang & Zhang, 2025).
The core problem is that there is no realization of how the diversity in taste, form, and uniqueness may be a ddressed (S.
Li et al., 2025). Furthermore, the possible contribution of media in influencing perception and promoting regional foods has
not been explored sufficiently. It remains unclear how culinary practices can be employed to promote cultural sensibility
and understanding (Yong et al., 2022) ; (Hashemi et al., 2025) . The gap in the research is that the use of gastronomy as an
intangible cultural heritage has not been fully exploited (Masmoudi, 2025). It also demonstrates the absence of particular
analysis on how media involvement can enhance agritourism activities in places like the Mekong Delta.
Clarifying this connection is necessary to demonstrate the importance of gastronomic media engagement for both
cultural preservation and tourism development (Pahor Zvanut & Zabukovec Baruca, 2025). The cultural value of cuisine is
a theoretical approach adopted in this study to examine how rural areas ac t as cultural commodities that help in the
preservation of intangible heritage (Hølleland et al., 2017). According to cultural theory, culture should be maintained as a
form of cultural capital, which includes culinary practices. Thus, while being a culinary science study, this research focuses
on cuisine and how it can act as a mediator for intergenerational and geopolitical memory transmission.  Additionally, this
study adopts concepts from the uses and gratifications theory as a framework for assessing the intermediate role of viewer

LITERATURE REVIEW
Theory background
Cultural values have been transmitted widely through digital media, which increases attention to heritage and media
convergence (Balogun & Aruoture, 2024) . Cuisine is also a widely  discussed intangible heritage that provides access to
cultural experiences and historical memory that spreads intensely on social media (Bessiere, 1998 ; XIA et al., 2024) .
Nevertheless, other studies conducted do not often describe how the content related to digital food connects heritage
objectives with emotional and exploration interests of viewers in riverine lands (Timothy & Boyd, 2006 ; Zheng et al.,
2023). Recent bibliometric reviews show that little attention has been given to the dynamics of media in the study of
culinary heritage (Masmoudi, 20 25). Similarly, the digital media review in tourism also provides disjointed frameworks,
particularly in the context of agriculture and gastronomy (Pahor Zvanut & Zabukovec Baruca, 2025) .
To address this gap, this study adopts Cultural Heritage Theory to capture symbolism, uniqueness and value to the
community in culinary traditions (Silverman, 2014 ; Muñoz-Viñas, 2023) . It also applies Uses and Gratifications Theory
to explain the reasons why audiences look for information, entertainment, and recognition  from media content (Katz et
al., 1973) . These perspectives enable the investigation of short movies about cuisines and affiliating them to
participation and agritourism function (Chi et al., 2024 ; Sun et al., 2025) .

Developing hypotheses
Culinary symbolism and experience of the viewer
Symbolism of culinary culture means the meanings and values embedded in local food that reflect cultural identity,
traditions and communal narratives (Cwiertka, 2007 ; Ghaderi & Shekari, 2025) . The communication situation involves
using local dishes to convey identity based on participation in culture and emotional engagement with the audience (Smith,
2015). According to the findings, the study of symbolic stimulation may produce a positive effect on a higher level of
identification, cultural reality, and emotional involvement (Stone et al., 2018). Persons are more likely to appreciate content
that reminds them of their heritage and culture in a creative manner. Th ese characteristics contribute to content satisfaction
by providing culturally relevant information (Wang et al., 2025). They also generate positive emotional responses, such as
pride, nostalgia, or a sense of community connection (Hernández-Garrido et al., 2026).
Furthermore, symbolism i n culinary culture does not only meets information requirements  but also gives rise to
denotative reactions (Jiang et al., 2014) . In this aspect, food evokes emo tions and memories, and viewers perceive the
dishes as familiar and appealing until they learn the traditions behind them (Chatterjee & Chattaraj, 2025).
According to research, whenever the audience feels such emotions, it becomes easier for the content to make them have
a positive emotion towards the content (Xu et al., 2025) . This process improves the user experience and raises
consciousness regarding its cultural  values. On that basis, the study hypothesizes that culinary cultural symbols are
positively associated with content satisfaction and viewers’ emotional responses. This relationship is examined within the
specific geographical context in which the culinary content is presented.
H1.a Symbolism of culinary culture has a positive relationship with content satisfaction.
H1.b Symbolism of culinary culture has a positive relationship with positive emotional response.

The effect of dish distinctiveness on the experience of viewers
The uniqueness of dishes refers to distinctive features that differentiate one dish from another (Wang & Guo, 2026). In
other words, it reflects culinary creations shaped by cultural context and the creative ability of cooks (Di Giovine &
Brulotte, 2018) . In riverscape regions, both local ingredients and preparation techniques are used to capture viewers’

attention (Yousaf, 2022). The recent studies indicate that unusual or innovatively served dishes are likely to produce more
satisfaction among the audience. The experimental results show that audiences perce ive these culinary expressions as new
experiences. This finding helps explain the mechanism behind this phenomenon (Ahmed et al., 2024) . In particular, when

Moreover, the communication of dish uniqueness may be associated with positive emotional responses , which can be

the identity and richness of the culture depicted (Steriopoulos et al., 2024).
The analysis of relevant publications shows that dish uniqueness is positively associated with content satisfaction. This
satisfaction helps stimulate positive emotional responses among viewers. Based on this relationship, it can be assumed that
emphasizing the uniqueness of cuisine through the media will help attract the attention of the audience.
H2.a Uniqueness of dishes has a positive relationship with content satisfaction.
H2.b Uniqueness of dishes has a positive relationship with positive emotional response.

The importance of community food values in increasing viewer engagement
The role of food within a community refl ects its social functions associated with culinary practices (Arcadu &
Migliorini, 2024). It also shapes how peopl e define their identity in relation to food and culture (Pratt, 2008). This value is
frequently expressed through rituals and customs manifested in local culinary traditions (Fattah et al., 2025) . In
communities that live beside rivers, food is often a representation of communal rituals and traditions that are meaningful to
viewers (Stone et al., 2018). According to prior studies, the social aspect enhances viewers’ enjoyment of the content.
Viewers gain satisfaction from discovering the role of foo d in fostering community unity and preserving cultural traditions
(Cheraghi et al., 2025) . As a result, viewers may have a more immersive and warm viewing experience through
representations of the food as a link to a given culture (de la Barre & Brouder, 2013; Zhao et al., 2026).
Furthermore, an important reason for a positive reception by the target audience is the community value of food. By
displaying the power of food to trigger shared memories, cultural pride and a sense of belonging, media content helps to
build strong emotional connections (Ye, 2026; Grosglik & Lerner, 2021) . These relationships enhance content satisfaction
and also contribute to the development of a stronger emotional bond (Gao et al., 2026) . Research on emotionally resonant
storytelling suggests that content that provokes an immersive experience and emotional engagement promotes greater
appreciation and connection of the viewer (Ahmed et al., 2024) . This emotional involvement not only improves their
viewing experience, but it also improves the degree to which they are attached to the values of the community depicted.
Based on previous analyses of the community role of food, the study develops the following hypothesi s: community
food values are positively associated with viewer satisfaction and positive emotional responses.
H3.a Community value of food has a positive relationship with content satisfaction.
H3.b Community value of food has a positive relationship with positive emotional response.

Satisfaction with content and its association with exploration and sharing
Content satisfaction refers to the extent to which the audience thinks about media content as  providing information,
feelings, or entertainment (Chi et al., 2024; Tahat, 2025). When content about river food clearly express es cultural aspects
and different types of cuisine, it positively impacts viewers’ emotional state and enjoyment (Xia et al., 2023). Those people
who become interested in the experiences depicted in content and the customs of different cultures may develop a stronger
interest in culinary culture (Mainolfi et al., 2022 ; Phrammanachot et al., 2026) . Studies have shown that satisfied viewers
are more likely to try to find more information or actively interact with the information provided. Through this interaction,
they are able to be more connected to the cultural setting of the riverine regions (Wang, 2024).
More than exploration, content satisfaction also affects the audience’s likelihood of sharing what they watch (Ganobčík et
al., 2026; Jung & Hwang, 2023). The audience which consumes content related to food tends to share it because of the desire
to contribute to their friends’ improved perception of culture and similar interests (Mukherjee, 2024). Recent studies indicate
that emotional satisfaction increases viewers’ intention to share content through social media and word of mouth (Kusuma et
al., 2026). This behavior reflects their commitment to cultural values (Martínez García de Leaniz et al., 2025). Acts of sharing,
even when personal and spontaneous, go toward disseminating cultural messages to more people (Farzin & Makvandi, 2025).
Therefore, content satisfaction increases viewers’ interest in exploring culinary culture. It also encourages information
sharing, which supports the spread of cultural values within the community. On that basis, this study proposes two
hypotheses regarding the role of satisfaction in forming the intention to explore and share content.
H4.a Content satisfaction has a positive relationship with content sharing behavior
H4.b Content satisfaction has a positive relationship with culinary tourism intention

Positive emotional response is a system that comes with positive psychological interventions, such as joy and
happiness. It is defined as the emotional boost that comes with the audience relating with cultural media prod uctions. The
following might help a person’s mental health, their ability to cope with life stress, as well as individual development

MATERIALS AND METHODS
Questionnaire development
The assessment instruments used in this study are standardized scales. The theoretical framework is grounded in
Cultural Heritage Theory (Timothy & Boyd, 2006; Bessiere, 1998) and Uses and Gratifications Theory (Katz et al., 1973).
To increase the reliability and validity of these constructs, the operationalization of these constructs has been based on th e
items already investigated and validated in the literature (Kim et al., 2012).
The scale uses a 5 -point Likert scale ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree) to measure the strength of
viewers’ impressions and attitudinal responses (Likert, 2017). According to the research objectives, each measurement item is
developed to represent specific theoretical constructs derived from the theoretical background (Kim et al., 2010).
All scale items were written in English and translated into Vietnamese language by a university lecturer specialized in
English language education. A back -translation procedure was then performed to ensure semantic and conceptual
equivalence (Chen & Boore, 2010) . Minor revisions focusing on local language words and meanings were made based on
comparison between the original and back-translated versions. A pilot study was performed among 20 lecturers specializing
in tourism and 10 final-year undergraduate students in the same discipline as indicated in Table 1. The majority of feedback
concerned the wording and consistency of subject references (Byon & Zhang, 2010).

Data collection and sample size
Samples for this research were collected at agrotourism and farm -to-table ecotourism sites in the Mekong Delta.
Tourists were briefly introduced to the study through a 10 -15 minutes face -to-face session. In this interaction, they were
asked to watch a 3 -5 minutes video on the riverine cuisine and the cultural background.
Participants who agreed received a QR code for the video and were then asked to complete an online questionnaire
that was designed to assess their media experience. All of the responses gi ven by the survey were kept securely in
Google Drive (Appendix A) in encrypted folders accessible only by the research team, to ensure anonymity and prevent
unauthorized access (Alamoudi et al., 2024) . At the survey sites, the research team was supported by 10 trained final
year students who helped approach tourists, explain the study and guide participation. In a study design that is conscious
of minimizing interviewer bias, all assistants had a standardized script to use when approaching participants. Tourists
were clearly info rmed that no personal data would be collected and could withdraw at any time. The questionnaire was
designed with only mandatory questions to ensure data completeness (Newcomer & Triplett, 2015) .
The purpose of the study and the method applied, the cross -sectional survey studies were used as the recommended
way to estimate sample size (Wagner & Grimm, 2023) . The minimum sample size was determined to be 280 based on
the 10 -fold rule, with 7 concepts; each concept consists of 4 observed variables (Hair et al., 2017) . In fact, the
convenience and feasibility of sampling at tourist sites offer advantages in terms of speed and accessibility. However,
limitations in representativeness still pose a challenge for ensuring reliable results (Daniel Staetsky, 201 9).
However, the intended sample size was 500 because of the potential non -responses, and 358 valid responses were
used in the analysis. Moreover, in this last sample, 34 respondents were recruited through snowball sampling to expand
on the idea of rec ruitment which is narrowly based on convenience sampling.
The independent t -test was conducted to compare the main group and the subgroup. The results showed no
statistically significant difference between the two groups based on the measured variables (p  > 0.05). Thus, the findings
indicate the compatibility of groups and enhance the validity of the primary data (Walters, 2021) .
During the collection process, incl uding before participation, tourists were informed that no personal data would be
collected, and they were encouraged to discontinue if privacy concerns arose. Data collection began in the month of
May and was finished on the 24th of December 2024, when th e link for the survey was deactivated.
Finally, the research team manually reviewed the survey responses in order to discard low -quality responses, such as
responses where the same answer was chosen for 20 questions in a row, to ensure the integrity of the dataset (Wang, 2024).
To reduce the bias i ntroduced by generic methods, set up Google Forms to be anonymous in order to avoid duplicate and
incomplete responses. Concurrently, two diagnostic checks were to be performed in order to evaluate the possibility of
common method bias. In particular, the single-factor test by Harman showed that no single factor could explain most of the
variance (under 50 percent) and values of VIF in the PLS-SEM model had to be less than 5 (Hair Jr. et al., 2017).

Data analysis
The first, according to the data processin g guidelines in social research. Nominal level analysis applies demographic
frequency distributions to provide information about the study and sample attributes. Furthermore, frequency check is a
standard initial procedure of social research to determine d emographics and potential outliers.
This issue has set the questions in the survey form as mandatory, helping to limit the case of missing data right from
the beginning. In addition, the data was not standardized because the Likert scale is ordinal, and P LS-SEM does not
require standardization for such non -metric data (Sarstedt et al., 2022).
Second, all the analysis is done using software SmartPLS. Internal consistency is evaluated with Cronbach’s Alpha
while outer loading and VIF are used to evaluate the  constructs for scale consistency and multicollinearity respectively
(Bonett & Wright, 2015) . In the measurement model analysis phase, SmartPLS software was used to test the overall
reliability through Cronbach's Alpha coefficient. Next, the consistency between measured variables and multicollinearity
were evaluated by using the outer loading factor and VIF index; all values were lower than 5, the popular recommended
threshold (Hair et al., 2019) .
Thirdly, construct validity was assessed using confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) employs composite reliability and
average variance extracted (Fornell & Larcker, 1981) . All latent constructs were tested in terms of convergent validity
and composite reliability by applying the CR and AVE indices.
Fourth, the rela tionships among variables were tested using partial least squares structural equation modeling (PLS -
SEM) to test both direct and indirect relationships among cognitive measures. This approach is suitable for exploratory
approaches, complex models, medium s ample sizes, and for non -normally distributed data. Utilizing its analytical
technique, PLS -SEM is unique among CB -SEM methods for the following: It requires fewer statistical assumptions, and
focuses on prediction and explanation rather than validation of  a theoretical model (Hair. et al., 2017 ; Hair et al., 201 9).

DISCUSSION
Cuisine has become an increasingly important tool of cultural communication in the form of short -form video
platforms, where response from viewers is more relevant to  tourism research. According to previous researches, culinary
content may help in preservation of intangible heritage and enable development of tourism (Chi et al., 2024) ; (Connell,
2012). This particular point of view is consistent with the claim that gastronomy is a cul tural resource associated with
authenticity-seeking behavior (Bessiere, 1998) and reinforces the position of heritage tourism in frameworks of cultural
tourism (Timothy & Boyd, 2006) . Consistent with this view, the present study identifies symbolism, uniqueness, and
community as core cultural values associated with cuisine (Di Giovine & Brulotte, 2018).
Beyond the cultural meaning, the findings show that regional cuisine videos are perceived as entertaining, and they
motivate the viewers to look for information or to share something. Emotional reactions seem to have a stronger impact on
sharing behavior compared to only being satisfied (Grosglik & Lerner, 2021) . This pattern supports the resonance -based
storytelling, which focuses on emotional immersion as a factor of audience connection (Vorderer & Halfmann, 2019) .
Accordingly, culinary heritage communication should integrate culturally based storytelling in order to generate emotional
communication (Moreira et al., 2024) . Further analysis shows significant relationships between content characteristics,
emotional reactions and behavioral int entions. Younger audiences tend to share content more, as expected in previous
studies. However, these trends should be interpreted cautiously since there were no group differences for tests of
significance (Yousaf, 2022; Chi et al., 2024) . Content relevance and emotional response are closely related to symbolic
meaning and dish uniqueness (Stone et al., 2018). In addition, content satisfaction and emotional reactions partially mediate
the effects of cultural values on exploration and sharing intentions (Mainolfi et al., 2022).
Compared with previous research, the results show similarities and differences in the way culinary media affect
audiences. While informational and emotional framing  affects sharing and visiting intentions (Jung & Hwang, 2023) , the
present results confirm that emotional responses are stronger predictors than satisfaction (Y. Zheng et al., 2024). In contrast
to influencer centered approaches, the focus of this study is the cultural symbolism and community values ingrained in
culinary stories (Di Giovine & Brulotte, 2018). Recent research is also showing an interest, identity , and cultural meaning

to drive audience engagement (Chen et al., 2025) . Whereas previous works have focused on novelty, spatial context, and

community values in the determination  of emotional involvement and behavioural intentions. Emotional reactions and
content satisfaction influence intentions to explore culinary tourism and share media content. Demographic imbalances and

conveying the culinary heritage and triggering interest in a digital tourism context.

CONCLUSIONS AND IMPLICATIONS
The study shows how cultural features of Mekong Delta cuisine in short -form videos relate to viewers’ satisfaction,
emotions, content sharing, and culinary tourism intention. The characteristics of the surveyed tourists should be considered
when applying these findings to other audiences.  From a cultural heritage perspective, cul inary symbols and distinctive
dishes help viewers connect with the region’s food culture. Rituals and historical accounts can increase content satisfaction
and appreciation of cultural authenticity (Chi et al., 2024) . The uniqueness of local dishes may also attract attention and
encourage viewers to learn more about them (J. Wang et al., 2025) . Through these features, culinary videos can help
present regional identity to wider audiences (Stone et al., 2018).
Additionally, uses and gratifications theory explains how the media content meets the cognitive and emotional needs of
the viewers. Videos that offer information and enjoyment can encourage audience engagement, especially when they evoke
positive feelings (Y. Zhang et al., 2025) . Emotional response is therefore relevant to how viewers engage with culinary
heritage content  (Mainolfi et al., 2022; Xu et al., 2025) . These responses also help explain viewers’ interest in sharing
culinary videos and exploring local food during travel. Positive emotions may encourage people to share such content on
social media, allowing stories of local cuisine to reach others (Wang, 2024).
As the results of the study show, media creators and cultural stakeholders can target viewers and promote content
sharing and cultural exploration by using symbolic and unique aspects of riverine culinary cultures.  In practice, they can
include cultural ceremonies and local specialties in stories that address viewers’ interests and evoke positive emotions (Wu
et al., 2026; Moreira et al., 2024).Beyond tourism promotion, sharing these stories may help more people become familiar
with local culinary heritage (Wang, 2024). Community storytelling offers local stakeholders a way to present food
traditions and strengthen their connection with the destination (Butler & Carr, 2024).
These conclusions should be read in light of the study’s sample, which included tourists already engaged with culinary
media. Future studies could include more diverse viewers and use experimental designs to ex amine how emotional
responses relate to sharing and culinary tourism intention. Live streaming and virtual reality could also be studied to
understand responses to other media formats (Tang & Zhang, 2024).
