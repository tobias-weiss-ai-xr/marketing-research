Abstract: As a core theoretical and practical framework in the field of contemporary marketing communication, Integrated
Marketing Communication (IMC) emphasizes the strategic integration of various communication tools-such as advertising,
public relations, sales promotion, direct marketing, digital marketing, content marketing, and social media marketing-to achieve
consistent communication with consumers and build long-term relationships. University IMC course bear the responsibility of
cultivating applied professionals who possess both systematic planning capabilities and the ability to execute strategies in real-
world scenarios. However, current teaching practices often suffer from a disconnect between theory and practice, fragmented
case studies, and insufficient training across the entire campaign lifecycle, leaving students unable to independently develop
complete marketing communication plans. The "comprehensive campaign planning practice method" utilizes a complete IMC
campaign plan as the instructional vehicle, guiding students through a closed-loop training process-from problem definition to
solution delivery-that encompasses brief interpretation, market research and insight generation, strategy formulation, core
concept distillation, creative execution, media mix and scheduling, budget allocation, and performance evaluation. This method
integrates constructivist learning theory, experiential learning theory, and project-based learning concepts, emphasizing authentic
contexts, teamwork, and iterative processes. This paper systematically elaborates on the essence, characteristics, and theoretical
foundations of the comprehensive campaign planning practice method; a nalyzes the current state and challenges of IMC course
instruction; and details the design and implementation pathway for applying this method within the curriculum. The method
effectively enhances students' systems thinking, problem-solving skills, teamwork abilities, and professional competence,
serving as a vital pathway for transforming IMC Course from mere "knowledge transmission" to "competence cultivation."
Keywords: Comprehensive Campaign Planning Practice Method, Integrated Marketing Communications (IMC), Practical
Competence Cultivation.

1. Introduction
With the rapid development of digital technology and
profound changes in the media landscape, consumer
touchpoints have become increasingly fragmented,
fundamentally altering the way brands communicate with
consumers. Traditional marketing communications relying on
single media channels or isolated tactics struggle to
effectively reach target audiences or establish enduring
relationships. It is against this backdrop that the theory of
Integrated Marketing Communication (IMC) has emerged
and continued to evolve. The theory of Integrated Marketing
Communication (IMC), proposed by Professor Don Schultz
in the 1990s, advocates for the unified integration of all
marketing-related communication activities [1]. It emphasizes
systematic planning centered on a consistent brand message
and consumer touchpoints to maximize communication
effectiveness and build long-term brand equity. This theory
rapidly became a guiding framework for the global
advertising, public relations, and marketing sectors,
subsequently becoming a core component of curricula in
business, journalism, communication, and advertising
programs at universities. In the context of Chinese higher
education, IMC Course are typically offered to students
majoring in advertising, marketing, internet and new media,
and public relations; these Course are tasked with both
imparting theoretical knowledge and cultivating practical
skills. Ideally, through this coursework, students should not
only master the fundamental concepts, evolution, core
elements, and workflows of IMC but also acquire the ability
to independently execute a complete planning process-
ranging from market insights to the implementation of a
campaign. However, actual teaching practice often suffers
from significant issues, such as an overemphasis on theory at
the expense of practice, the use of fragmented case studies,
and a lack of training that covers the entire operational
workflow. Many students can recite the definition of IMC and
the "5W2H" framework yet struggle to independently draft a
comprehensive, logically consistent, and actionable campaign
proposal. Classroom discussions often focus on retrospective
reviews rather than proactive strategic design or creative
execution training, while assignments frequently require only
the completion of isolated modules (such as a single
advertisement concept or media plan), making it difficult for
students to grasp the true essence of "integration."
The "comprehensive campaign planning" practice method
is a systematic pedagogical approach designed to address
these specific challenges. Centered on the "complete
marketing communication campaign" as the vehicle for
instruction and assessment, it requires students to work in
teams to tackle real-world or highly realistic corporate briefs.
They must execute the entire planning process-from problem
definition to effectiveness evaluation-according to
professional standards, ultimately submitting an actionable

proposal and presenting it in a formal defense. This method
emphasizes completeness, authenticity, teamwork, and a
closed-loop process; by simulating the project workflows of
professional advertising agencies or brand teams, it enables
students to internalize knowledge and transfer skills through
"learning by doing." The significance of this study lies in two
main areas: first, theoretically, it enriches the teaching
methodology for Integrated Marketing Communication (IMC)
Course by organically integrating theories of constructivism,
experiential learning, and project-based learning with the
practice of developing comprehensive IMC campaigns;
second, practically, it provides actionable pathways and
design frameworks for reforming IMC curricula in higher
education institutions.
2. Definition, Characteristics, and
Theoretical Basis of the
Comprehensive Campaign Planning
Practice Method
2.1. Defining the Comprehensive Campaign
Planning Practice Method
The comprehensive campaign planning practice method
uses a complete IMC campaign plan as the core instructional
vehicle. Students work in project teams to undertake real or
highly realistic corporate briefs. Adhering to professional
industry standards, they systematically complete the entire
process-ranging from brief interpretation, market research
and multi-dimensional insight generation, communication
strategy formulation, core concept distillation, and creative
execution development to media strategy and touchpoint
management, scheduling and budget allocation, effectiveness
evaluation design, and final proposal integration and
presentation. Throughout this process, they engage in
multiple rounds of iterative optimization under the guidance
of faculty members and corporate mentors.
Its core focus is not merely on "writing a proposal," but on
using the comprehensive campaign-a complete work product-
to compel students to experience the full logical chain of
"defining the problem and solving the problem." The standard
industry structure for such campaigns typically includes
modules such as brief interpretation (background, objectives,
tasks), research and analysis (five-dimensional insights
covering the environment, industry, company, competitors,
and consumers), communication strategy, core concept,
creative execution, media usage and scheduling, budget
allocation, and effectiveness evaluation. Students must ensure
systematic integration around a unified core message and
brand touchpoints, rather than simply stacking various
communication tactics together[2].
2.2. Key Characteristics of the Comprehensive
Campaign Planning Practice Method
2.2.1. Completeness and Authenticity
The method mandates coverage of the entire process, from
problem definition to effectiveness evaluation, thereby
avoiding the fragmented tendencies often found in traditional
teaching-such as focusing on creativity without strategy or
analysis without implementation. Priority is given to using
real corporate briefs or actual requirements from trending
brands; where constraints exist, highly realistic, localized
case studies are employed to ensure the problem scenarios
closely mirror actual industry practices.
2.2.2. Team-based Approach and Closed-loop Process
Working in groups of four to eight, students simulate the
operations of a professional agency project team. They assign
specific roles-such as strategy, creative, media, execution, and
client liaison-to cultivate collaboration and communication
skills. The approach emphasizes a complete closed-loop
process, extending from goal setting to evaluation and
feedback; students are required not only to propose plans but
also to design measurable performance indicators and
optimization mechanisms.
2.2.3. Iteration and Diverse Evaluation
Through interim presentations, multiple rounds of revision,
and formal final defenses, the program reinforces process-
oriented guidance and reflective optimization, mirroring the
"proposal–feedback–iteration" workflow of real-world
projects. Evaluation criteria go beyond the quality of the final
proposal to encompass process performance, team
contribution, professionalism during the defense, and
corporate feedback, thereby establishing an assessment
system that integrates both process and outcome.
2.3. Theoretical Foundations
The proposal and application of the "comprehensive
campaign planning" practical method rest on solid theoretical
foundations in pedagogy and marketing communication.
First, constructivist learning theory posits that knowledge
is not passively received but actively constructed by learners
through exploration and social interaction within authentic
contexts. Comprehensive campaign planning provides highly
realistic problem scenarios, compelling students to integrate
fragmented IMC concepts-such as touchpoint management,
brand consistency, and consumer insights-into actionable
solutions, thereby facilitating deep knowledge construction[3].
Second, experiential learning theory describes learning as
a cyclical process comprising "concrete experience, reflective
observation, abstract conceptualization, and active
experimentation." As students draft plans, receive feedback,
and iterate on their work, they navigate a bidirectional
transformation-moving from practice to theory and back to
practice-to achieve genuine deep learning.
Third, the pedagogical concepts of project-based learning
and work-process-oriented instruction advocate for
restructuring course content around complete work processes.
Comprehensive campaign planning is modeled on the actual
workflow professional agencies use to deliver client
campaigns; by reorganizing course material to align with real-
world tasks, it fulfills the requirement for cultivating applied
talent through "learning by doing" and "doing while
learning." Finally, viewed through the lens of IMC theory
itself, the comprehensive campaign planning approach aligns
perfectly with the "outside-in" communication planning logic
proposed by Schultz. It compels students to prioritize
consumer insights and problem definition before formulating
strategies and creative concepts; this avoids the traditional
"inside-out," product-centric mindset and helps students truly
grasp the essence of IMC.
3. Current Status and Issues in IMC
Course Instruction
There remains a significant disconnect between theory and
practice in university-level IMC Course regarding teaching
objectives, content organization, instructional methods, and
assessment criteria.

45
Regarding teaching objectives, while most syllabi
emphasize "mastering basic IMC theories and methods" and
"developing preliminary planning skills," actual instruction
often treats "theoretical mastery" as the primary measurable
goal. Consequently, there is a lack of clear process-based
support and evaluation standards for the higher-level
competency of independently executing a complete,
comprehensive campaign.
Regarding content organization, textbooks and lectures are
typically structured around theoretical modules (e.g., IMC
concepts and evolution, consumer behavior, touchpoint
management, advertising strategy, media strategy, and
effectiveness evaluation) rather than being reconstructed
around a "complete workflow." After completing these
modules, students often struggle to organically integrate them
into a logically coherent, comprehensive campaign plan [4].
Regarding instructional methods, while case-based
learning is widely adopted, it often remains at the level of
"post-hoc review"-analyzing successful classic cases-leaving
students with few opportunities for proactive strategic design
and the practical implementation of creative ideas. Although
some Course include group projects, these often require only
the completion of specific components (such as writing ad
copy or drafting a media plan) rather than offering end-to-end
process training[5].
Regarding assessment, final exams or theoretical
assignments still carry significant weight. Evaluation criteria
for campaign proposals often prioritize formatting standards
and theoretical citations, while underemphasizing the rigor of
strategic logic, the depth of insights, the innovativeness of
concepts, and the feasibility of implementation. Furthermore,
opportunities for real-world corporate projects are limited,
making it difficult for students to receive authentic feedback
from the industry. The direct consequence of these issues is
that, upon graduation, students entering advertising agencies,
brand marketing departments, or new media operations roles
require a considerable amount of time to become competent
in drafting and pitching comprehensive proposals; some
students even exhibit a disconnect in capabilities—being
adept at taking exams yet unable to develop actual proposals.
The "comprehensive campaign planning" practical approach
is a teaching method specifically designed to systematically
address these issues.
4. Design and Implementation of the
"Comprehensive Campaign
Planning" Practical Method in
Integrated Marketing
Communication Course
4.1. Principles of Instructional Design
4.1.1. Principles of Real-World Orientation and Process
Completeness
Prioritize the use of actual corporate briefs or needs from
trending brands; if resources are limited, select localized,
simulated cases where students possess some prior
knowledge, data is accessible, and the scope of the problem is
clearly defined. Mandate coverage of all core campaign
modules and require students to complete the entire
workflow-from the initial brief to final evaluation-thereby
preventing tendencies to focus solely on either the creative
aspect or the analytical aspect.
4.1.2. Principles of Team Collaboration and Iterative
Feedback
Organize students into groups of 4–8 to simulate
professional agency roles, fostering skills in collaboration,
communication, and project management. Incorporate
interim presentations and multiple rounds of revisions, with
timely intervention from instructors and corporate mentors to
prevent students from investing excessive time in the wrong
direction.
4.1.3. Principles of Multidimensional Evaluation and
Theory-Practice Integration
Conduct comprehensive evaluations based on proposal
quality, presentation performance, team contribution, attitude
throughout the process, and corporate feedback, emphasizing
competency-based assessment. Upon completion of the
campaign, guide students to distill methodologies from their
work, facilitating the transition from practice to theoretical
understanding.
4.2. Specific Implementation Process
4.2.1. Preparation and Launch Phase
Instructors systematically introduce the campaign structure,
evaluation criteria, and industry-standard logic (e.g., problem
definition vs. solution, or the 5W2H framework) while
showcasing exemplary campaign cases. Subsequently, the
project brief is issued (corporate representatives may be
invited to present requirements in person or online); students
form teams-either independently or via instructor assignment-
and allocate roles (e.g., Strategy Lead, Creative Lead, Media
Lead, Execution & Coordination Lead). This phase focuses
on establishing a conceptual understanding of "why we
conduct comprehensive campaigns" and "what such a
campaign looks like."
4.2.2. Research and Insight Phase
Groups conduct desk research and basic consumer research
(surveys, interviews, observations, or secondary data analysis)
based on the brief, developing insights across five dimensions:
the environment, the industry, the company, competitors, and
target consumers. Students are required to identify core issues
and opportunities, produce an "insight report," and discuss
within their groups whether the problem has been correctly
defined. Instructors conduct progress checks-either in class or
via online platforms-to ensure research goes beyond a
superficial level.
4.2.3. Strategy and Concept Phase
Building on the insights gained, groups formulate
communication objectives and overarching strategies, distill
core concepts, and complete a mid-term presentation. This
presentation serves as a critical milestone where instructors
and corporate mentors focus their review on: the accuracy of
problem definition, the alignment of strategy with the insight
logic, and the ability of the core concept to provide a unifying
theme and drive effective communication. A first round of
iteration is conducted based on the feedback received.
4.2.4. Creative Execution and Media Implementation
Phase
Groups develop creative executions (e.g., advertising,
content, events, PR, digital marketing, social media) centered
on the core concept, while also formulating media mix
strategies, touchpoint management plans, schedules, and
budget allocations. This phase emphasizes "integration"-all
creative and media activities must serve a unified core
message and align with brand touchpoints. Instructors may

46
organize specialized workshops to provide targeted guidance
on creative ideation and media strategy.
4.2.5. Proposal Integration and Presentation Phase
Groups compile a comprehensive proposal document
(typically covering the cover page, table of contents, brief
analysis, insights, strategy, concept, creative, media, schedule,
budget, evaluation, etc.) and deliver a formal presentation.
Corporate representatives or industry experts may be invited
to evaluate the presentations, simulating a real-world pitch
scenario. Final revisions are made based on the feedback
received, and the final version is submitted.
4.2.6. Reflection and Theoretical Synthesis Phase
Students submit individual and group review reports
summarizing their experiences, shortcomings, and key
takeaways. Instructors organize a class-wide summary
session, guiding students to abstract their practical
experiences into a methodology, thereby elevating their
understanding from merely "knowing how to create a
proposal" to "comprehending the logic behind a
comprehensive campaign."
4.3. Teaching Organization and Support
The instructor's role shifts from a traditional knowledge
transmitter to that of a project mentor and process facilitator,
requiring a significant investment of time in providing
ongoing guidance and feedback. The course can be integrated
with online platforms (such as Xuexitong and Rain Classroom)
to facilitate resource sharing, progress tracking, and formative
assessment. Institutions with the necessary resources should
actively incorporate lectures by industry experts, workshops,
or real-world projects to enhance the professional quality and
industry relevance of the plans. Additionally, establishing a
repository of comprehensive campaign cases created by
outstanding students can provide localized resources for
future instruction.
4.4. Design of the Assessment Mechanism
Assessment should combine formative and summative
approaches: process performance (quality of research, interim
presentations, and teamwork) accounts for 30%– 40%; the
quality of the final comprehensive campaign plan (depth of
insight, strategic logic, conceptual innovation, media
feasibility, budget reasonableness, and professional
documentation) accounts for 40%– 50%; and presentation
performance and corporate feedback account for 10%–20%.
Grading criteria should be announced in advance to ensure
transparency and operational clarity.

5. Conclusion
Centered on comprehensive, authentic, and closed-loop
training in marketing communication campaigns, the
"comprehensive campaign planning" practical approach
effectively addresses key issues in traditional IMC Course,
such as the disconnect between theory and practice,
fragmented case studies, and insufficient end-to-end process
training. It not only equips students with the ability to manage
the entire process-from insight generation to final execution-
but also systematically cultivates systems thinking, problem-
solving skills, teamwork, and professional competence. By
integrating concepts from constructivism, experiential
learning, and project-based learning, this method aligns
closely with the "outside-in" logic inherent in IMC theory,
serving as an effective pathway for transforming IMC Course
from knowledge transmission to competency development.
Looking ahead, as new scenarios-such as digital marketing
tools, AI-assisted planning, the metaverse, and immersive
communication-continue to emerge, the comprehensive
campaign planning method itself must undergo continuous
iteration. Further integration of data-driven decision-making,
AI tool application, and cross-media storytelling training can
bring instruction closer to the latest industry practices.
Meanwhile, strengthening deep industry-academia
collaboration, building localized case repositories, and
developing standardized assessment tools will facilitate the
method's wider adoption across universities, thereby
contributing significantly to the cultivation of versatile
professionals capable of thriving in the marketing
communication landscape of the new era.
