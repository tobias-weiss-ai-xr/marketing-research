Abstract

Digitalization has transformed smartphones from communication devices
into markers of identity, lifestyle, and social belonging. This study examines
whether Fear of Missing Out (FOMO), Shopping Lifestyle, and Social Media
Marketing influence iPhone purchase decisions among Generation Z in
Kakas Barat District, North Sulawesi. Using a quantitative cau sal-associative
design, data were collected through a five-point Likert questionnaire from 97
Generation Z respondents selected through purposive sampling and
analyzed using multiple linear regression with SPSS 25. The findings show
that FOMO, Shopping Lif estyle, and Social Media Marketing jointly have a
positive and significant effect on iPhone purchase decisions (F = 20.284; p <
0.001). Individually, FOMO (B = 0.407; β = 0.399; t = 4.550; p < 0.001),
Shopping Lifestyle (B = 0.280; β = 0.285; t = 3.332; p = 0.001), and Social Media
Marketing (B = 0.186; β = 0.186; t = 2.242; p = 0.027) each show a positive and
significant association with purchase decisions. The model explains 39.6% of
the variance in purchase decisions (R² = 0.396). The results indicate th at
iPhone purchase decisions in this setting are shaped by interconnected
psychological, lifestyle, and digital -marketing factors, with FOMO showing
the largest standardized coefficient.

INTRODUCTION
Digitalization has changed the meaning of smartphones in contemporary
consumer culture. Beyond communication and technical functionality, smartphones
increasingly operate as symbols of identity, lifestyle, social belonging, and
participation in digital communities. The t hesis underlying this article reports that
iPhone's global market share increased from 18.8% in 2022 to 23.5% in the first
quarter of 2025, while the estimated number of iPhone users in Indonesia increased
from 5.2 million in 2021 to 10.4 million in 2025 ( IDC, 2025; Statcounter GlobalStats,
2025). This development provides a relevant context for examining why young
consumers choose a premium smartphone even when the decision cannot be
explained solely by functional considerations.

Generation Z is particular ly relevant because its consumption behavior is
closely connected to digital media, social validation, and rapidly changing trends. In
the thesis, Generation Z is defined as individuals born between 1997 and 2014,
corresponding to approximately 14–29 years old in the study context. Their purchase
decisions may be shaped by the desire to remain socially connected, the fear of being
left behind by current trends, shopping -oriented lifestyles, and continuous exposure
to social media content. The literature rev iewed in the thesis links FOMO to
purchase-related behavior, while studies on shopping lifestyle and social media
marketing indicate that lifestyle orientation and digital promotional exposure can
also contribute to consumer decisions (Ali et al., 2023; Ku rniawan & Indriana, 2023;
Ramadhani et al., 2023; Susanto & Wijaya, 2025).
The study is framed by the Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB). In the thesis,
FOMO is conceptually associated with subjective social pressure, Shopping Lifestyle
with attitudes toward  consumption and lifestyle expression, and Social Media
Marketing with information and access that can support consumers in translating
purchase intentions into actual decisions. From this perspective, purchase decisions
are not isolated acts but are influ enced by psychological, social, and environmental
stimuli.
The research context is Kakas Barat District, Minahasa Regency, North
Sulawesi, an area described in the thesis as semi -rural but increasingly exposed to
digital technology and social media. The lo cal social environment is relatively close -
knit, making social visibility and peer influence potentially important in
consumption decisions. Previous studies have examined FOMO, lifestyle, social
media marketing, and smartphone or premium -product purchases  in different
settings. However, the thesis identifies a contextual gap because the three variables
had not been jointly examined in relation to iPhone purchase decisions among
Generation Z specifically in Kakas Barat.
Accordingly, this article addresses f our research questions: (1) whether FOMO,
Shopping Lifestyle, and Social Media Marketing jointly affect iPhone purchase
decisions; (2) whether FOMO affects iPhone purchase decisions; (3) whether
Shopping Lifestyle affects iPhone purchase decisions; and (4)  whether Social Media
Marketing affects iPhone purchase decisions among Generation Z in Kakas Barat.
The objective is to present the empirical results of the thesis in a concise journal -
article format and interpret the findings in relation to the theoretic al and empirical
literature reviewed in the study.

METHODS
This study employed a quantitative approach with a causal -associative design.
The research was conducted in Kakas Barat District, Minahasa Regency, North
Sulawesi, during approximately three months  from June to August 2026. The
population consisted of Generation Z residents of Kakas Barat who were born
between 1997 and 2014, aged approximately 14 –29 years, and who had purchased an
iPhone or had a strong intention to purchase one. The thesis reports a population of
2,860 Generation Z residents based on 2025 local population data. A 10% margin of
error was applied using the Slovin formula, resulting in a target sample of 97
respondents.
Respondents were selected through purposive sampling. Primary data  were
obtained through a self-report questionnaire distributed directly and through Google
Forms. The questionnaire used a five -point Likert scale ranging from 1 = strongly
disagree to 5 = strongly agree. The instrument contained five items for each
construct: FOMO, Shopping Lifestyle, Social Media Marketing, and iPhone Purchase
Decision, giving 20 substantive items in total. Before the main survey, the instrument
was tested with 30 respondents outside the research sample. All items were reported
as valid because their calculated correlations exceeded the critical value of 0.361.
Reliability was assessed using Cronbach's alpha. The coefficients were 0.902 for
FOMO, 0.775 for Shopping Lifestyle, 0.907 for Social Media Marketing, and 0.863 for
Purchase Decisio n, all above the 0.60 criterion used in the thesis. The analysis
therefore proceeded with the complete set of questionnaire items. Descriptive
statistics were used to summarize the data, followed by classical assumption tests
and multiple linear regression  using SPSS version 25. The assumptions reported in
the thesis included normality, multicollinearity, heteroscedasticity, and
autocorrelation. The residual normality test produced an Asymp. Sig. of 0.200;
tolerance values were above 0.10 and VIF values bel ow 10; the Glejser significance
values were above 0.05; and the Durbin–Watson statistic was 2.190.

and 40.2% reported two to four hours per day. These characteristics indicate that the

Within the TPB framework used in the thesis, the three predictors represent

pressure to keep up with trends; Shopping Lifestyle reflects a consumption
orientation in which products can express identity and lifestyle; and Social Media

simultaneous significance suggests that iPhone purchase decisions in the research
setting are better understood as a combined psychological -social-digital
phenomenon.
Effect of Fear of Missing Out on iPhone Purchase Decisions
The second research question is answered by the partial regression result for
FOMO. FOMO had a positive coefficient (B = 0.407), a standardized coefficient of β =
0.399, and a t value of 4.550 with p < 0.001. The result indicates that higher FOMO
scores were associated with h igher iPhone purchase decisions after controlling for
Shopping Lifestyle and Social Media Marketing. Among the three predictors, FOMO
had the largest standardized coefficient and the highest t statistic in this model.
The finding is consistent with the the sis literature review, which links FOMO
with purchase -related behavior among younger consumers (Kristiyanto, 2022;
Kurniawan & Indriana, 2023; Putri & Setiawan, 2022; Ramadhan & Fauziah, 2024). In
the Kakas Barat context, the thesis interprets the result i n relation to a relatively close
social environment in which individuals can easily observe the consumption choices
of peers. Consequently, iPhone ownership may function not only as a functional
choice but also as a way of remaining connected to prevailing social trends.
Effect of Shopping Lifestyle on iPhone Purchase Decisions
The third research question is supported by the Shopping Lifestyle result. The
variable had a positive regression coefficient (B = 0.280), β = 0.285, t = 3.332, and p =
0.001. Thus, stronger shopping -oriented lifestyle tendencies were associated with
higher iPhone purchase decisions, controlling for FOMO and Social Media
Marketing.

The result is compatible with the thesis discussion that iPhone consumption

Effect of Social Media Marketing on iPhone Purchase Decisions
The fourth research question is also supported. Social Media Marketing
produced a positive coefficient (B = 0.186), β = 0.186, t = 2.242, and p = 0.027 .
Therefore, exposure to and perceptions of social -media marketing were positively
associated with iPhone purchase decisions. Its standardized coefficient was smaller
than those of FOMO and Shopping Lifestyle, but its effect remained statistically
significant.
The finding is consistent with studies cited in the thesis that identify a role for
social media marketing in consumer decisions, including Ramadhani et al. (2023) on
smartphone purchasing and Sendow and Luntu (2023) on young consumers in North
Sulawesi. The thesis also discusses differences from studies in which social media
marketing was the strongest predictor. Such differences may reflect contextual
factors, including the structure of local social networks and differences in how social
media market ing is operationalized. In Kakas Barat, social -media content may
reinforce information and social proof that already circulate through personal peer
networks.
Model Explanatory Power and Research Limitations
The R² value of 0.396 indicates that the three p redictors explain 39.6% of the
variation in iPhone purchase decisions. This result provides empirical support for the
proposed model but also shows that purchase decisions remain multidimensional.
The thesis identifies several possible factors outside the model, including family
financial capacity, brand loyalty, family influence, price, and technical product
considerations.
The thesis also identifies four principal limitations. First, the cross -sectional
design captures consumer behavior at one point in ti me and therefore cannot
describe changes associated with new iPhone launches or changing social -media
trends. Second, the self -report questionnaire may be affected by social -desirability

CONCLUSION
The study answers all four research questions. First, FOMO, Shopping Lifestyle,
and Social Media Marketing jointly show a positive and statistically significant effect
on iPhone purchase decisions among Generation Z in Kakas Barat (F = 20.284, p <
0.001), with the model explaining 39.6% of the variance. Second, FOMO has a
positive and significant partial effect (B = 0.407; β = 0.399; p < 0.001). Third, Shopping
Lifestyle has a positive and significant partial effect (B = 0.280; β = 0.285; p = 0.001).
Fourth, Social Media Marketing also has a positive and significant partial effect (B =
0.186; β = 0.186; p = 0.027).
Taken together, the findings indicate that iPhone purchas e decisions in the
research setting are associated with a combination of psychological pressure to keep
up with trends, shopping-oriented lifestyle, and exposure to social -media marketing.
The standardized coefficients show that FOMO had the largest contri bution within
the estimated model, while the other two variables also made significant
contributions. Because 60.4% of the variance remained unexplained, the results
should be interpreted as evidence for the contribution of the studied variables rather
than as a complete explanation of Generation Z's purchase decisions. Further
research should broaden the population and incorporate financial, family, brand, and
product-related factors.
