A R T I C L E  I N F O   A B S T R A C T
Article history:  Generation Z has grown up in a digital era characterized by the
widespread use of social media and financial technology (FinTech).
Although this generation has greater access to financial information
and digital financial services, many young adults still struggle in
manage their personal finances effectively. This study examines the
effects of financial literacy, social media marketing, and financial
technology on the financial well -being of Generation Z in Karo
Regency, with economic behavior as a mediati ng variable. The study
collected data from 200 Generation Z respondents through structured
questionnaires . The data were analyzed using Structural Equation
Modeling–Partial Least Squares (SEM -PLS). The results indicate that
financial literacy, social media marketing, and financial technology
each positively and significantly affect  both economic behavior and
financial well-being. Furthermore, economic behavior has the strongest
direct effect on fi nancial well -being and significantly mediates the
relationships between financial literacy, social media marketing,
financial technology, and financial well -being. These findings indicate
that improving financial knowledge, encouraging the productive use o f
social media for financial education, and promoting effective financial
technology adoption can enhance financial well -being when
accompanied by responsible economic behavior, including budgeting,
saving, expenditure control, and rational financial decis ion-making.
This study contributes to the literature by integrating financial literacy,
social media marketing, and financial technology into a comprehensive
framework with economic behavior as a mediating mechanism. The
findings also offer practical impli cations for policymakers, educational
institutions, financial service providers, and FinTech companies in
developing sustainable financial education programs and strategies to
improve Generation Z's  financial well -being.

Introduction
Financial well -being is a situation in which a person
controls their daily finances, can withstand financial
shocks, and feels secure about future financial prospects;
it is an important indicator of qualit y of life and overall
psychological well -being (Al-Zahra & Putuhena, 2025) .
Financial well-being is becoming increasingly relevant for
Generation Z, who tend to prioritize current consumption
over long-term financial security (Nurhayati et al., 2026) .
The achievement of financial well-being for Generation Z
requires three main pillars: strengthening economic
literacy to form the right financial mindset, optimizing
financial technology as a tool for mon ey management,
and habituating wise economic behavior in daily life. The
three are interconnected and reinforce each other (Rahayu
& Andriansyah, 2025)
A preliminary survey of 30 respondents  found that
66.67% of Gen Z have not achieved financial well -being.
This finding is in line with data from the Financial
Services Authority (OJK, 2025) , which states that online
loan users are dominated by Millennials  and Gen Z aged
19-34 years. Of the tot al national loans that reached Rp
76.16 trillion, Gen Millennials and Gen Z held the largest
position, which amounted to Rp 38.34 trillion. This event
further emphasizes the urgency of addressing the low
level of financial well-being among Gen Z.
According to  (2025), financial literacy is one of the
factors that plays a role in influencing financial welfare,
where financial knowledge is proven to have a positive
influence on financial welfare.  Financial well-being is also
closely related to an individual's level of financial literacy
understanding, including basic financial knowledge and
financial management skills, which play an important role
in supporting daily activities (Museliza et al., 2023) .
Previous research has shown that the use of financial
technology can significantly improve financial well-being,
with economic behavior acting as a crucial mediator
(Adella Dwi Rahayu, 2025) . Other factors , such as social
media marketing,  also significantly influence financial
well-being. Social media marketing has been shown to
affect financial well -being negatively. This suggests that
the more often students are exposed to social media
advertising and promotions, the more likely their
financial situation is to decline (Nurhayati et al., 2026) .
Rahayu and Andriansyah (2025)  stated that economic
behavior is one of the factors that contribute positively
and significantly to financial welfare.
The develo pment of financial literacy in Indonesia in
recent years has shown a positive trend, although public
understanding of financial management is still a concern.
Based on the results of the National Survey on Financial
Literacy and Inclusion (SNLIK) conducted  by OJK and
BPS, the financial literacy index increased to 66.46% and
the financial inclusion index to  80.51%. The results of the
2025 SNLIK increased compared with the 2024 SNLIK,
which showed a financial literacy index of 65.43% and a
financial inclusion  index of 75.02% (OJK, 2025) . This
condition shows that there is still a gap between the level
of access to financial servic es and the quality of public
understanding in managing finances , which can
ultimately affect people's welfare.
Several previous studies have found a positive
relationship between financial literacy and financial well-
being (Mutmainah & Hindasah, 202 6), and according to
Andiani and Maria (2023), financial literacy and the use of
financial technology affect the financial behavior of
Generation Z,  which can ultimately affect the level of
financial well-being of individuals. According to  (2025),
the use of financial technology alone is not enough to
improve financial welfare if it is not accompanied by wise
economic behavior. Research results from Maulida et al.
(2024), Marwa (2025), and Wahyuni et al. (2024) found
that financial literacy has a positi ve and significant effect
on financial welfare.
On the other hand, several studies show that financial
literacy does not significantly affect financial well-being.
According to  (2026), Suharjana et al. (2026),  Prameswari

Although various studies have identified t he
importance of Generation Z's financial well-being, there is
still a gap in an integrative understanding of how these
three factors interact with each other and affect
Generation Z's financial well -being. The main problem
raised by this study is the low level of welfare in
Generation Z, which has the potential to increase the risk
of financial hardship.
The main purpose of this study is to analyze the
influence of financial literacy, social media marketing,
and the use of financial technology on financia l welfare
through the economic behavior of Gen Z in Karo Regency.
The novelty of this research lies in the integration of the
three variables as independent variables in one analytical
framework. In addition, this study uses the latest data and
focuses on Generation Z, especially in Karo Regency, so it
is expected to provide stronger and contextual insights.
Method
This type of research uses a quantitative approach. This
research was conducted in Karo Regency with a
population of 151,590 people registered with the Karo
Regency Population and Civil Registration Office (BPS,
2025). This study uses the Slovin formula (Nurhayati et
al., 2026)  with a margin of error of 10%. Thus, 100
respondents were obtained. The Slovin formula is shown

Regarding sampling, the researcher used purposive
sampling, where, according to Nurhayati et al. (2026),
respondents were selected based on the researcher's
consideration and certain criteria that had been set. The
sample selection criteria include active social media users,
familiarity with financial technology (fintech) services,
and experience in managing personal finances. The data
used are primary data obtained from questionnaires that
are distributed online through Google Forms  to
Generation Z in Karo Regency.
In this study, five main interrelated variables were
used, namely financial literacy (X1), social media
marketing (X2), and the use of financial technology (X3)
as exogenous variables, financial well -being (Y) as an
endogenous variable, and Econo mic Behavior as an
intervening variable . This research model examines the
influence of financial literacy (X1), social media marketing
(X2), and the use of financial technology (X3) on financial
well-being (Y), both directly and through economic
behavior as the intervening variable.
A preliminary survey of 30 respondents was conducted
to test the validity and reliability of the indicators by
examining correlations using Pearson's r and Cronbach's
alpha. The data analysis technique used is Structural
Equation Modeling (SEM) with the Partial Least Squares
(PLS) approach to analyze the complex relationships
between variables in this study.
The SEM -PLS analysis consists of two main parts,
namely the outer model and the inner model. Indicator
testing, also known  as outer model evaluation, was
carried out to assess the relationship between latent

Test of Validity and Feasibility of Research Instruments
Convergent validity testing aims to evaluate the degree
of conformity  between the indicator and the latent
construct it represents. The assessment is based on the
value of the loading factor , which reflects the strength of
the relationship between the indicator and the construct.
An indicator is declared to have good reflec tive ability if
the loading factor value (λ) exceeds 0.70. However, in
studies that are still in the early stages or exploratory in
nature, indicators with loading factor values ranging from
0.50 to 0.60 can still be maintained because they are
considered to have met the minimum acceptable limit in
construct measurement.

Discussion
The Influence of Financial Literacy on Financial
Welfare
The results of the hypothesis test show that Financial
Literacy has a positive and significant eff ect on the
Financial Welfare of Generation Z in Karo Regency. These
findings confirm that the higher respondents'
understanding of basic financial concepts, financial
products and services, and skills to manage income and
expenses, the better their perceiv ed financial welfare .
These results are consistent with the view Rahayu &
Andriansyah (2025)  and Museliza et al., (2023)  which
states that financial knowledge plays an important role in
supporting daily financial m anagement, and is in line
with the findings (Karyada et al., 2024) , (Wulandari and
Siregar, 2025), and (Ramadhini, 2023) which consistently
found a positive relationship between financial literacy
and financial well-being.
The relevance of financial literacy to individual
financial achievement is further supported by the findings
of (Ruhmi & Tanjung, 2023) among MSME actors in
Medan City, which shows  that financial literacy has a
positive and significant influence on financial
performance. The similarity in the direction of influence
on two different groups of respondents—Generation Z in
Karo Regency and MSME actors in Medan City —shows
that the role of financial literacy as the foundation for
rational financial decision -making is consistent across
segments in the North Sumatra region. However, these
findings differ from the results of studies by Habibah &
Saputra (2026), Prameswari et al. (2021), and Trisuci
(2023), which did not find a significant effect of financial
literacy on financial welfare. This difference is suspected
to be caused by differences i n respondent characteristics,
where the purposive sampling criteria in this study
require respondents who already have experience in
managing personal finances, so that their literacy is easier
to translate into financial decisions that have a real impact
on their financial well-being.
The Influence of Social Media Marketing on
Financial Well-Being
Social Media Marketing has been proven to have a
positive and significant effect on Financial Welfare. This
finding is interesting because it differs from the results of
Nurhayati et al. (2026), which found a  negative influence
of social media marketing on students' financial well -
being due to exposure to advertisements and promotions
that encourage consumption. This difference in the
direction of influence can be  explained by the findings of
Nurhayati et al. ( 2026), which specifically examine the
relationship between social media marketing,
consumptive behavior, and financial self -control on the
financial well -being of students in Medan City. The
research indicate s that the impact of social media
marketing on financial well -being depends largely on
how individuals respond through consumptive behavior
and self-control, rather than being directly negative.
In other words, social media exposure can have a
positive impact when met with wise economic behavior,
as shown by the results of the Economic Behavior
mediation in this study (Table 8). For Generation Z in
Karo Regency, social media also functions as a channel for
disseminating financial information, promoting digi tal
financial products, and financial education content from
content creators, so that its use can selectively support, not
hinder, the achievement of financial welfare. This result is
also in line with the views of Sawitri and Amalia (2025)
and Sari et al . (2026), which show  that the intensity of
social media use in Generation Z does not necessarily
cause financial pressure if it is not followed by excessive
consumptive behavior.

The Influence of Financial Technology on
Financial Welfare
The use of Financi al Technology has a positive and
significant effect on the Financial Welfare of Generation Z
in Karo Regency. These findings are in line with research
which shows that the use of financial technology can
support better financial management, as well as rese arch
(Riswanto et al., 2025) , which found that the higher the
use of financial technology, the higher the financial well -
being felt thanks to the ease of transactions offered. These
results are reinforced by Ruhmi & Tanjung (2023), which
found that fintech peer -to-peer lending and payment
gateways each have a positive and significant influence on
the financial performance of MSME actors in Medan City.
The consistency of the direction of fintech's i nfluence on
financial outcomes in these two different research
contexts strengthens the argument that financial
technology, when used appropriately, can improve
transaction efficiency, facilitate access to financial
services, and ultimately support the fin ancial well-being
of its users.
However, these findings need to be observed along
with warnings (Siskawati & Ningtyas, 2022)   and
(Mas'udiyah & Sutjahyani, 2025) , which remind us  that
the ease of digital transactions has the potential to have a
negative impact if it is not balanced with adequate
financial literacy . Given that the sample criteria in this
study require respondents to be familiar with fintech
services and have relatively good financial literacy (as
shown in the H1 test results), the use of financial
technology in this group of respondents tends to be well
managed so that it has a positive impact on their financial
well-being.
The Influence of Economic Behavior on
Financial Welfare
Economic Behavior showed a positive and significant
effect on Financial Well -Being, with the largest path
coefficient among  all variables tested (β=0.388). These
findings confirm that daily economic habits, such as
saving discipline, managing budgets, paying obligations
on time, and avoiding consumptive behavior, are the most
proximal determinants of a person's financial well -being.
These results are consistent with research (Ramadhini,
2023) that shows that financial behavior has a positive and
significant influence on financial well -being, as well as
research (Sutini & Wiyanto, 2024)   which proves that
financial behavior can improve the financial well-being of
individuals. These findings also strengthen the argument
(Rahayu & Andriansyah, 2025) that economic behavior is
one of the three main pillars of achieving financial well -
being for Generation Z, a longside financial literacy and
financial technology optimization.
The Role of Economic Behavior Mediation
The results of the indirect influence test (Table 8) show
that Economic Behavior plays a significant partial
mediator in the relationship between Fin ancial Literacy,
Social Media Marketing, and Financial Technology on
Financial Well -Being. This means that the three
exogenous variables not only affect Financial Welfare
directly, but also through the improvement of the
respondents' Economic Behavior. The se findings are in
line with the results of the study (Rahayu & Andriansyah,
2025), which found that the use of financial technology
can improve financial welfare, with economic behavior
playing a crucial mediating role.
From a practical pers pective, these findings indicate
that increasing financial literacy, exposure to educational
social media marketing, and ease of access to financial
technology will have a stronger impact on Generation Z's
financial well -being when paired with stronger  healthy
economic behaviors, such as saving habits, budgeting,
and controlling consumption. In other words, the three
exogenous factors act as providers of resources and means
(enablers). At the same time,  economic behavior acts as a
bridge that converts these  resources into real financial
well-being.

Conclusion
This study analyzes the influence of financial literacy,
social media marketing, and the use of financial
technology on the financial welfare of Generation Z in
Karo Regency , with economic behavior as a  mediating
variable. Based on the results of the analysis using
Structural Equation Modeling -Partial Least Squares
(SEM-PLS), all research hypotheses were accepted.
The results of the study show that financial literacy,
social media marketing, and the use of financial
technology have a positive and significant effect on the
financial welfare of Generation Z. In addition, these three
variables have also been proven to have a positive and
significant effect on economic behavior. These findings
indicate that i ncreasing financial knowledge, productive
use of social media, and the appropriate use of financial
technology can encourage the formation of better
economic behavior while improving individual financial
well-being.
This study also proves that economic beh avior plays a
role as a partial mediating variable in the relationship
between financial literacy, social media marketing, and
the use of financial technology on financial well -being.
Economic behavior is an important mechanism that
bridges the influence o f these three variables so that
individuals can optimize financial resources through the
habits of managing budgets, saving, controlling
consumption, and making rational financial decisions.
Thus, the improvement of the financial well -being of
Generation Z  is not only determined by the level of
literacy and access to financial technology, but also by the
ability to implement this knowledge into responsible
economic behavior.
Theoretically, this study enriches the development of
studies on financial welfare by integrating financial
literacy, social media marketing, and financial technology
in a research model mediated by economic behavior.
Practically, the results of this study provide implications
for local governments, educational institutions, financial
institutions, and financial technology service providers to
strengthen financial literacy education programs, increase
the dissemination of financial education content through
social media, and encourage the formation of healthy
economic behavior to improve the financial welfare of
Generation Z sustainably.
