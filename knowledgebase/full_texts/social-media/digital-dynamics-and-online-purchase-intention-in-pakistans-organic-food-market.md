Background: Digital platforms are having a growing influence on consumer buying
decisions, especially in developing countries like Pakistan. Trust is crucial when making
the shift of digital interaction to purchase intention in the online organic food context,
as consumer doubts about product quality, safe ty, authenticity and certification can
create a problem.  Objective: The aim of the study was to examine the effect of social
media marketing, website quality, electronic word of mouth (e -WOM) and perceived
value on online purchase intention of Pakistani customers of organic foods. Method: The
approach was positivist with descriptive survey design, cross sectional using
quantitative method, based on the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) and The
theory of planned behaviour (TPB). Data were acquired using an o nline questionnaire
from 545 Pakistani customers having experience of, or plan to purchase, organic food
online. The proposed hypotheses were analysed using partial least squares structural
equation modelling.  Results: Social media marketing (SMM), website  quality (WQ),
and electronic word of mouth (e -WOM) significantly and positively influenced both
trust and online purchase intention (PI). WQ was the strongest direct predictor of PI,
whereas e -WOM had the strongest effect on trust. Trust significantly med iated the
relationships of SMM, WQ, and e -WOM with PI. Consumer knowledge significantly
strengthened the effects of social media marketing, WQ, and e -WOM on trust. But, in
the full model, perceived value (PV) did not significantly predict trust nor PI and was
not supported as a moderation and mediation variable.

Introduction
With the prevalence of eCommerce in today's digital age, comprehending the concept of purchase
intent has become essential for businesses aiming to prosper in the fast-evolving digital marketing
world (Sunyoto et al., 2025 ). Consumer intention to purchase indicates a conscious decision made
by consumers to purchase a product or service because their expectation aligns with their
perceptions of product quality, and it is deemed as a good predictor of actual purchase (Saleem et
al., 2022).
Marketing has always been a key component to the success of a firm and with the introduction of
digital technology, it has made shopping more competitive as  customers are not restricted to
geographical location. Online shopping is crucial to ensure the deeper penetration of the digital
economy as Pakistan is grappling with ongoing problems in digitalization of the economy (Saleem
et al., 2022 ). Social media or other online tools help to promote products and services, thereby
building the Purchase intention (PI) of consumers, while presenting a blend of consumer interest and
the likelihood of purchase (Tandon et al., 2020). This digital revolution has been accelerated by the
COVID-19 pandemic, especially in developing nations which have seen consumers move away
from traditional retail to online shopping (Amankwah-Amoah et al., 2021).
Digital platforms play a crucial role in directly connecting with consumers, raising awareness of the
brand, and influencing the decision to purchase (Aji et al., 2020). In online businesses, there are still
some issues regarding service quality including slow response and lack of information, which is so
important for customers to be loyal and purcha se its products again (Moslehpour et al., 2021 ).
Marketing strategies need to be adjusted to the digital world in which the purchasing behavio r of
consumers is influenced by online interactions and trust in e-commerce (Jain et al., 2026).
Social media marketing (SMM) has a great contribution in influencing PI by increasing brand
visibility and consumer involvement (Agnihotri et al., 2021). Similarly, electronic word of mouth (e-
WOM) is a strong instrument in online marketing and consumers depend on other people's reviews
and recommendations when making decisions for purchases (Albayrak et al., 2021 ). Positive e -
WOM plays an important role in influencing consumer trust and consumer purchasing decision
(Hidayat et al., 2021 ), and building trust in e-commerce platform, sellers, and products are
important factors for success in the internet sales industry. Likewise trust is significant in the
purchase decision in online: Trust positively affects PI and the consumers need reliable information
and assurance about the products before making an online purchase (Tam et al., 2024).
The traditional farming along with use of hazardous pesticides in Pakistan made consumers aware
of the harmful effect of their food on the environment and health, thus more and more people  are
interested in the organic foods which also contribute to the GDP and employment in the country
(Basha et al., 2019). Consumer trust in quality and certification of organic products is crucial to their
acceptance of the category, and the trend toward increased organic food consumption h as
continued to pick up momentum throughout the COVID -19 pandemic due to health concerns.
Given the scarcity of research in Pakistan on the consumption of organic foods, this study explores
the interplay between SMM, WQ, e-WOM, and Perceived Value PV on the intention to purchase

Digital Dynamics and Consumer Decision-Making
Based on the literature, there are three factors that affects t he intention to buy products online:
technological, relational and consumer -related factors. Technology Acceptance Model (TAM)
puts the perceived usefulness and ease of use of technology as the key determinants of technology
acceptance (Bazine, 2025), and Theory of Planned Behaviour (TPB) introduces attitudes, subjective
norms and perceived behavioral control as key factors to behavioral intention (Huang et al., 2026).
In online purchasing, the various types of digital marketing, including SMM, WQ and e-WOM can
help to improve consumer engagement, information availability and product evaluation. Likewise,
PV can make the PI more powerful by making consumers perceive greater benefits for the
purchased product in comparison with the costs (Chae et al., 2020). But these relationships can be
especially trust dependent because consumers are uncertain and at risk when maki ng their online
purchases (Hidayat et al., 2021 ). Consumer knowledge can also affect these relations by how
people process information online and rate unfamiliar and credence products like organic food (He
et al., 2025 ). Though these findings, there is very little literature that combines these aspects in a
single context, especially in the organic food market in Pakistan. This study thus aims to investigate
the influence of digital dynamics in online PI via trust with the consideration of consumer
knowledge as a possible moderating variable.
Research Gap
Although several studies were conducted in Pakistan (Saleem et al., 2022 ) and around the world
(Aprianto et al., 2021 ) the research that has investigated the combined  effect of four variables
(SMM, WQ, PV and e -WOM) on consumers' PI while considering two moderating variables
(consumer knowledge and trust) is limited. Individual studies have explored these different factors
in isolation; however, a comprehensive analysi s of the combined and synergistic impact of these
factors on the decision -making process of consumers in a new digital economy like Pakistan is
lacking. This study fills in that gap.
Objectives of the study
The objectives of this study are to investigate the direct impact of SMM, WQ, e -WOM and PV on
PI; to examine the moderating effect of consumer knowledge on the relationship between the
digital dynamics and trust; and to investigate the mediating effect of trust in the relationship
between the digital dynamics and PI.
This study is one of the first studies to simultaneously consider the influence of SMM, WQ, e-WOM
and PV on PI among Pakistani consumers of organic food. The results will enable companies to make
informed changes to their digital marketing s trategies, build trust with consumers and boost sales,
sales revenue, and competitiveness in the digital world.

Methodology
Consumer behavior marketing studies have been carried out using a positivist research philosophy
(Bambore et al., 2017; Madzvamuse, 2026) which is also followed in this study, while the deductive
research approach is followed in which the hypotheses built on TAM and TPB are tested
empirically as suggested by (Bambore et al., 2017 ). Given the nature of the study, which was
positivist in orientation and the desire to use efficient, cost effective methods for dealing with large
data sets, a quantitative methodology was chosen.
Study Design
Cross sectional survey design was the design used to collect data at one time. The questionnaire was
based on the scales used in the previous literature, and the items were rated on a five -point Likert
scale that ranged from 1 (Strongly Disagree) to 5 (Strongly Agree) for the measu res of PI, Trust,
Consumer Knowledge, SMM, WQ, e-WOM and PV.
Individual online consumers of organic food in Pakistan was the unit of analysis. The researchers
adopted non -probability sampling methods: convenience sampling and snowball sampling
following the usual practice in online questionnaires studies (Saunders et al., 2019). Data screening
resulted in 545 usable responses that were analysed. The respondents were mostly young (25 -30
year old – 76.7%) and highly educated (64.0% – master's degree).
Data Analysis
Analysis of data used was Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM) which
was carried out in two stages, namely measurement model assessment (outer loading, reliability,
and validity) and assessment of structural model (path coeff icient, coefficient of determination,
effect size, and predictive relevance). As per the criterion proposed by (Hair et al., 2014), the items
with outer loading which were lower than 0.40 were deleted and those in the range of 0.40 – 0.70
were retained when their removal did not improve composite reliability (CR) or average variance
extracted (AVE).
Reliability and Validity
Reliability was examined by Cronbach's alpha and composite reliability and convergent validity
was examined by AVE, which were based on the model of (Hair et al., 2011 ). Standard PLS -SEM
reporting rules were followed in evaluating the discriminant validity by applying the Fornell -
Larcker criterion, the Heterotrait-Monotrait (HTMT) ratio and cross-loadings.
Hypothesis Testing
Hypotheses to explore proposed relationships b etween SMM, WQ, electronic word of mouth (e -
WOM), PV, trust, consumer knowledge, and intention to purchase online for organic food among
Pakistani consumers were developed. For the purpose of studying these relationships, four direct -
effect hypotheses were  analyzed, which were: whether SMM, WQ, e -WOM, and PV positively
influence PI (H1 –H4). The following four hypotheses tested the implications of these digital

dynamics for consumer trust (H5–H8), and the last one examined whether consumer trust positively
affects consumer PIs (H9). Furthermore, consumer knowledge was examined as a moderating

WOM, PV and trust (H10 –H13). Based on the literature review, the SMM, WQ, e-WOM and PV

Structural Model and Hypothesis Testing
Table 4 shows the results of the analyses in the structural model, including the correlation
coefficients, standardized path coefficients, significance levels and hypothesis decision s for all
seventeen hypotheses.
Direct effects on Purchase Intention (H1-H4)
SMM (β = 0.216, p < 0.007), WQ ( β = 0.333, p < 0.001), and e -WOM (β = 0.137, p < 0.026) all
showed statistically significant, positive structural paths to PI, supporting H1, H2, and H3. Relative
to the three direct predictors, WQ was found to be the strongest, meaning that a well designed and
easy to navigate, secure website is the single most important digital touch -point that influences
online buying in this sample. PV had a high simple correlation with PI (r = 0.703, p < 0.005), but it
was not significant when the full structure with the mediation of Digital Dynamics and Trust was
estimated, so that H4 was not supported in the mediated model, suggesting that the effect of PV on
PI was not strong when other digital dynamics and Trust were included.
Digital dynamics and Trust (H5-H8)
All three variables (SMM, WQ, and e -WOM) significantly and positively influenced Trust, thus
supporting the H5, H6, and H7, with e-WOM having the highest beta value of all the predictors in
the model, which suggests that peer reviews and recommendations play the dominant role in
creating trust among Pakistani consumers when buying organic food products online. PV did not
significantly predict Trust, so H8 was not supported meaning that, unlike the other three digital
dynamics, favorable value perceptions alone do not translate into greater trust in this market.

Trust to Purchase Intention (H9)
Trust was significantly and positively related to PI, supporting H9 and confirming Trust's role as a
key direct driver of online purchase decisions, independent of the digital dynamics that build it.
Moderation by Consumer Knowledge (H10-H13)
Consumer Knowledge significantly strengthened the effects of SMM, W Q, and e -WOM on Trust
(H10, H11, H12 supported), indicating that more knowledgeable consumers extract greater trust
benefit from the same digital marketing exposure. However, Consumer Knowledge did not
significantly moderate the PV -to-Trust relationship (H 13 not supported), reinforcing that PV

Mediation by Trust (H14-H17)
Trust significantly mediated the relationships between SMM, WQ, and e -WOM and PI (H14, H15,
H16 supported), confirming th at these three digital dynamics influence PI both directly and
indirectly through building Trust. Trust did not significantly mediate the PV -to-PI relationship
(H17 not supported), consistent with the earlier finding that PV does not operate through the Tr ust
pathway.
Overall Pattern of Hypotheses Tested
Out of the seventeen hypotheses tested, twelve were supported and five were not (H4, H8, H13, H17,
and the trust -mediated portion of H4). All five unsupported hypotheses involve PV, indicating a
consistent pattern: PV did not exhibit a significant direct path to PI in the full model, did not
significantly predict Trust, was not moderated by Consumer Knowledge, and was not mediated by
Trust. In contrast, SMM, WQ, and e -WOM were consistently significant across  all direct,
moderated, and mediated pathways, underscoring that these three digital dynamics and not PV
are the primary drivers of both Trust and PI among Pakistani online organic-food consumers.

Discussion
This section discusses the findings derived from the structural model and study's seventeen
hypotheses to interpret each result.
Descriptive Statistics of the Study Variables
The means scores of the seven study variables, ranging from PI (3.59) to PV (3.71), all fell in a narrow
band of 0.22 points on five-point scale from neutral to agree (Table 1). This trend is reflective of the
fact that even the consumers of Pakistan are s till traversing the developing world of the internet
and there is a need for continuous efforts to develop the digital skills of the users as the digital
transformation expands (Saleem et al., 2022). Remarkably, the mean for PV (3.71) is higher than all
other variables in the model, although the pos itive perception of the value of the product does not
result in a statistically significant structural path to Trust and PI in the full model described in the
structural results portion. Similarly, the relatively high means for SMM and WQ echo the literature
emphasis on social media and digital platforms as key touchpoints to engage consumers directly
and stimulate their PIs(Aji et al., 2020) and thus, their high and consistent performance throughout
the structural model.
Measurement Model: Reliability and Validity, and Item Retention
Tables 2 and 3 provide a psychometric basis for the tests of the hypotheses. Based on the loadings
of the items in this study, the dependent variable and the mediating variable were measured with
high level of item -to-construct correspondence: the six items of PI loaded between 0.68 and 0.87
and the five items of Trust loaded between 0.86 and 0.89; removal of the latter (PI3) below the
recommended outer -loading threshold reflects the standard measurement -model purification

practice. This is a key issue with the hypothesis tests discussed below, since the results of these tests
could not be relied upon if the Trust/purpose Intention measure is weak or unreliable.
The Cronbach's alpha and composite reliability were above 0.70 for eac h construct, and the
average variance extracted (AVE) was above 0.50 for all constructs, thus satisfying internal
consistency reliability and convergent validity requirements. The validity of the discriminant was
confirmed as the variables SMM, WQ, e -WOM, PV, Trust and Consumer Knowledge were
empirical separate constructs and not overlapping measures of the same underlying attitude,
which was supported by the joint support of the Fornell -Larcker criterion and Heterotrait -
Monotrait (HTMT) ratios within accep table limits. This is important to consider when interpreting
the structural results as this prevents any construct overlap between the divergent pattern later
observed for PV (non-significant paths to Trust) and the convergent patterns for SMM, WQ, and e-
WOM (significant paths to Trust). The variance inflation factor (VIF) values less than 5 are also used
to indicate that multicollinearity is not likely to be an alternative explanation for the poor
structural performance of PV, and hence the hypothesis-testing results presented in the subsequent
section are more likely to be valid.
Structural Model and Hypothesis Testing
As shown in Table 4, the correlation and standardized path coefficients provide a clearer
understanding of how the study variables are rel ated to one another. The results also help explain
the strength and direction of these relationships and provide a basis for assessing the proposed
hypotheses.
Social Media Marketing and Purchase Intention (H1)
Social networks have become extremely importa nt marketing tools that offer brands special
possibilities to connect with their target audience, and the data confirm the hypothesis that SMM
positively impacts buyers' intention to purchase. The correlation coefficient is r = 0.756 with a p -
value of 0.00 7 which shows that both variables are positively related and the correlation
coefficient is high, while the standardized regression weight (β = 0.216, p < 0.007) shows that both
variables are correlated and statistically significant, which supports H1. In Pakistan, consumers
seem to respond to content that is interactive and engaging on social networks, and targeted social
media ads have been proven to greatly increase consumers' willingness to purchase (Mandagi et al.,
2021). This result corroborates the previous study, which emphasized the central role of social media
in influencing consumers' decision to buy online (Bilgin, 2018; Mandagi et al., 2021 ; Siddik et al.,
2022), which is also consistent with a previous study that found that the social media is now
becoming an integral part of Pakistani consumers' daily routine (Babatunde et al., 2021).
Website quality and desire to buy from it (H2)
Often, a brand's first touch point with potential customers is a website, and, as noted above, the data
corroborates the hypothesis that quality websites positively affect PI. This relationship is
statistically significant as shown by the correlation coefficient (r = 0.766, p < 0.001) and
standardized regression weight (β = 0.333, p < 0.001), and supported completely by the result. The

result is consistent with the previous studies that have shown t hat having usable and easy -to-use
websites and making them entertaining increases customers' satisfaction and PI (Hossain et al.,
2021), which shows the positive effects of responsive a nd secure websites on customer trust and
willingness to buy.
Electronic Word of Mouth and Purchase Intention (H3)
Online reviews, social media comments and peers' recommendations have also been found to have
a significant impact on customer behaviour which corroborates the hypothesis that e-WOM has a
positive effect on PI among Pakistanis. This relationship is statistically significant, as evidenced by
the correlation coefficient (r = 0.796, p < 0.026) and standardized regression weight (β = 0.137, p <
0.026), which back up H3. The results are in line with the literature related to e -WOM in general
indicating that engaging and credible e -WOM content has a significant impact on consumer
confidence and purchase likelihood (Shukla et al., 2023).
Perceived Value and Purchase Intention (H4)
PV, which refers to the overall judgement of benefits and costs of a product, is a significant factor in
consumers' decision-making process and the simple association with PI is positive and strong (r =
0.703, p < 0.005), similar to previous resear ch that showed that PV (such as quality, price, and
emotional benefits) has a significant relationship with the PI of consumers (Chae et al., 2020 ;
Curvelo et al., 2019 ; Sharma et al., 2020 ). Once the complete structural model was estimated,
however, this path was not statistically significant and thus was not supported, suggesting that the
influence of PV may be mediated by other paths than direct.
Trust and Social Media Marketing (H5)
SMM isn't just about selling products but also increasing trust between customers and brands and
these results confirm the hypothesis that SMM can be used to positively influence consumer trust.
This relationship is statistically significant with a correlation coefficient of r = 0.745, which is less
than 0.001 and a standardized regression weight of β = 0.280, with a p value of less than 0.001, thus
supporting H5. This is consistent with previous stud ies on the positive impact of SMM on trust
(Prahasta et al., 2025), interactive and authentic content in social media builds trust relationships
(Islam et al., 2024; Ünalmış et al., 2026) and regular, honest communication builds consumers' trust
and loyalty (Sunyoto et al., 2025).
The overall quality and trustworthiness of websites (H6)
A good functi oning website is a fundamental element in building trust and there was strong
evidence to support the hypothesis that websites can have a positive impact on consumer trust. H6
is supported as the correlation coefficient (r = 0.768, p < 0.001) and standardized regression weight
(β = 0.367, p < 0.001) are statistically significant. This finding corroborates the findings of earlier
studies by (Chae et al., 2020 ; Qalati et al., 2021 ), on the positive effect of WQ on trust, and also
supports the findings of (AlAwadhi et al., 20 24), who found that ease of use of a website leads to
consumer confidence and trust in the website.

eWOM and eTrust in building relationships, trust and credibility (H7)
Online reviews and recommendations from peers are important factors in influencing the
perceptions and trust of consumers, and the data confirm the hypothesis that positive e -WOM
increases consumer trust. This relationship is statistically significant as suggested by the coefficient
of the correlation (r = 0.851, p < 0.001) and standardized regression weight (β = 0.591, p < 0.001) and

Perceived Value and Trust (H8)
PV has been identified as one of the factors influencing customer behaviour in terms of commitment
to a relationship, purchasing intention and trust (Chae et al., 2020 ) and previous studies have
shown that decreased value is associated with decreased customer trust (Sharma et al., 2020 ).
However, the correlation coefficient (r = 0.703, p < 0.310 ) and the standardized regression weight
(β = 0.345, p < 0.310) suggest that this relationship was not statistically significant; thus, H8 was
not supported, which contradicts the general literature that states the more value perceived, the
higher the level of trust (Sharma et al., 2020) and that trust in this market is more associated with
relational and social cues than with value calculations alone.
Trust and Purchase Intention (H9)
Trust is one of the most important factors in consumer decision making and is expected to decrease
the perceived risk and uncertainties, and the data were observed as strong support of the hypothesis
that the increase of the consumers' trust has positive effects on PI. This association is statistically
significant as supported by the correlation coefficient (r = 0.716, p < 0.004) and standardized
regression weight ( β = 0.182, p < 0.004), which is consistent with the previous study finding that
there is a positive correlation between trust and PI (Curvelo et al., 2019; Saleem et al., 2022; Sharma
et al., 2020).
Consumers' knowledge of the market (H10–H13)
Consumer knowledge significantly moderated the relationship between SMM and trust (β = 0.375,
t = 4.61, p < 0.000), between WQ and trust (β = 0.234, t = 2.780, p < 0.005) and between e-WOM
and trust (β = 0.269, t = 4.491, p < 0.000), supporting H10, H11 and H12, respectively. However, the
association between PV and the trust was not significantly mediated by the consumers' knowledge
(β = 0.054, t = 1.249, p < 0.212), so that H13 hypothesis was not supported, meaning that the
relationship of perception of th e exposure through social media/website with the perception of
trust was not significantly mediated by knowledge.

Overall Pattern of Findings
The hypotheses were partially supported together by 12 (H1, H2, H3, H5, H6, H7, H9, H10, H11, H12,

Conclusion
The findings of this research indicate that SMM, WQ and e -WOM have significant direct and
indirect impacts on the intention of Pakistani consumers towards buying organic food.  From these
criteria, e-WOM proved to be the most significant predictor of trust, and WQ's direct impact on PI
is the most significant, highlighting the significance of credible reviews, secure websites,
transparent i nformation and user -friendly websites. Another significant finding was that
consumers' knowledge further confirmed the link between SMM, WQ, and trust, which shows that
trust increases when consumers have kn owledge of how digital marketing information impa cts
their level of trust.
