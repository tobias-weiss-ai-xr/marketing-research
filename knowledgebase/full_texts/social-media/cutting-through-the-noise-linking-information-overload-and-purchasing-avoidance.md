This study examines how information overload in data -saturated digital markets
influences purchasing avoidance behavior. Specifically, it investigates whether
information overload is associated with higher avoidance  through three
psychological mechanisms that is consumer confusion, social media exhaustion,
and decision difficulty. An online survey was conducted among 156 Indonesian
university students who actively engage with social commerce platforms. The data
were analyzed using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM)
4 to estimate both direct and indirect relationships among information overload,
consumer confusion, social media exhaustion, decision difficulty, and purchasing
avoidance. The results show that information overload significantly increases
consumer confusion, social media exhaustion, and decision difficulty. Consumer
confusion, social media exhaustion, and decision difficulty complementary partial
mediate the relationship between information overload and purchase avoidance
behavior.  The findings suggest that reducing decision difficulty at the point of
purchase is critical for mitigating avoidance behavior in digital markets. Managers,
particularly in SMEs, should simplify information architecture, eliminate
nonessential product options, and adopt good –better–best default structures.
Practical tools such as concise Frequently Asked Questions positioned near calls -
to-action and lightweight decision aids can help consumers process information
more efficiently and move toward purchase decisions. This study contributes to the
literature by integrating cognitive overload, platform -induced fatigue, and choice
complexity within a single empirical model. Consumer confusion, social media
exhaustion, and decision difficulty function as jointly significant, equally critical,
and complementary parallel mediating mechanisms connecting information
overload to purchase avoidance.

1. INTRODUCTION
Rapid developments in information technology have brought about major changes in the way
consumers interact with products and services, especially through digital platforms (Yuwono, Suroso, et
al., 2024). With greater access to a wider range of information and choices, consumers can now make
decisions more quickly and with more complete data (Yuwono, Novandari, et al., 2024). However, behind
this progress, the digital environment poses serious challenges, including information overload. This
phenomenon occurs when the amount of information received by consumers exceeds their cognitive
capacity to process it effectively. As a result, consumers not only face difficulties processing information
but also tend to make suboptimal decisions, often accompanied by feelings of regret or dissatisfaction

Research shows that the more choices available, the more likely consumers are to feel overwhelmed,
slow down the decision -making process, or even decide not to buy at all, a condition known as
purchasing avoidance (Iyengar & Lepper, 2000) . In addition, information overload is often strongly
linked to consumer confusion , which arises from too much, inconsistent, or conflicting information.
Confused consumers not only feel frustrated but also lose trust in the brand, which can ultimately hinder
their purchasing decisions (H. Hu & Krishen, 2019).
In the era of social media, this challenge has become even more complex (Yuwono, Cahyani, et al.,
2026). Social media exhaustion occurs when consumers feel overwhelmed by the flood of information,
promotional content, and digital interactions. This exhaustion not only reduces consumers' engagement
with brands but also diminishes their cognitive ability to p rocess relevant information. This ultimately
reduces consumers' interest and trust in the products or services offered (Chong et al., 2016; Herman et
al., 2026). The interaction among information overload, consumer confusion, social media exhaustion,
and decision difficulty is a highly relevant issue in modern marketing (Novandari et al., 2025)
Understanding the mechanisms underlying these variables not only helps companies identify the
obstacles consumers face but also enables them to design more effective communication strategies
(Yuwono, Novandari, et al., 2026) . By minimizing the negative impact of information overload,
companies can enhance the consumer experience, strengthen brand trust, and encourage more positive
purchasing behavior (Suwandari et al., 2026).
Although previous research has acknowledged the effects that an excessive amount of information
has on consumers' emotions, that is confusion, fatigue, and difficulty, it is unclear whether these
emotions are predictive of purchasing avoidance  (Furkan & Yuwono, 2025) . This ambiguity arises
because many studies have not sufficiently analyzed cognitive strain and emotional exhaustion
separately to determine their individual roles in shaping behavioral outcomes (Furkan et al., 2025). The
novelty of this study lies in the simultaneous and comparative modeling of the three mediators,
consumer confusion, social media exhaustion, and decision difficulty, within a single structural model,
which represents a distinct contribution compared to  the previous studies described above. In an
attempt to help address this gap, the current investigation will explore how excessive information may
indirectly affect consumer purchasing avoidance through the mediating variables of confusion, social
media exhaustion, and decision difficulty. Examining these mediators will allow the researchers to create
a more detailed picture of how consumers make choices in information -rich digital environments and,
ultimately, to demonstrate that the assumption that all instances of overload result in avoidance
behaviors is too simplistic.
2. LITERATURE REVIEW
This research is anchored in the Stimulus -Organism-Response (SOR) paradigm proposed by
Mehrabian & Russell (1974). The SOR framework posits that external environmental stimuli (S) influence
an individual's internal cognitive and emotional states (O), which subsequently dictate their behavioral
responses (R). In the context of the data-saturated digital market investigated in this study, Information
Overload acts as the external environmental Stimulus (S) originating from social commerce platforms.
This overwhelming stimulus is hypothesized to trigger a tripartite of internal Organism (O) states
representing both cognitive strain and emotional fatigue: Consumer Confusion, Social Media Exhaustion,
and Decision Difficulty. Ultimately, these internal states drive the behavioral Response (R), which in this
model is characterized by Purchasing Avoidance. By employing the SOR theory, this study systematically
unpacks how structural platform overloads are internalized by consumers before translating into
avoidance mechanisms.

 2.1 The Effects of Information Overload on Consumer Confusion
An overload of information is a state in which a consumer has acquired more information than their
cognitive abilities can analyze successfully. With the overload of information, consumers might end up
confused about what to buy based on the many available  sources of information they have received;
when they receive an abundance of information that is ambiguous or contradictory, they are likely to
experience confusion. Numerous studies show that when presented with too many choices or an
overwhelming amount of information in the purchasing process, consumers tend to be confused by what
distinguishes one product or brand from another (H. Hu & Krishen, 2019) . In addition, many studies
have found that to help alleviate the confusion related to information overload, consumers need to be
able to process relevant information using their prior experiences with purchasing products, as well as
by having the proper c ontext about those products. The presence of these factors can reduce a
consumer's level of confusion when selecting a product to meet their specific needs. On the other hand,
research by Sachdeva (2020) reveals that consumers who experience information overload often feel
frustrated, which further exacerbates their confusion. Consumer confusion not only creates feelings of
uncertainty but can also reduce consumer trust in brands and lead to purchase avoi dance. Thus,
information overload can increase consumer confusion, which in turn can interfere with consumer
purchasing decisions. Therefore, the hypothesis proposed is:
H1: Information overload has a positive influence on consumer confusion.
2.2. The Effect of Information Overload on Social Media Exhaustion
Information overload, when individuals are inundated with too much information that is difficult to
process, can trigger mental and physical fatigue, a phenomenon known as social media exhaustion. This
fatigue results from excessive interaction with content on social media platforms, which often contains
repetitive, irrelevant, or excessive information. Research shows that social media, which continuously
streams new information, significantly affects users' fatigue levels. Users who are exposed to excessiv e
information on social media tend to feel tired, confused, and even frustrated, which leads to a decline in
interest in continuing to interact with the platform (Sheng et al., 2023) . This relates to the theory of
cognitive overload, which posits that excessive information processing impairs users' ability to remain
focused and engage productively with social media content (Teng et al., 2022). Therefore, the hypothesis
proposed is:
H2: Information overload has a positive influence on social media exhaustion.
2.3. The Effect of Information Overload on Decision Difficulty
Individuals are susceptible to information overload when they encounter more data than they can
comprehend and use. Information overload can make decision-making difficult due to the overwhelming
number of options available to individuals and the poorly de signed information structure. Research
conducted by Ying et al. (2025)  indicates that when people are inundated with information overload,
their ability to make rational evaluations of all options is negatively affected; as a consequence, they take
longer to identify and evaluate options, and will experience more confusion. As a result of having too
many irrelevant items, individuals will spend additional cognitive resources filtering through
unimportant items to find and compare the relevant ones, which could lead to increased cognitive fatigue
(X. Hu et al., 2024). In conclusion, the following hypothesis will be proposed:
H3: Information overload has a positive influence on decision difficulty.
2.4. The Effect of Consumer Confusion on Purchasing Avoidance
Consumers who experience confusion tend to feel frustrated and unable to make decisions with
confidence. This, in turn, can increase the tendency to avoid purchasing decisions or even avoid making
decisions altogether, known as purchasing avoidance (H. Hu & Krishen, 2019). Research by Bettman et
al. (1998) reveals that when consumers feel confused, they are more likely to avoid making purchase

2.5. The Effect of Social Media Exhaustion on Purchasing Avoidance
Continuous use of social media can leave consumers feeling overwhelmed by the constant flow of
information, whether it is personal content or product promotions. This condition not only reduces
interest in further interaction with social media platforms  but can also influence consumers' purchasing
decisions (Njeri et al., 2026). Fernandes & Oliveira (2024) found that consumers who experience social
media exhaustion tend to avoid promotional content that they consider excessive or irrelevant. This
worsens consumer engagement with brands and increases the likelihood of purchase avoidance
(Pabalkar et al., 2019) . In addition, social media exhaustion can reduce the effectiveness of marketing
messages, causing consumers to feel pressured and ultimately avoid the purchasing process (Furkan &
Yuwono, 2025). Therefore, the hypothesis proposed is:
H5: Social media exhaustion has a positive influence on purchasing avoidance.
2.6. The Effect of Decision Difficulty on Purchase Avoidance
Consumers who face decision -making difficulties tend to delay or avoid making purchases. This
phenomenon is known as purchasing avoidance, where consumers choose not to purchase because they
feel overwhelmed by the many choices available (Chernev, 2003). Decisions that are avoided can be
caused by fear of making the wrong choice, confusion between the many choices, or concerns about the
consequences of the decision (Yadav et al., 2026) . Research also shows that decision difficulty can
increase consumers' tendency to avoid making purchasing decisions, especially when they feel less
confident in assessing available alternatives or when the choices are too complex to understand
(Agnihotri et al., 2024). This is related to increased perceptions of uncertainty, which in turn exacerbates
purchase avoidance. Therefore, the hypothesis proposed is:
H6: Decision difficulty has a positive influence on purchasing avoidance.
2.7. The Mediation Effect of Consumer Confusion
Information overload occurs when consumers are exposed to so much information that they cannot
process it cognitively. In digital markets, information overload is often positively correlated with
consumer confusion , especially when information across products is inconsistent, ambiguous, or
incomparable (Mitchell et al., 2015) . Several studies have demonstrated that information overload
results in increased consumer confusion, disrupting the ability to distinguish meaningful product
differences and accurately assess product value (H. Hu & Krishen, 2019). Consumer confusion has been
shown to interfere with decision -making by increasing uncertainty and decreasing confidence in
purchase judgment. When a consumer cannot clearly evaluate an alternative, they may hesitate,
postpone, or abandon their purchase (Agnihotri et al., 2024) . In addition, confusion may lead to
diminished trust in brands or product information, which is associated with avoidance -oriented
behaviours in consumer settings (H. Hu & Krishen, 2019). Therefore, consumer confusion is a reasonable
underlying psychological mechanism  that can explain how information overload leads to purchasing
avoidance. Based on this reasoning, the current research proposes a model in which information
overload leads to greater purchasing avoidance, and increased consumer confusion mediates this
relationship.
H7: Consumer confusion mediates the relationship between information overload and purchasing
avoidance.

2.8. The Mediation Effect of Social Media Exhaustion
Consumers experience cognitive and emotional overload in online environments, and this overload
is compounded by continuous exposure to promotional, user-generated, and social interactions on social
media (Shahid et al., 2025) . The relationship between social media exhaustion and the stress and
overload resulting from excessive social media use has been supported by several empirical studies (Luo
et al., 2024). In relation to social media exhaustion, it has been noted that when consumers are in a state
of social media exhaustion, they may withdraw from using social media altogether as a coping
mechanism, including a decrease in attention to content and withdrawing from further interaction with
promotional messages (Z. Zhang et al., 2026) . In the context of marketing, this disengagement may
extend beyond social media use to include avoidance of promotional messages and reluctance to engage
in the purchase process (Furkan et al., 2025) . As exhausted consumers attempt to minimise  their
cognitive and emotional burden, they may disengage from further purchases as a coping mechanism.
Social Media Exhaustion, therefore, is anticipated to serve as an effective pathway through which
information overload leads to purchase avoidance in a digital environment.
H8: Social media exhaustion mediates the relationship between information overload and purchasing
avoidance.
2.9. The Mediation Effect of Decision Difficulty
When consumers are exposed to too much product information and a large number of alternatives,
this significantly impairs their ability to systematically evaluate the options before making a final
purchase decision. The greater the cognitive load required to make a purchase decision, the more
difficult it is to make that decision (Iyengar & Lepper, 2000). The greater the cognitive load associated
with making a purchase decision, the greater the feelings of uncertainty that result from having to
evaluate all of the alternative options, the greater the time spent evaluating those options and the greater
the likelihood there is that the consumer will experience "buyer's regret" associated with having made
a less-than-optimal choice. Decision difficulty has been repeatedly associated with increased avoidance
behaviour, as consumers delay or abandon their purchase decisions to avoid regret or negative
consequences of making a suboptimal purchase (Luo et al., 2024 ; Mitchell et al., 2015) . In an online
shopping environment where information overload is more likely, this affects how consumers' cognitive
processes translate their purchase intentions into actual purchase behavior. Decision difficulty has a
direct impact on the final stage of the decision -making process and thus serves as a critical link for
connecting information overload to purchasing avoidance behaviour. Therefore, decision difficulty will
serve as an explanatory mechanism for how information overload creates purchasing avoidance
behaviour.
H9: Decision difficulty mediates the relationship between information overload and purchasing
avoidance.

purchasing behavior . In summary, information overload is hypothesized to increase consumer

Consumer confusion has a positive influence on purchasing avoidance (H4). Social media e xhaustion
has a positive influence on purchasing avoidance (H5). Decision difficulty has a positive influence on
purchasing avoidance (H6). Consumer confusion mediates the relationship between information
overload and purchasing avoidance (H7). Social medi a exhaustion mediates the relationship between

in the digital environment to consumer behavioral responses through empirically testable cognitive and
emotional pathways.

3. METHOD
This study applies Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM) within a
quantitative methodological framework, using SmartPLS 4. PLS -SEM is especially suitable for the
present investigation because it can simultaneously estimate complex structural relationships that
include hierarchical latent constructs, accommodate models with formative measurement
specifications, and maintain r obustness with moderate sample sizes  (Hair et al., 2019) . This study
applies a quantitative approach to examine the relationship between information overload and
purchasing avoidance, with the effects mediated by social media exhaustion, decision difficulty, and
consumer confusion among students at Jenderal Soedi rman University. The quantitative approach was
adopted because the study seeks to evaluate the proposed relationships among the variables using
empirical evidence collected through a questionnaire administered to respondents. The research
specifically targets students who actively engage with social commerce platforms, meaning they have
firsthand experience interacting with promotional content and making online purchases directly within
or facilitated by social media ecosystems . Data collection was carried out  at Jenderal Soedirman
University in Banyumas Regency, Central Java, Indonesia. The study sample was confined to students at
Jenderal Soedirman University due to practical considerations, including easier access, a large student
population that enabled the intended sample size to be achieved more rapidly, and a range of majors and
backgrounds that helped reflect differences in social media user b ehavior. The choice of this research
setting is grounded in the view that the student population is a group that uses social media intensively
and is engaged in online transactions, thereby making it suitable for testing the relationship between
information overload and purchasing avoidance through the mediation of social media exhaustion,
decision difficulty, and consumer confusion.

conducted using a purposive sampling technique. Specific inclusion criteria required respondents to be
active users of social media platforms and to have a history of searching for product information and
making online purchases within a social commerce environment. The purposive sampling technique was

5. CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS
The findings of this study indicate that information overload significantly increases consumer
confusion, social media exhaustion, and decision difficulty, and that these three internal conditions
strongly predict purchasing avoidance . Mediation analysis revealed that the effect of information
overload on purchase avoidance operates through these three parallel mediators, with bootstrap
contrast analysis confirming no statistically significant difference in their relative mediating stren gth.
These findings confirm that information overload in social media environments is not only directly
associated with users’ cognitive and emotional experiences but also strongly predicts avoidance
behavior that has the potential to reduce purchase conversion rates.  These empirical findings robustly
validate the application of the Stimulus -Organism-Response (SOR) theory (Jacoby, 2002; Mehrabian &
Russell, 1974). As hypothesized, platform-induced information overload (Stimulus) successfully triggers
concurrent states of cognitive and emotional depletion (Organism) , namely confusion, exhaustion, and
decision difficulty, which ultimately culminate in purchasing avoidance (Response). Furthermore, these
findings strengthen the literature on cognitive load and information processing by demonstrating how
dimensions of overload , such as volume, speed, complexity, and frequency , trigger activate mediating
mechanisms that underlie these behavioral tendencies.
The findings offer vital practical implications for digital retailers, e-commerce platform developers,
and MSME managers operating within data -saturated social commerce ecosystems. To mitigate
purchasing avoidance, managers must prioritize simplifying the digital choice architecture. This can be
achieved by eliminating redundant product variations and employing intuitive 'good -better-best'
assortment frameworks that naturally guide consumer choices without inflicting cognitive strain. Digital
marketers should also refine their communication strategies by avoiding the excessive bombardment of

well as conducting experiments to strengthen the evidence of causality. Future research could a lso
