Abstract
Background: Technological advancements and digitalization have sparked new shopping trends,
such as international personal shopper services. Batam is a city with significant potential for
these services. However, building customer trust is a major challenge, as most transactions are
conducted online, which can impact customer experience.
Purpose: This study aims to analyze the influence of electronic word of mouth (EWOM), social
media marketing, service quality, and brand image on customer trust and their impact on customer
experience among users of international personal shopper services in Batam.
Design/Methodology/Approach: This study employs an explanatory quantitative research
method with a purposive sampling approach. A total of 361 responses were collected; of these,
339 were deemed valid, having met the established criteria, and subsequently analyzed. Data
were gathered via an online questionnaire distributed through social media, using a five-point
Likert scale. IBM SPSS Statistics 27 was used for descriptive analysis, while SmartPLS-SEM
4.0 was used to evaluate the measurement and structural models.
Finding/Result: The results show that social media marketing (t=3.256), service quality (t=3.769),
and brand image (t=4.049) were found to be significant positive influences on customer trust. In
contrast, EWOM was found to have a positive but insignificant relationship with customer trust
(t=1.706). However, customer trust was found to have a positive and significant influence on
customer experience, with the strongest path coefficient among the relationships examined (t =
14.050).
Conclusion: The research findings indicate that social media marketing, service quality, and
brand image are significantly and positively associated with customer trust, whereas eWOM does
not show a significant relationship within the context and model of this study. Customer trust also
exhibits a positive and significant relationship with customer experience.
Originality/value (State of the art): This study extends previous research by integrating
marketing factors and conducting a comprehensive analysis of the relationship between
customer trust and customer experience within a single research model, while also examining the
context of international personal shopper services in Batam City, which has not been previously
investigated.

INTRODuCTION
The rapid advancement of technology has had a
significant impact on consumer behavior patterns and
habits. In today’s digital age, consumers can access
information, interact with others, and conduct buying
and selling activities online without having to visit
physical stores (Asrul, 2024). This provides convenience
for both consumers and producers in conducting
business transactions, leading to an increase in online
transactions in Indonesia (Sasabone et al. 2023). We
Are Social (2025) reported in April 2025 that Indonesia
has approximately 223.3 million active internet users.
On the other hand, approximately 191.4 million or
78.3% of the total population in Indonesia uses social
media. According to data from the Badan Pusat Statistik
(BPS), the value of online shopping or e-commerce
transactions in Indonesia reached Rp 487.01 trillion
in 2024. This figure represents a 7.3% increase from
2023 (BPS, 2025). Technological advancements, the
growing number of social media users, and shifts in
consumer behavior have sparked a new trend in the
economy called international personal shopper service
(Hidayat A. et al. 2022). International personal shopper
services help consumers obtain products from overseas
through individual intermediaries. Generally, these
businesses operate by leveraging the internet or social
media. (Sembiring et al. 2025).
International personal shopper services are popular
in major cities such as Batam (Suvia et al. 2022;
Yulfiswandi & Rosmawati, 2025). Batam’s
geographical location is highly strategic for operating
international personal shopper services (Suprapto &
Angelina, 2026). The city’s location, which is close
to countries such as Singapore and Malaysia, is a key
factor driving increased opportunities for international
trade (Anugrahini, 2025). Given Batam’s proximity to
several countries, operational costs and service prices
can be lower compared to other cities. Additionally,
Batam is a unique area featuring a free trade zone (FTZ)
that offers exemptions from import duties and taxes,
as well as streamlined investment licensing processes,
further reducing operational costs for businesses
(Azzahra et al. 2022). Consequently, international
shipping services in Batam have grown rapidly.
The business potential for international personal
shopper services in Batam is indeed very high, but this
industry comes with its own set of challenges. These
challenges must be overcome to prevent them from
disrupting business operations and sustainability. One
of the biggest challenges is how companies can build
customer trust so that consumers are willing to use
these international consignment services (Yulfiswandi
& Rosmawati, 2025). This is because nearly all
transactions in the international personal shopper
business are conducted online without any prior face-
to-face contact. Therefore, building customer trust will
be a crucial foundation (Ginting et al. 2023).
In general, customer trust can be influenced by or is
interrelated with several factors, such as electronic
word of mouth (EWOM), social media marketing,
service quality, and brand image (Kesumahati et al.
2025). These variables are closely related to building
customer trust. EWOM is reviews or information about
service providers circulating on social media or the
internet often shape consumers’ perceptions of those
providers (Lady et al. 2025). Meanwhile, Arifin &
Rahmat Syah (2023) stated that EWOM is the process
by which social media users share information with
the aim of conveying their experiences or knowledge
regarding a product or service they have used to other
social media users and build consumer trust (Arifin &
Rahmat Syah, 2023). This is a phenomenon resulting
from technological advancements. In traditional
cultures, information exchange between customers
still takes place through face-to-face interactions, and
the dissemination of information tends to be slower.
However, with rapid technological advancements,
information exchange increasingly occurs through
social media or the internet, where information spreads
much more quickly (Firman et al. 2021).
Social media marketing refers to the strategies used
by businesses to market their products or services
through social media. This process fosters interaction
between businesses and consumers (Kesumahati et al.
2025). In the digital age, the number of social media
users is on the rise. Consequently, using social media
as a platform for interaction can help businesses reach
a wider market. Consequently, sales can also increase
compared to before (Rachmat R & Ariyanti, 2023).
Meanwhile, Taufik H & Fiqri A (2023) argue that by
delivering content-driven marketing through the right
social media channels, trust and consumer perceptions
toward a service provider are gradually built. Therefore,
social media marketing can build customer trust.

Previous research has examined the relationship
between these factors and customer trust. For example,
Kesumahati et al. (2025) conducted an EWOM, social
media marketing, brand image, and service quality
as factors influencing customer trust in international
personal shopper services and found that all four
variables significantly influenced customer trust.
Other studies have also demonstrated the relevance of
EWOM, social media marketing, service quality, and
brand image in influencing customer trust and response
in the context of digital commerce (Le et al. 2024;
Pasaribu et al. 2022; Purba et al. 2023; Taufik H & Fiqri
A, 2023)   These findings suggest that building customer
trust in digital-based transactions is related to marketing
communications and customer perceptions of service
and brand factors.
However, several research gaps remain. First, previous
research on international personal shopper services
focused on identifying factors that influence customer
trust (Hidayat Arrosadi et al. 2022; Kesumahati et
al. 2025; Yulfiswandi & Rosmawati, 2025). While
customer trust is crucial in building relationships
between consumers and service providers, it can also
influence subsequent customer experiences. Research
in the broader e-commerce context has examined the
relationship between customer trust and customer
experience, but this research has generally focused
on traditional marketplace environments or digital
product purchases (Halim & Claudia, 2023; Pires et al.
2024; Suryadiningrat et al. 2022). Therefore, the role
of customer trust in shaping customer experience in
the specific context of international personal shopper
services remains relatively under researched.
Second, previous research findings regarding the
influence of EWOM on customer trust have not been
completely consistent. Some research finds a significant
relationship between EWOM and customer trust, such
as Kesumahati et al. (2025) and Le et al. (2024), while
others show different results depending on the research
context and respondent characteristics, such as Nur Idris
et al. (2023) and Lawone et al. (2026). This inconsistency
suggests the need for further empirical testing of the role
of EWOM in shaping trust, particularly in international
personal shopper services in Batam.
Service quality refers to the level of service provided by
companies. The better a company is at meeting customer
expectations, the more positive customers’ perceptions
of the company’s service quality will be, and the more
customer trust will be built (Pasaribu et al. 2022).
However, since service quality is heavily influenced by
consumer perception, companies and business owners
are required to meet the often-varying expectations of
consumers. Therefore, companies and business owners
must identify the appropriate target market and provide
clear and comprehensive information about the services
they offer (Purba et al. 2023). Providing consumers with
high-quality services influences their level of trust and
can deliver a positive experience.
Basrowi et al. (2023) stated that brand images refer to the
impression consumers have of a company or brand. This
impression can form after consumers see, read, hear, or
experience the products or services offered. It can also
be defined as the extent to which the company meets
consumers’ expectations and builds customer trust.
Therefore, the more fully consumer expectations are
met, the better the company’s brand image (Mandasari
et al. 2024). Building a strong brand image instills trust
and a sense of security in consumers when using a
service.
These four factors directly influence customer trust,
which ultimately impacts the overall customer experience
when using a service (Julianto & Kesumahati, 2024).
Meanwhile, Lawone et al. (2026) argue that customer
trust refers to the emotional response of consumers who
feel satisfied and get what they want. Customer trust
can also arise when customers decide to trust a product
based on external influences, such as the opinions
of others. The higher the level of customer trust, the
higher the customer expectations. Customer trust is
crucial for building customer experience and long-
term relationships between companies and consumers
(Longli & Kesumahati, 2023).
Hipólito et al. (2025) stated that customer experience is
the overall outcome of the interaction process between
consumers and producers, beginning with the first
encounter and extending through the postpurchase
phase. This experience can be both physical and
emotional. A consumer’s first impression can also
influence their experience with a product or service.
When a company provides services or products that
align with or even exceed consumer expectations, it
builds trust and enhances the customer experience.

METHODS
This research falls under the category of quantitative
research, employing an explanatory research approach
to analyze the relationships among EWOM, social
media marketing, service quality, brand image,
customer trust, and customer experience. An
explanatory research approach was chosen because this
study aimed to test theoretically derived relationships
between these variables, rather than simply describing
respondent characteristics (Sari et al. 2022).
Furthermore, PLS-SEM was used because the research
model comprises several latent constructs measured by
multiple indicators and involves the assessment of both
measurement and structural models. PLS-SEM is also
suitable for predictive-oriented research and models
with multiple relationships between latent variables
(Hair et al. 2021)
The population for this study consisted of all users of
international personal shopper services in Batam, the
exact number of whom is unknown. Therefore, this
study employed purposive sampling as its sampling
technique (Asrulla et al. 2023). Respondents were

that no single factor dominated the covariance structure
under this diagnostic (Hair et al. 2021).
PLS-SEM analysis consists of two main stages namely
measurement model evaluation and structural model
evaluation. Researchers use the measurement model
to assess construct reliability and validity, while
the structural model is evaluated to examine the
hypothesized relationships between constructs (Hair et
al. 2021).
The measurement model was assessed by examining
indicator reliability, reliability and validity testing, and
discriminant validity. Indicator reliability was assessed
using outer loadings. reliability was evaluated using
Cronbach’s alpha and composite reliability (rho_a).
Validity was assessed using average variance extracted
(A VE). Discriminant validity was evaluated using
the Fornell–Larcker, cross-loading, and heterotrait-
monotrait ratio (HTMT) (Hair et al. 2019).
Indicators were retained or evaluated based on their
loading values, along with the reliability and validity
of each construct. The evaluation was based not only
on individual loading thresholds but also on the overall
measurement quality of each construct. Therefore,
several indicators were removed because the overall
measurement scores did not meet the criteria: B5, CX4,

According to Hair et al. (2021), a research can be
considered free of common biases if the factors do not
explain more than 50% of the variance. Based on the
analysis using IBM SPSS Statistics 27 in Table 2, it can
be concluded that the data in this research is free from
common biases or errors. This can be seen from the
% of Variance value, which shows a result of 36.852,
which is less than 50%. Therefore, the research can
proceed with the analysis using SmartPLS-SEM 4.
Variance Inflation Factor (VIF)

According to Hair et al. (2019), an indicator’s VIF value
must be below 5 to be considered free from common
method bias. Table 3 shows that the data is free from
common method bias. This is evident from the VIF
values   for all indicators, which range from 1.113 to
1.617, with all variables having values   below 5.
validity and Reliability Testing
According to Hair et al. (2021), to conduct an outer
loading test, each indicator has a factor loading on its
variable. Indicators with loadings ranging from 0.40 to
0.708 or higher can still be used in a research. However,
if an indicator’s loading is lower than 0.40, it must be
removed from the model because it is deemed unable
to adequately represent or explain the model. Based
on the test results in Table 4, all indicators showed
values above 0.6. This indicates that all indicators met
convergent validity and could adequately describe the
model.
Meanwhile, in validity testing, each indicator must
explain its respective variable. If an indicator cannot
explain its variable, the research results will be
inaccurate. An indicator can be said to explain its
variable well if the A VE value for each variable is
greater than 0.5 (Hair et al. 2019). Based on Table 4,
it can be concluded that the indicator (questionnaire)
explains the variables well, or by more than 50%.
This can be seen in the results of the Average Variance
Extracted (A VE) values for each variable, which are
above 0.5.
In reliability testing, Cronbach’s alpha is the lower
bound, and composite reliability (rho_a) is the
upper bound for internal consistency reliability. The
minimum value for reliability is 0.6 and the maximum
is 0.95 for the results to be a good representation
(consistent and stable) (Hair et al. 2021). Based on
Table 4, the Cronbach’s alpha and composite reliability
(rho_a) values range from above 0.6 to below 0.95.
It can therefore be concluded that all questionnaire
items yielded consistent and stable responses. This
indicates that no responses deviated significantly
from the expected range. Additionally, the reliability
test assesses how accurately an indicator measures its
variable.

Goodness of Fit
The GoF test is conducted to determine whether the
model is sound or suitable for use overall. Ghozali
& Kusumadewi (2024) argue that the criteria for the
GoF test can be divided into three categories: weak
(≥ 0.10), moderate (≥ 0.25), and strong (≥ 0.36).  To
obtain the GoF value, the researcher used the formula
GoF = √Comm x R². Comm was calculated by finding
the average of the AVE, yielding a result of 0.527.
Meanwhile, R² was calculated by finding the average
of the R-Square, yielding a result of 0.529. These two
values were multiplied and then squared. The resulting
GoF value for this research model is 0.52, which is
above 0.36. Therefore, the research model is deemed
valid and can be used for hypothesis testing and
drawing research conclusions.

This study analyzes the influence of the relationship
between the variables of electronic word of mouth
(EWOM), social media marketing, service quality,
brand image, customer trust, and customer experience
on users of international personal shopper services in
Batam City. The results of the study show different
relationship patterns for each variable. One interesting
finding is that EWOM does not show a statistically
significant relationship with customer trust, although it
has shown a positive relationship. Meanwhile, social
media marketing, service quality, and brand image have
a positive and significant relationship with customer
trust. Furthermore, customer trust has a positive and
significant relationship with customer experience.
These findings indicate that the formation of trust in
international personal shopper services is not only
related to information originating from other consumers
but also to consumer perceptions of communication,
service quality, and the image of the service provider.
The Influence of EWOM on Customer Trust
The results of this study indicate that EWOM has a
positive path coefficient on customer trust, but the
relationship is not statistically significant. Therefore,
although the estimated direction of the relationship
is positive, the statistical results obtained are not
sufficient to conclude that EWOM is significantly
related to customer trust among the respondents in this
study. This indicates that H1 is not supported.
This finding is interesting to investigate further
because it differs from previous studies that found a
significant positive relationship between eWOM and
customer trust. For example, Kesumahati et al. (2025)
conducted a study in the context of international
personal shopper services in Batam City and found
that eWOM significantly influenced customer trust.
These differences in results indicate that the influence
of eWOM on trust does not always appear consistently
across all research contexts. Therefore, the presence of
information or reviews from other consumers does not
necessarily result in higher levels of trust.
Many factors may influence these differences in
findings. One possible interpretation is that the influence
of eWOM may depend on how consumers assess the
credibility, quality, relevance, and authenticity of the
information contained in the reviews. For example,
research conducted by Lady et al. (2025) found that
review quality is related to consumer trust in the
marketplace environment, while perceptions of fake
reviews can influence trust in the rating system. These
findings suggest that consumers do not always treat all
online reviews with equal weight and readily trust the
reviews provided.
This possibility is relevant to the characteristics of
international personal shopper services. In conventional
marketplace transactions, consumers can use various
information available on the platform, such as product
reviews, consumer ratings, transaction volumes, and
transaction protection mechanisms. Meanwhile, in
international personal shopper services, consumers
interact with intermediaries who help them obtain
products from abroad. Therefore, consumers not only
need information about the product but also need to
build trust in the parties carrying out the purchasing
and delivery processes.
In the context of international personal shopper services,
eWOM can be one of the initial sources of information
that consumers obtain, but consumers may also consider
information obtained through direct interactions with
the service provider. For example, consumers can gain
information through communication with the service
provider, responses to questions, clarity of transaction
steps, and their experience of using the service. This
is a possible interpretation, but not a mechanism that
was directly tested in this study. This study did not
specifically measure review credibility, review-reading
frequency, consumer behavior, or prior experience with
the service provider.
This finding also differs from several previous studies,
such as those conducted by Arifin & Rahmat S. (2023),

CONCuLSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS
Conculsions
This study aimed to analyze the relationship between
EWOM, social media marketing, service quality,
brand image, customer trust, and customer experience
among users of international personal shopper services
in Batam City. The results show that social media
marketing, service quality, and brand image have a
positive and significant relationship with customer
trust, while EWOM has a positive but insignificant
relationship with customer trust. Furthermore, customer
trust has a positive and significant relationship with
customer experience. The findings regarding social
media marketing, service quality, brand image, and
customer trust are generally consistent with previous
research, while the eWOM results are inconsistent
with several previous studies, including research in
the context of international personal shopper services.
These differences indicate that the relationship between
eWOM and trust can vary depending on the research
context and how consumers evaluate information
available online.
Recommendations
The theoretical framework of this study demonstrates
that the formation of customer trust in international
personal shopper services in Batam City involves
several marketing factors at varying levels of
relationship. Furthermore, the insignificant eWOM
finding indicates inconsistent findings and the need for
further research to examine factors such as credibility,
quality, authenticity, quantity, and source of reviews,
which were not measured in this study. Further research
is also recommended to expand the characteristics of
more diverse respondents, research areas, and consider
other variables that can explain customer trust and
customer experience, which are still limited in this
research.

personal shopper service transactions involve a
relationship between the consumer and the service

be confident that the service provider can fulfill their
agreed-upon requests at a reasonable cost. Thus, the
level of trust can be related to how consumers evaluate
the overall experience of interacting with the service

This finding aligns with the research of Hipólito et al.

experience. Therefore, this study extends this discussion

interpreted as evidence that high Customer Trust leads
to a better Customer Experience. This study used cross-

Experience that are not explained by Customer Trust
and may originate from other factors not included in
the research model.
Managerial Implications
These findings offer several practical implications for

important aspects to consider in building customer
trust. Service providers can maintain informative
and courteous communication through social media,

service delivery, and maintain a brand image that aligns
with the customer experience. Although EWOM did
not show a significant relationship with customer trust,
customer reviews can still be monitored and managed
