Abstract
This study examines the influence of social media influencer marke ting on Hotel Revenue per Available
Room (RevPAR) through the mediating role of purchase intention among hotel customers in Chennai.
Primary data were collected from 200 respondents using a structured questionnaire, and path analysis was
employed to test the hypothesized relationships. The findings reveal that Instagram (β = 0.517), Facebook
(β = 0.477), and Twitter (β = 0.671) significantly and positively influence customers' purchase intention.
Furthermore, purchase intention has a significant positive ef fect on RevPAR (β = 0.158), confirming its
mediating role between influencer marketing and hotel revenue performance. Among the direct effects,
Instagram significantly enhances RevPAR (β = 0.287), whereas Twitter does not exhibit a significant
direct influ ence on RevPAR (β = 0.012). The results suggest that influencer marketing, particularly
through Instagram, effectively increases customers' purchase intention, which subsequently contributes to
improved hotel revenue performance. The study provides valuabl e insights for hotel managers and
marketers in designing effective digital influencer strategies to maximize occupancy and revenue
generation in the competitive hospitality industry.

Keywords: Instagram, Facebook, Twitter, Influencer Marketing, Purchase Intention, Hotel Customers,
RevPAR.

Introduction
The hospitality industry has increasingly adopted influencer marketing as a strategic digital marketing
approach to enhance brand awareness, customer engagement, and financial performance. Hotels
collaborate with social media influencers to showcase their services, facilities, and unique guest
experiences through authentic and engaging content, thereby influencing travellers’ perceptions and
booking decisions. As travellers increasingly rely on online reviews and influencer recommendations,
influencer marketing has emerged as an effective promotional tool for increasing hotel occupancy and
ultimately improving Revenue per Available Room (RevPAR), a key performance indicator of hotel
profitability. In Chennai, a rapidly growing tourism and business destination, influencer marketing has
become particularly relevant due to the expanding use of social media platforms among domestic and

Revenue per Available Room (RevPAR) reflects the efficiency of hotel revenue management by
combining occupancy rates and average daily room rates into a single performance metric. While
traditional marketing channels remain important, digital influencer marketing provides hotels with an
opportunity to reach targeted audiences more effectively and build consumer trust through credible
endorsements. Influencers create personalized travel experiences that reduce information uncertainty and
encourage prospective guests to consider hotel bookings. Consequently, influencer-generated content has
become an essential determinant of customer engagement and hotel performance, particularly in
competitive hospitality markets such as Chennai, where travelers actively seek authentic recommendations
before making accommodation decisions (Sigala, 2018; Casalo, et al. 2018; Ki, et al. 2020; Gretzel, 2018).
Purchase intention serves as a critical mediating variable between influencer marketing and hotel RevPAR
because favourable influencer content often shapes consumers' attitudes, trust, and willingness to book
hotel services. The Theory of Planned Behaviour and consumer behaviour literature suggest tha t
marketing communications influence behavioural intentions, which subsequently translate into actual
purchasing behaviour. In the hotel sector, effective influencer campaigns enhance perceived credibility,
destination attractiveness, and service quality, thereby increasing booking intentions that contribute to
higher occupancy rates and improved RevPAR. Understanding this mediating relationship is particularly
valuable for hotel managers in Chennai seeking to maximize marketing return on investment while
strengthening long -term customer relationships in an increasingly digital marketplace (Ajzen, 1991;
Fishbein & Ajzen, 1975; Lim, et al. 2017; Sokolova & Kefi, 2020).
The present study aims to examine the influence of influencer marketing on hotel RevPAR whi le
investigating the mediating role of purchase intention among hotel customers in Chennai. The findings
are expected to contribute to hospitality marketing literature by integrating digital marketing strategies
with hotel financial performance indicators and consumer behavioural theories. Furthermore, the study
offers practical implications for hotel managers and marketing professionals in designing effective
influencer campaigns that enhance customer purchase intentions and improve revenue performance in the
highly competitive hospitality industry (Hudson , et al. 2016; Leung , et al. 2013; Xiang , et al. 2015;
Mariani, et al. 2018).

Research Gap
Although recent studies have established that influencer marketing can shape consumers’ attitudes,
purchase intentions, and hotel-booking decisions, limited research has examined its direct contribution to
hotel financial performance, particularly Revenue per Available Room (RevPAR). Existing hospitality
research largely focuses on influencer credibility, engagement, purchase intention, and booking intention,
while the mechanism linkin g influencer -generated persuasion to measurable hotel revenue remains
insufficiently explored. For example, Szymkowiak et al. (2021) examined social media influencers and
purchase intention in hospitality, whereas Alizadeh et al. (2026) identified continui ng gaps in hospitality
influencer-marketing research and called for greater investigation of mechanisms and outcomes.
Therefore, a significant research gap exists in empirically testing whether purchase intention mediates the
relationship between influencer marketing and RevPAR, particularly in the context of hotels in Chennai.
This study addresses this gap by integrating influencer marketing, purchase intention, and RevPAR into a
single empirical framework.

Statement of the Problem
The rapid growth of social media has made influencer marketing an important tool for hotels to attract
potential customers and shape their purchase decisions. However, despite increasing investment in
influencer promotions through platforms such as Instagram, Facebook, and Twitter, limited empirical
evidence explains how such marketing activities contribute directly to hotel Revenue per Available Room
(RevPAR). In particular, the role of p urchase intention as a mediating mechanism between influencer
marketing and RevPAR remains insufficiently examined. Therefore, the problem addressed in this study
is to determine whether influencer marketing significantly influences hotel RevPAR and whether purchase
intention mediates this relationship, with specific reference to the hotel industry.

Research Methodology
Research Design
The present study adopts a descriptive research design to systematically examine the role of influencer
marketing in driving hotel Revenue per Available Room (RevPAR) and to assess the mediating influence
of purchase intention among hotel customers in Chen nai. The descriptive design is appropriate because
the study seeks to describe customers’ perceptions and responses toward influencer -generated hotel
content and identify the relationships among influencer marketing, purchase intention, and RevPAR
without manipulating the study variables. Previous hospitality research has similarly examined influencer
marketing and consumer responses through empirical survey -based approaches, demonstrating the
relevance of descriptive investigation in understanding influenc er effects on hospitality decisions
(Bastrygina, et al. 2024; Choi, et al. 2024).

Explanation of Questionnaire Construction
The questionnaire for the study “Influencer Marketing as a Driver of Hotel Revenue per Available Room
(RevPAR): The Mediating Influence of Purchase Intention” was constructed as a structured instrument
comprising four major sections: demographic profile, influencer marketing, purchase intention, and
Revenue per Available Room (RevPAR). The demographic section contains seven items  to capture the
respondents’ background characteristics. Influencer marketing was operationalised through 15 self -
designed items covering three dimensions  Instagram Marketing, Facebook Marketing, and Twitter
Marketing with five items for each dimension. Th e items were framed using simple statements suitable
for hotel customers and measured on a Likert -type scale. The development of the influencer -marketing
items was guided by recent empirical research demonstrating the use of structured multi -item scales for
social-media influencer marketing and purchase intention.
The questionnaire further included five items measuring Purchase Intention and five items measuring
RevPAR, with the reported Cronbach’s alpha values of 0.90 and 0.80, respectively, indicating acceptable-
to-high internal consistency. For influencer marketing, the three dimensions obtained reliability

Population and Sample Size
The population of the present study comprises hotel customers in Chennai, pa rticularly customers who
have experience staying at hotels and are exposed to social-media-based hotel promotions and influencer
marketing. Hotel customers constitute an appropriate population because recent hospitality research
demonstrates that influence r-generated content can affect consumers’ engagement, booking intentions,
and purchase-related decisions in the hotel and tourism context (Bastrygina , et al. 2024; Nadzri , et al.
2024). From this population, a sample of 200 hotel customers will be selected for the study using a suitable
sampling technique. A sample size of 200 is considered adequate for conducting descriptive and inferential
statistical analyses in a questionnaire -based hospitality study and provides sufficient observations for
examining relationships among influencer marketing, purchase intention, and hotel performance. The use
of customer respondents is also consistent with recent hospitality studies that collect primary data directly
from consumers to examine the effects of influencer marketing on behavioural intentions (Yin, et al. 2024;
Nadzri, et al. 2024).

Tool for Data Analysis
Path analysis will be used as the principal statistical tool to examine the hypothesised relationships among
influencer marketing, purchase intention, and Revenue per Available Room (RevPAR). It extends multiple
regression by estimating the strength of direct and indirect relationships among variables and is
particularly appropriate for testing the mediating role of purchase intention in the proposed research model
(Streiner, 2005; Jollineau & Bowen, 2023).

Instagram records both the highest direct effect (β = 0.377) and indirect effect (β = 0.108), suggesting that
it is the most effective social media platform for converting influencer marketing efforts into consumers'
booking intentions and enhanced hotel finan cial performance. These findings support the theoretical
proposition that consumers' purchase intention is a crucial psychological mechanism linking influencer

The results further imply that hotel managers should prioritize influencer marketing strategies that
strengthen customers' purchase intentions, particularly through visually engaging platforms such as
Instagram. The stronger mediation effect observed for I nstagram suggests that rich visual content,
authentic influencer endorsements, and interactive communication significantly enhance travellers’
willingness to book hotel services, thereby increasing RevPAR. Conversely, Facebook and Twitter remain
valuable channels for building awareness and influencing booking decisions indirectly through purchase
intention, even though their direct contributions to revenue performance are comparatively weaker.
Overall, the findings reinforce the Stimulus–Organism–Response (S–O–R) framework, where influencer
marketing serves as the stimulus, purchase intention represents the internal psychological response, and
hotel RevPAR reflects the behavioural outcome. Therefore, investments in influencer collaborations
should emphasize content credibility, engagement, and consumer trust to maximize booking intentions
and improve hotel revenue performance. These findings are consistent with previous studies
demonstrating that social media influencers significantly affect consumers' purcha se intentions and
organizational performance in hospitality and tourism contexts (Casaló, et al. 2018; Jiménez-Castillo &
Sánchez-Fernández, 2019; Sokolova & Kefi, 2020; Leung, et al. 2019).

Managerial Implications
The findings provide important managerial implications for hotel managers seeking to improve RevPAR
through influencer marketing. Among the three social media platforms, Instagram (β = 0.517) and Twitter
(β = 0.671) have the strongest positive influence on purchase intention, followed by Facebook (β = 0.477).
This suggests that hotels should prioritize collaborations with credible influencers on visually engaging
platforms, particularly Instagram, while also leveraging Twitter for timely promotions and customer
engagement. Since purchase intention significantly influences RevPAR (β = 0.158), marketing managers
should focus on influencer -generated content that enhances trust, destination appeal, and booking
confidence. Campaigns featuring authentic reviews, immersive hotel experiences, and user -generated
content can effectively strengthen customers' intention to book, ultimately leading to higher occupancy
levels and increased room revenue.
The mediation results further indicate that purchase intention serves as an important mechanism through
which influencer marketing contributes to hotel performance. Instagram also demonstrates a significant
direct effect on RevPAR (β = 0.287), suggesting that investments in Instagram -based influencer
campaigns can generate both immediate revenue benefits and indi rect gains through increased purchase
intention. In contrast, Twitter does not exhibit a significant direct impact on RevPAR (β = 0.012),
indicating that its effectiveness lies primarily in influencing customer intentions rather than directly
increasing revenue. Therefore, hotel managers should adopt an integrated digital marketing strategy that

Conclusion
The findings of the study confirm that influencer marketing is a significant driver of hotel RevPAR, with
purchase intention acting as a meaningful mediator among hotel customers in Chennai. The path analysis
results reveal that Instagram (β = 0.517), Facebook (β = 0.477), and Twitter (β = 0.671) all have significant
positive effects on customers' purchase intention, indicating that influencer-generated content across these
platforms effectively enhances consumers' willingness to book hotel services. Furthe rmore, purchase
intention has a significant positive impact on RevPAR (β = 0.158), demonstrating its mediating role in
translating influencer marketing efforts into improved hotel revenue performance. Among the social media
platforms, Instagram also exerts  a significant direct influence on RevPAR (β = 0.287), highlighting its
effectiveness in driving both customer engagement and financial outcomes. However, the direct effect of
Twitter on RevPAR is not statistically significant (β = 0.012), suggesting that its influence is primarily
indirect through purchase intention rather than direct revenue generation. Overall, the study concludes that
hotels in Chennai should strategically invest in influencer marketing, particularly on Instagram and
Facebook, while focusing on campaigns that strengthen customers' purchase intentions, thereby improving
occupancy, bookings, and ultimately RevPAR.

Limitations and Future Research Direction
This study has certain limitations that should be acknowledged. First, the research was conducted using a
sample size of 200 hotel customers in Chennai, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to
other regions, hotel categories, or broader customer populations. Second, the cross -sectional research
design captures customer perc eptions at a single point in time and therefore does not establish causal
relationships between influencer marketing, purchase intention, and RevPAR. Third, the study relied on
self-reported responses, which may be subject to response bias and social desir ability bias. Additionally,
the research focused only on purchase intention as a mediating variable, while other factors such as brand
trust, customer engagement, perceived credibility of influencers, customer satisfaction, and eWOM were
not examined. Future research should consider larger and more diverse samples across multiple cities or
countries to enhance external validity. Longitudinal studies could provide deeper insights into the long -
term effects of influencer marketing on hotel performance. Furthe rmore, future studies may incorporate
additional mediators and moderators, compare different categories of influencers (e.g., mega, macro,
micro, and nano influencers), and investigate the role of emerging social media platforms and artificial
intelligence-driven influencer strategies in improving hotel RevPAR.
