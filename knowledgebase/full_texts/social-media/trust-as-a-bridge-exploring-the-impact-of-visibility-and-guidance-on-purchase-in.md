ABSTRACT
The rapid growth of e-commerce in developing countries has contributed to the rise of social commerce, a dynamic
platform that integrates economic transactions with social interactions. Considering the ongoing expansion of the
digital economy, social commerce has emerged as an essential tool for Malaysian firms to engage with Millennials,
who are the predominant consumers of social media. This study investigates the influence of affordance on the
purchasing intentions of Millennial consumers in Malaysia. It focuses on the independent variables of visibility and
guidance shopping, while also considering trust as a mediating factor in the intention to purchase. The study
employed PLS-SEM in analysing the influence of these characteristics on consumer behaviour in the field of social
commerce. In addition, this study was extended to examine heterogeneity among sub-samples of respondents
by gender, relative to their intention to purchase on social media platforms using multi-group analysis (PLS-MGA).
The results provide valuable insights for companies seeking to utilise social media platforms to enhance consumer
engagement and increase revenue. The findings emphasise the importance of fully utilising the capabilities of
social commerce.

1. INTRODUCTION
1.1 Research Background
In recent years, social commerce has emerged as a
power ful force in the retail landscape, revolutionising how
consumers interact with brands and make purchasing
decisions (Shukla, 2019). Social commerce, which
integrates social media platforms with e-commerce
functionality, enables users to shop directly through
social networks, blending the social experience with
online shopping convenience (Attar et al., 2022). This
paradigm shift is particularly evident in Asia, where
the convergence of digital and social media has
accelerated the growth of social commerce. (Chen &
Liu, 2022; Hajli, 2015; Huang & Benyoucef, 2013).

Since its early beginnings in the 2000s, social commerce
has undergone significant evolution, initially propelled
by social media platforms like Facebook and Twitter that
used social interactions and user-generated content
to enhance sales and brand loyalty (Attar et al., 2022).
Between 2010 and 2015, the development of platforms
like Instagram, along with the rise of mobile internet,
accelerated this growth, particularly in Asia, where
digital adoption was increasing (Eferin et al., 2019).
In Malaysia, social commerce began to take hold in
the early 2010s as local businesses experimented with
Facebook and Instagram for marketing and direct
sales. By 2015-2020, platforms such as Shopee and
Lazada, which integrated social features like product
reviews, high-quality visuals, and guided shopping
experiences, played a crucial role in shaping Malaysia’s
social commerce landscape by enhancing the overall
shopping experience and influencing purchase
decisions. Currently, extensive mobile internet usage
and the widespread popularity of social media are
fuelling expectations that social commerce in Asia will
reach approximately $247 billion by 2027 (Hou et al.,

2. LITERATURE REVIEW
2.1 Social Commerce
In Malaysia, where over 85% of internet users are
active on social media, the emphasis is increasingly on
enhancing product visibility through high-resolution
videos, interactive images, and detailed product
descriptions, which help create a more engaging and
informative shopping experience (Kim & Park, 2013;
Bourg et al., 2023) through the e-commerce platform.
Additionally, platforms have introduced guidance
shopping features in which sellers provide direct
guidance and support throughout the buying process,
helping consumers make informed decisions (Huang &
Benyoucef, 2013; Sun et al., 2019; Saffanah et al., 2023).
Platform-specific trends, such as TikTok’s live shopping,
enable customers to view products in real-time and
interact with sellers, making the shopping experience
more dynamic and transparent (Bhisana et al., 2024).
Similarly, social media platforms such as Instagram and
Facebook have embedded features that enable users to
explore products through videos and shoppable posts,
further streamlining the purchasing process (Carah et
al., 2023). Overall, social commerce in Malaysia and Asia
is evolving rapidly, driven by the need for better product
visibility and guided shopping experiences that cater
to consumers' evolving preferences.
The continued integration of enhanced product displays
and interactive shopping guidance within social media
platforms underscores the importance of adapting
to technological innovations to meet consumer
expectations and improve purchase intentions. Social
commerce is a new form of electronic commerce
that emerged from interactions among consumers on
social media platforms. This new commercial platform
also enables consumers to influence others’ consumer
behaviour and purchase decisions through social
interactions (Yang et al., 2021; Chen et al., 2024).
As a result, consumers on social commerce platforms
often make purchase decisions based on information
and feedback from other users (Hajli & Sims, 2015; Zhang
& Benyoucef, 2016; Yusuf et al., 2018; Hongsuchon & Li,
2022; Wang et al., 2023). Social commerce enhances
companies' marketing performance by enabling
them to communicate and interact with their target
audiences (Bahcecik et al., 2019). In particular, the
underlying social interactions enhance the quality
of customer relationships (Hajli, 2013) and increase
consumers' buying intentions (Ismagilova et al., 2019).
As a result, social commerce enables companies to
promote their brands and products on online social
platforms by investing in the quality of their relationships
with potential customers.
2.2 Visibility
Visibility affordance refers to the extent to which a
product is made noticeable and demonstrable to
consumers through digital platforms (Dong et al., 2016).
In social commerce, increased product visibility plays a
critical role in shaping consumer purchase intentions. The
prominence of a product on online platforms enhances
consumers’ ability to discover, evaluate, and interact
with it. Empirical research in live-streaming commerce
indicates that visibility, together with guided shopping,
significantly influences consumers’ purchasing decisions
(Sun et al., 2019). Furthermore, the usability of a platform,
particularly in terms of how easily users can locate
and explore a product, reflects the perceived ease
of use and has a direct impact on consumer attitudes
and purchase intentions (Majeed et al., 2024). When
digital interfaces are well-designed and optimised
for clear product presentation, they contribute to a
more seamless shopping experience and increase
the probability of purchase. According to a previous
study by Tandon et al. (2021), visibility, formed through
observing peer usage and exposure to marketing
communications such as advertisements, influences
consumers' intentions to use food delivery applications
by mediating the effect on their consumption values.
Therefore, visibility is commonly used as an independent
variable in research that examines consumer behaviour.
Goi et al. (2026) emphasise that online visibility is essential
for ensuring brand discoverability and relevance in a
highly saturated digital environment. Collectively, these
studies underscore visibility affordance as a strategic
mechanism that enhances consumer engagement and
purchase behaviour in online commerce.
H1: Visibility has a positive relationship with purchase
intention.
2.3 Guidance Shopping
Guidance shopping affordance refers to the platform’s
ability to deliver personalised, collaborative services
that help consumers find products that match their
preferences. This affordance is typically enabled
through technological features that offer tailored
recommendations and expert advice, facilitating a
more efficient and satisfying shopping experience
(Dong et al., 2016). In live-streaming commerce,
interactive elements such as real-time question-and-
answer sessions and detailed product comparisons
increase consumer engagement. These features help
customers feel more informed and confident in their
choices, which positively influences their intention to

2.4 Trust in Social Commerce
For customers to engage positively and purchase
products, trust is crucial. Trust is the positive credibility
customers perceive when shopping online. According
to Cheskin Research and Studio Archetype/Sapient
(1999), consumers navigate through three stages
of trust in online transactions. Initially, control over
personal data and information regarding search results
establishes trust. The second stage focuses on securing
data integrity, which is essential for linking trust to both
extrinsic and intrinsic buying intentions. Successful
security maintenance completes the trust-building
process. Furthermore, trust is characterised as a general
belief in the ethical and socially appropriate behaviour
of the other party in a social exchange, with a focus on
their ability, integrity, and benevolence (Gefen et al.,
2003; Hwang & Kim, 2007). Online trust encompasses
consumers’ perceptions of a site’s capability to provide
accurate information and meet expectations, along
with impressions of the firm’s intentions and system
quality (Bart et al., 2005). Research indicates that
consumer trust in commerce, both online and offline,
extends to multiple entities, including the company,
seller, product, and the market/channel (Komiak &
Benbasat, 2004).
Moreover, Williams (2018) highlights that baseline trust
and varying levels of trust in seller-advisors significantly
influence consumer choices. The findings indicate
that seller-advisors can effectively steer consumers
towards more expensive options. However, disclosing
a seller-advisor’s incentives after a decision has been
made may lead to consumer backlash. Trust levels
may also fluctuate based on the interaction stage
between buyer and seller (McKnight et al., 2004). The
integration of livestreaming enhances social presence
and interaction, thereby improving the shopping
experience, reducing uncertainty, and fostering
greater trust in sellers (Hajli, 2015). Customers and sellers
develop affective trust, often referred to as emotional or
relational trust (Guenzi & Georges, 2010; Lewis & Weigert,
2012), through emotional connections. Effective product
presentation via social media involves descriptions,
visuals, and videos, which can positively influence
consumer intention and trust in vendors. While many
studies link consumer intention with trust in sellers, few
examine trust in products. Notably, Wongkitrungrueng
and Assarut (2020) and Chandrruangphen (2022)
emphasise the importance of product trust, with the
former suggesting that affective trust is related to
emotional evaluations of product characteristics.
Chandrruangphen (2022) further demonstrates that
product trust directly enhances customer intentions to
engage with livestreaming on social media platforms,
ultimately leading to purchases.
H3: Trust in social commerce mediates the relationship
between visibility and purchase intention.
H4: Trust in social commerce mediates the relationship
between guidance and purchase intention.

3. METHODOLOGY
This explanatory and quantitative study aims to explore
and predict the relationship between individual and
organisational variables and the dependent variable,
w h ich i s pu rchase i ntention among m i l lenn ia l s i n Ma laysia,

review, a framework and hypotheses were developed
to test the relationship between these variables. A

involves two stages, the measurement model and
the structural model, as advocated by Anderson
and Gerbing (1988). Additionally, this work employed
nonparametric multigroup analysis (PLS-MGA) to identify
significant factors across subsamples that may affect
relationships among the variables under investigation
(Hair et al., 2017). Consequently, SmartPLS version 3.9.2
was employed to examine the structural model and to

4.1 Multi-Group Analysis (MGA)
To further enrich the analysis, this study examined
potential subgroup differences in the dataset,
particularly by gender, by comparing responses from
male and female participants. The objective was to
assess whether gender functions as a moderating
variable in influencing Purchase Intention within the
context of Social Commerce (Henseler et al., 2016; Hair
et al., 2021a). In doing so, the study employed Partial
Least Squares Multi-Group Analysis (PLS-MGA), a robust
non-parametric technique suited to evaluating group-
specific differences in path coefficients, especially when
distributional assumptions cannot be met (Henseler et
al., 2009; Hair et al., 2017).
The rationale for utilising PLS-MGA lies in its ability to
provide statistical validation of observed heterogeneity
across sub-samples through bootstrapped significance
testing. By conducting gender-based comparisons,
the analysis aims to ensure the model’s generalizability
and minimise potential biases arising from sample
composition. As such, PLS-MGA facilitates a nuanced
understanding of whether structural paths differ
significantly between male and female respondents.
Table 6 outlines the comparative findings, including path
coefficient estimates, p-values, and t-statistics derived
from the non-parametric bootstrapping procedure
(Hair et al., 2017; Ramli et al., 2019). The first comparison
between male and female groups yielded a negligible
difference in path coefficients of -0.011, with a non-
significant p-value of 0.922 (two-tailed), indicating no
meaningful gender-based divergence. A similar pattern
was observed in the second analysis, which yielded a
path coefficient difference of -0.004 and a p-value of
0.988, indicating no statistical significance.
These outcomes collectively suggest that gender does
not exert a moderating influence on the relationships
within the structural model. The absence of significant
differences in path estimates supports the conclusion
that Purchase Intention in Social Commerce contexts
is similarly shaped for both male and female users,
despite potential individual differences in behaviour.
Consequently, the model exhibits cross-gender
consistency, thereby strengthening its predictive power
across demographic segments. Equivocally, Tengli
and Srinivasan (2022) reported that purchase intention
and behaviour did not differ significantly by gender,
suggesting that male and female consumers exhibit
similar purchasing tendencies.

5. CONCLUSION
This study investigated the influence of guidance
shopping and visibility on purchase intention among
Malaysian Millennials in social commerce, with trust as
a mediator and gender as a moderator. Findings clarify
the interplay of social commerce features and trust in
shaping consumer behaviour in digital marketplaces.
First, guidance shopping and visibility both had a
positive and statistically significant direct influence on
purchase intention. The structural model explained
41.4 percent of the variance in intention. These results
reinforce prior research showing that enhanced product
visibility and interactive support features improve
consumer engagement and conversion in social
commerce environments.
Second, trust in social commerce significantly mediated
the relationships. Both the indirect paths from guidance
shopping and visibility to purchase intention via trust
were statistically significant, indicating complementary
partial mediation. This corresponds with broader
evidence that consumer trust amplifies the effect of
platform features on purchasing decisions. Third, no
significant gender differences were observed in the
Multi-Group Analysis. Male and female respondents
exhibited similar path coefficients and non-significant
p-values, implying that gender does not alter how
guidance, shopping, visibility, and trust influence
purchase intention. This finding supports cross-gender
generalisability of the structural model. The results of
this study reveal that gender does not significantly
moderate the relationships between guidance,
shopping, visibility, trust, and purchase intention. This
suggests that the structural model demonstrates cross-
gender generalisability, where the influence of these
key predictors on purchase intention remains consistent
across male and female consumers.
These findings suggest that gender does not significantly
influence purchase intention across different consumer
contexts. The results further indicate that both male and
female consumers demonstrate similar behavioural
patterns in forming purchase intentions. Overall, the
study highlights the consistency of the proposed model
across demographic groups, reinforcing its robustness
and generalisability in explaining consumer behaviour
in social commerce settings, particularly in relation
to Visibility and Guidance Shopping. This reinforces
the notion that marketers and platform designers can
prioritise universal strategies to enhance purchase
intention, while remaining mindful of potential gender-
specific responses in more targeted contexts.
Theoretically, this study contributes by integrating trust
as a key mediating construct linking affordability-
related functionalities to consumer purchase decisions
in social commerce. This expands the literature,
aligning with recent reviews that underscore trust as a
central antecedent of purchase intention. In practice,
the evidence suggests that firms should optimise
guidance features and visibility while simultaneously
implementing trust-building measures, such as user
reviews, transparent policies, and secure transactions, to
convert social engagement into purchasing behaviour.
In conclusion, trust emerges as a crucial mechanism
that enhances the effectiveness of social commerce
features. Firms that invest in visibility and guidance, but
fail to cultivate trust, are unlikely to maximise purchase
intention. This insight echoes findings that trust in social
