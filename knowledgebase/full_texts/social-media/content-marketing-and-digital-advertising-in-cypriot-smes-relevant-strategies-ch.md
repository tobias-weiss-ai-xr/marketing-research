# Content Marketing and Digital Advertising in Cypriot SMEs: Relevant Strategies, Challenges and Opportunities

SMEs: Relevant Strategies, Challenges and Opportunities

Abstract: This empirical study  seeks to examine the role of content marketing and digital advertising within Cypriot small
and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) by essentially exploring the degree to which these businesses utilise digital marketing
tools. In addition, it delves into the relevant challenges faced by these enterprises located in different regions in Cyprus and
the outcomes gained of implementing such digital strategies. More specifically, this research highlights the  significance of
these advanced strategies in a rapidly evolving business landscape. A qualitative research method was
applied interviewing owners and business executives via semi -structured in -depth interviews. The findings reveal that
while the majority of  the firms investigated by the research team have incorporated social media, email marketing, and
search engine advertising into their strategies, a gap in strategic planning, budget allocation, and performance measurement
is traced, respectively. Most of the them have focused on ad hoc tactics within their digital marketing endeavours, a fact that
strategically showcases limitations with regard to their potential for sustained growth and competitiveness, especially when
it comes to long-term goal achievement. However, interviewees underscored the considerable opportunities that effective
content marketing and targeted advertising may provide with r egard to customer loyalty, brand visibility, and sales. The
study wraps up by recommending certain key structured digital marketing strategies, suggesting ways to improve training
for social media managers, and pushing for better access to resources and analysis tools.
Keywords: Digital Marketing, Content Marketing, Digital Advertising, SMEs, Cyprus
1. Introduction
In developing countries, SMEs contribute to 40% of the GDP (Thaha  et al., 2021). Technology is advancing day
by day, and these businesses have more opportunities  (Lykourentzou et al., 2026;  Petropoulos., 2025),
especially through digital marketing. Digital marketing allows them to promote products and services in  a
timely, personalized and cost-effective way (Baines et al., 2013).  Digital marketing also helps SMEs move from
building brand awareness to making sales and better meeting customer needs.  In this regard, their ability to
learn and adapt is important for future growth (Pradhan et al., 2018).  Two of the most prominent digital
marketing practices are content marketing and  paid digital advertising. Content marketing is based on the
production of consistent and valuable content to raise brand awareness by using video blogs and social media
(Cheung et al., 2020; Leung et al., 2022; Shahab et al., 2021; Stubb et al., 2019).  The success of content
marketing is based on the distribution of information through social media networks and search engine
optimization (Feng et al., 2021; Cheng et al., 2020). On the other hand, digital advertising helps businesses reach
their target audience by using measurable techniques to drive website traffic and raise brand awareness, while
providing ongoing optimization to improve the return on investment (Apostolopoulos et a., 2025;  Blake et al.,
2015; Buhalis & Mamalakis, 2015; Goldfarb, 2014).  However, for SMEs, especially in resource -constrained
environments, it is important to make strategic decisions in terms of how to allocate budgets for organic content
versus advertising (Kotler et al., 2021). Previous studies have  investigated some aspects of SEO, SEM, and
content-based engagement (Panchal et al.,  2021; Ahmad et al., 2022), although limited research has been
conducted to compare the long -term effect of content marketing versus the immediate gratification of
advertising for SMEs. There is also limited research conducted while considering recent advances in AI -based
personalization, user-generated content, influencer marketing, and video advertising.
This study aims to examine how SMEs can use content marketing and paid digital advertising, including PPC, to
achieve maximum promotional impact. This will be achieved by studying their meanings, characteristics, and
uses, as well as how SMEs select betwee n them depending on their respective
industries, audiences and subsequently, resources. The research will also seek to understand how SMEs may
benefit from the integration of these strategies in order to increase their effectiveness, awareness, and loyalty,
as well as how they may make the most of emerging digital marketing trends in different industries with different

2. Literature Review
The international literature has been interested in Content Marketing in SMEs (Feng et al., 2021; Ho et al., 2020).
Content marketing is often described as “the strategic creation and distribution of valuable content to attract,
engage and retain clearly defined audiences” (Holliman & Rowley, 2014). However, it is also differentiated as
having a focus on trust and relationships rather than content use in more general terms (Mathew & Soliman,
2020). The interest in content marketing for SMEs comes in two for ms: cost savings in media and brand assets
that grow exponentially (Lou & Xie, 2021; Ahmad et al., 2017). The practical application for firms includes using
various content forms (videos, blog posts, white papers, infographics, etc.) for raising brand awareness and
stimulating demand (Tiago & Verissimo, 2014). Social platforms are central distribution rails which link content
quality to visibility and engagement (Ho et al., 2020).  Content marketing in SMEs involves valuable content and
distribution, and in such cases, the importance of SEO and social media is particularly high (Feng et al., 2021).
For high-performing content marketing, content formats need to be adapted to audience needs and consistency
is also important (Shahab et al., 2021; Cheung et al., 2020). The development of a defined strategy and content
alignment with the marketing funnel and measurement systems establish the foundation for content marketing
success (Cheng et al., 2020; Lee et al., 2018). SMEs typically activate a strategy that includes search, social,
display, and email (Simonetti &  Bigne, 2024; Vitalis et al., 2023). Search engine marketing (SEM), integrating
search engine optimization (SEO) and pay -per-click (PPC) strategies, has the potential to enhance visibility and
attract hi gh-intent traffic, as Lee (2018)  observed. Combining SEO and PPC with content marketing have a
competitive advantage and progressively greater benefits, according to Rohimi (2024). Furthermore, display
inventory, encompassing banners and rich media content, enables ad networks to broaden their reach; however,
the efficacy of banners hinges on their ability to initially capture audience attention, a point emphasized by
Drèze and  Hussherr (2003). Simonetti and  Bigne (2024) also discovered that variations in format and the
incorporation of motion elements can help with interaction.
Looking at different online marketing practices, social media ads promote awareness and consideration and
are utilised mainly for their accurate targeting capabilities (Vitalis et al., 2023). Native
marketing operates through various formats which function within editorial environments but businesses need
to maintain transparency for preserving their audience's trust according to Manic (2015).  Additionally, display
and retargeting operations increase customer retention through high relevance but the practice of showing too
many ads leads people to develop "ad blindness" which decreases their effectiveness and reduces their return
on investment (Manchanda et al., 2006). Video ads can raise attention and intent when placement, duration,
and relevance are well-optimized (Vaiciukynaite et al., 2017). Email serves as an essential CRM tool which helps
businesses keep their customers but people tend to resist when they see direct persuasion messages; research
shows that providing information through educational methods produces better results (Thomas et al., 2022;
Campbell &amp; Kirmani, 2000). Platform analytics (CTR, CPA)  enable granular optimization (Kireyev et al.,
2016). A multi-touch approach to customer journey analysis helps businesses understand their campaigns better
because customers base their decisions on multiple touchpoints instead of single interactions (Kireyev et al.,
2016). When considering the variety of platforms, YouTube can increase brand awareness among younger age
groups, but it requires creative content that grabs attention, such as eye -catching thumbnails ( Febriyantoro,
2020). TikTok can allow content to spread quickly through short videos, particularly for Gen Z (Dwinanda et al.,
2022). Meta properties support engagement and measurable outcomes when content resonates and is well
targeted (Sugeng & Chusjairi, 2022; Baek & Morimoto, 2012). Because organic reach has tightened on major
platforms, paid support is often necessary to seed distribution, even for content that has potential to perform
(Ho et al., 2020). The effectiveness of social media channels is im proved through adaptation of content to the
unique features of each platform and how users interact with them (Apostolopoulos et al., 2025; Lee et al., 2018;
Mangold & Faulds, 2009).

3. Methodology
Given the study’s aim, to understand how Cypriot SMEs perceive and apply content marketing and paid digital
advertising, we adopted a qualitative research design. Qualitative inquiry privileges depth over breadth, seeking
to surface meanings, interpretations, and lived experiences that are not easily reduced to numbers (Gittins et
al., 2025). It is particularly suitable in the social sciences for interpreting human experience and context,
which reflects to Dilthey’s interpretive tradition (Makkreel, 2014). It has also been recommended for unpacking
complex social, economic, and entrepreneurial environments (Neergaard & Ulhøi, 2007) and is well aligned to
entrepreneurship scholarship exploring complex phenomena (Van Burg, 2022). Accordingly, a qualit ative,
interview-based approach was judged fit for purpose. We employed convenience sampling to  facilitate access
and cooperation (Moser & Korstjens, 2018), while ensuring participants were “information-rich cases” suitable
for in-depth analysis (Patton, 2002). Inclusion criteria focused on owners or marketing -responsible staff within
SMEs who were actively involved in digital promotion (content and/or ads). Targeted recruitment of participants
sharing relevant roles and exposure supports deeper interpretation of the focal phenomenon (Makris et al,
2024).
The final sample comprised 14 SMEs operating across all districts of the Republic of Cyprus (Nicosia, Limassol,
Larnaca, Paphos, Famagusta), spanning sectors such as food service, advertising, tourism services, apparel,
wellness/medical, legal, IT, retail,  real estate/hospitality, media, and financial services. Participants were
owners/founders or marketing leads (including in- house and hybrid arrangements with agencies/freelancers).
Interviews were conducted in April–May 2025, either face -to-face or via on line/telephone, modes considered
as valid as in-person for qualitative interviewing (Sturges & Hanrahan, 2004). Most interviews were conducted
in Greek, with two in English. Average duration was ~30 minutes. All participants received an information sheet,
provided written informed consent, and opted for anonymity. We used pseudonyms (R1– R14) and removed
identifying details to protect confidentiality (Orb et al., 2001). Recruitment ceased at thematic saturation (Fusch,
2015). Furthermore, data were gathered through semi-structured, in-depth individual interviews; an established
and effective method in qualitative research (Apostolopoulos et al., 2019; Flick, 2018). Semi -structured
interviews balance a consistent core protocol with flexibility to probe emergent issues, enabling authenticity and
nuance in participants’ accounts (Kvale & Brinkmann, 2009). Confidentiality and anonymity principles were
rigorously applied throughout (Orb et al., 2001).
We used qualitative analysis to interpret participant experiences, perceptions, and practices. An abductive
stance combined inductive and deductive procedures. Inductively, open coding allowed concepts
to emerge from the data without imposing  a priori fram es, revealing new topics and meanings grounded in
participants’ language (Bennett et al., 2019; Sousa, 2014; Morse, 1994). Deductively, we also applied a
sensitizing framework derived from the literature review and research questions to organize data aroun d
theoretically relevant constructs and to enhance validity (Bell & Waters, 2018). We also iterated between our
emergent themes and the literature to position findings and identify gaps (Okoli, 2023).
Pattern identification thus proceeded both bottom-up, themes arising directly from data, independent of prior
theory (Frith & Gleeson, 2004) and top-down, guided by pre-selected concepts and prior studies (Hayes, 1997).
Such abductive blending is widely used to strengthen primary qualitative research (Azungah, 2018). Four
overarching themes structured the analysis and later results: content marketing use, digital advertising use,
comparative and integrated approaches, constraints and future strategies.

4. Findings
4.1 Current Usage of Content Marketing in SMEs
Cypriot SMEs actively combine content marketing with paid ads, using formats such as carousels, images, short-
form video (Reels, TikTok), Stories, newsletters, blogs, and occasional webinars. Content mixes educational tips,
seasonal offers, testimonials an d behind the scenes material. Instagram and Facebook are most used in B2C,
LinkedIn in B2B, YouTube for long-form content and TikTok for younger audiences. The posting strategies of
different organizations show distinct patterns because some companies use planned schedules which include
weekly and monthly content themes while others share content based on random opportunities.

“We follow a monthly content calendar, with weekly blog articles and 3 –5 social media updates per
week” (R10).
“We also use Reels and TikTok videos and send newsletters to previous guests with offers” (R7).
“We publish social media posts featuring the projects our agency has run and other behind- the-scenes

also write articles on our own platform and publish them on other well-known platforms. (R12)”
4.2 Paid Advertising Practices
Google Search Ads are preferred for high-intent conversions, while Meta (Facebook/Instagram) supports reach
and retargeting. LinkedIn is used selectively for B2B but seen as costly in Cyprus. Ad management is  mainly in-
house, with occasional consultant input; monthly budgets range from €200 to €3,000.
“Our goal is best served by Google Ads because there is search intent. They bring the best ROI” (R3).
“We use Facebook and Instagram Ads because they give us easier access to the local audience” (R4).
4.3 Challenges Faced
The main constraints are limited budgets, time, and  expertise, compounded by algorithm changes and larger
competitors. Many SMEs struggle to balance marketing with other responsibilities.
“The main challenge is lack of time, since the person handling marketing has other responsibilities” (R4).
“Limited budget for advertising and advanced tools and difficulty competing with larger teams” (R10).
4.4 Measurement and Outcomes
SMEs track their business performance through GA4 and platform analytics systems and their CRM software
which they use from time to time. Core KPIs include CTR, CPC, conversions, and ROI/ROAS. The conversion rates
for Search Ads perform well while video co ntent helps brands reach more people and get better engagement.
The production of organic content establishes trust between businesses and their audience yet it fails to reach
large audiences when companies do not pay for promotion.
“We monitor CPC, CTR, conversion rate and ROI to assess performance” (R10).
“Google Analytics, Instagram Insights, and CRM help us track leads and campaign performance” (R8).
4.4.1 Effectiveness of Organic vs. Paid
Participants view organic and paid as complementary. They believe that organic builds trust and long- term
engagement, while paid ensures visibility and immediate results.
“Ads bring immediate results, whereas organic helps with branding and audience
retention” (R4).
“Both are essential, organic content has very little reach without paid advertising” (R1).
4.5 Case Patterns and Strategy Shifts
B2B businesses use LinkedIn and SEO for their marketing efforts which they schedule using organized calendars.
Local B2C businesses use short-form videos and geo-targeted Meta ads (Facebook and Instagram adverts). Many
SMEs have shifted from boosting posts  to structured, full -funnel campaigns, with budgets split differently by
sector (e.g., 60-70% organic vs. 30-40% paid or heavily paid-focused for performance targets).
“Instead of simply boosting posts, I moved to creating more structured campaigns with specific goals
and better targeting” (R5).
“We have adjusted our strategies to place emphasis on video -first content and to follow changes in
social media algorithms” (R14).

5. Discussion
Our results align closely with established work showing that SMEs benefit most when they treat content and
paid media as complements rather than substitutes. International guidance (Tiago & Veríssimo, 2014) frames
content as the long-term engine of trust and search visibility, while paid channels supply controllable reach and
short-term demand capture, precisely what our participants described. The growing video- first emphasis and
the use of short-form formats mirror global best practices, as do the reporte d gains from Search Ads for high-
intent traffic and Meta for broad reach and retargeting. Best- practice playbooks also stress measurement
discipline and iterative optimisation (clear KPIs, A/B testing, funnel tracking). Here, Cypriot SMEs show progress,
using GA4, platform analytics, and in a few cases CRM, yet they still trail the more mature “data- driven”
benchmarks seen internationally. Finally, our findings that  organic visibility now often depends on paid
amplification indicates the need for broader industry observations and practitioner surveys.
In Cyprus’s small and relationship -driven market, limited  budgets and lean teams shape SME marketing
choices. In this light,  Meta and Google Resource constraints drive reliance for their cost control
and targeting and LinkedIn’s higher CPMs restrict its us e to selective B2B goals. The execution of campaigns
faces obstacles because business owners must handle daily operations while marketing activities continue and
their lack of skills in analytics and video production and PPC advertising hinders their work.  The natural decline
of organic reach becomes more severe for accounts which have fewer following because businesses need to pay
for advertising to keep their online presence in the current environment. SMEs experience multiple advantages
from their custom er accessibility, well- established community relationships and genuine business approach
which makes human -centered storytelling, UGC and micro- influencer collaborations most effective.
Organizations which want to grow through data- based methods need to establish CRM systems and GA4
platforms for their operations.
6. Conclusion
Digital marketing is shown to be integral to SME growth. Cypriot SMEs in our sample recognise marketing as a
core function and are investing in digital tools, though adoption  remains limited by budgets,
skills gaps and organisational resistance (Peter & Vecchia, 2020; Sharabati et al., 2024).
Moreover, the main discovery shows that content and paid advertising function as complementary elements
which work together. Content serves to build audience trust while it establishes enduring value which paid
media delivers instantaneously through its re ach and performance metrics. Specifically, organizations selected
Google to reach customers who showed strong purchase intent but they used Meta for broad audience exposure
and follow-up advertising and LinkedIn served specific business-to-business marketing needs. Short-form video
was shown to be the most effective format, supported by other media. Authentic, human -centred storytelling
drove engagement (Lopes et al., 2022), with trends such as AI -assisted creation, UGC and micro -influencers
expected to develop as trends.
Measurement practices are improving, with GA4 and platform analytics common, though analytical depth is
constrained by resources. SMEs combining up to date content with targeted paid campaigns and KPI tracking
reported growth in awareness, traffic and thereafter, sales.
Taking everything into account, SMEs in Cyprus are leaning toward hybrid approaches. Paid media offer speed
and broad reach, while organic content builds trust. However, more precise planning, meaningful upskilling and
established processes (such as conten t calendars) are required for further development. This study
contributes to digital marketing literature by  emphasising how small-market enterprises may align consistent,
