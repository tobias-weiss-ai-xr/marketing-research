Abstract
Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) play a key role in economic growth, particularly
in Southeast Asia, yet many face financial, human, and technological constraints that
limit marketing capability and sustainable competitive advantage. This systematic
literature review examines hybrid marketing strategies in SMEs by analyzing how market
orientation and entrepreneurial orientation support sustainable competitive  advantage.
Following the PRISMA 2020 protocol, searches were conducted in Scopus for studies
published between 2020 and 2025. From 5,739 records, 23 studies met the inclusion
criteria and were synthesized thematically. The findings show that SMEs increasi ngly
combine conventional marketing practices with digital tools, including social media, e -
commerce, customer relationship management, analytics, and digital branding. Market
orientation strengthens customer responsiveness, while entrepreneurial orientati on
supports innovation and strategic adaptation. Together, both orientations help SMEs
build resilience and competitiveness, particularly when supported by digital readiness,
managerial capability, and platform access. However, the review is limited by database
coverage, the exclusion of grey literature, and the geographic concentration of available
studies. Future research should expand regional diversity and examine the long -term
effects of hybrid marketing strategies on SME performance.

Keywords : digital marketing; entrepreneurial orientation; hybrid marketing; market
orientation; sustainable competitive advantage.

INTRODUCTION

Small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) have long been recognized as critical
drivers of economic growth, significantly contributing to employment creation and gross
domestic product (GDP) enhancement (Becha et al., 2025; Gherghina et al., 2020; Qi et
al., 2025). Globally, SMEs represent the backbone of many economies; for instance, in

Southeast Asia, SMEs contribute approximately 40 -60% to GDP and account for up to
70% of employment (Islam, 2021 ). Despite their substantial economic roles, SMEs
frequently encounter critical challenges related to resource limitations, including
financial, human, and informational constraints, that impede their marketing capabilities
and strategic effectiveness.
In recent years, the context in which SMEs operate has dramatically shifted due to
accelerating globalization, rapid technological advancement, and the increasingly
volatile market conditions exacerbated by the COVID-19 pandemic (Ganlin et al., 2021;
Klein & Todesco, 2021). Consequently, SMEs face intensified competition and complex
consumer dynamics that demand innovative and adaptive marketing strategies. Several
researchers have underscored the centrality of effective marketing practices in ensuring
SMEs’ survival and su stainable competitive advantage (Chooset & Sukhabot, 2025 ;
Song & Wang, 2024 ; Tariq, 2025 ). However, despite substantial interest in SME
marketing, there remains an evident paucity of comprehensive, systematic reviews that
clarify the contemporary state of research in this domain.
The marketing landscape for SMEs is further complicated by the disruptive
influence of digitalization and the ongoing shift toward knowledge -driven economies
(Bargoni et al., 2024 ; Bawa et al., 2025 ; Hokmabadi et al., 2024 ). The proliferation of
social media platforms, e -commerce ecosystems, and data analytics tools has created
both unprecedented opportunities and new competitive pressures for SMEs (Adeola N.
Raji et al., 2023; Costa & Castro, 2021; Huang & Kumarasinghe, 2024; Loo et al., 2024).
While some SMEs have embraced digital marketing to expand market reach and engage
customers innovatively, many remain constrained by inadequate digital literacy, limited
access to technological infrastructure, or misalignment between digital tools and thei r
strategic objectives (Abdurohim et al., 2025 ; Oladipupo & Adesunkanmi, 2024 ). This
uneven adoption raises questions about which marketing approaches deliver value for
SMEs and which merely add complexity without sustainable returns.
Additionally, cultural, institutional, and sectoral  contexts influence SME
marketing practices. For instance, SMEs in emerging economies may rely more heavily
on informal networks and relationship -based marketing, while those in advanced
economies increasingly adopt data -driven, analytics -based strategies (Alkalha et al.,
2025). This diversity suggests that a one-size-fits-all framework is insufficient; a nuanced
synthesis is needed to capture how SMEs adapt their marketing to local and global forces
simultaneously.
Current literature predominantly approaches SME marketing from fragmented
angles, focusing on isolated topics such as entrepreneurial marketing (Breit & Volkmann,
2024; Felicetti et al., 2024; Koziol et al., 2025), market orientation (Manafe et al., 2024),
or digital marketing adoption (Figueiredo et al., 2021; Su et al., 2023). Such fragmented
analysis leaves critical gaps regarding a holistic understanding of SMEs’ marketing
strategies and implementation mechanisms. Therefore, an overarching synthesis that
systematically reviews existing studies across strategic and operationa l marketing
dimensions within SMEs is crucial.
This study aims to address this gap by conducting a systematic literature review
encompassing various marketing dimensions ranging from market orientation and
information management to strategic and operational marketing processes in SMEs. Key
research questions guiding this inquiry include:
RQ1. How do SMEs conceptualize and implement market orientation in response to
evolving market conditions?

RESEARCH METHOD
This study adopts a Systematic Literature Review (SLR) methodology to
synthesize existing research on marketing strategies in Small and Medium Enterprises
(SMEs). The SLR approach was selected because it provides a transparent, replicable,
and comprehensive framework for identifying, evaluating, and synthesizing prior studies
(Marzi et al., 2025; Paul et al., 2021). The review follows the PRISMA 2020 (Preferred
Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses) protocol (Page et al., 2021)
to ensure methodological rigor, minimize bias, and enhance the reliability of findings.

Data Sources and Search Strategy
The primary database for this review was Scopus, selected for its broad coverage
of peer -reviewed publications in business, management, marketing, and
entrepreneurship. The search was conducted on July 28, 2025, covering publications
from January 2020 to July 2025. The review instruments consisted of three components:
the search strategy, the coding sheet, and the quality assessment checklist. The search
strategy was used to identify relevant studies, the coding sheet was used to extract
comparable information from each included article, and the quality assessment checklist
was used to evaluate methodological and topical relevance. The coding sheet included
information on author, year, title, journal, country or region, research design, SME
sector, marketing strategy focus, theoretical orientation, key findings, and relevance to
market orientation, entrepreneurial orientation, and sustainable competitive advantage.
The search strategy applied Boolean operators across the title, abstract, and
keyword fields. The search terms combined SME -related terms, marketing strategy
terms, and operational marketing terms: (“small and medium -sized enterprises” OR
“SMEs” OR “SMB” OR “small businesses”) AND (“marketing strategy” OR “strategic
marketing” OR “market orientation” OR “entrepreneurial marketing” OR “branding” OR
“digital marketing” OR “pricing” OR “promotion” OR “distribution”). The search was
limited to peer -reviewed journal articles and conference papers written in English and
published between 2020 and 2025. To strengthen the validity of the search string, a

Quality Assurance Process
A multi -stage quality assurance process was applied throughout the SLR to
safeguard methodological rigor and maintain the integrity of the review process (Han et
al., 2020; Khayat et al., 2025 ; Pollmeier et al., 2025 ). Two reviewers  independently
screened the titles and abstracts using the predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria.
Although a formal inter-reviewer agreement statistic was not calculated during the initial
screening process, consistency was strengthened through the use of a shared  screening
guide, standardized eligibility criteria, and consensus -based discussion. Any
disagreement between reviewers was discussed until a final decision was reached, and
unresolved cases were checked by a third reviewer.
At the full-text review stage, each article was assessed against the same eligibility
criteria, with particular attention to its relevance to SME marketing strategies, research
design, empirical basis, and contribution to the review questions. Data extract ion was
conducted using a structured coding sheet covering study metadata, methodological
characteristics, marketing strategy dimensions, and key findings. The extracted data were
cross-checked by the review team to reduce coding errors and ensure consistency across
the included studies. Although percentage agreement and Cohen’s kappa were not
reported, the use of independent screening, consensus resolution, third -reviewer
checking, standardized coding, and cross -checking procedures provided procedural
safeguards to support the transparency and reliability of the final synthesis.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

Conceptualization and Implementation of Market Orientation in SMEs (RQ1)
In response to evolving market conditions, small and medium enterprises (SMEs)
increasingly adopt dynamic market orientation (MO) strategies. These strategies are
conceptualized around three foundational pillars: customer orientation, competitor
awareness, and cross-functional coordination. Rather than applying a uniform approach,
SMEs tailor their MO practices based on their geographic, digital, and organizational
contexts, demonstrating flexibility in aligning business operations with customer n eeds
and market shifts. Drawing synthesis from 23 empirical studies (see Table 3), it is evident
that SMEs conceptualize and implement market orientation (MO) by integrating both
traditional and digital approaches. A common thread across these studies is the emphasis
on customer-centric strategies, competitor monitoring, and internal co ordination as the
core pillars of MO.

 CONCLUSION, LIMITATIONS, AND SUGGESTIONS

Conclusion
This systematic literature review synthesized current research on marketing
strategies in Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs), with particular attention to market
orientation, entrepreneurial orientation, and sustainable competitive advantage. Based on
the 23 studies included in the final synthesis, the findings show that SMEs use a
combination of conventional and digital marketing practices to respond to competitive
pressure, changing customer behavior, and resource constraints.  The reviewed studies
indicate that social media marketing, e -commerce, customer relationship management,
digital branding, and data-supported customer engagement have become important tools
in SME marketing practice. However, these findings should be inter preted as patterns
identified across the reviewed literature, not as evidence of a year -by-year temporal
trend.
The review also shows that market orientation and entrepreneurial orientation play
complementary roles in strengthening SME competitiveness. Market orientation
supports customer responsiveness, competitor awareness, and market intelligence, while
entrepreneurial orientation supports innovation, proactiveness, and strategic adaptation.
Together, these orientations help SMEs improve resilience and competitive positioning,
especially when supported by managerial capability, digital readiness, and access to
suitable platforms. At the operational level, branding, communication, and distribution
appear as relatively strong areas in the reviewed studies, whereas pricing remains less
developed due to continued reliance on intuition, limited analytical tools, and weak
pricing systematization.
This review contributes to the SME marketing literature by integrating strategic
and operational marketing dimensions into a single synthesis. It also highlights several
gaps that require further research, particularly pricing strategy, digital capability
development, platform dependency, and the long -term performance effects of hybrid
marketing practices. For policymakers and SME support institutions, the findings suggest
the need for practical programs that strengthen digital literacy, data -informed decision-
making, and structured marketing capabilities. Future studies should expand the
geographic scope of evidence, include more diverse SME sectors, and apply longitudinal
or comparative designs to examine how hybrid marketing strategies influence sustainable
competitive advantage over time.

Limitations
Despite the comprehensive scope of this systematic literature review (SLR) on
marketing strategies in Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs), several limitations must
be acknowledged. First, the review is limited by the geographical focus of the studies
included. Although global in scope, a significant portion of the research centers on SMEs
in Southeast Asia and developed economies, potentially limiting the generalizability of
findings to SMEs in emerging or underrepresented regions. Additionally, the study
focuses only on peer -reviewed journals and reputable conference proceedings from

specific databases, which may introduce publication bias by excluding grey literature,
such as industry reports, white papers, and unpublished studies, which could provide
practical insights from real-world SME experiences.
Another limitation lies in the methodological diversity of the studies included. The
review encompasses empirical, conceptual, and experimental research, yet
methodological variation across these studies may lead to data quality and reliability
inconsistencies. For instance, studies employing qualitative methods may provide rich,
context-specific insights but lack generalizability, whereas quantitative studies may
overlook the nuances of SME behavior in different markets. Moreover, the review was
conducted using studies published only from 2020 to 2025, meaning that insights from
earlier foundational research may be underrepresented, which could affect the
development of a comprehensive historical perspective on SME marketing evolution.
Furthermore, the review focuses primarily on strategic and operational marketing
practices within SMEs, which might not fully capture the complexity of marketing in
these organizations. Although mentioned, several factors, such as organizational culture,
leadership styles, and external institutional influences, are not explored in depth,
potentially limiting the understanding of how these factors interact with marketing
strategies. Lastly, relying on secondary data from existing studies without primary data
collection could introduce biases inherent in the original research methodologies,
affecting the overall interpretation of SME marketing practices. These limitations
underscore the need for further research, including broader data sources, more diverse
geographic and sectoral contexts, and a more granular exploration of the internal and
external factors influencing SME marketing strategies.

Suggestions for Future Research
Future research should expand the scope to include SMEs from underrepresented
regions and industries, as this would help address the limitations associated with
geographic and sectoral  biases in the current literature. A deeper exploration into the
internal dynamics of SMEs, such as organizational culture, leadership styles, and
innovation capabilities, would provide a more holistic understanding of how these factors
intersect with marketing strategies. Additionally, further research could investigate the
long-term impacts of hybrid marketing strategies and the effectiveness of specific digital
tools, especially for smaller, resource -constrained SMEs. Collecting primary data to
validate and enrich the secondary data findings would also be valuable, offering a more
practical, real-world perspective on implementing marketing strategies in SMEs. Lastly,
exploring the role of sustainability and social responsibility in SME branding and
marketing would offer timely insights into how these enterprises can align their
marketing practices with broader societal and environmental concerns, fostering long -
term sustainability and brand loyalty.
