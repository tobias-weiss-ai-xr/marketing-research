# Platform-Generated Cues in Live-Streaming Commerce: An Empirical Study from an Emerging Market

Financial Research Review

Financial Research Review. 2026, 1(1). https://doi.org/10.66348/frr.26.v1.n1.a51 https://spiderpublishing.com/frr

Platform-Generated Cues in Live-Streaming Commerce: An
Empirical Study from an Emerging Market
Mahmud Wahid1,*, Huam Hon Tat2, M. Nasir Shamsudin1
Abstract: This study investigates how platform -generated, non-human cues influence consumers’
online shopping behavior in live -streaming social commerce. Drawing on Signaling Theory and
Cue Utilization Theory, the study examines the effects of live -streaming functio nality, customer
response speed, promotional tools, follower count, and online reviews in the context of Bangla-
desh’s emerging social commerce market. Data were collected through an online questionnaire from
157 social commerce users in Dhaka, Bangladesh, and analyzed using Partial Least Squares Struc-
tural Equation Modelling (PLS-SEM) with SmartPLS. The findings show that promotional tools and
follower count have significant positive effects on online shopping behavior, while live streaming
significantly increases follower count. The mediation analysis further confirms that follower count
fully mediates the relationship between live streaming and online shopping behavior. However,
customer response speed and online reviews do not significantly influence online shopping behav-
ior, and online reviews do not mediate the relationship between promotional t ools and behavior.
These findings suggest that visible social proof and promotional stimuli may exert stronger behav-
ioral influence than functional or review -based cues in live -streaming commerce. The study con-
tributes to social commerce and influencer mar keting literature by showing how platform -gener-
ated cues shape consumer behavior in an emerging market where digital trust remains contextually
fragile.
Keywords: Live-streaming commerce; Platform-generated cues; Follower count; Promotional tools;
Online shopping behavior; Social commerce; Bangladesh

1. Introduction
With the proliferation of user -generated content on social media, individuals can
emerge as prominent creators by actively generating and sharing personal narratives and
product reviews. These individuals are termed “social media influencers” (Freberg et a l.,
2011; Khamis et al., 2017; Lim et al., 2017). Social media influencers have garnered significant
interest from companies and brands, serving not only as potential marketing conduits but
also as valuable social relationship assets for collaboration. This dynamic can foster endur-
ing relationships centred on marketing and sales (Augustine, 2019).
Bangladesh provides a relevant emerging-market context for examining live-stream-
ing and social commerce because of its expanding digital user base and growing depend-
ence on social media platforms for online buying and selling. Recent industry data show
that Bangladesh had a rapidly expanding online population, while DataReportal reported
that Facebook’s advertising reach was equivalent to 34.3% of the country’s total population
and 77.1% of the local internet user base in January 2025 (DataReportal, 2025). By late 2025,
Facebook’s advertising reach had further increased to the equivalent of 36.3% of the national
population, indicating the platform’s continued centrality in Bangladesh’s digital commu-
nication and commerce ecosystem (DataReportal, 2026). These figures suggest that social
media platforms are no longer used only for interpersonal communication; rather, they in-

2. Literature Review
2.1 Social Media Marketing
Despite being content -driven, social media influencer marketing mirrors celebrity
endorsements in mass media (Lou and Kim, 2019). Audience involvement exceeds celeb-
rity endorsement (Arora et al., 2019). The traditional system uses unidirectional transmis-
sion, so followers can't respond to celebrities' statements. Social media influencers pro-
mote parasocial relationships with followers via limited two-way communication, includ-
ing comments and answers. This remains PSR because to two-way communication limits,
which would not appear as broadly or powerfully as in actual social engagement. Thus,
social media influencers are distinct ( Belanche et al., 2020). Client complexity has in-
creased with digital technology. Digital and social media marketing helps companies
achieve their marketing objectives at low cost (Ajina, 2019). Due to the decline of tradi-
tional communication and societal dependenc y on physical establishments, corporations
must use digital and social media marketing techniques to preserve and grow market
share. Companies must assess existing marketing approaches (sales, profitability, growth
rate, customer satisfaction and loyalty) and creative marketing tactics and consumer value
propositions (value, brand, and relationship equity) (Kannan and Li, 2017). An interdisci-
plinary and cross -functional process that uses social media —often in conjunction with
other communication channels —to achieve organisational goals by creating value for
stakeholders has been proposed as a new definition of social media marketing (Felix et
al., 2017).
2.2 Live Streaming
Live streaming has become an important mechanism in social commerce because it
combines product demonstration, real -time communication, and audience participation
within a single digital environment. Unlike static e -commerce pages, live-streaming ses-
sions allow sellers and influencers to present product features, demonstrate usage, answer

questions, and respond to consumer concerns immediately (Li et al., 2020). From an infor-
mational perspective, live streaming reduces product uncertainty by allowing consumers
to observe product appearance, functionality, and usage conditions more vividly t han
through text or image -based descriptions alone. Such informational value is particularly
important in online shopping contexts where consumers cannot physically inspect prod-
ucts before purchase.
In addition to informational value, live streaming also creates social interaction ef-
fects. Viewers can observe the comments, likes, questions, reactions, and participation of
other users during the broadcast. These visible audience activities may generate  a sense
of social presence and collective engagement, which can influence how consumers evalu-
ate the seller, product, or influencer. Prior research suggests that real-time interaction and
audience participation can strengthen perceived engagement and enco urage viewers to
remain connected with the seller or influencer (Tsai & Pai, 2013; Li et al., 2020). Therefore,
live streaming may influence consumer behavior not only by providing product -related
information but also by creating a socially active environment where consumers interpret
participation as a cue of popularity, trust, and market acceptance.

2.3 Customer Response Speed
Customer response speed refers to the promptness with which sellers, influencers, or
page administrators respond to consumer enquiries, comments, complaints, or purchase-
related requests in social commerce settings. In online environments, where consumers
cannot physically meet sellers or inspect products before purchase, response speed can
function as a service -related cue that reflects seller attentiveness, reliability, and opera-
tional readiness. Prior studies suggest that timely responses in digital interactions can im-
prove customer trust, perceived service quality, and satisfaction, which may subsequently
influence purchasing behavior and loyalty (Li et al., 2019; Sheng, 2019; Primaputri &
Hasyim, 2023).
Within the present conceptual model, response speed is treated as a functional plat-
form-based cue rather than an emotional or social cue. Its expected influence on online
shopping behavior is based on the assumption that consumers interpret quick replies a s
evidence of seller responsiveness and lower transaction risk. However, in live -streaming
commerce, response speed may compete with more visible and socially persuasive cues
such as follower count, promotional offers, and live audience activity. Therefore , while
fast responses may improve service perceptions, their behavioral effect may depend on
whether consumers prioritize functional efficiency or social proof during purchase deci-
sion-making.
2.4 Promotional Tools
Promotional tools refer to marketing instruments used by sellers, brands, or influ-
encers to stimulate consumer attention and encourage purchase -related responses. In so-
cial commerce, these tools may include discounts, coupons, giveaways, limited -time of-
fers, bundle deals, free delivery, sponsored posts, and live -session-exclusive promotions.
These promotional instruments are especially common in influencer-led and live-stream-
ing commerce because they can create urgency, increase product visibility, and moti vate
consumers to act during or shortly after a promotional exposure (Vigna & Mainardes,
2019; Patmawati & Miswanto, 2022).
It is important to distinguish promotional tools from their outcomes. Promotional
tools are the strategic inputs used by marketers, whereas outcomes such as brand aware-
ness, engagement, sales, and online reviews represent consumer or market responses to
those inputs. Prior research indicates that well -designed promotional strategies can in-
crease consumer interest and encourage purchase behavior, particularly when consumers
perceive the offer as valuable, time -sensitive, or socially endorsed (Agustian et al. , 2023;
Kumar et al., 2024). In social commerce environments, promotional tools may also stimu-
late online review activity because consumers who are exposed to attractive offers may be

more likely to comment, share feedback, or participate in post -purchase discussions.
Therefore, this study conceptualizes promotional tools as platform -enabled marketing
stimuli that may directly affect online shopping behavior and indirectly shape consumer-
generated review activity.
2.5 Follower Count
In influencer marketing, follower count influences customer engagement and cam-
paign efficacy. Leung et al. (2022) found that larger follower numbers promote credibility,
authority, customer trust, and user involvement. Studies demonstrate a complicated in-
verted U-shaped association between follower numbers and engagement, which fans but
does not exhaust engagement. Thus, although moderate follower numbers may increase
audience engagement and client loyalty (Walsh et al., 2024; Wies et al., 2022), large counts
might seem fake and lessen the effect. The number of actual or false followers may also
affect marketing performance (Pittman & Abell, 2021; Liu & Di, 2023). When cho osing
influencers, marketers must consider engagement quality as well as following numbers.
Evania et al. (2023) Such an understanding will help build influencer connections and im-
prove marketing efficiency.
2.6 Online Review
Influencer marketing uses online reviews to impact customer opinions and pur-
chases. Positive online customer reviews and influencer endorsements boost consumer
trust and buy intent (Zhang et al., 2020; Kordrostami, 2022). Consumers use online re-
views to ev aluate product quality and reliability (Fachmi & Sinau, 2022; Wilis & Faik,
2022). However, positive and negative variables complicate things. Positive remarks boost
customer confidence, whereas negative ones lower it (Fachmi & Sinau , 2022; Ullal, 2021).
The proximity of internet reviews to promoted items greatly affects influencer marketing
efficacy for firms seeking favourable results. Appealing to internet reviews may raise mo-
rale and persuade (Kordrostami et al., 2022). Market man ipulating brands must assess
their activities strategically. They strive to improve customer involvement via influencer
marketing (Wilis & Faik, 2022; Sharma & Kumar, 2023).
Overall, the reviewed literature suggests that platform generated cues operate
through different mechanisms in social commerce. Live streaming offers informational
value and social interaction cues. Response speed is a functional service responsiveness
cue. Promotional tools are marketer -controlled stimuli. Follower count is visible social
proof. Online reviews are consumer -generated evaluative feedback. These cues relate to
the more general concepts of trust, credibility, and engagement, but are not concep tually
identical. Some cues reduce uncertainty by providing information, others by influencing
consumers through popularity, urgency, or collective validation. Therefore, this study in-
vestigates these non -human cues as distinct but related antecedents of o nline shopping
behaviour in the live streaming social commerce.
3. Theoretical background and hypothesis development
3.1 Theoretical Foundation
In digital and social commerce environments, consumers often make purchase deci-
sions under conditions of uncertainty. They may not be able to physically inspect prod-
ucts, verify seller credibility, or directly evaluate service quality before purchase. Unde r
such conditions, consumers rely on observable cues available on the platform to infer un-
observable qualities such as seller reliability, product attractiveness, market acceptance,
and transaction safety. To explain this process, the present study integrates Signaling The-
ory and Cue Utilization Theory.
Signaling Theory explains how observable indicators help reduce information asym-
metry between sellers and consumers (Spence, 1973; Connelly et al., 2011). In social com-
merce, sellers, influencers, and platforms provide visible signals that consumers may us e
to assess credibility and reduce perceived risk. In this study, follower count and online

reviews are treated as market-based signals because they reflect visible audience size, col-
lective evaluation, and prior consumer experience. A high follower count may signal pop-
ularity and social approval, while online reviews may signal the perceived qua lity, relia-
bility, or acceptability of a product or seller. Live -streaming activity may also function as
a visibility signal because it demonstrates seller presence, product availability, and active
engagement with consumers.
Cue Utilization Theory complements this perspective by explaining how consumers
use available cues as shortcuts when intrinsic product qualities are difficult to evaluate
directly (Cox, 1967; Olson & Jacoby, 1972). In this study, response speed, promotiona l
tools, follower count, and online reviews are conceptualized as extrinsic heuristic cues.
Response speed may act as a cue of seller attentiveness and service readiness. Promotional
tools, such as discounts, giveaways, limited-time offers, and free delivery, may act as cues
of value and urgency. Follower count may serve as a popularity cue, while online reviews
may operate as evaluative cues that help consumers simplify purchase decisions.
Although the two theories are related, they explain different aspects of consumer in-
ference. Signaling Theory emphasizes how observable indicators communicate credibility
and reduce information asymmetry, whereas Cue Utilization Theory emphasizes how
consumers use these indicators as decision shortcuts  (see Table A1 in the Appendix) .
Therefore, the present study conceptualizes platform-generated cues as non-human infor-
mational inputs that influence online shopping behavior either directly or indirectly. Spe-
cifically, live streaming, response speed, promotional tools, follower count, and online
reviews are examined as visible platform -based cues that may shape consumer behavior
in Bangladesh’s live-streaming social commerce context.
3.2 Hypotheses Development
The correlation among live streaming capabilities, following metrics, and customer
behaviour is becoming more significant in influencer marketing studies. Live streaming
offers a dynamic medium for real -time interaction, enabling influencers to display ite ms

live broadcasting cultivate a feeling of urgency and connection, which are essential factors
in shaping consumer choices.
Furthermore, follower count is a vital factor in assessing the efficacy of live streaming

The connection between promotional tools and how people buy things has been a big
focus of marketing research. This shows how important it is to know how various types

reputation and adversely influence purchasing choices (Lii et al., 2023). The interaction
between promotional tools and online reviews underscores the need for firms to equili-

purchasing behaviour and online sentiment.

4. Data and Methodology
This study primarily examines the influencer marketing paradigm in Bangladesh,
which is mostly characterised by Facebook marketing, or F -Commerce. Facebook has the
most substantial consumer traffic among internet platforms in this country. The introduc-
tion of Facebook live streaming capabilities has led to a significant increase of online re-
tailers using this tool to showcase their items. Given the substantial population of this
nation, over 170 million, a significant portion utilises Face book and Messenger as their
preferred social networking sites (SNS). The launch of the Live Streaming function has
garnered quick success in the industry. The live streaming feature allows advertisers to
present their items online with real -time connection and immediate conversation. Con-
sumers may see the streaming content even if they miss the live broadcast, since a rec-

orded version is accessible on the vendor's portal. The marketers provide logistical help
that facilitates product ordering. In this setting, several individuals with strong commu-
nication skills, as well as those previously famous on Facebook and YouTube f or their
personal films, have gained popularity in product sales. Marketers, sellers, and influenc-
ers maintain their own Facebook pages and groups to facilitate communication with their
audiences more effectively.
This study adopted a survey-based research design to examine the influence of plat-
form-generated cues on consumers’ online shopping behavior in live -streaming social
commerce. The questionnaire consisted of 34 self-report measurement items and eight de-
mographic and screening items. The study measured six constructs: live streaming func-
tionality (LIV), customer response speed (RES), promotional tools (PRM), follower count
(FLO), online reviews (REV), and online shopping behavior (BEH). All construct items
were measured using a five -point Likert scale ranging from 1 = strongly disagree to 5 =
strongly agree. The measurement items were adapted from previously validated scales in
the social commerce, influencer marketing, online review, and digital consumer behavior
literature. Minor wording adjustments were made to ensure contextual relevance to Bang-
ladesh’s Facebook-based social commerce environment.
To enhance content validity, the questionnaire underwent a multi -stage validation
process. First, the original English version was translated into Bangla to improve respond-
ent comprehension. The Bangla version was then reviewed by two academicians and two
industry experts with knowledge of social commerce, digital marketing, and consumer
behavior. Their feedback focused on wording clarity, contextual appropriateness, con-
struct relevance, and the suitability of the items for Bangladeshi online shoppers. Base d
on their recommendations, several items were refined to improve clarity and contextual
fit. Before the main data collection, the questionnaire was also checked for readability,
logical flow, and completion time. This process helped ensure that the instru ment was
understandable, contextually appropriate, and suitable for the target respondents.
Because no complete sampling frame exists for Bangladeshi consumers who pur-
chase products through influencer -led live -streaming and Facebook -based social com-
merce, this study used purposive sampling followed by snowball sampling. Purposive
sampling was appropriate because the study required respondents who had prior experi-
ence with online shopping and exposure to social media influencers or live -streaming
commerce. A screening question was included at the beginning of the online question-

5. Data Analysis and Results
The present study employed Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling
(PLS-SEM) using SmartPLS software to test the proposed conceptual framework. The
choice of PLS-SEM was guided by its robustness in handling complex models that include
multiple constructs, indicators, mediation, and moderation effects. According to Hair et
al. (2011) and Henseler et al. (2016), PLS -SEM is particularly suitable when your research
aims to make forecasts and create some theory but not confirm the entire theory. In par-
ticular, it corrects the latent variable (measured variab le) errors in the observed variable
models and is vital to the validity and reliability of the constructs.
The PLS-SEM method is also applicable to models involving interrelated independ-
ent and dependent variables, allowing researchers to a single method to more effectively
explore causal relationships. Two essential stages in analysis --the measurement model
and the structural model --are combined in this technique. Therefore, it supports a rigor-
ous examination of both the reliability of indicators and the validity of constructs as well
as hypothesized relationships within one comprehensive framework. It also bri ngs new
relationships to light and enriches the theoretical understanding of emerging research
fields as a method of Al-Emran et al. (2019) pointed out.

5.2 Common Method Bias
Given that the data for this study were collected through a self -administered ques-
tionnaire, it was essential to examine potential common method bias (CMB) to ensure the
validity of the results. Following the recommendation of Podsakoff et al. (2003), two di-
agnostic methodologies were employed: the correlation matrix procedure and the full col-
linearity test. The inter-construct correlation matrix was first analysed to identify any cor-
relations exceeding the significant threshold of 0.90, which could indicat e a potential risk
of shared method variance (Kline, 2011). The identified correlations among the latent var-
iables ranged from 0.605 to 0.799, with the most robust correlation observed between Re-
sponse speed and Promotional tools (r = 0.799). Since all val ues remained within the es-
tablished limits, it can be concluded that common method bias does not pose a significant
issue in this dataset.
Next, the thorough assessment of collinearity was conducted by examining the inner
VIF values for each construct. The VIF values ranged between 2.627 and 4.074, all below
the recommended threshold of 5.0 (Hair et al., 2019). The results indicate that there  is an
absence of significant multicollinearity or inflated variance due to common method bias.
The combination of both diagnostic tests indicates that CMB is unlikely to influence the
integrity of the structural model or its interpretation. As a result, t he data were deemed
suitable for additional analysis through Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Model-
ling (PLS-SEM).
5.3 Structural Model
The structural model's explanatory power was evaluated through the coefficient of
determination (R²) and effect size (f²) metrics. The findings indicate that the model ac-
counts for a significant amount of variance in the endogenous constructs, with R² valu es
of 0. 749 for Online Shopping Behavior  (BEH), 0. 526 for Follower count  (FLO). Cohen
(1988) and Hair et al. (2019) assert that these values demonstrate strong explanatory
power, surpassing the conventional thresholds of 0.50 for moderate and 0.75 for substan-
tial levels of explained variance. The f² effect size measures the extent to which an exoge-
nous construct contributes to the R² value of an endogenous construct in the structural
model (Cohen, 1988; Hair et al., 2019), reflecting how much removal of a certain predictor
would decrease explained variance for dependant variables. General guidelines for f² are
0.02 = small effect; 0.15 = medium effect; 0.35 = large effect.
The f² values of this study show how much predictive strength each construct has.

6. Discussion and Implication
This study provides important insights into the empirical investigation of social com-
merce and electronic word-of-mouth in the context of influencer -led online settings, par-
ticularly focusing on live streaming. The findings indicate that various characteri stics of
influencers or platforms do not uniformly drive consumers' behavioural responses. Cer-
tain elements—such as the interactivity of live streaming, promotional activities, or the
level of engagement from followers —play a more significant role in shapi ng consumer
behaviour compared to functional or informational aspects, like response timing or online
ratings in the realm of e-commerce.
The analysis reinforces the idea that live streaming significantly contributes to fol-
lower growth by fostering real-time engagement, enhancing social presence, and creating
emotional connections. The results align with previous studies suggesting that the con-
nection between influencers and their audience is reinforced due to their increased per-
ception of authenticity and trustworthiness during interactive live sessions (Hu et al.,
2017; Chen & Lin, 2018). However, a preliminary engagement step is necessary for audi-
ence growth, as live streaming alone does not directly influence consumer behaviour. This
suggests that before observable behavioural effects can occur, engagement must first be
transformed into an increase in audience size. This supports the notion that live streaming
value chains are organisational in nature, creating a network of followers rather than re-
lying on a straightforward transactional approach (Park & Lin, 2020).
As a result, follower count emerged as a significant medium that effectively conveys
the impact of influencer activity and potentially influences behavioural responses. This
aligns with the theory of social influence, which demonstrates that perceived popu larity
and social proof amplify persuasive effects (Cialdini, 2009). A larger follower count signi-
fies credibility and trust, encouraging others to adopt the observed practices. The number
of followers, as utilised in this study, acts as a composite measure of the influencer's reach
and audience interaction, illustrating the concept that relational and social signals have a
more significant impact on behaviour than informational signals.
The findings further show that promotional tools have a significant direct effect on
online shopping behavior. This supports prior research suggesting that discounts, givea-
ways, limited-time offers, free delivery, and other promotional incentives can stimu late
consumer attention and encourage purchase-related responses in digital commerce envi-
ronments (Vigna & Mainardes, 2019; Xue et al., 2022). In live -streaming and Facebook -
based commerce, promotional tools may be especially persuasive because they create  ur-
gency and reduce the perceived cost of immediate action. These tools may also increase
consumer participation by making the shopping encounter more interactive and reward -
oriented.

Promotional tools also had a significant positive effect on online reviews, suggesting
that promotional exposure may encourage consumers to comment, share experiences, or
participate in review-related activity. However, online reviews did not significantly  me-
diate the relationship between promotional tools and online shopping behavior. This find-
ing indicates that although promotional tools may stimulate review activity, reviews did
not serve as a meaningful transmission mechanism between promotions and beha vioral
outcomes in this sample. One possible explanation is that consumers in live -streaming
commerce may respond more directly to promotional urgency and visible social proof
than to review-based evaluation. Another possible explanation is that review cre dibility
may vary across social commerce environments, particularly where consumers perceive
some reviews as promotional, incomplete, or difficult to verify. Therefore, the non-signif-
icant mediating role of online reviews should be interpreted cautiously a s a context-spe-
cific empirical finding rather than as evidence that reviews are generally unimportant.
Similarly, customer response speed did not have a significant effect on online shop-
ping behavior. Although prior research suggests that quick responses can improve trust,
satisfaction, and perceived service quality, the present findings indicate that respo nse
speed alone may not be sufficient to shape behavioral outcomes in live -streaming social
commerce. In this context, consumers may pay greater attention to more visible and so-
cially validated cues, such as follower count and promotional offers. However, this does
not mean that response speed lacks managerial value. Fast responses may still contribute
to customer satisfaction, complaint handling, and post -purchase service quality, even if
they did not directly predict online shopping behavior in the presen t model. The non -
significant effect may also reflect sample characteristics, measurement limitations, or the
possibility that response speed matters more at later stages of the customer journey, such
as order confirmation, delivery follow-up, or complaint resolution.
6.1 Theoretical Implication
This study contributes to the literature on social commerce, influencer marketing,
and live-streaming commerce by shifting attention from human -centered influencer at-
tributes to platform-generated cues. Much of the existing influencer marketing literature
explains consumer persuasion through source credibility, parasocial relationships, attrac-
tiveness, expertise, authenticity, and interpersonal trust. While these factors remain im-
portant, the present study shows that non-human cues embedded within social commerce
platforms can also shape consumer behavior. Specifically, follower count and promotional
tools emerged as stronger predictors of online shopping behavior than response speed
and online reviews, indicating that visible social proof and promotional st imuli may be
particularly influential in live-streaming commerce.
The findings also extend Signaling Theory by showing that platform-generated indi-
cators can function as credibility and popularity signals in emerging social commerce
markets. Follower count, in particular, acted as a significant predictor of online shoppi ng
behavior and fully mediated the relationship between live streaming and behavior. This
suggests that live streaming may not influence consumer behavior directly; rather, its be-
havioral effect may depend on its ability to generate visible audience size a nd perceived
market acceptance. Therefore, follower count operates not merely as a descriptive popu-
larity metric but as a socially interpretable signal that reduces uncertainty and strengthens
consumer confidence.
The study further contributes to Cue Utilization Theory by demonstrating that con-
sumers may use platform-visible cues as heuristic shortcuts when evaluating sellers, prod-
ucts, and purchase opportunities. Promotional tools may operate as value and urgency
cues, while follower count may operate as a popularity heuristic. However, the non -sig-
nificant effects of response speed and online reviews suggest that not all platform -gener-
ated cues carry equal diagnostic value in live -streaming commerce. This finding re fines
cue-based explanations of digital consumer behavior by showing that the persuasive

strength of a cue depends on its visibility, immediacy, perceived credibility, and relevance
to the purchase context.
6.2 Managerial Implication
The findings provide several practical implications for brands, sellers, and influenc-
ers operating in live-streaming and Facebook-based commerce. First, sellers should treat
live streaming not only as a direct selling tool but also as a follower-building mechanism.
Since live streaming significantly influenced follower count, and follower count subse-
quently influenced online shopping behavior, managers should design live sessions that
encourage audience retention, page following, sharing, and repeat participation. This can
be achieved through consistent scheduling, product demonstrations, interactive question-
and-answer segments, and post-live engagement activities.
Second, follower count should be managed as a social proof asset. A visible and ac-
tive follower base may strengthen consumer perceptions of popularity, credibility, and
market acceptance. However, managers should not focus only on increasing follower
numbers artificially. The value of follower count depends on whether it is supported by
genuine engagement, relevant content, and consistent interaction. Inflated or inactive fol-
lower bases may weaken credibility if consumers perceive them as artificial or mani pu-
lated.
Third, promotional tools should be used strategically because they showed a signifi-
cant direct effect on online shopping behavior. Discounts, bundle offers, free delivery,
giveaways, and limited -time deals can stimulate consumer action, particularly in liv e-
streaming contexts where urgency and visibility are high. However, promotional cam-
paigns should be designed carefully so that they do not appear excessive, misleading, or
manipulative. Clear conditions, transparent pricing, and realistic claims can help maintain
trust while still encouraging purchase-related responses.
Fourth, although response speed and online reviews were not significant predictors
in this study, managers should not ignore them. Response speed remains important for
customer service, complaint management, order confirmation, and post -purchase satis-
faction. Similarly, online reviews may still influence consumer trust when they are credi-
ble, detailed, recent, and verifiable. Therefore, sellers should continue to maintain respon-
sive communication and encourage authentic review practices, even if these cues did not
directly predict online shopping behavior in the present model.
Finally, the findings suggest that managers in emerging social commerce markets
should prioritize visible, credible, and socially interpretable cues. In contexts where con-
sumers face uncertainty about seller reliability and product quality, platform cues such as
follower count, live interaction, transparent promotions, and authentic engagement can
help reduce perceived risk and strengthen consumer confidence.
6.3 Limitations And Future Research
Although this study contributes to the understanding of platform -generated cues in
live-streaming social commerce, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the
study used purposive and snowball sampling because no complete sampling frame exists
for Bangladeshi consumers who purchase products through influencer -led or Facebook-
based social commerce. Although these sampling techniques were appropriate for reach-
ing respondents with relevant online shopping experience, they limit the generalizability
of the findings. Future research may use larger samples, probability -based sampling
methods, or panel data to improve representativeness.
Second, the sample was concentrated mainly in Dhaka and consisted largely of
young, digitally active consumers. This group is highly relevant for studying social com-
merce because young urban consumers are frequent users of Facebook, YouTube, Insta-
gram, and live-streaming platforms. However, the findings may not fully represent older
consumers, rural consumers, or individuals with lower levels of digital access and online
shopping experience. Future studies may compare urban and rural consumers or examine

whether platform-generated cues operate differently across age, income, education, and
geographic groups.
Third, this study examined general online shopping behavior rather than product -
specific purchase decisions. Consumer responses to live streaming, promotional tools, fol-
lower count, and online reviews may vary across product categories. For example, con-
sumers may rely more heavily on visual demonstration cues when buying fashion, cos-
metics, or lifestyle products, whereas reviews and response speed may be more important
for electronics, household goods, or higher -risk purchases. Future research may test the
model across different product categories to identify category-specific differences.
Fourth, the study relied on cross -sectional self-reported survey data. Although ap-
propriate procedures were used to assess reliability, validity, and common method bias,
cross-sectional data cannot fully capture changes in consumer behavior over time. Future
studies may use longitudinal designs, experiments, or behavioral data from social com-
merce platforms to examine how platform cues influence actual purchase behavior, repeat
purchase, engagement, and loyalty.
Finally, the non -significant effects of response speed and online reviews should be
interpreted within the boundaries of the present sample and measurement approach.
These constructs may still influence consumer behavior under different conditions, such
as when review credibility is high, response speed is measured at a specific customer -
service stage, or consumers are purchasing high -involvement products. Future research
may examine moderating variables such as perceived review authenticity, product in-
volvement, platform trust, seller reputation, and consumer skepticism to better explain
when response speed and online reviews become more influential.
7. Conclusion
This study examined how platform-generated cues influence online shopping behav-
ior in Bangladesh’s live -streaming social commerce context. The findings show that pro-
motional tools and follower count significantly influence online shopping behavior, while
live streaming affects behavior indirectly through follower count. In contrast, response
speed and online reviews did not show significant direct effects. These results suggest that
visible social proof and promotional stimuli may be more influential than f unctional or
review-based cues in this context. The study contributes to social commerce literature by
highlighting the role of non -human, platform-based cues in shaping consumer behavior
in an emerging digital market.
