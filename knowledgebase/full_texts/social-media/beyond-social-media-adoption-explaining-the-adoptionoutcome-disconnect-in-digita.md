ABSTRACT
Social media marketing (SMM) gives micro and small enterprises (MSEs) relatively accessible ways to reach customers,
communicate with markets and collect feedback, yet activity on social platforms does not necessarily produce equivalent customer
or firm va lue. This study examines that distinction using survey evidence from 400 Indian MSEs that actively use SMM. The
empirical design evaluates social media budget, time spent, posting frequency, platform breadth, content breadth and an adopt ion
index in relati on to customer engagement, brand loyalty and business performance. The outcome measures demonstrate
exceptionally high internal consistency, with Cronbach’s α=.990 for customer engagement, .986 for brand loyalty and .987 for
business performance. The high reliability supports measurement consistency, although it does not by itself establish construct
validity or causal relationships. The regression evidence provides limited support for a direct adoption -to-outcome relationship.
The customer-engagement model is not statistically significant (R ²=.010, F(5,394)=.830, p=.529), and the brand-loyalty model is
likewise non-significant (R²=.004, F(5,394)=.301, p=.912). The business-performance model explains 2.2% of the variance and is
not statistically significant at the omnibus level (F(5,394)=1.74,  p=.124). Two coefficient -level signals nevertheless warrant
attention: time spent on social media is positively associated with performance (β=.154, p=.025), whereas the adoption index is
negatively associated with performance (β=−.143, p=.043). Because the overall performance model is non-significant and the study
is cross-sectional, these coefficients are treated as diagnostic signals rather than robust causal effects. The central finding is
therefore not that SMM is ineffective, but that observable adoption intensity is an incomplete explanation of value creation. Platform
presence, posting activity and broader adoption can create opportunities for interaction, but those opportunities require
complementary capabilities such as content quality, responsive ness, customer insight, analytics and integration with customer -
management routines. The paper consequently develops the concept of an adoption–outcome disconnect and positions it within the
resource-based view and dynamic capabilities perspectives. The th eoretical contribution lies in reframing SMM from a simple
adoption problem to an adoption–capability–outcome problem and in treating weak direct effects as theoretically informative rather
than as evidence of research failure. For managers, the implicatio n is to evaluate social media through conversion measures
response quality, meaningful engagement, customer retention, inquiries and business outcomes rather than platform count alone.
Future research should test the proposed conversion logic directly through validated capability measures, mediation and
moderation models, multi -source performance indicators and longitudinal designs. Methodologically, the study  separates the
descriptive evidence of adoption from the inferential evidence of outcomes and avoids treating individual significant coefficients as
proof of a significant regression model. This distinction is important because the sampled firms are active adopters, meaning  the
analysis addresses variation in intensity among users rather than the initial decision to adopt social media. The findings also suggest
that the performance consequences of digital activity may be contingent on managerial attention and the quality of organisati onal
routines that surround social -media use. Accordingly, the paper co ntributes a more cautious interpretation of adoption –
performance research and specifies measurable directions for future empirical testing. The resulting framework distinguishes
digital presence, digital activity and digital capability as analytically different constructs.

1. INTRODUCTION
The rapid diffusion of social media has altered the architecture of firm –customer communication. Unlike conventional one -way
promotional media, social platforms allow firms and customers to create, distribute, discuss and respond to content, making
interaction part of the communication process itself. Kietzmann et al. (2011) describes social media through functional dimensions
including conversations, sharing, presence, relationships and reputation, while Kaplan and Haenlein (2010) emphasise its
participatory and interactive character. For MSEs, these properties can reduce the cost of market communication while also

providing access to customer feedback and market signals. The strategic value of those opportunities, however, depends on wha t
firms do with them.

The central strategic issue is therefore not whether an enterprise maintains a Facebook, Instagram, YouTube or WhatsApp presence.
It is whether social media activity is converted into customer and firm value. Prior studies frequently report positive assoc iations
between SMM adoption and SME performance. Chatterjee and Kar (2020), for example, provide evidence from India linking social
media adoption with business outcomes, sales and customer connections. More recent evidence continues to report positive
performance relationships, while also showing that adoption and performance depend on organisational and technological conditions
(Fu et al., 2024; Tatik & Setiawan, 2025). Research on SMM capabilities further suggests that the value of social platforms depends
on complementary marketing capabilities rather than on platform access alone (Laradi et al., 2024).

The present study begins with a different empirical observation. In the 400 -enterprise dataset examined here, social media use is
widespread, yet the reported regression models explain very little variation in customer engagement, brand loyalty and busine ss
performance. This pattern is theoretically useful because it challenges a common assumption in the literature: that more social media
activity should naturally generate better outcomes. A weak direct relationship raises a more precise question what organizational
processes are required for social media activity to become customer and firm value? Recent evidence supports the relevance of this
question. Matosas -López (2024), for instance, shows that the predictive value of social -media customer engagement for SME
operating revenue depends on how social media is managed, while research on digital competence similarly identifies capability as
a mechanism through which digital activity can influence SME outcomes.

The paper therefore uses the term adoption –outcome disconnect to describe a condition in which observable SMM intensity does
not, by itself, explain corresponding variation in customer or firm outcomes. The concept is deliberately narrower than a claim that
social media has no value. Instead, it distinguishes between having access to digital resources, using those resources frequently, and
possessing the capabilities required to convert activity into value. This distinction is consistent with the resource-based view (RBV),
which argues that widely available resources become strategically valuable through complementary capabilities and organisational
processes (Barney, 1991). It also accords with dynamic capabilities theory, which focuses on sensing opportuni ties, seizing them
and transforming organisational routines in changing environments (Teece et al., 1997).

Four objectives guide the study. First, it describes the level and profile of SMM activity among Indian MSEs. Second, it exam ines
whether budget, time, platform breadth, content breadth and adoption intensity directly predict customer engagement and brand
loyalty. Third, it evaluates whether these activity measures predict business performance. Fourth, it uses the observed direct effects
including weak and non -significant relationships to develop a capability -oriented explanation and identify specific prior ities for
mediation, moderation, construct validation and longitudinal research. The empirical analysis is therefore positioned as a diagnostic
test of direct relationships, while the broader conversion mechanism remains a theoretical proposition for subsequent testing.

Research question: To what extent does social media adoption intensity translate into customer engagement, brand loyalty and
business performance among Indian MSEs, and what does a weak direct relationship reveal about the capabilities and organisational
processes required to convert SMM activity into value?

2. LITERATURE REVIEW
2.1 social media as an interactive business capability
Social media provides MSEs with a communication environment in which promotion, customer service, relationship development
and market sensing can occur through connected interactions. Customers can ask questions, provide feedback, share experiences
and influence other users, while firms can observe market responses in near real time. These functions make social media more than
a promotional channel; they create an information and interaction infrastructure. Yet the infrastructure itself is widely acc essible.
The strategic distinction therefore lies in the routines that govern content creation, responsiveness, learning and integrati on with
other customer-facing activities. This capability perspective is consistent with research that treats social media techno logy as an
enabler of customer relationship processes rather than as an automatic source of performance (Trainor et al., 2014).

2.2 SMM adoption and SME performance
Much empirical research reports positive associations between social media use and SME outcomes. Odoom et al. (2017) linked
interactivity, compatibility and cost effectiveness to social media usage and performance benefits, while Chatterjee and Kar (2020)
provided evidence from Indian SMEs. Recent studies continue to report positive relationships between SMM and SME/MSME
performance, including evidence using SEM and capability -oriented models (Fu et al., 2024; Tatik & Setiawan, 2025). These
findings establish an important baseline for the present study. At the same time, they leave open a measurement question: does
adoption intensity capture the organisational capability through which social media creates value? If platform count, posting
frequency and time are treated as equivalent to strategic capability, models may overestimate what adoption indicators can explain.
The present study addresses this issue by testing direct activity–outcome relationships and then interpreting weak effects through a
capability lens.

2.3 Customer engagement as a conversion mechanism
Customer engagement extends beyond exposure or the frequency with which content is displayed. It encompasses cognitive
attention, emotional involvement and behavioural participation and can therefore serve as an intermediate mechanism between
SMM activity and downstream outcomes. Social platforms provide the infrastructure for engagement, but engagement depends on
the relevance of content, interaction quality, responsiveness and the firm's ability to learn from customer signals. Recent S ME
evidence reinforc es this conditional view: Matosas -López (2024) found that social -media engagement could forecast operating
revenue for some SMEs when social-media management was outsourced and integrated with other digital marketing activities, but
not for SMEs using inte rnal, non-specialised management. The implication for the present study is important: aggregate adoption
measures may omit the managerial mechanisms through which activity becomes meaningful engagement.

2.4 Brand loyalty and business performance
Brand loyalty represents a downstream relationship outcome and is unlikely to depend on social media activity alone. Repeated
digital interactions may strengthen familiarity, trust and advocacy, but loyalty is also shaped by product quality, price, se rvice
experience, trust and switching costs. Consequently, a weak direct association between SMM activity and loyalty is theoretica lly
plausible when complementary determinants are not included in the model. The same logic applies to business performance: sales,
profitability and growth reflect multiple operational and market conditions. A direct adoption coefficient should therefore n ot be
interpreted as a complete account of performance creation. The capability perspective predicts that social media is more li kely to
contribute to loyalty and performance when firms possess the routines required to interpret customer signals and integrate them into
broader marketing and service processes.

2.5 Systematic literature review evidence
A PRISMA 2020–style literature screening process was used to organise the evidence base. The review identified 228 records,
removed 37 duplicates, screened 191 records, excluded 79 at the screening stage, assessed 112 full texts, excluded 67 at elig ibility
and retained 45 studies in the final matrix. The review indicates that SMM is frequently associated with engagement, loyalty and
performance, while integrated empirical testing within Indian MSEs remains comparatively limited. Recent review evidence
likewise points to the need for clearer outcome frameworks and stronger attention to capability and context in digital marketing
research (Tran et al., 2024). The review is used here to establish the research problem and map the literature rather than to  claim
that the present study is itself a systematic-review meta-analysis. This distinction is important because the empirical contribution of
the paper rests on the 400-enterprise survey and its regression analysis.

3. RESEARCH GAP AND THEORETICAL FOUNDATION
3.1 Research gap
Three related gaps motivate the study. First, social media research often uses adoption as an explanatory construct without
adequately separating behavioural intensity from strategic capability. Second, customer engagement and brand loyalty are frequently
treated as outcomes without sufficient empirical attention to the mechanisms connecting activi ty with downstream value. Third,
evidence from Indian MSEs remains less developed than evidence from larger firms and other national settings. A fourth

methodological issue follows from these gaps: cross -sectional direct-effect models can identify associations but cannot establish
whether engagement, loyalty, digital competence or organisational readiness transmit the effects of SMM activity over time. T he
present study addresses the first three gaps empirically and uses the fourth to define a focused future research agenda.
3.2 Resource-Based View
The resource-based view provides a useful starting point because social media accounts, platform access and basic digital tools are
widely available and therefore do not constitute distinctive resources by themselves. Competitive value is more plausibly associated
with complementary resources and capabilities, including customer knowledge, content design, analytics, responsiveness,
managerial attention and the ability to integrate digital interactions with business processes. This logic also explains why adoption
intensity may be a weak proxy for performance capability: two firms can use the same platforms with similar frequency while
possessing very different abilities to interpret feedback, tailor content and act on customer information. The present findin gs are
consistent with this possibility, but they do not independently prove the RBV mechanism; that mechanism requires direct
measurement of capability constructs in future research.
3.3 Dynamic capabilities perspective
Dynamic capabilities extend this reasoning by focusing on organisational processes for sensing changes, seizing opportunities  and
transforming routines. Social media can generate real-time information about customer preferences, complaints, competitor actions
and emerging trends, but information has value only when the firm can interpret it and respond. Recent research likewise trea ts
SMM capability as a strategic resource whose relationship with firm performance depends on the capabilities embedded in soci al-
media management (Laradi et al., 2024). Accordingly, a higher number of platforms may increase activity without increasing value
when the organisation lacks routines for prioritisation, content adaptation, response management and learning.
3.4 Adoption–outcome disconnect
The paper conceptualises SMM effectiveness as a conversion process rather than a simple adoption -to-performance relationship.
Adoption activity provides the behavioural input; content quality, responsiveness, customer learning and digital integration represent
potential conversion capabilities; customer engagement and loyalty represent intermediate relational outcomes; and business
performance represents a downstream outcome. The present data permit testing of the direc t activity–outcome relationships, but
they do not permit a definitive test of the complete sequential pathway. The adoption–outcome disconnect is therefore proposed as
a theoretical and empirical diagnostic construct, not as a fully validated causal mechan ism. This boundary is central to the paper's
contribution: weak direct effects motivate a more complete model in which capability and relational mechanisms can be tested
explicitly through CFA/SEM, mediation, moderation and longitudinal designs.

5. RESEARCH METHODOLOGY
The study adopts a quantitative, descriptive and analytical cross -sectional design. The unit of analysis is the individual MSE
represented by an owner, manager or marketing decision maker. Purposive sampling was used to identify operational enterprises
that actively used SMM, making the sample appropriate for examining variation among adopters rather than adoption versus non -
adoption. Seven platforms were considered: Facebook, Instagram, Twitter/X, YouTube, WhatsApp, Pinterest and LinkedIn. The
questionnaire used five-point Likert items for the major perceptual constructs. Because the design is cross-sectional and purposive,
the analysis is intended to identify associations within the sampled adopter population rather than establish causal effects or
statistical generalisation to all Indian MSEs.

A total of 700 responses were received, of which 400 were complete and valid for the final analysis, giving N=400. The data w ere
screened for incomplete and inconsistent responses and analysed using SPSS. Descriptive statistics, reliability analysis, correlation
analysis and multiple linear regression were used to evaluate the proposed direct relationships. The analytical sequence is
deliberately aligned with the study's contribution: descriptive statistics establish the adoption context, reliability evalua tes internal
consistency, correlations provide preliminary diagnostics and regression tests whether adoption -related activity measures explain
customer engagement, brand loyalty and business performance. No causal interpretation is assigned to the cross -sectional
coefficients.

6. RESULTS
6.1 Descriptive profile of adoption
The adoption profile shows that 45.0% of enterprises had used SMM for one to five years, while 22.5% had less than one year o f
experience and another 22.5% had six to ten years of experience. Only 10.0% reported more than ten years of SMM use. The
distribution indicates that SMM is established within the sampled MSEs but remains relatively recent for many firms. Facebook
was used by 75.0% of firms, Instagram by 62.5%, YouTube by 45.0%, LinkedIn by 25.0% a nd other platforms by 20.0%. The
platform profile therefore indicates broad exposure to social media, particularly mainstream consumer -oriented channels, but
platform presence alone does not demonstrate strategic effectiveness.

The profile also reports that 65.0% of firms manage SMM internally or through the owner, while 35.0% use an external agency.
Text/images were reported by 70.0% of firms, videos by 62.5% and interactive content by 30.0%. In terms of time, 27.5% spent
two to five hours per week and 25.0% spent six to ten hours. Posting was most commonly one to two times per week (30.0%) or
once every one to two days (25.0%). These figures establish a meaningful level of activity, but they should be read as behavi oural
indicators rather than evidence of capability or effectiveness. This distinction is central to interpreting the subsequent regressio n
results.

6.4 Correlation evidence
The correlation analysis provides an important diagnostic for the paper's central argument. The adoption index is strongly associated
with time spent (r=.634, p<.01) and posting frequency (r=.657, p<.01), and it is also related to budget allocation (r=.275, p<.01) and
platform count (r=.118, p<.05). These relationships indicate that the  index captures an intensity dimension of SMM activity. In
contrast, the outcome variables show weak or non -significant relationships with the adoption index. Customer engagement has a
near-zero association with the adoption index (r=.041), while brand loyalty and business performance also show weak relationships.
The contrast between strong interrelationships among activity indicators and weak activity –outcome relationships provides the
clearest descriptive evidence for the adoption–outcome disconnect.

7. DISCUSSION
7.1 The adoption–outcome disconnect
The central empirical pattern is a separation between SMM activity and customer or firm outcomes. The sampled MSEs are active
social media users: Facebook is used by 75.0% of firms and Instagram by 62.5%, while video and other content formats are also
common. Yet the direct models explain only 1.0% of the variance in engagement and 0.4% in loyalty. The finding should not be
interpreted as evidence that social media lacks value. Rather, it indicates that platform presence and activity intensity are  weak
substitutes for the capabilities that determine how firms use those platforms. This interpretation is consistent with recent capability-
oriented research showing that SMM capability can be related to performance even when simple adoption measures do not capture
the relevant mechanisms (Laradi et al., 2024).

than with a simple platform -count explanation. Time can include answering customer questions, monitoring feedback, adjusting

7.3 The negative adoption coefficient and diminishing returns
The negative adoption-index coefficient should not be interpreted as evidence that social media is harmful. The overall performance
model is not statistically significant, the study is cross-sectional and the index is an activity measure rather than a validated capability
construct. A plausible interpretation is that broader activity can become inefficient when it exceeds a firm's capacity to plan, produce
relevant content, monitor responses and maintain consistency. The result therefore raises the possibil ity of diminishing returns,
platform overload or an omitted capability mechanism. These explanations remain hypotheses for future testing rather than
conclusions established by the current coefficient.

7.4 Why customer engagement may remain neutral
Customer engagement may remain weakly related to adoption intensity because presence and exposure are not equivalent to
meaningful interaction. The descriptive profile indicates substantial activity, yet activity measures do not reveal whether content is
relevant, whether responses are timely or whether customer feedback changes subsequent decisions. Evidence from SMEs also
suggests that the relationship between engagement and business outcomes can depend on how social media is managed and
integrated with o ther digital activities (Matosas -López, 2024). The present findings therefore support a capability -based
interpretation while recognising that engagement quality, rather than engagement opportunity, is the more plausible conversio n
mechanism.

7.5 Implications for integrated models
The theoretical framework proposes a sequential relationship from SMM activity through customer engagement and loyalty toward
business performance, with digital competence and organisational readiness potentially shaping the strength of those paths. T he
present direct regression evidence does not establish that complete chain. This is a strength rather than a weakness if the bou ndary
is stated clearly: the current study identifies where the direct adoption model performs poorly and uses that empirical patte rn to
specify what future models should test. CFA/SEM can examine construct validity and mediation; PROCESS or SEM can test
conditional effects; and longitudinal or multi-source designs can address temporal ordering and common-method concerns.

7.6 Comparison with prior evidence
The findings should be interpreted as a qualification of, rather than a rejection of, prior positive evidence. Chatterjee and Kar (2020)
reported performance benefits from SMM adoption among Indian SMEs, while Odoom et al. (2017) linked usage motivations t o
performance benefits. More recent studies similarly report positive SMM–performance relationships in SME/MSME settings (Fu et
al., 2024; Tatik & Setiawan, 2025). At the same time, capability-oriented research shows that the value of social-media activity can
depend on how firms manage and integrate it (Laradi et al., 2024; Matosas -López, 2024). The present study adds a specific
qualification: adoption intensity is not necessarily an adequate proxy for the capabilities through which digital activity be comes
value.

8. THEORETICAL CONTRIBUTIONS
• Reframes SMM from a simple adoption variable into an adoption –capability–outcome problem, separating behavioural
intensity from the organisational processes that can convert activity into value.
• Shows that high activity levels do not automatically imply stronger customer engagement, brand loyalty or business
performance in the sampled Indian MSEs, while avoiding the unsupported conclusion that SMM itself is ineffective.
• Identifies managerial time as a potentially more informative signal of strategic involvement than platform breadth, while
explicitly treating the observed coefficient as a diagnostic association rather than causal evidence.
• Uses RBV and dynamic capabilities to explain why widely available digital resources may require complementary
organizational routines customer knowledge, responsiveness, analytics and integration before they create differentiated value.
• Establishes a defensible basis for future mediation, moderation, non-linear and longitudinal testing without claiming that the
present direct regressions validate the complete conversion mechanism.

10. LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE RESEARCH
The study has several limitations that should guide interpretation. Its cross -sectional design prevents temporal and causal
conclusions, while purposive sampling limits statistical generalisation to the wider population of Indian MSEs. The measures rely
substantially on respondent perceptions, which may introduce common -method and recall bias. The adoption index captures
behavioural intensity but is not itself a validated measure of SMM capability. The regression models also omit potentially important
determinants such as product quality, customer experience, trust, competitive intensity, industry structure, digital competence an d
organisational readiness. Finally, the very high internal -consistency coefficients indicate that future work should examine item
redundancy and construct validity rather than relying on Cronbach's alpha alone. These limitations do not invalidate the repo rted
associations; they define the boundary of the claims that can be made from the current design.

Future research should move from direct -effect testing toward explicit conversion models. A multi -stage design could measure
SMM activity and capability at T1, customer engagement at T2, loyalty at T3 and business performance at T4, allowing stronger
tests of temporal ordering. Confirmatory factor analysis should establish the measurement structure, followed by SEM or
PROCESS-based mediation and moderation analysis. Non -linear models should test whether platform expansion produces
diminishing returns, while multi-source performance indicators can reduce common-method bias. Researchers should also compare
sectors because the value of visual content, messaging, video and platform breadth may differ across food, retail, hospitalit y and
professional services. Finally, capability measures should distinguish content quality, responsiveness, analytics use, customer insight
and digital integration from simple adoption indicators.

11. CONCLUSION
Social media marketing is widely accessible to Indian MSEs, yet accessibility should not be equated with effectiveness. In th e
present sample, SMM adoption intensity does not significantly explain customer engagement or brand loyalty, and the overall
business-performance model is also non -significant. At the coefficient level, managerial time shows a positive association with
performance, while the adoption index shows a negative association; because the omnibus performance model is non -significant
and the design is cross-sectional, these coefficients should be treated as signals for further investigation rather than causal effects.
The principal contribution is therefore a sharper distinction between adoption activity and value -creating capability. Social media
can create opportunities for reach, interaction and learning, but those opportunities require organisational routines that co nvert
information and interaction into customer and firm value. The adoption –outcome disconnect consequently prov ides a focused
platform for future research on capability, mediation, moderation, non-linearity and longitudinal change.

12. RESEARCH AGENDA GENERATED BY THE FINDINGS
The empirical pattern generates a focused research agenda rather than a conclusion that SMM does not work. Future studies should
first distinguish adoption intensity from SMM capability by separating behavioural indicators such as platform count, posting
frequency and time spent from capability dimensions such as content quality, responsiveness, analytics use and customer insigh t.
