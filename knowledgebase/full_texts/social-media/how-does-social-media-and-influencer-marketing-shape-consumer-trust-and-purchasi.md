ABSTRACT: The pet industry has adopted influencer -driven marketing as its main advertising method because social media
networks have experienced fast growth lately which has changed the traditional marketing methods. Pet influencers on Instagram
use the platform to create content which emotionally connects with consumers while provid ing relatable material that changes
consumer behaviour. The study focuses on the research question which examines the impact of social media and influencer
marketing on consumer trust and buying behaviour in the pet industry. The study uses thematic analys is to extract findings from
academic research, industry reports and digital case studies through its qualitative literature -based research method. The study
results demonstrate that emotional connection establishes consumer trust in this environment because it operates independently
of logical judgment. Influencers create emotional connections with their audience by showing their pets as family members and
tell stories about their products through pet care and companionship. People evaluate trustworthiness by using perceived
authenticity and relatability as their main evaluation criteria while social proof from engagement metrics and community validation
strengthens trustworthiness. The analysis shows that ethical conflicts arise from undisclosed sponsorship s, deceptive advertising
and the way pet ownership gets portrayed. The case study examples of Doug the Pug and Nala Cat show how emotional storytelling
and authenticity and commercialization create their interactive dynamics which drive their operational s ystem. The study
concludes that influencer marketing not only shapes purchasing behaviour but also redefines the nature of trust in the pet industry,
with important implications for consumers, marketers and regulatory frameworks.

INTRODUCTION
The global pet care industry has witnessed remarkable expansion over the past decade driven by changing consumer lifestyles,
rising disposable incomes and an increasing tendency to treat pets as integral members of the family. The global pet industry  is
estimated to reach a value of over $500 billion by 2030. It is because of all its segments, which include food and healthcare,
grooming and accessories, will continue to experience growth (Morgan Stanley, 2022). The market shows rapid growth which has
brought new changes to consumer purchasing patterns owing to digital platforms now dominating consumer purchase decisions.
Social media has emerged as a central arena where pet -related content is created, shared and consumed, significantly shaping
how consumers perceive products and brands within the pet industry.
The digital landscape has seen a significant change through the emergence of pet influencers who use Instagram , TikTok and
YouTube for their content creation. Pet accounts attract millions of followers through the ir unique personalities , stories, visuals
etc. which create new digital personas from their animal content. Pet influencers establish themselves as important brand partners
who advertise various products which include pet food and lifestyle items, thus acti ng as vital links between businesses and their
customers. The influencers who create content for Abidin (2016) use their authentic presentation of curated material to shape
how audiences perceive their content. The pet industry gives special importance to this phenomenon because its content contains
both entertaining elements and emotional stories which promote products through indirect methods that achieve stronger
persuasive effects.

The emotional aspect of pet ownership determines how well influencer marketing works in this particular field. Contemporary
consumers increasingly view pets not merely as anim als but as family members, a trend commonly referred to as "pet
humanization" or anthropomorphism (Dotson Hyatt, 2008). The emotional connection between people and their pets affects their
purchasing choices because consumers decide which products to buy based on their need to show care and love and take
responsibility for decisions. The marketing messages create emotional connections with customers through pet stories which
people can relate to become and it becomes the most effective tool for changing customer behaviour and attitudes.
Social media platforms enable pet owners to connect with other pet owners who share their interests by sharing their pet
experiences and asking for pet -related advice which validates their pet ownership choices. Consumers in  the current ecosystem
tend to choose influencer recommendations as their preferred method to obtain  primary information. People tend to trust
traditional advertising less than they trust content that comes from their peers or influencers because the latter two sources appear
more genuine and relatable to them (Veirman et. al., 2017). Pet influencers create strong personal connections with their followers
which leads to followers developing parasocial relationships that create one-sided emotional ties which resemble real-world social
connections (Horton and Wohl, 1956). The establishment of trust between these two parties leads to higher product acceptance
because the audience views product endorsements as more trustworthy. People use both product attributes and their emotional
responses and their trust in the product to decide which products to buy.
The study needs key concept definitions, which are essential for understanding its dynamic study. Consumer trust refers to th e
confidence that consumers place in a brand, product or information source, particularly under conditions of uncertainty (Gefen et.
al., 2003). In digital environments, trust develops through cues which include authenticity , consistency and social validation,
instead of relying on direct ex periences. Influencer marketing exists as a strategic method which enables people with dedicated
social media followers to promote products and services through their perceived credibility and their ties to their audience (Freberg
et al., 2011). The combined digital platforms, algorithms, user interactions and content practices of social media ecosystems create
a system which determines how information gets created, shared and consumed.
This research paper investigates a key research question: How does soci al media and influencer marketing shape consumer trust
and purchasing decisions in the pet industry? The question becomes important because the pet industry combines three elements
which include emotional consumption, digital influence and commercial activity. Existing research has studied influencer marketing
and online consumer behaviour yet, researchers need to study these dynamics based on pet ownership, which includes emotional
and ethical aspects.
This research demonstrates that influencer marketing c reates a new form of consumer trust which begins with rational product
assessment and develops into emotional trust through social connections. The three factors which include emotional engagement,
perceived authenticity and social validation mechanisms show how influencers affect consumer decision-making. The pet industry
shows strong emotional connections between people and their pets which makes influencer marketing an effective tool to control
consumer behaviour.

LITERATURE REVIEW
The growing influence of social media and digital platforms has fundamentally transformed how consumers access information ,
evaluate products and develop trust. Th is study analyses existing research through three theoretical frameworks which define
consumer trust, influencer marketing and social media user behaviour while examining unique aspects of pet industry and its
dynamics. The literature review establishes a vital research gap which exists between pet ownership emotional attachment and
trust development through influencer content.
Consumer Trust in Digital Contexts
Consumer trust has long been recognized as a central determinant of purchasing behaviour, particularly in contexts characterized
by uncertainty and information asymmetry. In traditional offline settin gs, trust is built through direct contac t, physical product
assessment and established brand recognition. Consumers in digital environments must find different ways to evaluate credibility
because these environments lack the traditional trust cues that exi st in other settings (Gefen et al., 2003) argue that online trust
is constructed through institutional assurances, interface quality and informational cues because people lack personal knowledge
about others. The online trust system functions differently from the offline trust system because it uses digital content to establish
authenticity and credibility through digital content. The rise of user -created content has made user reviews , ratings and
testimonials into essential information sources. Research by  Cheung and Thadani (2012) demonstrates that electronic word  of
mouth (eWOM) significantly influences consumer attitudes and intentions which consumers view as more trustworthy than
traditional advertisements.
Consumers use peer reviews as a collective validation method which enables them to evaluate product quality through other
people's experiences. The study conducted by Filieri in 2015 found that consumers assess the legitimacy of online reviews bas ed
on their assessment of review consistency, review detail and review authenticity. Digital credibility in this situation emerges as a
social construct which develops through personal understanding and group validation. Social media platforms create an
environment where user-generated and influencer-created content leads to overlapping peer recommendations and commercial
endorsements which results in confusion about trustworthiness.
Influencer Marketing and Credibility
Influencer marketing has emerged as a domina nt strategy within digital advertising which uses the perceived credibility and
relatability of individuals who have extensive online followings as its primary approach. The effectiveness of this approach can be
understood through the lens of source credibility theory which states that a message becomes persuasive when people view the
speaker as trustworthy and expert and attractive (Hovland and Weiss, 1951). Through their content creation, ongoing interaction
and personal branding social media influencers develop their authenticity to their followers.
Trustworthiness describes how people view an influencer's honesty and integrity, which becomes stronger when the influencer
shows their genuine self and maintains values that their audience holds important. Ex pertise exists as a combination of
demonstrated knowledge and actual work experience, yet people often believe someone to be an expert because they have seen
that person before. Attractiveness includes physical beauty but it also consists of personal quali ties that make people charming
and easy to connect with, which help build connections with the audience. The study results demonstrate that all three dimensions
of study dimensions which create better influencer endorsement results, help viewers develop be tter product attitudes toward
the endorsements which they see (Ohanian, 1990).
The primary way that influencers achieve their goals is through building parasocial relationships, which involve audiences creating
one-sided emotional bonds with media personal ities (Horton and Wohl, 1956). Social media platforms enable users to establish
relationships through their interactive elements, which include comments , direct messages and live content, that create a false
sense of closeness between users. The researcher s demonstrate that people who experience parasocial interactions develop
higher purchase intent when they see online influencers because their emotional connection with the influencers makes them
believe the influencers have actual expertise (Lou and Yuan, 2019).
Influencer marketing connects personal communication with business communication because it creates a new way to share
genuine beliefs through advertising methods. The hybrid communication model improves persuasive power, yet it creates doubts
about how well organizations maintain transparent operations, which leads to ethical problems when they fail to show that their
content has received financial backing.

Pet Industry Specific Dynamics
The pet industry creates spe cial conditions which make the digital consumer behaviour patterns that existing theories explain ,
more difficult to understand. The most important of these elements exists as anthropomorphism which describes the practice of
people assigning human traits t o nonhuman creatures. Consumers often interpret pets ’ behaviours, preferences and emotions
through a human lens which results in them making decisions that show greater understanding of their pets' feelings (Dotson and
Hyatt, 2008). Marketing professionals use this practice to promote their products by portraying them as things that pets need or
enjoy which will lead customers to buy the products because they think they will fulfil their pets' emotional requirements.
The pet industry has a vital ethical con sumption element because customers now prioritize animal welfare , sustainability and
ingredient transparency. According to Carrington, Neville and Whitwell (2014) , ethical considerations have a strong effect on
consumer purchasing patterns but people often  struggle to match their declared values with their true actions. In influencer
marketing, ethical messages reach audiences through stories that describe care , rescue and responsible ownership which build
trust while they distract from commercial purposes.
Pet industry’s decision-making processes show strong emotional influence because pet owners form deep emotional connections
with their pets. Pet owners evaluate pet products through two main criteria, their functional performance and their price but also
through how they believe the products will affect their pets ’ health. The emotional connection people have with their pets
increases their tendency to believe in persuasive messages that showcase love and care and responsibility through content which
they find either familiar or inspiring on social media platforms.

Research Gap
The pet industry needs more research because existing studies about consumer trust and influencer marketing together with social
media behaviour research have not yet established their intersection point. Research studies about influencer credibility and digital
trust assessment usu ally examine both factors in non -specific environments because they overlook the special emotional
connection which pet owners ’ experience. Digital influencers play an essential role in shaping emotional consumption through
their ability to create human -like content which research studies on anthropomorphism and emotional consumption fail to
recognize.
The research investigates the relationship between pet owners' emotional attachment to their pets and how they establish trus t
through influencer-based mechanisms. The study combines digital marketing knowledge with consumer psychology insights and
pet industry research to explain trust development processes which lead to consumer decision-making in this emerging market.

METHODOLOGY
The researchers used a qualitative research design which relies on existing literature to investigate how social media and influencer
marketing affect consumer trust and purchasing decisions within the pet industry. A qualitative research method becomes essential
because the study i nvestigates exploratory research questions which require understanding fundamental processes instead of
evaluating direct cause-and-effect links. Qualitative research enables researchers to understand complex social situations through
studying how differen t knowledge systems create meaning , tell stories and establish patterns (Creswell and Poth, 2018). The
research study creates a theoretical foundation of influencer-driven trust development by merging existing academic research with
industry reports and real-world digital examples.
The primary method employed is thematic analysis, a widely used qualitative technique for identifying, analysing and interpreting
patterns within textual data. Thematic analysis becomes the best approach for literature -based rese arch because it enables
researchers to methodically arrange multiple sources into unified themes which answer their research inquiries (Braun and Clarke,
2006). The method establishes an inductive approach for data analysis through which researchers can de velop fundamental
concepts which include emotional engagement , authenticity, social validation and ethical concerns. The digital environment
enables consumers to establish trust through various elements which work together to create trust and drive their d igital
behaviour.
The study's data sources are divided into three primary categories  and t he theoretical framework of the study uses academic
literature as its primary source which includes peer -reviewed journal articles and scholarly books that study cons umer trust and
influencer marketing, social media behaviour and anthropomorphism. The sources provide established frameworks through which
current digital phenomena can be understood which includes source credibility theory, parasocial interaction and electronic word-
of-mouth. The study uses industry reports together with market analyses to show how current trends in global pet care and digital
marketing practices which are common in the industry affect the discussion. The Morgan Stanley reports together wi th other
market research organization reports provide insights into how consumers behave , how markets grow and how social media has
become an essential factor in consumer purchase decisions.
The study uses digital case studies which include well -known pet influencers and pet brands that include Tucker Budzyn and The
Farmer's Dog. The researchers used the cases as illustrative material to demonstrate how theoretical concepts show themselves
in real -world situations instead of treating the cases as empirical  datasets. The pet industry shows emotional storytelling ,
authenticity and social proof through its influencer content , brand collaborations and audience engagement patterns. The study
analysis gained analytical strength by using these cases which connected theoretical concepts with real life practices.
The coding process uses an iterative approach that works with thematic analysis through its interpretive nature. The research team
conducted a review of both literature and case materials to find common concepts that shape trust formation in consumer

Authenticity, Relatability, and Influencer Credibility
The authenticity of an influencer functions as the main factor which determines their credibility to the public and this affects how
consumers build their trust toward them. The public views social media content as more appealing than traditional advertising
because social media content sho ws genuine human behaviour instead of following scripted patterns. Influencers who share
"everyday life" moments, which include feeding routines , playtime and minor challenges, create a sense of transparency which
enhances their perceived authenticity (Audrezet et. al., 2020).
Relatability stands as a fundamental element that drives audiences to trust influencers who share their authentic life experiences.
The different ways micro-influencers and celebrity influencers operate show this phenomenon. Micro -influencers achieve better
audience interaction and trust because they connect with their followers on a personal level compared to celebrity influencer s
who have bigger fan bases (De Veirman et al., 2017). Pet industry professionals use relatable content to connect with customers
through their shared experiences of pet care which includes handling health problems and selecting suitable products.
The success of Nala Cat demonstrates that businesses can achieve financial success through authentic and relatable products. Nala
Cat’s content shows normal human activities and emotional moments which make followers feel closer to her. The trust which
developed over time made it possible to launch partnered products successfully because people believed in their authentic nature
which drove their buying choices. Consumers who view the influencer as genuine are more likely to interpret product
endorsements as sincere recommendations rather than paid promotions.
The analysis demonstrates that authenticity functions as the  main factor which people use to evaluate trustworthiness. In digital
environments where information is abundant and often ambiguous, consumers rely on cues such as consistency, transparency,
and relatability to assess credibility. Authenticity projection enables influencers to influence consumer perceptions which results
in changes to both product attitudes and purchasing behaviour.

Storytelling and Parasocial Relationships
The storytelling process helps people develop parasocial relationships which serve as the main way influencers connect with their
audiences. Influencers create ongoing pet narratives that develop continuous relationships. Followers develop a sense of
ownership over the pet and its owner. The stories contain several important life events and obstacles and emotional events which
make the audience become more involved with the story and its characters.
The concept of parasocial relationships which Horton and Wohl established in 1956 gains its strongest impact through social media
platforms that use interactive features to create an appearance of real-time user interaction. Followers can comment on posts and
receive responses and participate in discussions, which creates an illusion of reciprocity. The audience develops trust through this
perceived closeness, which makes them see the influencer as a trustworthy source of information who shares personal
experiences. The case of Tucker Budzyn demonstrates how storytelling and parasocial interaction reinforce trust. Tucker Budzyn's
content includes emotional narratives which show how pets react to new experiences and how they interact with family members.
The stories use captions which present the pet's viewpoint to enhance relatability and emotional engagement with the audience .
The audience demonstrates strong emotional connection through their comments and shares which show how followers feel with
affection and personal identification. The relationship between people creates a trust relationship which functions like real  social
trust. The relationship exists as one -sided yet it still creates emotional and cognitive patterns which human beings create during
social encounters. The product endorsements in these stories function as suggestions from a trustworthy friend, which boosts
their ability to persuade people.
Anthropomorphism and Perceived Product Value
The social media content about pets uses anthropomorphism as its main feature which affects how people understand their pets
and the related products. Influencers establish a product evaluation method which uses human emotions and preferences as they
show their understanding of animal behaviour needs and wants which researchers found in their study (Dotson and Hyatt 2008).
The process shows itself through influencers who use pet voices to display their product opinions through their use of captio ns
and dialog. A pet might show affection for a certain brand of food through its behaviour or it might show preference for a certain
toy through its actions. The representations lead people to value emotional satisfaction and enjoyment more than they value
functional product attributes which include nutritional content and durability.
Multiple influencer accounts show products as items that improve pet happiness and well -being rather than showing them as
methods that address actual issues. The new presentation meth od changes consumer value assessment by making them focus
more on emotional results than on actual measurement standards. Pet owners make product choices based on their intention to
create the ideal experience for their pet which they understand through anthropomorphic stories.
The analysis shows that anthropomorphism alters how people perceive product quality. Pet owners make product assessments
based on how well products match their pets ’ assumed personality traits and personal preferences. The pet indust ry consumer
pattern needs emotional and symbolic elements to shape its development process.

affects their content interpretation. According to Cialdini (2009) people show a stronger tendency to follow social proof bec ause
it operates as their guide during moments when they experience uncertainty.

Perceived Expertise vs Emotional Authority
The primary conflict which exists in influencer marketing originates from the difference between people who perceive expertis e
and those who possess emotional authority. Traditional authority source s like veterinarians and scientific experts acquire their

emotional bonds with others influencers build their authoritative status.
The marketing approach used by The Farmer's Dog demonstrates this pattern because the company partners with influencers to

the influencer's personal experiences which they acquired through life experience instead of their official qualifications.
People now trust emotional authority because they find it relatable while they believe experts possess professional knowledge.
People who have developed a bond with influencers will trust their recommendations more than expert advice which they consider

Ethical Concerns and Trust Distortion
Influencer marketing delivers major advantages which improve audience involvement and power to sway others yet it creates
serious ethical problems which need proper ethical solutions to maintain transparent operations with accurate information and
responsible conduct. The main problem exists because hidden sponsorships exist between promotional content and advertising
which fails to show its commercial nature to the audience.
The practice of hidden advertising results in consumer deception which p revents people from making correct decisions (Evans et.
al, YEAR NEEDED). The pet industry faces misleading health claims which create problems because pet owners lack necessary skills
to examine information about pets. The product promotion by influencers  creates dangerous health risks because they promote
items as natural and healthy and veterinarian-approved without showing any proof that supports their claims.
The Juniper Fox case shows how influencer culture creates complex ethical challenges that nee d to be addressed. The popular
account delivers entertaining content yet it faces backlash because its content promotes exotic pet ownership without showing
the actual difficulties that come with owning such pets. The way people perceive products through s ocial media channels creates
behaviour patterns which lead to animal welfare violations because people fail to understand the actual treatment of animals.
Digital environments create opportunities for trust manipulation according to these problems. Emotion al signals together with
perceived authenticity create a situation where consumers become more vulnerable to commercial advertising which hides its real
purpose while showing false product advantages. The situation creates a need to examine how brands and platforms and
influencers should practice their ethical duties for transparent and precise information exchange.
Thematic analysis shows that trust in the pet industry operates as a complex social construct which develops through three ma in
factors: emotional bonds between people and their pets, their social relationships and their usage of advanced technology. The
use of influencer marketing helps marketers increase customer engagement while providing customers with better information
about product selection, but this method creates additional risks which need thorough evaluation.

Implications for Consumers
Social media and pet influencers now dominate consumer behaviour, which necessitates better media literacy skills for customers.
The increasing emotional impact of digital content in combination with its immersive nature makes it challenging for consumers
to tell apart real product endorsements from planned marketing efforts. People use their understanding of authenticity and
relatability to evalua te influencer content, which results in them accepting promotional messages without questioning their
validity, according to research findings (Lou and Yuan 2019). In the pet industry, where decisions are closely tied to perceptions of
