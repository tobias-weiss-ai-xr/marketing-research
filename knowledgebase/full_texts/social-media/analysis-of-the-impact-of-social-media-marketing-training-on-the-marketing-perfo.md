Abstract
Micro enterprises play a vital role in Indonesia's economy, yet many continue to face challenges in utilizing social media
strategically to enhance their marketing performance. Although previous studies have extensively examined digital marketing
capabilities and business performance, limited attention has been given to evaluating the direct effectiveness of social media
marketing training as a practical intervention for improving marketing outcomes among micro -enterprises. This study aims to
examine the effect of social media marketing training on the marketing performance of Indonesian micro-enterprises. A quantitative
cross-sectional survey was conducted involving 110 micro -enterprise owners who had participated in at least one social media
marketing training program. Respondents were selected using purposive sampling, and data were collected through a structured
questionnaire adapted from previously validated measurement scales. The measurement instrument demonstrated satisfactory
validity and reliability , while diagnostic tests confirmed that the data met the assumptions required for ordinary least squares
regression. Simple linear regression analysis was employed to test the proposed relationship between social media marketing
training and marketing perf ormance. The findings indicate that social media marketing training exerts a positive and statistically
significant influence on marketing performance, suggesting that improvements in participants' knowledge and practical skills
contribute to enhanced sale s growth, customer acquisition, promotional effectiveness, market expansion, and adaptability to
changing market conditions. These findings reinforce the importance of capacity -building initiatives in strengthening the digital
marketing capabilities of mic ro-enterprises. The study contributes to the digital marketing and entrepreneurship literature by
providing empirical evidence of the effectiveness of training -based interventions while offering practical implications for
policymakers, training providers, and business development organizations seeking to improve the competitiveness and
sustainability of micro-enterprises through digital capability enhancement.

1. Introduction
Micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs) occupy a strategic position in Indonesia's economic structure by
serving as a major source of employment and contributing substantially to national economic output. Despite their
significant economic role, many micro-enterprises continue to experience limitations in adapting their marketing
practices to the rapidly evolving digital marketplace, where consumer purchasing behavior has increasingly shifted
toward online platforms. Among various digital marketing cha nnels, social media has emerged as one of the most
accessible and cost -effective tools for micro -enterprises because it enables businesses to communicate directly with
customers, expand market reach, and strengthen customer engagement with relatively limit ed financial resources.
Recent studies have demonstrated that digital marketing capabilities, particularly the effective utilization of social
media, significantly enhance the marketing performance of small and medium -sized enterprises across developing
economies (Chinakidzwa & Phiri, 2020; Sharabati et al., 2024; Fu et al., 2024).
Although the potential of social media marketing has been widely acknowledged, its adoption among micro -
enterprises remains fragmented and is frequently characterized by unsystematic implementation rather than strategic
planning. Fu et al. (2024), employin g the Technology –Organization–Environment (TOE) framework, identified

insufficient technical competencies and organizational resources as the primary barriers preventing MSMEs from
maximizing the benefits of social media marketing adoption. Similarly, Umboh and Aryanto (2022) found that digital
literacy mediates the relation ship between digital marketing capabilities and business performance, indicating that
access to digital technologies alone does not necessarily improve marketing outcomes unless entrepreneurs possess
adequate knowledge and practical skills to utilize these  technologies effectively. This competency gap highlights the
importance of capacity-building interventions, particularly social media marketing training, as a practical mechanism
for improving the competitiveness of micro-enterprises, which generally operate with more limited human and financial
resources than small and medium-sized firms.
Existing literature has extensively investigated the relationship between digital marketing, social media utilization,
and MSME performance. Studies conducted in Indonesia have consistently reported that social media marketing
contributes positively to bus iness sustainability and marketing performance, particularly during the COVID -19
pandemic (Patma et al., 2021; Syaifullah et al., 2021). Other empirical studies further confirmed the positive effects of
digital marketing on marketing and financial performance among Indonesian MSMEs (Daud et al., 2022; Sultoni et al.,
2022; Munir et al., 2023). Beyond Indonesia, Marolt et al. (2022) demonstrated that social media utilization enhances
SME business performance through relational social commerce capability and competitive advantage in Slovenia. From
a theoretical perspective, these studies are largely grounded in the Resource -Based View (RBV), which argues that
organizational capabilities constitute valuable strategic resources capable of generating superior per formance.
Supporting this perspective, Sipos et al. (2025) reported that digital marketing and communication capabilities are
significant predictors of marketing innovation among SMEs across multiple countries.
Despite the growing body of evidence, several research gaps remain. First, previous studies have predominantly
employed complex multivariate models involving mediating and moderating variables, such as digital literacy (Umboh
& Aryanto, 2022), relational s ocial commerce capability and competitive advantage (Marolt et al., 2022), or
entrepreneurial self -efficacy (Mu et al., 2020; Adeniyi, 2023), and have generally relied on Structural Equation
Modeling (SEM). While these models offer substantial theoretical insights, they are less suitable for evaluating practical
intervention programs designed to assess the effectiveness of a single managerial action. Second, the majority of prior
research has conceptualized digital marketing as a broad organizational capability (Chinakidzwa & Phiri, 2020; Sultoni
et al., 2022; Udayana et al., 2024), whereas relatively little attention has been devoted to examining social media
marketing training as a specific, measurable, and replicable intervention that can be implemented b y government
agencies or business development organizations. Third, inconsistencies remain in the operationalization of marketing
performance. Although Lestari et al. (2024) recently validated a multidimensional marketing performance measurement
framework for Indonesian MSMEs using confirmatory factor analysis, empirical evidence regarding its application in
assessing the direct effect of social media marketing training remains limited.
Addressing these gaps, the present study investigates the effect of social media marketing training on the marketing
performance of micro-enterprises using a quantitative associative research design and a simple linear regression model.
Unlike previous studies that primarily examined digital marketing as a broad organizational capability, this study
specifically evaluates social media marketing training as a practical intervention using a parsimonious bivariate
analytical approach. Such evidence is particularly valuable for local governments, MSME support agencies, financial
institutions, and training providers seeking to evaluate whether investments in social media marketing training directly
translate into measurable improvements in marketing performance before incorporating more sophisticated explanatory
variables.
Accordingly, this study aims to examine the implementation of social media marketing training among participating
micro-enterprises, evaluate their marketing performance following the training, and empirically assess the magnitude
of the influence of socia l media marketing training on marketing performance. By focusing specifically on micro -
enterprises, this research contributes to the literature by extending the application of the Resource -Based View within
the context of capacity -building interventions at the micro-business level, which remains relatively underrepresented
compared with studies involving small and medium -sized enterprises. Furthermore, this study adopts the validated
multidimensional marketing performance construct proposed by Lestari et al . (2024), thereby strengthening the
consistency of marketing performance measurement in future MSME research. From a practical perspective, the
findings are expected to provide evidence-based recommendations for government agencies, financial institutions, and
MSME development organizations in designing, implementing, and evaluating social media marketing training
programs while also offering guidance to micro-entrepreneurs regarding the digital marketing competencies that should
be strengthened to improve their competitiveness and long-term marketing performance.

2. Method
2.1. Research Design
This study adopted a quantitative cross -sectional associative research design to examine the relationship between
social media marketing training and the marketing performance of micro -enterprises. The quantitative approach was
considered appropriate because the objective of the study was to empirically test the hypothesized causal relationship
between the independent variable (social media marketing training) and the dependent variable (marketing
performance) using statistical analysis. A causal-associative design enables the examination of whether changes in the
independent variable are associated with variations in the dependent variable while providing empirical evidence
regarding the direction and magnitude of the observed relationship.
A cross-sectional survey was implemented to collect data from respondents at a single point in time. This design was
selected because it provides an efficient means of capturing participants' perceptions and experiences following their
participation in soc ial media marketing training while minimizing the time and resources required for longitudinal
observation. Cross-sectional surveys are widely employed in entrepreneurship, marketing, and small business research
to examine relationships among organizationa l capabilities, training interventions, and business performance under
naturally occurring conditions (Chinakidzwa & Phiri, 2020; Sultoni et al., 2022). Accordingly, the present study focuses
on identifying the extent to which participation in social media  marketing training contributes to variations in the
marketing performance of micro-enterprises.
The unit of analysis comprised individual micro -enterprises whose owners or managers had participated in at least
one social media marketing training program. Business owners were selected as respondents because they are directly
involved in strategic mark eting decision -making, the implementation of social media marketing activities, and the
evaluation of business performance. Their responses therefore provide first -hand information regarding the
effectiveness of the training program and its subsequent cont ribution to marketing outcomes. Primary data were
obtained using a structured questionnaire consisting of standardized measurement items adapted from previously
validated studies to ensure the content validity and comparability of the research findings.
The conceptual model examined in this study consisted of one independent variable, namely social media marketing
training, and one dependent variable, namely marketing performance. The proposed relationship was tested using simple
linear regression analysis to estimate the direct effect of social media marketing training on marketing performance. A
parsimonious analytical model was intentionally adopted to generate findings that are readily interpretable by
policymakers, MSME development agencies, and train ing providers seeking empirical evidence regarding the
effectiveness of social media marketing training as a capacity-building intervention for micro-enterprises.
2.2. Participants and Sampling
The target population of this study comprised micro -enterprise owners operating within the study area who had
participated in a social media marketing training program. Because the research specifically examined the effectiveness
of training on marketing performance, respondents were selected using a purposive sampling technique to ensure that
all participants possessed relevant experience with the phenomenon under investigation. This non-probability sampling
approach is widely adopted in entrepreneurship a nd small business research when respondents are required to satisfy
predefined eligibility criteria.
To be included in the study, respondents were required to meet three criteria. First, the business had to be classified
as a micro -enterprise according to the criteria stipulated in Indonesian MSME regulations. Second, the owner or
manager must have partic ipated in at least one social media marketing training program organized by either
governmental or non -governmental institutions. Third, the business had to actively utilize at least one social media
platform, such as Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp Business , or TikTok, for marketing activities. These criteria were
established to ensure that respondents had sufficient exposure to social media marketing practices and were therefore
able to provide reliable assessments regarding the effectiveness of the training.
The minimum sample size was determined based on commonly accepted recommendations for regression analysis
to ensure sufficient statistical power for estimating the proposed simple linear regression model.  Data were collected
using a structured questionnaire distributed directly to eligible respondents. Only fully completed questionnaires were
retained for subsequent statistical analysis.
2.3. Variable Measurement
This study examined two research constructs: social media marketing training as the independent variable and
marketing performance as the dependent variable. All measurement items were adapted from previously validated
instruments to ensure content validit y and enhance comparability with prior empirical studies. Minor modifications

were made to the wording of several items to improve their contextual relevance for Indonesian micro-enterprises while

The construct of social media marketing training was adapted from the digital marketing capability framework
because the selected indicators represent competencies that are expected to be developed through structured social
media marketing training . This construct reflects the extent to which training programs improve participants'
competencies in utilizing social media as a marketing tool. It was operationalized using five indicators: respondents'
understanding of the training materials, content creation skills, ability to manage social media platforms (e.g., Instagram,

and ability to evaluate promotional performance following training activities.
The dependent variable, marketing performance, was measured using indicators adapted from the multidimensional
marketing performance framework validated by Lestari et al. (2024). This construct represents the effectiveness of
marketing activities in achiev ing desirable business outcomes. Five indicators were employed to capture marketing
performance, including sales growth, customer growth, promotional effectiveness, market expansion, and the
enterprise's ability to adapt to changing market conditions. Thes e indicators collectively provide a comprehensive
assessment of marketing performance within the context of Indonesian micro-enterprises.
All measurement items were assessed using a five-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly
agree). The five-point Likert scale was selected because it provides an appropriate level of measurement for capturing
respondents' perceptions while facilitating statistical analysis in quantitative marketing research. Higher scores indicate
stronger agreement with each statement and represent higher levels of social media marketing training effectiveness
and marketing performance.
2.4. Instrument Development and Measurement Scale
Primary data were collected using a structured self-administered questionnaire designed to measure the constructs of
social media marketing training and marketing performance. The questionnaire was developed by adapting
measurement items from previously va lidated instruments reported in the relevant marketing and entrepreneurship
literature. Adopting established measurement scales enhances the content validity of the instrument and facilitates
comparison with findings from previous empirical studies. To ensure contextual suitability, the wording of several items
was carefully refined to reflect the operational characteristics and business environment of Indonesian micro-enterprises
while preserving the conceptual meaning of the original constructs.
Prior to the main data collection, the questionnaire underwent a comprehensive review to evaluate the clarity,
readability, and relevance of each measurement item. This review process was intended to minimize ambiguity, improve
respondents' comprehension, and ensure that all statements accurately represented the underlying theoretical constructs.
Particular attention was given to the terminology used in the questionnaire to ensure that it was readily understood by
micro-enterprise owners with diverse educational and business backgrounds. As a result, the final instrument comprised
measurement items that were considered appropriate for capturing respondents' perceptions regarding the effectiveness
of social media marketing training and its influence on marketing performance.
All questionnaire items were measured using a five -point Likert scale, ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5
(strongly agree). The five -point Likert scale was selected because it provides an appropriate balance between
measurement sensitivity and respondent convenience, thereby reducing response burden while maintaining sufficient
variability for statistical analysis. Moreover, the five -point scale has been extensively employed in marketing,
entrepreneurship, and small business research to assess percepti ons, attitudes, and behavioral evaluations, making it
particularly suitable for quantitative studies involving owner-managed enterprises (Marolt et al., 2022; Mu et al., 2020).
Higher scores reflected stronger agreement with the statements and indicated hi gher perceived levels of social media
marketing training effectiveness and marketing performance.
2.5. Instrument Quality Assessment
Prior to hypothesis testing, the psychometric properties of the research instrument were rigorously evaluated to ensure
that the measurement scales were both valid and reliable for assessing the proposed research constructs. Evaluating
instrument quality is a critical step in quantitative research because the validity and reliability of measurement items
directly influence the accuracy, consistency, and credibility of the subsequent statistical analysis.
Construct validity was assessed using Pearson's product–moment correlation analysis by examining the relationship
between each measurement item and the total score of its corresponding construct. This procedure was employed to
determine the extent to which  each item accurately represented the underlying theoretical concept being measured.
Items with statistically significant item–total correlations at the 5% significance level were considered valid and retained
for subsequent analyses, whereas items failing to meet the established validity criteria would be excluded from the final
measurement model.

The internal consistency reliability of the instrument was evaluated using Cronbach's alpha coefficient, which

Following widely accepted methodological recommendations in behavioral and social science research, a Cronbach's

2.6. Preliminary Data Diagnostics
Prior to estimating the regression model, a comprehensive series of diagnostic tests was conducted to verify that the
dataset satisfied the assumptions underlying the ordinary least squares (OLS) regression technique. Assessing these
assumptions is an essential step in quantitative analysis because violations may produce biased or inefficient parameter
estimates, compromise the accuracy of statistical inference, and ultimately reduce the credibility of the empirical
findings. Therefore, diagnostic testing was performed before hypothesis testing to ensure that the estimated regression
model provided valid and reliable results.
The normality of the regression residuals was first evaluated using the Kolmogorov –Smirnov test. This procedure
was employed to determine whether the residuals were normally distributed, which is one of the fundamental
assumptions of OLS regression. A significance value greater than 0.05 indicated that the residuals did not significantly
deviate from a normal distribution, suggesting that the normality assumption had been satisfied and that subsequent
parametric analyses could be conducted appropriately.
The linearity assumption was subsequently assessed using the Test for Linearity, with particular emphasis on the
Deviation from Linearity statistic. This test was conducted to verify whether the relationship between social media
marketing training and mark eting performance could be adequately represented by a linear model. A non -significant
deviation from linearity (p > 0.05) indicated that the observed relationship remained sufficiently linear, supporting the
suitability of applying simple linear regression to estimate the proposed relationship between the study variables.
To evaluate the assumption of homoscedasticity, heteroscedasticity was examined using the Glejser test and
complemented by visual inspection of standardized residual scatterplots. The Glejser test was employed to determine
whether the variance of the resid uals remained constant across different levels of the independent variable, while the
scatterplot provided a graphical assessment of residual dispersion. The absence of statistically significant Glejser test
results (p > 0.05), together with a random distr ibution of residuals around the horizontal reference line without a
discernible pattern, indicated that the assumption of homoscedasticity was fulfilled and that the regression model was
not affected by heteroscedasticity.
Collectively, these diagnostic procedures provided empirical evidence that the dataset satisfied the principal
assumptions of the OLS regression model. Consequently, the estimated regression coefficients could be interpreted with
greater confidence, ensuri ng that the subsequent hypothesis testing and statistical conclusions were based on a
methodologically sound and statistically robust analytical framework.
2.7. Data Analysis
All statistical analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics  to examine the proposed relationship between
social media marketing training and the marketing performance of micro -enterprises. The analytical procedure was
carried out in a systematic sequence to ensure the reliability, validity, and robustness of the empirical findings. Initially,
descriptive statistical analysis was employed to summarize respondents' demographic characteristics and to describe
the distribution of the research variables. Measures of central tendency and dispersion were also examined to provide a
comprehensive overview of respondents' perceptions regarding social media marketing training and marketing
performance.
Before testing the proposed hypothesis, the quality of the measurement instrument was assessed through validity and
reliability analyses. Subsequently, several diagnostic tests were performed to verify that the dataset satisfied the
assumptions underlying ordinary least squares (OLS) regression, including tests of normality, linearity, and
heteroscedasticity. These preliminary analyses were conducted to ensure that the regression estimates were unbiased,
efficient, and statistically reliable, thereby enhancing the credibility of the subsequent inferential analysis.
The proposed hypothesis was tested using simple linear regression analysis because the conceptual framework
comprised a single independent variable, namely social media marketing training, and a single dependent variable,
namely marketing performance. This analytical technique was considered appropriate for estimating the direct effect of
social media marketing training on marketing performance while simultaneously determining the direction and
magnitude of the relationship between the two constructs. The use of simple linear regression is consistent with previous
empirical studies examining the influence of training interventions and capability development on organizational and
business performance (Mu et al., 2020; Adeniyi, 2023).

The regression analysis generated several statistical indicators to evaluate the proposed model. The standardized
regression coefficient was used to determine the direction and magnitude of the effect of social media marketing training
on marketing performance. The coefficient of determination (R²) was employed to assess the proportion of variance in
marketing performance explained by the independent variable, thereby indicating the explanatory power of the model.
Furthermore, the statistical significance of the estimated regression coefficient was evaluated using the t-test, while the
associated p-value was used to determine whether the observed relationship was statistically significant.
Hypothesis testing was performed using a significance level of 5% (α = 0.05) . The proposed hypothesis was
supported when the regression coefficient was positive and statistically significant ( p < 0.05), indicating that
participation in social media marketing training significantly improved the marketing performance of micro-enterprises.
Conversely, a significance value greater than 0.05 indicated insufficient empirical evidence to support the pr oposed
relationship. The findings were interpreted based on the estimated regression coefficient, coefficient of determination,
t-statistic, and significance value to provide a comprehensive assessment of the magnitude, direction, and statistical
significance of the hypothesized relationship.

3.6. Discussion
The positive effect of social media marketing training on marketing performance can be explained by the
improvement of participants' competencies in content creation, platform management, customer interaction, and
promotional evaluation, which enable micro-enterprises to implement more effective marketing activities (Chinakidzwa
& Phiri, 2020; Sharabati et al., 2024). Within the framework of the Resource -Based View, the results suggest that
training-induced competencies in content creation, platform management, customer interaction, and promotional
evaluation functio n as valuable, difficult -to-imitate organizational capabilities that generate superior marketing
outcomes even among the smallest business units, thereby extending the applicability of the Resource -Based View to
the micro-enterprise level, which has received comparatively limited empirical attention relative to small and medium-
sized enterprises.
The magnitude of the explained variance (38.3%) is broadly consistent with, and in some cases exceeds, the effect
sizes reported in studies employing more complex multivariate models with mediating or moderating variables (Marolt
et al., 2022; Umboh & Arya nto, 2022), suggesting that a substantial portion of the training -performance relationship

                Saputra et al. / International Journal of Research in Community Service, Vol. 7, No. 4, pp. 249-262, 2026                             261

can already be captured through a direct, parsimonious bivariate specification. This finding lends support to the
argument that, for practical intervention evaluation purposes, a straightforward causal -associative design can provide
policymakers and MSME d evelopment agencies with sufficiently robust and readily interpretable evidence without
necessarily requiring complex Structural Equation Modeling procedures (Mu et al., 2020; Adeniyi, 2023).
These results are also consistent with earlier Indonesian studies reporting that digital and social media marketing
contribute positively to MSME performance and sustainability (Patma et al., 2021; Syaifullah et al., 2021; Sultoni et
al., 2022). However, whereas those studies generally examined digital marketing as a broad organizational capability,
the present study isolates social media marketing training as a specific, measurable, and replicable intervention, thereby
providing more actionable evidence fo r institutions designing capacity -building programs for micro -enterprises. The
demographic pattern observed in this study, in which culinary micro-enterprises predominantly relied on Instagram and
WhatsApp Business, further reinforces the relevance of the training content emphasized in the Fu et al. (2024) digital
marketing capability framework, particularly platform management and customer interaction skills, as priority
competencies for Indonesian micro-entrepreneurs.
From a practical standpoint, the results imply that government agencies, MSME development organizations, and
financial institutions can reasonably expect measurable improvements in marketing performance from structured social
media marketing training programs, even when implemented as a stand-alone intervention. Nevertheless, because social
media marketing training accounted for only 38.3% of the variance in marketing performance, the remaining
unexplained variance suggests that complementary factors, such as digital literacy, financial capital, product quality,
and entrepreneurial self-efficacy, likely operate alongside training to jointly determine marketing outcomes, an avenue
that future research employing more elaborate explanatory models could usefully pursue.
4. Conclusion
This study provides empirical evidence of a significant positive association between social media marketing training
and the marketing performance of Indonesian micro -enterprises, indicating that participants reporting higher training
effectiveness also reported better marketing performance. The findings demonstrate that structured training programs
contribute positively to improving entrepreneurs' marketing capabilities, which are reflected in better promotional
effectiveness, customer growth, market expansion, and overall marketing performance. These results support the view
that strengthening digital marketing competencies through targeted training represents an effective strategy for
improving the competitiveness of micro-enterprises in an increasingly digital business environment.
From a theoretical perspective, this study extends the digital marketing and resource -based capability literature by
demonstrating that social media marketing training constitutes a valuable organizational resource capable of generating
superior marketing outcomes. Unlike previous studies that primarily focused on complex mediation or moderation
mechanisms, the present research provides direct empirical evidence of the relationship between training interventions
and marketing performance, offering a simpler yet practically relevant evaluation framework for micro-enterprises.
Practically, the findings suggest that government agencies, business development organizations, and training
providers should prioritize continuous and application -oriented social media marketing training as part of micro -
enterprise empowerment programs. Future research is encouraged to incorporate larger and more geographically diverse
samples, employ longitudinal research designs to examine long-term training effects, and include additional explanatory
variables such as digital literacy, innovation capability, or entrepreneurial orientation to develop a more comprehensive
understanding of the mechanisms through which digital marketing training enhances business performance.
