Abstract
This study investigates the intersection of digital literacy and modern marketing by
examining "mocking" as a sophisticated persuasive strategy. Utilizing a genre-based rhetorical
move framework and multimodal discourse analysis, this research analyzes a video -advertisement
corpus of four Samsung ads targeting Apple on YouTube and Instagram (2017 –2024).
The objective is to explore how advertising success relies on the audience's digital literacy to decode
intertextual references, satirical nuances, and informal discourse. Sarcasm, sarcastic irony, and
parody serve as primary analytical references. The findings indicate that Samsung maintains
traditional adverti sing generic conventions while adapting mocking strategies as creative,
noncanonical moves to meet social media communication expectations. Specifically, Samsung
retains six of nine rhetorical moves –  four being obligatory – while four non -canonical moves
dominantly construct the competitive persuasive narrative. From a media literacy education
perspective, these findings underscore that being a digitally native audience is insufficient without
critical advertising literacy. The capacity to deconstruct how humor and rule -breaking generic
moves are weaponized as commercial tools is paramount. By exposing the structural mechanics of
corporate satire, this research bridges applied linguistics with media pedagogy, offering a practical
framework for educators to teach students how to resist sub -cognitive persuasion critically.
Ultimately, this study expands the understanding of genre adaptation in digital advertising by
integrating mocking strategies amidst multimodal advertising, highlighting its critical implica tions
for media literacy education in an increasingly commercialized digital ecosystem.
Keywords: digital literacy,  genre-based approach, mocking strategy, persuasion, social
media advertisements.

1. Introduction
The development of advertising genres over  time is accompanied by the evolution of generic
conventions that adapt to the mode of the platform to which the genre is intended. This
development arises from the need for marketers, as linguistic actors, to achieve the communicative
goals of an advertis ement amidst the growing relevance of advertising platforms. Social media has
become a highly demanded advertising platform, driven by the extensive use of platforms, which
has made marketers increasingly aware of the business opportunities available withi n these
channels (K V et al., 2021). As of April 2025, social media users reached 5.03 billion, accounting for
64.7 percent of the global population, including active users of Instagram and YouTube ( Kepios,
2025). The considerable impact of social media on customers' everyday lives has rendered these
platforms essential for marketers to promote their products ( S. et al., 2016 ). Given its vast user

It encourages using shorter, visually appealing, interactive formats tailored to real -time audience

specific behaviors and preferences on each platform. From a media literacy e ducation perspective,

Consequently, this dynamic shift points out the importance of media literacy education,
which shifts from teaching basic decoding skills to fostering critical digital literac y.
As advertisements increasingly blur the lines between organic content and commercial persuasion
– such as through influencer marketing and native advertising –  media literacy education is
essential to empower users to critically analyze the underlying i ntent, ideological agendas, and
algorithmic targeting behind these evolving genres ( Akseiit et al., 2026; Hobbs, 2021 ).
By equipping individuals with the ability to deconstruct persuasive digital narratives, media
literacy education transforms active platf orm participants into critical, autonomous citizens
capable of navigating a commercialized digital ecosystem ( Yu et al., 2025 ). From a media literacy
education perspective, this strategic evolution highlights the need to foster critical persuasion
knowledge among digital consumers. As brands increasingly blur the boundary between
entertainment and commercial intent through rule -breaking narrative structures, media literacy
education must pivot toward decoding these modern, non -traditional genres ( Paul et al ., 2024 ).
While a "media -savvy" audience might easily consume and engage with playful content, they still
require advanced critical analysis to deconstruct how humor, irony, and manufactured authenticity
are systematically deployed to bypass cognitive resi stance and manipulate brand attitudes ( Yoo,
Cho, 2025). Ultimately, integrating these shifting generic conventions into media literacy curricula
empowers users to enjoy the creative and interactive dimensions of social media culture without
overlooking the  underlying persuasion techniques and economic agendas of digital advertising
(Foster et al., 2024).
An advertisement can be recognized as a genre, as discourse constructs in a professional
environment require adherence to certain norms within the advertisement ( Khedri et al., 2022).
As competition in social media advertising increases, generic conventions in the advertising genre,
especially in the form of videos, are being created and violated to win the attention of potential
buyers. Ads launched on soci al media belong to a digital genre with flexible moves that can adapt
non-linear information transmission ( Askehave, Nielsen, 2005; Li et al., 2022). With the rise of
social media platforms such as YouTube and Instagram, brands have begun adopting alternat ive
advertising strategies, such as mockery blatantly at competitors, to attract a younger and more
media-savvy audience. The noticeable shift in advertising moves and strategies makes brands likely
to adopt a more playful and relatable tone to connect wit h their audiences. Focusing on
authenticity, humor, and qualities that today's consumers appreciate more than relying on a
traditional, formal marketing structure. Companies aim to create personal and memorable content
through this approach, helping them build stronger engagement and lasting brand loyalty.
The generic conventions of the advertisement genre serve to enhance the purpose of
communication while helping the creator organize marketing concepts. The conventions are built
through moves in the advertising genre. There are nine rhetorical moves in the advertisement
genre: headlines, targeting the market, justifying the product or service, detailing the product or
service, establishing credentials, celebrity or typical user endorsement, offering incentives, using
pressure tactics, and soliciting response ( Bhatia, 2014; Bhatia, 1993; Kathpalia, 1992 ). Despite
moves that have long been proposed by experts, in a world evolving to multimodality, creators
creatively modify some of what is lexico -grammatically recognized as traditional ad genre moves
(Bhatia, 2014 ). Modifications are done by utilizing the relevance of tone and trending topics,
especially in social media, such as mocking strategies. Modifying generic conventions and
constraints with a risky strategy is often a marker of user expertise. However, caution must be
exercised, as the results can lead to creative innovation or embarrassing failure (Jones, 2012).
Video advertisements with mocking strategies aired on social media often use visual elements

advertisements ( Gatti et al., 2015; Sabr i, Hana, 2021 ). Therefore, this research investigates how
mocking strategies are embedded within the genre convention of Samsung's video advertisements
by identifying obligatory and non- obligatory moves and examining their persuasive functions in
social media.

2. Materials and methods
This study employs a qualitative method to comprehend linguistic devices and modalities in
both verbal and visual forms. Qualitative research involves interpretation that connects the world
to representations of notes, conversations, interviews, photographs, memos, and self -recordings
(Creswell, 2013). This research's analysis focuses on the rhetorical moves (Bhatia, 2014) underlying
persuasive communication, alongside non- canonical moves that offer flexibility and serve  as
creative alternatives employed by Samsung in its social media advertisements, utilizing a mocking
strategy in its video advertising content. The frequency of the moves' appearances determines their
status to be the obligatory or optional moves in the a dvertisement to help conclude how the
advertisement maintains the rhetorical moves and the new non -canonical moves as an adjustment
to the development of the conventional advertising world. Dynel's concept ( Dynel, 2018) identifies
sarcasm and sarcastic iro ny as strategies within existing mockery, while parody in the context of
mocking strategy is examined through Hutcheon's framework (Hutcheon, 2000).
The research materials were chosen according to the prevalence of mocking strategies
employed in Samsung's advertising videos from 2017 to 2024. The videos include Upgrade to a
Samsung Galaxy Note 8  (Samsung, 2017), Ingenious (Samsung, 2018), Do more with an Apple
than Apple ever did?  (Samsung, 2024 ) and Upgrade Your Upgrade  (Samsung, 2024 ). As the
object of this research is an advertising video, the multimodal discourse theory ( Kress, Van
Leeuwen, 2020) serves as the basic framework for the comprehensive visual analysis required.

3. Discussion
Samsung is consistently ranked among the top two smartphone makers, alongside Apple,
Xiaomi, Oppo, Vivo, and others. This ranking is based on Samsung's market share in 2023, which
reached 28 % in the North American smartphone market, while Apple simultaneously led with over
52 % of the market share ( Onyango, 2025 ). Apple's position ahead of Samsung spurred
competition between the two companies. From 2018 to 2024, Samsung has consistently utilized
social media such as Ins tagram and YouTube to release ads that mock Apple and highlight its
advantages over Apple, as Instagram has become a public forum that reaches out to
entrepreneurial things because of its social alert ( Sari et al., 2022 ). Through ads that mock Apple
published on YouTube and Instagram, Samsung creatively manages some of what Bhatia proposed
as rhetorical moves for the advertisement genre (Bhatia, 2014).
With an in- depth review of four Samsung video advertisements, six of the nine rhetorical
moves in the advertisement genre that Samsung maintains in its video advertisements can be
discussed in this section. The six moves appear consistently in almost every  Samsung video
advertisement, although not all the moves' appearances are equally prominent. Moves whose
appearance reaches 75 % to 100 % of the total four advertisements are labeled as obligatory moves,
which derive headlines, target the market, justify the product or service, and detail the product or
service and become the obligatory rhetorical moves in Samsung video advertisements. According to
Swale's private conversation with Onder in 2011, there is no specific frequency limit for a move to

Headlines
Among the Samsung advertisements analyzed, the most prominent realization of the headline
move appears in the video titled Do more with an Apple than Apple's ever done . The  sentence
"Do more with an apple than Apple's ever done" functions as a headline not  only based on its
declarative form but also on the ambiguity and humor created by the wordplay. The phrase makes a
double meaning by juxtaposing "apple" and "Apple" – the difference in capitalization features the
letter "a" in both words. Apple builds con text ambiguity between the real apple fruit and the Apple
brand as Samsung's competitor, as this ad does not discuss fruits but focuses on introducing
Samsung's smartphone products. This technique aligns with the role of headlines in advertisements
created to attract readers' attention (Bhatia, 2014; Chaidet, Pupipat, 2021).
What strengthens the rhetorical effect of the headline is the use of intersemiotic
complementarity ( Kress, Van Leeuwen, 2020), where visual and verbal modes reinforce one
another. The s cene features the text "Do more with an Apple than Apple's ever done" against the
background of an actor pointing a red Apple fruit at the camera, which draws greater focus than the
other objects behind the text. The scene is shot in a medium close- up shot , which supports a
surface-level meaning that creates a literal meaning to the word "Apple" as a real apple fruit. This
condition creates a metaphorical contrast for the textual reference to the brand Apple, which
stimulates viewer inference and engagement. This interplay is a deliberate communicative strategy
to foreground critique through playful ambiguity. As a big brand that blatantly beats its
competitors, Samsung applies this sensational headline to increase its engagement with the
audience. A stunning headline positively influences the increase of online video audience
engagement on social media ads ( Dai, Wang, 2023). Samsung uses these moves in three of its four
video ads, which means that the communicative function of these moves is considered essential,
and they become obligatory moves that Samsung maintains in its ads on social media.
Targeting The Market
The four Samsung advertising videos in this study show that Samsung is targeting its market
at Apple users and potential Apple users to switch to Samsung. This narrowed targeting is
considered to help brands structure their media more effectively ( Fulgoni, 2018 ).
In advertisements, Samsung focuses on story themes that consistently compare its products with
Apple-identified products. In the ad Upgrade Your Upgrade, Samsung highlighted the lag of
upgrading Apple devices in each new product; in the Ingenius  ad, Samsung depicted Apple users
who came to Apple's Genius Bar to ask why their devices could not do what Apple could. These ads
emphasize th e advantages that Samsung provides that cannot be found in Apple, implying that

Samsung’s owned advertisement content video widely distributes its targeting to audiences

Justifying The Product or Service
This move is also one of the moves that Samsung uses consistently. To exemplify this move,
the present study highlights an Upgrade to a Samsung Galaxy Note 8  as the most representative
case among the four analyzed videos. This move involves the advertiser providing rational and
functional reasons on why Apple users and potential users should switch to Samsung, just as
justifying the product or service provides reasons why the product offered will fulfill the buyers'
needs ( Bhatia, 2014; Moes et al., 2022 ). The reasons are delivered through contrasting
comparisons of what Samsung highlights between its products and Apple products, which focuses
on Apple's weaknesses, such as its small storage, less modern digital feature innovation, and
unproven devices.
Samsung also utilizes visual depictions between the two brands, where a scene shows how
Samsung positions its products and Apple in contrasting lighting after both have just been lifted
from the water incident. In this scene, the Apple user, as the carrier is a man, is depicted holding an
iPhone to remind the audience that the phone that survives even though it has entered the water is
a Samsung smartphone. The possessive attribution relation between Apple products and the owner
emphasizes the different values between Samsung and Apple products ( Kress, Leeuwen, 2020).
In addition, the brighter light on the Samsung side and the darker on the Apple side align with
salience in compositional meaning, where the placement of a feature is intended to attract more
attention from the audience.
The spotlight on Samsung is not necessarily to highlight Samsung's ability to survive water
better than Apple but also as an invitation to assess the superior function of the brand. Apple is
known as a hedonic brand because its users are primarily concerned with self-expression compared
to the quality of a brand ( Salehi et al., 2021 ). The pairing of Samsung as a utilitarian brand
concerned with functional quality and Apple provides a utilitarian dominance effect, where the
attention and focus of potential buyers will be more focused on Samsung ( Okada, 2005; Shao, Li,
2021). This move helps direct potential consumers' views towards Samsung as a more valuable
brand, not only focusing on lifestyle and prestige.
Detailing the Product or Service
Not all of the analyzed Samsung ad videos included the segment that details the product or
service. In the three ads that use this move, the delivery is subtle and tries not to be the primary
concern in the video. The most significant appearance of this move is in the Upgrade Your
Upgrade ad.
Samsung places the coverage text containing detailed information about Samsung Galaxy AI
features that potential buyers may need at the bottom center of the video frame with a very small
font size. Through the s mall text, Samsung is honest to its consumers by including a disclaimer
about the expected results: “The accuracy and the reliability of the generated output are not
guaranteed." This honesty shows the transparency of the Samsung brand for the shortcomings that
might occur, avoiding expectations that do not match the reality of potential customers. The tiny
font size of the text and placement at the bottom is a strategy to ensure that the ad's focus on
playing Apple remains the main attention of the audienc e (Kress, Van Leeuwen, 2020). However,
coloring the text with a bright color that contrasts with the color of the video as a background
highlights it so that it can still be noticed by the audience's eyes that there is information that can
be obtained (Duong, 2022).

The use of celebrities or typical users for endorsement is the move with the least frequency of
use. These moves help Samsung attract potential buyers who align with the endorsed celebrity to
play a role in advertising. Endorsements from celebrities, mainly social media influencers, will
enhance the trust of prospective consumers who have common interests with the influencer ( Ge et
al., 2021). Samsung only uses this move in the Do more with an apple than Apple's ever done  ad.

Competitive Referencing
The first non- canonical rhetorical move found in Samsung video advertisements is
competitive referencing. This type of move applies to the intentional mentioning of Apple as the
Samsung brand's competitor in ads, whether overtly or subtly. Although this concept is not listed in
the existing classification of moves in ads, its repeated occurrence and role in building the
audience's drive to engage with the ad make it justifiable to classify it as a distinct move category in
this study. This idea is also aligned with t he competitor -centered assessments idea of corporate
strategies that directly show comparisons with specific competitors (Day, Wensley, 1998).
Comparing products or brands in advertisements can increase the positive engagement of the
audience towards the advertisement (Jain et al., 2015). In the previous ad, Upgrade to a Samsung

Fake User Experience
Marketers present scripted experiences as if they were real user experiences.
The advertisement, which portrays the user’s feelings, leads the audience to judge the product
based on a fake user experience. Deliberately created advertising strategies can in fluence a user’s
perception by shaping their behavior on the internet ( Hettler et al., 2024). Fake user experience is
dominant in the advertisement Upgrade to a Samsung Galaxy Note 8  in 2017, which shows the
storyline of the bad experience of Apple users who finally switched to Samsung.
There are many ways to demonstrate a product ( Ringler et al., 2024). One does not show the
whole real process and only fixates on the result displayed. This situation occurs in the scene where
Apple users run out of memory w hile trying to take a picture, without Samsung explaining the
reasons for the device’s memory exhaustion. In addition, there is also a scene where Samsung and
Apple devices both fall into the water, but the Samsung smartphone still survives. The explanatio n
does not cover the water depth, the fall depth of the two smartphones, or whether the two
smartphones from different brands are in the exact same original condition as each other. The use
shapes potential users' views of Samsung and Apple, inviting them to quickly conclude the
advantages and disadvantages without thinking critically about the facts. This advertising video
uploaded on YouTube uses the theme of user -generated vlogs that focus on fictional user
experiences through content marketing, which of ten successfully increases the perception and
purchase intention of potential users as an audience (Diwanji, Lee, 2022).
Referential Mocking
This study categorizes move -referential mocking as a situation where the audience refers to
the competitor through mockery and critical evaluation. The mockery directed at Apple as a
competitor in this study includes sarcasm, sarcastic irony, and parody. The occurrence of this type
of mockery is different in each Samsung ad. The Ingenious ad is the most appropriate to represent
the analysis of these moves, as this thirty-second ad displays the most mockery.
The central theme of the Ingenious ad is a question-and-answer section between Apple users
and Apple employees titled Genius, who have been specially trained to solve user problems at the
Genius Bar. An exaggerated storyline parodies the situation between Apple employees and users,
highlighting the humorous and awkward aspects of their interactions. Parody is a form of
repetition that emphasizes ironic differences ami dst repeated similarities ( Hutcheon, 2000).
The naivety of Apple users who deliberately come to the genius bar to ask how to use features on
the Samsung Galaxy S9 device but not on the iPhone device highlights Apple's innovation lag and
Samsung's superiority. In addition, through the medium close -up shot and eye -level angle,
Samsung allows the audience to be equal to the actors in the scene and seems to feel that the
audience becomes more involved and can see more clearly what is happening in the s cene (Kress,
Van Leeuwen, 2020). As a result, criticism and mockery are made entertaining but still effective in
conveying persuasive communication from Samsung, which is ultimately humorous.
In addition, the word “ingenious” becomes sarcastic irony towards the image of the genius staff
itself. Ironically, the genius cannot answer and solve the questions that Apple users ask about the split-
screen feature available on Samsung devices. This condition exemplifies sarcastic irony by contrasting
expectations with reality in order to critique that reality (Dynel, 2018). In addition, the content of the
conversation between the two actors also produces another mockery strategy, namely sarcasm.

Referring to the Manufacturing Company
The last non- canonical rhetorical move in Samsung video advertisements consistently
identifies the company by featuring the Samsung logo. The placement of the logo at the end is a
visual reminder that the entire content in the advertisement that has just been watched is a work
by Samsung dedicated to its products.
Black and white design is li nked to social position, individuality, and authenticity – essential

seems simple and indicates that Samsung wants to show its authority with elegance  and not much
of a fun purpose.

4. Results
The analysis of the four Samsung advertisements shows that amidst the advancement of
social media that allows marketers to maximize their creativity in making advertisements,
Samsung can adapt to maintaining rhetorical moves while utilizing mocking strategies as an
adjustment in achieving persuasive communication goals. There are four rhetorical moves from
Bhatia ( Bhatia, 2014 ) that Samsung makes as obligatory moves as the four moves’ occurrence
reaches more t han 75 %. Moves that become obligatory include headlines, targeting the market,
justifying the product or service, and detailing the product or service. These moves can be used
more generally in Samsung's advertising themes that adopt mocking strategies wi thout involving
representative individuals personally, as in the move “celebrity or typical user endorsement,” and
without being so obvious as to provide incentives to expect direct feedback from the audience, as in
the move offering incentives.
In addition to the rhetorical moves by Bhatia ( Bhatia, 2014 ), moves whose frequency of
occurrence reaches 75  % to 100 % are also found in the non -canonical moves category. All non-
canonical moves can be classified as obligatory moves in Samsung advertisements  that use the
mocking approach, as their occurrence is consistent with a reasonably stable percentage where only
the fake user experience move gets a score below 100 %, which is 80 %. Noncanonical, including
competitive referencing, fake user experience, a nd referential mocking, is consistently used to
spoof and mock Apple while justifying Samsung's advantages that make it appear superior to Apple
even though Apple still occupies the first position, leading the smartphone market in most
countries in the world (Onyango, 2025).
The strategy of lifting the theme of mockery in Samsung's competitive advertising against Apple's
loss of innovation features from Samsung, which continues to be carried out from 2017 to 2024,
indicates that Samsung is continuously increasing its brand awareness to society that functionally,
Samsung is a better smartphone choice than Apple. The combination of rhetoric with non-canonical
moves that carry the function of persuasive communication into emotional entertainment through
sarcasm, sarcastic irony, and parody as the mocking strategy adapts to the needs of the social media
market, which is no longer only focused on information about products being sold and pressure to buy
products that are being marketed. As emotional appeals have become increasingly central in digital
marketing, social media platforms provide a unique space for emotionally intelligent advertising
(Yaseen et al., 2022). This communication strategy shows that Samsung maintains the basic rhetorical
structure in the advertising genre and expands its communicative scope by adapting content to suit
social media's more fluid, interactive characteristics based on emotional resonance and popular culture.
One way to achieve further realism in social media is through interacti vity ( S et al., 2020 ).
The emotional engagement built through the mocking strategy in this Samsung video advertisement
also makes the ad more memorable and shareable, an essential factor in a social media ecosystem that
relies heavily on engagement and virality.

5. Conclusion
The data shows that, in general, Samsung maintains the generic conventions of the advertising
genre while preserving most of the rhetorical moves. Six out of nine existing rhetorical moves for
advertisement are found to appear with different frequencies, and four moves with an occurrence
frequency above 75 % become obligatory moves in Samsung advertisements. The  obligatory moves
identified include headlines, targeting the market, justifying the product or service, and detailing the
product or service, as moves offering incentives and celebrity or typical user endorsement become the
optional actions in Samsung advertisement videos due to their occurrence in only 25 % and 50 %
among all four advertisements. These moves may involve the form of verbal, visual, or a combination of
the two, where the perspective of multimodal discourse analysis assists in reaching the analytical
outcomes. These moves demonstrate that, despite Samsung’s use of the mockery theme in the
advertisements, the fundamental framework of the advertising genre remains intact. From a media
literacy perspective, this structural preservation serves as a critical pedagogical template;
it demonstrates that beneath highly entertaining and disruptive digital content, the found ational,
persuasive architecture of traditional marketing remains intact, providing a concrete baseline for
students to learn how to identify hidden commercial intents.
In addition to using rhetorical moves, Samsung also employs several non -canonical moves
that are consistent across all the commercial videos examined. The categories of non -canonical
moves include competitive referencing, fake user experience, referential mocking, and referring to
the manufacturing company. These moves become a tool for Sams ung to convey the mockery
message to Apple through sarcasm, sarcastic irony, and parody. The combination of rhetorical and
non-canonical moves implied by mocking strategies not only criticizes and mocks Apple as a
competitor but also highlights Samsung's i nnovation, which is beyond Apple, raising brand
awareness that Samsung is a better choice than Apple functionally. Consequently, these non -
canonical tactics highlight the evolution required in media literacy curricula. Educators must move
beyond teaching b asic text-decoding to foster "critical persuasion knowledge," training audiences
to deconstruct how advanced literary and visual devices like parody, irony, and manufactured user
experiences are strategically weaponized to lower consumer cognitive resistance.
Overall, amidst the advancement of advertising modes in social media platforms, mocking
strategies can be an alternative to advertising creativity that can attract audience attention in
conventional advertising. Mocking strategies that achieve emotiona l engagement can also attract
the interactive side of social media, which prioritizes shareability, virality, and engagement.
Ultimately, this points out the urgent need for algorithmic and digital advertising literacy within
modern education. Because these creative mocking strategies exploit the viral, participatory nature
of social media, media literacy education must empower users to look past the emotional
amusement of shareable memes and corporate banter, allowing them to remain critical,
autonomous consumers within a highly commercialized digital ecosystem.
