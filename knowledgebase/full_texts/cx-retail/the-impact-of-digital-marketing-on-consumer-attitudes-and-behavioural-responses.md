1. Introduction

Digital technologies have shifted the relationship between businesses and consumers away from predominantly one -
way communication and toward an environment that is continuous, interactive, and measurable. Consumers are no longer
merely recipients of promotional messages. They search for information, compare prices and quality, consult user reviews,
communicate with brands, create content, and influence other people's  decisions. Digital marketing therefore operates not
only at the point of purchase, but throughout the cognitive, affective, and behavioural processes that precede and follow it
(Stephen, 2016; Dwivedi et al., 2021).
In practice, digital marketing encompasses social media advertising, search engines, email communication, influencer
marketing, mobile  applications, algorithmic recommendations, branded content, and electronic communication among
consumers. Each instrument may serve a different purpose. A brief advertisement can trigger need recognition; a detailed
product page can support information search; reviews and comparisons can alter the ranking of alternatives; a personalised
offer can encourage purchase; and a request for feedback can turn a post-purchase experience into a public recommendation.
The traditional decision -making process is commonly described through five stages: need recognition, information
search, evaluation of alternatives, purchase decision, and post-purchase behaviour. In digital settings, however, this process is
not necessarily linear. Consumers may return from evaluation to search, revise their attitude after reading a negative comment,

personalisation, interac tivity, entertainment, credibility, and electronic word -of-mouth (eWOM) —shape attitudes and

communication depends more on usefulness, credibility, and authentic social evidence than on entertainment alone. The
findings extend evidence from a Southeast European setting and show that favourable attitudes translate into purchases
only when perceived value, affordability, payment, and delivery conditions permit conversion. Managers should therefore
combine transparent information and trustworthy reviews with financially clear offers and sustained post -purchase
support.

Consumer attitude occupies a central place in this process. It captures the overall favourable or unfavourable evaluation
of a brand, product, advertisement, platform, or purchasing behaviour. According to the Theory of Planned Behaviour, a more
favourable attitude increases the likelihood that an intention to act will  be formed. However, the transition from intention to
actual behaviour is shaped by perceived control, social norms, and situational conditions (Ajzen, 1991). In digital marketing ,
exposure alone does not automatically produce a purchase: the message must first be processed, trusted, and judged useful
or relevant.
Albania offers a pertinent setting in which to examine these relationships. Internet use, smartphones, social networks,
and online sales channels have become routine elements of consumer practice.  Research conducted in the country has
highlighted the role of social media and content applications in marketing communication (Vukatana et al., 2022), the influence
of digital marketing and interpersonal recommendations on consumer behaviour (Braholli et  al., 2023), and the association
between demographic characteristics and online shopping activity (Braimllari & Nerjaku, 2021). Fejzaj and Gogo (2025) likewise
note the growing importance of online marketing in Albanian consumers' decisions.
Existing Albanian evidence is fragmented in three specific respects. First, studies commonly examine a single platform
or channel rather than comparing the distinct effects of informativeness, personalisation, interactivity, entertainment,
credibility, and eWOM within o ne model. Second, most work stops at purchase intention and therefore does not distinguish
engagement, completed purchasing, and post -purchase recommendation. Third, the mediating role of attitude, the
moderating role of media literacy, and differences acr oss decision-journey stages have not been tested jointly in this market.
The present study addresses these omissions through an integrated model that separates promotional stimuli, internal
evaluation, and multiple behavioural responses.
The purpose of this study is to examine how the dimensions of digital marketing shape consumer attitude and, through
it, purchase intention, online engagement, purchase behaviour, and electronic recommendation intention. The study also
investigates the moderating role of  digital literacy and variations across stages of the decision process. Its contribution is
threefold: it integrates three theoretical perspectives, develops a multidimensional model suited to the Albanian market, and
offers managerial guidance on content, credibility, interaction, and converting engagement into behaviour.

1.1. Theoretical foundation and hypothesis development

The Stimulus–Organism–Response framework explains how features of the online environment affect internal cognitive
and emotional evaluations and subsequently produce approach or avoidance responses (Mehrabian & Russell, 1974). In the
present model, the six digital communication features are stimuli, attitude is the organismic evaluation, and intention,
engagement, purchasing, and recommendation are responses. The Theory of Planned Behaviour further explains the attitude–
intention–behaviour sequence ( Ajzen, 1991), while the customer -journey perspective locates these effects across need
recognition, search, evaluation, purchase, and post-purchase activity (Lemon & Verhoef, 2016).

1.2. Digital communication features and attitude

Informativeness reduces uncertainty by supplying relevant, timely, and decision -useful details about price, attributes,
availability, warranties, and delivery. Information that improves comparison and perceived usefulness should support a more
favourable evaluation (Davis, 1989; Tilahun & Berhan, 2023). Accordingly, H1 proposes that perceived informativeness has a
positive effect on consumer attitude.
Personalisation can reduce search costs by aligning recommendations and offers with individual interests, although its
benefits depend on transparent and proportionate data use. When relevance outweighs privacy concerns, consumers should
evaluate the commu nication more favourably. Accordingly, H2 proposes that perceived personalisation has a positive effect
on consumer attitude.
Interactivity enables questions, feedback, participation, and greater consumer control. Prompt and useful responses
can reduce unc ertainty and signal accessibility, thereby encouraging favourable evaluations and approach behaviour.
Accordingly, H3 proposes that perceived interactivity has a positive effect on consumer attitude.
Entertainment can attract attention and strengthen recal l through enjoyment, creativity, and emotional stimulation
(Kim & Ko, 2012; Duffett, 2017). When this appeal remains connected to relevant product information, it should contribute
positively to evaluation. Accordingly, H4 proposes that perceived entertainment has a positive effect on consumer attitude.
Credibility reflects perceived honesty, competence, transparency, and realism. In settings characterised by sponsored
content, uncertain sellers, and manipulated reviews, credible sources reduce scepticism and perceived risk (Lou & Yuan, 2019;
Sokolova & Kefi, 2020). Accordingly, H5 proposes that digital marketing credibility has a positive effect on consumer attitud e.
Electronic word-of-mouth provides experience -based social evidence through ratings, comment s, recommendations,
and user -generated content. Such evidence can strengthen knowledge and confidence before purchase (Ismagilova et al.,
2020). Accordingly, H6 proposes that positive eWOM has a positive effect on consumer attitude.

1.3. Attitude and behavioural responses

Attitude represents the consumer’s overall favourable or unfavourable evaluation and is expected to guide subsequent
responses. A favourable evaluation should increase readiness to buy (H7), online engagement (H8), and electronic
recommendation intention (H10), while purchase intention should predict self -reported purchase behaviour (H9). Because
promotional features are interpreted before they affect readiness to buy, H11a –H11f propose that attitude mediates the
relationship between each of the six features and purchase intention.

1.4. Moderation and decision-journey differences

Media literacy should improve consumers’ ability to locate, compare, and verify online information; H12 therefore
proposes that it strengthens the effect of  informativeness on attitude. Because experience and information processing may
vary with age, H13 proposes differences in model relationships across age groups. Finally, because online channels serve
different functions before, during, and after purchase,  H14 proposes that their perceived influence differs across decision -
journey stages.

2. Materials and Methods

2.1. Research approach and study design

The study was grounded in a positivist paradigm an d followed a deductive approach, through which relationships
derived from the theoretical framework were tested empirically. Informativeness, personalisation, interactivity,
entertainment, digital marketing credibility, electronic consumer -to-consumer comm unication, consumer attitude, and
behavioural responses were operationalised through measurable indicators and incorporated into an integrated structural
model.
A quantitative, cross -sectional survey design was used, with the individual consumer as the uni t of analysis. To be
eligible, participants had to be at least 18 years old, reside in Albania, use at least one digital platform, and have search ed
online for information about products or services during the previous six months. They were also required t o have made, or
seriously considered, a purchase influenced by advertisements, recommendations, reviews, or other forms of digital content
during that period. Quotas were established for gender (target: 50% female, 50% male), age group (targets: 18 –24: 18%; 25–
34: 28%; 35–44: 26%; 45–54: 16%; 55+: 12%), region of residence (Tirana: 48%; other regions: 52%), educational attainment
(secondary: 34%; undergraduate /college: 44%; postgraduate: 22%), and employment status (employed: 62%; unemployed:
11%; student: 21%; other: 6%), with targets based on the most recent national census data to better reflect the distribution of
the active population of digital platform users in Albania. In the final sample of 487 valid responses, the gender split was 56.9%
female and 42.1% male, with 1% selecting another option or not reporting their gender. Participants aged 25 –34 represented
28.1%, those aged 35–44 made up 26.3%, and the remainder were distributed across other age groups in proportions close to
the established quotas. By region, 46.2% of respondents lived in Tirana and 53.8% in other areas. Educational and employment

2.2. Sample and data collection procedure

The final sample comprised 487 Albanian c onsumers who met the eligibility criteria. Given the number of constructs,
the number of indicators, and the complexity of the relationships specified in the model, this sample size was considered
adequate for assessing both the measurement and structural models.
Participants were recruited through a combination of purposive and quota sampling. Indicative quotas were used to
achieve a more balanced distribution by gender, age, region of residence, educational attainment, and employment status.
This strategy was intended to prevent the sample from being concentrated in a single consumer category and to reflect the
diversity of digital-channel users in Albania more closely.
The questionnaire was distributed primarily online through  social networks, email, professional groups, and online
communities. Where feasible, digital distribution was supplemented by assisted completion in physical settings. This made it
possible to include consumers who use digital platforms in purchasing deci sions but are less likely to participate in online
surveys.
Screening questions were presented before the main questionnaire. Responses from individuals who did not meet the
study criteria were excluded from the final dataset.

2.3. Measurement instrument and construct operationalisation

Data were collected through a structured questionnaire organised into several sections. The first section contained
screening questions, and the second gathered information on digital platform use and participants' experie nce with online
search and purchasing. Subsequent sections measured perceptions of digital marketing characteristics, attitudes toward
brands that communicate through digital channels, purchase intention, online engagement, self-reported purchase behaviour,
and electronic communication intention. The final section covered digital literacy and demographic characteristics.
The constructs were measured with indicators adapted from scales used in previous research. Their wording was
adjusted to the Albanian consumer context and to the aims of the study. The adaptation process included an initial translation
from English to Albanian, followed by back -translation by an independent bilingual expert to ensure conceptual equivalence.
Two academic experts in marketing and digital communication reviewed the scales for content validity and alignment with the
study objectives. Before the main data collection, the adapted scales were pre -tested for clarity, cultural relevance, and
comprehension in the Albanian setting. Pre liminary validation procedures included exploratory and confirmatory factor
analysis on the pilot data to assess construct validity and dimensionality. The analysis showed that the measures were
appropriate and reliable for the target population. Most stat ements were rated on a seven -point Likert scale ranging from 1,
“strongly disagree,” to 7, “strongly agree.”
Informativeness was measured through the usefulness, clarity, timeliness, and relevance of information communicated
through digital channels. Personalisation concerned the alignment of messages, offers, and recommendations with consumer
interests. Interactivity covered opportunities to communicate, respond, and receive feedback from brands. Entertainment
referred to attractive, creative, and emotiona lly engaging content, while credibility captured the honesty, transparency, and
realism of marketing messages.
Electronic consumer-to-consumer communication was measured through the influence of other consumers' comments,
ratings, experiences, and recommendations. Consumer attitude was operationalised as an overall favourable or unfavourable
evaluation of brands and digitally mediated experiences. Behavioural responses included purchase intention, content
engagement, completed purchases, and willingness to recommend a product or publish an online review. The complete list of
indicators is provided in Appendix A (Supplementary Material).

2.4. Pilot testing and instrument quality control

Before the main data collection, the questionnaire was pilot -tested with 42 participants whose characteristics were
similar to those of the target population. The pilot assessed wording clarity, comprehensibility of terms, completion time,
possible repetition among statements, and the technical performance of the online form.
Participant feedback led to the revision of several statements that were perceived as overly general or too similar.
Preliminary scale consistency was also assessed using Cronbach's alpha and item-total correlations. Following this process, the
instrument was considered suitable for administration to the main sample.
Response quality was checked before statistical analysis. Incomplete questionnaires, duplicate submissions, cases
showing near -uniform responses across the scale, and questionnaires completed too quickly to allow careful reading were

2.5. Ethical considerations and common method bias

Participation was voluntary and based on informed consent. The questionnaire introduction explained the academic
purpose of the study, how the data would be used, the protection of anonymity, and the participants' right to discontinue at
any time. No information that could directly identify respondents was collected.
Several procedural measures were applied to limit bias arising from collecting predictors and outcomes through the
same instrument. Anonymity was assured, participants were told that there were no right or wrong answers, and the
statements were worded neutrally. Indicators belonging to different constructs were placed in separate sections, and their
order was partly varied to reduce automatic responding and obvious associations between variables.
Common method bias was also assessed statistically. In addition to Harman's single -factor test, full-collinearity values
were examined so that the assessment did not rely on a single diagnostic procedure.

2.6. Data analysis strategy

Data analysis followed the partial least squares structural equation modelling approach (PLS -SEM), in line with the
methodological recommendations of Hair et al. (2022). PLS-SEM was selected because of the model's complexity, the number
of latent constructs, and the inclusion of direct, mediating, and moderating effects.
The analysis began with descriptive statistics for the sample and the indicators. Frequencies and percentages were
calculated for categorical variables, whereas means, standard deviations, skewness, and kurtosis were examined for Likert -
scale indicators.
The measurement model was then assessed. Indicator reliability was examined through outer loadings, and internal
consistency through Cronbach's alpha and composite reliability. Convergent validity was eva luated using average variance
extracted (AVE), while discriminant validity was assessed through the heterotrait -monotrait ratio (HTMT), following Henseler
et al. (2015).
Once the measurement model had been established, the structural model was evaluated. Collinearity among predictor
constructs was first examined using the variance inflation factor (VIF). Standardised path coefficients, coefficients of
determination (R²), and confidence intervals were then assessed.
The statistical significance of direct and  indirect effects was tested with a 5,000 -subsample bootstrap procedure.
Decisions on the hypotheses were based on standardised coefficients, t values, p values, and 95% confidence intervals.
The mediating role of consumer attitude was evaluated through the indirect effects of the digital marketing dimensions
on purchase intention. Mediation was interpreted by considering the significance and direction of the direct and indirect effects
together.
The moderating effect of digital literacy was tested by creating an interaction term between informativeness and digital
literacy. Multigroup analyses were also conducted by age, gender, frequency of online purchasing, and level of digital litera cy.
Measurement invariance was examined before group differences were interpreted.
Age differences were assessed through multigroup analysis, while differences across decision stages were evaluated
using repeated-measures analysis.

3.6. Interpretation of findings

The findings show that digital marketing affects consumer behaviour not only through exposure to commercial
messages, but also through the evaluations consumers form of the brand, the product, and the digital experience. The
coefficient of determination for consumer attitude was higher than those for the behavioural outcomes. Communication
stimuli inf luence beliefs, feelings, and evaluations more directly, whereas purchase behaviour is also constrained by price,
income, availability, payment arrangements, and delivery. The gap between attitude, intention, and completed purchase can
consequently be interpreted partly as a financial-value and affordability boundary: consumers may respond positively to digital
communication while judging the total monetary sacrifice to be too high (Zeithaml, 1988; Sweeney & Soutar, 2001).
Perceived value provides a useful contextual interpretation of this gap because consumers assess what they receive
relative to monetary and non-monetary sacrifice (Zeithaml, 1988; Sweeney & Soutar, 2001). Online channels make this trade -
off more visible through price comparison, delivery charges, discounts, warranties, returns, and payment options. Affordability
and financial well -being were not modelled as separate constructs; they are boundary conditions that may explain why
favourable attitudes and intentions do not always become complet ed transactions (Netemeyer et al., 2018).
Online promotion should therefore be understood as a multidimensional stimulus rather than a homogeneous
instrument: a message may be informative but unpersuasive, entertaining but untrustworthy, or personalised ye t intrusive
(Bashar et al., 2024). This distinction helps explain why the six coefficients differed materially in magnitude.
Credibility emerged as the strongest predictor of consumer attitude. This finding is especially salient in digital settings,
where consumers must distinguish promotional content from independent testimony, genuine offers from exaggerated claims,
and trustworthy sellers from uncertain sources. It is consistent with research on influencer marketing and sponsored content,
which emphasises the role of expertise, authenticity, message value, and influencer -product fit (De Veirman et al., 2017; Lou
& Yuan, 2019; Sokolova & Kefi, 2020; Pan et al., 2025). In Albania, credibility has tangible foundations: transparent prices,
delivery costs, return conditions, warranties, payment methods, and seller identity.
This result is consistent with evidence that message value, expertise, authenticity, and sponsorship transparency shape
trust and purchase intention in influencer contexts (Lou & Yuan, 2019;  Sokolova & Kefi, 2020). Realistically framed claims and
clear disclosure may therefore strengthen, rather than weaken, acceptance.
Positive electronic communication among consumers was the second strongest predictor. Online comments, ratings,
and reported experiences provide social evidence and information grounded in actual use. Consumers draw on these sources
to reduce uncertainty and verify brand claims. The result accords with the meta -analysis by Ismagilova et al. (2020) and with
Albanian studies that  have underscored the importance of other users' recommendations and experiences. The substantial
weight of eWOM makes transparency and ethical review management even more important.

The finding also accords with meta -analytic evidence that eWOM increases purchase intention by strengthening
knowledge and confidence (Ismagilova et al., 2020) and with Albanian studies documenting the role of comments, experiences,
and recommendations (Braholli et al., 2023; Vukatana et al., 2022; Fejzaj & Gogo, 2025 ).
Informativeness was another important driver of attitude. Consumers use digital channels to understand product
characteristics, compare alternatives, check prices, and reduce pre -purchase uncertainty. The moderating effect of digital
literacy shows that  information is more influential when consumers can locate, compare, and verify it. For users with more
limited digital skills, clarity of presentation, ease of navigation, and access to a human representative may be as important  as
the amount of information provided.
The connection with perceived usefulness is direct: information that saves time and supports a better-grounded choice
increases the value of both the platform and the brand (Davis, 1989; Tilahun & Berhan, 2023). The significant moderation result
further shows that relevance and verifiability, rather than sheer information volume, are central to this effect.
Personalisation and interactivity had positive effects, although smaller than those of credibility, eWOM, and
informativeness. Personalisat ion can shorten search time and make an offer more relevant, but its effect weakens when
consumers suspect that personal data are being used without adequate transparency. Interactivity makes the brand more
accessible and allows consumers to obtain answers  about payment, delivery, returns, or warranties. These findings suggest
that technology should function as a service tool, not merely as a promotional mechanism.
Entertainment had the smallest coefficient among the digital marketing dimensions. This does not make entertaining
content unimportant: it can attract attention, strengthen message recall, and encourage engagement. It is not, however,
sufficient to support a purchase decision when the product, price, benefits, or source of the offer are unclear. E valuating
performance solely through likes, views, or shares may therefore overstate a campaign's actual effect.
Prior research nevertheless identifies enjoyment and emotional appeal as important features of social -media
communication (Kim & Ko, 2012; Duff ett, 2017). The present result refines that evidence by showing that views, likes, and
shares should not be equated with purchasing: entertaining content appears more consequential when it is connected to the
product and supported by useful, credible information.
The positive effects of attitude on purchase intention, online engagement, and electronic communication intention
support both the Stimulus -Organism-Response model and the Theory of Planned Behaviour. Digital stimuli first shape the
consumer's cogn itive and emotional state and then influence intentions and responses. Purchase intention nevertheless
explained only part of the reported behaviour, confirming that the movement from willingness to action can be interrupted by
economic and logistical constraints.
This pattern supports the treatment of attitude as the central evaluative mechanism linking online stimuli to responses.
It also confirms that the four outcomes are not interchangeable: consumers may engage without buying, intend to buy without
completing a transaction, or purchase without becoming advocates (Petty & Cacioppo, 1986).
Stage-specific analysis showed that digital marketing was perceived as most influential during information search and
evaluation of alternatives. At these points, cons umers need diagnostic content: detailed characteristics, comparisons, prices,
reviews, and answers to concrete questions. The lower post -purchase score reveals an underused area. Many firms focus on
completing the sale and devote less attention to post -transaction support, complaint handling, customer retention, and
encouraging recommendations.

3.7. Theoretical contributions

The study contributes to the literature on digital marketing and consumer behaviour in several ways.
First, it brings together three theoretical perspectives that explain different but connected aspects of decision -making.
The Stimulus -Organism-Response model accounts for how characteristics of the digital environment affect the consumer's
internal state and subsequent re sponses. The Theory of Planned Behaviour helps explain the relationship between attitude,
intention, and realised behaviour. The consumer journey perspective, in turn, identifies the stages at which these effects
become most visible.
Taken together, these perspectives offer a more complete account than any one of them could provide in isolation. The
findings show that digital marketing stimuli do not lead automatically to purchase; they first shape how consumers evaluate
the brand, product, and digital expe rience. That evaluation is then expressed in intention, engagement, behaviour, or
recommendation.
Second, digital marketing is not treated as a single undifferentiated variable. The model distinguishes informativeness,
personalisation, interactivity, entertainment, credibility, and electronic consumer-to-consumer communication (eWOM). This
operationalisation allows the relative contribution of each dimension to be assessed and shows that different forms of digita l
communication do not influence consumer attitude to the same extent.
Credibility, electronic communication among consumers, and informativeness exerted stronger effects than
entertainment. Theoretically, this shifts attention away from exposure alone and toward content quality, trust in the source,
and the explanatory value of information.

Third, the model separates four consumer outcomes: online engagement, purchase intention, purchase behaviour, and
recommendation intention. This distinction matters because a low -commitment response - liking, clicking, or sharing a post -
is not equivalent to a purchase. Purchase intention likewise does not necessarily become realised behaviour.
Fourth, the study confirms the mediating role of consumer attitude. The indirect effects show that digital marketing
characteristics influence purchase intention through consumers' evaluations of the brand, product, or digital experience. This
provides empirical support for positioning attitude as the central mechanism in the model.
Fifth, the study extends research on digit al consumer behaviour to the Albanian context. Examining a Southeast
European market sheds light on how trust, digital experience, payment infrastructure, and market conditions shape the
effectiveness of digital marketing. The framework also provides a basis for future comparisons with Kosovo, North Macedonia,
Montenegro, and other countries in the region. It additionally locates these marketing effects within the financial context o f
consumption, where price transparency, perceived value, affordability, an d payment conditions can enable or constrain the
conversion of intention into behaviour.

3.8. Managerial implications

The findings have several direct implications for businesses that use digital channels to communicate with consumers.
First, firms should prioritise useful and credible content. Product descriptions, prices, availability, delivery costs and
timeframes, return conditions, and warranties should be visible and easy to understand. A high frequency of messages cannot
compensate for incomplete or low-quality information.
Credibility, the most influential dimension in the model, must be built through sustained transparency. Businesses
should clearly identify the seller, the promotional nature of the content, the terms of the offer, and their post-purchase
responsibilities. Exaggerated claims may attract short-term attention, but they weaken consumer attitude and trust.
Second, online ratings and recommendations should be treated as a core component of marketing strategy. Firms
should make it e asy for consumers to publish genuine experiences, respond to comments, and address complaints
transparently. Automatically deleting criticism or using fabricated reviews can damage brand credibility.
Third, personalisation should rest on proportionate and transparent data use. Consumers should understand why a
recommendation is being shown and should be able to manage their communication preferences. Personalisation is valuable
when it reduces search effort and improves offer relevance; when it becomes intr usive, it can generate discomfort and
resistance.
Fourth, interactivity should be supported by explicit response standards. Chatbots and automated systems can handle
routine questions, but consumers should be able to reach a representative when the issue c oncerns payment, returns,
warranties, or complaints. Speed should not be achieved at the expense of accuracy or individualised handling.
Fifth, entertaining content should be connected to useful information and campaign objectives. The results show that
entertainment can capture attention, but its effect on consumer attitude is more limited when credibility and usefulness are
absent. Campaigns should therefore not be evaluated solely by the number of likes, views, or shares.
Performance measurement should f ollow the stages of the consumer journey. Reach and message recall capture
awareness; visits, searches, and time on page reflect information search; product comparisons and review consultation relate
to evaluation; conversion and cart -abandonment rates cap ture purchase; and satisfaction, return visits, complaints, and
recommendations represent post -purchase outcomes. Financial indicators such as average order value, payment failure,
delivery-cost sensitivity, and conversion by payment method can clarify whe ther engagement produces economically
meaningful outcomes.
This structure helps firms avoid confusing the attention generated by content with economic results. A campaign may
produce high engagement but low conversion, or it may encourage purchases without  creating satisfaction and loyalty after
the transaction.
The lower score for the post -purchase stage suggests that firms should invest more in communication after the sale.
Order-status updates, technical support, complaint resolution, review requests, an d offers for existing customers can
strengthen the relationship and encourage recommendation. For Albanian digital marketers, concrete actions could include
implementing automated SMS or Viber notifications for delivery tracking in Albanian, setting up ded icated after-sales service
channels through popular platforms such as WhatsApp, Facebook Messenger, or Instagram DM, and inviting customer
feedback through short surveys or personalised follow -up messages. Additionally, businesses can encourage satisfied
customers to leave reviews by providing small incentives or recognition, and address public complaints transparently to
demonstrate accountability. Given rising concerns with trust and service quality in Albania, investing in visible, responsive, and
culturally appropriate post -purchase communication will help turn buyers into loyal advocates and differentiate brands in a
competitive market.
To summarise, key actionable recommendations for digital marketers in Albania include: (1) prioritising visible and
transparent information on prices, availability, delivery, and warranties at every customer touchpoint; (2) actively managing
online reviews and consumer feedback, enabling and incentivising genuine user commentary, and responding promptly to both
positive a nd negative input; (3) using personalisation tools in a proportionate and transparent manner, making

3.9. Limitations and directions for future research

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting the findings.
First, the cross -sectional design relies on data collected at one point in time. The observed relationships therefore
cannot be interpreted as conclusive evidence of causality. Although the model is theoretically grounded, it cannot determine
with certainty whether exposure to digital marketing produces a more favourable attitude or whether consumers who already
hold positive attitudes evaluate digital content more favourably.
Future studies could employ longitudinal designs to follow changes in attitude and behaviour over time, or experiments
that manipulate source credibility, the intensity of personalisation, review valence, and sponsorship transparency. They could
also incorporate perceived value, price sensitivity, household income, financial literacy, payment security, and perceived
financial well-being to test more directly the financial conditions under which purchase intention becomes realised behaviour.
Second, the data are based on self -reports. Responses may be affected by memory, subjective perception, and social
desirability. Future resea rch should combine survey evidence with objective data such as clicks, time on page, transactions,
cart abandonment, repeat purchases, and actual campaign-performance indicators.
Third, the model considers products and services together, although the effects of digital marketing may vary by sector.
Credibility may carry more weight in banking, healthcare, or education, whereas entertainment and personalisation may be
more prominent in fashion, tourism, or food services. Testing the model within individual s ectors would permit more precise
interpretation.
Fourth, the study focuses primarily on positive dimensions of digital marketing. Privacy concerns, perceived
intrusiveness, advertising avoidance, influencer scepticism, and information overload were not inc luded as central constructs.
These factors may operate as negative stimuli or as moderators of the relationships identified here.
Fifth, the use of purposive and quota sampling limits the generalizability of the results to the entire population of
Albanian consumers. Although recruitment sought to include different categories and regions, the sample is not a national
probability sample. This non -probability sampling method may introduce biases, as it does not ensure that every member of
the target populatio n had an equal chance of being selected. In addition, there is potential for self -selection bias, as
participation was voluntary and individuals who chose to respond may differ systematically from those who did not, for
example in their interest in digital channels or their digital literacy. As a result, the findings may be more reflective of consumers
who are already engaged, digitally confident, or more interested in online purchasing than the broader population. This self -
selection may have influenced the observed attitudes and behaviours, potentially over-representing more engaged or digitally
confident consumers and under-representing those less active or less comfortable with digital platforms. Future studies should
use more representative procedures, such as probability -based sampling, and achieve a better balance between urban and
rural areas to mitigate these potential biases and enhance the validity of generalisations.
Further research could test measurement invariance by region, age, educational level, and digital experience.
Comparisons among Albania, Kosovo, North Macedonia, Montenegro, and other Southeast European markets would help
clarify the roles of culture, institutional trust, digital infrastructure, and payment systems.

4. Conclusion

This study examined the influence of digital marketing on consumer attitude and behavioural responses throughout the
decision-making process. Digital marketing was conceptualised as a multidimensional phenomenon comprising
informativeness, personalisation, interactivity, entertainment, credibility, and electronic consumer -to-consumer
communication (eWOM).
All six dimensions had positive effects on consumer attitude, although the strength of these effects varied. Credibility
had the strongest influence, followed by electronic communication among consumers and informativeness. Entertainment,
while positive, had the smallest effect. Consumers therefore appear to assign greater importance to information quality, source
trust, and other users' experiences than to the creative appeal of content alone.
Consumer attitude emerged as a central mediating mechanism. Digital marketing characteristics influenced purchase
intention through the evaluations consumers formed of the brand, produ ct, and digital experience. The positive effects of
attitude on intention, engagement, and recommendation supported the theoretical framework.
The analysis also showed that purchase intention does not translate fully into realised behaviour. Purchases depend on
price, income, availability, payment methods, and delivery conditions. Digital marketing effectiveness should therefore be
judged not only through attitudes or intentions, but also through concrete behaviour. The findings therefore support a

Digital marketing exerted its strongest influence during information search and the evaluation of alternatives, whereas

Overall, digital marketing is most effective when it provides useful information, credible sources, open communication,
