Abstract. Customer experience is increasingly valued as a critical factor for long -term competitive
positioning in saturated mobile telecommunications markets. As the operational differences become
increasingly non-existent and technology barriers are smaller, customer satisfaction levels, product quality,
and value perception contribute to l oyalty and long -term performance. Recent scientific literature has
focused on integrative models of customer satisfaction, which illustrate that customers’ loyalty is
determined by their perception of quality, perceived value, and satisfaction in a multi -faceted manner,
especially in high-technology service industries. Nevertheless, there is little empirical data from the mobile
telecommunications markets in developing European economies. This research utilises a quantitative
research design comprising a cross-sectional research to analyze the relationships among perceived service
quality, perceived value, customer satisfaction, and customer loyalty within a telecom business. Data were
collected from 300 customers of a leading mobile operator via an online s urvey with an adapted
questionnaire. Data were analyzed using descriptive statistics, internal consistency analysis, exploratory
factor analysis, Pearson correlation analysis, and multiple regression models. In this analysis, the
relationship between perceived service quality and perceived satisfaction as well as loyalty is well indicated
and the multidimensional structure of perceived service quality is confirmed. The perceived value stands
out as the most significant factor for customer satisfaction, whil e customer satisfaction is identified as the
strongest predictive agent for customer loyalty. The strong internal reliability measures that have been
established confirm that the measurement instrument is relatively reliable. These results provide further
support for the soundness of these integrated customer satisfaction concepts which validate the usefulness
of integrated customer satisfaction models in this particular sector and are valuable for operators better to
optimize customer experience, satisfact ion, and loyalty  and to offer support to operators who wish to
improve the customer experience and loyalty in digital markets.

Keywords: mobile telecommunications, perceived service quality, customer satisfaction, customer loyalty,
perceived value, customer experience, ECSI model.

Introduction
With digitalization, the increase of mobile internet services and increasing dependence of
consumers on mobile connectivity in carrying out daily tasks, the telecom industry is undergoing
vast changes. Network infrastructure bears many functional and technical demands due to these
services. With this in mind, service quality has become an important assessment metric in

Literature review
Considered broadly in telecom, service quality is multifaceted and integrates the subjective
perceptions of users about service delivery with technical network performance. Mobile services
are defined as the functio nal translation of network capabilities through applications (Cox, 2012;
Sauter, 2021), and are usually mobile calls (voice communication, mobile internet access, and
multimedia services) with distinct performance requirements. As mobile networks operate w ith
heterogeneous traffic types, sensitivity to latency, packet loss, and throughput vary across services
(Janevski, 2019), service quality cannot be consistently evaluated across services. Thus, research
has shown that in particular for data -intensive and multimedia use cases, user satisfaction is
determined not only by objective measures on performance, but also subjective aspects such as
perceived reliability, service continuity, coverage, and customer support, which are essential for
the experience (Varela et al., 2014; Zhou et al., 2019; Kalia et al., 2021).
Where the research is more oriented on the objective performance measurements in QoS,
customer satisfaction models focus on mechanisms for subjective evaluation. In the literature,
integrated evaluation frameworks linking customer satisfaction and loyalty to perceived service
quality are proposed to cope with this complexity. Such models give a deeper insight into the
mobile telecommunications customer experience by integrating technical performance, perceptual
and behavioral factors. Though very little data exist on the relative importance of various aspects
of service quality, the evidence points to perceived value and perceived service quality in
determining satisfaction and loyalty outcomes (Al-Shehri et al., 2017; Mazhar et al., 2023).
According to recent empirical research in this industry, customer satisfaction and perceived
service quality are positively related. Suchanek and Bucicova (2025) show that, even in post -
pandemic conditions, overall customer satisfaction is significantly impacted by perceptions of
service reliability, network coverage, and customer support quality, using a structural model based
on the European Customer Satisfaction Index. According to their findings, satisfaction is
influenced by perceived service quality at all stages of market maturity.
Simultaneously, perceived value has become a significant factor in determining customer
satisfaction, sometimes outweighing the direct impact of perceived service quality. Consumers
assess perceived benefits as service dependability, flexibility, and support quality in addition to
price when determining value.
Research from developing telecom markets shows that perceived value has a significant
positive impact on customer satisfaction, which in turn leads to increased levels of customer loyalty

On the basis of empirical data reported by Ting et al. (2020), more satisfied customers have

This study aims to fill this gap by looking at the relationships among perceived service quality,
perceived value, customer satisfaction and customer loyalty in that context. Given the previous
literature reviewed and the identified empirical voids, the following hypotheses are formulated:
H1: In the mobile telecom sector, perceived service quality has a significant positive effect
on customer satisfaction.
H2: Perceived value exerts a significant positive effect on customer satisfaction in mobile
telecommunications services.
H3: Customer satisfaction has a significant positive effect on customer loyalty in mobile
telecommunications services.

Methodology
This research instrument was a structured questionnaire that was designed from the European
Customer Satisfaction Index (ECSI) model. Although the causal structure from the original ECSI
model, namely relationships between operator image, customer expectat ions, perceived service
quality, perceived value, customer satisfaction and customer loyalty, was not changed, the
questionnaire was adapted from the conceptual form and the items in use proposed by Suchanek
and Bucicova (2025). At the same time, the instr ument was adapted to the specific characteristics
of Romania's telecommunications market and the post-pandemic time in which services were used.
The data were captured using an online survey which was given to customers of a single major
mobile telecommunications firm in Romania.
To adjust to the recent developments in mobile service consumption, that is, increased
mobile data usage, intensive use of digital applications, video streaming and increased mobility,
the questionnaire was conceptually and contex tually modified in relation to the initial instrument
introduced by Suchanek and Bucicova (2025). A comprehensive examination of the mobile service
experience could be achieved by establishing the psychological construct of perceived service
quality as having a multidimensional nature.
There are two primary sections to the questionnaire. The sociodemographic variables
(gender, age group, education level, employment status, type of residence, and predominant use of
mobile services) in the first section are u sed to characterize the sample and function as control
variables in further analyses.  The study ’s six latent constructs : operator image (IM), customer
expectations (EXP), perceived service quality (PQ), perceived value (PV), customer satisfaction
(CS), and customer loyalty (CL), are represented by measurement items in the second section. All
items were measured using a seven-point Likert scale.
The operator’s image reflects customers’ general perceptions of the provider’s standing in
the market, reputation, and credibility.  This study considers items that measure the operator’s
perceived social responsibility, perceived innovativeness, and overall brand image.  To act as
benchmarks, customer expectations (measured by expected service quality, suitability to everyday

Customer satisfaction, also a type of overall evaluation of service experience, encompasses overall

Several statistical methods were used with R software for data processing and analysis in
order to test these hypotheses. Each approach was chosen in accordance with its applicability to
the study’s goals and the European Customer Satisfaction Index model’s conceptual framework.
First, customer perceptions of the study ’s primary constructs : perceived service quality,
perceived value, customer satisfaction, and customer loyalty , were compiled using descriptive
statistics. Thus, in order to assess the overall level and degree of variation of customer evaluations,
mean and standard deviation estimates were calculated for each item and latent construct.
Cronbach’s alpha coefficients of each construct applied to each of the variables of the
measurement instruments were presented as test of reliability of the measure. The questionnaire
items test in accordance with theoretical construct are consistent and are reliably distributed,
evidenced by their relatively high coefficients of reliability. An investigation to test whether the
adapted questionnaire from the European Customer Satisfaction Index model could obt ain an
accurate impression of the customers’ perceptions in the specified domain, was conducted in form
of exploratory factor analysis. The Kaiser–Meyer–Olkin index and Bartlett’s test of sphericity were
employed to test the suitability of the dataset for factor analysis before factor extraction. The
validity of the measurement framework was confirmed with the analysis of the factor solutions,
which discovered idiosyncratic latent dimensions that closely match with theoretical constructs and
accounted for most of the total variance.
Then the relationships between perceived service quality, perceived value, customer
satisfaction, and customer loyalty were explored through a Pearson correlation analysis. The
strength as well as the direction of the relationships between the constructs were assessed with the
help of Pearson correlation coefficients. Results provided preliminary evidence to support the
proposed hypotheses, as perceived service quality and satisfaction were positively related, and
those pertaining  to satisfaction and loyalty were strong and statistically significant.  Multiple
regression models were also estimated  to help test the research hypotheses and ascertain main
determinants of customer satisfaction and loyalty. Customer loyalty was expressed as a function of
satisfaction and specific service quality components, while customer satisfaction was expressed as
a function of perceived service quality dimensions and perceived value. The effect of each predictor
was estimated by standardized regressi on coefficients and potential multicollinearity between
independent variables was assessed through calculation of variance inflation factors. The
questionnaire participants gave informed consent for their participation before they finished the
survey, they engaged only voluntarily to participate, and responses were collected anonymously to
ensure data integrity and confidentiality.

Conclusion
This research combines technical, perceptual and relational aspects of an ECSI -based model to
investigate the determinants of perceived quality, customer satisfaction and loyalty in the Telecom
industry. The results indicate that while network performance is still a requirement for a good user
experience, it is not sufficient alone. Overall customer satisfaction is primarily a function of the
perceived value and the quality of interactions with t he operator, particularly in the auxiliary
services and customer support.
Customer retention is based on an integrated experience that takes into account the
economic, functional and relational aspects, not only the technical attributes, as shown by the
