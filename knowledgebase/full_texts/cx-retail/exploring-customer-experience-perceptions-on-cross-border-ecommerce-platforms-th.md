Abstract
Against the backdrop of global trade restructuring and the integration of the digital economy,
cross-border e-commerce platforms face dual challenges: rising export costs due to shifts in U.S.–
China tariff policies and intensifying market competition. In this context, the present study focuses
on electronic products as a core category of cross-border e-commerce and highlights “e-commerce
politeness” as a key factor in shaping customer experience. It examines how politeness influences
customer perceptions an d explores ways to enhance customer experience through improved
politeness practices, thereby strengthening the international competitiveness of cross -border e-
commerce platforms in the electronic products sector. Using the Critical Incident Technique (CIT),
the study analyzes customer reviews of electronic products on cross-border e-commerce platforms,
systematically collecting both satisfactory and unsatisfactory incidents to gain deeper insights into
customers’ perceptions of service quality and politene ss during the consumption process. The
findings clarify the pivotal role of e -commerce politeness in cross -border electronic product
consumption experiences, emphasizing that politeness construction should primarily focus on two
dimensions: product quality and logistics services. Furthermore, the study proposes collaborative
improvements across multiple stakeholders within the ecosystem to advance e -commerce
politeness, offering both theoretical and practical support for optimizing service design and
addressing current and future challenges faced by cross-border e-commerce platforms.

Keywords: Cross-Border E -Commerce Platforms, DHgate, Electronic Products, E -Commerce
Politeness, Critical Incident Technique.

distant factories and global consumers. It not only meets the demands of consumption upgrading
but also drives the continuous expansion of market scale. However, this booming industry is facing
severe challenges. In recent years, the United States has repeatedly imposed additional tariffs on
goods imported from China, covering numerous categories. Particularly since February 2025,
intensive tariff policies have significantly increased the export costs of Chinese cross-border sellers,

2.2 Development and Current Status of DHgate
DHgate is a major cross-border e-commerce platform connecting Chinese small and medium-sized
enterprises with international buyers (Guo et al., 2018). The platform integrates B2B (business-to-
business) and B2C (business -to-consumer) models, combining value -added services with supply
chain and logistics services to form diversified profit models (He & Xu, 2018). Unique business
models such as small -scale wholesale and support for customized production constitute its core
competitiveness. In recent years, DHg ate has actively expanded into emerging markets and
invested heavily in upgrading platform technologies, such as optimizing search algorithms,
introducing AI-driven translation and customer service tools, and building a more transparent
supply chain tracking system.
However, during its rapid expansion, the platform still faces numerous challenges, many of which
are clearly reflected in the dimension of e -commerce politeness. For example, the difficulty of
maintaining unified service standards and ensuring consistent service quality across a vast number
of small and medium -sized suppliers directly manifests in differences in the enforcement of
politeness norms. Due to variations in supplier size, cultural background, and service awareness,
performance in key politeness dimensions—such as communication language, response timeliness,
and attitude in problem -solving—remains uneven. Some suppliers’ curt replies or delayed
communication lead to significant gaps in customers’ politeness experience. Meanwhile, issues of
efficiency and fairness in cross -border dispute resolution expose weaknesses in the practice of
politeness in conflict scenarios. Complex procedures or insufficient cross-cultural communication
may result in prolonged processing cycles, shirking of responsibility, or responses lacking empathy,
thereby weakening customers’ perception of politeness in dispute resolution. Zhang et al. (2020)
pointed out that while export-oriented platforms like DHgate have created significant advantages
for China’s cross -border trade  through rapid development, information asymmetry among

communication language, attitude, and style. At the same time, changes in the policy environment

to promote the formation of a systematic politeness mechanism for the platform.

price nor product, has become increasingly critical. In online environments, written
communication is the primary mode of exchange. The absence of paralinguistic cues such as tone,
facial expressions, and body language in face-to-face communication makes accurate information
transmission and intention interpretation more prone to deviation, thereby placing higher demands

e-commerce politeness is conceptualized as a multidimensional construct, encompassing accurate
wording and friendly tone, as well as response timel iness, proactive communication, willingness
and ability to solve problems, transparency of information, and key behaviors that demonstrate
empathy. Together, these dimensions shape customers’ overall perception of interaction politeness
and their subsequen t evaluation of experience. Under the changing U.S. –China tariff policies,
cross-border e -commerce platforms need to seize the trend of American consumers bypassing
intermediaries to shop directly, and strengthen politeness construction to improve customer
retention and achieve sustainable growth. This study employs the Critical Incident Technique (CIT)
to systematically analyze cross -border e -commerce customers’ experiences according to
politeness-related dimensions, providing feasible recommendations for platform development.

3. Research Methodology
3.1 Critical Incident Technique (CIT)
CIT was proposed by Flanagan in 1954 as a research method. Its core idea lies in systematically
collecting and analyzing specific, observable critical incidents that have a significant impact on
particular phenomena (such as satisfaction or dissatisfaction ), and then classifying and
summarizing them to reveal the behavioral elements, potential causal relationships, and underlying
mechanisms that lead to these outcomes. Since its introduction, this method has been widely
applied across multiple fields. For example, in the domain of mobile applications and information
systems, Salo & Frank (2017) used CIT to discover a close connection between user behavior and
specific situational characteristics, thereby expanding the theoretical knowledge available at the
time. With the rise of e -commerce, CIT has been introduced into the fields of e -commerce and
human-computer interaction. For instance, Ingaldi & Brožová (2020) applied it to evaluate the
service quality of e -commerce platforms, using customer feedback to id entify shortcomings in
politeness experience and providing data support for improving human -computer interaction
services. In the field of consumer behavior education and marketing, Bitner et al. (1990) employed
CIT to collect and categorize cases from air line, hotel, and restaurant customers, identifying
specific incidents and behaviors that influence customer experience. In psychology and task
analysis, Koch et al. (2009) used CIT to conduct work analysis of instructors and supervisors
within Germany’s statutory accident insurance and prevention institutions.
CIT has already been successfully applied in service management, e -commerce, psychology, and

3.2 Research Design
In this study, the concept of “politeness” is redefined as the quality of customer –platform
interaction, specifically referring to whether the platform’s information feedback and service
responsiveness meet customers’ expectations for humanized and efficie nt experiences. To
systematically evaluate customers’ overall perceptions and attitudes toward e-commerce politeness,
the research focuses on shopping experiences in the electronic products category on DHgate. By
collecting and analyzing satisfactory and u nsatisfactory critical incidents encountered during
shopping, the study conducts an in-depth investigation. Using CIT, it extracts and analyzes specific
positive and negative events reflected in customer reviews, systematically identifying the core
factors influencing politeness perceptions. Based on the findings, the study proposes
recommendations for platform improvement, aiming to provide both theoretical foundations and
practical pathways for cross-border e-commerce platforms to better respond to fluctuations in the
international trade environment and achieve sustainable development.

4. Data Analysis
4.1 Data Collection
The data for this study were drawn from the electronic products category on DHgate, with all data
sourced from publicly accessible user reviews. To ensure compliance with ethical standards, all
collected data were anonymized, removing any information that could potentially identify
individual users.
A purposive sampling strategy was adopted to ensure that the dataset combined both market
representativeness and content depth. The specific selection criteria and procedures were as
follows:
a. Sales ranking:  Products were selected based on “sales order,” focusing on top -ranked
items. These products had large user bases and high volumes of reviews, making their
feedback most representative of mainstream market choices and general consumer
opinions, thereby ensuring market-level representativeness.
b. Time frame:  Reviews were collected between February and April 2025, capturing
consumer feedback during a stable development period for DHgate’s electronic products
category. This ensured both timeliness and dynamic relevance of the data.
c. Review selection:  For each selected product, 10 –15 reviews were targeted. Both
satisfactory reviews (5 -star) and unsatisfactory reviews (1 -star) were included, with
priority given to detailed reviews containing specific usage scenarios or descriptive details,
thereby ensuring depth and richness of data quality.
Following this process, the study constructed a qualitative dataset comprising 508 user reviews,
including 268 satisfactory incidents and 240 unsatisfactory incidents. The dataset’s focused source
(single platform and vertical category), continuous time span, sales-based representativeness, and

4.3.3 Validity Analysis
Validity analysis is a critical process for evaluating whether the research method accurately
measures the intended content. To ensure the scientific rigor and reliability of the findings, this
study conducts validity assessment from three perspectives: ex pert validity, content validity, and
face validity, as detailed below.

4.3.3.1 Expert Validity
Expert validity refers to the evaluation of the professionalism, rationality, and scientific rigor of
the measurement tools used in a study by experts in a specific field, ensuring that the tools can
accurately reflect the research topic while meeting academic norms and professional requirements.
Haynes et al. (1995) noted that the most commonly used assessment tools for a given construct
and function should undergo expert review for content validity. In this study, three experts from
different backgrounds (a professor specializing in e -commerce, a cross -border e -commerce
operations manager, and an experienced cross -border e -commerce seller) jointly evaluated the
validity of the research data and made minor revisions. Therefore, this study meets the criteria for
expert validity.

4.3.3.3 Face Validity
Thomas et al. (1992) proposed that face validity is a construct used to assess how test items appear
to respondents and others. Face validity refers to judging from appearance whether a measurement
tool seems reasonably capable of measuring the research to pic, that is, whether the form and
content of the tool allow respondents to intuitively perceive its relevance to the topic. The data in
this study were derived from customer reviews written and published under product pages after
purchasing and actually using goods or services. These constitute real-time feedback information,
which effectively reduces recall bias caused by memory distortion or time delays, and more
authentically reflects customers’ actual experiences. Based on this, the study concludes tha t the
collected data possess high face validity.

5. Conclusion
The results of the above study reveal that cross -border e -commerce platforms face a series of
politeness-related problems, such as product quality failures, low efficiency in customer service
responses, overly rigid compensation mechanisms, delays in logis tics services, and mismatches
between product descriptions and actual items. These problems significantly reduce customers’
trust and satisfaction with the platform. However, the emergence of these issues is not solely due
to the lack of responsibility of a single actor, but rather because of coordination problems among
multiple stakeholders within the cross-border e-commerce ecosystem.
The findings of this study have broad implications for global e-commerce market participants. The
research shows that by improving the politeness of product quality and logistics services,
enterprises can not only cope with cost challenges brought by tarif fs but also establish
differentiated advantages that go beyond price competition. The insights of this study provide
critical guidance for expanding into emerging markets. The concept of “e -commerce politeness”
proposed here offers a practical framework fo r enterprises to build trust bridges with buyers. By
eliminating cultural and geographical barriers, it helps enterprises establish and maintain long -
term, stable trade partnerships. Based on these insights, e -commerce platforms can more
effectively respon d to the complex challenges of international trade and achieve sustainable
development in the rapidly changing global market.
Focusing on the core category of electronic products, data analysis clearly shows that the most
influential factor affecting e-commerce politeness is product quality, followed by logistics service.
Therefore, this study provides optimization recommendation s from five stakeholder
perspectives—government, platform, merchants, logistics providers, and customers —based on
product quality and logistics service.

5.1 Government: Regulation and Institutional Safeguards
Product Quality: Strengthen market supervision and law enforcement, enhance qualification
review of cross-border merchants, establish and strictly enforce product quality standards, conduct
regular inspections, and impose severe penalties on violators to i ncrease the cost of non -
compliance. At the same time, urge platforms to improve rules, regulate platform order, simplify
cross-border rights protection procedures, set up online complaint platforms, and provide
translation and arbitration support to solve language and evidence problems. This effectively
reduces consumer rights protection costs and cycles, safeguards consumer rights efficiently, and
promotes product quality supervision.
Logistics Service: Strengthen international logistics system guarantees, actively promote
international logistics cooperation, ensure full tracking of parcels, and implement compensation
mechanisms for lost items. This reduces problems such as “slow logistics and unaccounted losses,”
enhances reliability in logistics, and optimizes customer experience.

5.2 Platform: Service and Rule Optimization
Product Quality: Ensure product quality by setting strict merchant entry thresholds and requiring
detailed product quality certifications. Establish dedicated inspection teams for regular audits,
promptly remove products inconsistent with descriptions, simplify compensation procedures, and
clarify refund standards. Intervene in cases where merchants unreasonably reject returns or refunds,
