Abstract. Organizations seeking to improve performance, customer experience, and remain competitive in a rapidly
changing and technology-driven environment must undoubtedly implement digital transformation initiatives. Despite
large investments, many digital transformations do not achieve their goals, with human capital–related factors fre-
quently identified as critical constraints. Human capital encompasses a broad and interrelated set of factors, making
its evaluation in digital transformation contexts particularly complex, yet essential. Although prior research recognizes
human capital as a pivotal resource in digital transformation scenarios, a systematic and integrated understanding of
how it is conceptualized, assessed, and empirically examined remains limited. Using the SPAR-4-SLR protocol and
TCCM framework, this systematic literature review addresses this gap by synthesizing and structuring prior research
on human capital assessment research in digital transformations implementation. It systematically analyses the theo-
retical frameworks adopted, the organizational contexts in which empirical research is conducted, the variables and
levels of analysis used to operationalize human capital and related outcomes, and the methodological approaches ap-
plied. The findings provide an integrated overview of current scientific knowledge and highlight gaps in research across
different industries and levels of analysis, suggesting future studies should explore less digitally advanced sectors and
include individual, unit, and organizational level perspectives.

2. Methodology
2.1. Review design
This study employed a systematic literature review (SLR)
method to recognize, analyze and summarize academic
articles on the human capital assessment in the con-
text of digital initiatives. It was used to ensure a clear
and transparent process that can be replicated by other
researchers to identify, assess, and integrate existing stud-
ies. This approach enables systematic consolidation and
categorization of prior research, thereby enhancing the
accuracy and consistency of the findings.
The SLR is based on a detailed, step-by-step process,
which create a structure and distinguish SLRs from other
types of systematic review (Rocco et al., 2022, p. 105).
The framework of SPAR-4-SLR has been utilized. It en-
ables to organize the review process while helping re-
searchers identify potential challenges in advance, limit
subjective bias and strengthen transparency of analysis
(Paul et al., 2021).
2.2. Process
Structure of analysis is based on SPAR-4-SLR. Protocol
consists of three stages and six sub-stages that flow se -
quentially, namely
1. assembling, which involves identification and ac-
quisition of literature that have not been synthe-
sized,
2. arranging, which involves organization and pu-
rification of literature that are in the process of
being synthesized, and
3. assessing, which involves evaluation and report-
ing of literature that have been synthesized (Paul
et al., 2021)
The structure of this research due to framework is
presented in Figure A1 (Appendix).

Arranging
In the review 3196 journal articles were identified after
the arranging step. Garima et al. (2025) emphasize that
the Theories, Characteristics, Contexts, and Methodol-
ogy (TCCM) framework supports an extensive examina-
tion of the articles, including various aspects of analysis.
Due to this reason TCCM framework was employed in
this study.
To achieve data purification, the criteria used for
journal articles selection in this study were defined to
ensure the appropriateness of the selected articles to the
research topic and field of study. The English language
and subject area were specified as inclusion criteria. After
these steps, 625 articles were retained. Duplicates were
removed because the same articles could be found in se-
lected databases. 505 articles left after these actions.
To maintain the quality of the analyzed sources,
and in line with guidelines of Paul and Criado (2020),
only articles in Q1 and Q2 high impact academic jour -
nals were selected for further analysis. After arranging,
390 articles were retained.
Journal articles not relevant to the study  – such as
research on human capital in economics, analyses of
separate human capital factors rather than human capi-
tal construct, and studies missing the context of digital
transformation – were excluded.
After the arranging step, 26 journal articles were ana-
lyzed. The final selection of articles selected for further
analysis is presented in Table 2.

The study uses TCCM framework. This methodology

coded in MS Excel and subsequently summarized.
During the final phase, tables and figures were devel-

Human Capital Theory
This theory was the first to systematically examine hu-
man capital. Economists such as Schultz (1961) and
Becker (1964) conceptualized human capital as an in-
vestment in skills, through education and training to
drive productivity and economic growth. Becker (1964)
defined human capital as the knowledge, information,
ideas, skills, and health of individuals. Accordingly, the
studies on human capital were guided by economic terms
such as stakeholder value bargaining power and econom-
ic value (Boon et al., 2018).
Scholars of human capital theory linked differences
among employees to variations in individual outcomes.
As a foundation framework, it highlights the need to in-
vest in employees through training and education and
underscores the value that human capital generates for
organizations.
Intellectual Capital Theory
This theory implies that intellectual capital consists of
three main components: human capital, social capital,
and organizational capital (Singh & Rao, 2016). These
three dimensions “ . . . interact dynamically to support in-
novation, strategic differentiation and long-term perfor-
mance” (Procacci et al., 2025, p. 618). Human capital is
considered a key intangible asset that reflects employees’
knowledge, skills, competencies, experience, and abilities
(Rehman et al., 2022). Accordingly, human capital rep -
resents the primary driver within the intellectual capital
framework, as it activates and leverages organizational
and relational capital to enable organizations gain a last-
ing position in market.
Resource-Based View
RBV theory states that resources within organization can
improve organizational performance and strengthen
long-term competitiveness. Wan et  al. (2011) dis-
cussed that these resources are “ . . . valuable, rare,
imperfectly imitable, and nonsubstitutable” (p. 1340).
According to this theory, “ . . . resources and capabili-
ties together form the tangible and intangible assets
of the firm” (Gillani et al., 2024, p. 3). Human capi-
tal is viewed as an important organizational resource
that can support a firm’s competitive position (Ploy-
hart, 2021). The main idea of competitive advantage
was grounded in the fact that human capital can be
“determinant of outputs” , human capital resources are
“heterogeneously distributed among firms” , specific-
ity, complexity and causal ambiguity are common to
human capital (Boon et al., 2018). Thus, human capi-
tal includes employees’ knowledge, skills, and abilities
(Coyle-Shapiro et al., 2006, as cited in Grant & Y eo,
2024). Later on, some scholars started to emphasize
human capital as a collective organizational resource
that arises from employees’ knowledge, skills, abilities,
and other characteristics (KSAOs) (Ployhart et  al.,
2014). In digital transformation processes, employees
require particular skills and abilities to benefit from
digital technologies and to successfully accomplish
transformation goals (Correani et  al., 2020). Thus,
within the RBV framework, human capital constitutes
an important strategic resource that enables organiza-
tions to develop, implement, and sustain digital trans-
formation initiatives that enhance long-term competi-
tiveness.
Dynamic Capabilities Theory
This theory emphasizes the significance of an organiza-
tion’s dynamic strategic capabilities. According to Wang
et al. (2012) capabilities could be “the ability to prepare,
extend, and renew the entire intellectual capital stock
within firms to quickly achieve evolving strategic goals
for short-term and long-term superior performance”

als, networks, as well as organizational structures and
processes forms a distinctive combination of a firm’s re-

When implementing digital transformation, dynamic ca-

and renew their capabilities to respond effectively to rapid

Knowledge-Based View Theory
This theory highlights that organizational strategy should
focus on effectively combining and using knowledge, as it
represents the organization’s most valuable asset. Knowl-
edge is most effectively transferred through organization-
al structures, processes, and technologies, while knowl -
edge dissemination is considered a critical component
of competitive advantage (Grant, 1996). As part of hu-
man capital, knowledge plays important role in fostering
digital transformations and innovations (Rehman et al.,
2022). This theory shows that organizations should fo-
cus on managing and sharing knowledge effectively, as it
strengthens human capital and supports innovation and
digital transformation.
Resource Orchestration Theory
This theory shows the importance of managing re-
sources in a purposeful and effective way to strengthen
performance of firm. It focuses on how organizations
obtain, combine, and use their resources to improve
their position (Y ang et al., 2025). According to Sirmon
et  al. (2011), managers need to effectively coordinate
organizational assets and align capabilities to achieve
superior performance. Resource orchestration becomes
particularly important, because it enables organization
to strategically structure, bundle, and leverage their hu-
man and technological resources to successfully execute
digital initiatives.

3.4. Methods (M) used in studies
After reviewing the articles, it could be seen that these
studies incorporate various quantitative, qualitative and
mixed data collection techniques (Table  7). The most

4. Conclusions
This study shows that the most common theoretical
frameworks used in human capital research in digital
transformation implementation are Resource-Based and
Human Capital Theories. These findings reveal that ex-
isting literature predominantly conceptualizes human
capital as a central organizational factor connected to
stronger performance in digital transformation initiatives
and organization’s survival in a competitive market. This
perspective is consistent with prior research, emphasiz-
ing human capital’s importance in the implementation of
digital transformations (Verhoef et al., 2021; Hanelt et al.,
2020). Human Capital Theory emphasizes spending on
education, training, and skills as key drivers of produc-
tivity and value creation, while the Resource-Based View
frames human capital as a beneficial and inimitable re-
source that enables organizations to develop and sustain
digital transformation initiatives. Overall, the findings
demonstrate that research in this field is largely ground-
ed in economic and strategic perspectives, where human
capital is non-negotiable and core resource to achieve
successful and competitive outcomes of digital transfor -
mation. Based on these findings, it is recommended that
organizations adopting digital initiatives must explicitly
recognize the strategic importance of human capital and
prioritize digital competencies, capability development,
and talent retention to ensure sustainable transformation
results.
An analysis of organizational contexts shows that
most studies were conducted in the Asian region
and Europe, suggesting that human capital in digital
transformation implementation is perceived as strategi-
cally significant in these regions. In addition, research
has predominantly focused on the service, technology,
manufacturing, and finance and insurance sectors. This
concentration of studies indicates that research attention
is largely directed toward industries with high levels of
digitalization, fast adoption of new technologies, and in-
tense competition. According to the Coherent Solutions
(2025) guide on digital transformation trends, industries
including healthcare, financial services, retail, and manu-
facturing are among those seeing the most significant ad-
vancements in digital transformation, as organizations in
these sectors are actively adopting technologies like AI-
assisted tools, predictive maintenance, and data-driven
platforms that improve operational efficiency, customer
experience, and overall business value. These develop-
ments show that technology alone is not enough—suc-
cessful digital transformation also depends on how well
organizations develop and use their human capital.
The analysis of the reviewed studies indicates that
human capital is predominantly conceptualized as an
independent variable in digital transformation research,
most often examined at the organizational level, with
fewer studies adopting an individual-level perspective.
Its operationalization ranges from a broad human capital
construct to more specific forms, such as IT or mana-
gerial human capital. Digital transformation is typically
treated as the main dependent variable, despite varia-
tions in terminology (e.g., digital strategy implementa-
tion, technology adoption, digital innovativeness), which
largely capture the same organizational-level phenom-
enon. Also, human capital is not examined solely as a
direct antecedent of digital transformation, but in some
studies also acts as a mediating or influencing factor
between digital transformation and broader outcomes
such as organizational performance, resilience, and com-
petitiveness. Overall, the findings indicate that research
predominantly examines human capital and digital

The methodological analysis shows that research on
human capital regarding digital transformation is pre-
dominantly quantitative. Most studies rely on primary
data collected from employees through online surveys
and structured or semi-structured interviews, while sec-
ondary data analysis and systematic literature reviews are
less common. PLS-SEM is the most frequently applied
analytical method, followed by regression analysis, with
covariance-based SEM and mediation or moderation
analyses used less often. Qualitative approaches, such
as thematic or content analysis, are comparatively rare.
Overall, the findings indicate a strong preference for
quantitative, variance-based designs, with limited use of
in-depth qualitative or mixed-method approaches. Based
on these findings, it is recommended that organizations
assessing human capital in digital transformation should
adopt more comprehensive evaluation approaches. While
quantitative tools such as surveys and performance met-
rics provide measurable insights, they should be com-
plemented with qualitative assessments to better capture
employee perceptions, and behaviour, which significantly
influence transformation success.
Regarding the findings, there are some suggestions
for future research. First, future research should further
investigate how human capital contributes to technology
adoption and value creation in digitally intensive indus-
tries, while also expanding the scope to less digitally ma-
ture sectors to increase the broader applicability of the
findings. Second, given that most studies focus on the
organizational level of human capital, further research
should include multiple levels of analysis to better un-
derstand interactions across individual, unit, and organi-
zational levels within digital transformation processes.
Considering limitations, searching for articles was
carefully designed to keep the quality and reliability of
the selected articles. The analysis included only articles
from selected databases, through clearly defined key-
words, and was limited to peer-reviewed journal arti-
cles. Journal articles met criteria of English language and
ranking rate of journals of Q1 and Q2. Although these
criteria helped ensure high academic standards, they
could have reduced the extent of the review by excluding
relevant articles published in other languages, databases,
or lower-ranked journals. Also, the strong focus on digi-
tally intensive sectors (technology, finance, manufactur -
ing, services) may underrepresent less digitally mature
industries, limiting the applicability of conclusions across
diverse organizational settings.
