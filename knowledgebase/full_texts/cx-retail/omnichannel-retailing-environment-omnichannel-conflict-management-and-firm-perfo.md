Abstract
Amid escalating digitization and intensifying market complexity, omni -channel retailing has emerged as a
strategic imperative for retail firms. This study, anchored in contingency theory, investigates the intricate
interplay among the omni -channel retailin g environment, omni -channel conflict management, and firm
performance, with empirical evidence drawn from 342 Chinese cosmetics firms. Utilizing Partial Least Squares
Structural Equation Modelling (PLS -SEM), the research delineates how environmental contin gencies positively
shape conflict management strategies, which exhibit statistically significant impacts on firm performance. The
results demonstrate that the omni -channel retailing environment significantly influences omni -channel conflict
management, which in turn has a significant effect on firm performance.
Keywords: Omni-channel retailing, Omni-channel retailing environment, Omni -channel conflict management ,
Firm performance
1. Introduction
The retail industry has undergone profound transformations as a result of the rapid expansion of e-commerce and
digitalization (Verhoef et al., 2015). The external environment constitutes a fundamental basis for strategic
management research. In the current omni -channel era, retailing market is undergoing significan t changes,
prompting this study to focus on omni-channel retailing. With the advancement of e-commerce, consumers in the
retail sector are demonstrating increasingly diverse behavioral patterns (Swoboda et al., 2021). This shift can be
attributed to evolving retail market conditions and the transformation of retailers‘ business models (Kim & Chun,
2018; Pu et al., 2021; Verhoef et al., 2015). Consumers are gradually adopting new consumption patterns,
attitudes, and structures that markedly differ from those  of the past, reflecting shifting consumer demands
(Swoboda et al., 2021). These developments present both opportunities and challenges, thereby stimulating
extensive academic inquiry (Son et al., 2021; Thaichon et al., 2020). Rigby (2011) noted that with the evolution
of consumer demand, retailing is undergoing rapid transformation to Omni-channel Retailing, which has become
a burgeoning trend across most industries (Bijmolt et al., 2021). Omni-channel retailing has emerged as a critical
business strategy adopted by numerous firms. Through an omni-channel strategy, customers can access multiple
shopping channels, necessitating the seamless integration of online and offline platforms, which in turn is
essential for firms capitalizing on new retail opportunities and enhancing market position  (Melero et al., 2016).
Therefore, the market-driven nature of the retail market requires close alignment with the omni -channel retailing
environment. To succeed in this evolving landscape, compani es are actively seeking to develop appropriate
strategic frameworks for their operations (Ishfaq et al., 2024). The first step involves examining the
characteristics of the omni-channel retailing environment to identify its key components and influencing f actors
on other variables. Subsequently, a comprehensive review of relevant literature is conducted to enhance
understanding of the cosmetics industry and inform the development of an updated omni -channel model. Based
on this foundation, the study employs quantitative methods to validate the proposed environmental variables.
1.1 Problem Statement
This study identifies several theoretical gaps. First, understanding the determinants of firm performance remains
a central objective in organizational research (Miller et al., 2013). Despite its significance, firm performance is a

complex construct that poses challenges for operationalization, reflecting inconsistencies across research
domains and suggesting underdev eloped theoretical foundations of the performance concept (Barney, 1991;
Miller et al., 2013; Urquhart & Zhang, 2022). Most existing studies have neglected measurement issues
associated with omni-channel retailing performance (Miller et al., 2013). While considerable scholarly attention
has been devoted to the benefits of omni -channel integration for firms (Du et al., 2018; Eyuboglu et al., 2017;
Liu & Son, 2024; Melero et al., 2016), limited research has examined the relationship between omni -channel

Second, since the onset of the pandemic in 2020, global supply chains have experienced significant disruptions,
creating new opportunities for domest ic cosmetics enterprises (Hu et al., 2020). The rapid advancement of
digitization and e-commerce has intensified market competition and introduced substantial challenges for firms
across sectors. This underscores the need to investigate not only the advant ages of omni -channel retailing
(Saghiri et al., 2017) but also its potential barriers (Verhoef, 2015). As Zhang et al. (2023) argue, firms must
evaluate factors such as market size, channel operating costs, and competitive intensity when determining
whether to integrate new channels into their existing portfolios. Effective organizational learning, coordination,
and experimentation are essential to managing inter-channel conflicts until a successful omni-channel strategy is
fully implemented (Picot-Coupey et al., 2016).
According to contingency theory, no single strategy is universally optimal for all organizations; rather, local
cosmetics firms must adapt their strategies to dynamic market conditions, innovate in channel management
capabilities, and streng then internal competencies to capitalize on emerging opportunities and address evolving
challenges (Peters et al., 1985). Therefore, to advance omni -channel research and make meaningful theoretical
contributions, it is imperative to integrate these diverse theoretical perspectives.
1.2 Importance of This Study
This study significantly contributes to the current body of knowledge in several key areas. First, it enhances our
understanding of the determinants of firm performance by developing a conceptual mode l that elucidates the
factors influencing firm performance. The study investigates the relationship between the omni -channel retailing
environment, omni-channel conflict management, and firm performance from a contingency perspective, with a
focus on the c osmetics industry. This helps firms better understand omni -channel retailing practices. Then, it
provides insights for Chinese cosmetics enterprises on how to enhance firm performance through omni -channel
conflict management, which is crucial for addressin g application challenges in this domain. By illustrating the
multidimensional nature of omni -channel retailing, the study identifies both the benefits and potential
weaknesses in firm performance and channel management.
1.3 Literature Review
This study ill ustrated the relevant literature about firm performance, omni -channel retailing environment and
omni-channel conflict management, as well as contingency theory.
1.3.1 Firm Performance
Firm performance denotes the evaluative outcomes of a company‘s efforts across multiple dimensions, and
enhancing such performance has remained a pivotal objective for organizations globally (Taouab & Issor, 2019).
Nevertheless, the definition and measurement of firm performance exhibit notable variations across distinct
industries (Taouab & Issor, 2019). Some researchers argued that both online and offline social networks exert a
positive influence on firm performance (Tajvidi & Karami, 2021), with marketing capabilities functioning as a
mediating mechanism. Some authors menti oned that marketing innovation contributes positively to firm
performance (Gupta et al., 2020). Notably, the most fundamental proposition in strategic management research
centers on performance disparities among enterprises (Wang & Gao, 2021). Enterprises pursue not only
short-term performance but also long -term performance sustained by sustainable competitive advantages.
Consequently, a firm‘s sustainable competitive advantage derives not merely from external factors but also
impact from internal determina nts (Barney, 1991; Teece et al., 1997). Against this backdrop, the present study
seeks to examine the key determinants of firm performance.
1.3.2 Omni-channel Retailing Environment
Previous literature has explored the conceptual evolution of retailing (Ye et al., 2018) . Omni-channel retailing
integrates mult iple retail channels and information media, enabling retailers to interact with consumers
seamlessly across various platforms (Kolbe et al., 2022) . Many scholars agree that omni -channel retailing
remains in its nascent stages both theoretically and practically. Saghiri et al. (2017) emphasize the importance of

1.3.3 Omnichannel Conflict Management
Conflict is a process wherein one party perceives that its interests are being opposed or adversely affected by
another party (Wall Jr & Callister, 1995) . Traditi onally, conflict has been regarded as dysfunctional within
channels or between channel members (Menon et al., 1996) , which refers to detrimental behaviors within an
organization that hinder its ability to achieve its objectives (Rose & Shoham, 2004) . Actually, conflict exerts a
complex influence on channels (Menon et al. 1996) that including detrimental effects, such as diminishing
market performance or undermining strategic quality, as well as positive ef fects, such as foster higher levels of
innovation and competitiveness within channels, enhance both the quality of strategy and market performance
(Chang & Gotcher, 2010; Claro et al., 2018; Menon et al., 1996) . In an omni -channel retail environment,
different channels often have their own performance m etrics and incentives (Grewal et al., 2021) . Pricing and
promotion strategies can vary significantly across channels, leading to omnichannel conflict (Druck, 2018). Ye et
al. (2018)  noted that conflict among staff across different channels is one of the barriers to successful
omni-channel retailing.
Extensive literature exists on conflict management, indicating that organizations can benefit from effective
conflict handling (Claro et al., 2018; Thomas & Schmidt, 1976) . Most conflict management approaches ar e
contextually effective, and proper conflict management can contribute to a firm's competitive advantage (Song et
al., 2000). From a managerial perspective, conflict management is an integral part of organizational life. Based
on a two-dimensional conflict model, five conflict-handling modes are identified: collaborating, accommodating,
compromising, forcing, and avoiding (Song et al., 2000; Thomas, 1992; Thomas & Schmidt, 1976).
Although numerous studies have highlighted the impact of channel conf lict (Alsubari et al., 2024; Eyuboglu et
al., 2017; Ji ng et al., 2024) , few have provided empirical validation for conflict management strate gies,
especially in the omni -channel retailing environment. To survive from omni -channel retailing, firms need to
consistently leverage their resources and capabilities across marketing, logistics and supply chain management,
as well as organizational mana gement. This study therefore focuses on leveraging the positive effects of
functional conflict while mitigating the negative impacts of dysfunctional conflict through effective conflict
management behaviors. An effective omni -channel conflict management is  vital for maintaining a positive
customer experience. Customers expect a seamless and consistent experience across all channels. Specifically, it
incorporates conflict management variables into the omni -channel retailing strategy to examine the mechanisms
linking omni-channel conflict management with firm performance.
1.3.4 Conflict Theory
Modern conflict theory posits that conflict exists universally —between individuals, social classes, interest
groups, teams, organizations, and even different cultures a nd value systems. Some authors viewed conflict as a
result of choice, where individuals make decisions based on their own interests, and the incompatibility of these
choices leads to conflicts between individuals. When such "choices" occur at the group level, inter-group conflict
ensues (Pichler, 1998). Previously, there was a widespread belief that conflict was inherently harmful and should
be eradicated to achieve societal harmo ny. While, Menon et al. (1996)  argued that conflic t is not solely
destructive. By integrating structural -functional analysis with social conflict theory, they proposed that conflict
possesses both positive and negative functions. Under specific conditions, conflict can serve positive roles, such
as preventing the rigidity of social systems, enhancing the adaptability of social organizations, and promoting
social integration. Moreover, conflict can sometimes have positive effects on group performance (Druck, 2018).
By encouraging managers to maintain an appropriate level of conflict, it can foster continuous innov ation and
ensure that the group remains dynamic, thereby promoting organizational development (Druck, 2018).
In the field of management, conflict management is viewed as a comprehensive process encompassing
recognition, analysis, and resolution, thereby addressing the entire life cycle of conflict, specifically,  conflict
management focuses on the actual behaviors, responses, and intentions during conflicts. It analyzes the
underlying patterns, coping strategies, and methods to effectively manage conflicts at different stages (Verma,
1998). Thomas and Schmidt (1976)  identified five distinct strategies that individuals employ when addressing

interpersonal conflict, respectively, avoidance, competition, accommodation, compromise method, and  the
collaboration method. This research applied the conflict management strategy in omnichannel field, exploring
the relationship between omni -channel retailing environment, omni -channel conflict management and firm
performance.
1.3.5 Hypotheses
The omni -channel retailing environment is a complex, multi -dimensional, multi -level, and multi -objective
system that integrates natural, social, and economic factors, which means that enterprises may struggle to adapt
to the changes in consumer demand and market s tructure (Du et al., 2018) . In such complex and volatile
competitive marketing environments, firms can implement omnichannel retailing strategies to to satisfy
environmental demands (Lin et al., 2020). However, based on the literature review, this research finds that while
channel conflict among various channels is inevitable, conflict manage ment has long been recognized as a
critical component of strategic management (Du et al., 2018) . Nonetheless, limited studies have focused on
conflict management within the context of omni -channel retailing strategies (see Table 1). Verhoef et al. (2015)
argue that omni-channel retailing can mitigate channel conflicts through integration across all channels. However,
combining online and offline channels inevitably introduces new conflicts. Cross -channel integration can
simultaneously generate inter-channel conflicts, thereby reducing efficiency and performance  (Rose & Shoham,
2004; Tang et al., 2017).

Thus, this research argues that conflict management is a critical component of omni -channel retailing strategy,
referred to as omni -channel conflict management. It is proposed that to enhance the performa nce of
omni-channel retailing, firms need to implement effective omni-channel conflict management, which constitutes
an essential aspect of the overall omni -channel retailing strategy. Enterprises must address cross -channel
conflicts and select appropriate channel strategies, which are deemed crucial for success (Du et al., 2018).
H1: Omnichannel retailing environment has positive effect on omnichannel conflict management.
Previous research finds that conflict significantly influences sustainable competitive advantage (Tang et al.,
2017). By investigating manufacturers' channel strategies, several studies have identified the effects of channel
conflict. Kim and Chun (2018) discovered that retailer competition exacerbated by inter -channel dynamics in
retailing can negatively impact performance. When manufacturers introduce an online channel, the competition
intensifies. Moreover, task and emotional conflicts tend to negatively influence strategy and firm performance.
Specifically, emotional conflict, a dysfunctional phenomenon, reduces channel efficiency and overall firm
performance (Rose & Shoham, 2004).
Therefore, some scholars have argued that due to operational and strategic barriers, the successful
implementation of an omni-channel strategy may be idealistic (Hajdas et al., 2022). However, conflict is not only
inevitable but can also possess positive value. According to contemporary conflict theory, an increas ing number
of studies highlight the benefits of conflict, focusing on conflict avoidance behaviors, conflict coordination
learning, joint marketing strategy quality, and joint profit performance  (Chang & Gotcher, 2010) . Ishii (2020)
research further indicates that in dual -channel systems, manufacturers often adopt conf lict management
strategies, which prove effective in enhancing overall channel system performance. Consequently, integrating
conflict management into firms' channel strategies can positively impact their performance (Claro et al., 2018).
Therefore, it can be concluded that through the implementation of omni -channel conflict management, firms can
strategically address channel conflicts by fostering brand spirit, promoting cooperation, adopting avoidance
strategies, or establishing a competitive edge. Consequently, these measures can also enhance firm performance.

H2: Omni-channel conflict management has a positive influence on firm performance.
According to a review of previous studies, many scholars have highlighted that the core of omni -channel
retailing lies in seamlessly and consistently leveraging firms' resources and capabilities (Ye, Lau, & Teo, 2018).
According to the research, strategy formulation can also focus on conflict resolution approaches (Harreld et al.,
2007). According to (Porter, 1997)  framework, strategy is intricately linked with its external environment,
including factors such as industry dynamics, entry barriers, and competitive rivalry. Wilden et al. (2013) further
underscored the significance of environmental conditions in shaping dynamic capabilities. In an increasingly
competitive landscape, the outcomes of firms' action s are contingent upon the evolving environment. In highly
competitive environments, the acquisition of scarce resources serves as a key source of economic profits for
firms, which in turn drives the enhancement of skills and knowledge.
A firm's successful execution of strategy should reflect its adaptation to the fast -changing market environment.
Moreover, the effectiveness of a strategy is contingent upon the turbulent environment. Conflict management are
proposed to assist managers in enhancing existing firm capabilities, thereby better aligning with environmental
demands (Pavlou & El Sawy, 2011).
Therefore, omnichannel conflict management mediate the relationship between omni -channel retailing
environment and firm performance. Environment analysis is the first step for strategy, but, omnichannel conflict
management of the organization determine the implementation.

2. Method
The research methods employed in this paper are designed to address existing research gaps and achieve the
stated objectives. Based on the research questions and theories outlined in previous chapters, this section utilizes
a quantitative research approach  to test the hypotheses, collecting data from respondents through a structured
questionnaire survey.
2.1 Samples
In this study, the sample was selected from participants listed in China‘s Beauty Expo, organized by the China
Council for the Promotion of Int ernational Trade (CCPIT). For this study, stratified sampling is deemed an
appropriate method as it allows the population to be divided into distinct groups (Etikan & Babatope, 2019). The
target population comprised approximately 1000 samples, divided into two groups: the retailer group (40%) and
the brand group (60%). The actual collected samples included 160 from the retailer group and 190 from the
brand group, totaling 350 valid responses.
2.2 Measurement of Variables
This study adopted a three -dimensional measurement approach for omni -channel retailing environment:
information technology  (Jiang & McCabe, 2021) , competitive intensity (Wilden et al., 2013) , and customers'
omni-channel shopping intention (Ryu et al., 2023).
This study measures omni -channel conflict management using three dimensions: Cooperative behavior,
Avoidance behavior, and Competitive behavior, all adapted from (Chen et al., 2005).
Firm performance has been defied as the outcome of evaluating effort by many authors, whic h is typically
regarded as the dependent variable  (Landi et al., 2022) . This study adopts measurement scales from  (Pan et al.,
2024), (Sahoo & Vijayvargy, 2021) , and (Pinheiro et al., 2022)  across three dimensions: market performance,
social performance, and operational performance.

This study contributes to the existing literature on omni -channel retailing by examining the interactions among
the omni-channel retailing environment, omni-channel conflict in explaining firm performance. By analyzing the
mediating effect of omni -channel conflict management, this research extends conflict theory through the
integration of contingent theory and sustainable competitive advantage to explain the comprehensive research
framework. The findings provide insights for  retailing firms, enhancing the effectiveness of omni -channel
strategies.
This research extends the theory of omni -channel retailing in the retailing industry by focusing on how these
elements interact to influence firm performance. Unlike previous literat ure that often investigates specific
dimensions, this study adopts a process -oriented approach to explore the interaction mechanism. Through
rigorous research design and data analysis, it finds that the omni -channel environment, omni -channel conflict
management may have varying degrees of impact on explaining the variability in firm performance, all of which
are critical influencing factors. Therefore, firms cannot solely rely on implementing an omni -channel conflict
management to improve performance; adapt ing to the changing environment is equally essential. The findings
suggest that retailing firms are influenced by the omni -channel environment. This revision helps to make the
conflict theory more accurately reflect omni -channel retailing in the real world , enhancing the theory's
explanatory and predictive power.
This study not only enhance concept of omni -channel retailing, but also examine the mechanism between
omni-channel retailing environment and omni -channel conflict management from a firm performance
perspective, standing on contingency theory. Additionally, this research also assist retailing firms in identifying
the uncovered key factors affecting the performance of firms in the context of omni -channel retailing and
examining the benefits of solving  the conflicts between channels. These findings have practical implications for
the retailing industry.
