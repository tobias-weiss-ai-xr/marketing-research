Abstract

Purpose: This study investigates, through causal paths, how omnichannel
integration influences customer trust and, jointly, determines purchase intention in
the retail sectors of Ecuador, Peru, and Colombia.
Method: A quantitative approach was adopted; a PLS -SEM model was validated
using 292 questionnaires from consumers who simultaneously employ physical
stores and digital channels. Reliability, convergent and discriminant validity, and
bootstrap resampling ensured model robustness.
Originality/Relevance: The research addresses the theoretical gap concerning
omnichannel dynamics in Latin American emerging markets, responding to calls
for contextualizing e -commerce theories in developing economies with diverse
consumption habits and digital maturity levels.
Results: Perceived omnichannel integration exerted direct and significant effects
on trust (β = 0.400; p < 0.001) and on purchase intention (β = 0.532; p < 0.001).
Trust, in turn, affected intention (β = 0.342; p < 0.001), partially mediating the
relationship. The  model explains 54.5 % of the variance in purchase intention,
demonstrating solid explanatory power.
Theoretical/Methodological Contributions: The study empirically confirms the
mediating role of trust within an integrated model, enriching omnichannel
marketing literature and validating Spanish-language scales for emerging contexts.
Methodologically, it confirms PLS -SEM’s suitability for moderat e, multi-country
samples and provides metrics for future comparative research.

1 Introduction

In recent years, omnichannel retailing has become a central research stream for
understanding transformations in consumer behavior and commercial management in digital and
hybrid environments (Asmare & Zewdie, 2022). The increasing integration of physical and digital
channels within the customer journey not only reshapes consumer expectations but also requires
firms to reconsider how they build trust and stimulate purchase intention (Osiyevskyy et al., 2024).
From a theoretical perspective, this phenomenon is best conceptualized as a multidimensional
system involving technological, relational, and perceptual interactions (Ciasullo et al., 2022).
Despite these advances, a persistent gap in empirical evidence remains regarding the relationship
between omnichannel retailing, customer trust, and purchase intention, particularly in emerging
markets, where consumption dynamics differ from those in developed economies (Tran Xuan et
al., 2023). Therefore, establishing robust links between theoretical frameworks and e mpirical
validation in specific commercial contexts is essential.
E-commerce growth in Latin America has been substantial, driven by increased digital
access, evolving consumption habits, and the demand for more integrated shopping experiences
(Díaz, 2024). According to Statista (2024), the sector is expected to grow by 51.4% between 2024
and 2029, reaching approximately 281.3 million users. This expansion has reinforced the need for
omnichannel strategies, defined as the seamless integration of customer touchpoints across
physical, digital, and mobile channels (Hübner et  al., 2022). Although channel preferences vary
by product category, with physical stores remaining dominant in sectors such as grocery retail,
78% of Latin American consumers prefer hybrid purchasing experiences that combine online and
offline interactions (America Mall & Retail, 2024).

increasingly value personalization, channel consistency, and frictionless purchasing processes (Li

2 Literature Review

2.1 Omnichannel Retailing as a Determinant of Trust and Purchase Intention

Within contemporary retail contexts, omnichannel retailing has emerged as a strategic
approach aimed at integrating physical and digital channels to enhance customer experience and
generate commercial synergies (Lorenzo -Romero et al., 2020). Unlike multich annel strategies
characterized by the coexistence of independent channels omnichannel retailing seeks
convergence across channels, enabling consumers to perceive a continuous and frictionless
interaction (Rahman et al., 2025). This approach reflects the ev olution of digital consumer
behavior, where individuals seamlessly navigate between online and offline platforms to search,
compare, and purchase products (Yin et al., 2022). Recent literature emphasizes that the
implementation of coherent omnichannel expe riences has become a critical driver of customer
retention and loyalty, particularly in emerging markets (Thaichon et al., 2024).
In Latin America, studies such as Barón (2023) highlight that the adoption of omnichannel
strategies still faces structural challenges related to technological infrastructure, reverse logistics,
and workforce capabilities. Nevertheless, Hua et al. (2025) d emonstrates that channel integration
enhances perceived value and customer satisfaction, especially when firms incorporate features
such as real-time order tracking, in-store pickup options, and personalized digital customer service
(Taheri et al., 2024). In this sense, omnichannel strategies not only improve operational efficiency
but also influence key psychological constructs, including customer trust and purchase intention
(Hsu et al., 2024).
Customer trust, in turn, has been widely recognized as a central construct in relationship
marketing, given its role as a mediator between service perception and customer loyalty (Riaz et
al., 2024; Mishra et al., 2025). In omnichannel environments, trust is redefined through consistency
across channels, the security of digital transactions, brand reputation, and the effective
management of customer service interactions (Lee & Park, 2024; Natarajan et al., 2024). Empirical
evidence suggests that consumers a re more likely to trust brands that maintain informational,
visual, and service consistency across both physical and digital channels (Frasquet et al., 2024).
Furthermore, a firm’s ability to protect user privacy, fulfill promises, and effectively resolve
conflicts becomes a critical dimension in trust formation, particularly in markets characterized by
high levels of informality and perceived risk (Martínez-Navarro et al., 2022; Rizomyliotis, 2024).

3.1 Descriptive Results

To better understand consumer characteristics within integrated shopping environments, a
descriptive analysis was conducted based on a sample of 292 participants who reported using both
digital and physical channels to search for information or complete purchase decisions in the retail
sector. The sample included consumers from three Latin American countries: Ecuador (n = 107;
37%), Perú (n = 95; 33%), and Colombia (n = 90; 31%), providing a balanced representation of
relevant cultural and commercial contexts for the study.
Regarding sociodemographic variables, female participants represented 59.1% of the
sample, while males accounted for 40.9%. Age distribution was primarily concentrated in the 25-
34 age group (36.6%), followed by 18 -24 (33.1%), indicating that most responde nts belong to
digitally native or highly digitally engaged segments. In terms of education, 60.1% of participants
reported holding a university degree, while 6.5% had completed postgraduate studies, reflecting a
relatively high educational level associated  with greater technological proficiency and more
demanding consumption patterns.
In terms of digital behavior, the results show that WhatsApp is the most frequently used
platform (31.3%), followed by Instagram (24.7%) and TikTok (22.0%), highlighting the relevance
of mobile and visually oriented platforms in omnichannel interaction str ategies. This behavior is
consistent with purchasing patterns, as 45% of participants reported preferring to initiate their
purchase process in physical stores, while 41% already begin in digital environments distributed
between social media (21%) and mobi le applications (20%). Only 14% reported starting their
purchase journey via websites, suggesting a gradual shift toward more immediate and user-friendly
channels.
Furthermore, 83% of respondents indicated that they compare prices or products across
channels before making a purchase decision, either frequently (55%) or always (28%), reflecting
a strategic and multichannel -oriented purchasing behavior. In addition, pu rchase frequency
analysis revealed that 55.7% of respondents make purchases monthly at their preferred store,
followed by 33.7% who purchase on a weekly basis, confirming a consistent and recurring
consumption pattern.
