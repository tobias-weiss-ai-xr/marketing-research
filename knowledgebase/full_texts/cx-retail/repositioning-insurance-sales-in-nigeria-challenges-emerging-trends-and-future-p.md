Abstract
The insurance industry continues to play an integral part in financial systems worldwide by
providing risk shift, safeguarding assets, raising capital and ensuring economic health. The
Nigerian insurance industry still has a number of issues of low penetra tion, low consumer
confidence, inadequate awareness of the industry and poor sales performance, despite being
the largest economy in Africa. The industry's contribution to development and financial
inclusion has been limited by these difficulties. At the s ame time, the growth of the digital
economy has revolutionized the way consumers act, how businesses operate and how services
are provided in various sectors. As a result, insurance companies are increasingly forced to re-
think the way they sell insurance products to compete in a tech-focused world. The study will
seek to critically review the issues that face the insurance sales environment in Nigeria, discuss
the technological and market trends affecting insurance distribution environment and assess
the f uture prospect of insurers in Nigeria in the digital economy. The study builds on the
Technology Acceptance Model, Diffusion of Innovation Theory and literature on digital
transformation and Insurtech and concludes that digital transformation and integrating digital
technologies, customer-centric approaches, data -driven systems and innovative distribution
channels are key considerations in repositioning insurance sales. The paper concludes that
digital transformation offers insurance companies unparalleled opportunities to boost
insurance penetration, enhance customer experience and extend market reach in Nigeria. But
it will take some investments in technology infrastructure, regulatory change, digital literacy
and trust building in order to realize those benefits.

1. Introduction
The global insurance industry has undergone significant transformation over the last two
decades, driven by advances in information and communication technologies, changing
customer expectations, increasing competition, and evolving regulatory environments
(Deloitte, 2025). Historically, insurance sales was a heavily face -to-face process between
insurance providers, agents/brokers and policyholders. The advent of digital technologies has
changed the way insurance products are created, marketed, distributed and consumed (GrECo
Group, 2025). Insurance is an important way of reducing risks and improving economic
resilience. Insurance encourages investment, entrepreneurship and economic stability through
risk sharing and the payment of compensation for financial losses. Thus, the level of economic
growth and financial inclusion is generally higher in countries with higher insurance sectors
(Pandey & Ara, 2026).  Despite its significance, insurance in Nigeria is still very
underdeveloped compared to developed and e merging economies. Insurance penetration has
continued to remain at less than 1 per cent of Gross Domestic Product (GDP) consistently in
the industry reports as compared to the global averages. This is a sustainable structural problem
such as limited aware ness, negative public perception, lack of product innovations, poor

2. Review of Literature
2.1. Theoretical Foundation
The Diffusion of Innovation (DOI) Theory, developed by Rogers (2003), explains how new
ideas, technologies, and innovations are communicated and adopted within a social system over
time. The theory states that the diffusion speed of an innovation is influenced by consideration
of its traits, as perceived by prospective adopters (Rogers, 2003). Rogers described five key
characteristics of innovations that affect the rates of innovation adop tion: relative advantage,
compatibility, complexity, observability, a nd trialability. The relative advantage is the
superiority of an innovation to other innovations that are already available, and compatibility
is the degree to which the innovation fits with the values, experiences, and needs of the people
it is intended f or. Observability and trialability are related to the visibility of the results and
having a chance to experiment with the innovation before adopting it, while complexity
concerns ease or difficulty of using the innovation (Rogers, 2003). A theory of parti cular
relevance in the context of today's business climate is digital insurance sales. Access to online
insurance platforms, mobile apps and digital service channels is heavily reliant on consumer
perceptions of their utility and value. When customers feel  that digital insurance services are
convenient, reliable, secure, and offer quicker access to insurance products, they are more
likely to accept it. Therefore, the Diffusion of Innovation Theory is beneficial to understand
the process of how digital techn ologies affect customers' acceptance and how insurance sales
are expanding in the digital economy (Yayock et al., 2023).
The Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), developed by Davis (1989), is one of the most
widely used theories for explaining users’ acceptance of information technologies. According
to the model, there are two main factors that influence a person's willingness to use a
technological innovation: perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use (Schorr, 2023).
Perceived usefulness is defined as an individual's belief that using a specific technology will
help them perform better or get desired results, and perceived ease of use refers to how easy a
technology is to use and learn, also known as ease of use (Mogaji et al., 2024). On the insurance
market side, TAM offers a good indication of customers' use of digital insurance channels.
Consumers are more prone to use an online insurance platform if they feel that this platform
makes insurance easier to access, process faster and service is improved. In the same way, a
user-friendly interface, safe payment systems and effective customer support mechanisms are
beneficial to the user’s opinion of ease of use. Hence, the Technology Acceptance Model helps

The Resource -Based Theory (RBT), advanced by Barney (1991), argues that ‘sustainable

2.2. Empirical Reviews
This section reviews empirical studies examining the relationship between digital
transformation, Insurtech adoption, and insurance sales performance in Nigeria and other
developing economies, providing a foundation for understanding the challenges and
opportunities in repositioning insurance sales within the digital economy. Alghathian and
Alahmad (2025) found that emerging technologies like AI, machine learning, blockchain, and
the Internet of Things (IoT), present both opportunities and challenges for the insurance sector,
particularly due to the heightened risk of cyberattacks on sensitive customer data. Khakhar et
al. (2024) in their results show clear trends in the factors influencing insurance penetration,
with developed countries showing higher penetration rates due to things like income levels, the
efficacy of regulations, and financial literacy. Further, Nasution et al. (2025) found that digital
business transformation has impact on the performance of Indonesian insurance firms.
Mulyana et al. (2023 ) conclude that hybrid IT governance mechanisms influence digital
transformation and organizational performance in the banking and insurance industry of
Indonesia. Ahmad et al. (2025) revealed how large and continuing databases impact the
operational processes and general insurance markets, facilitating the acquisition of optimal
policyholder behavior and speeding up claims processing. The study delves into how AI
improves traditional insurance processes, spanning from the first point of contact to claims
processing and how potential changes brought about by blockchain technology. Yi et al. (2025)
revealed that R&D initiatives of  Insurtech significantly enhance insurers’ solvency
performance, which is pronounced for property and joint -venture insurers, even though this
positive relationship weakened following the COVID-19 pandemic.

4. Challenges Confronting Insurance Sales in Nigeria
4.1 Low Insurance Penetration and Distribution Constraints
Nigeria's insurance penetration rate is estimated to be as low as 1% to 3% and is one of the
lowest in the continent of Africa (Akintaro, 2025). This low uptake is despite an estimated
premium pool of about $68 billion in Africa, suggesting that when peopl e can access it, they
want it. The basic problem is not one of willingness to pay, it is a matter of designing and
delivering products to people in their places (Anaesoronye, 2026). A traditional approach of
agent-based distribution doesn't reach up to 90% of the population, including rural and informal
communities. The market is highly urbanised and dominated by brokers who account for close
to 80% of insurance business and this concentration is the main constraint in this sector.
Conventional channels hav e hindered the industry from expanding and reaching untapped
segments (Ehiorobo, 2020).

4.2 Trust Deficit and Claims Settlement Concerns
In Nigeria, the lack of trust by people is a major challenge to insurance uptake. Nigerians have
been observed to be more trustworthy of roadside money changers than insurance companies,
as only 10% expressed confidence in the insurers' ability to pay claims reliably while over 60%
indicated challenges in claims in the past year. The trust deficit has been caused by past
experiences with delayed claims, ambiguous policies, and a lack of responsiveness to
customers' needs (Capital Express Assurance Limited, 2026). As industry leaders have said, it
is not a test during the policy process, it is a test at the claim process. When claims are processed
promptly, efficiently, and smoothly, customers will have faith in the insurance. Conventional
marketing tactics have failed to close this reputation gap between what customers expect and
what they actually get (Anaesoronye, 2026b).

4.3 Product Complexity and Accessibility Barriers
The typical Nigerian citizen often finds insurance products complex, hard to grasp and
unapproachable. Traditionally, product design has been more influenced by underwriting
criteria than by customer needs, and policies are not well-designed to address the needs of retail
and low -income customers. The low financial literacy and lack of understanding about
insurance benefits among the general population (Oyewo, 2025) further complicates the
product complexity. Accessibility is not just a product problem; it' s a channel problem too.
Physical branch networks are mostly concentrated in urban areas, while digital channels have
been slow to grow. Among those who have bought insurance, their experience has been
disappointing and stifled uptake even more (Kajwang, 2022).

4.4 Limited Technological Adoption
Even though digital technologies have the potential to transform the insurance industry, the
Nigerian insurance industry has not been quick in the uptake of advanced tools. Ehiorobo
(2020) reported that although there are certain information technology too ls that have a
significant influence on sales, very few companies interviewed in his study were familiar with
AI. The industry still has a lot of manual processes, paper-based workflows and a lack of data
analytics capabilities (Capital Express Assurance Limited, 2026). This “technological lag” has
a number of impacts, including the inability to reach customers via digital channels, inefficient

5. Emerging Trends and Innovations
5.1 Insurtech and Digital Distribution Platforms
One of the most defining changes in the insurance sales landscape in Nigeria, in the times of
Insurtech is the growth of platforms that enable new players to enter the market and offer
insurance products. This is a significant watershed moment in the journey of the new regulatory
framework for technology -driven insurance services from NAICOM and the issuance of the
first partnering Insurtech licence to CBI Partnering Insurtech Limited. These platforms serve
as a digital marketplace that offers infrastructure that allow customers to compare, buy, manage
and initiate claims for insurance products via web and mobile channels (National Insurance
Commission (NAICOM), 2026). The impact that can be achieved is significant. According to
industry estimates, Insurtech innovation has the potential of making over 200 million Nigerians
fully participate in the insurance sector by 2030 (Anaesoronye, 2026). By eliminating
longstanding barriers to access and enabling a more streamlined interaction with the customer,
the platform model enables policy discovery, purchase, administration and claims initiation via
digital channels. Several access points are being used to access customers in rural and
underserved communities, such as mobile apps and USSD solutions (Nguyen, 2020).

5.2 Embedded Insurance and Mobile-First Distribution
Another trend that has potential applications in Nigeria is embedded insurance, which is a
process of embedding insurance into the normal flow of business and processes in digital
platforms. Fintech and ride -hailing apps are providing protection to merchan ts, gig workers
and consumers, who traditional methods could not reach. This model minimises friction in the
process of acquiring policies, and helps make insurance more compatible with customers'
existing digital habits (Indepth Research Institute, 2026).  The booming digital landscape in
Africa offers a pre-existing digital infrastructure that can enable this change: Africa has more
than 500 million mobile subscribers and more than 350 million mobile wallets. Meanwhile,
with mobile phones, insurers are abl e to reach their own customers, collect payments and
provide services directly, with startups creating mobile first insurance products that are more
affordable. Customers can now buy insurance policies using USSD codes and file claims on
WhatsApp thus making insurance part of their daily lives (Financial Nigeria, 2025).

5.3 Artificial Intelligence and Customer Engagement
While not yet widely adopted in the Nigerian insurance sector, AI applications are starting to
revolutionize customer interactions and streamline processes. Businesses are now using
conversational AI to communicate policy in layman's terms, chatbots to answer basic questions,
and fraud -detection tools are in the test phase. Others are moving to digital sign -ups,
fundamental analytics, and API-driven automation, but the use of advanced AI is less common
(Orimisan, 2025). AI has the potential to create a stro ng impact in overcoming trust issues.
Confidence in the insurance industry can be regained and the customer experience enhanced
by the use of automated claims processing, clear policy comparisons and customer interaction.
But industry giants have warned of  new dangers from AI, such as data privacy issues, cyber
security threats and algorithmic bias. For successful AI adoption, strong governance
mechanisms and consumer safeguards are essential (Pandieswari & Arumugam, 2026).

5.4 Regulatory Reform and Digital Modernization
The regulatory framework is moving towards digital transformation. The NIIRA 2025 brings
major changes with new requirements such as more rigorous capital rules, enforcing insurance
coverage requirements, and digitization. The Act allows NAICOM to regulate  and supervise
all insurance companies doing business in Nigeria, enhance consumer protection and boost the
efficiency of insurance operations. NAICOM's effort in creating Insurtech licensing
framework, experiment with regulatory sandbox, and partnering wi th fintech associations are
also clear evidence of its innovation efforts. The shift to principles-based instead of rule-based
supervision, adoption of risk -based capital regimes, and widened regulatory innovation
sandboxes, are examples of relaxed and inn ovation-friendly aspects of regulation. These are
developments that dovetail with the need of the regulators to promote innovations while
ensuring protection of consumers as they attest that both innovation and consumer protection
is not opposites, rather they are complementary (Federal Government of Nigeria, 2025).

6. Future Prospects and Strategic Imperatives
6.1 Market Potential and Growth Trajectory
Nigeria has immense prospects for insurance sales in the future. The low level of insurance
penetration in Africa is seen as a multi -billion-dollar opportunity instead of constraint by
industry leaders (Enhancing Financial Innovation & Access (EFInA), 2018). The sector has an
addressable population of more than 200 million, is experiencing a significant shift in digital
infrastructure and has progres sive regulatory reforms in place allowing it to change. The
harmonization of the initiatives by the regulators, insurance firms and technology vendors may
yield insurance penetration in the range of 3 -5% in the next 5 -7 years (Anaesoronye, 2026;
Orimisan, 2026). The Nigeria Insurance Industry Reform Act (NIIRA) 2025 offers a legislative
structure that promotes th e modernization and development of the insurance industry. This
focus on digitalisation, compulsory insurance policing and consumer protection opens
opportunities for increased market participation. The digitization provisions of the Act which
came with in creased regulatory vigilance is a pointer to the Government's resolve on the
transformation of the sector towards the goal of a $1 trillion economy (National Insurance
Commission (NAICOM), 2025).

6.2 Strategic Imperatives for Industry Repositioning
As insurance companies aim to stay competitive in today's digital economy, investing in digital
capabilities is more essential than ever. Insurers need to focus on going digital and shift from
incremental change to holistic transformation of their operatio ns and strategies. This
encompasses investments in cloud infrastructure, API connections, digital payment solutions,
and customer engagement platforms. Furthermore, technology collaborations between fintech
and Insurtech firms can greatly hasten capability building and market penetration (Malifete et
al., 2026). At the same time, a customer -centric product-design approach is essential, as one
that is product -oriented needs to be replaced by a customer -oriented approach focused on
unmet needs to the underser ved segment, namely the retail customers and the low -income
group. Microinsurance products, policy language and payment terms can be simplified to
increase market uptake, while product design should be consumer -receptive, using customer
research and behavi oural insights to guide product development. Rebuilding trust is also
important, and it will continue to be essential to build it with service excellence, with tangible
improvements in claims processing, customer service, and transparency. Claims management
systems and systems for inputting customer feedback can be facilitated through digital
platforms to increase the level of accountability; industry leade rs have stated that trust is
boosted where claims are managed in a timely, transparent, and seamless manner (Adebayo &
Oladipo, 2024).

7. Conclusion and Recommendations
This study has discussed the repositioning of insurance sales in Nigeria in the digital economy
era, identified the lingering problems, growing trend and future prospects. In addition to the
lack of demand, the analysis shows that key inhibitors to Nigeria 's low insurance penetration
include distribution failures, lack of trust, product complexity, and limited technological
adoption. Conventional agent-based approaches have not reached the majority of population,
especially in informal and rural areas.
But the digital economy offers transformative opportunities to the insurance industry. Mobile-
first distribution, AI -enabled customer engagement, embedded insurance, and Insurtech
platforms are proving to be effective strategies for reaching new markets and creating a better
customer experience. The regulatory Framework, especially the NIIRA 2025 and the
NAICOM's framework for Insurtech, create an enabling environment for innovatio n and
growth. Based on this analysis the following recommendations are made:

For Insurance Companies:
• Invest in digital transformation, including cloud infrastructure, API integrations and
customer engagement platforms
• Create product designs tailored to customers' needs in underserved segments such as
microinsurance and simplified policies
• Implement claims processing and build trust through technology, and through delivery of
efficient and transparent service.
• Build partnerships with fintech actors to broader outreach, via digital channel

For Regulators:
• Take forward the provisions of NIIRA 2025, including its consumer protection and
digitization requirements
• Increase the size of and expand regulatory sandboxes to support innovation with the right
level of regulation
• Improve SupTech technology to improve oversight and enforcement
• Encourage internalization and collaboration among insurers, fintechs, and other players in
the ecosystem, in order to foster innovation.

For Ecosystem Partners:
• Fintech firms should create systems that can fit into insurance systems, which would allow
for effective policy administration and claims handling.
• Technology providers need to spend more on AI uses that make customers feel better

8. Suggestion for Further Studies
Future research can expand on this study by incorporating additional geographical locations
beyond Nigeria to highlight regional differences in digital insurance penetration and
penetration results, and compare these. The study could also have a cross-sectional survey or a
longitudinal survey design to explore the evolution of digital transformation in insurance sales,
as Insurtech innovations grow and regulations are strengthened and consolidated with time. In
addition, data on customer behaviour could be added to the firm-level data for future research,
offering a more comprehensive picture of how customers take up digital insurance.
Furthermore, mediating and moderating factors like trust, financial literacy, cybersecurity
perception and socio-cultural considerations can also be incorporated in future models to fully
capture the variations of insurance sales performance in the digital economy.
