ABSTRACT

The rapid growth of online purchasing behavior and the increasing integration of digital and physical sales
channels have pushed automotive aftersales businesses to adopt omnichannel strategies that connect customers
seamlessly across touchpoints. This stu dy analyzes the My Suzuki application as an omnichannel strategy for
automotive spare parts sales in Samarinda using SWOT (Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats) and
Value Proposition Canvas (VPC) analysis. This study uses a mixed-method descriptive combining qualitative and
quantitative through the distribution of questionnaires to 30 internal and 30 external respondents. The analysis
was conducted using the Internal Factor Evaluation (IFE) and External Factor Evaluation (EFE) matrices to
determine the strategic position of the application. The results indicate that the My Suzuki application is positioned
in Quadrant 1 (growth -oriented strategy), with an internal coordinate value of 0.50 and an external coordinate
value of 0.74, reflecting strong internal capabilities and significant external  opportunities. Key strengths include
comprehensive product information, stock availability, standardized pricing, and product authenticity.
Meanwhile, the VPC analysis indicates a strong alignment between customer needs and the value proposition
offered. However, this study also identified the need for further development of the digital payment system and
service-time transparency. These findi ngs suggest that the 'My Suzuki' application has a strategically favorable
position as an omnichannel channel for automotive spare parts, and this study contributes an integrated SWOT –
VPC analytical framework for evaluating omnichannel readiness in the aut omotive aftersales sector. Practically,
the findings offer managerial direction for strengthening digital payment options and service-time communication
to improve customer experience.

INTRODUCTION

In a highly competitive business environment, companies increasingly rely on
distribution channels to deliver customer service before, during, and after a purchase, since this
service is an important component of any business that affects customer satisfac tion, loyalty,
and retention (Surahman et al., 2023) . In addition, the development of new trends and
technologies is changing the development of modern distribution channels. Samarinda City
Communication and Informatics Office (Hayati et al. , 2023) , shows data on users of
information and communication technology devices, namely 97.23% of Samarinda people
make online purchases and 100% use smartphones.

Figure 1. Data Samarinda City who is shopping online (Hayati et al., 2023)

As businesses grow, an omnichannel distribution model that integrates online and offline
sales channels becomes necessary, giving customers the option to purchase either through a
mobile device or in a physical store. Suriansha (2021) demonstrated that omnichannel
implementation successfully integrates online and offline sales channels through technology to
create an optimal customer experience. Similarly , Firdausy and Ahmadi (2025) examined
omnichannel marketing as a trend and innovation shaping consumer experience in the digital
era, showing that combining online and offline channels supported by technologies for
personalization and operational efficiency increases customer satisfac tion, loyalty, and
engagement. The development of digital technology has driven a significant transformation in
distribution and marketing strategies, particularly through an omnichannel approach that
integrates various customer interaction channels simult aneously (Verhoef et al., 2022). This
approach not only improves operational efficiency but  also plays a role in creating a more
holistic and integrated customer experience.
According to data from Badan Pusat Statistik Provinsi Kalimantan Timur  (2025) the
number of registered motorcycle users in East Kalimantan reached 3,834,286 units in 2025, of
which 1,062,755 units were registered in Samarinda city. The scale of this local and national
motorized-vehicle market is one of the factors driving Suzuki to develop the 'My Suzuki'
application as an omnichannel channel for spare parts sales. Prior omnichannel research has
largely concentrated on general retail settings, leaving the automotive aftersales sector, where
product authenticity, service scheduling, a nd spare -parts logistics create distinct customer -
experience challenges, comparatively under -examined, and recent evidence confirms that
consumer responses to omnichannel operations in the automotive aftermarket differ from

LITERATURE REVIEW

Omnichannel distribution involves offering products through multiple channels such as
physical stores, e-commerce, social media, and mobile apps with the aim of creating a seamless
shopping experience for customers regardless of which channel they choose w ith a modern
distribution channel strategy approach that integrates customers both online and offline
(Surahman et al., 2023).
In conducting data analysis, researchers used SWOT, a method introduced by Albert
Humphrey, a consultant management at the Stanford Research Institute in the 1960s which was
carried out for evaluate companies big in the United States (Oktavera, 2023). SWOT analysis
is a strategic planning approach for examining internal strengths and weaknesses together with
external opportunities and threats. A recent integrative literature review confirms that SWOT
remains a widely used and valid strategic planning tool across marketing, management, and
other applied fields (Benzaghta et al., 2021).
Then researchers complete the analysis using VPC as a visual tool developed by Alex
Osterwalder, Yves Pigneur, and Alan Smith  to help teams and organizations in design, test,
and build proposition mark in a way more effective (Asiva et al., 2025) . VPC allows more
matching systematic between need users (customer profile) and solutions offered (value map).
Taking together, these three streams of literature explain why the two analytical tools are
used jointly in this study rather than separately. Omnichannel integration is theorized to
improve customer experience mainly by removing friction across touchpoints (Verhoef et al.,
2022), but this improvement only materializes when a firm's internal capabilities and external
market conditions actually support it, which is precisely what SWOT is designed to diagnose.
SWOT, in turn, identifies strategic position but does not by itself indica te whether what a
company offers matches what customers value, a gap that the Value Proposition Canvas is
designed to close by mapping customer jobs, pains, and gains against a firm's products, pain
relievers, and gain creators. Applying SWOT and VPC toget her therefore allows this study to
assess both the strategic feasibility and the customer -experience relevance of the 'My Suzuki'
omnichannel initiative, a combination that has been more common in general retail and small-
enterprise contexts than in automotive aftersales.
Thus, this research not only provides empirical contributions, but also offers a more
comprehensive strategic analysis model in the development of customer experience -based
omnichannel systems.

RESEARCH METHOD

In this process, the researchers uses a mixed -method d esign combining a naturalistic
qualitative approach and with a quantitative approach to strengthen the analysis  (Harrison et
al., 2020) . The data obtained were then analyzed and described to facilitate reader
understanding through SWOT analysis combined with the Value Proposition Canvas approach
(Clara & Abdilla, 2025) on the 'My Suzuki' application in Samarinda.
A purposive sampling technique was used to select both respondent groups, a non -
probability method chosen to ensure that participants possessed the specific expertise,
operational involvement, or direct user experience needed to provide relevant input for the
SWOT and VPC matrices. The 30 internal respondents were active employees or management
personnel at authorized Suzuki dealerships in Samarinda (e.g., PT Samekarindo Indah)
positioned in departments directly managing the digital ecosystem, spare parts l ogistics, or
customer touchpoints (Part Counters, Digital Marketing Admins, Service Advisors, and Branch
Managers), with a minimum employment tenure of six months to ensure familiarity with
Suzuki's operational and digital transformation processes. The 30 external respondents were
residents of Samarinda who had downloaded and actively used the 'My Suzuki' application and
had completed at least one omnichannel transaction within the preceding 12 months, defined
as checking part availability or ordering throu gh the application followed by fulfillment or
installation at an authorized Suzuki dealer. Data from internal respondents were used to
construct the SWOT matrix, while data from external respondents were used to map the
Customer Profile (Jobs, Pains, and Gains) in the VPC. The questionnaire instrument was tested
for validity and reliability prior to use, with an average Likert -scale (1–5) validity score of 4,
indicating an acceptable level of item validity.

The SWOT results position the 'My Suzuki' application in Quadrant 1, indicating that the
company's internal and external conditions strongly support an aggressive, growth -oriented
strategy, while the VPC results indicate suitability between customer needs and the value
offered. This positioning suggests that the application combines internal competitive
advantages with the ability to respond to external opportunities, consistent (Puyt et al., 2023).
However, a closer reading of the weaknesses identified in Table 1 shows that despite this
strong strategic position, several important issues remain, namely limited payment methods,
dependence on fixed operating hours, and the flexibility offered by general marketplace
competitors. From an omnichannel-theory perspective, these limitations can reduce the quality
of the customer experience, which should otherwise be the application's main competitive
advantage (Shi et al., 2020).
This study has several limitations. The IFE and EFE weighting and rating procedures,
and the  VPC 'Fit' determination, rely on the research team's interpretation of interview,
observation, and questionnaire data from a relatively small, single-city sample, so the findings
should be read as an in-depth strategic assessment rather than a generalizable measurement of
application performance. Future research could validate the VPC fit assessment against actual

CONCLUSION

In line with the first research objective, the SWOT analysis places the 'My Suzuki'
application in Quadrant 1, showing that its internal strengths, comprehensive product
information, national stock and pricing, and original spare parts, align well with external
opportunities for expanding the omnichannel aftersales strategy. In line with the second
objective, the VPC analysis shows that the value offered by the application is generally aligned
with customer needs, particularly for the home -delivery and dealer -pickup service options.
Managerially, the home-delivery and nearest-dealer pickup options, together with promotional-
information features, represent practical strengths that the company can continue to leverage,
while the offline dealer channel remains a useful alternative for cu stomers without reliable
internet access. At the same time, the weaknesses identified in this study, namely the absence
of QRIS payment, unclear service-time information, and limited flexibility relative to general
marketplaces, indicate priority areas for improvement. Addressing these gaps would help the
'My Suzuki' application move toward a more integrative and adaptive omnichannel strategy
that better matches evolving digital consumer behavior, subject to the limitations noted above.
