Abstract
Artificial intelligence (AI) has become a central force reshaping how hospitality and tourism firms
communicate with customers, moving service interaction from human-only channels toward hybrid human-
AI communication systems such as chatbots, virtual conci erges and AI-personalised messaging. Grounded
in the Stimulus -Organism-Response (S -O-R) framework and customer engagement theory, this study
examines whether customer engagement mediates the relationship between AI-enabled communication and
customer experience in hospitality and tourism settings. Primary data were collected from 200 customers
who had used an AI-enabled communication channel (chatbot, virtual assistant, AI-personalised messaging,
or voice concierge) with a hotel, airline, travel agency, or tourism destination provider within the preceding
six months. All three constructs demonstrated strong internal consistency (Cronbach's alpha = .87, .81 and
.82 for AI-enabled communication, customer engagement and customer experience, respectively). Ordinary
least squares regression combined with a -sample bootstrap procedure showed that AI -enabled
communication significantly and positively predicted both c ustomer engagement (β = .439, p< .001) and
customer experience (total effect β = .469, p< .001), and t hat customer engagement significantly predicted
customer experience when controlling for AI-enabled communication (β = .271, p< .001). The bootstrapped
indirect effect (.119, 95% CI [.058, .192]) excluded zero, confirming that customer engagement partially
mediates the AI-enabled-communication-customer-experience relationship, accounting for approximately 25
per cent of the total effect. These findings extend S-O-R-based theorising in hospitality technology research
and offer managers evidence-based directi on for designing AI communication that cultivates engagement
rather than efficiency alone.

1. Introduction
The hospitality and tourism industry has historically been defined by high -contact, interpersonal
service encounters in which the quality of human interaction determined the customer's overall
experience (Lemon & Verhoef, 2016)  [1]. Over the past decade, the diffusion of artificial
intelligence (AI) technologies including natural -language chatbots, voice- activated concierge
systems, recommendation engines and sentiment -analysis-driven messaging has begun to
reconfigure the communicative architecture of guest-facing service (Buhalis & Moldavska, 2022)
[2]. Hotel brands such as Hilton, Marriott and KLM have deployed AI-based conversational agents
to manage routine enquiries, booki ng modifications and loyaltyprogramme engagement around
the clock, illustrating that AI-enabled communication is no longer an experimental add-on but an
operational pillar of contemporary guest -experience management. Industry evidence reinforces
the scale of this shift: a widely cited PwC report on the travel and hospitality sector notes that a
substantial share of firms is already investing in AI -driven technologies specifically to enhance
customer engagement, while hospitalitytechnology providers report that large hotel properties are
allocating a considerable share of IT budgets to AI-enabled guest-communication solutions.
Despite this rapid adoption, the academic literature has been slower to explain precisely how AI -
enabled communication produces  positive customer experience outcomes. Much existing work
treats customer experience as a direct outcome of AI attributes such as ease of use or
Anthropomorphism (Melian-Gonzalez et al., 2021) [3],

1.1 Research problem and objectives
Although hospitality firms are investing heavily in AI -enabled
communication, there remains limited empirical clarity on the
mechanism by which such investment converts into improved
customer experience. Accordingly, this study pursues three
objectives: (i) to empirically test the direct effect of AI-enabled
communication on customer experience; (ii) to test whether
AIenabled communication significantly predicts customer
engagement, and whether customer engagement in turn predicts
customer experience; and (iii) to test, using bootstrapped
mediation analysis, whether customer engagement mediates the
relationship between AI-enabled communication and customer
experience in hospitality and tourism settings.

2. Literature review
2.1 AI-enabled communication in hospitality and tourism
AI-enabled communication refers to the use of machine-
learning-driven conversational and content -personalisation
systems chatbots, virtual assistants, recommendation
algorithms a nd automated messaging to conduct or support
interactions between a service firm and its customers ( Buhalis,
Leung, & Baum, 2023)  [5]. In tourism and hospitality, such
systems are typically deployed across the customer journey:
pre-purchase (search and recommendation), transactional
(booking, check -in), and post -purchase (support, re -
engagement and review solicitation) touchpoints. A systematic
literature review of AI -enabled customer experience resear ch
notes that while these tools are transforming operational
capacity, the mechanisms through which AI affects customer
emotional responses, decision -making and perceived service
quality remain comparatively under-explored, underscoring the
need for theory -driven mediation research of the kind
conducted here.

2.2 Customer experience: Conceptual foundations
Customer experience (CX) is conceptualised as a
multidimensional construct encompassing customers'
cognitive, emotional, behavioural, sensorial and soci al
responses to a firm's offerings across the entire purchase
journey (Lemon & Verhoef, 2016)  [1]. Verhoef et al. (2009) [6]
similarly frame CX as arising from the totality of a customer's
interactions with a firm, spanning cognitive, affective,
emotional, social and physical dimensions  a conceptualisation
particularly relevant to AI -enabled settings, where a single
chatbot interaction can simultaneously carry functional,
emotional and social content.

2.3 Customer engagement as a relational -marketing
construct
Customer engagement (CE) is defined by Brodie et al. (2011)
[4] as a psychological state that arises through interactive, co -
creative customer experiences with a focal object (such as a
brand) within a specific service relationship, comprising
cognitive, emotional and behavioural dimensions. Unlike
satisfaction, which is primarily a post -consumption evaluative
judgment, CE is conceived as an iterative, dynamic process that
both precedes and results from repeated brand interactions
(Hollebeek, Glynn, & Brodie, 2014)  [7]. Because AI -enabled
communication tools generate frequent, low -friction
touchpoints, they are well positioned to cultivate the sustained
cognitive and emotional 'investment' that defines engagement,
which can then translate into the evaluative outc omes captured
under customer experience.

2.4 Linking AI -enabled communication, customer
engagement and customer experience
A growing stream of hospitality and tourism research models
customer engagement (or closely related constructs such as
satisfaction and trust) as a mediating mechanism between AI
stimuli and downstream customer outcomes. Using the S -O-R
framework, one large -sample study of travellers found that
satisfaction with AI chatbot use mediates the relationship
between chatbot experience attrib utes and consumer
engagement. Related work on AI-enabled social media content
in the hotel industry similarly models customer brand
engagement as an 'organism' variable arising from AI -driven
stimuli, which subsequently shapes customer satisfaction and
revisit behaviour.
A broader systematic review of 55 peer -reviewed empirical
studies on AIbased customer engagement and social -media
marketing in tourism and hospitality further confirms that AI -
driven personalisation and interactive content constitute
recurrent antecedents of engagement across the sector. This
study builds directly on that stream by empirically testing
customer engagement as the mediating variable using primary
hospitality-and-tourism customer data.

3. Conceptual framework and hypothesis development
Building on the Stimulus -Organism-Response (S -O-R)
paradigm originally proposed by Mehrabian and Russell (1974)
[8] and subsequently adapted to digital and AI-mediated service
contexts, this study models AI -enabled communication as the
environmental stimulus (S), customer engagement as the
internal organism-level response (O), and customer experience
as the resulting behavioural and evaluative outcome (R).

Fig 1: Conceptual Framework (Stimulus-Organism-Response Model)

3.1 AI -enabled communication and customer experience
(Direct effect)
AI-enabled com munication tools reduce response latency,
extend service availability beyond staffed hours, and allow
personalisation at a scale unattainable through human agents
alone, which is theorised to directly shape guests' perceived
responsiveness and personalisat ion both established
antecedents of customer experience quality.

H1: AI-enabled communication is positively associated with
customer experience in hospitality and tourism settings.

3.2 AI-enabled communication and customer engagement
Consistent with S-O-R logic, AI -enabled communication
characteristics function as external stimuli capable of activating
the cognitive and emotional investment that defines customer
engagement.

H2: AI-enabled communication is positively associated with
customer engagement in hospitality and tourism settings.

3.3 Customer engagement and customer experience
Once activated, customer engagement is theorised to enhance
customer experience by deepening perceived relational value
and psychological ownership of the service relat ionship, over
and above the direct functional benefits that AI communication
provides.

H₃: Customer engagement is positively associated with
customer experience in hospitality and tourism settings.

3.4 The mediating role of customer engagement
Integrating H 1-H3, this study's central hypothesis is that
customer engagement mediates the relationship between AI -
enabled communication and customer experience that is, part of
the effect of AI -enabled communication on customer
experience operates indirectly, through the psychological state
of engagement that AI interactions cultivate.

H₄: Customer engagement mediates the relationship between
AI-enabled communication and customer experience in
hospitality and tourism settings.

4. Research methodology
4.1 Research design and sample
This study employed a quantitative, cross -sectional survey
design. The target population comprised customers who had
interacted with at least one AI-enabled communication channel
(chatbot, virtual assistant, AI-personalised messaging, or voice
concierge) provided by a hotel, airline, travel agency, or tourism
destination organisation within the preceding six months; a
screening question confirmed eligibility before respondents
proceeded. A total of 200 valid, fully completed responses were
retained for analysis after data screening, consistent with
recommended minimum sample sizes for regression -based
mediation models with the study's three latent constructs.

4.2 Sample profile
Table and Figure 2 summarise the demographic composition of
the sample. Gender distribution was balanced (49.5% male,
49.5% female, 1.0% other). The largest age cohort was 26- 35
years (33.0%), followed by 18- 25 years (28.0%), 36- 45 years
(22.0%), 46-55 years (12.0%) and 56 years and above (5.0%).
Most respondents reported leisure travel as their primary visit
type (59.0%), followed by a mix of leisure and business (23.5%)
and business travel alone (17.5%). By industry context, the
largest share of respondents had interacted with AI
communication tools in a hotel setting (44.5%), followed by
tourism/destination organisations (22.5%), airlines (20.5%) and
travel agencies (12.5%).

Composite reliability was assessed using Cronbach's alpha.
Bivariate relationships among the th ree construct -level mean

6. Discussion
6.1 Interpretation of findings
The results provide empirical support for all four hypotheses.
AI-enabled communication was positively and significantly
related to both customer engagement and customer experience,
and customer engagement in turn was positively and
significantly related to customer experience. Critically, the
bootstrapped mediation test confirmed that customer
engagement partially mediates the rel ationship between AI -
enabled communication and customer experience, accounting
for roughly a quarter of the total effect. This indicates that AI -
enabled communication improves customer experience partly
through its direct functional benefits (speed, availa bility,
relevance) and partly by cultivating a deeper psychological state
of engagement that itself enhances the customer's evaluative
experience consistent with S -O-R theorising and with prior
findings that satisfaction or engagement -type constructs
mediate AIchatbot- to-outcome relationships in travel and
hospitality contexts.

6.2 Theoretical implications
This study contributes to hospitality and tourism marketing
theory in three ways. First, it provides empirical, bootstrapped
mediation evidence rather than purely conceptual argument for
customer engagement's mediating role in the AI -
communication-to-experience pathway, extending the S -O-R
framework's application in AI -service research. Second, the
finding of partial (rather than full) mediation clarifie s that AI's
effect on customer experience operates through both direct and
engagement-mediated channels, a nuance with meaningful
implications for how AI investment is theorised and evaluated
in service -marketing research. Third, the significant industry -
context effect on customer experience suggests that the strength
of AI's benefits may be moderated by sub-sector characteristics,
an avenue meriting further theoretical development.

6.3 Managerial implications
For hospitality and tourism managers, the resu lts suggest that
AI-enabled communication investment should be evaluated not
solely on efficiency metrics (response time, cost per contact)
but on its capacity to cultivate genuine customer engagement,
since engagement itself carries an independent, signif icant
effect on customer experience. Practically, this implies
designing AI communication with attention to personalisation
depth, tone and interactivity rather than automation volume
alone, and monitoring engagement -oriented indicators (repeat
interaction frequency, willingness to provide feedback through
AI channels) alongside traditional satisfaction scores. The
significant industry -context difference with
tourism/destination-sector respondents reporting comparatively
lower customer experience suggests that destination-marketing
organisations in particular may benefit from re -evaluating the
design and personalisation depth of their AI communication
tools relative to hotel and airline counterparts.

6.4 Comparison with prior findings
The direct and mediated pathways found here are broadly
consistent with, and extend, prior empirical findings for
instance, evidence that satisfaction with AI chatbot use
mediates the chatbot -experience-to-engagement relationship
among travellers, and evidence that AI -driven so cial-media
stimuli shape customer brand engagement and, subsequently,
satisfaction and revisit behaviour in hotel settings. This study
extends that literature by positioning engagement itself (rather
than satisfaction) as the principal mediator and by empi rically
confirming the mediation pathway using primary data across
multiple hospitality and tourism sub-sectors rather than a single
service channel.

6.5 Industry context
These findings align with broader industry trends:  A PwC
report on the travel and hospitality sector notes that a substantial
share of firms is already investing in AI -driven technologies to
enhance customer engagement, and hospitality- technology
providers report that large hotel properties allocate a
considerable share of IT budgets to AI -enabled guest -
communication solutions (Figure 5). The present study's finding
that hotel- sector respondents reported the highest customer
experience scores is broadly consistent with hotels' relatively
advanced AI adoption relative to other hospitality and tourism
sub-sectors.

7. Limitations and future research directions
This study has  several limitations that suggest avenues for
future research. First, the crosssectional design precludes strong
causal inference; longitudinal or experimental designs would
strengthen causal claims about the AI -communication-to-
engagement-to-experience pathway. Second, the sample, while
adequate for regression -based mediation analysis (N = 200),
was drawn using non -probability sampling, which may limit

generalisability; future studies should consider probability -
based sampling across a broader range of countries and cultural
contexts. Third, all constructs were measured via self -report in
a single instrument; although Harman's single -factor test
suggested common method bias was not a major concern
(37.4% variance explained by the first factor), future studi es
could strengthen this by incorporating multi -source or multi -
wave data collection. Fourth, this study measured constructs
using reflective meanscore composites rather than a full latent -
variable structural equation model; future research employing
covariance-based or partial -least-squares SEM with formal
measurement-model assessment (composite reliability, AVE,
discriminant validity) would provide a more rigorous test of the
proposed relationships. Finally, given the significant industry-
context effect o bserved here, future research should formally
test industry sub-sector as a moderator of the
AI-engagement-experience relationship, and examine
additional moderators such as generational cohort and prior AI
familiarity, consistent with disparities documented in related
chatbot-engagement literature.

8. Conclusion
This study empirically tested a conceptual model in which
customer engagement mediates the relationship between AI -
enabled communication and customer experience in hospitality
and tourism, using primary survey data from 200 customers. All
four hypotheses were supported: AI -enabled communication
significantly predicted both customer engagement and customer
experience, customer engagement significantly predicted
customer experience, and a bootstrapped mediation test
confirmed that customer engagement partially mediates the
AIenabled-communication-to-customer-experience
relationship, accounting for approximately one -quarter of the
total effect. Grounded in the Stimulus -Organism-Response
framework and c ustomer engagement theory, these findings
offer both a theoretical contribution empirically clarifying the
psychological mechanism underlying AI's effect on customer
experience and a practical one, directing managerial attention
toward engagement-oriented AI communication design. As AI-
enabled communication continues to expand across the
hospitality and tourism sector, nurturing the engagement it
generates will be central to converting technological investment
into genuinely superior customer experience.
