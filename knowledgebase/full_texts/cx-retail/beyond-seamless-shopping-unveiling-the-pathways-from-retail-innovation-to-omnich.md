Abstract
This study explores the impact of retail innovation on enhancing omnichannel customer experience and loyalty,
with particular emphasis on the mediating roles of perceived value and trust, and the moderating effects of
technological readiness and switching cost. Anchored in the Stimulus–Organism–Response (S–O–R) framework,
a conceptual model is developed and tested within the context of Indonesia’s dynamic retail sector. A cross -
sectional survey of 240 omnichannel retail consumers was conducted, and the data  were analysed using Partial
Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling. The findings reveal that retail innovation significantly enhances
perceived value, integration quality, and customer engagement. Technological readiness positively moderates these
relationships, suggesting that digitally capable consumers respond more favourably to retail innovations.
Conversely, the relationship between customer engagement and switching cost is non -significant, indicating that
emotional involvement alone may not dete r switching behaviour. Instead, functional convenience and perceived
friction appear more influential. This study extends the S–O–R model by incorporating contextual moderators and
highlights cultural nuances influencing consumer decision -making in emergin g markets. From a practical
perspective, the findings suggest that retailers should complement emotionally engaging strategies with efforts to
reduce channel-switching barriers and align innovations with consumer readiness. These insights offer valuable
guidance for building sustainable customer loyalty through locally attuned omnichannel strategies.
Keywords: retail innovation, omnichannel experience, perceived value, customer engagement, technological
readiness, switching cost
1. Introduction
The contemporary retail environment has been significantly reshaped by technological
advancements, redefining how retailers engage with consumers and manage multi -channel
operations. Retail innovation —defined as the strategic integration of technologies ac ross
various channels—has become a central driver of omnichannel experiences, fostering consumer
engagement and enhancing loyalty (Cloughton, 2020; Pantano et al., 2022). Rather than merely
implementing novel technologies, retailers now strive to offer seamless customer journeys by
integrating digital and physical platforms—an approach that Tueanrat et al. (2021) describe as
the architecture of connected touchpoints underpinning omnichannel strategy.
In Indonesia, retailers such as Uniqlo, MAP Club, and Alfamart demonstrate the practical
implementation of digital tools, including self-checkout kiosks and mobile loyalty applications,
to create unified shopping experiences. Nevertheless, despite consider able investment in

innovation enhances operational efficiency and accessibility, it does not inherently secure

Trust and perceived value have emerged as key mediating variables in this context. According
to Erjavec & Manfreda (2022), trust strengthens consumer confidence in digital channels, while
perceived value—both utilitarian and hedonic—plays a vital role in satisfaction and customer
retention (Ameen et al., 2020). The effectiveness of such innovations is also heavily influenced
by consumers' technological readiness; tech -savvy users are more inclined to embrace and
benefit from omnichannel features, whereas others may experience apprehension or resistance
(Chang & Chen, 2021; Nikhashemi et al., 2021) . Compounding this dynamic are switching
costs—psychological, procedural, and financial—which may deter consumers from trying new
channels or departing from familiar ones. High switching costs can result in passive loyalty or
even disengagement if the innovations fail to meet expectations in terms of usability and value
(Richards & Liaukonytė, 2023; Shakir Goraya et al., 2022).
This study, therefore, investigates how retail innovation shapes the omnichannel customer
experience and loyalty, with a particular emphasis on the mediating roles of trust and perceived
value, and the moderating effects of technological readiness and swit ching costs. Focusing on
Indonesia as a dynamic emerging market, this research provides context -specific insights into
the development of customer -centric omnichannel strategies aimed at fostering sustainable
loyalty. Although numerous studies have explore d the contributions of retail innovation and
omnichannel strategies to consumer outcomes, several critical gaps remain. First, existing
literature often examines individual aspects—such as technological implementation or channel
integration—in isolation, w ithout fully accounting for their interactions with consumer
perceptions of value and trust (Anshu et al., 2022; Erjavec & Manfreda, 2022) . Second, most
empirical studies are rooted in developed markets, leaving the unique characteristics of
emerging economies like Indonesia under-investigated (Alexander & Kent, 2022).
Furthermore, research seldom incorporates moderating variables such as technological
readiness and switching costs in models connecting retail innovation to customer loyalty.
Despite growing recognition of their importance in shaping consumer behaviour and  channel
adoption, these factors remain under-theorised in the omnichannel literature (Nikhashemi et al.,
2021; Richards & Liaukonytė, 2023) . This limits both theoretical development and practical
application in designing responsive omnichannel strategies. To address these gaps, this study
proposes an integrative framework that examines the influence of retail innovation on
omnichannel custome r experience and loyalty, mediated by trust and perceived value, and
moderated by technological readiness and switching costs. By grounding the research in the
Indonesian context, the study aims to contribute meaningful insights for both scholars and
practitioners navigating the evolving omnichannel retail landscape.

2. Literature Review
2.1 Stimulus–Organism–Response (S–O–R) theory
The Stimulus–Organism–Response (S–O–R) theory offers a robust framework for examining
the impact of retail innovation on customer engagement and loyalty within omnichannel
contexts. Retail innovation serves as the stimulus —comprising technological advancem ent,
cross-channel integration, and novel service strategies —which influences consumers’ internal
evaluations and behaviours. As Lim et al. (2022) suggest, different retail formats may moderate
this interaction, emphasising the need to tailor innovation to specific consumer environments.
The organism component reflects internal cognitive and emotional responses. Here, perceived
value is pivotal, as consumers assess the trade -off between the benefits and costs of retail
innovations. Higher perceived value enhances engagement and strengthens loyalty (Alexander
& Kent, 2022). Technological readiness further moderates this process; consumers with higher
readiness are more receptive to innovations, enhancing the perceived shopping experience (Gao
& Huang, 2021).
Within the response phase, channel switching costs act as a boundary condition. High switching
costs may deter engagement with new innovations, potentially weakening loyalty gains
(Nguyen et al., 2022) . Finally, favourable perceptions and readiness lead to increased
engagement and long-term brand loyalty. In sum, the S –O–R theory contextualises how retail
innovation translates into consumer engagement and loyalty, highlighting the mediating and
moderating roles of value, readiness, and switching costs.
2.2 Retail Innovation (RI)
Retail innovation (RI) has become pivotal in shaping consumer perceptions and experiences in
contemporary retail environments. It encompasses the application of advanced technologies—
such as artificial intelligence, self-service kiosks, and digital loyalty programmes—to enhance
the customer journey and increase perceived value (Cloughton, 2020; Pantano et al., 2022) .
Effective RI strategies enrich both functional and emotional dimensions of value, thereby
improving customer satisfaction and engagement (Youssef et al., 2022) . Within Indonesia’s
fast-evolving retail landscape, RI serves as a strategic tool for enhancing the omnichannel
customer experience. Additionally, it supports real-time personalisation, agile service delivery,
and responsiveness to rapidly shifting consumer expectations (Grewal et al., 2021).
Furthermore, RI contributes to deeper levels of customer engagement by enabling personalised
and interactive retail platforms (Alexander & Kent, 2022; Ameen et al., 2020). When deployed
effectively, innovation aligns with evolving customer needs, cultivating emotional attachment
and encouraging participatory behaviours (Pilawa et al., 2022) . Innovations that improve
convenience, entertainment, and control further enhance the cognitive, emotional, and
behavioural facets of customer engagement (Gao & Huang, 2021).

2.3 Omnichannel Integration Quality (OIQ)
Omnichannel integration quality refers to the degree of coherence and synchronisation across
retail touchpoints that enables a seamless, consistent shopping experience. High OIQ increases
customer empowerment and reduces friction, thereby improving perceiv ed value (Mencarelli
et al., 2021; Quach et al., 2022) . It also facilitates smoother transitions between channels and
ensures consistent brand interactions, which enhance overall satisfaction (Alexander & Kent,
2022). Moreover, effective integration enhances consumers’ sense of control and mitigates
uncertainty, promoting more favourable evaluations of service quality and perceived value
(Verhoef et al., 2023).
Superior omnichannel experiences additionally drive customer engagement by offering
seamless, relevant, and contextually rich interactions across online and offline environments
(Gao & Huang, 2021; Zhang et al., 2024) . When service processes are integrated, content is
consistent, and functionalities are reciprocal across channels, customers are more inclined to
build lasting relationships with the retailer (Erjavec & Manfreda, 2022).
H3: Omnichannel Customer Experience has a positive effect on Perceived Value.
H4: Omnichannel Customer Experience has a positive effect on Customer Engagement.
2.4 Perceived Value (PV) and Customer Engagement (CE)
Perceived value (PV) represents the consumer’s evaluation of the benefits received relative to
the costs incurred in a retail interaction. It incorporates both utilitarian and hedonic aspects,
influenced by the overall shopping experience (Mencarelli et al., 2021; Molinillo et al., 2021).
Higher PV has been associated with increased consumer engagement, manifested through
stronger emotional connections and proactive behavioural involvement (Hollebeek et al., 2020).
Empirical studies affirm that when consumers perceive value as fair and rewarding, they
develop deeper psychological commitment and display participative behaviours (Lin et al.,
2022).
H5: Perceived Value has a positive effect on Customer Engagement.
2.5 Technological Readiness (TR)
Technological readiness (TR) reflects an individual’s tendency to adopt and effectively use
technological tools. It plays a moderating role in the relationship between retail innovation and
omnichannel experience (Ameen et al., 2020; Cloughton, 2020). Consumers with higher TR are
better positioned to appreciate the benefits of technology -enabled services, thereby
experiencing heightened satisfaction and engagement (Pantano et al., 2022). Those with strong
technological self -efficacy tend to find innovative services more accessible and enjoyable,
which further motivates their engagement with omnichannel offerings (Chang & Chen, 2021).

2.6 Channel Switching Cost (CSC)
Channel Switching Cost (CSC) encompasses the perceived monetary, psychological, and
procedural costs associated with moving between different retail channels. High CSC can
reduce customers' willingness to engage with new channels, even when they hold posit ive
perceptions of the retailer's offerings (Richards & Liaukonytė, 2023) . CSC moderates the
relationships between perceived value and customer engagement, as well as between
omnichannel experience and engagement. Increased switching friction may inhibit active
consumer engagement (Tueanrat et al., 2021). Nonetheless, when perceived value is high, CSC
can act as a constraint -based loyalty mechanism that sustains customer commitment despite
barriers (Adapa et al., 2020).

innovation on omnichannel customer experience and loyalty. It further investigates the
mediating roles of perceived value and trust, as well as the moderating effects of technological
readiness and switching cost. A cross -sectional survey approach was employed, utilising a

3.2 Sample and Data Collection
The target population of this study consists of consumers with prior experience in omnichannel
shopping, specifically those who have interacted with both online and offline retail platforms.
A non -probability purposive sampling technique was adopted to ens ure the selection of
respondents possessing relevant digital and physical retail exposure. Eligibility criteria included
individuals aged 18 and above, residing in urban areas of Indonesia, and having completed
purchases through both online and offline channels within the preceding six months.
Data collection was conducted using a structured online questionnaire distributed via social
media platforms and consumer communities between January and March 2025. A total of 240
valid responses were obtained. This sample size exceeds the recommended threshold for Partial
Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling (PLS -SEM), which is well -suited for analysing
models with complex mediation and moderation relationships (Hult et al., 2021).
As shown in Table 1, the sample predominantly comprised female respondents (54.17%), with
the 26–35 age group representing 50%. In terms of occupation, 58.33% were professionals,
while 41.67% were students. Most respondents held a bachelor’s degree (58.33%), and monthly
spending was largely concentrated between IDR 1–3 million (50%). Overall, the demographic
profile reflects digitally literate and economically active consumers, well -aligned with the
objectives of this omnichannel retail study.

3.3 Measurement of Constructs
All constructs in this study were measured using multi -item scales adapted from previously
validated research, with each item assessed on a five -point Likert scale (1 = strongly disagree
to 5 = strongly agree). Retail Innovation (RI) was measured using four items capturing aspects
of technological adoption, process innovation, and customer interaction strategies (Alexander
& Kent, 2022; Cui et al., 2022; Youssef et al., 2022) . Omnichannel Integration Quality (OIQ)
comprised five items assessing perceptions of consistency, channel alignment, and seamless
service delivery across platforms (Gao & Huang, 2021; Prassida & Hsu, 2022; Quach et al.,
2022). Perceived Value (PV) was operationalised through three items reflecting both utilitarian
and hedonic benefits derived from retail innovations (Adapa et al., 2020; Cloughton, 2020;
Dastane et al., 2020) . Customer Engagement (CE) was measured using four items covering
cognitive, emotional, and behavioural dimensions of consumer involvement (Algharabat et al.,
2020; Meyer‐Waarden et al., 2023; Pangarkar et al., 2022). Technological Readiness (TR) was
assessed using four items representing consumers’ confidence, optimism, and willingness to
adopt new retail technologies (Ameen et al., 2020; Nikhashemi et al., 2021) . Lastly, Channel
Switching Cost (CSC) was measured with four items addressing perceived effort, time
investment, psychological discomfort, and financial burden associated with switching between
retail channels (Mencarelli et al., 2021; Nguyen et al., 2022).
3.4 Data Analysis Method
This study employed Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling (PLS -SEM) as the
primary analytical technique due to its appropriateness for examining complex models
involving both mediation and moderation, as well as its robustness in handling non-normal data
and moderate sample sizes (Hair et al., 2024) . Data analysis was conducted using SmartPLS
4.0, which supports reflective measurement models. A two -step analytical procedure was
applied: first, the measurement model was evaluated through assessments of indicator
reliability, internal consistency, convergent validity (A VE), and discriminant validity (Fornell–
Larcker and HTMT); second, the structural model was assessed via path coefficients, R², f², Q²,
and significance using 5,000 bootstrap resamples (Hair et al., 2019). Moderation effects of
technological readiness and switching cost were tested using product indicators, while
mediation effects of perceived value and trust were examined via indirect effect procedures
following current PLS-SEM guidelines.

reliability and robustness.

Table 6 presents the analysis of specific indirect effects, examining the mediating roles of
various constructs within the structural model. While several indirect paths were statistically
non-significant (p > 0.05), a number of key mediation effects emerg ed. Notably, the pathway
from Retail Innovation (RI) → Omnichannel Integration Quality (OIQ) → Perceived Value
(PV) → Customer Engagement (CE) was significant (β = 0.482, t = 6.297, p = 0.000), providing
strong support for a cascading mediational influence of RI on CE through OIQ and PV .
Another important finding is the significant indirect effect from Technological Readiness (TR)
→ RI → OIQ → PV (β = 0.351, p = 0.000), as well as the extended path via OIQ → PV → CE
(β = 0.230, p = 0.001), indicating that TR impacts CE through a chain of m ediating variables.
The full mediation pathway —TR → RI → OIQ → PV → CE —also proved significant ( β =
0.314, p = 0.000), highlighting a multi-layered influence.

Conversely, most indirect effects involving Channel Switching Cost (CSC) were non -
significant, suggesting limited mediating influence on switching behaviour. Overall, these
findings underscore the importance of mediated relationships in the formation of cu stomer
engagement and perceived value.
The findings of this study broadly validate the hypothesised  relationships, reinforcing the
conceptual framework based on the Stimulus –Organism–Response (S –O–R) model. Retail
Innovation (RI) emerged as a key stimulus, exerting significant influence on Omnichannel
Integration Quality (OIQ), Perceived Value (PV), and  Customer Engagement (CE),
underscoring its role in enhancing customer experience. Similarly, Technological Readiness
(TR) demonstrated meaningful moderating effects, particularly in relation to OIQ and CE,
consistent with prior research indicating that te chnology adoption is heavily shaped by
consumers’ readiness (Gao & Huang, 2021).
However, the non -significant relationship between CE and Channel Switching Cost (CSC)
offers an important insight. Contrary to theoretical expectations, emotional engagement with a
brand may not suffice to deter switching behaviour. In the Indonesian context, this finding can
be interpreted through a pragmatic lens: customer loyalty often hinges more on convenience,
trust, and service reliability than on emotional affinity (Alexander & Kent, 2022; Nguyen et al.,
2022). Given Indonesia’s dynamic and fragmented digital ecosystem, consumers may maintain
brand loyalty while remaining willing to switch platforms when confronted with inefficiencies
or friction.
This nuanced perspective enriches the literature by illustrating that high engagement does not
necessarily equate to lower switching tendencies. Indonesian retailers must therefore look
beyond emotional appeal, focusing instead on seamless service integrat ion, reliable channel
performance, and ease of use. These findings further emphasise the importance of incorporating
technological readiness into digital strategy development. For practitioners, the results provide
actionable insights into tailoring omnich annel strategies to reflect both psychological drivers
and local market conditions in emerging economies.
5. Conclusion
This study confirms the pivotal role of retail innovation in shaping omnichannel experiences,
perceived value, and customer engagement, supported by the S –O–R theoretical framework.
While most relationships were significant, the unexpected non -significance between
engagement and switching cost highlights a nuanced behavioural insight: emotional connection
alone may not deter consumers from changing channels, particularly in Indonesia’s pragmatic
and convenience -driven market. These findings enrich current u nderstanding by integrating
structural outcomes, theoretical alignment, and cultural context. However, the study is limited
by its cross -sectional design and reliance on self -reported data, which may not fully capture
longitudinal behavioural shifts. Futur e research should consider experimental or longitudinal
approaches, explore industry-specific differences, and incorporate additional cultural variables.
These directions will advance theoretical precision and provide deeper insights into customer
dynamics within evolving omnichannel ecosystems.

The findings offer several actionable insights for retail practitioners operating in omnichannel
environments. First, investment in retail innovation should prioritise technologies that not only
enhance operational efficiency but also create tangible value  for customers, such as
personalised interfaces, unified service experiences, and seamless channel integration. Second,
firms must address technological readiness by educating and supporting consumers through
intuitive digital features and onboarding support. Third, given that engagement alone does not
prevent switching, retailers should reduce friction by simplifying channel transitions and
ensuring consistent service quality. Lastly, omnichannel strategies must reflect local market
dynamics—particularly i n Indonesia —by combining emotional appeal with functional
reliability. Retailers who align innovation with customer expectations and cultural behaviour
are more likely to foster lasting loyalty and sustained engagement in a competitive retail
landscape.
