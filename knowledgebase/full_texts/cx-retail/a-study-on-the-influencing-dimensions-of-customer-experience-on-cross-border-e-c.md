Abstract
Against the backdrop of the rapid development of cross -border e-commerce, platform service
quality and consumer experience have become key factors affecting transaction trust. This study
adopts the perspective of “e -commerce politeness” and takes the outdo or products category on
the DHgate platform as the research object. Using the critical incident technique (CIT) to analyze
user reviews, it systematically identifies satisfactory and unsatisfactory incidents that influence
perceptions of e-commerce politeness. A total of 501 valid user reviews were collected, including
253 satisfactory incidents and 248 unsatisfactory incidents. The study finds that product quality,
logistics service, and seller description consistency are the core dimensions affecting perceptions
of e -commerce politeness: in satisfactory incidents, product quality accounts for the largest
proportion, followed by logistics service; in unsatisfactory incidents, product quality also ranks
first, followed by seller description consistency and l ogistics service. Customer service is
frequently mentioned after problems occur, and the absence or unreasonableness of compensation
mechanisms can exacerbate negative experiences. Based on these findings, the study offers
recommendations at the platform, seller, and consumer levels, providing empirical evidence and
practical references for cross -border e -commerce platforms to optimize user experience and
reduce perceptions of “platform impoliteness.”

Keywords: Cross -Border E -Commerce Platform; DHgate; E -Commerce Politeness; Outdoor
Products; Critical Incident Technique (CIT).

1. Introduction
In the context of the rapid development of the global digital economy, cross -border e-commerce
has become an important force driving the growth of international trade. As a cross-border B2B e-
commerce platform, DHgate has attracted numerous small and mediu m-sized buyers and end
consumers worldwide with its rich product categories and broad overseas market coverage. Among
many product categories, outdoor products have gradually become an important growth segment
in cross-border transactions due to their strong functionality, clear usage scenarios, and relatively
high brand premium. Kuang et al. (2022) provide data support for this trend, indicating that among
many export categories, outdoor products rank third with a share of 7.6%. However, the inherent
characteristics of cross-border e-commerce transactions—cross-regional, cross-cultural, and non-
face-to-face—create significant information asymmetry for consumers during the purchasing

2. Literature Review
2.1 Development and Current Status of Cross-Border E-Commerce Platforms
As digital intermediaries connecting sellers and consumers across countries, cross -border e -
commerce platforms have experienced rapid development in recent years. Liu et al. (2021) point
out that while China’s cross-border e-commerce is growing steadily, it still faces challenges such
as low customs clearance efficiency, complex regulation, and payment risks; Hazarika and
Mousavi (2021) also emphasize that research in this field remains fragmented. Derindağ (2022)
further notes that cross-border e-commerce needs to provide an “engaging user experience.” These
macro-level analyses indicate that whether specific service links in platform operations run
smoothly directly affects users’ overall experience.
At the consumer level, the characteristics of cross -border e -commerce—cross-regional, cross -
cultural, and non-face-to-face—make information asymmetry particularly prominent. Valarezo et
al. (2018) found that the frequency with which consumers check other users’ reviews before cross-
border online shopping positively influences their participation, and that internet trust is a key
driving factor. Huang and Chang (2019) confirmed that consumers’ trust in foreign website sellers
depends on site policies and rep utation, while price competitiveness, communication costs, and
waiting costs determine perceived value. Chen et al. (2022) further showed from the perspective
of interfirm cooperation that corporate reputation significantly affects interfirm trust. Togethe r,
these studies reveal that reputation, trust, and online reviews are core elements for reducing
consumers’ perceived risk and facilitating transactions, which aligns closely with the connotations
of E-Commerce Politeness—such as reliable responses and respectful treatment.
Cross-border e-commerce logistics, as a key link affecting customer experience, has also received
attention. Giuffrida et al. (2017) noted that research on logistics and e -commerce remains largely
descriptive and lacks in-depth exploration of complex issues; Giuffrida et al. (2021) further found
that firms’ logistics risk management strategies depend on the types of uncertainty they face. Chen

2.2 Outdoor Products
Outdoor products, due to their strong functionality and clear usage scenarios, prompt consumers
to pay far more attention to details such as material and sizing than they do for ordinary daily
necessities. Yoo (2013), in an empirical study on mountaineerin g equipment, found that wearing
comfort includes multiple dimensions such as mobility/pressure and moisture management, and
that consumers’ expectations and satisfaction regarding comfort are both relatively high. Sim et al.
(2012) further pointed out that  growth in the outdoor sports apparel market stems not only from
professional mountaineering enthusiasts but also from ordinary consumers wearing such items as
everyday outerwear, which drives continuous iteration in product technology and design. These
characteristics mean that when outdoor products are traded online via cross -border e-commerce
platforms, consumers—unable to directly experience the products —become significantly more
sensitive to whether the physical item matches its description. Tseng et a l. (2021), in research on
counterfeit outdoor products, corroborated this from the negative side: consumers’ perceived risk
negatively affects their purchase attitudes and intentions, indicating that authenticity issues and
description deviations can severely undermine consumers’ transaction confidence.
In online shopping environments, purchasing decisions for outdoor products are driven by multiple
psychological factors. Chiu et al. (2018) found that attitude, subjective norms, and both positive
and negative anticipated emotions significantly influence c onsumers’ desire to purchase sports
goods online. For Chinese consumers, Chiu and Choi (2018) likewise confirmed that attitude,
subjective norms, perceived behavioral control, and positive anticipated emotions significantly
affect purchase intention. These  studies indicate that when buying outdoor products online,
consumers’ decisions are based not only on product information but also on emotional experiences
and social norms. Moreover, Mao (2021), through text -mining analysis of nearly 28,000 online
reviews of sports goods, found that extremely positive or negative reviews concentrate more on
customer service, while more moderate reviews focus on the product itself. This finding suggests
that when experiences deviate sharply from expectations, the performance of customer service will
significantly amplify consumers’ perceptions of e-commerce politeness.
The above literature collectively shows that outdoor products in cross -border e -commerce
transactions exhibit typical features of high information asymmetry, high perceived risk, and multi-
stage experience dependence. From browsing product descriptions to after-sales rights protection,
any deviation in the experience at each interaction link may be magnified into an overall negative
evaluation of the platform. Therefore, using outdoor products as a research entry point helps
systematically identify which specific interaction links on cross-border e-commerce platforms are
prone to triggering users’ perceptions of “platform impoliteness,” thereby providing precise
evidence for optimizing platform service processes.

customer complaints in e -commerce service interactions and found that co mplaints often stem
from consumers’ perceptions of moral violations. This finding echoes Fang et al. (2011), who
integrated the IS success model with justice theory and demonstrated that distributive, procedural,
and interactional justice significantly aff ect online trust and satisfaction. Kim et al. (2024), from
the perspective of service recovery, found that merchants’ apologies for negative reviews have a
more significant effect on perceived justice than mere compensation, thereby influencing trust and
continued use intention. These studies combine the politeness framework with justice theory,
enriching the connotation of e -commerce politeness—namely, that politeness not only includes
positive service behaviors but also entails avoiding moral boundary-crossing behaviors that would
be perceived as “impolite.”
In summary, existing research has explored e-commerce politeness from multiple angles, but there
is relatively little work addressing cross -border e-commerce B2B contexts, and few studies use
outdoor products—a category with high information sensitivity —as a case. Therefore, this study
will focus on outdoor products on the DHgate platform to systematically identify which interaction
links in the user experience constitute perceptions of E-Commerce Politeness.

3. Research Methods
3.1 Critical Incident Technique
The critical incident technique (CIT) was formally proposed by Flanagan (1954). By collecting
and analyzing concrete behavioral facts that have significant positive or negative effects, CIT
provides a basis for solving practical problems. The method was su bsequently widely introduced
into the service research field: Gremler (2004) conducted a systematic review of 141 service
studies and confirmed that CIT has unique advantages in identifying satisfactory and unsatisfactory
incidents in service encounters. On this basis, Edvardsson and Roos (2001) further developed the
“criticality-critical incident technique” (CCIT), emphasizing that events should be understood
from the dynamic perspective of individual customers. In e-commerce and digital service contexts,
CIT has also shown good applicability: Ingaldi and Brožová (2020) used CIT to assess the service
quality of an online store and found that positive incidents accounted for less than 50%; Salo and

3.2 Research Design
This study employs CIT and takes DHgate outdoor products as the research object. By analyzing
user reviews, it identifies critical incidents that affect e-commerce politeness experience. The data
come from publicly available user reviews on the DHgate platform, yielding 501 valid user reviews,
of which 253 are satisfactory incidents and 248 are unsatisfactory incidents. Three coders
independently classified the incidents according to the coding framework, and the core dimensions
affecting users’ e -commerce politeness experience were identified based on the classification
results.

4. Data Analysis
4.1 Data Collection
The data for this study were drawn from DHgate’s outdoor products, and all data are publicly
accessible user reviews on the platform. To ensure ethical compliance, all collected data were
anonymized to remove any information that could identify individual users. For sample selection,
a purposive sampling strategy was used to ensure that the data possess both market
representativeness and content depth. The specific selection criteria and procedures are as follows:
1) Using “sales ranking” as the criterion, top-selling products were selected. These products have
a large user base and review volume, and their feedback best represents mainstream market choices
and general consumer opinions, ensuring market -level representativeness of the sample. 2) The
time range for review collection was limited to February–April 2025, a period of platform stability,
to ensure the timeliness and dynamism of the data. 3) From each selected product, 10–15 reviews
were purposefully chosen. Selection balanced satisfactory reviews ( 5-star) and unsatisfactory
reviews (1 -star), and prioritized reviews with detailed content that included specific usage
scenarios or detailed descriptions to ensure depth and richness of data quality.
Through the above process, this study ultimately constructed a qualitative analysis dataset
containing 501 user reviews. Due to the dataset’s focused source (single platform and vertical
category), continuous time span, sales -based representativeness, and high information density in
review content, it is suitable for exploratory thematic mining and provides a solid empirical basis
for an in-depth understanding of consumer experience in this market.

4.3 Reliability and Validity Testing
To ensure the scientific rigor and reliability of the classification results, this study conducted tests
from both reliability and validity perspectives. For reliability, the stability of the classification
results was assessed by calculating individual coder consistency and inter-coder consistency, using
the formula proposed by Holsti (1969). For validity, verification was carried out across three
dimensions: expert validity, content validity, and face validity. The specific testing procedures are
described below.

4.3.1 Personal Classification Consistency

the same coder at different times or when classifying the same set of incidents. This study invited
three individuals with relevant professional background s to participate in the coding work: a
university instructor in e -commerce, a cross -border platform operations practitioner, and an
experienced cross -border e -commerce buyer. The three coders independently classified the
satisfactory and unsatisfactory inc idents according to a unified coding framework. To reduce
memory effects and test the stability of the coding results, two independent coding rounds were
conducted with a 14-day interval between them. Each coder’s personal classification consistency
was ca lculated by measuring the match between their initial coding results and their

4.3.3 Validity Analysis
Validity is an important indicator for measuring the accuracy of research instruments or
classification systems. This study examined validity from three aspects: expert validity, content
validity, and face validity.
Expert validity refers to judgments by domain experts on the representativeness and
appropriateness of the research instrument or classification system. Haynes et al. (1995) noted that
establishing content validity typically relies on systematic judgments by field experts. This study
invited three experts to independently review the definitions of classification dimensions and the
reasonableness of event categorization, and revised the coding framework based on expert
feedback to ensure high expert validity.
Content validity refers to whether the measurement tool comprehensively covers the core
dimensions of the target concept. Haynes et al. (1995) emphasized evaluation from the
perspectives of item relevance, representativeness, and clarity. The coding framework in this study
was developed from a systematic collation of DHgate outdoor product users’ satisfactory and
unsatisfactory incidents, referenced service quality dimensions in existing literature, and was
validated through coder consistency checks to ensu re that the classification dimensions
comprehensively reflect consumers’ perceptions of e -commerce politeness on cross -border
e-commerce platforms, thereby ensuring content validity.
Face validity refers to whether, from the perspective of research participants, the measurement tool
“appears” to measure what it claims to measure. Thomas et al. (1992) pointed out that face validity
affects participant acceptance and data quality. This study used publicly accessible user reviews as
data—direct feedback from users—and employed intuitive category labels with concrete examples
to demonstrate that the classification terms are easy to understand and that event categorization
matches actual perceptions, thereby ensuring face validity.
In summary, through expert review, coder consistency testing, and user feedback, this study
established the three types of validity, providing assurance for the scientific rigor of the data
analysis.

5. Conclusions and Recommendations
5.1 Conclusions
The study results indicate that in satisfactory incidents, product quality is the primary driver of
users’ positive experiences, followed by logistics service; in unsatisfactory incidents, product
quality likewise accounts for the largest share, followed b y seller description consistency and
logistics service. This finding reveals the dual nature of e-commerce politeness: on one hand, high
product quality and efficient logistics services can significantly enhance users’ positive perceptions;
on the other ha nd, poor quality, mismatched descriptions, and logistics problems are the main
sources that trigger users’ perceptions of “platform impoliteness.”
It is noteworthy that customer service ranks fourth among satisfactory incidents and last among
unsatisfactory incidents, but this does not imply it is unimportant. Combined with analysis of
specific incidents, customer service is often emphasized by users only after problems occur—when
product quality or logistics deviate seriously from expectations, the attitude and problem -solving
ability of customer service significantly affect users’ overall evaluation of the platform. In addition,
the compensation mec hanism, as an independent dimension in unsatisfactory incidents, reflects
users’ expectations for fair compensation after suffering losses; its absence or unreasonableness
directly exacerbates negative experiences.
In summary, this study confirms the multidimensional composition of e -commerce politeness in
cross-border B2B e -commerce platforms, with product quality, description consistency, and
logistics service as core influencing dimensions. The findings provide em pirical support for
DHgate and similar platforms to optimize user experience and reduce perceptions of “platform
impoliteness,” suggesting priority actions in product quality control, standardization of
information descriptions, and reliability of logistics services, supplemented by efficient customer
service and fair compensation mechanisms.

5.2 Recommendations
Based on the above conclusions, this study offers the following recommendations for cross-border
e-commerce platforms at the platform, seller, and consumer levels.

5.2.1 Recommendations for Platforms
Cross-border e -commerce platforms should strengthen routine sampling inspections and credit
rating systems for merchants’ product quality, incorporate “description consistency” into merchant
assessment indicators, and demote or delist merchants with freque nt “item not as described”
complaints. At the same time, platforms need to optimize logistics service monitoring systems,
integrate data from mainstream logistics providers, proactively intervene in cases of abnormal
delays or lost packages, and provide au tomated compensation channels to reduce users’ negative
experiences in the logistics link. In addition, platforms should establish standardized customer
service response and dispute resolution procedures to ensure users receive timely and respectful
responses when problems occur.

5.2.2 Recommendations for Sellers
Sellers should present product information truthfully and in detail, especially for outdoor products
where material, sizing, and functional details are prone to perception bias; they should avoid
exaggerated claims. Sellers should also establish proactive after-sales communication mechanisms,
apologize promptly when users express dissatisfaction, and offer reasonable remedial measures
(such as partial refunds or exchanges) rather than shirking responsibility or issuing mechanical
replies. For negative revie ws, sellers should respond publicly with a sincere attitude, placing
“apology” before “compensation” to help restore user trust.

5.2.3 Recommendations for Consumers
Before purchasing, consumers should carefully read product descriptions and other users’ genuine
reviews, paying particular attention to specific problem descriptions in neutral and negative
reviews to reduce risks from information asymmetry. When encounte ring poor experiences,
consumers should actively submit detailed problem descriptions and evidence through the
platform’s formal channels; this not only helps protect their own rights but also provides valuable
feedback data for platforms and sellers to improve services.
