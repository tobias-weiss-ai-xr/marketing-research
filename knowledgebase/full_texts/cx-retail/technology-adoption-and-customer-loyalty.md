Abstract
This research examined how technology advancement is associated with the customer loyalty of
the Tier -1 commercial banks in Rivers State, Nigeria, by using a multidimensional measure
involving ease-of-use, performance expectancy and perceived quality. The study assumed
positivist philosophical paradigm, cross -sectional research design, and primary data was
collected using a sample size of 362 customers in the six Tier -1 banks. The results, whic h were
discussed with the help of the Structural Equation Modell ing (SEM) and AMOS version 24,
demonstrated that the positive correlation between technology adoption and the customer loyalty
was significant. The research also extended the service -dominant logic, diffusion of innovation
theory, the unified theory of acc eptance and use of technology, and the technology acceptance
model through demonstrating through empirical evidence the influential role played by various
stages of technological adoption in terms of ease of use, performance expectancy and perceived
quality, and also, created the importance of initial adoption stage of technology and, in the same
way, facilitating customer retention and emotional attachment in the services of technology
enablement. In addition, the research offered an effective practical in formation to managers of
commercial banks in Rivers State, Nigeria; and made a conclusion that adoption of technology
had improved the customer loyalty of Tier -1 commercial banks, where the customer experience
was enhanced. It was suggested to Simplify the  technology interfaces to make them available to
more people, develop specific and anticipative service functions, encourage consistency in service
quality and reliability across all streams, adopt proactive feedback and experience monitoring
platforms, and leverage emotional branding and encourage relationship marketing.

Keywords: Innovation, Feedback, Affective, Loyalty, Customer

2.1.3  Customer Loyalty
Customer loyalty constitutes a firm sense of commitment to buy or patronise a favourite service
or product in the future regardless of situation related factors and marketing campaigns in order to
switch (Oliver, 1999). It is the robustness of the interpretation between the mentality of a customer
on a retailer/brand and their recurrent buying habit. Broken down, it focuses on the customer
experience (attitude) and the customer action (behaviour) in the long term (Wallstrom, Lidholm
and Sundstrom, 2023). It  is defined as an attitudinal and behavioural commitment of a customer
to a bank (or another service provider), whereby, the customer demonstrates a high, persistent
intention or preference to continue utilising the banks services (attitudinal loyalty), and
demonstrates an repeated use of the services (behavioural loyalty) in the face of attractive
substitutes or marketing campaigns to switch. The long -term sustainability and success of
commercial banks would be impossible without customer loya lty, which implies the readiness of
customers to do business with a given bank in the long run (Przo et al., 2021). Service quality
affects customer loyalty a lot and satisfaction in its turn has a significant impact on customer
loyalty (Pham and Vy, 2016). When custom ers are satisfied, they will be more likely to continue
doing business with the bank and make additional purchases, as well as recommend the bank to
other people (Dagasaner & Karaatmaca, 2025). The quality of the service that the bank provides
can make it stand out and gain a competitive advantage; this is necessary to improve the quality of
customer experience and customer loyalty (Khan and Fasih, 2014). The provision of quality
service is vital in a situation where the banks need to set themselves apart in the market, as well as
customers (Bhat, 2005). It is possible to improve customer service by banks using technology,
maintaining high standards of service delivery, and redesigning business processes (K.T., 2021).
Banks can affirm their role in the market by integrating the effectiveness of operations with a better
customer experience (Alam et al., 2025).

2.2. Theoretical Framework
2.2.1 Technology Acceptance Model (TAM)
Technology Acceptance Model was developed by Davis (1986) and improved by Bagozzi and
Warshaw (1989) which came to be one of the most significant models to explain individual
acceptance and use of technology. Primarily, TAM assumes that perceived usefulnes s and
perceived ease of use are the baseline factors of behavioural intention that subsequently forecast
actual use of a system. Simply put, persons tend to embrace and utilise a technology once they
view it as helpful to them in terms of enhancing perform ance and be easy to use. It is this
conceptualization that has rendered TAM a broadly -use theoretical framework in any industry,
especially in the service sector like banking where technological use is at the heart of any customer
interaction (Azeez et al., 2025; Putra et al., 2019).
To elaborate on this theoretical basis, recent research findings have proved the applicability of
TAM in the banking industry especially in connexion with digital banking services provision. As
an example, Kaulu et al. (2024) demonstrated that customer -related issues with cybercrime and
security are key determinants of the use of electronic banking channels. They do not find that
perceived usefulness or ease of use has lost its significance: they find that there are other constructs
including security perce ptions that increase the explanatory power of TAM in the salient risk
context. Likewise, Naeem et al. (2023) based on a systematic review of TAM adaptations in
banking found that contextual variables, including trust, perceived risk, and regulatory
environment, are needed to introduce more resolute explanation of adoption behaviour. These
results underline the necessity to generalise TAM to include more variables related to sectors
dynamics.

2.2.2 Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT)
Venkatesh, Morris, Davis, and Davis (2003) propounded the Unified Theory of Acceptance and
Use of Technology (UTAUT). It combines the aspects of eight other previous models including
TAM, the Theory of Planned Behaviour or the Diffusion of Innovation Theor y in the attempt to
introduce a more holistic explanation of adoption of technology. UTAUT cited four determinants,
namely performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence and facilitating conditions as
predictors of technology acceptance and behaviour of usage. This model has been extensively used
and also expanded to UTAUT2 (Venkatesh, Thong, and Xu, 2012), which uses consumer use
contexts.
As presented by Rahi and Ghani (2019), the UTAUT variables along with e -service quality
strongly suggest internet banking adoption. On the same note, the extended applications of
UTAUT to the UAE context demonstrated by Bouteraa et al. (2023) found that th e quality of the
system, awareness, and bank reputation is associated with the intention to use green banking
technology. Such studies attest to the ability of UTAUT to describe the phenomena of
technological adoption, as well as environmentally integrated innovations in the banking industry.
Combined, the UTAUT framework serves as a powerful set of constructs that describe the
motivation of technology adoption in banking. Diminishing the position of performance
expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence and facilitating conditions, the model demonstrates
how customers choose to achieve and maintain the use of digital banking innovations. Of
significance, the factors do not act alone but are combined to create trust, satisfaction, and
involvement of customers in banking technologies. Whenev er customers feel that the banking
technologies are useful, simple to use, socially approved, and properly supported, the chances of

2.2.3. Diffusion of Innovation Theory (DOI)
Diffusion of innovation theory Rogers (1962, updated later in 2003) proposed the Diffusion of
Innovation (DOI) Theory. This theory describes the spread of innovations among the populations,
and the factors that affect the adoption. Rogers created five cate gories of adopters that include
innovators, early adopters, early majority, late majority, and laggards, according to their
predisposition to adopt innovations (Bouteraa, Kooli, Abderrazak & Ben Hassen, 2023).
In his DOI theory, Rogers identifies five groups of adopters of new technologies, namely;
innovators, early adopters, early majority, late majority and late laggards according to the
willingness and rapidity by which their attitude toward innovations can be adopted (Rogers, 2003).
This classification is very applicable to the banking sector as it has recently become the place of
tremendous technological advancement due to the introduction of internet banking, mobile apps,
contactless payments, chatbots powered by artificial intelligence, and blockchain -based financial
platforms. Using the DOI model will allow banks to comprehend the heterogeneity of customer
adoption behaviours and its adoption of specific strategies towards technology -based service
provision (Rogers, 2003; Ozdemir, Trott and Hoecht, 2008).
Innovators are a small segment of customers who are highly influential and are technologically
savvy as well as risk takers. They are the pioneers in testing new banking functions like biometric
access, crypto-wallets, or AI investment applications. Although they are numerous in low quantity,
their contribution is paramount since they generate early feedback by which banks can extract and
refine the new technologies before expanding them to the high customer base (Rogers, 2003;
Ozdemir, Trott & Hoecht, 2008; Bouteraa, Kooli, Abderrazak and Ben Hassen, 2023).

3.0 Methodology
3.1 Research Design
The proposed study used the cross-sectional methodology using surveys to offer a holistic
perspective into the research issue. Cross -sectional was the most appropriate in describing the
presence of some characteristics in a population, and the relationship among variables at one time
(Chindengwike, 2022). This approach will enable gathering data about the technological adoption
of consumers (Zhu and Wang, 2022).

3.2  Population of the Study
The population of the study includes an unlimited number of customers of retail banking company
of six (6) tier -1 banks in Rivers State, Nigeria, that use technology based services (Ariza et al.,
2023). The target group is limitless as the customer base of  commercial banks is constantly
changing (Enoch & Bala, 2021). According to Proshare June 2025 Tier 1 Banks Report on
performance metrics, there are Six Tier 1 banks in operation in Nigeria which include; Ecobank
Transnational Incorporated (ETI), Access Ho ldings, Zenith Bank, First Bank Holdings (FBNH),
United Bank for Africa (UBA) and Guaranty Trust Holding Company (GTCO). These banks are
all found in Rivers State, and therefore, can be accessed by the target population.

3.3  Sample and Sampling Techniques
The study used Cochran formula to determine the importance of sample size of an infinite
population to use in this study and resulting in a sample size of 384. The sampling is the purposive
sampling technique.

3.4 Nature/Sources of Data
The approach to research was quantitative, that is, aimed at gathering and processing numerical
data that would help to formulate hypotheses and generalise the results to test them effectively and
accurately (Bell et al., 2022). It used primary data source s by directly collecting information
through respondents to render relevance in comparison with secondary data that were retrieved
through available records.

3.5 Data Collection/Instrumentation Methods
As the quantitative component of the research, we have taken data with the help of structured
questionnaires, which will be given to the chosen sample of bank customers. The questionnaire
incorporated a blend of structured questions, which aimed at identifying the respondents based on
how they feel about the technology adoption, the degree to which they are satisfied with the
services offered by the bank and how loyal they are to the bank they have.

3.6 Instrument validity/Reliability
The questionnaire in this research was also checked by the other researchers and eight experts in
similar spheres in order to be sure of all these items being understandable, relevant and appropriate.
An adequate literature review and consultation with fiv e experts in the subject of the construct
validated the content validity, which ensured that the items covered the multidimensionality of the

5.0 Conclusion and Recommendations
5.1 Limitations of the Study
Though this research work presents a good information and contribution, it should be noted that
there were some limitations.
The use of cross-sectional design is one of the limitations in this study. The data were collected at
one time, and it makes it difficult to realise the causal relation between the constructs of technology
adoption, including ease of use, performance expectancy, and perceived quality and their influence
on customer experience and loyalty. A longitudinal study would have provided a more detailed
insight into the dynamics of both of these relationships and their change over time.
The paper has mainly explored three critical areas of technology adoption, which included, ease
of use, performance expectancy, and perceived quality, and two customer loyalty dimensions, that
is, affective and conative loyalty. Despite the importance of t hese constructs, they are not quite
inclusive to all the elements of technology adoption such as influence of social enablers or
facilitating conditions, and neither cover all the dimensions of loyalty such as cognitive loyalty.
Such narrow focus may have ignored other factors, which influence customer loyalty in banking
industry.

5.2 Conclusion
The conclusion of the research is that the adoption of technology increases customer loyalty. The
ease of use, performance expectancy and perceived quality are strongly related to affective loyalty
among Tier-1 commercial banks in Rivers State, Nigeria. The study offers empirical evidence on
the DOI theory to emphasise that the adherence to innovations depends on perceived features, such
as relative advantage, complexity and compatibility (Rogers, 2003). The results show that ease of
use underscores the simplification of the digital banking services.

customer loyalty because customers will appreciate the value of the technology as well as
the service environment.

customers may not fully utilise banking technologies due to lack of awareness or due to
poor digital literacy. The introduction of training sessions, tutorials, or in-app instructions
