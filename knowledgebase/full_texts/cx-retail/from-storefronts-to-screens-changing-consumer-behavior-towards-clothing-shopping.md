Abstract:
The rapid expansion of digital commerce has transformed consumers’ clothing shopping behaviour, shifting
purchasing activities from traditional physical stores to online platforms. However, physical stores continue to
offer important experiential benefits, particularly product examination, fitting, personal assistance, and
immediate purchase. This study examines changing consumer behaviour towards clothing shopping in the
digital era by comparatively analysing consumer preferences for online and offline shopping channels. The
study identifies key factors influencing channel preference, including convenience, price attractiveness,
product variety, trust, perceived risk, physical product evaluation, pers onal assistance, and shopping
experience. A quantitative, cross -sectional research design is proposed, with primary data collected through a
structured questionnaire from clothing consumers. Descriptive statistics, reliability and validity assessment,
correlation analysis, comparative tests, and multivariate/structural analysis are employed to examine
relationships among the study constructs and test the proposed hypotheses. The study contributes to the
literature by providing an integrated understanding of  how consumers evaluate competing clothing -shopping
channels and by highlighting the emerging shift from channel -specific preferences towards Omni channel
shopping behaviour. The findings are expected to provide useful implications for clothing retailers i n
developing customer-centric digital, physical, and integrated retail strategies.

Introduction:
Background of the Study
The rapid development of digital technologies has fundamentally transformed the retail environment and the
way consumers search for, evaluate, and purchase products. The proliferation of smartphones, high -speed
internet, mobile applications, digital paymen t systems, social media, and e -commerce platforms has enabled
consumers to access products beyond the geographical and temporal limitations of traditional retailing.
Clothing and fashion retailing has been particularly affected by this transformation becau se consumers can
now browse extensive product assortments, compare prices, read customer reviews, receive personalized
recommendations, and complete purchases without physically visiting a store. Consequently, the traditional

especially important in clothing retail because apparel purchases involve both functional and experiential

Recent research in fashion retail indicates that integrated promotions, customer service, transactions, perceived
ease of use, and enjoyment can influence consumers' Omni channel experiences and satisfaction (Khalid,

and channel-flexible consumer rather than simply an online or offline shopper.
Changing Clothing Shopping Behaviour
Consumer clothing-shopping behaviour has evolved from a predominantly store -based activity into a multi -
stage and technology -enabled decision -making process. In traditional retailing, consumers gener ally visited
physical stores, examined products, interacted with sales personnel, tried garments, and made purchase
decisions based on direct product experience. Digital retailing has introduced additional stages, including
online information search, price  comparison, review evaluation, social -media discovery, digital
recommendations, and mobile purchasing. The increasing availability of digital information has also altered
consumers' expectations regarding convenience and decision-making. Consumers can compare multiple brands
and products within a short period and use reviews and ratings to reduce uncertainty before making a purchase.
In apparel retailing, however, online purchasing continues to involve specific challenges because consumers
cannot directly touch, examine, or try products before purchase. Issues relating to size, fit, fabric quality,
colour accuracy, product authenticity, delivery, and return procedures can therefore influence perceived risk.
Research on online apparel consumers confirms the importance of factors such as attitude, electronic word -of-
mouth, perceived risk, and perceived behavioural control in shaping purchase -related outcomes (Patel et al.,
2023). The digital transformation has also expanded the role of social and emerging tech nologies in fashion
consumption. Consumers increasingly encounter fashion products through social media, user -generated
content, influencer recommendations, personalized recommendations, and other digital touchpoints. Moreover,
technological developments a re beginning to influence the way consumers search for products. Recent
evidence suggests that generative-AI-based shopping discovery is becoming another digital touchpoint through
which consumers can obtain product recommendations and information, althoug h questions concerning trust,
privacy, and the role of human interaction remain important.

Online versus Offline Clothing Shopping
Online and offline clothing shopping provide distinct benefits and limitations that  can shape consumer channel
preferences. Online shopping is primarily associated with convenience, accessibility, product variety, price
comparison, time efficiency, and the ability to shop from any location. Consumers can access a large
assortment of prod ucts and obtain information without the time and effort associated with visiting multiple
physical stores. Digital platforms can also use consumer data to personalize recommendations and promotions,
potentially improving the relevance of the shopping exper ience. Offline clothing shopping, in contrast,
provides sensory and interpersonal experiences that remain difficult to reproduce digitally. Consumers can
physically examine fabrics, assess colours, try different sizes, evaluate fit, interact with sales per sonnel, and
obtain immediate possession of purchased products. These characteristics can be particularly important for
apparel because perceived product quality and fit are difficult to assess completely through a digital interface.
Emerging research on di gital fashion experiences similarly highlights the importance of developing
technologies that can compensate for the absence of physical product interaction in online environments.
Therefore, neither channel can be considered universally superior. Online s hopping may offer greater
convenience and variety, whereas physical stores may provide stronger product confidence and experiential
value. Recent fashion-retailing research further suggests that consumers' experiences across multiple channels
can influence overall satisfaction, emphasizing the importance of channel integration rather than treating online
and offline retailing as completely separate alternatives.
Research Problem:
Despite the rapid expansion of digital clothing retailing, physical stores con tinue to occupy an important
position in consumers' shopping journeys. This creates a strategic challenge for clothing retailers:
understanding whether consumers are genuinely replacing physical shopping with digital shopping or simply
incorporating online  channels into broader, multi -channel purchasing journeys. The answer cannot be
determined by examining online purchase intention alone. The problem becomes more significant because
clothing is an experiential product category in which consumers evaluate s everal attributes simultaneously.
Convenience, price, product variety, trust, perceived risk, product evaluation, fitting, personal assistance, and
shopping experience may influence consumers differently depending on the channel being used. Consequently,
retailers require a clearer understanding of the relative importance of these factors in determining online and
offline clothing-shopping preferences.

Literature Review:
Online Clothing Shopping Behaviour
The growth of e -commerce and digital technologies has significantly cha nged the way consumers search for
and purchase clothing. Online clothing platforms provide consumers with convenience, wider product
assortment, price comparison, product information, customer reviews, and the ability to shop irrespective of
location and s tore timings. These benefits have made online channels increasingly attractive, particularly for
consumers who value time efficiency and accessibility. However, clothing is an experiential product, and
online purchasing involves uncertainty because consume rs cannot physically examine or try garments before
purchase. Factors such as size and fit, fabric quality, colour, product appearance, delivery, and return policies
can therefore influence online purchase decisions. Patel et al. (2023), in their study of 432 online apparel
consumers, found that attitude, purchase intention, perceived risk, electronic word -of-mouth (eWOM), and
perceived behavioural control are important in explaining online apparel purchasing behaviour. Their findings
demonstrate that online clothing purchases involve both functional benefits and psychological considerations.
Trust is also important in online fashion retailing because consumers must rely on information provided by
retailers and other consumers. Reliable product descriptions,  reviews, ratings, secure payment systems,
transparent return policies, and brand reputation can reduce uncertainty and increase consumers' confidence in
online purchases. Thus, convenience, price attractiveness, product variety, information availability, trust, and
perceived risk emerge as important dimensions of online clothing-shopping behaviour.
Offline Clothing Shopping Behaviour
Despite the rapid expansion of online retailing, physical stores continue to provide distinctive benefits for
clothing consu mers. Unlike online channels, physical stores allow consumers to directly examine products,
touch fabrics, assess colours and quality, try different sizes, and evaluate the fit and appearance of garments
before making a purchase. These characteristics are particularly relevant to clothing because consumers often

to possess advantages that are dif ficult to reproduce completely through digital platforms. Importantly, the

Online versus Offline Clothing Shopping and Omni channel Behaviour
The comparison between online and offline shopping suggests that neither channel provides a universally
superior shopping experience. Online channels generally offer greater convenience, accessibility, assortment,
price comparison, and information availability, whereas offline stores provide physical product evaluation,
fitting, personal assistance, immediate possession, and experiential value. Consequently, consumers may select
different channels according to their i ndividual preferences, product characteristics, purchase situations, and
perceived risks. Recent research supports the movement from channel -specific shopping towards integrated
Omni channel behaviour. In the Indian context, a study of 302 millennials exam ined the antecedents of Omni
channel shopping for fashion products and demonstrated the relevance of Omni channel approaches to fashion
purchase intentions. Similarly, Khalid (2024), using data from 509 consumers with fashion Omni channel
shopping experience, found that perceived ease of use, enjoyment, integrated promotions, integrated customer
service, and integrated transactions positively influence Omni channel experiences and customer satisfaction.
These findings indicate that consumers increasingly mo ve between digital and physical channels during
different stages of the purchasing process. For example, a consumer may discover a clothing product through
social media, compare prices online, visit a physical store to examine or try the product, and subse quently
purchase through either channel. Therefore, the traditional distinction between an "online shopper" and an
"offline shopper" may no longer fully explain contemporary clothing-shopping behaviour.
Key Determinants of Clothing Shopping Channel Preference
The literature indicates that consumers' preferences between online and offline clothing shopping are
influenced by several interrelated factors. Convenience and time efficiency are major advantages of online
shopping, while price and product variety p rovide consumers with greater opportunities to compare
alternatives. Trust can encourage online purchases by reducing uncertainty, whereas perceived risk related to
product quality, fit, payment, delivery, and returns can discourage online purchasing. Evid ence from online
apparel research confirms the importance of perceived risk and eWOM in shaping apparel purchase behaviour.
In contrast, physical product evaluation, fitting, personal assistance, immediate possession, and shopping
experience represent impo rtant advantages of offline shopping. These factors can be particularly influential
when consumers are uncertain about size, fit, fabric, or product quality. The literature therefore suggests that
clothing-shopping channel preference is multidimensional ra ther than determined by a single factor.

Synthesis of Literature and Research Gap
The reviewed literature establishes that online and offline clothing -shopping channels offer different but
complementary benefits. Existing st udies have provided valuable insights into online apparel purchase
behaviour, perceived risk, eWOM, Omni channel shopping, and customer satisfaction. However, much of the
existing research examines these dimensions separately, with greater emphasis on onli ne purchasing or Omni
channel experiences. A comparatively limited body of research provides an integrated comparison of the
factors influencing consumers' preferences for online and offline clothing shopping. In particular, there is a
need to simultaneously examine online-oriented factors such as convenience, price, variety, and trust alongside
offline-oriented factors such as physical evaluation, fitting, personal assistance, and shopping experience.
Therefore, the present study addresses this gap by deve loping a comparative framework of online and offline
clothing-shopping preferences. It seeks to understand whether consumers are shifting from physical stores to
digital platforms or increasingly combining the two channels as part of an emerging Omni chann el clothing-
shopping journey.
Research Gap and Hypothesis Development
Research Gap
The rapid growth of e-commerce has substantially transformed clothing-shopping behaviour; however, existing
research has largely examined online apparel purchasing, consumer  trust, perceived risk, or Omni channel
experiences as separate areas of investigation. Previous studies have established that convenience, information
availability, trust, perceived risk, and electronic word -of-mouth influence consumers' online apparel de cisions
(Harrigan et al., 2021; Patel et al., 2023). Similarly, research on Omni channel retailing demonstrates that
consumers increasingly interact with retailers through multiple online and offline touchpoints (Cui et al., 2022;
Khalid, 2024). Neverthele ss, comparatively limited research has simultaneously examined the key factors
influencing consumers' preference for online and offline clothing -shopping channels within an integrated
comparative framework. In particular, there is a need to examine whether  the functional advantages of online
shopping, especially convenience and product variety, significantly encourage consumers to prefer digital
clothing-shopping channels, while the experiential advantages of physical stores, particularly physical product
evaluation, continue to influence offline shopping preference. Addressing this gap can provide a more balanced
understanding of whether digitalization is replacing traditional clothing retailing or encouraging consumers to
combine both channels. Accordingly , the present study develops a focused comparative framework
incorporating convenience, product variety, physical product evaluation, and shopping experience as key
determinants of consumers' clothing-shopping channel preferences.

Methodology
Research Design
The present study adopts a quantitative, cross -sectional, and explanatory research design to examine changing
consumer behaviour towards clothing shopping in the digital era. The study focuses on comparing consumers'
preferences for online and offline clothing -shopping channels and identifying the factors that signi ficantly
influence these preferences. A structured questionnaire is used to collect primary data from consumers who
have experience with clothing shopping through both online and offline channels.
Population and Study Area
The target population comprises c onsumers who have purchased or regularly shop for clothing through online
platforms and/or physical retail stores. The study is conducted in the Vindhya region of Madhya Pradesh,
India, providing a regional context for examining the changing behaviour of c onsumers in both digital and
traditional clothing markets. The region provides an appropriate setting for comparing online and offline
shopping because consumers increasingly have access to e -commerce platforms while continuing to use
conventional clothing stores.
Sampling and Sample Size
A non -probability purposive sampling technique is adopted to ensure that respondents have relevant
experience with clothing shopping. Respondents are required to be consumers who have experience with at
least one online or  offline clothing-shopping channel. To obtain adequate observations for statistical analysis
and hypothesis testing, the study targets a sample of approximately 400 respondents. The final sample size will
be determined after excluding incomplete or invalid responses.
Data Collection Instrument
Primary data are collected through a structured questionnaire developed from constructs identified in the
existing literature. The questionnaire consists of two parts. The first part collects demographic and general
shopping information, including age, gender, education, occupation, income, shopping frequency, and
preferred clothing -shopping channel. The second part measures the constructs included in the conceptual
framework.
All construct items are measured using a f ive-point Likert scale, ranging from 1 = Strongly Disagree to 5 =
Strongly Agree. The questionnaire is initially reviewed for clarity, relevance, and content validity before the
final data collection.
Measurement of Variables
The study examines four independent variables and two corresponding shopping-channel preferences.

Pilot Testing
A study involving approximately 30 –50 respondents is conducted to assess the clarity, rel iability, and
suitability of the questionnaire. Feedback obtained from participants is used to identify ambiguous or repetitive
items and to improve the wording of the questionnaire. The reliability of the constructs is assessed using
Cronbach's alpha, with a value of 0.70 or above considered acceptable for internal consistency.
Data Analysis
The collected data are analysed using SPSS and/or SmartPLS, depending on the final analytical model.
Descriptive statistics, including frequency, percentage, mean, and  standard deviation, are used to describe
respondents' demographic characteristics and shopping behaviour.
The measurement model is evaluated through factor loadings, Cronbach's alpha, composite reliability (CR),
and average variance extracted (AVE) to est ablish reliability and convergent validity. Discriminant validity is
assessed using the heterotrait–monotrait (HTMT) ratio.
For hypothesis testing, Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling (PLS -SEM) is employed to
examine the proposed relationships between the independent variables and online/offline shopping preferences.
Bootstrapping is used to determine the significance of the estimated path coefficients. The analysis reports β
coefficients, t-values, p-values, R², effect size (f²), and predictive relevance (Q²) where appropriate.
In addition, comparative analysis is conducted to examine differences in consumers' perceptions and
preferences between online and offline clothing -shopping channels. Appropriate statistical tests are selected
according to the measurement characteristics and distribution of the data.
Ethical Considerations
Participation in the study is voluntary. Respondents are informed about the academic purpose of the research
and are assured that their responses will be treated con fidentially and used only for research purposes. No
personally identifiable information is required in the questionnaire. Informed consent is obtained from
respondents before data collection.

The preliminary model explaining online shopping preference produced an R² of 0.167, indicating that

Summary of Findings:
Overall, the analysis demonstrates satisfactory internal consistency across all six constructs. The preliminary
structural relationships provide support for H1 and H4, while H2 and H3 are not statistically significant at the
5% level. The results suggest that convenience is an important factor associated with online clothing -shopping
preference, whereas the overall shopping experience appears more important for offline shopping preference.
The findings also reinforce the relevance of examining online and offline clothing shopping simultaneously.
The relatively balanced preference distribution and the presence of consumers using both channels sugg est that
contemporary clothing consumers may increasingly exhibit hybrid or Omni channel shopping behaviour rather
than exclusive loyalty to a single channel.
Discussion:
The present study examined changing consumer behaviour towards clothing shopping by c omparing the
factors associated with online and offline shopping preferences. The pilot analysis of 200 respondents provides
preliminary evidence that consumers' channel preferences are influenced by different characteristics of digital
and physical retail environments. Overall, convenience emerged as an important factor associated with online
clothing-shopping preference, whereas shopping experience was more strongly associated with offline
clothing-shopping preference. These findings support the broader v iew that digitalization has not simply
replaced physical retailing but has created a more diverse and interconnected clothing-shopping environment.
Convenience and Online Shopping Preference
The findings provide support for H1, demonstrating a significant positive relationship between convenience
and online clothing -shopping preference (β = 0.391, p < 0.001). This indicates that consumers who perceive
online clothing shopping as convenient are more likely to prefer digital channels. The result is consistent  with
the broader literature on online retailing, where convenience, accessibility, time savings, and reduced shopping
effort are frequently identified as important advantages of e-commerce.
For clothing retailers, this finding suggests that online platfor ms should focus on reducing the effort involved
in product search, selection, payment, and delivery. Simple navigation, efficient search functions, personalized
recommendations, convenient payment methods, and reliable delivery can strengthen the perceived
convenience of online shopping.

Product Variety and Online Shopping Preference
H2 was not supported at the 5% significance level (β = 0.111, p = 0.090). Although product variety showed a
positive association with online shopping preference, the relationsh ip was not sufficiently strong in the pilot
sample. This suggests that simply offering a large assortment of clothing products may not be enough to
determine consumers' preferred shopping channel. One  possible explanation is that consumers may
increasingly expect extensive product variety from both online and large -format physical retailers.
Furthermore, excessive choice may create information overload rather than necessarily improving the shopping
experience. Consumers may therefore place greater importanc e on factors such as convenience, trust, product
quality, fit, reviews, and return policies when selecting a clothing-shopping channel.
Physical Product Evaluation and Offline Shopping Preference
H3 examined the relationship between physical product evalua tion and offline shopping preference. Although
the relationship was positive, it was not statistically significant in the pilot results (β = 0.110, p = 0.127). This
finding is noteworthy because physical examination is traditionally considered one of the m ajor advantages of
physical clothing stores. The result may indicate that technological developments are gradually reducing some
of the disadvantages associated with online clothing shopping. Detailed product images, customer reviews, size
guides, videos, virtual try-on technologies, and flexible return policies may enable consumers to make clothing
decisions without physically examining the product. Nevertheless, physical evaluation remains conceptually
important, and its role should be examined again using the larger main-study sample.
Shopping Experience and Offline Shopping Preference
H4 was supported, with shopping experience demonstrating a significant positive relationship with offline
clothing-shopping preference (β = 0.337, p < 0.001). This indicate s that consumers who value the experiential
aspects of physical retailing are more likely to prefer offline clothing shopping. The  finding highlights an
important strategic opportunity for physical retailers. Rather than competing with online retailers sol ely on
price and product assortment, physical stores can differentiate themselves through superior customer
experiences. Store atmosphere, personalized assistance, product trials, visual merchandising, social interaction,
and immediate product availability  can provide experiential value that digital platforms may find difficult to
replicate completely.
Online–Offline Convergence
An important broader observation from the pilot results is that consumers should not necessarily be viewed as
either online or off line shoppers. Approximately 30% of respondents indicated that they use both channels
equally, while the overall preference levels for online and offline shopping were relatively similar. This
provides preliminary support for the emergence of hybrid and Omni channel  clothing-shopping behaviour.
Consumers may therefore use online and offline channels at different stages of the same purchase journey. For
example, consumers may search for products online, compare prices, visit a store to assess fit, and
subsequently complete the purchase through either channel. This suggests that the future of clothing retailing
may depend less on choosing between online and offline channels and more on integrating both into a seamless
consumer experience.

Major Implications
Theoretical Implications
The study contributes to consumer-behaviour and retailing literature by examining online and offline clothing -
shopping preferences within a single comparative framework. The findings suggest that different channel
characteristics inf luence consumers differently: convenience is particularly relevant to online preference,
whereas shopping experience is particularly relevant to offline preference. The  study also supports the
emerging conceptualization of consumers as Omni channel  shoppers. Rather than assuming that digital
shopping necessarily substitutes for physical retailing, the findings indicate that both channels may continue to
serve distinct functions within the consumer decision-making process.
Managerial Implications
The findings provide several implications for clothing retailers.
First, online retailers should prioritize convenience. Simplified navigation, efficient search, mobile -friendly
interfaces, secure digital payments, faster delivery, easy returns, and personalized recommendations can
enhance the overall online shopping experience. Second, retailers should recognize that product variety alone
may not guarantee online preference. Product assortment should be accompanied by reliable information,
accurate size specifications, customer reviews, high -quality product visuals, and effective recommendation
systems. Third , physical retailers should focus on experiential differentiation. Since shopping experience
significantly contributes to offline preference, stores can strengthen their competitive position through
knowledgeable sale s personnel, attractive store environments, personalized assistance, trial facilities, and
engaging customer experiences. Finally, retailers should adopt an Omni channel strategy rather than treating
online and offline operations as separate businesses. Fe atures such as online inventory visibility, click -and-
collect services, online reservation followed by in -store trial, unified loyalty programmes, and consistent
customer service across channels can help retailers integrate the strengths of both environments.
Consumer and Industry Implications
From the consumer perspective, the findings demonstrate that digitalization has expanded rather than
eliminated consumer choice. Consumers can select channels according to convenience, experience, product
characteristics, and situational requirements.
For the clothing industry, the results suggest a transition from a traditional store -versus-screen perspective
towards an integrated retail ecosystem. Retailers that successfully connect digital convenience with physical
experience may be better positioned to respond to changing consumer expectations.
Limitations and Future Research
Limitations
The study has several limitations. First, the analysis is based on a cross -sectional design, which captures
consumer behaviour at a particular point in time and ther efore cannot establish changes in behaviour over an
extended period. Second, the study uses a regional sample, which limits the generalizability of the findings to
consumers outside the selected geographical area. Consumer behaviour may differ across regio ns because of
variations in digital accessibility, income, retail infrastructure, and cultural preferences. Third, the study relies

and actual pur chasing behaviour. Fourth, the proposed framework includes only four principal determinants.
Other variables such as trust, perceived risk, price attractiveness, electronic word -of-mouth, social -media

validated using the final main-study dataset.
Future Research
Future studies can extend this research in several directions. First, researchers can use larger and
geographically diverse samples to improve the generalizability of the findings. Co mparative studies across
urban and rural consumers, different Indian regions, and different age groups could provide additional insights
into digital clothing-shopping behaviour. Second, future research can incorporate additional determinants such
as trust , perceived risk, price attractiveness, electronic word -of-mouth, social -media influence, delivery
service, return policy, and brand loyalty to develop a more comprehensive model of channel preference. Third,
longitudinal research could examine how consume rs' online and offline shopping behaviour changes as digital
technologies develop. This would be particularly valuable given the rapid growth of artificial intelligence,
virtual try-on technologies, personalized recommendations, social commerce, and augmen ted reality in fashion
retailing. Finally , future studies should investigate Omni channel  consumer journeys rather than focusing
exclusively on channel preference. Examining how consumers move between social media, e -commerce
platforms, mobile applications , and physical stores during a single clothing purchase could provide a deeper
understanding of the emerging digital-era consumer.
Conclusion:
The study demonstrates that the transformation of clothing retailing should not be understood simply as a
movement from physical stores to digital screens. The preliminary findings suggest that convenience is an
important driver of online clothing-shopping preference, while shopping experience contributes significantly to
offline preference. At the same time, product  variety and physical product evaluation did not demonstrate
statistically significant effects in the pilot analysis. The relatively balanced use of online and offline channels
indicates that consumers may increasingly combine the strengths of both environ ments. The future of clothing
retailing is therefore likely to be characterized by integrated online –offline experiences, where digital
convenience and physical engagement complement one another. Retailers that successfully develop seamless
Omni channel experiences may be better positioned to respond to the evolving expectations of contemporary
clothing consumers.
