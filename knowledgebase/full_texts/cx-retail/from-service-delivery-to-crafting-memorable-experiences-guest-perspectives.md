Abstract
This study explores the role of staff service in creating memorable hotel experiences
(MHEs) through the lens of customer perceptions. Drawing on 30,783 online reviews from 100
hotels across the world’s ten most visited cities, a large -scale text analysis identified five staff -
related components that significantly contribute to MHEs: emotional competence, responsiveness
and problem-solving, professional competency, personalization, and adaptability and flexibility.
Beyond identifying these dimensions, the s tudy offers deeper insights into how guests interpret
and value employee interactions that shape their overall experiences. The findings advance
theoretical understanding of employee competencies within the experience economy and
emphasize the importance o f care, pride, and authenticity in service encounters. Practically, the
study provides actionable implications for hospitality managers seeking to strengthen human
resource strategies and enhance guest experiences that are distinctive, emotionally engaging , and
memorable.
Keywords: Experience economy, m emorable hotel experience, online review, employee
competencies, data mining.

Introduction
The rise of the experience economy has become increasingly influential in shaping tourism and
hospitality practices. Pine and Gilmore (1999) define experiences from a business perspective as
“events that engage individuals in a personal way” (p. 12). It highlighted a shift from the traditional
“delivery-focused” service economy, which prioritizes quality offerings, to a “staged” economy
where businesses design experiences that leave lasting impressions  (Pine and Gilmore 1999) .
Building on this, Hemmington (2007) advanced the conceptualization of hospitality beyond
service management, framing it as a commercial experience . His framework emphasized five
dimensions central to the hospitality encounter,  consisting of the host-guest relationship, acts of
generosity, theatricality and performance, delightful surprises, and assurances of safety and
security (Hemmington, 2007).
Within this evolution, hospitality organizations are increasingly expected to transition from
traditional "service providers" to "experience providers"  (Bharwani and Jauhari 201 7). Even
though a well-designed physical environment and effective servicescape design showed positive
impacts on customer satisfaction (Bitner 1992; Nowacki and Niezgoda 2023), poor interpersonal
interactions may undermine their overall experience (Sukhu and Bilgihan 2023). This shift raises
a critical question for both scholars and practitioners of what competencies hospitality employees
should possess in order to consistently create distinctive and memorable customer experie nces
(Bharwani and Jauhari 2017).
In today’s experience economy, experiential outcomes such as customer word -of-mouth
(Hurombo, Basupi and Chatibura 2025) as well as online reviews (Gabbard and Dominic 2023)
reflect not only that guests enjoyed their stay, but also that the experience left a strong and lasting

Literature Review
Memorable Hotel Experience
The notion of the memorable experience was widely regarded as a new source of competitive
advantage in hospitality and tourism, attracting growing scholarly and managerial attention (Tung
et al. 2017). Kim, Ritchie, and McCormick (2012) define d memorable tourism experiences as
those “positively remembered and recalled after the event has occurred” (p. 13).  Experiences are
associated with strong affective responses , whether positive or negative , that generate a lasting
“gut feeling ” (Herjanto and Gaur 2017). However, positive experiences are of particular
importance, as they have been shown to drive favorable outcomes such as satisfaction, loyalty, and
behavioral intentions ( Kim, Ritchie, and McCormick 2012). Memory is a crucial mechanism in
shaping post-consumption evaluations and future decision-making; it functions as the single most
important personal source of information guiding tourists’ repeat visitation decisions (Sthapit and
Coudounaris 2017). Accordingly, delivering memorable tourism experiences has become a key
determinant of competitiveness in the tourism and hospitality industries (Kim and Ritchie 2014;
Glaap and Ermolaeva 2024).
In this study, a memorable hotel experience (MHE) is defined as a positive experience that
lasts in the guest’s memory after their stay (Sthapit 2018) . Beyond the immediate service
interaction, such experiences foster a stronger and more enduring relationship  that generates
beneficial outcomes for hotels . Memorable experiences lead to higher guest satisfaction,
strengthen intentions to return, and stimulate positive word -of-mouth communication (Agapito
and Sigala 2024; Kim and So 2022).

Scholars agree that memorable experiences are complex phenomena shaped by a
combination of contextual, relational, and psychological factors (Herjanto and Gaur 201 7).
Destination drivers such as destination charisma and environmental conditions (Curtin 2010),
along with local culture, activities, hospitality, infrastructure, environmental management, and
physiography (Kim 2014), have been shown to play a central role . Relational aspects, including
personalization and the authenticity of host - guest relationships, also emerge as critical in shaping
positive impressions (Ariffin 2013). Psychological dimensions further enrich this understanding,
with self -determined motivations, particularly the desire to learn , demonstrated to enhance
engagement ( Sie, Phelan and Pegg 2018), while perceptions of authenticity and sincerity
significantly shape heritage experiences (Taheri, Gannon and Kesgin 2020). More recently, in line
with the growing digitalization of tourism, smart tourism technologies have also been found to
play a pivotal role in shaping memorable experiences, mediated through marketing and
organizational practices (Elshaer and Marzouk 2022).
In the hotel context, research consistently reveals a set of recurring themes that determine
memorable guest experiences. These include the quality of guest rooms, human interaction,
physical design, culinary offerings, value for money, location, and overall atmosphere (Castellani
et al. 2020; Hurombo, Basupi and Chatibura 2025; Sthapit and Coudounaris 2018). Building on
this and drawing on insights from the world’s top hotels, Herjanto and Gaur (2017) emphasize the
central role of staff hospitality and professionalism, alongside guests’ perceptions of their travel
experiences and the effective management of hotel spaces.

The role of staff in creating memorable hotel experiences
In tourism and hospitality,  hospitality employees, especially front-line employees, have been
highlighted due to their vital role in contributing to service quality and guest satisfaction (Marneros,
Papageorgiou, and Efstathiades 2020). With the expansion of the experience economy, the role and
competencies of hospitality employees have increasingly been of concern (Bharwani and Jauhari
2013), along with wider developments in the hospitality sector (Agapito and Sigala 2024) .
Excellent service delivery , with the central role of employees, enhances customer satisfaction,
generates an emotional sense of memorable experience, and ultimately fosters guest loyalty
(Ariffin and Maghzi 201 2). Despite variations in hotel standards, the most critical factors
influencing customer satisfaction are the quality and perceived value of services provided (Soifer,
Choi and Lee 2021). Expanding this perspective, V o et al. (2021), in their study of Vietnam’s luxury
hotel market, demonstrated that guest internet reviews reflect how staff service quality improves
customer satisfaction, engagement, and loyalty. At the other end of the spectrum, studies of budget
hotels show that, even when pricing is comparable, staff service quality often explains differences
in customer evaluations and the popularity of these properties (Permatasari, Maghrifani, and
Wibowo 2025).
Human interaction is a prerequisite for memorable hotel experiences (MHEs), which may
be categorized into staff-guest interactions and guest-guest interactions (Hurombo, Basupi, and
Chatibura 2025). The competence of hotel service teams is widely recognized as a critical element
in enhancing service quality and meeting guest expectations (Wang et al. 2020). Staff encounters,
consistently identified as at the core of memorable experiences beyond tangible aspects of the

User Generated Content
The growth of user -generated content (UGC) has transformed the way travelers make
decisions (Chiappa and Alarcón-del-Amo 2015). In the digital era, online reviews are among the
most influential forms of electronic word -of-mouth (eWOM). They provide peer -based insights
and recommendations that influence choices of destinations, attractions, and accommodations ,
often outweighing conventional marketing messaging (Liu et al. 2020). As such, eWOM
significantly shapes consumer perceptions and impacts hotel reputation (Gabbard  2023; Watson
and Wu 2022). Beyond influencing decisions, online reviews have become a vital resource for
understanding consumer behavior in tourism and hospitality ( Joseph, Vasundhara, and Thomas
2024), offering hoteliers practical insights into how service quality affects customer satisfaction
(V o et al. 2021).
Building on this, UGC has evolved into a powerful research tool for examining customer
expectations and assessments of product and service delivery (Brito, Ban, and Kim 2024; Hu et al.
2021; Kaveski Peres and Pacheco Paladini 2021 ). Studies using online reviews have identified a

Methodology
Research Design:
Content analysis was conducted using an inductive approach, which objectively examines
textual data without relying on preconceived theories or frameworks, thereby allowing the
emergence of insights unique to the study (Bengtsson 2016). To address the resea rch aims, this
study adopts a mixed -methods design that combines large -scale text mining with qualitative
thematic analysis to investigate how memorable experiences are expressed in online customer
reviews within the hospitality context. This approach aligns with recent calls by Agapito and Sigala
(2024) to employ a mixology of research methods in order to capture a more comprehensive
understanding of subjective experience.

Thematic analysis is widely recognized as a powerful tool for identifying and interpreting
themes within qualitative data, providing a structured way of organizing and making sense of
complex textual material (Braun and Clarke 2006). Nevertheless, coding can at times become
overly static and reductionist, fragmenting data and minimizing its contextual depth (Alvesson and
Sköldberg 2018). To counterbalance this limitation, automated text mining in the present study
was complemented with a hermeneutic approach of a subsample of reviews, thereby strengthening
the robustness of the findings while preserving their contextual richness  (Alvesson and Sandberg
2021).
Data selection
TripAdvisor was chosen as the data source for this study due to its widespread use and continued
status as one of the world’s largest travel review platforms. In 2024, its community posted nearly
80 million new contributions, including 31.1 million reviews and 38.1 million photos/videos,
across its website and app  (Tripadvisor 2024). Its wide -ranging coverage of accommodations,
restaurants, and travel services, and reliability make it a robust platform and appropriate for the
recent study.
Reviews were extracted from the TripAdvisor Travelers’ Choice Best Hotels, which are
determined annually based on the quality and quantity of traveler reviews. From each of the Top
10 most visited cities in 2024, (Euromonitor International 2024), the top 10 ranked hotels were
selected. Review data were collected across a five -year period (January 2021  – August 2025) to
ensure both recency and robustness in capturing evolving guest perceptions.

Data analysis procedure
After extracting data using the TripAdvisor Review Scraper, the text underwent a pre -processing
stage to ensure both quality and relevance. First, only English -language reviews exceeding 25
words were retained, as brief comments often lack sufficient narrative detail (Boyd 2017). Second,
the dataset was limited to four- and five-star reviews, reflecting the study’s emphasis on positive
and memorable experiences. Third, reviews were filtered to include only those explicitly
referencing memorable experiences, using keywords such as “memorable,” “unfor gettable,”
“extraordinary,” “lasting impression,” “experience,” “once in a lifetime,” and “special occasion”
(Sthapit 2018).  Finally, the data were cleaned, organized, and standardized to remove unnecessary
content, thereby facilitating computational processing and enhancing analytical performance.
Following this process, the final dataset comprised 30,783 reviews that were ready for subsequent

The analysis proceeded in three stages.
Stage 1 focused on text mining analysis. The corpus was imported into V oyant Tools, an
open-source text analysis environment  (Gregory, Geiger , and Salisbury 2022) , where topic
modeling and frequency analysis were performed to detect recurrent themes across the dataset.
The resulting clusters were then synthesized and grouped by the researchers into broader factors
influencing customer experience.

Stage 2 centered on staff-related collocation analysis. To explore employee-related aspects
in more detail, collocate analysis was performed using staff -related keywords extracted  from
V oyant's text-mining process (e.g. staff, team, receptionist, concierge, server, housekeeper,
manager, waiter). Using t he words most frequently associated with these terms, specific staff
attributes were identified as initial codes and collated into potential themes. Then the specifics of
each theme were reviewed and validated in order to refine them, as well as the overall narrative
given by the analysis, generating definitions and titles for each theme.
Stage 3  involved a hermeneutic analysis approach . To complement the computational
findings, a random sub-sample of 500 reviews was subjected to interpretation, which exceeded the
sample size for saturation (Hennink and Kaiser 2022) . This stage allowed for the recognition of
contextual nuances and latent meanings not easily captured by automated techniques, including
affective tones, metaphors, and subtle forms of guest–host interaction (Paterson and Higgs 2005).
Insights derived from this stage enriched the understanding of memorable experiences and
validated the thematic categories generated through computational analysis.
To enhance credibility, coding decisions from both the collocation and hermeneutic phases
were discussed iteratively among the research team until agreement was reached.

researchers rigorously classified and categorized the extracted keywords (Table 4). Keywords
associated with hotel staff were also visualized in the word cloud presented in Figure 2.  Further

Emotional competence refers to the emotional expressions and interpersonal behaviors
demonstrated by staff during guest interactions. These include warmth, empathy, understanding,
emotional resilience, and the ability to motivate and positively influence guests (Bharwani and
Jauhari 201 3; Goleman 1995). Responsiveness and problem-solving reflect staff’s ability to
promptly and effectively address guest inquiries, concerns, or issues, showing a genuine
willingness to assist, clear communication about actions taken, and timely service delivery
(Parasuraman et al. 1988; Sharma and Srivastava 2018). Professional competency refers to the
ability to perform duties effectively while maintaining professional ethics, adopting a team -
oriented mindset, and applying technical and communication skills (Cheng and Wong 2015).
Personalization involves proactively understanding, anticipating, and tailoring services and
experiences to meet individual customer needs and preferences ( Lei et al. 2024; Sørensen and
Jensen 2015). Adaptability and flexibility describe the knowledge, skills, and abilities required
to remain open to change, respond promptly to unforeseen events, and adjust actions to
accommodate varying customer needs (Warier 2014; Secchi, Roth, and Verma 2020).

Emphasize authenticity and genuineness
It was evident that the emotional dimension was repeatedly highlighted and carried the
greatest weight among all staff -related reviews. Beyond general remarks such as “friendly”,
“kind”, or “welcoming”, guests particularly stressed the actions that made them feel truly cared
for and warmly received, describing staff as “warm and sincere” and “patient and affectionate.”
Enthusiasm that was driven by genuine intent was valued even more than professional support or
technical service. As one guest put it: “This level of service went far beyond professional courtesy
- it felt like genuine hospitality.”

of escape (Pine and Gilmore 1999 ). While prior research suggests that memorable hospitality

(Herjanto and Gaur 2017), when it came to human interaction, guests expressed a preference for
qualities that felt close to the heart , those that created a sense of familiarity and belonging, much
like being at home. As one review noted: “We felt so welcome and at home, we enjoyed ourselves
immensely!” Another guest echoed this sentiment: “Amazing hospitality that made it a home away
from home. [GM] and his team are ever so attentive and welcoming. The hotel experience is so

Exceeding Expectations through Dedication
To go beyond being a “service provider”  to being an “experience provider”, hospitality
employees must demonstrate their abilities in a way that creates the greatest value for guests, often
by exceeding expectations. Guests cognize and deeply appreciate the display of effort, passion,
and drive for excellence. As one guest summarized , “The staff went above and beyond in every
way.” Another guest also highlighted that “The thoughtfulness and effort put into celebrating with
us made the day feel extra special. It was a heartwarming gesture that I’ll always remember and
appreciate, showcasing their dedication to going the extra mile for their guests.” Guests frequently
noted that when staff go beyond scripted duties, acting deliberately, willingly, and with dedication
to create the best possible guest experience. “Thank you for all the amazing details and for reading
our minds in every activity and time that we spend in the hotel.”

emotional gift of experience. Expressions such as “I want to express my gratitude, especially to
Paul Peker, for his support during my stay here” and “I am deeply indebted to Mr. Eddie for his
thoughtful consideration and the profound impact he had on my overall experience” illustrate that
staff are not merely fulfilling professional duties, they are perceived as active creators of customer
experience.
Notably, guests often make a point to mention staff by name in their reviews. “Especially
Milan and Marcus from reception, Fanny and Danny from Caprice, TMs from IRD and HSKP ,
their services are terrific”, thus underscoring the significance of individual employees in shaping
hospitality experience. This practice goes beyond standard complementing of the hotel itself, it
reflects the recognition of personal, human connections. In these cases, service is no longer
scripted but personalized, indicating that the staff member s took ownership of the guest’s
experiences.

Implications
This study contributes to the literature on experience management in hospitality, customer –
employee interactions, and the application of textual analysis using large datasets of online hotel
reviews. It advances theoretical understanding of the experience economy by focusing on its
central dimension, human interaction (Hemmington 2007; Hurombo, Basupi, and Chatibura 2025),
and by exploring how employees can elevate hotel encounters into memorable experiences.
In the experience economy, customers seek products based on their direct and subjective
experiences (Oh, Fiore, and Jeoung 2007). Within this paradigm, service providers take on the role
of performers, and the service environment becomes the stage where experiences are enacted (Pine
and Gilmore 1999). Consequently, the role of employees has evolved from merely transferring
pre-produced or pre-designed goods and services to actively participating in the design, production,
and delivery of experiential products (Bharwani and Jauhari 201 7; Sotiriadis and Varvaressos
2016). Building upon this shift, this study provides an empirical contribution to the growing body
of research on the transformation of human resource management in the experience economy
(Sotiriadis and Varvaressos 2016).
While previous research has proposed extensive lists of employee competencies (He,
Ahmad, and Razak 2024; Tule 2016 ), this study reinforced and illustrated those most valued by
guests in the context of the changing experience economy. Its significance lies in offering an in -
depth customer perspective, revealing which employee attributes stand out, truly shape lasting

Limitations and suggestions for future research
This study has several limitations that open suggestions for future research. First, the analysis
focused exclusively on positive reviews. While positive assessments are valuable for identifying
employee attributes that create memorable experiences, negati ve experiences may also provide
important insights into service gaps and critical incidents that shape lasting impressions. Analysis
of negative reviews may well highlight a significant body of reviews where staff were the cause
of the negative perceptions about the experience. Future research should therefore consider both
positive and negative reviews to capture a more understanding of guest-employee interactions.
Such analysis would offer a more holistic view of the competencies required of hospitality
employees. Furthermore, a comparative investigation across different hotel types (e.g. budget hotel,
