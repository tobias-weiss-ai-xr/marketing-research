 This study investigates the influence of Personalization (PN), Cognitive
Customer Experience (CCX), Emotional Customer Experience (ECX),
and Omnichannel Integration Quality (OIQ) on Customer Satisfaction
(CS) and Customer Stickiness (CK) in the Indonesian modern retail
sector, with CS as a mediating construct. A quantitative cross-sectional
design was employed, collecting data from 240 omnichannel retail
consumers in Indonesia and analyzing it using Partial Least Squares
Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM) with a two -stage higher -
order construct approach in SmartPLS 3. The findings show that ECX
and OIQ significantly drive CS, while CCX, OIQ, and CS
independently shape CK. PN did not significantly influence either
outcome. All satisfaction -mediated indire ct pathways were not
supported, suggesting that CS functions as a direct retention lever
rather than a universal mediator in omnichannel retail contexts.

1. INTRODUCTION
The rapid expansion of
omnichannel retailing has fundamentally
restructured global commerce, yet it has
introduced a critical strategic dilemma,
while digital integration broadens consumer
reach, it drastically lowers switching
barriers, fostering pervasiv e brand -
switching behavior and eroding
conventional customer loyalty [1]. In hyper-
competitive emerging markets like
Indonesia, where modern retail formats
must constantly counter both low barrier-to-
exit digital platforms and resilient informal
retail s ectors, relying on transactional
incentives and price wars is no longer
financially viable or sustainable [2], [4].
Consequently, understanding and securing
Customer Stickiness (CK), defined as the
sustained, habitual disposition of consumers to
repeatedly engage with and remain anchored to
a specific retail ecosystem despite negligible
switching costs, has become an imperative
survival determinant rather than a mere
relational objective [3], [4]. Despite its strategic
urgency, conventional retail models frequently
fail to explain how retailers can cultivate such
deep-seated psychological and behavioral
attachment, particularly within complex
omnichannel touchpoints where offline and
online interactions intersect [4], [5].
Deciphering the underlyin g architectural
drivers that elevate transactional interactions

into persistent consumer stickiness thus
represents an urgent empirical priority [4].
Three constructs are theorized as
central drivers of this process.
Personalization (PN), the tailoring o f
products, services, promotions, and
interfaces to individual consumer
preferences and behaviors, encompassing
product recommendations, dynamic
pricing, and individualized service delivery,
raises perceived switching costs by creating
an accumulated sense  of preference
alignment that competitors cannot easily
replicate [6], [7], [8]. Customer Experience
(CX), understood as the holistic, subjective
response of consumers to their interactions
with a firm [9], is increasingly disaggregated
into Cognitive Customer Experience (CCX),
a rational, utilitarian evaluation of
functional quali ty and convenience, and
Emotional Customer Experience (ECX), a
hedonic, affective response and experiential
pleasure, a distinction consistent with the
Stimulus–Organism–Response (S -O-R)
paradigm, in which environmental stimuli
trigger internal cognitive and affective states
that in turn drive behavioral outcomes [2],
[5], [6]. Omnichannel retailing itself involves
the synergistic management of channels and
touchpoints to optimize the shopping
experience and performance across channels
[1], with the  key distinction from
multichannel retailing lying in the degree of
channel integration achieved [10], [11].
Omnichannel Integration Quality (OIQ)
captures the seamlessness and consistency
of service across a retailer’s offline, online,
and mobile chan nels, reflecting structural,
operational, and informational consistency
across channels [12], [13], and is theorized to
amplify the effectiveness of both PN and CX
by creating a coherent, frictionless retail
ecosystem [1], [4], [14]. In this study, OIQ is
modeled as a higher -order formative
construct composed of Responsiveness,
Integrated Interaction Quality, Fulfillment,
and Efficiency [3], [15].
Despite this growing literature,
three gaps remain. First, PN, CX, and OIQ
have generally been ex amined as isolated
constructs rather than as interconnected
components of an integrated retail value
architecture [12], [14]. Second, although
Customer Satisfaction’s mediating role is well
documented in general marketing contexts [13],
its specific function as a psychological bridge
between omnichannel stimuli and the long -
term behavioral state of stickiness remains
underexplored [8]. Customer Satisfaction (CS)
itself  the evaluative judgment resulting from
the congruence between expectations and
perceived performance [16], Is positioned in
this study as a central mediating mechanism in
retail relationship models [13], [17], [18], while
CK extends beyond conventional loyalty to
capture active, habitual engagement resistant to
competitive swi tching, driven jointly by
constraint-based mechanisms (switching costs,
habit) and dedication -based mechanisms
(affective commitment, trust) [4], [5]. Third,
most prior evidence originates from developed-
market contexts, limiting its applicability to
developing economies such as Indonesia,
where formal and informal retail coexist and
digital adoption trajectories differ substantially
[4], [6], [7].
This study addresses these gaps by
developing and empirically testing an
integrated model grounded i n S -O-R
framework, which explains that environmental
stimuli shape consumers’ internal
psychological states, which in turn drive
behavioral responses [10], and in Expectation –
Confirmation Theory (ECT), which holds that
satisfaction arises when perceived performance
meets or exceeds prior expectations [17].
Together, these theories position PN, CCX,
ECX, and OIQ as stimuli (S), CS as the internal
evaluative state (O), and CK as the behavioral
response (R). Building on this framework, the
study aims to: ( 1) examine the direct effects of
PN, CCX, ECX, and OIQ on CS and CK; (2)
evaluate the mediating role of CS between these
antecedents and CK; and (3) generate empirical
evidence from Indonesia’s modern retail sector
to enrich the omnichannel stickiness lite rature
with developing -economy insights. By
empirically validating these relationships
within the Indonesian modern retail sector, this
study contributes both to the theoretical
refinement of the S -O-R/ECT sequence and to
practical guidance for retail mana gers

2. LITERATURE REVIEW
2.1 Direct Effect of Antecedents on
Customer Satisfaction
Personalized interactions
reduce pre -purchase friction
and generate a heightened
sense of relevance that
translates into elevated
evaluative satisfaction [2], [7],
[8], [17]. Cognitive experience
quality, operational speed,
system quality, and
navigational convenience
address consumers’ utilitarian
shopping goals, and a
frictionless environment yields
a positive rational assessment
that underlies satisfaction [5],
[6]. Emotional experience
quality, cultivated through
atmospheric design and
empathetic service, shapes how
consumers evaluate overall
service quality, such that
consistent affective comfort
directly enhances satisfaction
[5], [7]. Finally, omnichannel
integration quality seamlessly
harmonizes physical and digital
touchpoints by syn chronizing
pricing, inventory data,
promotional schemes, and post-
purchase fulfillment across all
platforms [14], [15]. When
consumers experience
consistent interaction quality,
responsive support, and
effortless channel transitions
without information al
discrepancy, cognitive friction
is minimized while perceived
service reliability is
substantially elevated [15], [16].
According to Expectation –
Confirmation logic, this
frictionless synergy consistently
meets or exceeds modern
consumers' performance
expectations during their holistic
journey, thereby directly
reinforcing overall customer
satisfaction [14], [16], [19].
Accordingly:
H1: PN has a significant effect
on CS.
H2: CCX has a significant
positive effect on CS.
H3: ECX has a significant
positive effect on CS.
H4: OIQ has a significant
positive effect on CS.
2.2 Direct Effect of Antecedents on
Customer Stickness
PN is theorized to build
stickiness by raising relational
switching costs: as platforms
become deeply customized to
individual users, migrating to a
competitor entail forfeiting this
tailored environment, while
personalized interactions also
cultivate institutional trust that
anchors consumers within the
retailer’s network [4], [8], [17], [19].
Cognitive experience q uality
fosters automated behavioral
habits: a consistently efficient
environment enables low -effort
routines that render alternative
platforms cognitively costly to
adopt, forming a structural
constraint on switching [4], [5],
[19]. Emotional experie nce quality
drives stickiness through affective
bonding, as repeated positive
moods build psychological
attachment that shields consumers
from competitive offers [4], [5],
[20]. OIQ functions as an
infrastructural retention
mechanism, embedding
synchronized channel capabilities
into daily habits and creating an
operational dependency
competitors cannot easily replicate
[3], [12], [14], [21]. Accordingly:
H5: PN has a significant effect

2.4 Customer Satisfaction as a
Mediator
Consistent with the S -O-R
sequence, satisfaction is
theorized as the intervening
evaluative state that converts
stimulus-level antecedents into
stickiness. Personalized
interactions are hypothesized to
generate satisfaction first,
which then catalyzes the shift
from transaction to long -term
commitment [4], [7], [13], [17].
For CX, functional efficiency
and affective pleasure are each
theorized to require prior
institutionalization into a stable
satisfaction state before producing
durable behavioral commitment,
since transient cognitive or
emotional reactions do not
automatically yield stickiness on
their own [4], [5], [6], [18].
Similarly, integrated infrastructure
is expected to build stickiness only
once it translates into a satisfied
shopper who values cross -channel
consistency [3], [13], [14], [21].

3. METHODS
This study employed a quantitative
cross-sectional survey design to examine the
effects of PN, disaggregated CX dimensions,
and OIQ on CS and CK within Indonesia’s
modern omnichannel retail sector. The target
population comprised modern retail
consumers in Indonesia who had purchased
from integrated omnichannel retailers using
both a mobile application and a physical
storefront within the preceding three months.
Respondents were recruited via digital
invitations targeting modern retail platform
users across  urban Indonesia, using
purposive sampling to include active multi -
channel consumers [15].
Primary data were collected through
an online questionnaire employing a 1 –5
Likert scale. We operationalized six primary
constructs using validated multi -item scal es
adapted from prior studies. PN was measured
with five items [7], [8], CCX with three items
[5], [6], and ECX with four items [2], [5]. CS
was assessed using four items [13], [18], while
CK was captured through six items, [4]. OIQ
was s pecified as a higher -order formative
construct built from four first -order
dimensions: Responsiveness (three items),
Efficiency (three items), Fulfillment (two
items), and Integrated Interaction Quality
(two items), each grounded in established
scales [3], [12], [13], [15]. Prior to full -scale
data collection, a pilot test was conducted to
verify the clarity of the instrument and
confirm its preliminary reliability.
A total of 240 valid responses were
obtained. Respondents were predominantly
young and digitally native: 44.6% were aged
17–21, and 55.4% were aged 22 –30; 58.3%
were university students, 26.7% private -
sector employees, and 15.0% held other
professional roles. Mobile -application
interaction was the most prevalent product -
research channel (41.2% via integrated social-
to-app advertising, 7.5% via in -store digital
screens, 6.3% via video-sharing platforms).
Sample adequacy was further
confirmed through an a priori power analysis
conducted in G*Power 3.1.9.7 (linear multiple
regression, R² dev iation from zero, fixed

Data were analyzed using Partial

multiple constructs and multi -stage

measurement model evaluation across two
stages, with Stage 1 assess ing the LOCs

convergent validity, and discriminant
validity, and Stage 2 assessing the formative
HOC through outer weights, their
significance, and outer VIF; structural model

and Q²; and non -parametric bootstrapping
with 5,000 subsamples to assess all
hypothesized direct and indirect paths.

4.3 Discussion
4.3.1 The Personalization –Privacy
Paradox in Omnichannel Retail
The non -significant effect of PN on
CK can be explained by the personalization–
privacy paradox. Although personalization
provides relevant and convenient offers,
consumers may simultaneously perceive
privacy risks, intrusiveness, and reduced
autonomy. Kim and Han [24] show that
situational privacy concerns can trigger  a
personalization backfire effect, weakening the
positive value of personalization. Therefore,
when privacy risks outweigh perceived
benefits, PN fails to generate continued
patronage or long -term behavioral stickiness
(CK). In the daily reality of Indone sian
modern retail, such as interactions within
local minimarkets and supermarkets
shoppers often feel reluctant to share personal
data or grant intrusive app permissions for
targeted discounts, preferring
straightforward transactions that do not
compromise their personal privacy, which
explains why personalization alone fails to
build sustained retail stickiness.

4.3.2 Emotional and Cognitive
Customer Experience: Different Pathways to
Customer Outcomes
The significant effects of OIQ on both
CS and CK indicate that seamless
coordination between online and offline
channels creates immediate and behavioral
benefits at once. Consistent information,
cross-channel continuity, convenience, and
reduced customer effort make the shopping
journey more seamless and reliable, thereby
enhancing satisfaction. At the same time,
these benefits encourage repeated interactions
because customers can move between
channels without disruption or unnecessary
effort. Recent omnichannel research confirms
that channel integration quality strengthens
customer satisfaction, patronage, and loyalty
by improving perceived value and continuity
across touchpoints [27], [28]. In the daily

reality of Indonesian modern retail, shoppers
can utilize omnichannel features to easily
browse promotional catalogs or check stock
availability online via mobile apps before
seamlessly completing their physical
transactions in -store, demonstrating how
friction-free cross -channel coordination
simultaneously boosts their satisfaction and
drives repeated patronage.

4.3.3 Satisfaction as a Direct but
Non-Mediating Pathway to Stickiness
The significant direct effect of CS on
CK, combined with the non -significant
mediation of CS between CCX, ECX, and OIQ
on the one hand and CK on the other, suggests
that retention occurs through multiple
parallel pathways rather than a single
satisfaction-centered route. Satisfaction can
directly encourage continued patronage,
while CX and OIQ appear to influence CK
through their own direct mechanisms, such as
engagement, psychological ownership,
perceived continuity, and habitual behavior.
Satisfaction is therefore an important
predictor of CK but does not function as the
central mechanism linking every antecedent
to retention. This multi -pathway perspective
is consistent with recent omnichannel
research emphasizing direct behavioral
pathways beyond satisfaction [29], [30], [31].
In the daily reality of Indonesian modern
retail, shoppers often return repeatedly
because of direct functional convenience or
efficient store layouts rather than waiting for
an emotional or mediated satisfaction state to
build up, demonstrating that multiple
independent drivers maintain customer
stickiness side by side.

5. CONLUSION
This study examined the influence of
PN, CCX, ECX, and OIQ on CS and CK in
Indonesia's modern omnichannel retail sector,
with CS positioned as a mediating construct.
Using PLS -SEM, the model demonstrated
substantial explanatory power for CS and
moderate explanatory p ower for CK, with
adequate predictive relevance. Among the
hypotheses tested, several were supported:
ECX and OIQ significantly predicted CS (with
ECX showing the strongest effect), while CCX,
OIQ, and CS each significantly predicted CK.
PN showed no signi ficant direct effect on
either outcome, and all four satisfaction -
mediated indirect pathways were not
supported, indicating that CS functions as a
direct rather than a universal mediating
driver of stickiness in this context.
 Theoretically, the study extends the S-
O-R and Expectation -Confirmation
frameworks by showing that customer
stickiness in omnichannel retail is generated
through a multi-pathway architecture of habit
formation, structural dependency, and
evaluative commitment rather than a single
linear stimulus –satisfaction–response
sequence, and identifies boundary conditions
under which technology -enabled
personalization loses its persuasive power
among digitally sophisticated consumers.
Practically, the findings offer retailers in
Indonesia and  comparable emerging
economies an evidence -based basis for
reallocating marketing investment toward
emotional experience design, dual -purpose
omnichannel infrastructure, and
institutionalized satisfaction management as
primary levers for building durable customer
stickiness.
 Despite its theoretical and practical
contributions, several limitations should be
acknowledged. First, the reliance on a cross -
sectional research design utilizing a
purposive sampling approach restricts the
ability to establish strict causal relationships
or observe how customer stickiness evolves
over time, as data were captured at a single
snapshot. Future research should consider
employing longitudinal designs to track these
behavioral shifts dynamically. S econd, the
scope of this study was limited to specific
modern retail formats in Indonesia,
suggesting that future investigations could
validate these findings across broader
geographic regions or alternative retail sectors
