Abstract: This study aims to analyze the role of brand trust as a mediating variable in the
relationship between promotion, electronic word-of-mouth (E-WOM), and price regarding
consumer loyalty toward Hangry culinary products. A quantitative approach was employed,
utilizing primary data collected via questionnaires distributed to 100 Hangry consumers in
Lenteng Agung who had made purchases through digital platforms. Purposive sampling was
used as the sampling technique. Data analysis was conducted using Structural Equation
Modeling–Partial Least Squares (SEM-PLS) with SmartPLS software, involving the
assessment of measurement and structural models, as well as bootstrapping to test for direct
and indirect effects. The results indicate that promotion has a positive but statistically
insignificant effect on brand trust. Conversely, both E-WOM and price have positive and
significant effects on brand trust. Furthermore, brand trust was found to have no significant
effect on consumer loyalty. Regarding indirect effects, brand trust did not mediate the
influence of promotion, E-WOM, or price on consumer loyalty; this was evidenced by p -
values of 0.676, 0.645, and 0.578, respectively. These findings suggest that enhancing
promotion, E-WOM, and price perceptions does not automatically foster consumer loyalty
through brand trust. Therefore, Hangry needs to strengthen other aspects that build consumer
loyalty, specifically customer experience, product and service quality, and the consistency of
the value delivered to consumers.

Keyword: Promotion, Electronic Word of Mouth, Price, Brand Trust, Consumer Loyalty

INTRODUCTION
The growth of technology-driven culinary businesses has made digital platforms
increasingly vital for communication and transactions between companies and consumers. PT
Modular Kuliner Indonesia (Hangry) is a techn ology-based culinary company operating
under a multi-brand restaurant and cloud kitchen model that leverages platforms such as
GoFood, ShopeeFood, and GrabFood. In this landscape, consumer loyalty is crucial, as
consumers face a multitude of product and brand choices.

Brand trust forms the foundation of the relationship between a brand and the consumer.
Amprawati (2024) explains that trust is established when consumers perceive a brand as
honest, reliable, and beneficial. Rianto (2022) identifies customer confidence—specifically
the belief that a brand will not disappoint—as a key factor in a brand's growth and popularity.
Ahmad Fadli (2024) notes that transparency regarding services, pricing, and quality can
foster consumer trust and loyalty.
Promotions serve as a communication channel between companies and consumers.
Munadiyan et al. (2023) explain that promotions are used not only to convey information but
also to influence consumers to purchase or use products based on their needs. Kotler et al.
(2024) define promotion as the activity of communicating, introducing, persuading, and
reminding consumers about products or services. Grewal et al. (2021) further explain that
marketing communication can be executed through advertising, sales promotions, personal
selling, direct marketing, and online marketing.
Electronic Word of Mouth (E-WOM) refers to communication regarding products or
services via the internet. Erislan (2024) describes E-WOM as digital communication—such
as consumer reviews, opinions, or recommendations shared over the internet and social
media. Chrismardani (2023) explains that E-WOM encompasses informal, internet-based
communication regarding the usage or characteristics of products, services, or sellers. In the
context of Hangry, E-WOM manifests as reviews, comments, ratings, and recommendations
on digital platforms.
Price is a critical element of the value proposition, as it reflects the relationship between
the benefits received by the consumer and the costs incurred (Grewal et al., 2021). Kotler et
al. (2022) defines price as the amount consumers are expected to pay in exchange for a
product or service. Ningsih et al. (2024) view pricing as a strategy for determining the selling
price of a product or service to support business objectives and competitiveness.
Relevant research indicates that the relationships between variables are not always
consistent. Adiani et al. (2025) examined E-WOM and brand trust as mediating variables in
the context of culinary products. Made et al. (2025) also investigated the role of brand trust in
mediating the impact of E-WOM on purchasing decisions. Meanwhile, Utami et al. (2023)
studied brand trust, brand image, and promotion within online fruit and vegetable purchasing
platforms. These prior research findings serve as a crucial basis for re-examining the role of
brand trust as a mediator regarding consumer loyalty toward Hangry.
Based on this background, this study aims to examine the influence of promotion, E-
WOM, and price on consumer loyalty, with brand trust serving as a variable mediati.

METHOD
This study employs a quantitative approach based on primary and secondary data
(Sugiyono, 2023). The research was conducted from May to June 2026, focusing on Hangry
culinary consumers in Lenteng Agung. Primary data were collected via a Likert -scale
questionnaire distributed to Hangry consumers.
The sample consisted of 100 respondents, selected b ased on the criteria of being
Hangry culinary consumers in Lenteng Agung and having consumed or purchased Hangry
products via digital platforms within the last three months.
The research variables comprise promotion (X1), electronic word-of-mouth (X2), and
price (X3) as independent variables; brand trust (Z) as a mediating variable; and consumer
loyalty (Y) as the dependent variable. Indicators and operational definitions were formulated
based on the theoretical framework used in the thesis, referencing sources such as Amprawati
(2024), Munadiyan et al. (2023), Erislan (2024), Kotler et al. (2022), Kotler et al. (2024), and
Grewal et al. (2021).
Data analysis was performed using Structural Equation Modeling–Partial Least Squares
(SEM-PLS) with SmartPLS 4. Hypothesis testing was based on a p -value < 0.05 or a t-
statistic > 1.96 at a 5% significance level, consistent with the testing methods employed in
the thesis (Ghozali & Kusumadewi, 2023; Sugiyono, 2023). Mediation testing was conducted
by analyzing the specific indirect effect using bootstrapping.

H1: Brand Trust → Consumer Loyalty
The results of the first hypothesis test indicate that the brand trust variable was rejected,
demonstrating that brand trust has no influence on consumer loyalty. This study aligns with
research conducted by Made et al. (2025), Adiani et al. (2025), and Utami et al. (2023),
which also found that brand trust does not influence consumer loyalty.
This conclusion is supported by the low path coefficient value and a p -value that
exceeds the established significance level.n

H2: Promotion → Brand Trust.
The results of the second hypothesis test indicate that the brand trust variable was
rejected; this demonstrates that the promotion variable has no influence on brand trust. This
study aligns with research conducted by Alyanda & Astuti (2026), Fikri (2025), and Yapputra
& Dahlan (2024), which also found that promotion does not influence brand trust.
This is evidenced by the low path coefficient value or a p -value that exceeds the
established significance level.

H3: Promotion → Consumer Loyalty
The results of the third hypothesis test indicate that the brand trust variable was
rejected; this demonstrates that the promotion variable does not influence consumer loyalty.

(2023), which also found that promotion had no effect on consumer loyalty.
This conclusion is supported by the low path coefficient value, a p-value exceeding the
established significance level, and the significance level itself.

With a p-value of less than 0.05, the Price variable is shown to influence consumer

H8: Promotion → Brant trust → Consumer Loyalty
The hypothesis test result for H8 led to its rejection; thus, it can be concluded that
promotion does not mediate the effect of brand trust on consumer loyalty toward Hangry.
This finding is supported by Sosrobahu and Isa (2025), who found that promotion has not yet
been able to significantly build brand trust. This indicates that promotional activities do not
always result in increased loyalty through brand trust.

H9: Electronic Word of → Brand Trust → Consumer Loyalty
The hypothesis test result for H9 led to its rejection; thus, it can be concluded that
electronic word-of-mouth (e-WOM) does not mediate the effect of brand trust on consumer
loyalty toward Hangry. This finding aligns with Utami et al. (2025), who found that brand
trust does not mediate the relationship between e-WOM and brand loyalty. Although e-WOM
can enhance brand trust, that increase is not strong enough to indirectly drive customer
loyalty.

H10: Price → Brand Trust → Consumer Loyalty
The hypothesis test result led to the rejection of H10 thus, it can be concluded that
Electronic Word of Mouth does not mediate the effect of brand trust on consumer loyalty
toward Hangry. This finding differs from that of Satrio and Putri (2023), who stated that
brand trust mediates the relationship between price and customer loyalty. This discrepancy in
results may be attributed to respondent characteristics, the specific subject of the study, and
consumer perceptions of price which were not strong enough to indirectly foster brand trust
and loyalty.

CONCLUSION
Research indicates that E-WOM and price have a positive and significant impact on
consumer loyalty, whereas brand trust and promotion do not have a significant effe ct. E-
WOM also significantly influences brand trust, while promotion does not. Price has a
significant impact on brand trust. Thus, E-WOM emerges as the strongest factor in enhancing
Hangry's consumer loyalty, whereas promotion has not yet become a primary factor in
shaping loyalty.
It is recommended that Hangry more actively boost E -WOM through Hangry
communities or groups, using them as platforms to share product information, gather reviews
and feedback, and encourage consumers to share positive experiences and recommendations.
Hangry is also advised to maintain pricing that aligns with product quality, ensure price
consistency, and offer special deals or loyalty programs that deliver value to consumers
without compromising product quality.
