# Interior Design as a Driver of Psychological Comfort and User Satisfaction: Evidence from Hotel Environments in Baghdad

343

*
Abstract

This study examines the impact of interior design elements on guests’
psychological comfort and customer satisfaction within the hospitality context o f
Baghdad. Drawing on Servicescape theory and Environmental Psychology, the
study develops and empirically tests a multidimensional model that
conceptualizes interior design as a composite construct comprising lighting, color
schemes, spatial layout, furniture design, and biophilic elements.
A quantitative research design was adopted, and data were collected from 312
hotel guests using a structured questionnaire. The data were analyzed using
Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM) with bootstrapping
procedures to assess the significance of the proposed relationships.
The results reveal that interior design has a significant positive effect on
psychological comfort, which in turn strongly influences customer satisfaction. In
addition, psychological comfort was found to partially mediate the relationship
between interior design and satisfaction, highlighting the underlying
psychological mechanism through which environmental stimuli shape customer
experience.
This study contributes to the li terature by extending Servicescape theory through
the operationalization of interior design as a multidimensional construct and by
providing empirical evidence from an underexplored Middle Eastern context.
From a practical perspective, the findings emphasi ze the importance of adopting
human-centered and biophilic design strategies to enhance guest well -being and
satisfaction in hospitality environments.

1. Introduction

Interior design has evolved into a strategic
component that plays a critical role in
shaping customer experience within
hospitality environments (Kotler, 1973). In
hotel settings, the physical environment is
not merely a functional space but a key
determinant of guests’ perceptions,
emotional responses, and behavioral
intentions (Wakefield & Blodgett, 1996;
Han & Ryu, 2009). Drawing on Servicescape
theory, the built environment —through
elements such as lighting, color schemes,
spatial layout, and furnishings —acts as a
stimulus that influences customer
satisfaction and loyalty (Bitner, 1992).
In recent years, the hospitality sector in
Baghdad has exper ienced significant
growth, leading to increased competition
among hotels. As a result, enhancing guest
experience has become a strategic priority
for hotel operators. One of the most
effective ways to achieve this is through
well-designed interior environments that
address not only functional needs but also
psychological and emotional dimensions. In
this regard, Environmental Psychology
provides a valuable framework for
understanding how environmental stimuli
influence individuals’ emotional states and
overall well-being (Mehrabian & Russell,
1974).
Despite the recognized importance of
interior design in hospitality settings, there
remains a notable lack of empirical
evidence regarding how specific design
elements influence psychological comfort
and customer  satisfaction, particularly
within developing contexts such as
Baghdad. Previous studies have primarily
focused on general environmental factors
without examining the multidimensional
nature of interior design or the underlying
psychological mechanisms that mediate the
relationship between physical
environments and customer outcomes (Han
& Ryu, 2009; Kim & Moon, 2009).
Accordingly, this study seeks to address this
gap by examining the impact of interior
design elements on psychological comfort
and customer satisfaction. More
specifically, it aims to analyze how
psychological comfort functions as a
mediating variable linking environmental
stimuli to behavioral outcomes.
Furthermore, the study intends to provide
practical design recommendations that can
enhance guest experience in hospitality
environments through evidence -based
interior design strategies
 Based on the
above discussion, the study addresses the
following research questions:
1. How do interior design elements
influence psychological comfort in
hotel  environments?
2. What is the impact of psychological
comfort on customer satisfaction?
3. Does psychological comfort mediate
the relationship between interior
design and customer satisfaction?
4. Which interior design elements have
the strongest influence on gu ests’
psychological comfort?

2. Literature Review and Hypotheses
Development
2.1 Theoretical Foundations

The relationship between physical
environments and customer behavior has
been extensively examined within
Servicescape theory, which posits that
envir onmental cues act as stimuli that
shape customers’ cognitive, emotional, and
behavioral responses (Bitner, 1992). From
this perspective, elements such as lighting,
spatial layout, and aesthetic design play a
critical role in influencing perceptions of
serv ice quality and overall satisfaction.This
view is further supported by Environmental
Psychology, which explains how
environmental stimuli evoke emotional
states —such as pleasure, arousal, and
comfort —that subsequently influence
behavioral outcomes (Mehrabian & Russell,
1974). In hospitality contexts, these
emotional responses directly affect guests’
comfort, satisfaction, and intention to
revisit (Han & Ryu, 2009; Jani & Han, 2015).
2.2 Interior Design Elements and
Psychological Comfort

Interior design elements constitute the core
components of the servicescape and
significantly influence users’ psychological
experiences. Lighting has been identified as
a key determinant of mood and well-being,
where appropriate lighting design enhances
emotional states and reduces stress (Wasti,
2025; Sharma & Stafford, 2020).
Similarly, color schemes shape emotional
responses, as harmonious and soft tones
contribute to relaxation, while poorly
selected colors may increase tension and
discomfort (Kim & Moon, 2009; Wu &
Cheng, 2018).Spatial layout also plays a
crucial role by influencing movement,
perceived crowding, and usability. Efficient
spatial organization enhances comfort and
reduces stress (Wakefield & Blodgett, 1996;
Nguyen & Leblanc, 2021). Furthermore,
furniture design contributes to both
physical and psychological comfort through
ergonomics and visual coherence within the
environment (Ali & Amin, 2014).
 Hypothesis 1 (H1)
 Interior design elements
have a significant positive effect on
psychological comfort
2.3 Biophilic Design and Psychological Well-
being

Biophilic Design has emerged as a key trend
in contemporary interior design,
emphasizing the integration of natural
elements into built environments. This
approach is based on the inherent human
affinity toward  nature, suggesting that
exposure to natural elements enhances
psychological well -being (Hwang & Lee,
2021).
Empirical studies confirm that incorporating
natural features—such as greenery, natural
light, and organic materials —reduces
stress, improves mood,  and enhances
overall user experience (Lee & Kim, 2025;
Odeh etal, 2025). In hospitality settings,
biophilic design contributes to creating
restorative environments that improve
guests’ comfort and satisfaction.

2.4 Psychological Comfort and Customer
Satisfaction

Psychological comfort represents a central
construct linking environmental stimuli to
behavioral outcomes. It reflects a state of
relaxation, emotion al balance, and
perceived well -being within a given
environment. Prior research has
demonstrated that psychological comfort
acts as a mediating mechanism between
physical environment and customer
satisfaction (Liu & Jang, 2021).Moreover,
environments that foster positive emotional
states are more likely to enhance customer
satisfaction and loyalty, indicating that the
effects of interior design extend beyond
direct influences to include psychological
processes (Al -Ansi & Han, 2022).
 Hypothesis 3 (H3)
 Psychological comfort
has a significant positive effect on customer
satisfaction

2.5 Direct Effect of Interior Design on
Satisfaction
In addition to its indirect influence through
psychological comfort, interior design may
also have a direct effect on custom er
satisfaction. Previous studies indicate that
well -designed environments enhance
perceived service quality and overall
experience, leading to higher satisfaction
levels (Han & Ryu, 2009; Ali & Amin, 2014).
 Hypothesis 4 (H4)
 Interior design
elements have a significant positive effect
on customer satisfaction

2.6 Mediating Role of Psychological
Comfort
Building on Environmental Psychology,
emotional responses are considered key
mechanisms linking environmental stimuli
to behavioral outcomes (Mehrabian &
Russell, 1974). In this context, psychological
comfort serves as a mediator through
which interior design influences customer
satisfaction.
 Hypothesis 5 (H5)
 Psychological comfort
mediates the relationship between interior
design and customer satisfac tion .
2.7 Research Gap
Despite the extensive literature on
servicescape and environmental
psychology, several gaps remain. First, most
studies have been conducted in developed
countries, with limited attention given to
developing contexts such as the Iraq i
hospitality sector. Second, prior research
has often examined individual design
elements in isolation rather than adopting a
multidimensional perspective. Third, the
mediating role of psychological comfort
remains underexplored in hospitality
environment s. Accordingly, this study
addresses these gaps by developing an
integrated model that examines the
combined effects of interior design
elements on psychological comfort and
customer satisfaction in hotels located in
Baghdad.

3. Methodology

A quantitative research design was
employed to examine the relationships
between interior design elements,
psychological comfort, and customer
satisfaction. Data were collected from 312
hotel guests in Baghdad using a structured
questionnaire based on validated scales
(Han & Ryu, 2009; Ali & Amin, 2014; Liu &
Jang, 2021) and measured on a five-point
Likert scale. A convenience sampling
approach was adopted due to accessibility
considerations.
Data were analyzed us ing Partial Least
Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-
SEM) via SmartPLS 4, which is suitable for
predictive and complex models.
Bootstrapping with 5000 resamples was
conducted to assess the significance of path
coefficients (Hair et al., 2019). Ethic al
considerations were ensured through
voluntary participation, informed consent,
and anonymity of responses.

3. 1.  Population and Sample
The study population comprises hotel
guests in Baghdad, as they represent the
most appropriate group for evaluating the
impact of interior design elements on
psychological comfort and customer
satisfaction. This is due to their direct
interaction with the hotel’s physical
environment, including lighting, spatial
layout, color schemes, furniture, and other
design features.
Baghdad hosts a substantial number of
hotels across different classification levels
(three- to five-star), serving a diverse range
of visitors, including business travelers,
tourists, and religious visitors. For the
purpose of this study, a selection of
op erational hotels was considered to
facilitate data collection from guests.
A convenience sampling technique was
employed due to accessibility
considerations within the hotel
environment. A total of 312 valid
questionnaires were collected and used for
stati stical analysis. This sample size is
considered adequate for Partial Least
Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-
SEM) and is consistent with recommended
guidelines in empirical research (Hair et al.,
2019).

4.Discussion

The findings of this study provide robust
empirical support for the central role of
interior design in shaping psychological

351

comfort and customer satisfaction within
hospitality environments. Consistent wit h
Servicescape theory (Bitner, 1992), the
results confirm that physical environmental
cues significantly influence guests’ cognitive
and emotional responses. However, this
study goes beyond prior research by
demonstrating that interior design operates
as a multidimensional construct with both
direct and indirect effects on customer
outcomes.
The significant relationship between
interior design and psychological comfort (β
= 0.62) aligns with previous studies
emphasizing the role of environmental
stimuli in shaping emotional well -being
(Han & Ryu, 2009; Jani & Han, 2015).
Nevertheless, the strength of this
relationship in the present study appears
higher than that reported in some earlier
works. This discrepancy may be attributed
to contextual differences, particularly the
setting of Baghdad, where variability in
design quality across hotels may amplify
guests’ sensitivity to environmental cues. In
emerging hospitality markets, design
elements may serve as stronger
differentiators compared to more
standardized environments in developed
countries.
The strong effect of psychological comfort
on customer satisfaction ( β = 0.70) is
consistent with prior findings (Liu & Jang,
2021; Al -Ansi & Han, 2022), yet the
magnitude observed here suggests that
emotional well -bei ng plays a more
dominant role than previously assumed.
This finding supports the core assumptions
of Environmental Psychology (Mehrabian &
Russell, 1974), but also indicates that
psychological comfort may function as a
primary driver of satisfaction rather  than
merely a mediating factor.
Interestingly, the direct effect of interior
design on customer satisfaction (β = 0.31)
remains significant, confirming that design
influences satisfaction beyond emotional
pathways. While this finding is consistent
with prior studies (Han & Ryu, 2009; Ali &
Amin, 2014), it also highlights a dual -path
mechanism in which interior des ign
simultaneously shapes both cognitive
evaluations (e.g., perceived quality) and
affective responses (e.g., comfort). This
dual influence has not been sufficiently
emphasized in earlier hospitality research.
The mediation analysis further reveals that
psychological comfort partially mediates
the relationship between interior design
and customer satisfaction. While previous
studies have reported similar mediation
effects (Liu & Jang, 2021), the persistence
of a significant direct path suggests that
existin g theoretical models may
underestimate the complexity of this
relationship. In particular, the findings imply
that interior design should be
conceptualized as both a direct experiential
factor and an indirect psychological trigger,
thereby extending current applications of
Servicescape theory.
From a critical perspective, these results
also raise important considerations
regarding the contextual dependency of
design effects. The prominence of interior
design in this study may reflect the
relatively heterogeneous quality of hotel
environments in developing contexts,
where improvements in design can produce

352

more noticeable impacts on customer
perceptions. This suggests that the strength
of design-related effects may vary across
different market conditions, an issue that
warrants further investigation.
Overall, this study contributes to the
literature by offering a more nuanced
understanding of how interior design
influences customer experience. It
demonstrates that design is not merely an
aesthetic attribute bu t a complex,
multidimensional construct that operates
through intertwined cognitive and
emotional mechanisms. By providing
empirical evidence from an underexplored
context, the study also highlights the
importance of incorporating contextual
factors into hospitality and environmental
psychology research.
5. Conclusion
This study set out to examine the impact of
interior design elements on psychological
comfort and customer satisfaction within
the hospitality context of Baghdad. Drawing
on Servicescape theory and Environmental
Psychology, the study developed and
empirically tested a structural model that
integrates key design dimensions with
psychological and behavioral outcomes. The
findings provide strong empirical evidence
that interior design significantly enhances
psychological comfort, which in turn plays a
critical role in shaping customer
satisfaction. In addition, interior design was
found to exert both direct and indirect
effects on satisfaction, confirming the
presence of a dual -path mechanism
thr ough which environmental stimuli
influence customer experience.
Importantly, the results highlight that
psychological comfort is not merely a
mediating variable but a central explanatory
mechanism linking physical environments
to customer outcomes. This un derscores
the importance of designing hospitality
environments that address both functional
and emotional dimensions of user
experience. Overall, the study
demonstrates that interior design should be
viewed not only as an aesthetic component
but as a strategic and psychological driver
of customer satisfaction in hospitality
settings.
6. Research Contributions
6.1 Theoretical Contributions
This study makes several important
contributions to the existing literature.
First, it extends Servicescape theory by
conceptualizing interior design as a
multidimensional construct rather than a
single environmental factor. This provides a
more nuanced understanding of how
specific design elements collectively
influence customer experience.
Second, the study contributes to
Environmental Psychology by empirically
validating psychological comfort as a central
mechanism —rather than a peripheral
mediator—linking environmental stimuli to
behavioral outcomes.
Third, the identification of both direct and
indirect effects of inte rior design on
customer satisfaction advances current
theoretical models by highlighting the

353

coexistence of cognitive and emotional
pathways in shaping customer responses.
Finally, the study enriches the hospitality
literature by providing empirical eviden ce
from an underexplored Middle Eastern
context, thereby addressing the
geographical bias in prior research and
enhancing the generalizability of existing
theories.
6.2.Practical Contributions
From a practical perspective, the findings
offer actionable insights for hotel managers
and interior designers.
First, the results emphasize that investment
in interior design should be treated as a
strategic priority rather than a purely
aesthetic consideration, given its significant
impact on customer satisfaction.
Second, hotel designers should adopt a
human-centered design approach, focusing
on enhancing psychological comfort
through elements such as lighting quality,
color harmony, spatial organization, and
furniture ergonomics.
Third, integrating natural elements through
Biophilic Design can significantly improve
guests’ emotional well-being and overall
experience.
Finally, the study suggests that enhancing
psychological comfort can lead to higher
levels of satisfaction, repeat visits, and
positive word-of-mouth, thereby providing
a sustainable competitive advantage in the
hospitality industry.
7. Limitations and Future Research :
While this study offers important insights
into the role of interior design in shaping
psychological comfort and customer
satisfaction, several limitations should be
considered. First, the focus on hotels in
Baghdad may limit the generalizability of
the findings; however, this context provides
a valuable empirical contribution by
capturing a relatively underexplored
hospitality market, t hereby extending
existing theories beyond their traditional
settings.
Second, the cross-sectional design restricts
the ability to infer causal relationships over
time. Nevertheless, this approach is
consistent with prior servicescape and
hospitality research and provides a robust
foundation for future longitudinal
investigations that can further validate the
observed relationships.
Third, the reliance on self -reported data
may introduce potential response biases.
However, this method remains widely
accepted in customer experience research,
particularly when examining subjective
constructs such as psychological comfort
and satisfaction. Moreover, the consistency
of the results across constructs suggests a
stable pattern of responses.
Building on these limitat ions, future
research is encouraged to adopt cross -
cultural comparative designs to examine
the generalizability of the findings across
different hospitality contexts. Longitudinal
studies would further clarify the dynamic
nature of the relationships identified in this
study. In addition, incorporating broader

environmental variables—such as acoustic
conditions, thermal comfort, and
multisensory design factors—would provide
a more comprehensive understanding of
how interior environments influence
customer ex perience.
