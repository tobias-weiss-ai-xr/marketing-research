ABSTRACT
The hospitality industry increasingly depends on non -financial performance
indicators such as employee relations, service quality, customer experience, and
organisational reputation to maintain competitiveness and sustainability.
However, hospitality estab lishments operate within broader socio -legal
environments that shape organisational practices and stakeholder interactions. In
Uganda, the introduction and enforcement of anti -homosexuality legal and
regulatory frameworks have generated growing debate rega rding their
implications for workplaces, service industries, and organisational performance.
Despite extensive legal and human rights discourse surrounding these
frameworks, limited scholarly attention has been directed toward their influence
on the non-financial performance of hospitality establishments. This systematic
literature review synthesises existing empirical and conceptual literature on the
relationship between anti -homosexuality legal and regulatory frameworks and
non-financial performance outcomes within the hospitality industry. Guided by
PRISMA guidelines. The review analysed 20 peer -reviewed studies from
different databases. The review was guided by three research questions focusing
on employee performance and workplace relations, service delivery and customer
experience, and operational and managerial challenges within hospitality
establishments operating under restrictive legal environments. Findings reveal
that restrictive legal and regulatory environments significantly influence
employee m orale, workplace inclusivity, organisational reputation, customer
perceptions, and service delivery processes. The review further indicates that
hospitality organisations operating within restrictive socio -legal contexts
experience compliance uncertainty, reputational vulnerability, stakeholder
tensions, and operational complexities that affect non -financial performance
outcomes. Additionally, the findings demonstrate that hospitality establishments
increasingly face challenges balancing legal compliance wi th global service
standards, diversity expectations, and customer -oriented practices. The review
identifies substantial empirical and geographical gaps, particularly within Sub -
Saharan Africa and Uganda’s hospitality sector, where limited research has
examined the organisational implications of anti -homosexuality legal
frameworks. The study contributes to the literature on hospitality management,
non-financial performance, and socio -legal regulatory environments by
providing an integrated understanding of h ow restrictive legal systems shape
organisational outcomes within service industries.

INTRODUCTION
The hospitality industry is one of the most
significant contributors to global economic
development, employment generation, tourism
growth, and urban service economies. As a
service-oriented sector, the industry depends
heavily on human interaction, custom er
satisfaction, employee engagement,
organisational reputation, and service quality to
sustain competitiveness and long -term
performance. Consequently, organisational
performance within hospitality establishments is
no longer evaluated solely through financial
indicators such as profitability and revenue
growth, but increasingly through non -financial
performance measures including employee
performance, workplace relations, customer
experience, service delivery quality,
organisational reputation, and operational
efficiency(Mjongwana & Kamala, 2018; Nyarku
& Ayekple, 2019) These non-financial indicators
are particularly important in hospitality
businesses where customer perceptions,
interpersonal interactions, and organisational
image significantly influence sustainability and
market competitiveness.
The growing importance of non -financial
performance has coincided with increasing
attention to the influence of external socio -legal
environments on organisational operations. Legal
and regulatory frameworks shape workplace
policies, organisational behavio ur, stakeholder
relationships, and operational practices across
industries (Huzar, 2025) . In socially sensitive
environments, restrictive legal systems may create
additional organisational pressures that affect
employee relations, service interactions, and
organisational reputation. Hospitality
establishments are particularly vulnerable to such
influences because of their dependence on diverse
employees, international clientele, customer -
oriented service delivery, and public visibility.
Over the past decade, Uganda has attracted
significant international attention due to the
enactment and implementation of anti -
homosexuality legal and regulatory frameworks.
These frameworks, grounded in legal, political,
religious, and cultural narratives , regulate same -
sex relations and influence broader societal
attitudes toward sexual diversity (James, 2021) .
Existing literature has largely focused on the
human rights, political, and socio -cultural
implications of anti -homosexuality legislation,
emphasising issues of discrimination, social
exclusion, and international human rights debates
(Lubaale, 2017; Wahab, 2016). However, limited
scholarly attention has examined how such legal
and regulatory environments influence
organisational outcomes within service -oriented
industries such as hospitality.

Hospitality establishments in Kampala City
operate within a complex socio -legal and
globalised environment characterised by
interactions with local communities, international
tourists, multinational partners, and a diverse
workforce. Kampala City serves as Uganda's
principal commercial, tourism, and administrative
centre and acts as a gateway to the country's
tourism industry. The city hosts a wide range of
hospitality establishments, including hotels,
restaurants, lodges, conference f acilities, and
other tourism -related enterprises that serve both
domestic and international visitors  (Annual
Performance Report Fy 2020/21 , 2020). Uganda
Tourism Board’s  registration, inspection, and
classification exercises have identified Kampala
as one of the areas with the highest concentration
of accommodation facilities in Uganda(ANNUAL,
2019; Annual Performance Report Fy 2020/21 ,
2020). Within such settings, anti -homosexuality
legal and regulatory frameworks may influence
employee morale, workplace interactions,
customer experiences, organisational reputation,
service quality, and stakeholder relationships.
Hospitality organisations may also face
operational challenges associated with legal
compliance, reputational risks, workforce
management, and balancing local regulatory
expectations with international service standards
and inclusivity expectations (Achyldurdyyeva et
al., 2023; Luiz & Spicer, 2021; Sousa-Silva et al.,
2024).
Scholars increasingly argue that restrictive socio-
legal environments influence organisational
climate and stakeholder relations in service
industries. (Luiz & Spicer, 2021)  found that
restrictive legal and cultural environments
undermine organisational inclusivity efforts and
employee well -being within multinational
organisations operating in Africa. Similarly,
(Achyldurdyyeva et al., 2023) established that
supportive workplace environments and inclusive
organisational cultures significantly improve
employee commitment and workplace relations.
Within tourism and hospitality contexts, (Sousa-
Silva et al., 2024)  further demonstrated that
diversity-supportive organisational practices
contribute positively to customer trust,
organisational reputation, and service
experiences. These findings suggest that legal and
regulatory environments may significantly shape
non-financial organisational outcomes within
hospitality establishments.
Despite growing international scholarship on
workplace diversity, customer experience,
organisational inclusivity, and non -financial
performance, substantial empirical gaps remain
within Sub -Saharan Africa and Uganda in
particular. Existing studies predominantly focus
on legal discourse, political narratives, or human
rights dimensions, with limited  attention to
organisational and hospitality-sector implications
of restrictive legal systems. Furthermore, little
systematic synthesis exists regarding how anti -
homosexuality legal and regulatory frameworks
influence non -financial performance outcomes
such as workplace relatio ns, service delivery,
customer experience, and operational
effectiveness within hospitality establishments.
This systematic literature review therefore
examines the influence of anti -homosexuality
legal and  regulatory frameworks on the non -
financial performance of the hospitality industry.
Specifically, the review synthesises literature
concerning the influence of restrictive legal
environments on employee performance and
workplace relations, service delivery and
customer experience, and operational and
managerial challenges within hospitality
establishments. By integrating evi dence from
hospitality management, organisational
behaviour, human rights, and socio -legal
scholarship, the study contributes to a broader
understanding of how legal -regulatory
environments shape organisational performance
within service industries operating in sensitive
socio-political contexts.
METHODOLOGY
This study adopted a systematic literature review
(SLR) approach to examine the influence of anti-
homosexuality legal and regulatory frameworks
on the non -financial performance of the
hospitality industry. The review was guided by the

Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic
Reviews and Meta -Analyses (PRISMA)
framework, which provides a transparent and
rigorous procedure for identifying, screening,
evaluating, and synthesising relevant scholarly
literature. The PRISMA approach was considered
appropriate because it enhances methodological
transparency, reproducibility, and systematic
synthesis of evidence across multidisciplinary
studies.
The review further incorporated elements of
thematic synthesis to enable analytical integration
of empirical and conceptual findings from
hospitality management, organisational
behaviour, human rights, and socio -legal
literature (Clarke & Braun, 2017). This approach
was suitable for the study because the topic
intersects legal, organisational, and service -
industry dimensions that require interpretive
synthesis rather than statistical meta-analysis.
Research Questions
The review was guided by the following research
questions:
• How do anti -homosexuality legal and
regulatory frameworks influence employee
performance and workplace relations in the
hospitality industry?
• What influence do anti -homosexuality legal
and regulatory frameworks have on service
delivery and customer experience in
hospitality establishments?
• What operational and managerial challenges
do hospitality establishments face in
managing non -financial performance under
restrictive legal and regulatory environments?
Search Strategy
A comprehensive literature search was conducted
across multiple academic databases to identify
relevant peer -reviewed studies. The databases
included Google Scholar, Scopus, JSTOR, Taylor
& Francis Online, and ScienceDirect. These
databases were selected because they provide
broad coverage of hos pitality management,
tourism studies, organisational behaviour, human
rights, and socio-legal scholarship.
The search process employed combinations of
keywords and Boolean operators to maximise
retrieval of relevant literature. The major search
terms included: “anti-homosexuality laws” AND
hospitality industry , “Legal and regulatory
frameworks” AND non -financial performance ,
“LGBT workplace inclusion” AND hospitality ,
“organisational reputation” AND restrictive legal
environments, “employee relations” AND anti -
homosexuality legislation , “service delivery”
AND hospitality industry, “customer experience”
AND hospitality performance, and “homophobia”
AND hospitality
Additional manual searches were conducted
through reference lists of selected articles to
identify further relevant studies that may not have
appeared during database searches.
Eligibility Criteria
The inclusion and exclusion criteria were
developed to ensure relevance, quality, and
consistency of the reviewed literature.
Studies were included if they:
• Examined anti -homosexuality laws,
restrictive legal environments, workplace
inclusivity, or socio -legal influences on
organisations;
• Addressed hospitality, tourism,
organisational behavio ur, workplace
relations, customer experience, or non -
financial performance;
• Were published in peer-reviewed journals;
• Were published in English language;
• Were published between 2015 and 2025; and
• Contained empirical, conceptual, qualitative,
quantitative, mixed -method, or systematic
review evidence relevant to the study
objectives.
Studies were excluded if they:

Study Selection Process
The study selection process followed the
PRISMA screening procedure. The literature
search initially identified 232 re cords through
database searching across Google Scholar,
Scopus, JSTOR, Taylor & Francis Online, and
ScienceDirect. After the removal of duplicate
records, 176 studies remained for screening. The
titles and abstracts of these studies were reviewed,
resulting in the exclusion of 112 records that did
not meet the inclusion criteria. Consequently, 64
full-text articles were assessed for eligibility.
Following a detailed evaluation of their relevance,
methodological quality, and alignment with the
study objectiv es, 44 articles were excluded.
Ultimately, 20 studies satisfied all inclusion
criteria and were retained for the final systematic
review and thematic synthesis.  The remaining
full-text articles were assessed for eligibility and
relevance to the research questions.
Quality Appraisal
To ensure methodological quality and credibility,
the selected studies were subjected to quality
appraisal based on relevance, methodological
rigour, clarity of objectives, consistency of
findings, and contribution to the review themes.
Both empirical and conceptual studies were
assessed for  clarity of research design ,
appropriateness of methodology; relevance to the
research questions;  credibility of findings; and
practical contribution to hospitality and
organisational studies.
Studies with weak methodological clarity,
insufficient relevance, or limited contribution to
the review objectives were excluded from the
final synthesis.
Data Extraction and Synthesis
Data extraction was conducted systematically
using a structured review matrix. Information
extracted from each study included: author(s) and
publication year;  study focus ; methodology;
major findings;  limitations; and  identified
research gaps.
The extracted findings were analysed using
thematic synthesis. Similar concepts, arguments,
and findings across studies were grouped into
broader analytical themes aligned with the
research questions. The final synthesis generated
three major themes:
• Employee performance and workplace
relations;
• Service delivery and customer experience;
and
• Operational and managerial challenges within
restrictive legal environments.
The thematic approach enabled integration of
diverse literature while identifying patterns,
contradictions, emerging debates, and gaps within
existing scholarship relating to hospitality
organisations operating under restrictive socio -
legal conditions.

DISCUSSION OF FINDINGS
The analysis generated three major thematic
areas:
Employee Performance and Workplace
Relations
The review found that anti -homosexuality legal
and regulatory frameworks influence employee
performance primarily through their effects on
workplace relations and organisational climate
rather than through direct impacts on job
performance. Across the reviewed studies,
restrictive socio -legal environments were
consistently associated with reduced workplace
inclusivity, psychological insecurity, fear of
social exclusion, and weakened interpersonal trust
(Dobrovic et al., 2018 ; Jin et al., 2024) . These
conditions collectively contribute to lower
employee morale, reduced organisational
commitment, and strained workplace relations, all
of which are critical determinants of non-financial
performance in the hospitality industry.
A recurring pattern in the literature is that legal
frameworks extend their influence beyond
compliance requirements and shape
organisational culture and employee behaviour.
The evidence suggests that when restrictive legal
environments reinforce exclusionary social
attitudes, employees may become less willing to
communicate openly, collaborate effectively, or
participate fully in workpla ce activities. This
creates workplace climates characterised by
uncertainty and caution, which can undermine
teamwork and organisational cohesion. Given that
hospitality operations depend heavily on
interpersonal interaction, communication, and
emotional labour, such workplace tensions are
likely to have significant implications for service-
oriented performance outcomes.
Achyldurdyyeva et al.  (2023) found that
supportive and inclusive workplace environments
contribute positively to employee engagement,
workplace satisfaction, and organisational
commitment. Similarly, Luiz & Spicer (2021)
consistently reported stronger employee well -
being and more positive workplace relations
where organisations promoted supportive cultures
and equitable treatment of employees. In contrast,
restrictive legal environments appear to limit
organisations' ability to fully implement
inclusivity initiatives, thereby creating tensions
between organisational objectives and external
regulatory expectations.
Another important finding is that hospitality
organisations operating in restrictive legal
contexts face an institutional dilemma. On one
hand, organisations are increasingly influenced by
global hospitality standards that emphasise
diversity, employee well -being, and inclusive
workplace practices Lyonga (2021). On the other
hand, they operate within domestic socio -legal
environments that may constrain the
implementation of such practices  (Newburry et
al., 2022). The evidence suggests that this tension

The review also found that literature relating
specifically to Uganda has focused predominantly
on political, legal, and human rights debates, with
limited attention given to workplace and
organisational consequences. James (2021);

empirical evidence explaining how anti -

affect employee relations, workplace climate, and
organisational behaviour within hospitality
establishments in Kampala City. This represents a
significant research gap because employee
performance and workplace relations emerged as
some of the most consistently affected dimensions
of non-financial performance across the reviewed
studies.
Overall, the evidence indicates that anti -
homosexuality legal and regulatory frameworks
influence employee performance indirectly by
shaping workplace relations, organisational
culture, and employee well -being. The review
therefore suggests that the most significant effect
of restrictive legal environments on hospitality
organisations is the creation of workplace
conditions that may weaken employee
engagement, teamwork, and organisational
cohesion, ultimately affecting broader non -
financial performance outcomes.
Service Delivery and Customer Experience
within hospitality establishments.
The review found that restrictive legal and
regulatory environments influence customer
experience indirectly through their effects on
organisational reputation, perceptions of
inclusivity, customer trust, and destination
attractiveness. Across the reviewed studies,
customer experience emerged as a critical non -
financial performance indicator that is shaped not
only by service quality but also by the broader
environment within which hospitality
organisations operate (Godovykh & Tasci, 2020;
Lemon & Verhoef, 2016).
The reviewed evidence indicates that customer
experience is a multidimensional construct
comprising emotional, cognitive, sensory, and
behavioural elements that collectively influence
customer satisfaction and loyalty (Godovykh &
Tasci, 2020). Similarly, customer engagement and
customer journey value were identified as

Operational and Managerial Challenges
The review found that hospitality establishments
operating within restrictive legal and regulatory
environments face managerial challenges arising
from competing institutional, stakeholder, and
organisational expectations. Across the reviewed
studies, a recurring pattern was the difficulty
organisations experience in balancing external
legal requirements with organisational goals
related to employee well -being, customer
satisfaction, reputation management, and long -
term sustainability (Newburry, 2022; Luiz &
Spicer, 2021).
A key finding emerging from the literature is that
restrictive socio -legal environments create
uncertainty for organisations seeking to align
workplace practices with broader stakeholder
expectations. Luiz & Spicer (2021)  found that
organisations operating in restrictive
environments often struggle to reconcile
internationally recognised inclusivity standards
with domestic legal and regulatory requirements.
Similarly, (Newburry, (2022)  demonstrated that
institutional pressures significantly influence
organisational decisions regarding inclusivity
policies and workplace practices. Collectively,
these findings suggest that organisations may face
challenges in developing and implementing
workplace policies that simultaneously satisfy
regulatory obligations and organisational
objectives.
The review further found that organisational
reputation is closely linked to stakeholder -
oriented management practices. Nyarku &
Ayekple (2019)reported that stakeholder-oriented
approaches contribute positively to organisational
reputation, employee commitment, and customer
trust. Likewise, Fatmy et al. (2022)  found that
inclusive organisational practices are associated
with positive organisational outcomes. Taken
together, these studies suggest that managerial
decisions relating to stakeholder engagement and
organisational practices have important
implications for non-financial performance within
hospitality establishments.
Another important finding is that hospitality
organisations operate within increasingly
interconnected tourism systems characterised by
diverse stakeholder expectations. The reviewed
studies indicate that customer trust, organisational
image, employee well -being, and service quality
are closely interconnected dimensions of non -
financial performance (Godovykh & Tasci, 2020;
Lemon & Verhoef, 2016) . Consequently,
managerial decisions affecting one aspect of
organisational performance may have
implications for other performance outcomes.

CONCLUSION AND
RECOMMENDATIONS
This systematic literature review examined the
influence of anti -homosexuality legal and
regulatory frameworks on the non -financial
performance of the hospitality industry. The
review found that these frameworks influence
employee performance primarily thr ough their
effects on workplace relations and organisational
climate. Restrictive socio -legal environments
were consistently associated with reduced
workplace inclusivity, weakened employee
morale, workplace uncertainty, and strained
interpersonal relations. The findings further
indicate that customer experie nce is affected
indirectly through organisational reputation,
perceptions of inclusivity, customer trust, and
destination attractiveness. Together, these factors
influence important non -financial performance
outcomes such as employee engagement, service
quality, customer satisfaction, and organisational
reputation.
The review also found that hospitality
establishments operating within restrictive legal
environments face significant managerial
challenges. Organisations must balance legal
compliance requirements with employee well -
being, customer expectations, organisational
reputation, and long -term sustainability
objectives. However, the review identified
substantial empirical gaps, particularly within
Uganda and Sub -Saharan Africa, where limited
research has examined the organisational
implications of anti -homosexuality legal and
regulatory frameworks. This study therefore
contributes to hospitality management scholarship
by demonstrating that legal and regulatory
environments are important contextual factors
shaping non -financial performance outcomes
within hospitality organisations.
To recommend, hospitality establishments should
strengthen workplace management practices that
promote professionalism, employee well -being,
and positive workplace relations. Since the review
found that restrictive socio -legal environments
influence perfor mance through workplace
climate, managers should prioritise effective
communication, conflict management, employee
support systems, and organisational practices that
foster teamwork and employee engagement. In
addition, hospitality organisations should invest in
customer-centred service strategies that
strengthen customer trust, organisational
reputation, and service quality, as these emerged
as critical determinants of non -financial
performance.
Policymakers, tourism authorities, and hospitality
training institutions should provide guidance and
capacity-building programmes that help
organisations navigate complex socio -legal
environments while maintaining service standards
and organisational sustainability. Future research
should focus on generating empirical evidence
from Uganda and other Sub -Saharan African
contexts to examine how anti-homosexuality legal
and regulatory frameworks influence workplace
relations, customer experience, ma nagerial
practices, and non -financial performance within
hospitality establishments.
