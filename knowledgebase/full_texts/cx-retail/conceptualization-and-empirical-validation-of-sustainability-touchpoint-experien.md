Abstract
Growing environmental concerns and changing consumer expectations have driven the Hospitality Industry to consider
sustainability as their strategic focus. However, limited research has been conducted to examine how customers’
experience sustainability across various service interactions. This study addresses this gap by conceiving Sustainable
Touchpoint Experience (STE) as a higher-order, customer-centric sustainability construct build out of Touchpoints
associated with Food, People, Planet and Governance. The study focuses at mediating role of customer trust and how
STE impacts dining loyalty and customer trust. Using survey data from 400 customers at restaurant businesses in
Eastern India, a conceptual model was tested. The higher-order measurement model, structural connections, mediation
effects, predictive-performance and managerial priorities were evaluated utilizing Importance Performance Map Analysis
(IPMA) and partial least squares structural equation model (PLS-SEM).
The outcomes demonstrate that STE is signiﬁcantly impacted on all four sustainability touchpoints, with the Food
Touchpoint bearing the biggest effect. Dining Loyalty (β = 0.506, p < 0.001) along with Customer Trust (β = 0.506, p <
0.001)   have both been considerably boosted through STE.  Also, Customer Trust had favourable effect upon Dining
Loyalty (β = 0.429, p < 0.001). The connection between STE along with dining loyalty is partly mediated through customer
trust, based on mediation research. The model shows acceptable out-of-sample predictive performance, good model ﬁt
(SRMR = 0.065), and satisfactory explanatory power (R²Customer Trust = 0.256; R²Dining Loyalty = 0.380). Furthermore,
IPMA shows the most signiﬁcant domain for managerial interventions is to improve customer loyalty through Sustainable
Touchpoint Experience and Food Touchpoint.
The study enhances the existing body of information on sustainability within the hospitality sector by introducing and
empirically validating Sustainable Touchpoint Experience as a higher-order experiential sustainability construct. It further
explains the way sustainability experiences contribute to customer loyalty by building trust. For restaurant managers
seeking to develop integrated sustainability initiatives that enhance customer relationships and promote lasting loyalty,
the ﬁndings provide useful insights.
1. Introduction
The Hospitality business is basically experience based, operating within a complex service ecosystem whereby
customers, staff members, society and the natural environment all contribute value creation, requiring constant
innovation (Kandampully et al., 2023; Torvinen et al., 2026). Sustainability is now recognised as a strategic priority for
hospitality companies in light of increasing stakeholders’ expectations, changing customer taste and growing
environmental concerns (Brundtland Commission, 1987; Han et al., 2019; Bordian et al., 2024). As a consequence,
restaurants are increasingly integrating sustainability practices such as ethical sourcing, waste reduction, environmentally
responsible operations, employee well-being and transparent governance to enhance organizational performance while
increasing customer satisfaction, loyalty and return visits (Chen & Shan 2010; Kim et al., 2016; Chaturvedi et al., 2024;
Theodoraki et al., 2024;).
In line with customer experience theory, customers from evaluations using a series of interconnected touchpoints
encountered before, during and after service consumption, integrating these interactions into an overall assessment of
the service provider rather than evaluating individual practices in isolation (Verhoef et al., 2009; Lemon & Verhoef, 2016;
Voorhees 2017; Aldulaimi et al., 2025). Extending the perspective, customers are more inclined to experience
sustainability through multiple service interactions during their whole journey (Kandampully et al., 2024). However
sustainability as a complete customer experience that differs across service touchpoints has attracted less consideration
than the concept of sustainability from an organizational and operational perspectives with an emphasis on green
practices, corporate social responsibility and environmental performance. (Pantouvakis & Gerou, 2022; Moliner-Tena et
Page 2/25

al., 2023; Bonfanti et al, 2025). Addressing this gap is essential for establishing customer centric understanding of
sustainability in Hospitality.
In consequence, there remains three major research gaps. First, customer focused sustainability experiences across
service touchpoints received little consideration in hospitality sustainability research. Second, less is understood
regarding the way sustainability experience inﬂuence loyalty among customers, particularly the mediating role of
customer trust (Morgan & Hunt, 1994). Third, there exists insuﬃcient data regarding which sustainable touchpoints
sustainability touchpoints should be assigned precedence so as to maximize customer relationship outcomes, given the
fact that environmental initiatives require substantial managerial effort. In order to address these gaps, this study
develops and assesses the Sustainability Touchpoint Experience (STE), a higher order customer centric sustainability
construct build out of Touchpoints associated with Food, People, Planet and Governance. It further investigates the way
STE impacts dinning loyalty and trust among consumers.
Third, given the fact that there is no suﬃcient information to support managerial commitment towards demand for
sustainability programs, as to which sustainability touchpoint should be assigned importance in an effort to maximise
customer relationship outcomes. In order to address these gaps, this study develops and assesses the Sustainable
Touchpoint Experience (STE), a higher order customer centric sustainability construct build out of Touchpoints
associated with Food, People, Planet and Governance. Additionally, it investigates at the way STE impacts dinning Loyalty
and trust among customers.
Four major contributors to the discipline in hospitality sustainability research have been provided in this study. In order to
generate a uniﬁed, customer cantered sustainability experience, it ﬁrst conceptualizes and empirically validates
Sustainability Touchpoint Experience as a hierarchical higher -order construct that includes Food, People, Planet and
Governance Touchpoints. Second, by emphasizing the mediating role of customer trust, it explains how long- lasting
sustainable experience translates into behavioural outcomes. Third, the study enhances traditional PLS-SEM applications
beyond explanatory modelling by incorporating managerial priorities through IPMA and predictive assessments using
PLSpredict (Hair et al., 2022). Finally by identifying the sustainability touchpoints that should be accorded importance so
as to foster long-term dining loyalty and customer trust, the ﬁndings give restaurant managers helpful recommendations.
Therefore, this study attempts to tackle three research questions:
1. What would be the most effective approach to conceptualise and execute a Sustainability Touchpoint Experience
(STE) as a higher order sustainability construct?
2. Does the Sustainability Touchpoint Experience impact customer loyalty and trust?
3. Does the relationship between dinning loyalty and Sustainability Touchpoint Experience rely on customer trust?
To address this issue, data collected from customers at traditional dining establishments in West Bengal and Odisha,
India was examined utilizing the PLS SEM software. The study offers three important contributions to the literature on
hospitability sustainability. Sustainability Touchpoint Experience is initially presented as a novel higher order formative
construct. Furthermore, the multidimensional structure of STE was veriﬁed by science. Lastly, it shows that customer
trust works as a procedure which transforms sustainability experiences into outcomes linked to customer loyalty.
This study offers three speciﬁc results to the realm of hospitality sustainability research. First, it identiﬁes and validates
Sustainable Touchpoint Experience (STE) as a more advanced customer centric sustainability construct comprising of
Food, People, Planet, Governance Touchpoints. Second it investigates the role of consumer conﬁdence in mediating the
connection among sustainability experiences with customer loyalty. Third, utilizing Importance-Performance Map
Analysis (IPMA), it determines the sustainability touchpoint having the greatest managerial capacity to boost customer
loyalty.

2. Review of Literature, Conceptual Framework and Hypotheses Development
2.1 Sustainability and Customer Experience in Hospitality
As customers expect businesses to demonstrate ethical, social, and environmental consciousness, sustainability has
grown into an essential strategic concern for hospitality organizations. Recent research suggests that customers tend to
be increasingly assessing sustainability through their direct service experiences, while prior hospitality studies primarily
focused on operational sustainability initiatives such as waste reduction, energy eﬃciency and sustainable sourcing (Han
et al., 2019; Su et al., 2021). As a consequence, sustainability is has become recognised as an experiential attribute that
impacts customer opinions and behavioural responses as opposed to an isolated organizational practise. Thus,
sustainability in customer experience is deﬁned as customer engagement which is based on economic, social and
environmental sustainability and is derived through sensations, feelings, emotions and actions evoked by sustainable
stimuli. (Signori et al., 2019; Calza et al., 2023).
Customers evaluation is developed through a series of interactions which happen across different points of contact
throughout the consumption journey, based on the literature on consumer experiences (Lemon and Verhoef, 2016; Calza
et al., 2023). These touchpoints are essential instances where customers receive information, develop impressions and
form judgements regarding service providers. Customer experiences in hospitality settings are inﬂuenced through
interactions with physical environments employees related merchandise, organizational communications and service
processes (Kandampully et al., 2018). Customers collected views across multiple sustainable touchpoints are therefore
likely to shape sustainability related experiences instead of single sustainability endeavour.
While scholarly interest in sustainable hospitality is growing, majority of the research done currently has concentrated on
sustainability elements individually. Environmental practices (Han et al., 2019), ethical sourcing (Kim & Hall, 2020),
employee welfare (Su et. al., 2021), and corporate social responsibility (Martinez & Rodriguez del Bosque, 2013) were all
examined as separate entities. Customers’ complete experiences regarding sustainability throughout their service journey
are not entirely comprehended through fragmented approaches. These disadvantages highlight the importance of
designing the sustainable experience in a more integrated and customer centric approach.
Existing hospitality sustainability research has mainly concentrated on organizational practices and operational
outcomes, with minimal emphasis on how customers experience sustainability throughout the service journey.
2.2 Sustainability Touchpoint Experience (STE) as a Higher-Order
Customer-Centric Sustainability Construct
Touchpoint analysis has become increasingly recognised in the Hospitality Industry as an essential tool for establishing
and optimizing sustainable experiences and further creating strategies to support businesses (Vajpai et al., 2025; Cui et
al., 2025; Baidya et al., 2023). This particular study offers Sustainable Touchpoint Experience (STE), a term that captures
customers’ overall opinion of sustainability which are inﬂuenced through interactions with sustainability related service
touchpoints when dining. STE focuses on how customers perceive and comprehend sustainability through their
interactions with a restaurant, in contrast with traditional sustainability frameworks that focus on organizational
practices.
The touchpoint theory of customer experience which argues that customers evaluate companies according to cumulative
experiences across multiple interactions and responses rather than single service attributes which serves as the basis of
the concept of STE (Lemon and Verhoef, 2016; LaSelle & Britton 2003; Meyer & Schwager 2007; Liu et al., 2024). Based on
this perspective, customers consider sustainability important when it is experienced through tangible and observable
interactions during service utilization (Verhoef et al., 2009; Becker & Jaakkola, 2020)
Page 4/25

Whenever customers visit traditional dining establishments consumers are presented with a number of sustainability
related signals. Their views regarding the authenticity and effectiveness of an organization’s sustainability commitment
are shaped with these interactions considered collectively. Food Touchpoint, People Touchpoint, Planet Touchpoint and
Governance Touchpoints constitute the four sustainability touchpoint elements based on which STE is characterized as
an experiential outcome.
The Sustainability Touchpoint Experience (STE) refers to the entire customer experience developed through interactions
with sustainability related touchpoints throughout the dining journey. STE is conceived as a higher order formative
construct having four lower order corresponding to dimensions: food touchpoints, people touchpoints, planet touchpoint
and governance touchpoint.
2.3. Dimensions of the Higher-Order Sustainability Touchpoint
Construct
2.3.1. Food Touchpoint as a Dimension of STE
One of the most apparent instances of environmentally friendly procedures is food, since it is the primary service
provided in restaurant settings. Customers are progressively assessing restaurants based on measures taken to
minimize food waste, ethical production procedures, organic and conventional goods, local procurement, and responsible
sourcing (Filimonau et al., 2020; Legrand et al., 2022).
Consumers’ evaluation of service quality and authenticity are directly affected through sustainable food practices, which
provide tangible evidence of an organization’s commitment to sustainability. Based on previous studies consumers think
that food that is ethically and sustainably produced offers symbolic and functional value (Hartmann & Siegrist, 2017; Han
et al., 2019). Consumers’ sustainability experiences are thus expected to be signiﬁcantly strengthened by food related
sustainability efforts.
H1a: Food Touchpoints positively inﬂuence Sustainability Touchpoint Experience
2.3.2 People Touchpoints as a Dimension of STE
Employees possess a signiﬁcant part in shaping customer experiences because they constitute the main point of contact
between organizations and customers (Bitner et al., 1990). In service environment, employees who emphasize on
sustainability demonstrate sustainability values through their actions, knowledge and interactions. Consumers often turn
to frontline workers to assess sustainability initiatives and assess the company’s credibility (Kim et al., 2017). Employee
engagement in sustainability initiatives, moral conduct and responsible service delivery, contributes to customers’
perceptions of the organizations’ commitment to sustainability. Employee related sustainability practices were proven in
research to signiﬁcantly boost positive customer evaluations and perceived authenticity (Berezan et al., 2014; Fatma et
al., 2018).
H1b: People Touchpoint positively inﬂuence Sustainability Touchpoint Experience.
2.3.3 Planet Touchpoint as a Dimension of STE
Planet Touchpoints include environmentally friendly practices that customers directly observe during service encounters.
These involve waste management systems, energy eﬃcient operations, waste conservation measures, recycling
initiatives, sustainable packaging, and environmentally responsible facility management.
Environmentally sustainable actions provide obvious signals of company’s responsibility and inﬂuence customer
perceptions of environmental stewardship (Han et al., 2019). Customers are increasingly requesting hospitality
companies to minimize environmental impacts and actively contribute to sustainability objectives (White et al., 2019).

Visible environmental behaviour may therefore enhance customers perception of sustainability performance and can
contribute to their overall experience.
H1c: Planet Touchpoints positively inﬂuence Sustainability Touchpoint Experience
2.3.4 Governance Touchpoints and Sustainability Touchpoint
Experience
Governance Touchpoints includes company policies, ethical standards, transparency processes, certiﬁcations and
accountability mechanisms that show an organizations commitment to sustainability. Eﬃcient governance enhances
organizational credibility by reducing information asymmetry and improving stakeholder conﬁdence (Freeman et al.,
2021).
Customers are increasingly seeking evidence that companies should actually implement sustainability commitment
rather than just making symbolic claims (Delmas & Burbano 2011). Transparency in sourcing processes, sustainability
reporting, moral conduct and compliance initiatives may all enhance perceptions of authenticity and trustworthiness. As
a consequence, governance related sustainability practices have the potential to positively beneﬁt sustainability results.
H1d: Governance Touchpoints positively inﬂuence Sustainability Touchpoint Experience.
As a consequence, the three signiﬁcant gaps are present. First, sustainability research in hospitality has paid insuﬃcient
attention to customer-centric sustainability experience across service touchpoints. Second, the ways in which
sustainability experiences foster customer loyalty remain neglected especially the signiﬁcance of customer trust. Third,
there exists no proof on which sustainability touchpoint provide the greatest managerial inﬂuence to enhance customer
experiences. Addressing these ﬂaws this study develops and validates Sustainability Touchpoint Experience (STE), a
higher-order customer centric sustainability construct and examines its effect on consumer conﬁdence and dining loyalty.
2.4. Sustainability Touchpoint Experience and Customer Trust
Trust involves customer’s willingness to rely on organization based on expectations regarding competence, integrity and
empathy (Morgan & Hunt, 1994). Trust becomes particularly important in sustainability contexts as customers often
cannot directly verify sustainability commitments and thus rely on company credibility (Gefen et al., 2003)
Based on customer experience studies, positive experiences improve trust by decreasing uncertainty and increasing
conﬁdence in corporate behaviour (Verhoef et al., 2009; Klaus and Maklan, 2013; Riaz et al., 2024). Similarly, sustainability
research demonstrates that authentic sustainability practices enhance stakeholders trust and corporate credibility
(Fatma et al., 2018; Vlachos et al., 2009)
Customers are more likely to perceive the organization as responsible, transparent and trustworthy when they encounter
environmentally conscious touchpoints on a regular schedule throughout their dining experiences. As a consequence
positive sustainability experiences should boost customer trust.
H2: Sustainability Touchpoint Experience positively inﬂuences Customer Trust.
2.5. Sustainability Touchpoint Experience (STE) and Dining Loyalty
Dining Loyalty involves a customer’s commitment to return to a restaurant and promoting it to others, despite the
availability of other dining options. In hospitality research, loyalty is generally regarded as a complex concept that
incorporates both behavioural intentions such as revisit intentions and attitudinal dimensions, such as positive word-of-
mouth and emotional attachment (Oliver 1999; Han et al., 2020). Given the restaurant industry’s intense rivalry,
maintaining client loyalty remains a crucial objective for service providers seeking long-term proﬁtability and a
sustainable competitive advantage.

Based on customer experience literature, loyalty develops as a consequence of customer cumulative evaluations of their
interaction with a service provider across multiple channels during the customer journey (Lemon and Verhoef, 2016;
Bonfanti 2026). Rather than being affected by personal experience, loyalty emerges when customers consistently
experience positive interactions with a service provider. Such experiences elicit favourable cognitive and emotional
responses, boosting customer’s desire to revisit and recommend the service provider to others (Verhoef et al., 2009;
Becker & Jaakkola, 2020). As a consequence customer experience is widely recognised as a important indicator of
customer loyalty in hospitality and service settings.
In recent years, sustainability has emerged as an increasingly important aspect of the customer’s experience. Based on
sustainability consumption literature, consumers are becoming more conscious about environmental and social issues
which means that they increasingly choose companies whose values align with their own sustainability concern (White
et. al., 2019). Customers often think about sustainability related practices such as responsible sourcing, waste reduction
initiatives, employee welfare initiatives, and transparent governance when forming overall perception of a service provider
(Font & McCabe, 2017). As sustainability becomes more incorporated in-service delivery, customers are going to come
across multiple sustainability related touchpoints during their dining experiences.
Sustainability Touchpoint Experience (STE) evaluates customers’ general impressions of sustainability according to
interactions with Food, People, Planet and Governance Touchpoint. From a customer journey perspective, these
sustainability related interactions continue to the overall dining experience and affect how customers think of the
restaurant. Positive sustainability experience may enhance customers’ perception of authenticity, responsibility and value
congruence, resulting in a greater incentive to maintain their relationship with the restaurant (Bhattacharya & Sen, 2004;
Martinez & del Bosque, 2013).
Previous study has shown that sustainability actions can positively inﬂuence customers’ behaviour patterns, including
returning to, referring and staying loyal (Han et al., 2009; Moise et al., 2021). Customers are more likely to develop
favourable feelings regarding and organization and show stronger loyalty intentions when they perceive its sustainability
efforts as signiﬁcant and trustworthy. As a consequence, the Sustainability Touchpoint Experience is expected to serve a
signiﬁcant part in promoting dining loyalty in restaurant settings.
Accordingly, the following Hypothesis is proposed:
H3: Sustainability Touchpoint Experience positively inﬂuences Dining Loyalty.
2.6. Customer Trust and Dining Loyalty
Customer Trust is a key component of long-term relationship between customers and service providers. It symbolises
customers’ trust in a businesses’ reliability, integrity and ability to uphold its promise (Morgan & Hunt, 1994). In
Hospitality settings, where service outcomes tend to be intangible and erratic, trust plays an important role in inﬂuencing
customer behaviour.
Based on Commitment-Trust Theory, conﬁdence reduces perceived risk and encourages consumers to remain loyal with
service providers, which renders it an essential indicator of relational continuity. (Morgan & Hunt, 1994). Based on
relationship marketing research, trust was an important component in customer loyalty, leading to purchase repeats and
positive word of mouth.
In restaurant settings, customers who trust the restaurant are more likely to visit again and recommend it to others. Prior
research shows a positive relationship between trust and loyalty in hospitality contexts (Chaudhuri & Holbrook 2001;
Martinez & del Bosque, 2013). As consequence higher levels of customer trust are expected to increase dining loyalty.
H4: Customer Trust positively inﬂuences Dining Loyalty

2.7. The Mediating Role of Customer Trust
Based on Relationship Marketing Theory, which has high relevance in research regarding sustainable competitive
advantage, trust acts as a relationship mechanism through which company actions inﬂuence long term behavioural
outcomes (Madhavaram 2024; Morgan & Hunt, 1994). As a consequence, sustainability experiences may impact
customer loyalty both directly and indirectly through the development of trust.
Positive sustainability experiences indicate business responsibility, consistency, and authenticity that increases customer
trust. Trust reduces perceived risk and improves customers’ motivation to maintain long term relationships with the
company. Prior studies also discovered trust as a mediator among sustainability initiatives with customer behavioural
results (Fatma et al., 2018; Rather et al., 2019).
Therefore, Customer Trust is expected to mediate the relationship between Sustainability Touchpoint Experience and
Dining Loyalty.
H4: Customer Trust mediates the relationship between Sustainability Touchpoint Experience and Dining Loyalty.
2.10. Conceptual Framework
Based on the previous theoretical justiﬁcation the proposed conceptual framework deﬁnes Sustainable Touchpoint
Experience as the main experiential mechanism through which sustainability related touchpoints inﬂuence customer
relationship outcome. Food, People, Planet, and Governance Touchpoints are shown as antecedents of STE, and
Customer Trust acts as an intermediatory connecting STE and Dining Loyalty.
3. Methodology
3.1. Research Design
According to the Theoretical framework outlined in preceding sections, Sustainability Touchpoint Experience (STE) is
characterized as the basic experiential mechanism through which sustainability related touchpoint inﬂuence customer
relationship outcomes. Food, People, Planet and Governance. Touchpoints were the dimensions of STE, and Customer
Trust serving as an intermediatory among STE with Dining Loyalty. Partial Least Squares Structure Equation Model (PLS-
SEM) version 4, which is suitable for estimating hierarchical component models and assessing both exploratory and
predictive links was utilized to evaluate the suggested model. (Hair et al., 2022).
3.2. Study Context
The empirical investigation was conducted in the restaurant business in eastern India. The restaurant industry provides
appropriate setting for studying sustainable experiences because customers encounter sustainability related practices
throughout their dinning experience, including food sourcing, staff relationship, environmental initiatives and
organizational transparency.
The study focuses on restaurant customers in the Indian states of West Bengal and Odisha. Both states have witnessed
substantial growth in the hospitality and food service industries, as consumer concerns regarding sustainability have
increased. As a consequence this situation offers a perfect environment to investigate how sustainability related
touchpoints impact customer experience, trust and loyalty outcomes.
3.3 Sampling and Data Collection
Between December 2005 and March 2026, an organised online questionnaire was implemented to collect data.
Respondents needed to satisfy two screening criteria: (1) be at least 18 years of age and (2) have visited a restaurant
Page 8/25

within the past six months.
Purposive sampling was employed to be sure respondents have suﬃcient dining experience to assess touchpoints
related to sustainability. Initially 432 responses were collected. Four hundred responses based on eligibility were stored
for analysis after screening for incomplete submission and inconsistent replies. The ﬁnal sample size exceeded the
minimum limit for PLS-SEM and was considered adequate for estimating a hierarchical component model of comparable
complexity (Hair et al., 2022).
3.4 Measurement Instrument
There were actually two parts in the questionnaire. The demographic data provided by respondents including gender, age,
income, education and consumption of dietary intake was collected in the ﬁrst section. Food touchpoints, people
touchpoints, planet touchpoints, governance touchpoints, customer trust and dining loyalty were each discussed in the
second section. All Items were obtained from established scales in the literature and adapted the restaurant industry. A
ﬁve point Likert scale featuring “1” indicating “strongly disagree” and “5” representing “strongly agree,” was utilized to
record responses.
After the initial survey, academic experts and hospitality practitioners reviewed the questionnaire on clarity, relevance and
content validity.
3.5. Data Analysis Procedure
Smart PLS 4 was implemented to evaluate the data. As recommended for hierarchical component models, the analysis
was done in two phases. The ﬁrst criteria utilized to evaluate the measurement model are indicator reliability, internal
consistency reliability, convergent validity and discriminant validity. The structural model was subsequently evaluated
utilizing the path coeﬃcient, coeﬃcient of determination (R²), effect sizes (f²), predictive relevance (Q²), and ﬁt metrics.
The signiﬁcance of the suggested associated connections was assessed using bootstrapping method with 5,000
subsamples.
3.6. Assessment of the Higher-Order Construct
A Sustainability Touchpoint Experience (STE) was developed as a second-order formative construct featuring four ﬁrst
order reﬂected dimensions: Food Touchpoints, People Touchpoints, Planet Touchpoint, and Governance Touchpoints. The
disjoint two-stage method was employed as suggested for reﬂective-formative hierarchical component models (Becker et
al., 2012; Sarstedt et al., 2019). The constructs of the higher order was evaluated by analysing indicator weights,
signiﬁcant levels, and multicollinearity using variance inﬂation factor (VIF)e values.
3.7. Mediation Analysis
Utilizing bootstrapping methods in Smart PLS, the mediating role for customer trust in the relationship between
Sustainability Touchpoint Experience (STE) and dining loyalty was assessed. Applying established PLS SEM guidelines,
5000 bootstrapping resamples were utilized to assess the signiﬁcance of the direct, indirect and total effects. By
comparing the indirect effects to the total effect, the Variance Accounted For (VIF) statistics was calculated to evaluate
the level of mediation. The resulting VAF value was subsequently utilized to classify the mediation effect as full, partial, or
absent.
3.8. Predictive Assessment
PLS predict was utilized to evaluate the model’s predictive ability in addition to exploratory evaluations. To evaluate out-
of-sample predictive performance, the PLS model’s prediction errors were compared to those of a linear benchmark
model in compliance with current PLS-SEM guidelines.

3.9. Importance- Performance Map Analysis (IPMA)
Importance-Performance Map Analysis was employed to support the structural model assessment and provide relevant
managerial insights. Dining Loyalty was chosen as the target construct, alongside Food Touchpoints, People Touchpoints,
Planet Touchpoints, and Governance Touchpoints acting as predecessor frameworks. IPMA facilitates for the
simultaneous evaluation of construct importance and performance, leading to the recognition of sustainability
touchpoints that offer the greatest managerial leverage to boost customer loyalty (Ringle and Sarstedt, 2020).

4.3. Assessment of the Higher order construct
Sustainability Touchpoint Experience (STE) was designed as a second-order formative construct that incorporated Food,
People, Planet and Governance Touchpoints. The formative measurement model was evaluated utilizing indicator
weights, statistical signiﬁcance, and collinearity diagnostics in line with a fragmented two-stage approach (Becker et al.,
2012; Hair et al., 2022).
Table 4 reveals signiﬁcant formative weights (ρ < 0. 001) for all four dimensions, indicating distinctive contribution to the
formation of STE. Food Touchpoints has the greatest effect (β = 0.403, t = 12.463), followed by People Touchpoint (β = 
0.373, t = 11.635), Planet Touchpoint (β = 0.359, t = 10.725), and Governance Touchpoint (β = 0.320, t = 9.436). These
ﬁndings demonstrate that customers sustainability experience are inﬂuenced by a number of different sustainability
related interactions throughout the dinning journey, with food-related sustainability practices providing the greatest
inﬂuence.
VIF values were utilized to examine potential multicollinearity among the formative dimensions. The VIF values revealed
zero multicollinearity issues and ranged from 1.152 to 1.283, signiﬁcantly lower than the proposed threshold of 3.3 (Kock,
2015). As a consequence, the higher order construct covers all four dimensions.
Overall, the results support the hypothesis H1a-H1d, showing that Sustainability Touchpoint Experience is a
multidimensional construct consisting Food, People, Planet and Governance Touchpoints.

4.7. Importance-Performance Map Analysis (IPMA)
To add to the structural model results, an Importance-Performance Map Analysis (IPMA) was conducted using Dining
Loyalty as the target construct. While the structural model assesses the signiﬁcance of the relationships amongst the
constructs, IPMA evaluates each construct’s importance (total effect) and performance (average latent variable score),
identifying managerial priorities for improving customer outcome (Hair et al., 2022).
The study constructs performance values and signiﬁcance are presented in the Table 8. Sustainable Touchpoint
Experience (STE) scored the highest signiﬁcance (0.600), indicating that increasing customer overall sustainability
experiences is expected to culminate in the greatest boost in dining loyalty. Customer trust scored second (0.319),
showing its essential importance as a relational mechanism which transforms sustainability experience into customer
loyalty.
Food Touchpoint is the most signiﬁcant among the ﬁrst-order sustainability dimensions (0.222), followed by Planet
Touchpoint (0.156), People Touchpoint (0.154), and Governance Touchpoint (0.112). These ﬁndings are consistent with
the higher-order construct assessment, that demonstrated that Food Touchpoint enjoyed the most signiﬁcant part in the
development of Sustainability Touchpoint Experience. Although the constructs’ performance scores were usually close,
Food Touchpoint has the lowest performance (47.881), whereas Governance Touchpoint has the highest performance
(50.714). This indicates that further improvements in food-related sustainability practices, such as sustainability
sourcing, menu transparency and responsible food management, are going to result in the greatest improvements in
customer sustainability experience and resulting dining loyalty.
Overall the IPMA ﬁndings add to the structural model by transforming statistically signiﬁcant relationships into actionable
managerial priorities. Based on the study, restaurant managers should continue to enhance the Sustainability Touchpoint
Experience while emphasizing on food-related sustainability initiatives to boost customer conﬁdence and dinning loyalty.

5. Discussion
This study aimed to develop and validate a Sustainable Touchpoint Experience (STE) as a higher-order customer-centric
sustainability construct, and also to explore its effect on consumer conﬁdence and dining loyalty in the restaurant
industry. The outcomes provide numerous theoretical and managerial insights into how sustainable initiatives inﬂuence
consumer choices in addition to providing sustainable empirical backing to the proposed conceptual model.
First the ﬁndings conﬁrm that Food, People, Planet and Governance Touchpoints collectively form the Sustainability
Touchpoint Experience (STE), that validates its conception as a hierarchical component model. Food Touchpoint were
found to have been the most accurate measure of STE among these variables. This ﬁnding is in accordance with research
on hospitality that show food is the main service offered by restaurants and is the most apparent illustration of
sustainability practice (Filimonau et al., 2020; Han et al., 2019). Customer evaluations of sustainability performance are
directly affected on their increasing understanding of ethical production, local ingredients, responsible sourcing, and food
waste reduction methods.
Second, the results indicate that customers trust is signiﬁcantly boosted through the Sustainability Touchpoint
Experience. Restaurants are regarded as more trustworthy, transparent and socially aware if they consistently address
and carry out environmentally friendly procedures across all customer touchpoint. These ﬁndings support relationship
marketing theory, which argues that consistent and dependable interactions between companies and their customers
build conﬁdence. Furthermore they complement the theory of sustainability metrics as signs of long-term commitment
and integrity in the organizations.
Third it was found the Dining Loyalty was signiﬁcantly affected immediately through Sustainability Touchpoint
Experience. Customers are more likely to go back to restaurants and recommend others to them when they experience
sustainability throughout their dining experience. This outcome supports studies on customer experience and
sustainable consumption showing that sustainability has evolved beyond an operational practice to a vital component of
value creation and customer relationship management.
The mediation study further demonstrates that the relationship between Dining Loyalty and Sustainability Touchpoint
Experience is partly mediated through Customer Trust. Sustainability Experience boost loyalty indirectly by establishing
trust among customers even when they directly encourage repeat customers. While recognizing that other phycological
mechanism might also have a part in the formation of loyalty the calculated VAF value indicates partial mediation,
indicating that trust is an important means through which long-term experience turn into good behaviour outcomes.
Finally by identifying managerial priorities, the IPMA results extend the structural model. The most important variable in
explaining loyalty is Sustainability Touchpoint Experience, that was subsequently followed by Customer Trust.
Improvements in sustainable food sourcing, quality and transparency are likely to result in the greatest boosts to
customer loyalty, based on Food Touchpoint, who demonstrated the greatest importance among the ﬁrst-order
sustainability dimensions but fairly low performance. On the reverse conjunction managers should ought to concentrate
on improvements in the food-related components of sustainability while preserving visible practices, as Governance,
People and Planet Touchpoints demonstrated relatively outstanding results but lower importance. These ﬁndings
together emphasize the signiﬁcance of combining managerial and explanatory analysis to support environmentally
conscious decision making in the hospitality sector.
6. Theoretical and Practical Implications
6.1. Theoretical Implications
Page 16/25

This distinctive study contributes to the thriving literature on customer experience, sustainability and hospitality in a
broad aspect. Sustainability Touchpoint Experience (STE), a higher-order customer centric sustainability construct, is
initially introduced and empirically veriﬁed. This research deﬁnes sustainability as an integrated customer experience that
results from interactions with Food, People, Planet and Governance Touchpoints, while previous studies have primarily
examined sustainability from organization or operational perspective.
Second by demonstrating that sustainability experiences emerge across many service interactions rather than through
single sustainability initiatives, the study extends the literature on customer journeys and customer experiences. A
theoretically informed framework for integrating multiple sustainability dimensions into one uniﬁed experiential construct
is provided through the hierarchical component model approach.
Third, by identifying Customer Trust as an essential psychological mechanism that links sustainability experience with
customer loyalty, this study advances relationship marketing theory. The mediation analysis demonstrates that
sustainability initiatives impact behavioural results giving a more comprehensive overview of loyalty development in
hospitality settings, directly as well as indirectly through the development of trust. Finally, through the use of Importance-
Performance Map Analysis (IPMA), the study extends beyond hypothesis testing in conventional PLS-SEM applications to
provide strategic ranking of sustainability dimensions. This combination of managerial and explanatory analysis provides
methodological value to sustainability research.
6.2. Practical Implications
The ﬁndings offer plenty of beneﬁcial implications for restaurant managers as well as other hospitality workers. First
rather than becoming a collection of separate environmental initiatives, sustainability should be treated as an integrated
customer experience. Regarding the quality of food offerings, employee interactions, environmental initiatives, and
corporate governance, managers ought to make sure that sustainability practices are consistently communicated and
experienced.
Second, the importance of accessible sustainability communication is highlighted through the substantial effect of
Sustainability Touchpoint Experience on Customer Trust. To improve customer conﬁdence, restaurants should ought to
provide trustworthy data on waste reduction ethical business practices, sustainable sourcing and environmental
responsibilities.
Third, Food Touchpoint was the highest managerial priority, based on the IPMA information. The greatest improvements
in customer loyalty will probably have to result through investments in locally sourced ingredients, sustainable
procurement, food safety, nutritional quality and transparent food information. While the performance in Governance,
People and Planet Touchpoints were largely satisfactory, customer relationship outcomes could be improved by
maintaining these standards together alongside targeted improvements in food-related sustainability.
Finally the ﬁndings demonstrate that rather than being created simply for marketing or regulatory compliance,
sustainability initiatives should be integrated into a long-term customer interaction approach. A comprehensive
sustainability experience may improve a restaurants long-term competitiveness, encourage repeat customers, and
improve trust among customers.
7. Conclusion
Sustainability Touchpoint Experience (STE), a higher order customer centric sustainability construct in the restaurant
industry, was developed and empirically validated in this study. The study demonstrated that customers interactions with
Food, People, Planet and Governance Touchpoints lead to sustainability experiences, drawing on literature on customer
journey, customer experience, and sustainable consumption.
Page 17/25

The ﬁndings that were generated using PLS-SEM and information collected from 400 dining customers in eastern India,
conﬁrmed that every one of the four sustainability touchpoints make a signiﬁcant contribution to the Sustainability
Touchpoint Experience. Furthermore, dining loyalty and customer trust are signiﬁcantly boosted through sustainability
Touchpoint Experience, and the relationship between sustainability experience and loyalty is primarily controlled by
customer trust. The IPMA ﬁndings demonstrate that Food Touchpoint is the most important sustainable dimension for
improving customer loyalty, and the predictive assessment further demonstrates satisfactory out of sample predictive
capacity.
Overall, by providing Sustainability Touchpoint Experience as a theoretically grounded higher order construct that
integrates different sustainability variables into one consumer experience framework, the study improves hospitality
sustainability research. The study offers a comprehensive overview of how sustainability experiences translates into
stronger customer relationships and offers practical recommendations for sustainability oriented restaurant
management by combining hierarchical component modelling a mediation analysis, predictive assessment and IPMA.
8. Limitations and Future Research
Considering its contributions, this paper presents a selection of signiﬁcant weaknesses that offer possibilities for
additional research. First the empirical investigation was limited to customers of the restaurants in the eastern Indian
states of Odisha and West Bengal, which may limit the ﬁndings relevance to other geographical regions and hospitality
contexts. Future study ought to verify the proposed model across a range of different countries, cultural settings, and
hospitality sectors, such as quick-service restaurants, hotels, and cafes.
Second since the study employed a cross-sectional research method, it wasn’t possible to investigate whether customer
perceptions evolved over time. Deeper comprehension of the way sustainability experiences inﬂuence loyalty and trust in
enduring customer relationships might be achieved through longitudinal studies.
Third, the sole variable which was demonstrated to facilitate the association between Dining Loyalty and Sustainability
Touchpoint Experience was Customer Trust. To develop a deeper understanding of the psychological mechanisms behind
sustainable behaviour among consumer, future research ought to look at additional mediators such as customer
satisfaction perceived value, customer engagement or emotional attachment.
Finally, although IPMA was utilized in this study to identify managerial priorities, segmentation methods such as Multi-
Group Analysis (MGA) might be utilized in future research to add to these ﬁndings as well as examine whether
demographic variables such as age, gender, or income moderated the relationships within the proposed model. The
practical relevance of Sustainability Touchpoint Experience across different customer groups could be further
strengthened through such assessments.
