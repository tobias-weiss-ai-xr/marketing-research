ABSTRACT: The fast-food restaurant industry in Indonesia is growing rapidly alongside increasingly dynamic and digital -oriented
lifestyle changes. Intense competition drives restaurants to not only attract new customers but also retain existing ones. This study
aims to analyze the influence of Customer Experience, Omnichannel Marketing, Service Quality, and Local Wisdom on Customer
Retention through Customer Relationship Management (CRM) as a mediating variable in fast -food restaurants in Batam City
(McDonald's, KFC,  A&W, Burger King, and Pizza Hut). The study employs a quantitative approach using survey methods. A
minimum sample of 310 respondents is selected using Simple Random Sampling from consumers aged 18 -45 years who have
visited fast-food restaurants in Batam at least once in the past six months. Data is analyzed using Partial Least Squares Structural
Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM). Ten hypotheses are formulated covering direct and indirect influences among variables. This
research is expected to provide theoretical and practical contributions for the fast-food restaurant industry in designing effective
customer retention strategies
KEYWORDS: Customer Experience, Service Quality, Local Wisdom, Customer Relationship Management, Customer Retention
I. INTRODUCTION
Fast food restaurants are one of the fastest -growing segments of the food industry, particularly in urban areas. Speed of service,
consistent taste, affordable prices, and easy access make fast food restaurants a top choice for highly mobile urban commun ities
(Abadi & Nurpratiwi, 2021; Zhong & Moon, 2020). In Indonesia, the food and beverage ecosystem will grow to approximately 4.85
million businesses by 2023, with the foodservice industry projected to reach US$30.2 billion and continue to grow through 20 28
(Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada, 2025; Statistics Indonesia, 2024).
However, increasing competition is pushing fast food restaurants to focus not only on acquiring new customers but also on
retaining existing ones. Customer retention is a key indicator of restaurant business success, as loyal customers tend to mak e
repeat purchases and recommend restaurants to others (Nadila & Hasan, n.d.). Additional challenges arise from the phenomenon
of consumer boycotts due to health, environmental, social, and political issues that influence public perception of brands (Watson
et al., 2024).
In efforts to increase customer retention, several strategic factors must be considered. A positive customer experience at every
touchpoint builds positive perceptions and fosters long -term brand engagement. Omnichannel marketing strategies are relevant
because customers interact through various channels, from food delivery and online ordering to drive -thru. Good service quality
builds customer trust and satisfaction, while the application of local wisdom can foster emotional closeness by adapting products
and services to local cultures.
Within the framework of the relationships between these variables, Customer Relationship Management (CRM) plays a
strategic role in helping restaurants understand customer needs and build meaningful, long -term relationships. This research
focuses on Batam City, a region with significant fast food industry growth and a multicultural community.
Based on this background, this study aims to analyze: (1) the influence of Customer Experience, and Service Quality on CRM;
(2) the influence of Customer Experience, Service Quality, and Local Wisdom on Customer Retention; and (3) the mediating role

II. LITERATURE REVIEW AND HYPOTHESIS
A. Customer Retention
Customer retention is the tendency of customers to remain loyal and maintain a long -term transactional relationship with a
company (Artha et al., 2022). According to (Adekunle et al., 2023), customer retention is a company's ability to retain custo mers
and ensure they continue using a product or service and prevent them from switching to competitors. This retention occurs when
customers experience good service quality and are satisfied with the service, which then drives revisit intention (Salamah et  al.,
2022).
B.Customer Experience and Customer Relationship Management
A positive customer experience in service interactions increases engagement and the quality of customer relationships, ultimately
strengthening CRM (Rather, 2021). Integrated customer experience management at every touchpoint helps companies build
stable and effective relationships through CRM (Chahal and Bala, 2021; de Keyser, 2020). The better the experience customers
receive during their interactions with a company, the greater the effectiveness of CRM in building trust and long-term relationships
(Fatma, 2020).
H1: Customer Experience has a positive effect on Customer Relationship Management.
C. Service Quality and Customer Relationship Management
Quality service increases perceived customer value, thereby strengthening CRM effectiveness (Slack et al., 2021). Service qua lity
dimensions such as reliability, responsiveness, assurance, empathy, and tangibles have a positive influence on building
interpersonal relationships with customers in CRM (Adegbola, 2020). Good service quality creates a sense of trust and comfort,
making it easier for companies to personalize customer relationships through CRM (Palazzo et al., 2021).
H2: Service Quality has a positive influence on Customer Relationship Management .
D. Customer Experience and Customer Retention
A good customer experience in restaurants, including a comfortable atmosphere, friendly staff, and quality communication, has
been shown to increase customer retention because customers remember and associate these positive experiences with the
brand (Buji sic et al., 2020). A quality customer experience in fast food restaurants increases customer loyalty and impacts
customer retention (Chahal et al., 2022; Han & Hyun, 2020).
H3: Customer Experience has a positive influence on customer retention.
E. Service Quality and Customer Retention
Service quality has a strong relationship with customer retention because good service quality creates customer satisfaction,
comfort, and trust, which encourages them to continue using the service in the long term (Slack et al., 2021). Quality servic e
increases satisfaction, which leads to increased customer retention (Rashid & Rahman, 2023; Ray et al., 2022). Service quality also
builds trust, which leads to long-term relationships (Sun & Kim, 2022).
H4: Service quality has a positive effect on customer retention.
F. Local Wisdom and Customer Retention
Cultural authenticity presented in service can create meaningful experiences for customers, thus increasing customer retentio n
(Prentice et al., 2021). Presenting food products that reflect local culture creates place attachment (Rather and Hollebeek, 2021),
which encourages customers to return (Lee & Kim, 2020). The implementation of local wisdom elements in a restaurant's menu
and atmosphere increases customer retention because customers enjoy a unique experience that is not easily replaced by other
restaurants (Hwang & Seo, 2021).
H5: Local wisdom has a positive effect on customer retention.
G. CRM Mediation: Customer Experience → Customer Retention
Customer experience has a significant influence on customer retention through CRM as a mediator. Positive customer experiences
foster stronger relationship quality, which in turn increases retention (Rather and Hollebeek, 2021). Engagement, as an eleme nt
of CRM, serves as a bridge between customer experience and customer retention (Kandampully et al., 2021; Rather, 2021).
H6: Customer Relationship Management mediates the relationship between customer experience and customer retention.

III. RESEARCH METHOD
This research used a quantitative approach with a survey method. This approach was chosen because it allows for objective
measurement and statistical analysis to test the relationships between variables: Customer Experience,  Service Quality, and Local
Wisdom as independent variables; Customer Relationship Management as a mediating variable; and Customer Retention as the
dependent variable.
The research subjects were consumers who had visited or used fast food restaurants in Batam City, namely McDonald's, KFC,
A&W, Burger King, and Pizza Hut. The target population was residents of Batam City who had consumed at these restaurants. The
sample s ize was determined using the technique of Hair et al. (2010) with a 1:10 ratio to the number of indicators. With 31
questionnaire questions, the minimum sample size required was 310 respondents.
Respondent criteria included: (1) being between 18 and 45 years old; (2) having made a purchase at one of the fast food
restaurants studied at least once in the last six months; and (3) being willing to complete the questionnaire in full. The sa mpling
technique used simple random sampling with an online questionnaire distributed to residents of Batam City.
Customer retention (Y) is measured as a company's ability to retain customers so they continue to use its services and avoid
switching to competitors (Adekunle et al., 2023). Customer experience ( X1) is the customer's perception and response formed
from all interactions with the restaurant, both before, during, and after consumption (Molinillo et al., 2022). Service quali ty (X2)
is the level of service excellence perceived by customers in meeting their expectations, including speed, friendliness, accur acy,
and consistency of service (Kim & Park, 2021). Local Wisdom ( X3) refers to local values, culture, and customs adapted by
restaurants in their products and services, including adapting menus and brand identity to the multicultural local culture of Batam
City (Primayanti & Puspita, 2022). Customer Relationship Managemen t ( Z) is a company's strategy for managing long -term
relationships with customers through the integrated use of data, technology, and interactions (Malki et al., 2024).
Data analysis used Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM) due to its ability to analyze complex
relationships between variables, including models involving mediating variables. Model evaluation was conducted in two stages :
(1) the outer model, which assessed the validity (outer loading and AVE) and reliability (composite reliability and Cronbach's alpha)
of each construct; and (2) the inner model, which examined the path coefficient, indirect effect, R -square, and Goodness of Fit
(GoF). The Common Method Bias test was conducted using Harman's single factor test to control for potential measurement bias.

picture of the gender profile of the restaurant customers studied. Based on age distribution, the 21 -30 age group dominated the
research sample with 174 people (44.3%), followed by the 31-40 age group with 97 people (24.7%), and the 17-21 age group with
95 people (24.2%). Meanwhile, the 41 -50 age group numbered 24 people (6.1%), and the 51 -60 age group only had 3 people
(0.8%). The dominance of the young productive age group (21 -30) reflects that the majority of restaurant customers come from
the millennial gener ation who tend to actively use various digital channels and are very responsive to omnichannel marketing
strategies. This makes this age segment relevant as the object of study in this research. Based on occupational distribution,  the
majority of responden ts were private sector employees (147 people) (37.4%), followed by students (89 people) (22.6%), civil
servants (83 people) (21.1%), self -employed (52 people) (13.2%), and housewives (22 people) (5.6%). The dominance of private
sector employees in the sample indicates that this group is the most active restaurant customer, likely because they have sufficient
mobility and purchasing power to dine at restaurants regularly. The diversity of respondents' professions provides a good
representation of the actual profile of restaurant customers.

Customer Relationship Management variable obtained an R -Square value of 0.629 (Adjusted R -Square 0.625), which means that
62.9% of the CRM variance can be explained by the Customer Experience, Service Quality, and Local Wisdom variables together.
Based on the criteria of Sarstedt et al. (2020), this value is included in the substantial influence category (>= 0.50). Meanwhile, the
Customer Retention variable obtained an R -Square value of 0.597 (Adjusted R -Square 0.596), which means that 59.7% of the
Customer Retention variance can be explained by the variables in the model. This value also falls into the substantial influe nce
category, indicating that the model has strong explanatory power for both dependent variables.
The test results show that Customer Experience has no significant effect on Customer Relationship Management (t-value
= 1.496; p = 0.135), so H1 is not accepted. This finding contradicts theoretical studies that state that positive customer experiences
at every touchpoint can strengthen CRM effectiveness (Chahal and Bala, 2021; de Keyser, 2020). However, this result is
understandable in the context of fast food restaurants in Batam City, where customer interactions tend to be transactional an d
brief, so customer experience does not necessarily directly encourage the formation of long-term relationships managed through
CRM. This finding is in line with research (Mokha & Kumar, 2022) which found that the relationship between Customer Experience
and CRM is indi rect and dependent on the service context. Furthermore, (Suharto & Yuliansyah, 2023) stated that Customer
Experience only impacts CRM if the company actively manages customer interaction data systematically.
The test results show that Service Quality has a significant effect on Customer Relationship Management (t-value = 1.739;
p = 0.082), thus H2 is accepted. This finding is consistent with the theoretical argument of (Adegbola, 2020) which states th at
service quality dimensions such as reliability, responsiveness, assurance, empathy, and tangibles have a positive effect on building
customer interpersonal relationships through CRM. This significance may be due to the behavioral patterns of fast food restaurant
consumers in Batam who prioritize speed and price over long -term relationships based on service quality. (Al -Gasawneh et al.,
2021) in their research also found that the effect of Service Quality on CRM varies depending on the type of industry and customer
characteristics. (Palazzo et al., 2021) added that Service Quality will only strengthen CRM if supported by an adequate custo mer
data management system, which is not necessarily available in all fast food restaurants.

V. CONCLUSIONS
This study analyzes the influence of Customer Experience, Service Quality, and Local Wisdom on Customer Retention through
Customer Relationship Management (CRM) as a mediating variable in fast food restaurants in Batam City. Based on the results o f
PLS-SEM testing on 310 respondents, the following conclusions can be drawn.
Customer Experience does not have a significant effect on Customer Relationship Management in fast food restaurants in
Batam City. This indicates that the transactional and short -lived customer experience in the fast food restaurant context is not
strong enough to directly influence the effectiveness of customer relationship management through CRM.
Service Quality has a significant effect on Customer Relationship Management. In the context of fast food restaurants in Batam,
customer perceived service quality appears to have a greater impact on immediate satisfaction than on fostering long -term
relationships through the CRM system.
Local Wisdom has a positive and significant effect on Customer Relationship Management and indirectly impacts Customer
Retention. The application of local wisdom in restaurant products and services has been shown to build emotional closeness th at
strengthens customer relationships and encourages retention, particularly in the multicultural city of Batam.
Customer Relationship Management has a positive and significant effect on Customer Retention, with the highest path
coefficient (0.773). These findings confirm that CRM is the most important determinant factor in customer retention in the fa st
food restaurant industry in Batam City. Effective CRM creates a sense of care and appreciation in customers, thus encouraging
them to continue using the restaurant's services.
Customer Relationship Management significantly mediates the relationship between Customer Experience and Customer
Retention, as well as the relationship between Service Quality and Customer Retention. This is consistent with the insignific ant
direct effect of these two variables on CRM. Conversely, CRM significantly mediates the effect of Local Wisdom on Customer
Retention, indicating that these two variables play a key role in driving CRM effectiveness in the context of this study.
Limitations and Suggestions for Future Research
This study has several limitations that require consideration. First, the scope of the study was limited to fast food restaur ants in
Batam City, so generalizing the results to other regions or restaurant types requires caution. Second, the data were collec ted
through self -report questionnaires, so the potential for Common Method Bias could not be completely eliminated, despite
Harman's single-factor test. Third, this study did not include demographic variables such as age, income, or frequency of visits as
potentially influential control variables. Future research is recommended to expand the scope to other cities, include modera ting
variables such as customer satisfaction or digital usage intensity, and explore dynamic models that can measure changes in
customer behavior over time.
