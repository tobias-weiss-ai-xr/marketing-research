Abstract
The real estate industry is undergoing rapid digital transformation, yet sales performance
remains difficult to secure in a market defined by high value, high risk, long decision cycles,
and a journey that moves between online and offline channels. Althoug h digital marketing,
technology acceptance, and customer experience have received considerable research
attention, much of this evidence comes from retail and electronic commerce, and relatively
few studies have linked digital transformation to sales outco mes in property markets. This
paper develops a conceptual framework, grounded in the Technology Acceptance Model and
Experience Economy theory, that explains how Personalized Technology Acceptance,
Customer Experience, Digital Engagement, and Omnichannel I ntegration influence Real
Estate Sales Performance. Four propositions are advanced, and a method for future empirical
testing using partial least squares structural equation modelling (PLS -SEM) is outlined. The
framework offers testable guidance for developers, brokerages, and digital marketing teams.
As a conceptual study, the propositions require future empirical validation.
Keywords: Real Estate Sales Performance , Personalized Technology Acceptance , Customer
Experience, Digital Engagement, Omnichannel Integration, Digital Transformation

Introduction
The real estate industry is undergoing rapid digital transformation, as property organizations
adopt artificial intelligence recommendation tools, virtual tours, social media marketing, and
integrated online and offline platforms to attract and convert buy ers(Al-haimi et al., 2025;
Prasad et al., 2026) . Yet real estate sales performance remains difficult to secure. Property
purchases involve high financial value, long decision cycles, high perceived risk, information
asymmetry between buyers and sellers(Broxterman & Zhou, 2023), and a journey that moves
between online and offline channels(Lemon & Verhoef, 2016; Verhoef et al., 2015), so buyer
confidence is especially important for conversion (Pavlou, 2003). Although digital marketing,
technology acceptance, and customer experience have received considerable research

experience, digital engagement, and omnichannel integration influence real esta te sales
performance in a high involvement property market context? This question connects to active
debates in the social sciences concerning how technology adoption shapes consumer
decision-making in high -risk purchase contexts (Davis, 1989; Venkatesh et al., 2012) , how
experiential value drives purchase outcomes beyond rational evaluation (Holbrook &
Hirschman, 1982; Pine & Gilmore, 1998), and how omnichannel integration affects consumer
trust and conversion in complex service environments (Lemon & Verhoef, 2016; Verhoef et

Technology Acceptance, Customer Experience, Digital Engagement, and Omnichannel
Integration influence Real Estate Sales Performance, and it advances four propositions for
future empirical testing.

Literature Review
Underpinning Theories
Digital Transformation in Real Estate Sales
Digital transformation in real estate sales is driven by the rapid emergence of property
technology. Property technology, often termed PropTech, refers to the application of digital
technologies to the search, valuation, transaction, and management of real estate (Al-haimi
et al., 2025; Baum & Dearsley, 2021; Prasad et al., 2026),and it represents the primary vehicle
through which the real estate industry is undergoing digital change. Several digital capabilities
are now commonly used in property sales. Artificial intelligence recommendation tools filter
large listing inventories against a buyer's preferences and reduce the cost of search,
consistent with the broader influence of artificial intelligence on marketing(Davenport et al.,
2020; Huang & Rust, 2021) . Virtual tours allow remote inspection of a property and widen
the reach of a listing (Al-haimi et al., 2025; Prasad et al., 2026) . Integrated online and offline
search connects portals, developer sites, messaging tools, sales galleries, and agents into one
continuous journey, consistent with broader developments in omnichannel retailing (Lemon
& Verhoef, 2016; Verhoef et al., 2015) . Together, these capabilities form the digital
infrastructure through which property organizations now seek to attract, inform, and convert
buyers.

Two features distinguish digital transformation in real estate from general digital retail. First,
lead management depends on customer relationship management systems, because the long
decision cycle requires organizations to guide qualified buyers across many contacts(Payne &
Frow, 2005), and the move from an online enquiry to an offline visit and a signed agreement
is a critical stage in the sales process (Lemon & Verhoef, 2016; Prasad et al., 2026) . Second,
building buyer confidence is particularly important, because high value, high risk, and
information asymmetry (Broxterman & Zhou, 2023; Prasad et al., 2026) require the digital
journey to reduce uncertainty about listing accuracy, developer credibility, price fairness, and
data security(Gefen et al., 2003; Prasad et al., 2026). Together, these two features mean that
digital transformation in real estate must be evaluated not only by reach and engagement,
but ultimately by its capacity to convert informed and confident buyers into completed
transactions.

Empirical evidence supports the importance of these digital capabilities: Prasad et al. (2026)
confirmed that virtual tours, social media marketing, and content marketing significantly
influence purchase intention in residential property markets, while Bro xterman and Zhou
(2023) documented that information frictions which digital tools are expected to reduce
remain a persistent challenge in real estate transactions, underscoring the importance of
studying how digital transformation translates into measurable sales outcomes.

Technology Acceptance Model
The Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) was proposed by Davis (1989), who identified
perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use as the main factors that shape a person's
intention to adopt and use a technology. Later research extended  acceptance models to
consumer settings by adding hedonic motivation, price value , and habit (Venkatesh et al.,
2012), In parallel, artificial intelligence has since become an increasingly important element
of marketing strategy (Davenport et al., 2020) . In this study, the Technology Acceptance
Model explains why property buyers form positive intentions to rely on digital tools during
search and decision-making. It supports three of the four antecedents, because Personalized
Technology Acceptance, Digital Engagement, and Omnichannel Integration each presuppose
that buyers find the relevant digital tools useful and easy to use.

Recent empirical work confirms that TAM based constructs remain relevant in digital
property search: Prasad et al. (2026) found that perceived usefulness of digital marketing
tools significantly mediates the relationship between digital channel adoption and purchase
intention in residential real estate, supporting the model's applicability to this context.

Experience Economy Theory
The Experience Economy theory was proposed by Pine and Gilmore (1998), who argued that
economic value has moved from commodities, through goods and services, to experiences,
and that organizations create the greatest value when they stage memorable and emotionally
engaging experiences. Marketing research has developed this idea through the customer
experience literature (Klaus & Maklan, 2013; Lemon & Verhoef, 2016) , and the brand
experience scale(Brakus et al., 2009) , and recent research highlights that new technologies
are reshaping the customer experience (Hoyer et al., 2020). In this study, the Experience
Economy theory explains why the quality of the buyer's journey, rather than the property's
features alone, shap es confidence and purchase decisions. It supports the Customer

Research Variables
This study examines five research variables, namely personalized technology acceptance,
customer experience, digital engagement, and omnichannel integration, and one outcome
variable, real estate sales performance. Each construct is defined below with its meaning, its
real estate context, and its main dimensions.

Personalized technology acceptance is the degree to which a buyer sees a personalized digital
platform as useful  easy to use, and well matched to personal preferences, and therefore
intends to rely on it during property search and decision -making(Davis, 1989; Prasad et al.,
2026) . In real estate, it appears in the use of artificial intelligence property matching,
personalized affordability tools, and adaptive virtual tours (Al-haimi et al., 2025) . Its main
dimensions are perceived usefulness, perceived ease of use(Davis, 1989)  and personalization
fit (Tam & Ho, 2006).

Customer experience based on the experience economy (Pine & Gilmore, 1998)  and the
customer journey perspective (Lemon & Verhoef, 2016), customer experience refers to  the
overall personal evaluation formed by  buyer throughout their buying journey, feelings, and
sensory experiences at each interaction. It is typically measured using customer experience
quality scales(Klaus & Maklan, 2013)and brand experience scales(Brakus et al., 2009). In real
estate sector, it encompasses discovery, evaluation, negotiation, signing, and post contract
services(Lemon & Verhoef, 2016). Its main dimensions include sensory experience, emotional
experience, cognitive experience, and behavioral experience.

Digital engagement based on customer engagement research (Brodie et al., 2011; Hollebeek
et al., 2014)  Digital Engagement is the depth and quality of a buyer's engagement with a
brand’s digital channels, encompassing the cognitive, emotional, and behavioral investment
that creates a sense of connection beyond a single transaction (Brodie et al., 2011)  In real
estate, it includes reading content, interacting on social media, using chat tools, and taking
part in online communities (Prasad et al., 2026). Its main dimensions are cognitive, emotional,
and behavioral engagement (Hollebeek et al., 2014).

Omnichannel integration is defined based on omnichannel retailing research (Beck & Rygl,
2015; Verhoef et al., 2015) . Omnichannel integration is the consistency and continuity that
an organization achieves across all digital and physical touchpoints, so that the buyer sees
one coherent journey, and channel integration has been linked to stronger sales
outcomes(Cao & Li, 2015). In real estate, it requires that listing data, prices, availability, and
messages stay consistent across portals, developer sites, social channels, sales galleries, and
agents. Its main dimensions are channel consistency, information integration, and a s mooth
move between online and offline stages(Beck & Rygl, 2015).

Personalized Technology Acceptance and Real Estate Sales Performance
The property search process involves substantial information overload, because buyers face
large listing inventories, complex financing options, and many location trade -offs (Eppler &
Mengis, 2004). When buyers accept and rely on a personalized platform that matches their
preferences, the cognitive cost of search falls, and buyers move more quickly from browsing
to serious enquiry, which can shorten sales time(Prasad et al., 2026). In this way, personalized
technology acceptance reduces friction at the earliest stage of the sales funnel and creates
the conditions for higher conversion.

A competent personalized system also signals organizational capability and lowers the
uncertainty that weakens early buyer confidence (McKnight et al., 2002)  , which supports a
higher conversion rate. Therefore, this study proposes the following.
Proposition 1: Personalized Technology Acceptance positively influences Real Estate Sales
Performance.

Customer Experience and Real Estate Sales Performance
Touchpoints that consistently produce positive feelings, such as confidence and reassurance,
accumulate into an overall evaluation that increases approach behavior and reduces late -
stage abandonment. Consistent with the experiential value perspective (Holbrook &
Hirschman, 1982), a strong journey also raises tolerance for a price premium, and a positive
experience after handover converts satisfaction into referral (Oliver, 1999). Therefore, this
study proposes the following.
Proposition 2: Customer Experience positively influences Real Estate Sales Performance.

Digital Engagement and Real Estate Sales Performance
Sustained, high quality digital engagement builds familiarity and relational value between a
brand and a prospective buyer, and relationship marketing research identifies such relational
investment as a driver of cooperation and loyalty (Palmatier et al., 2006). Engagement that is
interactive and value creating strengthens the buyer relationship in digital settings(Hollebeek
et al., 2014), so buyers who enter formal consultations with this accumulated familiarity need
less persuasion and progress faster to agreement. Participatory engagement, such as
interactive floor plan tools, also creates psychological ownership (Pierce et al., 2001, 2003) ,
which raises purchase motivation and reduces switching to competitors. Therefore, this study
proposes the following.
Proposition 3: Digital Engagement positively influences Real Estate Sales Performance.

Omnichannel Integration and Real Estate Sales Performance
In property markets, listing details, prices, and availability often differ across portals,
developer websites, and agents. Such inconsistency creates the discomfort that the theory of
cognitive dissonance links to contradictory information(Festinger, 1957), and it raises the risk
that buyers abandon the journey. Strong omnichannel integration keeps information
consistent across online and offline channels and signals professionalism, and the integration
of channels is a prominent theme in current retailing research. Consistency across channels
also allows confidence to transfer from the platform to the organization (Stewart, 2003) ,
which supports both conversion and advocacy. Therefore, this study proposes the following.
Proposition 4: Omnichannel Integration positively influences Real Estate Sales Performance.

Methodology
Sample and Data Collection
This section describes the sample and data collection procedures for the proposed empirical
test of the framework. The study employs a dyadic, quantitative, cross -sectional survey
design, which is the established approach for capturing both buyers and real  estate
developers’ perceptions across multiple constructs at a single point in time. Ethical approval
will be obtained from the relevant institutional review board prior to data collection, and all
procedures will comply with institutional requirements for resear ch involving human
participants. The following paragraphs specify the target population, sampling strategy,
recruitment channels, questionnaire procedures, and minimum sample size.

The target population is real -estate developers and individual residential property buyers
who have completed a digital or online to offline property purchase journey within the
previous twenty-four months. This recall window reflects the nature of residential property
purchase as a high-involvement decision with a lengthy search and negotiation cycle spanning

several months from initial digital search to signed agreement (Broxterman & Zhou, 2023;
Prasad et al., 2026) and the twenty-four-month boundary is designed to retain a sufficiently
large eligible pool while limiting the memory decay and retrospective bias that affect self -
reported perceptions of experiential journeys over longer periods (Podsakoff et al., 2003) .
The unit of analysis is the individual buyer and real estate developers. All four constructs
personalized technology acceptance, customer experience, digital engagement, and
omnichannel integration are buyer reported perceptions. On the other hand, the real estate
performance will be based on developers’ reported perceptions. Buyers’ data will be matched
with developer data based on the specific developer identified by the buyers. Each buyer’s
perception of personalized technology acceptance, customer experience, digital
engagement, and omnichannel integration will be aggregated match ed with the
corresponding developers’ perception of satisfaction with their real estate performance data.
Participation will be voluntary and anonymous. Before completing the survey, each
respondent will receive a consent statement explaining the study's purpose, the
confidentiality of responses, data storage procedures, and the right to withdraw at any time
without consequence. All items will use established scales where validated instruments exist,
adapted where necessary to the residential property context, and the questionnaire will
undergo cognitive pre -testing with a pilot group of fifteen to twenty respondents to check
wording clarity and item comprehension before full deployment (Collins, 2003). To reduce
the risk of common method bias, the survey will employ procedural safeguards including
multi-source data collection, scale variety, reverse -scored items at appropriate points, and
temporal separation of predictor and outcome items within the qu estionnaire(Podsakoff et
al., 2003). Attention-check items will be embedded at two points in the instrument to identify
inattentive respondents (Meade & Craig, 2012)  , and incomplete or failed -check responses
will be excluded from analysis prior to hypothesis testing.

Measures
Each construct will be measured reflectively by several survey items, where an item is a single
questionnaire statement that captures one aspect of the construct, and respondents will rate
each item on a seven-point Likert scale from strongly disagree to strongly agree. All items will
be adapted from  previously validated scales and reworded for the property context.
Personalized Technology Acceptance will be measured with around five items adapted from
Davis (1989) and Venkatesh et al. (2012); Customer Experience with around four items
adapted from Brakus et al. (2009); Digital engagement with around five items adapted from
Hollebeek et al. (2014); and Omnichannel Integrati on with around four items adapted from
Cao and Li (2015). Real estate sales performance will be captured using the participating real
estate developers’ satisfaction on their sales conversion rate, sales speed, transaction value
achievement, and post-sale advocacy.

Once data are collected, the psychometric properties of all constructs will be assessed
following established criteria. Internal consistency reliability will be examined through
Cronbach’s alpha and composite reliability, with values of 0.70 or above treat ed as
acceptable(Hair & Alamer, 2022; Nunnally, 1978) . Convergent validity will be examined
through the average variance extracted, with values of 0.50 or above treated as
acceptable(Fornell & Larcker, 1981) . Discriminant validity will be examined through the
heterotrait-monotrait ratio of correlations, with values below 0.85 indicating satisfactory
discriminant validity (Henseler et al., 2015). Together, these criteria provide a comprehensive

Data Analysis
The collected data will be analyzed with partial least squares structural equation modelling
(PLS-SEM), using SmartPLS (Ringle et al., 2015)  for the structural model and SPSS for
descriptive statistics. This method suits a predictive conceptual model with several related
constructs (Hair & Alamer, 2022). The analysis will follow two steps. First, the measurement
model will be examined through indicator reliability, composite reliability, average variance
extracted, and the heterotrait-monotrait ratio for discriminant validity (Henseler et al., 2015).
Second, the structural model will be examined through path coefficients and their
significance from bootstrapping(Hair & Alamer, 2022), the coefficient of determination, and
effect sizes(Cohen, 1988), in order to test the four propositions. Common method bias will
be reduced before data collection through anonymity and clear item wording, and checked
after data collection through Harman's single factor test and the full collinearity approach
(Kock, 2015; Podsakoff et al., 2003). These analytical steps provide a rigorous and transparent
basis for evaluating both the measurement quality and the structural relationships proposed
in the framework.

Discussion
Theoretical Contributions
This paper makes several contributions. It extends the Technology Acceptance Model to a
high-value and high -risk purchase by linking the acceptance of personalized property
technology to a concrete sales outcome rather than to adoption intention alone(Davis, 1989)
.It applies experience economy thinking to property sales by treating experiential value as a
driver of conversion, tolerance for a price premium, and advocacy (Pine & Gilmore, 1998). It
addresses the performance gap in digital marketing research by connecting four digital
transformation constructs directly to real estate sales performance, defined through
conversion, sales speed, value achievement, and advocacy, an outcome linkage that remains
underexplored in existing studies of property digitalization(Al-haimi et al., 2025; Prasad et al.,
2026).

Practical Implications
From a practical perspective, the proposed framework contributes to a better understanding
of how sales performance in property markets can be influenced through the alignment of
digitalization and customer-facing strategies. The proposed framework suggest s that digital
investment should be viewed as an interconnected system rather than isolated technological
initiatives. Cross-channel integration may represent an important mechanism through which
developers and brokerages create consistent customer journeys across digital and physical
touchpoints, supporting improved sales outcomes (Cao & Li, 2015) . Furthermore, the
framework highlights the importance of digital engagement where meaningful, relevant, and
responsive interactions are important. The framework extends understanding of how firms
can manage complex buyer journeys in property markets, where purchase decisions typically
involve extended information search, evaluation, and offline interactions. Collectively, the
framework provides a foundation for future empirical research examining how digital -
enabled customer processes contribute to improved real estate sales performance.

Limitation and Future Recommendation
This study has two main limitations. First, as a conceptual paper, its propositions are drawn
from theory and existing literature and have not yet been tested empirically. Second, the
framework is developed for high -involvement residential property in digi tally mature
markets, which may limit its generalization to other property types, emerging markets, or low
digital adoption contexts.

These limitations open several directions for future research. First, the framework should be
tested empirically using the survey design outlined above. Second, longitudinal designs can
track how acceptance, engagement, and experience shape sales outcomes across the long
property decision cycle, offering stronger evidence of temporal sequence than cross -
sectional data alone (Rindfleisch et al., 2008) . Third, the framework could be tested across
different countries, city tiers, and market segments to examine cultural and market
differences.

Conclusion
This conceptual paper has developed a framework that explains how digital transformation
shapes real estate sales performance. It addresses a context in which purchases carry high
value, high risk, and a long journey between online and offline channels, and in which digital
constructs have rarely been linked to sales outcomes. The framework proposes that
Personalized Technology Acceptance, Customer Experience, Digital Engagement, and
Omnichannel Integration each have a positive effect on Real Estate Sales P erformance, and
it advances four propositions and a method for future testing. As a conceptual study, these
propositions await empirical validation, but the framework provides a structured basis for
future research and practice.
