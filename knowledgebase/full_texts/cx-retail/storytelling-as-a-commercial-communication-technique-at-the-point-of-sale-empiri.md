Resumén

El storytelling como técnica de comunicación comercial en el punto de venta: evidencia

Objetivo del estudio: Este estudio analiza el storytelling como técnica de comunicación comercial
y venta para fortalecer la interacción entre asesores, marcas y consumidores.
Metodología/enfoque: Se adoptó un enfoque cuantitativo descriptivo. Se aplicaron encuestas
digitales a vendedores de tecnología en diez ciudades de Colombia.
Abstract

Study objective: This study analyzes storytelling as a commercial communication
and sales technique to strengthen the interaction between advisors, brands, and
consumers.
Methodology/approach: A descriptive quantitative approach was adopted. Digital
surveys were administered to technology salespeople across ten cities in Colombia.
Main results: The findings reveal that the majority of  salespeople believe buyers
value trust and emotional connection, while a high proportion state that narratives
successfully overcome high-price objections in omnichannel environments.
Theoretical/methodological contributions:  This research provides empirical
evidence that validates the transition from technical discourses to holistic relational
models, integrating archetype theory into commercial interactions.
Relevancy/originality: This study presents an original analysis examining the role
of the salesperson as a humanizing narrative mediator within the Colombian
technology retail sector.
Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs): This research aligns with SDG 8 (Decent
Work and Economic Growth) by promoting strategic communication competencies
that drive labor productivity and commercial growth for brands.

1 Introduction

According to Barthes (1997), in its almost infinite forms, narrative has been present in
every era, place, and society, accompanying the very history of humankind, for there has never
been a people without stories. Narratives have played a culturally signi ficant role by preserving
collective memory, transmitting moral values, and generating entertainment. In the field of
communication, stories play a fundamental role both in everyday conversations and in institutional
and political discourse (Polkinghorne, 1988), since stories make it possible to communicate large
volumes of information quickly and in a way that is easy to assimilate (Schank, 1990).
Nevertheless, telling a story entails exposure on the part of the narrator, who inevitably reveals
something of himself or herself and faces possible interpretations or criticism (Núñez, 2007). In
this context, storytelling has become a key strategy for building content with a high level of
emotional resonance, strengthening the authenticity and closeness of brand s in their relationships
with consumers (Lalama-Freire, 2024).
In this sense, brands act as storytellers and must recognize their strategic role in
communication processes, defining who they are, how they express themselves, and what
differentiates them in order to generate influence and trust among consumers. Because  of their

2 Methodology

The implementation of an in situ , real-time information-gathering methodology made it
possible to identify several findings in the 264 surveys conducted, leading to an understanding of
sales advisors’ perceptions of storytelling not only as a communication technique but also as a
sales technique. The researchers chose to focus on the technology sector in Colombia because one
of them had a connection with this sector. The main research question was: based on your
experience when closing a sale, how effective is it to use anecdotes or your own success stories
and/or stories of other consumers using the technology product? In particular, the study focused
on the use of anecdotes, especially those that mention personal success stories or stories referring
to situations involving other consumers who engaged with a technology product or service.
The research adopted a descriptive quantitative approach aimed at analyzing salespeople’s
perceptions of the use of storytelling as a communication and sales technique at the point of sale.
Information was collected through a structured questionnaire desig ned and administered through
Google Forms. Data collection was carried out digitally by accessing a database of Apple brand
salespeople in Colombia, a brand that does not have its own stores in the country and operates
exclusively through authorized resell ers. This procedure made it possible to reach sales advisors
located in different cities, ensuring geographic diversity and homogeneity in the participants’
profile. The data obtained were analyzed descriptively, allowing the identification of trends and
patterns associated with narrative practices in sales processes.

The survey results made it possible to understand perceptions of narratives in commercial
encounters and the broader picture of the consumer/salesperson relationship. The sample was

3 The Evolution of Traditional Selling

In the sale of technology products, which is the case addressed in this research, a
consumer’s evaluation of an unattractive product feature may be perceived positively if it is
communicated through a narrative exercise. According to Adaval and Wyer (1998), the high cost
of a product may go unnoticed, or if the price has not been mentioned at all, its absence may not
be noticed unless such knowledge is necessary. For this reason, the specific features of a product
take on meaning within the course of the imagined scenario. Some theories of consumption assume
that people evaluate products, examine their implications, and make an overall judgment, but this

Although salespeople know and use narrative in their sales processes, a full understanding

can connect in such a way that emotions are expressed through body language, sudden changes in
facial expressions or gestures, hand or foot movements, changes in tone and forms of speech, and
other codes such as winks or the use of symbolism with the fingers or hands.
Individual identity is formed and adjusted over time through a person’s actions and
experiences, including his or her behavior and consumption practices. The way a person chooses
to consume a brand is part of the components of this constantly evolving iden tity. According to
Schembri (2009), like human beings, brands can potentially include personalities that differentiate
them from other brands. This anthropomorphic connection, enacted within a particular space and
time, can nurture one’s own identity. Jenk ins (2003) argues that, through stories and their
connection, people can form a mental image of the possible scenarios - lived experiences and
future experiences - they may have with that brand. “People who encounter information in a story
tend to adopt a holistic view. This is because they construct a mental representation of a series of
events described in the story [...] that allows them to imagine their own experiences” ( Adaval &
Wyer, 1998).
Once the representation has been constructed, people can evaluate the implications “as a
whole,” without considering the particular or individual features that compose it. There may be
specific elements that attribute negative value to the product. However , these aspects, apart from
the sequence “as a whole,” carry less weight when they are presented through a story that has
connected them, identified them, and awakened sensations and reflections. The way product
information is presented can facilitate or i nterfere with the sales process. A deeper engagement
with storytelling as a sales strategy facilitates this process because it enables innovation in
commercial relationships by opening new discourses that begin with consumers’ concerns and,
therefore, by consolidating more effective interactions between consumers and salespeople.

4 Understanding the Consumer through Archetypes

tool for understanding consumer motivations from a symbolic and emotional dimension. These
models make it possible to structure stories that facilitate the individual ’s identification with
situations, characters, and experiences associated with the product, helping to give meaning to the
commercial interaction. Instead of merely describing functional attributes, archetype -based
discourse translates product features into meanings that are relevant to the consumer.

function as a strategic resource that makes it possible to simplify the complexity of the decision -
making process without reducing it.
From the salesperson’s perspective, the application of archetypes involves recognizing
signals in the consumer’s discourse and behavior in order to adapt the sales narrative. This process
does not respond to an explicit classification, but rather to a cont extual reading that allows the
story to be adjusted to the interlocutor’s expectations. Thus, storytelling is configured as a situated
practice, in which the salesperson selects and articulates narrative elements according to the
specific interaction.

5.1 Salesperson Experience, Trust, and Relational Role

6 Commercial Interaction through Storytelling

Consumer analysis requires identifying shared and differential attributes that guide the

transactional exchange and becomes a relational process. The strategies proposed by Smith (2020)

sales. These stages - writing, selection, consultation, evaluation, and contextualization - facilitate
a reflective process on previous commercial experiences and their narrative potential. Rather than
improvising stories, the salesperson builds a repertoire of stories that can be adjusted to different
interaction situations.
In the first interaction, which serves as the “icebreaker,” Smith (2020) notes that it is
necessary to provide a brief description of the salesperson’s occupation or of the brand’s “work.”
This description must be attractive and must not create a sense of tedium. This is where storytelling
becomes important. In developing the discourse, a series of problematic situations that are solved
through the occupation/product should be described. Likewise, discourses should be developed
that end with a single sentence describing how to solve the problem, as well as indicating how the
product can improve an experience, for example, going to the beach or diving underwater with a
waterproof camera. The commercial relationship is closed once the consumer’s full attention  has
been obtained, awakening a sense of trust. For this purpose, the sales advisor should not be seen
in a sales role. Rather, he or she should be associated with the figure of an assistant or helper who
provides services for the resolution of conflicts and/or needs.

7 Corporate Willingness to Implement Storytelling

In the competitive field of the technology market, it is necessary to recognize both
achievements and failures so that success can be achieved in the next encounter - and, even more
importantly, so that a relationship of trust with consumers can be gained.  Often, storytelling
strategies may not provide immediate and quantifiable results in terms of sales. It is important for
all employees to understand and consistently communicate the brand story and key messages. A
lack of coherence in the narrative can co nfuse consumers. In addition, measuring the impact of
storytelling on sales can be challenging. Determining which stories resonated most with consumers
and how that translated into sales can be complicated.
Companies that operate through digital channels and use storytelling must assume that
anyone can become the sender of the story and has control over what can be done with that story.

where consumers cannot physically touch or feel the product before buying it, storytelling can help
contextualize the product. It can explain how the product fits  into the consumer’s life and how it
will solve his or her needs or problems.

8 Conclusions and Recommendations

Storytelling or narrative has been consistently present in marketing and advertising
activities. “Point -of-sale advertising has evolved from an informational approach toward
experiential strategies centered on the consumer” (López Iglesias & Luque -Ortiz, 2024). Across
different media, the story has been a distinctive resource for persuasion. And since “persuasion is
not only automatic, but can be replicated as many times as desired” (Cobo, 2019), many companies
use it as a brand strategy. Storytelling as a persuasive tool can help organizations connect better
with their consumers, add a human component to sales interactions, and create stro nger
relationships. Overcoming the initial challenges can be an important step toward success in
adopting story -based sales strategies. With this finding, Denning’s (2004) statement becomes
meaningful:

Although sound business arguments are developed through the use of numbers, they
are usually approved on the basis of a story - a narrative that connects a series of events in
a certain type of causal sequence. Storytelling transforms those dry and abstract numbers
into a set of images that form part of a leader’s goals (Denning, 2004, p. 2).

Despite the challenges involved in implementing it, many organizations are recognizing
the value of storytelling in sales strategies and are working to overcome these barriers. This often
involves a combination of training, organizational changes, internal  alignment, and a deeper
understanding of how storytelling can benefit the company in the long term. The exercise of
creating and telling stories represents a learning process for the salesperson, who over time will
recognize which types of discourse are a ppropriate for different types of consumers. In addition,
by recognizing his or her own personality, the salesperson will be able to understand how and
when to use an archetype or a story centered on one of the themes considered most relevant when
selling technology products.
The results obtained support theoretical approaches that position storytelling as a central
mechanism in the construction of meaning in commercial contexts, in line with authors who
