ABSTRACT
Addressing empirical fragmentation in omnichannel literature, this study models how individual
psychological attributes and structural service flows drive collaborative customer behaviors within
emerging e -commerce markets. It evaluates the effects of Personality Traits and Cu stomer Journey
Design on Value Co -Creation Behavior, mediated by Omnichannel Trust and Omnichannel Customer
Experience, alongside the moderating influences of Digital Proficiency and Channel Preference.
Structural Equation Modeling (SEM-PLS via SmartPLS 4) was executed on survey responses gathered
from 300 active Indonesian omnichannel shoppers through purposive sampling. Findings demonstrate
that seamless journey designs and adaptive personality traits significantly elevate both customer trust
and experien tial quality, which directly propel value co -creation participation. Conversely, neither
digital proficiency nor channel preferences exhibit significant moderating effects. The proposed model
demonstrates moderate explanatory power, capturing variance across key outcomes with $R^2$ values
of 0.533 for Customer Experience, 0.501 for Trust, and 0.359 for Value Co -Creation Behavior.
Theoretically, this research extends Service-Dominant Logic and Trust Theory by establishing a dual -
mediation mechanism that bridges micro-level individual traits and macro -level service architectures.
The study's primary novelty rests on synthesizing behavioral readiness and touchpoint design into a
unified framework. Practically, e -commerce executives must emphasize friction -free cross-channel
integration and transparent system reliability over tailored channel isolation or reliance on user digital
capabilities.

I. INTRODUCTION
The rapid evolution of e -commerce has fundamentally reshaped corporate paradigms from
traditional product - and pric e-centric models toward holistic customer relationship management. To
sustain competitive advantage, businesses must deliver seamless, synchronized experiences across
physical and digital touchpoints (omnichannel). Grounded in Service -Dominant Logic (S -D L ogic),
modern marketing views consumers not as passive recipients of value, but as co -creators who actively
participate in value formation through reviews, feedback, and brand advocacy  a phenomenon captured
as Value Co-Creation Behavior (Vargo et al., 2017; Yi & Gong, 2013).
Despite the strategic emphasis on omnichannel deployment, significant empirical and
theoretical tensions persist regarding how cross-channel architectures truly foster co-creation behaviors.
Prior literature yields fragmented and contr adictory insights. On one side, scholars like Shamim et al.
(2023) and Gao & Jiang (2025) demonstrate that structural touchpoint design directly minimizes service
friction and cultivates trust. Conversely, researchers such as Becker & Jaakkola (2020) argue  that
physical-digital integration alone is insufficient, asserting that subjective consumer experiences and
individual psychological readiness dictate engagement outcomes. Furthermore, empirical findings
concerning moderating mechanisms remain highly unst able. While Cui et al. (2022) observe that high
digital proficiency and distinct channel preferences amplify customer participation, subsequent studies
by Gahler et al. (2023) and Widyastuti (2024) report insignificant moderating impacts, suggesting that
customer capabilities may not uniformly catalyze co -creation across diverse touchpoints. These
unresolved discrepancies highlight a critical research gap: existing frameworks predominantly analyze
structural journey designs, individual psychological traits,  trust, and multidimensional customer
experience in isolation rather than within an integrated structural mechanism.
To reconcile these conflicting perspectives, this study provides explicit novelty by
conceptualizing and empirically validating an integra tive dual -mediation model within the rapidly
expanding Indonesian e -commerce landscape. By simultaneously synthesizing structural parameters
(Customer Journey Design ) and micro -level psychological dispositions ( Personality Traits ) into a
unified structural model, this research uncovers how dual affective -cognitive pathways (Omnichannel
Trust and Omnichannel Customer Experience) convert antecedent drivers into active value co-creation.
Additionally, it re -evaluates the boundary conditions imposed by Digital Proficiency and Channel
Preference to resolve previous empirical ambiguities.
Theoretically, this investigation makes a substantial contribution to omnichannel marketing
literature by extending S -D Logic and Trust Theory beyond single-channel or purely operational
frameworks. It advances the theoretical understanding of how structural journey integration and
individual personality dynamics synergize to drive consumer co-creation behavior in emerging markets.
Practically, the find ings offer actionable guidance for e -commerce executives to strategically align
cross-channel journey architectures with customer trust mechanisms to stimulate sustainable, long-term
co-creation.

not merely embedded in physical goods and transferred to consumers (Goods -Dominant Logic), but is
continuously and collaboratively co-created through interactive service exchanges (Vargo et al. , 2017;
Yi & Gong, 2013). Within an omnichannel ecosystem, effective value co-creation relies heavily on the
structural architecture of service touchpoints (Customer Journey Design) and the baseline psychological

The Effect of Customer Journey Design on Customer Experience and Trust
Theoretical foundations of customer journey design center on reducing cognitive effort and
interactional friction across multi-touchpoint environments (Lemon & Verhoef, 2016). From a cognitive
processing perspective, a well -structured journey offers seamless channel transitions, uniform
information architecture, and continuous transaction flows. When service architectures minimize
cognitive load, consumers perceive the interaction as highly fluid and effortless, directly elevating their
overall cognitive and affective evaluation ( Omnichannel Customer Experience) (Gao & Jiang, 2025).
Furthermore, according to Trust Theory, institutional and structural predi ctability serves as a primary
foundation for trust formation (Gefen, 2000). When an omnichannel platform delivers consistent service
across distinct physical and digital interfaces, it signals organizational competence, system integrity,
and reliability. This structural coherence systematically reduces perceived vulnerability and transaction
uncertainty, thereby instilling firm belief in the system's dependability ( Omnichannel Trust) (Gahler et
al., 2023). Therefore, the following hypotheses are proposed:
• H1: Customer Journey Design has a positive effect on Omnichannel Customer Experience.
• H2: Customer Journey Design has a positive effect on Omnichannel Trust.

The Effect of Personality Traits on Customer Experience and Trust
Personality theory, particularly the Big Five framework, asserts that stable intrinsic
psychological traits dictate how individuals process environmental stimuli and evaluate digital service
interactions (McCrae & Costa, 1997). Consumers possessing high openness to experience ,
conscientiousness, and emotional stability display superior adaptive capacity when navigating complex
omnichannel ecosystems. Cognitively, highly adaptive individuals perceive technological interactions
as intuitive and enriching rather than burdensome, resulting in heightened cognitive -affective
satisfaction ( Omnichannel Customer Experience ) (Widyastuti, 2024). From a psychological trust
perspective, intrinsic traits dictate an individual's baseline propensity to trust digital architectures.
Individuals with rational and innovative personality profiles evaluate digital platforms based on
structural efficiency and systemic safety features, allowing them to form trust beliefs more readily when
systems fulfill their transactional expectations (Omnichannel Trust) (Gahler et al., 2023). Accordingly,
the following hypotheses are proposed:
• H3: Personality Traits have a positive effect on Omnichannel Customer Experience.
• H4: Personality Traits have a positive effect on Omnichannel Trust.

The Effect of Customer Experience and Trust on Value Co-Creation Behavior
The transformation of experiential perceptions and trust beliefs into active behavioral responses
is explained by the Cognitive-Affective-Conative framework (Lemon & Verhoef, 2016) and S-D Logic
(Vargo e t al., 2017). When consumers derive rich, frictionless, and rewarding experiences across
integrated touchpoints (Omnichannel Customer Experience), they feel a heightened sense of relational
reciprocity toward the brand (Yi & Gong, 2013). This positive eval uation drives them to extend their
role from passive service users to active co -creators by offering constructive feedback, submitting
product reviews, and advocating for the platform (Becker & Jaakkola, 2020). Similarly, Trust Theory
emphasizes that trust  reduces perceived behavioral risks associated with voluntary customer
contributions (Gefen, 2000). High trust reassures consumers that their personal data, time, and
intellectual contributions will be respected and utilized constructively by the firm. Thi s psychological

The Moderating Role of Channel Preference and Digital Proficiency
Contingency theory suggests that individual capabilities and contextual in clinations alter the
strength of relationship pathways between experiential evaluations and behavioral outcomes. Digital
Proficiency reflects an individual's self -efficacy and technical competence in utilizing digital tools.
Consumers with high digital literacy are theoretically equipped to extract greater utility from seamless
journey designs, thereby amplifying the positive influence of customer experience and trust on co -
creation participation (Shamim et al., 2023). Similarly, Channel Preference represen ts a focused
behavioral disposition toward preferred interaction media. When an omnichannel environment fulfills
specific channel preferences, the alignment between customer expectations and service delivery is
optimized, potentially strengthening the tran slation of trust and positive experiences into active value
co-creation (Cui et al., 2022). Therefore, the following direct and moderating hypotheses are posited:
• H7: Channel Preference has a positive effect on Value Co-Creation Behavior.
• H8: Channel Preference moderates the relationship between Omnichannel Customer Experience and
Value Co-Creation Behavior.
• H9: Channel Preference moderates the relationship between Omnichannel Trust and Value Co-
Creation Behavior.

III. RESEARCH METHOD
This study adopts an ex planatory quantitative research design to empirically test direct,
mediating, and moderating structural relationships (Hair et al., 2019). The target population
encompasses all e -commerce consumers across Indonesia with active omnichannel shopping
experience. Given the unknown, infinite nature of this population, a non-probability purposive sampling
strategy was employed (Hair et al., 2019). To ensure sample representative validity and data quality,
specific inclusion criteria were established with the following methodological justifications:
1. Age ≥ 17 years: Ensures respondents possess legal capacity and autonomous purchasing decision -
making capability in digital environments.
2. Transaction within the last six months: Guarantees recency of memory and accurate recall regarding
cross-channel shopping experiences.
3. Multi-channel interaction (minimum 2 distinct service channels): Essential to evaluate authentic
cross-channel touchpoint transitions (e.g., mobile apps, physical outlets, web platforms, and social
media) throughout the journey from pre-purchase search to post-purchase support.
Primary data collection was administered online via structured electronic questionnaires
distributed across major social media platforms and digital consumer communities in Indonesia. A total
of 345 questionnaire submissions were recorded during the data c ollection period. Following rigorous
data screening for incomplete entries, unengaged responses, and failing screening questions, 45
responses were discarded. This yielded 300 fully valid questionnaires suitable for structural analysis,
representing an eff ective usable response rate of 86.95%. The sample size of 300 meets PLS -SEM
parameter estimation requirements and statistical power guidelines for complex moderation -mediation
models (Hair et al., 2019).
Ethical approval and research integrity protocols were strictly maintained throughout the study.
Prior to filling out the survey, all participants were presented with a digital Informed Consent Form
detailing the research objectives, voluntary participation rights, and full confidentiality assurances.
Respondents were explicitly informed that their participation was completely voluntary, with the right
to withdraw at any stage without penalty. To protect personal identity, all data were gathered
anonymously and stored securely in encrypted databases accessi ble solely to the research team for
academic purposes.

IV. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Data collection yielded a total sample of 300 valid respondents who fulfilled all predefined
purposive sampling criteria. Demographically, al l participants were at least 17 years old, with the vast
majority falling into the digital-native productive cohort (18–35 years old). Every respondent confirmed
active transactional engagement on Indonesian e-commerce platforms within the preceding six months
and routinely utilized a minimum of two distinct service touchpoints (e.g., mobile applications, official
web portals, social commerce, or physical store visits) during their customer journey. These baseline
characteristics ensure that the sample accu rately represents the targeted Indonesian omnichannel
consumer population.

Comprehensive Discussion of Findings
Operational Primacy of Journey Architecture over Intrinsic Traits (H1–H4)
The empirical results confirm that Customer Journey Design has a dominant, highly significant
positive influence on both Omnichannel Customer Experience ( 𝛽 = 0.458, 𝑓2 = 0.223 ) and
Omnichannel Trust ( 𝛽 = 0.458, 𝑓2 = 0.208), accepting H1 and H2. Personality Traits also display
statistically significant positive effects on Expe rience ( 𝛽 = 0.329, 𝑓2 = 0.115 ) and Trust ( 𝛽 =
0.305, 𝑓2 = 0.092), supporting H3 and H4.
Comparing path magnitudes (𝛽 = 0.458 vs. 𝛽 ≈ 0.315) and effect sizes (𝑓2 > 0.200 vs. 𝑓2 ≈
0.100) reveals a vital practical insight: system-level journey integration is a far more powerful manager
lever than relying on consumer intrinsic traits. Grounded in Cognitive Load Theory and Lemon &
Verhoef's (2016) journey framework, when e-commerce platforms offer real-time data synchronization,
seamless channel switching, and process con tinuity, cognitive friction is minimized. From a Trust
Theory perspective (Gefen, 2000), structural reliability serves as tangible proof of organizational
competence. While adaptive personalities process digital touchpoints more easily, e -commerce firms
can actively control and optimize journey design to elevate overall trust and customer experience (Gao
& Jiang, 2025; Gahler et al., 2023).
Experiential Reciprocity vs. Trust-Based Safety in Co-Creation (H5 & H6)
Hypotheses H5 and H6 were accepted, demonstra ting that Omnichannel Customer Experience
(𝛽 = 0.351, 𝑝 < 0.001) and Omnichannel Trust (𝛽 = 0.171, 𝑝 = 0.016) significantly drive Value Co-
Creation Behavior. Comparing path coefficients shows that experiential evaluation (𝛽 = 0.351) is more
than twice as impactful as trust (𝛽 = 0.171) in converting passive buyers into active co-creators.
Grounded in Service -Dominant Logic (Vargo et al., 2017) and the Cognitive -Affective-
Conative framework (Lemon & Verhoef, 2016), exceptional cross -channel interactions trigger stro ng
relational reciprocity, prompting consumers to contribute voluntary feedback, submit product reviews,
and assist fellow shoppers (Yi & Gong, 2013). Meanwhile, Trust Theory (Gefen, 2000) explains that
system trust acts as a baseline requirement of psycho logical safety, reassuring customers that their
voluntary inputs and personal data will be handled securely. However, it is the rich, engaging experience
that serves as the direct catalyst for active participation (Becker & Jaakkola, 2020; Cui et al., 2022).
Critical Analysis of Non-Significant Moderating Effects in Indonesian E-Commerce (H8 & H9)
Hypothesis H7 confirmed a direct positive effect of Channel Preference and Digital Proficiency
on Value Co -Creation Behavior (𝛽 = 0.174, 𝑡 = 1.978, 𝑝 = 0.048). However, the moderation testing
yielded non-significant interaction effects for both H8 (𝛽 = 0.067, 𝑡 = 0.764, 𝑝 = 0.445) and H9 (𝛽 =
−0.038, 𝑡 = 0.471, 𝑝 = 0.637), leading to the rejection of both moderating hypotheses.

offers a crucial theoretical and practical insight when interpreted through the contextual features of the
Indonesian e-commerce ecosystem:

V. CONCLUSION
This study provides robust empirical evidence regarding the complex structural dynamics
driving Value Co -Creation Behavior within the Indonesian e -commerce ecosystem. The empirical
findings confirm that the successful activation of customer co-creation participation is determined by an
integrated Customer Journey Design and an understanding of customer Personality Traits, operating
through the dual -mediating pathways of Omn ichannel Customer Experience and Omnichannel Trust.
Structural journey architecture plays the dominant role in shaping both consumer experiential
evaluations and systemic trust. Accumulated positive experiences and robust trust beliefs subsequently
catalyze voluntary customer contributions, including writing product reviews, offering constructive
service feedback, and peer advocacy (Yi & Gong, 2013). Conversely, neither Digital Proficiency nor
Channel Preference exhibits significant moderating effects, reve aling that experience quality and trust
act as universal drivers of customer value co-creation regardless of user technical skill.
Theoretical Implications
This research substantially advances omnichannel marketing theory in several key ways:
1. Extension of Service-Dominant Logic (S-D Logic): While foundational S-D Logic emphasizes that
value is co-created through actor-to-actor service interactions (Vargo et al., 2017), this study extends
the theory into a multi-touchpoint digital environment. It proves that value co-creation does not occur
in isolation at a single contact point, but relies on the overarching structural integration of the entire
customer journey (Lemon & Verhoef, 2016). Seamless cross-channel flows reduce cognitive friction,
allowing consumers to transition smoothly from passive recipients to active value co-creators.
2. Advancement of Dual-Mediation Mechanisms: This study enriches Trust Theory (Gefen, 2000) and
customer experience literature by conceptualizing and empirically validating a dual -mediation
framework. The findings establish that cognitive evaluations ( Omnichannel Customer Experience )
and affective reliance ( Omnichannel Trust ) act as complementary mechanisms. Experiential
satisfaction serves as the primary direct catalyst for active participation ( 𝛽 = 0.351), while trust
provides the essential baseline of psychological safety (𝛽 = 0.171) necessary for voluntary customer
engagement (Gahler et al., 2023; Becker & Jaakkola, 2020).
3. Reconceptualization of Boundary Conditions in Digital Commerce:  By empirically refuting the
moderating roles of Digital Proficiency and Channel Preference, this research offers a critical

Managerial Implications
Practically, these findings provide clear strategic guidance for e-commerce executives:
• Prioritize Cross-Channel Journey Integration over Channel Isolation: E-commerce managers should
allocate capital investments toward harmonizing cross -channel service a rchitectures such as real -
time inventory synchronization, unified account management, and effortless navigation between
mobile applications, web portals, and social commerce rather than developing isolated, channel -
specific promotions (Gao & Jiang, 2025).
• Establish Transparent Trust Assurance Systems:  To sustain customer engagement, firms must
continuously reinforce structural trust. This includes implementing robust data privacy protections,
transparent transaction tracking, reliable customer service supp ort, and clear dispute resolution
protocols, which together mitigate perceived vulnerability during online interactions (Gefen, 2000).
• Shift Away from Capability -Based User Segmentation:  Because user digital proficiency does not
alter the effectiveness of customer experience and trust in driving co-creation, managers should avoid
fragmenting resources on skill -based user customization. Instead, design efforts should focus on
universal, friction -free UI/UX principles that deliver consistent, high -value expe riences to all
customer segments.
Limitations and Future Research Directions
Despite its theoretical and practical contributions, this study has limitations that present
opportunities for future scholarly inquiry:
1. Model Explanatory Power:  The structural model explains 35.9% of the variance in Value Co -
Creation Behavior ( R2 = 0.359), indicating that a substantial portion of customer co -creation
participation is influenced by factors outside the current framework (Hair et al., 2019). Future
research is encouraged to integrate additional relational and psychological constructs, such as
Customer Engagement, Brand Equity, Perceived Social Value, or Gamification incentives.
2. Contextual Scope:  The empirical investigation focused broadly on general e -commerce users in
Indonesia. To enhance external validity, future studies should test and compare this structural model
across specific industry verticals, such as digital banking, omnichannel fashion retail, online travel
agencies (OTAs), or quick-service restaurant (QSR) applications.
