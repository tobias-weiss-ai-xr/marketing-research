ABSTRACT
Background: The digital era compels traditional financial institutions, including pawnshops  to innovate.
However, studies on pawnshop digital transformation balancing operational efficiency with trust -based,
relational services remain limited. This study explores the dual impact of digital service innovation on customer
experience and operational  transformation in a pawnshop context. Method: This qualitative, interview -based
case study was conducted at a public pawnshop in Indonesia (Pegadaian), which also funded the research; this
dual role is disclosed for transparency. Data were collected through semi -structured interviews (n  = 4), non-
participant observation, and document analysis, and were thematically analyzed using an interactive model of
data condensation, display, and conclusion drawing. Findings: Results reveal a duality , digital tools enhance
transactional convenience and back -office efficiency but create a relational gap in customer trust for complex
transactions and introduce workforce adaptation challenges. Customers abandoned digital channels for high -
value or repeat pawn transactions, citing the absence of face-to-face reassurance. Frontline staff reported added
reconciliation duties and role ambiguity, relying on informal peer learning rather than formal retraining. Branch
managers observed routine transactions shift ing to digital channels, while trust -intensive services remained
counter-bound. Interpreted through Service-Dominant Logic and digital transformation theory, technology acts
as an operant resource that must augment, not replace, human-centric value co-creation. Conclusion: Successful
innovation requires a digitally -augmented service model that aligns internal process optimization with
preserving trust-based customer relationships. By sustaining trust -based access to microfinance amid digital
disruption, this model strengthens the economic resilience of low- and middle-income households and supports
sustainable, inclusive financial development. Novelty/originality of this article : This article contributes a
novel, integrated framework for digital transformation in trust-intensive microfinance, uniquely identifying the
persistent relational gap and workforce adaptation dissonance  as critical, underexplored factors in the
pawnshop sector's evolution.

KEYWORDS: digital transformation; pawnshop; service innovation.

The global financial services sector is undergoing a profound transformation driven by
digitalization, characterized by the integration of technologies such as artificial intelligence,
blockchain, and mobile platforms (Gretz et al., 2017). This paradigm shift transcends mere
automation; it is re -engineering the fundamental architecture of finance, enabling hyper -
personalized services, creating new asset classes like cryptocurrencies, and fostering

dissolving traditional industry boundaries, as technology firms and fintech startups now
compete directly with established banks and credit institutions by offering superior  user
experiences, lower costs, and greater accessibility. The imperative for incumbents is no
longer optional, it is existential. To remain relevant and competitive, they must evolve from
product-centric entities to agile, platform -based service providers that can leverage data,
anticipate customer needs, and operate with unprecedented efficiency and transparency
(World Bank, 2021).
This sweeping digital tide sets the urgent and inescapable context for all financial
actors, including the long-established and traditionally analog pawnbroking industry, which
must now navigate this new technological landscape to secure its future. For pa wnshops,
this is not a peripheral trend but a central strategic challenge that questions their traditional
operating model. The industry's historical reliance on physical presence, tangible collateral,
and face -to-face trust -building places it at a fascina ting crossroads. On one hand,
digitalization presents transformative opportunities to expand reach through online
platforms, enhance valuation accuracy with data analytics, streamline operations with
cloud-based management, and build transparency with secure digital records. On the other
hand, it threatens to make their brick-and-mortar, high-touch model seem antiquated if they
fail to adapt (Otoritas Jasa Keuangan, 2021).
The core task, therefore, is to chart a course of digital integration that does not discard
their unique strengths ; personalized service, expertise in physical assets, and community
trust, but rather amplifies them. They must adopt technology not as a replacement for their
human element, but as a tool to augment it, making their services more accessible, efficient,
and reliable while preserving the essential relational foundation that distin guishes them
from purely algorithmic lenders. The future viability of the pawnbroking industry hinges on
its ability to perform this delicate synthesis, transforming from a purely analog institution
into a hybrid entity that is both digitally empowered and  authentically human (Kaplan &
Haenlein, 2019).
This shift is fundamentally altering competitive landscapes, redefining value
propositions, and reshaping consumer expectations for speed, transparency, and hyper -
personalization. It compels all institutions, from global banks to community-based lenders,
to re-evaluate their operational models and customer engagement strategies. Within this
sweeping tide of change, traditional institutions like pawnshops occupy a unique position.
They are entrenched entities with legacy processes and strong community ties, yet they face
the same imperative to modernize or risk obsolescence.  Their transformation, however, is
not merely a matter of adopting new software; it is a more complex endeavor of integrating
disruptive technologies into a business model that is inherently personal, trust -based, and
built on the physical appraisal of tang ible assets. This complexity arises because the core
value proposition of a pawnshop extends far beyond simple financial transactions. It is
deeply rooted in interpersonal judgment, the tactile assessment of collateral, and the
cultivation of long -term, tr ust-based relationships with clients, often within a local
community context (Alamsyah & Othman, 2020).
Therefore, digitization in this sector cannot follow a straightforward lift and shift
model. It requires a nuanced redesign of service processes where technology is woven into
the fabric of these personal interactions without unraveling them. The challenge  lies in
deploying artificial intelligence for valuation estimates or blockchain for secure record -
keeping in a way that enhances, rather than replaces, the expert eye of the pawnbroker and
the reassurance of a handshake. It involves using mobile platforms  not to create distance,
but to foster more frequent and transparent communication. Successfully navigating this
endeavor means viewing technology not as an end in itself, but as a set of tools to augment
human expertise, strengthen relational trust, and m ake the essential, physical act of
pawning an asset more accessible, secure, and efficient. This is a strategic and cultural
undertaking that redefines how value is co-created at the intersection of bytes and tangible
goods (Kumar & Reinartz, 2018).

Therefore, while the broader sector's digitalization provides the context and the tools,
the specific journey for pawnshops involves navigating the delicate balance between
leveraging technological efficiency and preserving the human -centric essence of the ir
service, a challenge that forms the central investigation of this study.  This shift demands
that traditional institutions, including pawnshops, evolve to meet changing consumer
expectations for speed, convenience, and transparency (Ahmed & Saleh, 2017). Historically,
pawnbroking has been a vital source of microcredit, yet its o perational model often faces
perceptions of being outdated and lacking in innovation (Julio, 2018).  Within this broader
context, the specific challenge for pawnshops lies in strategically leveraging digital
innovation to enhance their core service value proposition without eroding the essential
human-centric and trust-based elements of their business. Th is is a delicate balancing act,
as the drivers of digital efficiency ; automation, standardization, and remote access , can
inherently conflict with the personalized, discretionary, and relational nature of traditional
pawnbroking. The strategic imperative, therefore, is to identify and implement digital
solutions that act as enablers rather than replacements. For instance,  a mobile app should
not merely digitize a form but should provide transparent, educational content that builds
a customer's confidence before they even enter the shop. A digital inventory system must
not depersonalize a pledged item but should offer a secure, traceable record that enhances
the customer's sense of custodial trust. The innovation must be directed towards
augmenting the pawnbroker's expertise , perhaps through data analytics that inform
valuation, and deepening the customer relationship ; through streamlined communication
channels that maintain a personal touch. Ultimately, the challenge is to engineer a symbiosis
where technology handles transactional friction and expands reach, thereby freeing human
agents to focus on the high-trust, high-judgment interactions that constitute the true heart
of the service. Failure to achieve this balance risks creating a hollow efficiency that alienates
the core clientele while failing to attract a new, digitally-native customer base that still seeks
assurance in sensitive financial matters (Nambisan et al., 2019).
The theoretical underpinning of this study integrates Service -Dominant Logic (S -D
Logic), which posits that value is co-created through interactions between the provider and
the customer Lee & Trimi (2018) with frameworks of digital business transformation . S-D
Logic shifts the focus from tangible goods (the pawned item) to the application of operant
resources (knowledge, skills) in service provision. Concurrently, digital transformation
theory provides a lens to analyze how technology reshapes internal processes and external
engagement models. The synthesis of these theories establishes the state of the art,
suggesting that successful innovation in this sector is not merely technological adoption but
a holistic redesign of service ecosystems to foster superior co-created experiences (Fowler
et al., 2018a, 2018b).
Despite growing literature on fintech and digital banking, there exists a reported gap in
detailed, qualitative investigations focused specifically on pawnshops' digital transition
from a dual operational and customer experience perspective. Existing studies often
emphasize macroeconomic impacts or technological feasibility in isolation,  leaving the
nuanced, lived experiences of both customers and employees during this transformation
underexplored (Dexler, 2016; Adams, 2018).  This gap raises a critical problem , without a
deep understanding of how digital tools are integrated into frontline service encounters and
back-end operations, pawnshops risk implementing solutions that are misaligned with user
needs, potentially diminishing trust and value co-creation. Such misalignment can manifest
in two detrimental ways. Externally, customers may perceive new digital channels as
impersonal, confusing, or insecure, leading to frustration and a retreat from the service
altogether, thereby negating any intended convenience. Internally, employees may be
burdened with clunky systems that disrupt their workflow rather than support it, creating
resistance, reducing efficiency, and impairing their ability to serve customers effectively.
The consequence is a double erosion: the erosion of the customer's trust in the institution's
competence and fairness, and the erosion of the employee's capacity to act as a skilled,
empathetic agent of value. This can sever the very link , the collaborative interaction
between informed staff and confident customers, that is central to service-dominant logic.

Therefore, the implementation of technology cannot be driven by Information
Technology (IT) specifications alone; it must be informed by a holistic, ethnographic
understanding of the lived experiences and practical realities of both the people using the
service and the people delivering it. Strategic digital investment, in this context, becomes an
exercise in human-centered design, where the measure of success is not just system uptime
or app downloads, but the strengthening of the relational and procedural fabric that
sustains the business.
Therefore, the purpose of this manuscript is to explore and analyze the process of
service innovation in pawnshops within the digital era.  The study aims to provide an in -
depth qualitative understanding of (1) how digital innovations are reshaping customer
experiences and perceptions of pawnshop services, and (2) how these innovations drive
operational transformation within pawnshop organizations. This dual focus is essential for
capturing the holistic and often reciprocal nature of technological change. By investigating
the customer perspective, the research seeks to uncover the lived reality of interacting with
a digital pawnshop interface; how it alters perceptions of convenience, fairness, and trust,
and where new points of friction or satisfaction emerge. Simultaneously, by examining
operational transformation, the study delves into the internal re -engineering of processes,
roles, and workflows; it seeks to understand how management strategies are implemented,
how employees adapt to new tools and responsibilities, and where efficiencies are gained
or new bottlenecks are created. Crucially, the qualitative approach allows the research to
connect these two domains, revealing how changes on the organizational side directly
manifest in, and are sometimes reshaped by , the experiences and behaviors of customers.
Ultimately, this comprehensive inquiry aims to move beyond speculation and surface-level
metrics to generate rich, contextual insights into the complex, human-centered dynamics at
the heart of a traditional industry's digital evolution.
This study is guided by a one-tailed proposition derived from the theoretical synthesis:
that a strategic, customer-centric integration of digital tools will positively enhance both the
perceived value of pawnshop services and the efficiency of their inter nal operations, as
evidenced through qualitative accounts of experience and transformation. This directional
proposition serves as a foundational lens, focusing the investigation on the anticipated
beneficial outcomes of well -orchestrated digital integrati on. It posits that when
technological implementation is deliberately aligned with genuine customer needs and
embedded within a supportive operational framework, a synergistic effect occurs. This
synergy is not merely additive but multiplicative, where impr ovements in back -office
efficiency, such as faster processing times or reduced errors , directly contribute to a more
seamless and reliable customer experience, thereby elevating the service's perceived value.
Conversely, a positive shift in customer perception, driven by increased convenience and
transparency, can generate higher engagement and trust, which in turn justifies and
sustains further operational investments. The qualitative methodology is uniquely suited to
test this proposition, as it seeks not to quantify a statistical correlation but to gather rich,
narrative evidence that illuminates the causal pathways and contextual conditions through
which this positive enhancement manifests, or, crucially, where it breaks down, in the real-
world setting of the pawnshop.

2. Methods

The author employs a qualitative case study approach to answer the research questions
regarding customer experience and operational transformation (Yin, 2018). This
methodology is particularly apt as it allows for an intensive, holistic exploration of a
contemporary phenomenon , digital transition , within its real -life context, where the
boundaries between the phenomenon (digital innovation) and the context (the pawnshop
organization) are not clearly evident. By focusing on Pegadaian Wonosari as a bounded
system, the researcher can delve deeply into the intricate interplay of processes,
perceptions, and social interactions that quantitative methods might overlook. The case
study design facilitates the collection of multifaceted data through interviews, observations,

and documents; enabling the construction of a thick, descriptive narrative that captures the
complexity and nuance of change. It prioritizes depth over breadth, aiming to uncover the
how and why behind the observable patterns, such as the mechanisms through which a new
app influences customer trust or how a digital workflow redefines an employee's role. This
approach is fundamentally interpretive, seeking to understand the meanings that
stakeholders themselves ascribe to the digital transformation, thereby generating insights
that are grounded in the empirical reality of the case and that can inform both theory and
practice for similar institutions navigating analogous challenges. This method is selected for
its capacity to provide a rich, contextual, and in-depth understanding of complex, real-world
phenomena within their natural setting, aligning with the study's interpretivist
epistemological stance and its ontological view of re ality as socially constructed through
participant experiences. This systematic documentation of procedures is intended to allow
other researchers to replicate or adapt the design in comparable trust -intensive financial
service settings.
This research was conducted at the main and two branch offices of a publicly -owned
pawnshop company (hereafter referred to as Pegadaian Wonosari) in the Gunungkidul
Regency, Special Region of Yogyakarta Province, Indonesia. The case was selected through
criterion-based purposive case selection (Yin, 2018), based on two explicit criteria ;  (1)
Pegadaian Wonosari has actively launched several digital service initiatives (e.g., mobile
appraisal, online collateral management), making it an information -rich case, and (2) the
concentration of diverse customer demographics and centralized operati onal units allows
for efficient, in-depth data collection. These criteria were established prior to fieldwork to
ensure the case could adequately address the research questions concerning both customer
experience and operational transformation. The fieldwo rk was carried out over a five -
month period from January to May 2025, allowing for extended engagement and
observation of digital service interactions.

2.1 Population, sample, and key informants

The study population encompasses all customers and employees of Pegadaian
Wonosari. A purposive sampling technique was used to select key informants who could
provide the most insightful data related to the research focus (Creswell & Poth, 2018). The
final sample comprised 4 participants, divided into two groups. Customers (n = 2) selected
based on their recent use of both traditional and digital pawnshop services (e.g., mobile app
transactions, online inquiries). This group included a mix of long-term customers with more
than three years of transaction history and newer customers wh o had primarily adopted
digital channels, ensuring variation in digital exposure and trust orientation. Their ages
ranged from 24 to 58 years, with occupations spanning informal sector workers, small
business owners, and salaried employees, reflecting the typical demographic profile of the
pawnshop's clientele.
Employees (n  = 2) including frontline staff (counter services), digital platform
administrators, and middle managers involved in digital strategy implementation.
Participants varied in tenure, ranging from two to over fifteen years of service, which
allowed the study to capt ure both established, tacit work practices and newer, digitally -
oriented roles. This variation was intentional, as it enabled comparison between staff whose
daily work was most directly affected by digitalization and those overseeing its strategic
implementation. This study is based on a single -case, single-region design involving only
one publicly-owned pawnshop in Gunungkidul Regency with 4 informants. Therefore, the
findings cannot be generalized to the entire pawnshop industry. The insights generated are
analytically rather than statistically generalizable, and their transferability to other
pawnshop contexts, regions, or institutional types remains to be empirically tested.

3. Results and Discussion

This section presents the findings from the qualitative analysis, structured around the
two core themes of customer experience and operational transformation. The analysis
reveals that these themes are not isolated but are deeply interconnected, each influ encing
and being shaped by the other in the context of digital service innovation. The data shows
that changes in operational processes, driven by new digital tools, have direct and
sometimes unintended consequences on how customers perceive and interact w ith the
service. Conversely, evolving customer expectations and behaviors in the digital era create
new demands and pressures that necessitate internal operational adjustments. By
examining these two themes in tandem, a comprehensive picture emerges of an
organization in transition, navigating the complex interplay between internal efficiency
goals and external relationship management. This integrated view reveals that the path of
digital transformation is not linear or siloed but is instead a dynamic negot iation between
competing priorities. Decisions made to streamline back -office operations, such as
implementing a centralized digital ledger, directly influence the frontline employee's
capacity to engage with customers, sometimes creating new points of fri ction or requiring
new explanatory scripts (Buhalis & Sinarta, 2019).
Conversely, customer feedback and adoption patterns of new digital services generate
crucial data that must loop back to inform and recalibrate internal training programs and
system design. The organization is thus caught in a continuous feedback loop wher e
operational changes reshape customer experiences, and evolving customer behaviors, in
turn, demand further operational adjustments. This analysis moves beyond simply
cataloging changes to illuminate the inherent tensions and trade -offs of modernization. It
shows that successful adaptation is not about achieving perfection in either domain alone,
but about cultivating an organizational agility that can consciously manage the ripple effects
between the internal engine of operations and the external frontier  of customer relations,
ensuring that advancements in one area do not come at an unsustainable cost to the other
(Prasetyo & Setyawan, 2020).
This integrated perspective allows for a richer understanding of the successes,
challenges, and critical friction points that define the digital transformation journey at
Pegadaian Wonosari, moving beyond a superficial account of technology adoption to
uncover the substantive shifts in practice and perception occurring at both the frontline and
the back office. It reveals that success is not merely measured by the deployment of a new

3.1 The duality of digital customer experience: Enhanced convenience and emergent trust
barriers

The results reveal a dual -faceted impact of digital services on customer experience at
Pegadaian Wonosari. On one facet, digital tools such as the mobile application for
preliminary collateral valuation were reported to reported enhance perceived convenience,
autonomy, and efficiency. Customers valued the ability to conduct initial assessments
remotely, which reduced time, effort, and uncertainty before visiting the physical branch.
This aspect aligns with broader trends in finan cial technology that prioritize accessibility
and user empowerment. Conversely, the second facet reveals a contrasting layer where
digital adoption introduces reported challenges related to trust -building and relational
depth. For high-value items, complex transactions, or first-time customers, there remains a
strong, persistent preference for direct, face -to-face interaction. Customers expressed
concerns regarding the a ccuracy of automated appraisals for non -standard assets, data
security within digital platforms, and a perceived lack of the empathetic negotiation and
personalized assurance that characterize traditional pawnbroker interactions (Setiawan &
Tarigan, 2021).
This duality creates what the study terms a relational gap in the digital customer
journey, where transactional efficiency is achieved at the potential cost of the relational
trust that has historically underpinned the pawnshop business model. Therefore, the impact
is not uniformly positive but is instead characterized by a trade -off, suggesting that digital
services are most effective for standardized, routine transactions, while the human element
remains irreplaceable for building the confidence necessary  for more sensitive or
substantial engagements. This finding necessitates a strategic approach where
digitalization complements rather than replaces the interpersonal foundations of the
service. Primarily, digital tools like the mobile application for prel iminary collateral
valuation (digital appraisal) were consistently reported to enhance perceived convenience
and autonomy. Customers appreciated the reduced time and effort required for initial
transactions, as illustrated by one participant.

"I can check the estimated value of my gold from home... it saves me a trip if the estimate
is too low." (Customer 07)

This aligns with S-D Logic's emphasis on value co-creation, where the digital platform
acts as an operant resource, enabling customers to become active participants in the service
process (Sebastian et al., 2020).  However, this increased convenience coexists with
reported emergent barriers related to trust and relational depth. For complex or high-value
transactions, a strong preference for face -to-face interaction persisted. Customers
expressed concerns about data security, the accuracy of digital appraisals for unique items,
and a perceived lack of empathetic negotiation in digital channels. This finding nuances the

3.2 Operational transformation: Streamlined processes and workforce adaptation challenges

On the operational front, the integration of digital platforms has initiated a reported
transformation, characterized by increased back -office efficiency but also new skill
demands and workflow dissonance. The implementation of a centralized digital collat eral
management system has reduced manual record -keeping errors and accelerated audit
processes. Manager 01 noted,

"The time for monthly reporting has been cut almost by half." (Manager 01)

This operational efficiency gain can be conceptually linked to the reduction in
procedural friction, supporting Fowler et al.'s (2018a) assertion on technology -driven
process optimization (Pegadaian, 2023).  Nevertheless, this transformation is not
frictionless. The data uncovers a critical challenge in workforce adaptation. The integration
of digital platforms has fundamentally altered the required skill set for frontline employees
at Pegadaian Wonosari. Staf f are now expected to master a dual role ; maintaining their
traditional expertise in asset appraisal, customer relations, and regulatory compliance,
while simultaneously developing digital literacy to operate new systems, troubleshoot
technical issues for customers, and guide them through online processes (Susanto & Chang,
2020). This shift has created a tangible performance gap and a sense of role ambiguity
among some employees. The research indicates that this adaptation challenge manifests as
increased time spent on training, initial resistance to changing established workflows, and
varying levels of proficiency with the new technology across the team. This friction points
to a broader organizational hurdle in digital transformation, where technological
investment must be matched by a commensurat e investment in human capital
development. Without targeted upskilling programs, clear communication of new role
expectations, and change management support, the full benefits of digital operational tools
cannot be realized, and employee morale and effecti veness may be compromised.
Therefore, the path to seamless digital transformation is contingent upon viewing
technology implementation and workforce development as two inextricably linked
components of the same strategic initiative (McKinsey & Company, 2020).
The new system requires frontline staff to master dual roles; traditional pawnbroking
expertise and digital literacy for customer guidance. This evolution demands that
employees not only possess the foundational skills of asset valuation, risk assessment, and
empathetic customer service but also become proficient navigators and interpreters of the

3.3 Synthesizing experience and transformation: Towards an integrated service ecosystem

The discussion synthesizes the two thematic strands to propose that the ultimate
success of pawnshop digital innovation hinges on integrating customer experience and
operational transformation into a coherent service ecosystem. The results show that
operational gains (e.g., faster processing) do not automatically translate into superior
customer value if the relational gap persists . This supports and refines the state of the art
presented by Gretz et al. (2017); successful innovation requires a design that aligns internal
efficiency metrics with external value perceptions.
Compared to previous studies in traditional banking, the pawnshop context reveals a
heightened sensitivity to the human -interactive element. This distinction arises from the
fundamental nature of the pawnbroking transaction, which is intrinsically more per sonal,
trust-intensive, and emotionally charged than many standard banking services. While
digital banking often focuses on routine transactions like fund transfers or balance
inquiries, a pawnshop transaction involves the temporary surrender of personal valuables,
often with sentimental worth, as collateral for a loan. This act requires a profound level of
trust in the institution's fairness, expertise, and discretion. Therefore, the digital interface
in this sector does not merely process information; it must also mediate a delicate
relationship built on appraisal integrity, negotiation, and assurance. The findings from
Pegadaian Wonosari demonstrate that where banking digitization might successfully
automate for efficiency, pawnshop digitization must augment for empathy and trust (Wirtz
et al., 2020).
The relational gap identified is more acute here because the service core is not purely
financial but also deeply custodial and advisory. This heightened sensitivity necessitates
that digital innovation in pawnbroking be designed with a primary focus on supporting and
enriching the human interaction, rather than aiming to fully replace it. It underscores that
the metric of success extends beyond transactional speed to include the preservation of
perceived fairness, expert judgment, and personal connection t hroughout the customer
journey. Therefore, the proposed model is not a full replacement but a digitally-augmented

service model, where technology streamlines operational flow while human expertise

framework for hybrid value co-creation in trust-based, asset-lending financial institutions,

While prior research often examined digital transformation at a macro or strategic
level, this study delves into the granular, experiential realities of both customers and
employees as they navigate the change. The proposed framework moves beyond a binary
view of traditional versus digital by illustrating how value is co -created in a hybrid
ecosystem. It posits that the optimal model is not a full migration online, but a synergistic
integration where digital tools handle transactional efficiency and accessi bility, while
human expertise concentrates on building trust, providing complex judgment, and
delivering empathetic service for sensitive interactions (Hanson et al., 2015). This
addresses Dexler's call for a deeper understanding of micro -level impacts by explicitly
mapping how digital transitions reshape daily routines, skill requirements, customer
perceptions, and ultimately, the very nature of the service encounter in a pawnshop. The
framework emphasizes that the success of the digital transition is contingent on this careful
balance, making a specific contribution to the literature on fintech and financial inclusion
by providing a roadmap for institutions whose core business is inherently relational and
asset-based, ensuring that technological advancement strengthens rather than undermines
their foundational value proposition.

4. Conclusions

This study concludes that digital innovation in traditional pawnshops is not a
monolithic process of technological adoption but a complex, dual-track transformation that
necessitates a balance between operational efficiency and relational value co -creation.
These findings were synthesized from interviews with 4 informants, approximately 15
hours of non -participant observation, and analysis of internal documents at Pegadaian
Wonosari. The main findings of this study indicate that while digital tools were reported to
enhance transactional convenience and streamline back -office processes, as expressed by
customers who valued the ability to check estimated values from home, and by managers
who noted that monthly reporting time had been cut by nearly half , these tools
simultaneously introduced new challenges in trust-building and personal interaction. These
challenges emerged directly from customers' statements expressing a continued preference
for face-to-face transactions for high-value or heirloom items, as well as from frontline staff
who reported role ambiguity and additional workload demands in mastering digital
systems without adequate formal training.
The primary theoretical contribution of this research is the articulation and empirical
support for a digitally -augmented service model in the pawnshop context. This model
refines the application of Service-Dominant Logic in the digital era by demonstrating, based
on the collected data, that technology functions most effectively when positioned as an
operant resource that augments, rather than replaces, human-centric relational value. The
study's original finding lies in identifying the persistent relational gap  in the digital
customer journey and the consequential workforce adaptation dissonance  in operations,
both of which are supported by direct quotations from customers, employees, and
managers. These findings highlight that the human element remains the critical linchpin for
successful integration.  Consequently, the study contributes to the broader discourse on
digital business transformation by offering a framework specific to asset -based lending
institutions, emphasizing that the measure of innovation success , as observed in this case ,
is the alignment between optimized internal workflows and preserved trust -based
customer experiences. This insight provides both scholarly value in advancing context -
specific theory and practical guidance for pawnshop managers navigating the digital
transition.
