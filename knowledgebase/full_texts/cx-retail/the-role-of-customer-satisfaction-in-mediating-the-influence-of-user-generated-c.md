ABSTRACT
Developments in tourism and digital technology have transformed consumer behavior regarding information seeking,
accommodation selection, and experience sharing via digital platforms. Consequently, User -Generated Content (UGC),
Service Quality, Customer Sa tisfaction, and Electronic Word of Mouth (e -WOM) have become critical aspects of the
hospitality industry. Pecatu Guest House has experienced a decline in occupancy rates despite the overall increase in
tourist visits to Bali. Furthermore, customer reviews  on digital platforms reveal mixed assessments regarding service
quality and the guest experience. This study aims to analyze the influence of UGC and Service Quality on e -WOM, with
Customer Satisfaction serving as a mediating variable at Pecatu Guest House. A quantitative approach utilizing a survey
method was employed. The study population consisted of 4,598 customers who stayed at Pecatu Guest House between
2022 and 2024. The sample was selected using probability sampling —specifically, simple random samp ling. Based on
33 indicators and a ratio of six respondents per indicator, a total of 198 respondents were obtained. Data were analyzed
using Structural Equation Modeling -Partial Least Squares (SEM -PLS). The results indicate that UGC has a positive and
significant effect on both e -WOM and Customer Satisfaction. Service Quality also exerts a positive and significant
influence on e-WOM and Customer Satisfaction. Additionally, Customer Satisfaction has a positive and significant impact
on e-WOM. Furthermore, Customer Satisfaction was found to mediate the effects of both UGC and Service Quality on e -
WOM, demonstrating a complementary partial mediation relationship. The findings suggest that enhancing the quality
of user-generated information and service quality can strengthen customer satisfaction and foster positive e-WOM. This
study offers implications for Pecatu Guest House, highlighting the need to ensure alignment between digital information
and actual service, improve the customer experience, and encourage customers to share positive experiences.
Keywords:  UGC; Service Quality; Customer Satisfaction; and e-WOM

1 INTRODUCTION
Digital transformation has altered how consumers obtain information and evaluate service experiences. In the
hospitality industry, prospective customers do not rely solely on official information from accommodation providers;
they also consider reviews, comments, photos, recommendations, and the experiences of other customers available via
digital platforms. These shifts have made User -Generated Content (UGC) and Electronic Word -of-Mouth (e -WOM)
increasingly significant in shaping consumer perceptions and b ehavior. Donthu et al. (2021) highlight the extensive
development of e -WOM research, while Filieri (2015) emphasizes that information characteristics and the perceived
usefulness of reviews play a role in the acceptance of e-WOM information.
This context is relevant to accommodation businesses in Bali. Data from the thesis indicates that Pecatu Guest House
experienced a 1.41 percent decline in occupancy rates between 2022 and 2023, followed by a further drop of 8.92
percent between 2023 and 20 24, despite the growth in tourist visits to Bali. Reviews on digital platforms also reveal
mixed assessments of customer experiences, particularly regarding cleanliness, facilities, comfort, and Wi -Fi. These
conditions suggest that the quality of digital i nformation and the quality of actual service must be examined
concurrently to explain customer responses.
UGC refers to content created and shared by users regarding products or services. In the tourism context, information
originating from other users helps consumers evaluate alternatives before selecting accommodation. Muda and Hamzah
(2021) demonstrate the importance of UGC source credibility to e -WOM, while Khan et al. (2022) show that UGC can
influence tourist satisfaction. Regitz et al. (2024) also indicate a relationship between UGC -based factors and customer
satisfaction. These findings suggest that UGC  has the potential to influence not only digital communication but also the
evaluation of customer experiences.
In addition to UGC, service quality is a crucial factor in shaping the customer experience. Service quality relates to a
customer's assessment of the service received compared to their expectations. Dimensions such as tangibles, reliability,
responsiveness, assurance, and empathy serve as the basis for measuring service quality (Adhikari et al., 2023; Ali et al.,
2021). Rita et al. (2019) demonstrated the relationship between e -service quality, customer satisfaction, and customer
behavior, while Pratama and  Suprapti (2023) identified the role of customer satisfaction in the relationship between
service quality and e-WOM.
This study employs the Expectancy Disconfirmation Model (EDM) as the primary theory to explain that satisfaction is
formed through an evaluation of the alignment between perceived performance and customer expectations. Attribution
Theory is used as a suppo rting theory to explain how customers interpret the causes of their service experiences and
subsequently respond through communication behavior. Based on this framework, the study examines the influence of
UGC and service quality on e-WOM, the influence of both on customer satisfaction, the influence of customer satisfaction
on e-WOM, and the role of customer satisfaction as a mediator.
Based on the study's conceptual framework, the hypotheses tested are: H1 UGC has a positive and significant effect on
e-WOM; H2 UGC has a positive and significant effect on customer satisfaction; H3 Service quality has a positive and
significant effect on e-WOM; H4 Service quality has a positive and significant effect on customer satisfaction; H5
Customer satisfaction has a positive and significant effect on e -WOM; H6 Customer satisfaction mediates the effect of
UGC on e-WOM; and H7 Customer satisfaction mediates the effect of service quality on e-WOM.

2 RESEARCH METHODS
This study employs an associative research design with a quantitative causal approach. The subjects of the study are
consumers who stayed at Pecatu Guest House after booking through online travel agents between 2022 and 2024. The
study population consists of 4,598 individuals. Probability sampling was used, specifically the simple random sampling
method. The sample size was determined based on 33 indicators, applying a ratio of six respondents per indicator,
resulting in a total of 198 respondents.
The research variables comprise User -Generated Content (UGC) as the first exogenous variable, Service Quality as the
second exogenous variable, Customer Satisfaction as the mediating variable, and Electronic Word of Mouth (e-WOM) as
the endogenous variable. UGC is measured via credibility, information quality, and usefulness, based on Aboalganam et
al. (2025). Service Quality is measured through tangibles, reliability, responsiveness, assurance, and empathy, based on
Adhikari et al. (2023) and Ali et al. (2 021). Customer Satisfaction is assessed using indicators of service quality,
satisfaction with the service, and the alignment of service with expectations. e-WOM is measured using the dimensions
of intensity, valence of opinion, and content.
Data were collected via questionnaires. The instrument was pre -tested on 30 respondents outside the main study
sample. Data analysis was conducted using SEM -PLS with SmartPLS 4.0. Measurement model evaluation covered
convergent validity, discriminant validity, and composite reliability. Structural model evaluation involved R -square, Q-
square predictive relevance, and the testing of direct and indirect effects using bootstrapping. The significance of
relationships was assessed at the 5 percent level.

Satisfied customers are more likely to provide recommendations and share positive experiences with others. Thus,

Customer satisfaction was found to mediate the effect of UGC on e -WOM. The indirect effect showed a coefficient of
0.301, a t -statistic of 8.667, and a p -value of 0.000. Since the effects of UGC on customer satisfaction, customer
satisfaction on e-WOM, and the direct effect of UGC on e-WOM were all positive and significant, the observed mediation

that user-generated information can shape satisfaction before prompting customers to share their experiences via e -
WOM.
Customer satisfaction also mediates the effect of service quality on e -WOM, with an indirect effect coefficient of 0.418,
a t -statistic of 7.657, and a p -value of 0.000. The direct effect of service quality on e -WOM remains significant and
positive; theref ore, the mediation observed is also complementary partial mediation. These results indicate that
improving service quality can generate e-WOM through two pathways: directly via the service experience and indirectly
via customer satisfaction. These findings  align with those of Pratama and Suprapti (2023) as well as Udayana and
Martini (2024).
From the perspective of the Expectancy Disconfirmation Model, the study results demonstrate that the alignment
between expectations and service performance serves as a crucial foundation for forming customer satisfaction. The
customer satisfaction indicato r with the highest value in this thesis is the alignment of service with customer
expectations. From the perspective of Attribution Theory, customers interpret the service experience they receive and
subsequently communicate their evaluations to others. Th ese two perspectives help explain how UGC and service
quality can shape satisfaction, while satisfaction, in turn, drives e -WOM.

4 CONCLUSION
This study demonstrates that User-Generated Content (UGC) and Service Quality are significant determinants of e-WOM
at Pecatu Guest House. UGC has a positive and significant impact on both Customer Satisfaction and e -WOM, while
Service Quality also positiv ely and significantly influences Customer Satisfaction and e -WOM. Furthermore, Customer
Satisfaction has a positive and significant effect on e-WOM.
Customer Satisfaction was found to mediate the impact of UGC on e -WOM and Service Quality on e -WOM, exhibiting
complementary partial mediation. Consequently, fostering positive e-WOM can be achieved not only through the direct
improvement of user -generated information quality and service quality but also by enhancing customer satisfaction.
This study was limited to a single case —Pecatu Guest House—and a sample of 198 respondents who had stayed there
between 2022 and 2024. Future research could test this model across multiple accommodation businesses, incorporate
variables such as perceived value, online customer reviews, trust, brand image, or customer loyalty, and employ
different research approaches.
