Abstract
With the rapid growth of e -commerce and online food delivery services, packaging has become increasingly
important in protecting goods during transportation and enhancing customer experience when presented in
nice packaging. However, this development has a lso led to widespread overpackaging, the excessive use of
packaging materials beyond what is necessary for product protection and presentation. Overpackaging results
in unnecessary resource consumption, increased waste generation, and additional environmental burdens. This
paper reviews the existing literature to examine the current situation, the major causes, and the impacts of
overpackaging across different aspects in the e -commerce era. In addition, the paper summarizes potential
mitigation strategies p roposed to address this problem. By synthesizing and analyzing findings from recent
academic research, this study aims to provide a comprehensive understanding of how overpackaging has
developed and how it affects environmental sustainability, economic eff iciency, and consumer behavior.
Finally, the findings emphasize the need for more sustainable packaging designs and improved waste
management practices to reduce the negative impacts of excessive packaging.
Keywords
overpackaging, e-commerce, environmental impact, sustainable packaging, waste management

1. Introduction
With the expansion of global consumer markets and the rapid development of e -commerce platforms,
packaging has become an essential component of product distribution systems. Packaging plays a critical role
in protecting products from physical damage during  transportation and ensuring that goods reach consumers
in acceptable condition. In addition, packaging is often used as a marketing tool to enhance brand identity and
influence consumer perception.
However, in many cases, packaging exceeds what is functionally required for protection and basic
presentation. This condition is commonly defined as overpackaging. The phenomenon has become
increasingly visible in online shopping and food delivery services , where products are frequently packaged
with multiple layers, oversized containers, and decorative materials that do not contribute to product safety [1].

The rise of e-commerce has intensified this issue. Products often pass through multiple handling stages and
long-distance transportation processes, which encourages the use of additional protective materials. At the
same time, consumer expectations for visually appea ling packaging have also influenced packaging design
decisions. As environmental sustainability becomes a major global concern, the environmental and economic
consequences of overpackaging have attracted growing attention from researchers and policymakers.
This paper aims to review existing academic literature to analyze the definitions, current situation, structural
causes, and impacts of overpackaging. In addition, it examines mitigation strategies to reduce excessive
packaging in modern supply chains.
2. Theoretical Background and Current Situation
2.1 Definitions, Characteristics, and Measurement of Overpackaging
Overpackaging generally refers to the use of packaging materials that exceed what is necessary for product
containment, protection, and basic presentation [1]. Previous studies have identified several characteristics of
overpackaging, including excessive l ayers, oversized containers, and the use of non -essential decorative
materials [1,3]. In many cases, packaging design prioritizes marketing value and visual appeal rather than
functional efficiency.
Research on paper and food packaging indicates that overpackaging can still occur even when recyclable
materials are used. This is because environmental performance depends not only on material type but also on
total material volume and design efficiency [ 1,4]. Therefore, using environmentally friendly materials does
not necessarily eliminate the problem if packaging remains excessive.
Measurement of overpackaging is often based on indicators such as material usage, packaging volume, and
the ratio between packaging weight and product weight. These indicators are commonly used in life -cycle
assessment studies to evaluate the environmental impact of packaging systems [4].
2.2 Current Situation of Overpackaging in the E-commerce Era
The rapid growth of e-commerce has significantly increased the demand for packaging materials. Compared
with traditional retail, online shopping requires products to undergo multiple transportation and handling
processes, leading to the use of additional p rotective packaging, such as plastic cushioning, foam, and larger
boxes.
Empirical studies indicate that packaging waste accounts for approximately 30 –40% of municipal solid
waste in many developed regions [5]. In the context of e -commerce, this proportion continues to grow as
secondary and tertiary packaging become increasingl y widespread. Moreover, packaging production and
disposal contribute substantially to lifecycle greenhouse gas emissions, particularly when plastic materials are
involved [4,5].
E-commerce logistics systems often prioritize product safety and customer satisfaction rather than
packaging efficiency [2,3]. As a result, companies tend to use more packaging materials to reduce the risk of
damage. At the same time, the popularity of “pr emium packaging” and unboxing experiences has further
increased the complexity and volume of packaging.
These trends indicate that the expansion of online retail has intensified the environmental burden associated
with packaging systems, making overpackaging a significant issue in modern consumer markets.
2.3 Structural Causes of Overpackaging
Overpackaging is not only the result of basic product protection or marketing strategies but is also
influenced by structural mechanisms within modern logistics systems. One key factor is the packaging
selection process used in automated warehouse operations. These systems often rely on standardized box sizes
and algorithm-based matching, which may lead to inefficient packaging decisions. For example, small products
are frequently placed in larger boxes due to limited size options or to prioritize operational speed over material
efficiency.

In addition, logistics systems are typically designed to minimize product damage and return rates. This
creates a risk-avoidance strategy that uses additional packaging to ensure product safety. While this approach
may reduce short -term financial risks, it  increases material consumption and long -term environmental costs
[2,3].
Supply chain fragmentation also contributes to overpackaging. Products may pass through multiple
distribution stages, and each stage may introduce additional packaging for protection or branding purposes.
This layered packaging effect significantly increases total material usage.
Consumer behavior further reinforces these structural factors. Research shows that consumers often
associate complex or premium packaging with higher product quality, which may encourage companies to
maintain excessive packaging practices [6]. These combined factors suggest that overpackaging is a systemic
issue embedded in both technological systems and market expectations.
3. Impacts of Overpackaging
Overpackaging has significant environmental, economic, and social impacts. From an environmental
perspective, excessive packaging increases the consumption of natural resources such as paper, plastic, and
energy used in manufacturing processes [4,5]. This leads to higher greenhouse gas emissions and greater
environmental degradation.
Life cycle assessment studies demonstrate that packaging materials contribute to environmental impacts
across all stages, including production, transportation, and disposal [4,7]. Increased packaging weight also
increases fuel consumption during transportation, further amplifying carbon emissions.
In terms of waste management, overpackaging contributes to a growing volume of solid waste. Recycling
systems often struggle to process complex packaging materials, especially when multiple materials are
combined. As a result, a significant portion of pack aging waste ends up in landfills, even when recyclable
materials are used [5,6].
Economically, overpackaging increases production costs for companies and adds to transportation expenses
due to heavier, bulkier packages. In addition, governments face higher costs for waste collection and
processing [7].
Socially, the issue of overpackaging has raised public awareness of environmental sustainability.
Consumers are becoming more conscious of packaging waste and are increasingly demanding environmentally
responsible packaging solutions. This shift in consumer behavior may influence future packaging practices.
4. Discussion and Mitigation Strategies
The literature suggests that overpackaging results from a combination of structural, economic, and
behavioral factors. While packaging is necessary to ensure product safety, excessive packaging creates a trade-
off between protection efficiency and environmental sustainability.
One important mitigation strategy is optimizing packaging design. Companies can reduce material usage
by improving packaging structures, minimizing space, and selecting materials with lower environmental
impact [2]. Design improvements can significantly reduce both resource consumption and waste generation.
Another strategy involves improving logistics systems. More advanced packaging algorithms could
optimize box selection based on product size and fragility, rather than relying on fixed box categories. This
would reduce inefficiencies in packaging decisions.
Policy regulations also play a critical role. Governments can implement standards that limit excessive
packaging or encourage the use of recyclable materials. At the same time, consumer education can promote
sustainable consumption behavior by increasing awareness of packaging waste [6].
Overall, reducing overpackaging requires coordinated efforts among businesses, policymakers, and
consumers. Technological innovation, regulatory frameworks, and behavioral changes must work together to
achieve more sustainable packaging systems.

5. Conclusion
Overpackaging has become a significant issue in the e -commerce era, driven by the growing demand for
product protection and an enhanced customer experience. This paper reviewed the definitions, current situation,
structural causes, and impacts of overpackaging, and discussed potential mitigation strategies based on existing
research.
The findings indicate that overpackaging contributes to increased resource consumption, environmental
pollution, and economic inefficiencies. Addressing this issue requires improvements in packaging design,
logistics systems, and policy frameworks.
Future research should focus on integrating packaging optimization technologies with sustainability
frameworks to reduce environmental impacts while maintaining product safety.
