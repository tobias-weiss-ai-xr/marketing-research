Digital technologies have changed how consumers move across physical and digital
channels, making coordination among touchpoints increasingly relevant to
customer retention. This study examined the association between Omnichannel
Marketing Integration and Customer Loyalty through the mediating roles of
Customer Experience and Perceived Convenience. Stimulus Organism Response
Theory and Relationship Marketing Theory were used to explain how channel
integration may shape customers' internal evaluations and su bsequent loyalty
responses. The study used a quantitative explanatory survey of 250 Indonesian
consumers who had used at least two marketing channels and completed a
transaction within the previous six months. Data were analyzed with Partial Least
Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM) using SmartPLS 4. Omnichannel
Marketing Integration was positively associated with Customer Experience,
Perceived Convenience, and Customer Loyalty. Customer Experience and
Perceived Convenience were also positively associated with Customer Loyalty, and
both indirect paths were statistically significant. The indirect coefficient through
Perceived Convenience (beta = 0.291) was larger than the indirect coefficient
through Customer Experience (beta = 0.202), suggesting t hat functional ease may
be particularly relevant in the omnichannel journeys represented in this sample.
These findings extend omnichannel research by considering experiential and
convenience-based evaluations within one model and provide practical directi on
for firms seeking greater consistency, accessibility, and efficiency across customer
touchpoints.

1. INTRODUCTION
Digital commerce has made it possible for consumers to move between stores, websites, mobile
applications, marketplaces, and social media within a single purchasing journey. The strategic issue is
no longer whether firms operate several channels, but whether customers experience those channels as
connected. When product information, prices, promotions, stock availability, and service procedures
differ across touchpoints, the presence of many channels can increase rather than reduce customer
effort. Research on omnichannel customer experience therefore emphasizes continuity and integration
across physical and digital interfaces rather than the simple accumulation of channels (Gerea et al.,
2021; Massi et al., 2023; Mele & Russo -Spena, 2021). This distinction provides the starting point for
examining how Omnichannel Marketing Integration relates to Customer Loyalty.
Consumers increasingly begin a shopping activity in one channel and complete it in another,
producing journeys that are less linear and more dependent on cross-channel coordination. A customer

integration is therefore relevant not only as an operational capability but also as a customer -facing
condition that may shape later relational outcomes.
Customer Loyalty is especially important in this setting because repeated patronage depends on
more than the availability of digital access. Prior research shows that loyalty and repurchase -related
outcomes are connected with customers' evaluations of serv ice journeys, perceived value, trust,
engagement, and brand relationships (Bowden & Mirzaei, 2021; Khandai et al., 2022; Reitsamer et al.,
2024; Vo et al., 2025). In omnichannel environments, these evaluations are formed across multiple
interactions rather than at one isolated point of contact. A technically integrated system may therefore
have limited relational value if customers do not experience the journey as coherent, comfortable, and
easy to complete. This creates a need to examine the psychological mechanisms that connect channel
integration with loyalty rather than relying only on a direct relationship between the two constructs.
Stimulus Organism Response Theory offers one explanation for these mechanisms by
distinguishing external conditions from internal evaluations and behavioral responses. In this study,
Omnichannel Marketing Integration represents the external stimulus becaus e customers encounter it
through coordinated information, services, and transaction processes. Customer Experience and
Perceived Convenience represent two forms of organism -level evaluation: the former captures the
broader quality of the shopping journey, while the latter reflects the effort, accessibility, flexibility, and
time efficiency perceived by customers. Customer Loyalty represents the response expressed through
continued use, preference, repurchase intention, and recommendation behavior. This inte rpretation is
consistent with recent applications of the SOR framework in digital and service settings (Duong, 2023;
Gonzales-Gutierrez et al., 2026; Jeong et al., 2022; Sohaib et al., 2022; Uzir et al., 2025). Relationship
Marketing Theory complements this logic by explaining why favorable evaluations accumulated across
interactions may support a more durable relationship with the firm.
Previous studies generally report favorable outcomes from stronger channel integration, but the
mechanisms examined are not uniform. Omnichannel integration has been associated with satisfaction,
loyalty intentions, purchase intention, service quality, and  customer engagement (Lazaris et al., 2021;
Le & Nguyen -Le, 2020; Ma et al., 2022; Natarajan & Ramanan V, 2023). A related stream of research
shows that Customer Experience is connected with satisfaction, engagement, revisit intention,
repurchase, and loyalty-related behavior (Baquero, 2025; Jaakkola & Terho, 2021; Mustafa et al., 2022;
Safeer et al., 2021). Another stream emphasizes service convenience, online convenience, payment
continuity, delivery flexibility, and information accessibility as condition s associated with future
purchase or continued use (Addae et al., 2024; Lina et al., 2022; Purohit et al., 2022; Saha et al., 2023;
Zhao et al., 2022). Taken together, this literature indicates that both experiential and functional
evaluations may matter, but it does not fully establish how they operate simultaneously within the same
loyalty model.

Convenience are often examined in separate models or assigned different positions in the nomological
structure, making their relative roles difficult to compare (Ladeira  et al., 2026; Pham, 2026). Second,
much of the evidence has emerged from specific industries or developed -market settings, while
emerging markets can present different combinations of digital adoption, physical retail use, payment
practices, and customer expectations (Cavusgil, 2021; Morris et al., 2023). The Indonesian setting is
therefore useful for examining whether customers' loyalty -related responses are associated more

2. LITERATURE REVIEW AND HYPOTHESIS DEVELOPMENT
2.1  Omnichannel Marketing Integration and Customer Loyalty
Omnichannel Marketing Integration refers to the extent to which customers encounter coordinated
information, services, and transactions when moving across a firm's channels. Integration can reduce
discontinuity during the customer journey because customers  are less likely to encounter conflicting
information or repeated processes when switching touchpoints. Prior studies have linked stronger
integration with satisfaction, purchase intention, loyalty intention, and relationship performance
(Cattapan & Pongsakornrungsilp, 2022; Lazaris et al., 2021; Ma et al., 2022; Zhao et al., 2022). From a
relationship marketing perspective, consistent interactions may provide a basis for continued
patronage because the firm becomes easier to use and more predictable across encounters. On this basis,
H1 proposes that Omnichannel Marketing Integration is positively associated with Customer Loyalty.
2.2  Omnichannel Marketing Integration, Customer Experience, and Perceived Convenience
Integrated channels can shape both the experiential and functional evaluations that customers form
during a shopping journey. Consistent information, synchronized transactions, and continuity of service
can improve the perceived quality of interactions acr oss touchpoints, which is central to Customer
Experience (Gerea et al., 2021; Jaakkola & Terho, 2021; Massi et al., 2023). The same coordination can
reduce the time and effort required to search, compare, pay, receive products, or resolve post-purchase
issues, thereby increasing Perceived Convenience (Addae et al., 2024; Purohit et al., 2022; Saha et al.,
2023). In SOR terms, these constructs represent internal evaluations generated in response to the
external stimulus provided by channel integration. Accor dingly, H2 proposes a positive association
between Omnichannel Marketing Integration and Customer Experience, while H3 proposes a positive
association between Omnichannel Marketing Integration and Perceived Convenience.
2.3  Customer Experience, Perceived Convenience, and Customer Loyalty
Favorable Customer Experience can strengthen loyalty -related responses because repeated positive
interactions contribute to satisfaction, perceived value, and a stronger relationship with the firm.
Evidence from service and digital settings has connected e xperience with revisit intention, advocacy,
engagement, and loyalty (Baquero, 2025; Correa et al., 2021; Mustafa et al., 2022; Reitsamer et al., 2024;
Safeer et al., 2021). Perceived Convenience can also support continued use by lowering non -monetary
costs such as time, effort, and procedural complexity (Lina et al., 2022; Pham, 2026; Saha et al., 2023).

2.4  Mediating Roles of Customer Experience and Perceived Convenience
The SOR framework implies that external stimuli can influence behavioral responses through internal
evaluations rather than only through direct paths. Applied to this study, channel integration may be
associated with loyalty partly because customers experi ence a better journey and partly because they
perceive the journey as easier to complete. Previous research has examined experience - and
convenience-related mechanisms in digital commerce, although they have not always been tested
simultaneously within one omnichannel model (Chen & Yang, 2021; Le & Nguyen-Le, 2020; Saha et al.,
2023). Testing both indirect paths allows the study to assess whether the experiential and functional
mechanisms coexist and how their estimated magnitudes compare in this sample. H6  therefore
proposes that Customer Experience mediates the relationship between Omnichannel Marketing
Integration and Customer Loyalty, and H7 proposes that Perceived Convenience mediates the same
relationship.

3. METHOD
3.1 Research Design
This study used a quantitative explanatory survey design to examine the statistical relationships among
Omnichannel Marketing Integration, Customer Experience, Perceived Convenience, and Customer
Loyalty. The design was appropriate because the study tested a predefined model containing direct and
indirect relationships among latent constructs measured with questionnaire indicators. Because the
data were collected through a survey at one period rather than through experimental manipulation, the
estimated structural paths are interpreted as associations within the proposed model rather than as
proof of causality. Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM) was selected because
the analysis required simultaneous assessment of the measurement mo del and the structural
relationships among multiple latent variables.
3.2 Research Setting, Population, and Sample
The study was conducted in Indonesia from January to April 2026 in retail settings where customers
could interact through integrated online and offline marketing channels. The target population
consisted of consumers who had purchased products from compani es operating through multiple
marketing channels. Purposive sampling was used so that respondents had relevant experience with
the phenomenon being measured. Eligible respondents were at least 17 years old, had purchased
products from the selected brands, had used at least two different marketing channels, and had
completed a transaction within the previous six months. Respondents who did not meet these criteria
were excluded. The final sample comprised 250 consumers.
3.3 Research Instrument
Data were collected with a structured questionnaire developed from prior studies on omnichannel
marketing, customer behavior, and relationship marketing. All items used a five -point Likert scale
ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). Omn ichannel Marketing Integration was
represented by six indicators covering consistency of product information, synchronization of prices
and promotions, ease of switching between channels, integration of transaction processes, consistency
of customer service, and availability of real-time stock information. Customer Experience contained six
indicators addressing ease of interaction, shopping comfort, emotional responses, service speed,
satisfaction with the shopping journey, and perceived value. Perceived Convenience was assessed with

3.4 Instrument Validation and Reliability
Before the main survey, three experts in marketing management and consumer behavior reviewed the
questionnaire for item clarity, relevance, and representativeness. The instrument was revised following
their feedback and then piloted with 30 respondents whose characteristics were similar to those of the
target population. In the main PLS -SEM assessment, convergent validity was evaluated through outer
loadings and Average Variance Extracted (AVE), using thresholds of 0.70 and 0.50, respectively.
Discriminant validity was assessed with the Heterotrait -Monotrait Ratio (HTMT), with values below
0.90 treated as acceptable. Internal consistency was assessed through Cronbach's alpha and Composite
Reliability, for which values above 0.70 were considered satisfactory. These procedures correspond to
the validity and reliability results reported in the measurement-model section.
3.5 Data Collection Procedure
The data collection process began with questionnaire development, expert review, pilot testing, and
revision of the instrument. Eligible respondents then completed the survey through online and offline
distribution channels. Online questionnaires were diss eminated through digital platforms and social
media, while offline questionnaires were administered at selected retail locations. Completed responses
were screened for incompleteness and potential outliers before valid cases were coded and prepared
for ana lysis. To reduce response -evaluation pressure and potential common -method concerns,
participants were informed that there were no right or wrong answers and that their responses would

3.5 Discussion
The positive association between Omnichannel Marketing Integration and Customer Loyalty
indicates that customers who perceived stronger coordination across channels also reported stronger
loyalty-related responses. The direct coefficient (beta = 0.277) was  statistically significant but smaller
than the coefficients linking integration with Customer Experience and Perceived Convenience,
suggesting that integration may matter partly through what customers experience after encountering
coordinated channels. Th is pattern is consistent with SOR logic because an external service
configuration is unlikely to determine a behavioral response independently of customers' internal
evaluations (Jeong et al., 2022; Sohaib et al., 2022). It also aligns with studies reporti ng that integrated
online and offline journeys are associated with satisfaction, repurchase intention, and loyalty -related
outcomes (Lazaris et al., 2021; Ma et al., 2022; Zhou & Hudin, 2025). For managers, the implication is
that adding or connecting channels is not sufficient if customers still encounter inconsistency during the
journey. The relational value of integration appears to depend on how that coordination is translated
into customer-level benefits.
Omnichannel Marketing Integration was strongly associated with Customer Experience (beta =
0.744), which supports the view that cross-channel continuity forms part of the experience rather than
merely operating in the background. Consistency in information, promotions, transactions, and service
can reduce interruptions as customers move between touchpoints, allowing the journey to be evaluated
as one connected interaction. This interpretation is consistent with service -journey research and with
studies show ing that seamless or authentic omnichannel arrangements can improve the quality of
customer interactions (Gerea et al., 2021; Jaakkola & Terho, 2021; Massi et al., 2023; Natarajan & Veera
Raghavan, 2023). Relationship Marketing Theory helps explain why this matters: repeated experiences
of continuity can make the firm's behavior more predictable and can support a stable relational
evaluation over time. At the same time, the cross -sectional design does not establish that integration
caused the reported exper ience. The result should therefore be understood as strong evidence of
alignment between perceived integration and perceived experience within the sampled customer
journeys.
A similarly strong association was observed between Omnichannel Marketing Integration and
Perceived Convenience (beta = 0.763). This coefficient suggests that customers who perceived better -
integrated channels were also more likely to report easier access to information, product comparison,
payment, delivery, returns, and time-efficient transactions. Such a pattern is plausible because channel
integration can reduce duplicated steps and make resources available at the point in the journey where
customers need them. Prior research has linked multichannel integration, online convenience, payment
continuity, and service convenience with favorable customer evaluations and continued-use intentions
(Addae et al., 2024; Lina et al., 2022; Purohit et al., 2022; Saha et al., 2023). Within the SOR framework,
Perceived Convenience can therefore be interpreted as a cognitive evaluation of the effort demanded by
the omnichannel environment. The result gives functional ease a central position in the model without
implying that convenience is the only benefit customers derive from integrated channels.
Customer Experience was positively associated with Customer Loyalty (beta = 0.271), indicating
that the quality of the reported shopping journey was related to customers' willingness to continue
using, prefer, and recommend the firm. This relationship is consistent with the relational argument that
favorable interactions can accumulate into stronger behavioral intentions and customer attachment.
Similar associations between experience, value, engagement, revisit intention, advocacy, and loyalty
have been re ported in service and digital settings (Baquero, 2025; Correa et al., 2021; Mustafa et al.,
2022; Reitsamer et al., 2024; Safeer et al., 2021). The present finding adds an omnichannel
interpretation: experience is formed across linked touchpoints, so an ot herwise positive interaction in
one channel may not compensate for repeated discontinuity elsewhere in the journey. Firms should
therefore evaluate experience across transitions between channels rather than only within individual

and logistics quality as factors connected with continued use a nd repurchase behavior (Kim & Yum,
2024; Lina et al., 2022; Ngo et al., 2025; Pham, 2026). This result does not invalidate experiential
explanations of loyalty; rather, it indicates that experience and convenience capture different evaluative
dimensions that can coexist. Within this Indonesian sample, the functional dimension appears to have
had the larger estimated relationship with Customer Loyalty.
The mediation results clarify why the direct association between integration and loyalty should not
be interpreted in isolation. The indirect path through Customer Experience was significant (beta =
0.202), and the indirect path through Perceived Convenience was also significant (beta = 0.291), while
the direct OMI-to-CL path remained significant. This pattern is consistent with complementary partial
mediation: Omnichannel Marketing Integration was associated with Customer Loyalty both directly and
through two positive intervening mechanisms. The result supports the central SOR proposition that an
external stimulus can be linked with a response through organism-level evaluations, while Relationship
Marketing Theory helps interpret how those evaluations can relate to continued customer relationships.
Previous work has considered experience - or convenience-based mediation in related digital settings
(Chen & Yang, 2021; Le & Nguyen-Le, 2020; Saha et al., 2023), but testing both paths in one model makes
their par allel roles visible. The larger indirect coefficient through Perceived Convenience further
suggests that the functional route deserves greater theoretical attention in omnichannel loyalty
research, while remaining specific to the present model and sample.
The Indonesian setting provides useful context for interpreting these relationships, but the results
should not be extended automatically to regional, cultural, or industry differences that were not
examined in the analysis. The sample was defined by recen t use of at least two marketing channels,
which means the findings reflect consumers who had direct experience navigating multiple touchpoints.
This characteristic is relevant because omnichannel integration becomes meaningful to customers when
channel transitions actually occur. Differences among brands, sectors, or channel combinations may
nevertheless influence how convenience and experience are evaluated. The practical meaning of
convenience, for example, may vary across retail categories because custom ers face different search,
payment, delivery, and service requirements. Future research can address this issue by linking
respondents to a clearly defined focal retailer and by comparing sectors or customer segments when
appropriate data are collected.
Taken together, the results position Omnichannel Marketing Integration as a customer -facing
stimulus whose relationship with loyalty is partly explained by two distinct evaluations: the overall
quality of the journey and the convenience of completing it. The theoretical value of the model lies less
in adding another direct predictor of loyalty and more in showing that experiential and functional
mechanisms operate simultaneously. This interpretation extends the SOR framework by separating two
organism-level responses that are related but conceptually different, while Relationship Marketing
Theory connects those evaluations with sustained customer relationships. The practical implication is
similarly specific: firms should synchronize information and processes across channels, but they should
assess success in terms of whether customers experience continuity and reduced effort rather than only
whether backend systems are technically connected. Because the evidence is based on self -reported,

4. CONCLUSION
This study examined how Omnichannel Marketing Integration was associated with Customer
Loyalty through Customer Experience and Perceived Convenience among 250 Indonesian consumers
with recent multichannel shopping experience. All seven proposed paths were statistically supported.
Omnichannel Marketing Integration was positively associated with Customer Experience, Perceived
Convenience, and Customer Loyalty, while both mediators were positively associated with Customer
Loyalty. The two indirect effects were  significant, with the indirect coefficient through Perceived
Convenience (beta = 0.291) larger than the indirect coefficient through Customer Experience (beta =
0.202). These findings indicate that channel integration is related to loyalty not only direct ly but also
through experiential and functional customer evaluations.
4.1 Theoretical and Managerial Implications
The model contributes to omnichannel research by placing Customer Experience and Perceived
Convenience as parallel organism-level evaluations within the Stimulus Organism Response framework
and by using Relationship Marketing Theory to interpret their connection with loyalty-related behavior.
The results suggest that customer -facing integration should be evaluated through the continuity and
ease it creates across touchpoints, not only through the existence of multiple channels. For managers,
this means that  synchronization of product information, promotions, service, payment, stock
information, delivery, and returns should be assessed from the customer's journey perspective. The
larger estimated coefficient associated with Perceived Convenience suggests that  reducing customer
effort deserves particular attention, although Customer Experience remains an independent part of the
loyalty mechanism.
4.2 Limitations and Directions for Future Research
Several limitations define the scope of these conclusions. The study used cross-sectional self-report
survey data, so temporal order and causal effects cannot be established. The analysis did not compare
the model across focal brands, industries, or Indone sian regions, which limits conclusions about
contextual heterogeneity. In addition, the larger coefficient for Perceived Convenience than for
Customer Experience should be interpreted descriptively because a formal difference test between the
two coefficients was not reported. Future studies can use longitudinal designs, specify a focal retailer
for each respondent, compare sectors or customer segments, and employ additional predictive and
model-diagnostic procedures when the required data are available. Th ese extensions would test
whether the pattern observed here remains stable across different omnichannel environments.
