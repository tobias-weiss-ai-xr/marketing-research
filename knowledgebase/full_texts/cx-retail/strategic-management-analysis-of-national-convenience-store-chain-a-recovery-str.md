Abstract
National convenience store chain A faces intense retail competition, market saturation,
and digital disruption that require a structured strategic response. This study aims to formulate
a strategic priority for improving the operational efficiency, outlet productivity, and
competitiveness of national convenience store chain A by applying the strategic management
framework of David and David. Using a descriptive strategic analysis approach, this study
employs the IFE, EFE, CPM, SWOT, and QSPM matrices.The input stage shows that national
convenience store chain A has a strong internal position (IFE = 2.97) and an above -average
response to external opportunities and threats (EFE = 2.99). The Competitive Profile Matrix
indicates that national convenience store chain A remains highly competitive (3.82), although
it is slightly below national convenience store chain B (3.92) and above national convenience
store chain C (2.92) and international convenience store chain D (2.28).The SWOT matrix
produces three fe asible alternatives, while the QSPM identifies AS1 (accelerating
omnichannel, loyalty, and digital store services) as the most attractive option with a TAS of
6.30, followed by AS3 (6.19) and AS2 (6.08). Therefore, national convenience store chain A
should prioritize digital service acceleration and use its extensive physical network to
strengthen customer experience, quick transactions, and sustainable retail productivity.

INTRODUCTION
National convenience store chain A is the pioneer and largest franchise retail network
in Indonesia, successfully bridging the fulfillment of daily basic necessities for both urban and
rural communities. Nevertheless, within the national economic landscape  over the past 2 –3
years, the aggressive growth of the conventional retail sector has begun to hit a saturation point,
leading to indications of a comprehensive decline in corporate productivity (Badan Pusat
Statistik, 2025). Internal macro data reflects a drop in Return on Assets (ROA) from an average
of 4.8% to 3.2%, accompanied by an average 8.5% decrease in sales productivity per square
meter of physical outlets (Saleh et al., 2024). This downward trend is primarily triggered by
ballooning operational burdens and supply chain logistics overhead costs, compounded by the
widespread shift in consumer preferences toward online shopping platforms (quick-commerce)
in the post -pandemic era (Saleh et al., 2024). If this fluctuating decline in productivity
efficiency is left unchecked without a structured strategic decision, the company's competitive
advantage risks being eroded by its primary horizontal competitors (Setiawan & Puspa, 2024).

Consequently, the Indonesian retail industry has faced increasingly complex
competition due to the rapid expansion of minimarket chains, rising operating costs, and digital
disruption, particularly in adapting to cashless and online transactions (Prasetyo & Wijaya,
2023). Under these dynamics, national convenience store chain A is required to maintain its
operational efficiency while strengthening its ability to serve customers through both physical
and digital channels. The main strategic challenge faced i s not only how to expand its store
network, but also how to improve the productivity of existing outlets. While a large network
can create economies of scale, it may also increase logistics costs, operational control
complexity, and the risk of outlet cannibalization in saturated urban areas (Hidayat & Pratama,
2025). At the same time, intense competition from national convenience store chain B, national
convenience store chain C, international convenience store chain D, quick -commerce
platforms, and digita l grocery players has significantly increased customer expectations for
price competitiveness, speed, convenience, and service integration (Setiawan & Puspa, 2024).
Based on this background, this study aims to evaluate the strategic position of national
convenience store chain A and formulate the most appropriate strategic priorities. This analysis
employs the Internal Factor Evaluation (which hereinafter will be abbr eviated as IFE) matrix,
External Factor Evaluation (hereinafter abbreviated as EFE) matrix, Competitive Profile
Matrix (CPM), SWOT matrix, and Quantitative Strategic Planning Matrix (QSPM). This
systematic analysis is expected to provide a comprehensive overview of the company's internal
strengths and weaknesses, external opportunities and threats, competitive position, and
ultimately recommend the most objective priority strategy to restore and sustainably enhance
its operational productivity.
LITERATURE REVIEW
Definition of Strategic Management
Strategic management is defined as the art and science of formulating, implementing,
and evaluating cross-functional decisions that enable an organization to achieve its long -term
objectives (David & David, 2016). In business practice, strategic management  integrates
marketing, finance, operations, human resources, and information systems to create and sustain
competitive advantage.

Competitive Advantage
Competitive advantage refers to the ability of a company to create superior value
compared with competitors in the same industry. In the retail context, competitive advantage
may be reflected in store network coverage, supply chain efficiency, customer loy alty, price
competitiveness, digital integration, and service convenience (Fred & Forest, 2017; Pratama &
Puspa, 2023).

Internal and External Corporate Factors
Strategic decisions must be based on a clear mapping of internal and external factors.
Internal factors consist of strengths and weaknesses that are relatively controllable by the firm,
while external factors consist of opportunities and threats that arise  from market, economic,
technological, regulatory, and competitive conditions (David & David, 2016).  The first is the
internal environment, which encompasses organizational functions within the full operational
control of the firm, classified into strengths and weaknesses. The second is the external
environment, which involves macro -level variables beyond  the company's direct control,
classified as market opportunities and threats arising from economic, technological, regulatory,
and competitive conditions.

Relevant Strategic Alternatives
According to David (2020), strategic alternatives may include intensive strategies,
integration strategies, diversification strategies, and defensive strategies. For moder n retail
firms such as national convenience store chain A, intensive and integration-oriented strategies
are particularly relevant because they relate directly to store productivity, market penetration,
digital service development, franchise growth, and supply chain control. Intensive strategies—
comprising market penetration, market development, and product development —serve to
strengthen the core business line's bargaining position. Concurrently, integration strategies,
which include forward, backward, and horizontal integrat ion, allow the firm to gain greater
control over its modern retail supply chain value network

RESEARCH METHOD

This study applies a descriptive strategic analysis approach based on the comprehensive
strategic management framework of David and David. The analysis uses secondary data and
strategic estimation to identify key internal and external factors affecting national convenience
store chain A. The analytical process follows three main stages: the input stage, the matching
stage, and the decision stage.
In the input stage, the Internal Factor Evaluation (IFE) Matrix is used to evaluate the
strengths and weaknesses of national convenience store chain A, while the External Factor
Evaluation (EFE) Matrix is used to assess the company's response to opportunities and threats.
The Competitive Profile Matrix (CPM) compares national convenience store chain A with
national convenience store chain B, national convenience store chain C, and international
convenience store chain D using key success factors in the modern retail industry.
In the matching stage, the SWOT Matrix combines internal and external factors to
generate feasible strategic alternatives. In the decision stage, the Quantitative Strategic
Planning Matrix (QSPM) is used to determine the most attractive strategic alternative based on
the weighted factors from the IFE and EFE matrices.
The scores in the matrices represent academic strategic estimates. A total weighted
score above 2.50 indicates an above -average strategic position, while the QSPM's Total
Attractiveness Score (TAS) indicates the relative attractiveness of each alternative strategy.

CPM, SWOT, and QSPM. These matrices are used to evaluate the strategic position of national
convenience store chain A and to determine the most suitable strategy for impro ving

CONCLUSION, LIMITATIONS, AND SUGGESTIONS

Conclusion
Based on the integrated IFE, EFE, CPM, SWOT, and QSPM analyses, national
convenience store chain A has a strong strategic position but faces increasing pressure from
intense minimarket competition, digital disruption, operational costs, and store density. The
IFE score of 2.97 and EFE score of 2.99 show that national convenience store chain A has
above-average internal and external readiness, while the CPM score of 3.82 indicates that
national convenience store chain A remains highly competitive although sl ightly below
national convenience store chain B. The  QSPM result identifies Alternative Strategies  1,
accelerating omnichannel, loyalty, and digital store services, as the most attractive priority with
a TAS of 6.30. Therefore, national convenience store chain A should strengthen its digital
ecosystem and use its extensive physical outlet network as a pl atform for faster transactions,
stronger loyalty, and improved customer experience rather than relying only on conventional
store expansion.

Limitations
This study is limited to a descriptive strategic analysis using secondary data and
academic estimation. The weighting, rating, and attractiveness score values in the matrices may
contain researcher judgment and do not represent official internal company ca lculations. In
addition, this study focuses on selected strategic factors that are considered most relevant to
the current competitive position of national convenience store chain A.

Suggestions for Future Research
Future research is suggested to use primary data from managers, customers, franchise
partners, and employees to improve the objectivity of the matrix scores. Further studies may
also compare national convenience store chain A with more retail competitors, examine the
financial impact of omnichannel transformation, and evaluate the implementation of digital
store services in different regional markets.
