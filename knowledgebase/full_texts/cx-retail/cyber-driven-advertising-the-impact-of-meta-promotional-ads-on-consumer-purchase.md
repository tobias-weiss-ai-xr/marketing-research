Abstract
The UK’s retail and fashion industries are experiencing a 45% increase in digital ad spending , with META’s AI -driven
promotional ads playing a pivotal role in shaping consumer behavior. Recent studies indicate that 72% of UK consumers engage
with at least one META advertisement weekly, and 63% report that social media ads influence their purchase decisions. With
the rise of machine learning (ML)-based targeting and real-time behavioural analytics, brands can now predict consumer intent
with 87% accuracy , revolutionizing digital advertising. This study investigates the impact of META promotional ads  on
consumer purchase intent by analysing user engagement patterns, ad personalization effectiveness, and cybersecurity concerns
related to data tracking. Using a mixed-methods approach, quantitative survey data (n = 500) and qualitative insights from
structured interviews (n = 30) were analyzed. Statistical tools such as SPSS, multiple regression, and sentiment analysis  were
employed to assess the correlation between ad exposure frequency, user engagement, and purchasing behavior.
Findings reveal that AI-enhanced META ads increase purchase intent by 41%, with ad personalization being a key factor (β =
0.78, p < 0.01). However, 54% of respondents express concerns over data privacy, and 47% distrust AI-driven ad targeting due
to algorithmic opacity and excess ive behavioural tracking. The study highlights the growing tension between marketing
efficiency and consumer cybersecurity expectations , suggesting that brands must balance hyper-personalization with ethical
data practices.
This research contributes to the understanding of cyber-driven consumer engagement, providing insights for brands to refine
AI-powered marketing strategies while navigating privacy regulations such as GDPR  and evolving consumer data protection
expectations.

1- INTRODUCTION
1.1Research Background
With consumers' attention being such a scarce resource in the modern advertising marketplace, companies have been in search
of innovative ways to attract customers' inte rest and influence their purchasing decisions. Due to advancements in digital
technology and the gradual dissolution of distinctions between the actual and virtual worlds, advertising has undergone
significant modifications in recent years. Promoting adver tisements utilising META (Mixed, Extended, and Augmented
Reality) technology is a modern and significant advancement in this field (Santini et al., 2016). From the earliest newspaper
ads to the birth of radio, television, and the Internet, advertising has experienced a long and interesting history. There has been
an on-going effort by marketers to find novel ways to get their ideas across to target audiences. However, advertising's primary
purpose of impacting buyer decisions has not altered. According to Wirtz, Sparks and Zimbres (2018), marketing in the Internet
age is a complex system that makes use of a wide range of tools and resources across a wide range of digital channels.
Innovations in technology have played a significant role in advertising's deve lopment. Newspapers and magazines allowed
companies to reach more people in the initial years of print advertising. With the introduction of radio, companies have been
able to reach customers through the medium of speech and music in commercials for the fi rst time. Advertising used to be
restricted to just written content, however, television changed all of that by including visuals and voice (Bayraktar et al., 2017).
The emergence of the digital era brought about a major shift in advertising methods in the  retail and fashion industry of the
UK. As a result of the development of personal computers and mobile devices, advertisers currently have more channels of
communication with their target audiences compared to previously. Barton, Zlatevska and Oppewal(2022) determined that the
targeting and interactivity in online advertising have allowed the fashion and retail industry of the UK to successfully reach out
to particular groups of people and establish more personal connections with their customers. It has b een identified that
companies can take advantage of user-generated content and build brand communities due to the accessibility of social media
(Riley, 2020).
There has been an immense shift in the history of marketing with the introduction of META adverti sing. As per Battisti,
Agarwal and Brem(2022), META marketing ad campaigns create a more interesting and dynamic platform for firms to engage
with their target audiences by integrating physical and internet settings. META advertising gives an extensive variety of options
for reaching and communicating with clients. It has been determined that Augmented Reality (AR) is a technology that applies
computer- generated imagery (CGI) to the actual world. In contrast to virtual reality (VR), this involves consumers  in fully
digital realms. Moreover, extended reality (XR) integrates physical and virtual aspects to create hybrid experiences (Alcañiz ,
Bigné and Guixeres, 2019). Innovations in both hardware and software have accelerated the progress of these fields.
Smartphones with high-powered CPUs and high-resolution screens have lowered the barrier to entry for augmented and virtual
reality. Technology advancements in areas like artificial intelligence, detecting movement, and spatial information processin g
have made it easier to blend virtual and augmented realities (Rauschnabel et al., 2022).
META promotional ads are based on the idea of dynamically combining online and offline aspects. In particular, augmented
reality (AR) uses computer-generated images and data to augment a user's experience and interaction with their physical
surroundings. Through the displays of mobile devices or AR glasses, users can experience a hybrid reality in which digital
information is displayed in the real world (Alcañiz, Bigné and Guixeres, 2019). The boundaries have been further expanded
by extended reality, which combines digital and physical components. According to Rauschnabel et al (2022), users can
engage with digital items and scenarios while maintaining situational awareness. The  usage of XR technology is expanding
beyond gaming and into other areas, such as remote teamwork and education. Bayraktar et al. (2017) identified that the
attention of the target audience is a major goal of every advertising campaign. The media ecosystem is becoming increasingly
distorted, making it harder than ever to get people's attention. When it comes to making a lasting connection, META
promotional ads stand far and wide above others in the field.
META advertising generates experiences that are innovative and attractive, making them intrinsically attention-grabbing. These
commercials provide a degree of involvement that is difficult to achieve in standard advertising forms by merging the physical
and digital worlds. Users can collaborate with companies and goods in exciting new ways, emerging with a positive impression
(Saima and Khan, 2020). The engagement aspect of META promotional advertisements extends beyond typical advertising
tactics. By extending beyond frequently passive viewing, these ads offer customers something extremely unique. As per Wang
et al. (2017), users are not passive observers, however, they engage with the ad in the same way that they interact with the
information it contains. Within the ad, they can learn more about the goods, give it a virtual try, and even make a purchase.
META advertising has the potential to revolutionise the way consumers experience products online, from initial discovery to
final purchase. As per Singhal, Jena and Tripathy (2019), META advertising ads have  given the retail and fashion industries
creative possibilities for personalised product display and client interaction. Nevertheless, by allowing customers to observ e
how the apparel and other items will appear on them, the purchasing experience becomes m ore engaging and authentic.
Moreover, various opportunities also exist for both marketers and clothing firms in the innovative new META advertising
technique. Customers can virtually try on an item to get a feel for how it fits and moves before making a pu rchase. These

 2.1- Introduction
The primary purpose of this literature review is to provide a comprehensive overview of earlier research projects that examined
the impact of META advertisements on consumer behaviour. By developing a narrative that goes beyond individual findings,
integrating various study findings intends to offer an in -depth overview of the impacts of META advertising. This study
identifies the gaps and points out that the present understanding of the subject is inadequate. By completing these gaps,
professionals and academics in the META promotion forum can explore previously unexplored areas. By addressing these gaps
and offering insights into the extremely competitive UK fashion and retail sectors as well as the successful use of META
promotional advertisements, this study helps companies and academic research equivalent.
Innovative methods to increase customer engagement and influence buying behaviour have emerged as a result of the fast
development of advertising techniques in modern marketplaces (Kant and Yadete, 202 3). META advertisements are one such
innovative and vital marketing technique. Within the ever-changing context of the UK fashion and retail business, this literature
study also explores the complex environment of META promotional commercials to provide a thorough theoretical framework
that sheds light on their influence on customer purchase intent. The combination of digital and physical components in META
advertisements signifies a new era in advertising, which guarantees customers better interactive and immersive experiences
(Zhang, Kim and Chakravarty, 2023). To fully grasp this change and its effects, an individual must delve extensively into the
theoretical frameworks that support persuasive psychology, experiential marketing, and consumer behaviour.
2.2- Augmented Reality and Consumer Behaviour
Augmented Reality (AR) transforms advertising, impacting consumer behaviour in META promotions. Integrating digital
elements into reality, AR redefines consumer engagement. Unlike traditional ads, AR lets users virtually interact with
products, fostering participation. Addressing uncertainty, AR provides tangible experiences like virtual try -ons, reducing
uncertainties in online purchases. This builds consumer trust,

influencing purchase decisions. Additio nally, AR creates memorable and shareable experiences, enhancing word -of-mouth
marketing. The social aspect amplifies impact through shared experiences. In the META context, AR's role is intricate, blending
real and virtual elements seamlessly. Yet, empirical evidence is crucial to validate positive impacts. The intersection of AR and
consumer behaviour in META promotions requires in -depth exploration. As the retail and fashion industry adopts META
strategies, understanding AR's influence becomes crucial for effective advertising.
2.3- Interactivity and Engagement
A key aspect of augmented reality's influence on consumer behaviour is its capacity to deliver captivating and participatory
experiences. In comparison to more conventional types of advertisement, META promotional commercials employ augmented
reality to enable people to engage with digital components in real -time (Cowan and Ketron, 2019). Nevertheless, users can
participate rather than simply observe these commercials as a result of how engagin g they have been, which fosters a sense of
co- creation. According to the theoretical perspective of interaction and engagement, advertising experiences that include a
high level of customer participation are more successful and lasting (Gavilanes, Flatten  and Brettel, 2018). The purpose of
META commercials is to promote products and services by attracting viewers' attention as well as promoting involvement
through the use of augmented reality (Yang and Shen, 2018). Therefore, understanding how the degree o f engagement within
augmented reality experiences impacts consumer behaviour becomes essential for interpreting the complexities of META
advertising.

 2.5- The Experience Economy Paradigm
The concept of the experience economy has evolved with the emergence of Extended Reality (XR), which encompasses AR

that is in line with experience marketing ideas, businesses in the UK's retail and fashion industries prefer to make an impression

META advertising affects consumer behaviour in the UK retail and fashion industries (De Gauquier et al., 2019).

 2.7- The Power of Social Influence
According to the psychological concept of social proof, consumers are more inclined to adopt a certain behaviour and belief
once they perceive other individuals engaging similarly through various platforms such as; Instagram, Facebook, Amazon and
YouTube (Abdullah et al., 2020). The use of social proof aspects in META advertising seeks to capitalise on the impact that a
community's behaviour has on individual decision -making. A social proof phenomenon known as the "bandwagon effect"
postulates that individuals often imitate the preferences as well as behaviours of others (Bindra et al., 2022). While used in
META advertising, social proof components intend to establish a virtual bandwagon effect, in which customers are more likely
to believ e in and take up the products or services being marketed whenever they perceive a community of other people
supporting them (Barton, Zlatevska and Oppewal, 2022). META advertisements frequently leverage components including
user reviews, ratings, and comments to draw attention to other individuals' satisfying experiences. According to Abdullah et al
(2020), customers' views can be greatly influenced by the appearance of these favourable endorsements, which helps to build
confidence in the marketed products.  Therefore, gaining insight into the subtleties of social proof in influencing customer
behaviour requires an understanding of how the bandwagon effect functions within the immersive and engaging setting of
META advertising.

alter the attitudes and purchasing intentions of consumers. As per Hajli et al. (2017), informational social influence represents
an essential tool in the retailing and fashion industries, where consumers frequently have to make decisions from an extensiv e
range of choices. META advertising with social proof components, including user-generated material and instantaneous

 2.9- Presence Theory
The idea of "presence" is a key to comprehending the effects of AR. According to presence theory, augmented reality
enhances the user's perception of being present in the advertising location by fluidly blending digital and physical elements
(Kim, 2021). With the use of augmented reality, META promotional advertising takes  users on an immersive experience
where digital components engage with their physical surroundings. Consumer sentiments and purchase intent are believed to
be more significantly influenced by this enhanced experience of presence. According to Cummings and  Bailenson (2016), by
interacting with META advertisements, users can improve their sense of "being in the moment" by reducing the distinction
between the real and virtual worlds. Presence theory asserts that customers' impressions of promoted goods and se rvices are
significantly influenced by how much they see themselves as being physically present in the upgraded environment (Kim,
2021).

2.10- Elaboration Likelihood Model (ELM)
The Elaboration Likelihood Model (ELM) offers an advanced theoretical fr amework for identifying how people receive
information and engage in persuasive behaviours. Within the context of META advertising, the ELM provides insightful
information on how consumers interact with and react to messages that are persuasively communica ted using extended reality
media, specifically in the UK retail and fashion sectors. According to Jayawardena et al. (2023), the central route and the
peripheral route are the two unique pathways that the ELM proposes which can be used for persuasion (see Figure 1). The
central route is a methodical and comprehensive information processing process in which consumers carefully consider the
arguments and substances that are presented. However, the peripheral approach affects individuals who lack deep cognitiv e
processing by using more surface -level cues, such as visual attractiveness and popular endorsements (Khan et al., 2023). As
per Luo and Ye (2019), customers who actively interact with the immersive content such as; in-depth product demos or virtual
try-on experiences and provide a careful review of the information in the advertisement can activate the central pathway in
META advertising. On the peripheral route, consumers can be persuaded to engage with META advertisements without
requiring an extensive amount of cognitive explanation through the visual appeal of augmented reality features and the inclusion
of prominent characters (Jayawardena et al., 2023).
