1.0 INTRODUCTION
1.1 Background of the Study
Marketing innovation has become a central concern for commercial banks seeking to differentiate
their service offerings and strengthen relationships with increasingly digitally-oriented customers.
Marketing innovation refers to the implementation of new marketing methods involving
significant changes in product or service design, packaging, placement, promotion, or pricing, with
the specific aim of better addressing customer needs, opening new markets, or repositioning a
firm's offering in order to increase sales (Asisi & Egessa, 2023). In the banking sector, marketing
innovation is comm only expressed through digital marketing campaigns, social media
engagement, personalised promotional messaging, and interactive online communication
platforms, all of which shape how customers perceive, access, and evaluate banking services.
Customer experience, in turn, reflects the cumulative impression a customer forms from every
interaction with a bank, encompassing convenience, service speed, trust, accessibility, and overall
satisfaction with the quality of service delivered.
1.1.1 Global Perspective
Globally, commercial banks have increasingly embedded marketing innovation into their customer
experience strategies as digital transformation reshapes retail banking. In Romania, a member of
the European Union, banking institutions have adopted automated  decision-making tools
alongside personalised digital marketing communication to influence customer perceptions, with
research showing that automated and data -driven marketing interactions shape both the positive
and negative dimensions of the contemporary  banking experience (Gavrila, 2024). Banks
operating within the European Union's stringent data protection environment have had to balance
personalised marketing innovation with transparency, since customers increasingly expect
communication that is both relevant and trustworthy. In Saudi Arabia, an Asian economy investing
heavily in digital transformation under its Vision 2030 agenda, service -sector firms, including
banks, have integrated digital transformation initiatives with customer experience manageme nt
and information technology innovation, and empirical evidence from Saudi service firms
demonstrates that customer experience exerts the strongest influence on overall firm performance
among the digital transformation capabilities examined (Masoud & Basa hel, 2023). These cases
illustrate that marketing innovation, whether expressed through automated personalisation in
Europe or integrated digital transformation strategies in the Gulf, has become a decisive lever for
shaping customer experience in global banking markets.

Within Africa, marketing innovation in banking has taken different forms depending on market
maturity and competitive pressure. In Nigeria, a leading economy in West Africa, microfinance
banks in Ogun State have a dopted service innovation strategies encompassing new service
concepts, processes, and technologies as a marketing and service differentiation tool, with findings
confirming that such innovation dimensions have a positive and substantial influence on customer
satisfaction (Shodiya, Akintaro, & Olopade, 2024). Nigerian banks operating in a highly
competitive and densely populated retail market have consequently prioritised innovations that
reduce transaction friction and increase responsiveness as a means of  retaining price-sensitive
customers. In Southern Africa, South African commercial banks, often referred to as the Big Four,
have invested substantially in social media marketing as a tool for building customer brand
involvement, with research among Generation X and Generation Y banking customers in Gauteng

entertainment positively influence customer brand involvement, which in turn shapes brand loyalty
(Mandhlazi, Duffett, Mokoena, & Mafini, 2026). These regional cases demonstrate that African
commercial banks are increasingly using marketing innovation, whether through service redesign
in West Africa or social media engagement in Southern Africa, to strengthen cust omer
relationships in markets characterised by growing digital adoption but uneven infrastructure.
1.1.3 Local Perspective
In Kenya, commercial banks operate within one of the most digitally advanced banking markets
in Sub-Saharan Africa, driven by high mo bile money penetration and an increasingly tech -savvy
customer base. Kenyan banks have responded by intensifying marketing innovation efforts,
including digital campaigns, mobile applications, and social media engagement, in order to remain
competitive ami d rising cost -to-income pressures and market entry by fintech firms (Asisi &
Egessa, 2023). Evidence from the Kenyan banking sector further shows that digital innovations,
including online platforms supporting market positioning and customer engagement, ar e
significantly associated with competitive advantage, with customer satisfaction identified as a
particularly strong contributor to that advantage (Odhiambo & Makau, 2024). Industry -wide
survey evidence corroborates these findings at the national level, i ndicating that Kenyan banking
customers increasingly evaluate their banks on the basis of digital convenience, responsiveness,
and personalised engagement rather than physical branch presence alone (KPMG, 2024). Within
Nairobi City County specifically, home to the headquarters of nearly all major commercial banks
and the country's most digitally engaged customer segment, marketing innovation has become
central to how banks such as Equity Bank, KCB Bank, and Co -operative Bank compete for and
retain customers, making the county a strategically relevant context for examining the relationship
between marketing innovation and customer experience.

Despite growing investment in marketing innovation by commercial banks in Nairobi City County,

Similarly, studies on marketing innovation among Kenyan financial and insurance firms have

digital infrastructure conditions and cannot be directly generalised to Nairobi City County
(Shodiya et al.,  2024; Mandhlazi et al., 2026). Consequently, there remains limited context -
specific empirical evidence on how marketing innovation strategies, specifically digital marketing,
social media engagement, personalised marketing, promotional campaigns, and onli ne
communication, influence customer experience among commercial bank customers in Nairobi
City County. This gap limits the ability of bank managers to make evidence -based decisions on
where to direct marketing innovation investment in order to improve cus tomer experience. This
study therefore sought to address this gap by examining the effect of marketing innovation
strategies on customer experience of commercial banks in Nairobi City County, Kenya.

2.1 Theoretical Review
2.1.1 Innovation Diffusion Theory
The Innovation Diffusion Theory, developed by Rogers (1962), explains how innovative ideas,
technologies, or practices spread within an organisation or industry over time. The theory identifies
five attributes that determine the adoption speed and effectiveness of innovations: relative
advantage, compatibility, complexity, trialability, and observability. Within the context of
commercial banks in Nairobi City County, the implementation of marketing innovations such as
digital campaigns, social media engagement, and personalised promotional communication is
expected to enhance customer experience by improving service accessibi lity, efficiency, and
personalisation. Relative advantage assesses the visible improvements such innovations offer over
traditional marketing approaches, while compatibility considers how well these innovations align
with existing customer expectations and  banking practices. The observability and trialability of
new marketing approaches influence how readily customers notice and engage with them, which
in turn shapes the pace and success of adoption.
The theory further holds that effective diffusion require s clear communication of the value of an
innovation to the intended audience. Banks that invest in customer education and clearly
demonstrate the tangible benefits of new marketing channels, such as faster transaction
confirmations or more relevant promoti onal offers, are more likely to achieve high rates of
customer acceptance and, consequently, stronger customer experience outcomes. Marketing
innovations that are perceived as easy to understand and low-risk to try tend to spread more rapidly
among customers, reinforcing engagement and satisfaction over time. This study employed the
Innovation Diffusion Theory as its guiding framework to examine how marketing innovation is
adopted by commercial banks and how this adoption translates into customer experience outcomes
in Nairobi City County.
2.2 Empirical Review
2.2.1 Marketing Innovation and Customer Experience
Asisi and Egessa (2023) examined market innovation and competitiveness among commercial
banks in Kenya, focusing on how innovative marketing and product strategies helped banks
withstand rising operational costs and competitive pressure from mergers, acquisitions, and fintech
entrants. The study, which drew on secondary industry data and reports from the Central Bank of
Kenya and the Kenya Bankers Association, found that banks demonstrating stronger market
innovation capability were better positioned to sustain competitiveness, although the study did not
isolate the specific effect of marketing innovation on individual customer experience outcomes, a
gap the present study addresses.

Odhiambo and Makau (2024) investigated the role of digital banking technological innovations in

found a significant relationship between market positioning innovation and competitive advantage,

Sifuna and Kamau (2024) studied marketing innovation strategy and performance at Britam

approaches, including digital promotion and customer engagement strategies, were positively
associated with organisational performance. While the study was conducted within the financial
services sector in Nairobi, its focus on an insurance firm rather than c ommercial banks, and on
organisational performance rather than customer experience, limits its direct applicability to the
present study's context. Similarly, Okundi and Muchemi (2022) examined marketing innovation
strategy and entrepreneurial performance among small and medium enterprises in Nakuru East
Town Sub -County, establishing that marketing innovation positively influenced business
performance; however, the study's focus on small enterprises outside the banking sector, and
outside Nairobi City County, further underscores the contextual gap this study addresses.
Beyond Kenya, Shodiya, Akintaro, and Olopade (2024) examined service innovation and customer
satisfaction among microfinance banks in Ogun State, Nigeria, using a cross -sectional survey

financial services are designed and communicated directly shapes custome r-level outcomes. In
Southern Africa, Mandhlazi, Duffett, Mokoena, and Mafini (2026) investigated the effect of social
media marketing dimensions on customer brand involvement and brand equity among South
African banks, finding that dimensions such as electronic word-of-mouth and entertainment value
positively influenced customer brand involvement, which subsequently enhanced brand loyalty.
Although both studies confirm a positive relationship between marketing -related innovation and
customer-level outcomes, neither was conducted within the Kenyan banking context, and neither
examined the broader set of marketing innovation indicators, including personalised marketing
and promotional campaigns, considered in the present study.

experience within the Romanian banking industry, finding that while automated and personalised

2.3 Conceptual Framework
The conceptual framework for this study depicts the relationship between marketing innovation
strategies, the independent variable, and customer experience, the dependent variable, among
commercial banks in Nairobi City County. Marketing innovation is operationalised through digital
marketing, social media engagement, personalised marketing, promotional campaigns, and online
communication platforms. Customer experience is operationalised through convenience,
satisfaction, service speed,  trust, accessibility, and overall service quality. The framework
proposes that improvements in marketing innovation practices directly enhance customer

3.0 RESEARCH METHODOLOGY
The study adopted a descriptive research design to assess existing marketing innovation strategies
and their effect on customer experience without manipulating the study environment. The target
population comprised customers of Equity Bank Kenya Limited, KCB Bank Kenya Limited, and
Co-operative Bank of Kenya Limited, with a total population of 130 respondents. Purposive
sampling was used to select cu stomers who actively utilised banking services and had adequate
experience with marketing innovations. Data were collected primarily using structured
questionnaires covering demographic characteristics, marketing innovation strategies, and
customer experience. Secondary data were also used to complement the primary information. A
pilot study was conducted at ABSA Bank to identify and correct weaknesses in the research
instrument. Content validity was established through expert review, while reliability was assessed
using Cronbach’s Alpha, with a threshold of 0.70 considered acceptable. Data collection followed
the necessary institutional and ethical requirements, including Kenyatta University and NACOSTI
clearance. Data were analysed using SPSS through descr iptive statistics, correlation, and
regression analysis to determine the relationship between marketing innovation strategies and
customer experience. Findings were presented using tables, charts, and graphs. Ethical principles,
including voluntary partici pation, confidentiality, anonymity, and the right to withdraw, were
maintained throughout the study.

These findings are consistent with the Innovation Diffusion Theory, which holds that innovations
perceived as advantageous, compatible, and observable are more readily adopted by their intended
audience, leading to improved outcomes for those who engage with them (Rogers, 1962). The

coefficient of determination further suggests that other factors not captured in this study, such as
product innovation, process innovation, and organizational cul ture, may also contribute

5.0 SUMMARY OF THE STUDY
The study examined the effect of marketing innovation strategies on customer experience of
commercial banks in Nairobi City County, Keny a. The descriptive findings revealed that
respondents generally agreed that banks had adopted marketing innovation practices, particularly
digital marketing and promotional campaigns, with an overall mean of 4.013. Customer experience
was similarly rated p ositively, with an overall mean of 4.056, with digital banking convenience
and overall positive experience receiving the highest ratings, while customer support services and
alignment with customer expectations received comparatively lower ratings. Correla tion analysis
confirmed a moderately strong positive relationship between marketing innovation and customer
experience (r = .501, p < .01), and regression analysis confirmed that marketing innovation
significantly predicts customer experience, explaining approximately 25.1 percent of its variation.
These findings collectively indicate that marketing innovation is an important, though not sole,
determinant of customer experience among commercial banks operating in Nairobi City County.

The stud y concludes that marketing innovation strategies have a positive and statistically

that invest in digital marketing, social media engagement, promotional campaig ns, and effective

which commercial banks can e nhance customer experience in an increasingly competitive and
digitally driven banking environment.
7.0 RECOMMENDATIONS
Based on the findings, the study recommends that commercial banks in Nairobi City County
strengthen personalized marketing initiatives b y leveraging customer data responsibly to tailor
promotional messaging and product recommendations to individual customer needs, since
personalized marketing recorded a comparatively lower mean score relative to other marketing
innovation indicators. Banks  should also invest in improving customer support responsiveness,
including expanding digital helpdesk capacity and reducing resolution times for customer queries,
since customer support services recorded the lowest mean score among the customer experience
indicators assessed. Bank management should continue to strengthen digital marketing and
promotional campaigns, which were found to be the most positively rated marketing innovation
practices, while ensuring that online communication platforms are consistently clear, accurate, and
responsive. Finally, given that marketing innovation explained just over a quarter of the variation
in customer experience, banks should consider complementing marketing innovation with parallel
investments in service delivery processes and staff training to more comprehensively enhance the
overall customer experience.
8.0 AREAS FOR FURTHER RESEARCH
Future research should consider examining additional forms of innovation, including product,
process, and organisational innovation, alongside marketing innovation, to establish their
combined effect on customer experience among commercial banks in Nairobi City County. Given
that the regression model explained approximately a quarter of the variance in customer
experience, further resea rch should investigate other potential determinants, such as staff
competence, regulatory factors, and macroeconomic conditions. Comparative studies across
