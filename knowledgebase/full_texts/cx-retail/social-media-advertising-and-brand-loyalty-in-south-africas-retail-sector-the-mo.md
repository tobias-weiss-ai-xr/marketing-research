Research Problem: Brand loyalty in South Africa’ s retail sector is
increasingly threatened by the fragmented attention and fluid
preferences of digital consumers. Although social media advertising
has become a dominant marketing tool, limited research has
examined how advertising spend influences brand loyalty within this
sector, particularly when moderated by customer engagement.
Understanding these dynamics is crucial for retailers seeking to
retain customers in a competitive digital landscape.
Methods/Theory: Guided by Engagement Theory, this study adopts
a quantitative research design to analyse the relationship between
social media advertising spend, customer engagement, and brand
loyalty. Data were obtained from 120 retail firms actively advertising
on platforms such as Facebook, Instagram, and TikTok. Secondary
data on advertising expenditure, platform engagement metrics, and
customer loyalty indicators were analysed using hierarchical
regression models, supported by diagnostic validity tests.
Results: Findings show that social media advertising spend exerts a
significant positive effect on brand loyalty. Customer engagement
also demonstrates a strong positive influence on loyalty outcomes.
Moreover, the moderating analysis reveals that customer
engagement significantly strengthens the relationship between
advertising spend and brand loyalty, indicating that advertising is
more effective when audiences interact actively with brand content.
Conclusion: Social media advertising contributes to enhanced brand
loyalty in South Africa’ s retail sector; however , its effectiveness is
dependent on the level of customer engagement. Paid advertising
alone cannot secure loyalty without meaningful user interaction.
Key Contribution to Knowledge:  This study enriches digital
marketing scholarship by empirically demonstrating the moderating

1.0 Introduction
Brand loyalty remains a cornerstone of long-
term competitiveness in modern retail markets,
especially in an era where consumer attention is
fragmented across digital platforms. In today’s
hyperconnected environment, brand loyalty is
shaped not only by product quality or price
competitiveness but also by how effectively
brands engage consumers through digital
means, particularly social media advertising.
With customers becoming more digitally
native, the relationship between how brands
advertise on social media and how loyal
customers remain has garnered significant
scholarly attention (Moodley and Ntobaki,
2024).
Globally, the retail sector has embraced
social media platforms such as Instagram,
Facebook, and TikTok not just for brand
awareness but for cultivating deeper consumer
relationships. In mature economies, paid social
media campaigns have evolved from basic
promotions to complex storytelling formats
aimed at building emotional connections. As
per Nair, Manohar, and Limbu (2025), the
strategic investment in paid digital ads now
directly contributes to both brand equity and
customer retention. However, emerging
markets like South Africa present unique digital
ecosystems where the effectiveness of social
media advertising on loyalty remains under-
explored and likely moderated by behavioral
and contextual dynamics.
In South Africa, where smartphone
penetration and internet access have expanded
rapidly, social media has become a vital
component of consumer culture. Retailers
increasingly rely on digital ad spend to reach
fragmented yet highly mobile audiences,
especially among urban youth demographics
(Mansour and V adell, 2025). However, ad
fatigue, algorithmic constraints, and consumer
skepticism pose challenges to sustaining
engagement and loyalty. This raises a critical
question: does increased social media
advertising necessarily lead to loyal customers,
or are other factors; such as consumer
interaction, more pivotal?
Customer engagement plays a central role in
the evolving social media–brand dynamic.
Highly engaged consumers are more likely to
comment, share, and emotionally connect with
content, thus serving as brand advocates and
repeat buyers (Moodley and Ntobaki, 2024).
Engagement is not merely a by-product of
advertising but an active mediator or moderator
in the advertising–loyalty link. In South
Africa’s competitive retail landscape, brands
that foster two-way communication and
emotional resonance on social media often
outperform those that focus solely on
promotional messaging (Nair et al., 2025).
While international research has
emphasized the strategic importance of
engagement, localized insights from South
Africa remain scarce. Most studies generalize
findings from global platforms without

considering South African socio-cultural

real driver of loyalty may lie not just in the
advertising spend, but in how that spend

Despite the increased marketing budgets
allocated to social media in South Africa’s retail
sector, empirical clarity on the relationship
between paid social media advertising and
brand loyalty is limited. More importantly, the
role of customer engagement as a moderating
factor remains under-researched. Without
dissecting this dynamic, marketers risk over-
investing in ad spend that yields minimal
returns in brand loyalty, especially if
engagement remains low or misaligned with
customer expectations (Moodley and Ntobaki,
2024).
Therefore, this study seeks to empirically
examine the impact of social media advertising
on brand loyalty in South Africa’s retail sector,
while testing whether customer engagement
strengthens or weakens this relationship.
Understanding this nexus can help brands
calibrate their advertising strategies not just for
visibility but for sustainable loyalty outcomes.
Ultimately, this research aims to provide
practical insights for marketers and brand
managers seeking to optimize their social
media investments. It also fills a critical gap in
literature by exploring how customer
engagement, measured through likes, shares,
comments, and interaction, may amplify the
return on advertising efforts in emerging digital
economies like South Africa. Against this
backdrop, the study tests the following
hypothesis:
H₀₁: Social media advertising spend has no
significant effect on brand loyalty among retail
consumers in South Africa.
H₀₂: Customer engagement has no
significant effect on brand loyalty among retail
consumers in South Africa.
H₀₃: Customer engagement does not
significantly moderate the relationship between
social media advertising spend and brand
loyalty among retail consumers in South Africa.

2.0 Literature Review
Concept of Brand Loyalty
Brand loyalty has been a central theme in
consumer behavior research, often linked to
sustained profitability and long-term customer
relationships. Scholars have conceptualized
brand loyalty from behavioral, attitudinal, and
composite perspectives. According to Branca
and Ciacci (2024), brand loyalty is both a
psychological commitment and a repetitive
purchasing behavior that reflects a consumer's
preference for a brand. Abbasi and Ebrahimi
(2024) emphasized the emotional attachment
customers form with brands, highlighting
loyalty as an outcome of perceived value and
satisfaction. Kondića (2024) proposed that
brand loyalty includes brand preference, trust,
and a willingness to recommend, contributing
significantly to customer equity. Mahdieh et al.
(2024) further included elements such as
customer satisfaction and commitment in
conceptualizing brand loyalty in competitive
markets. Qurban (2025) argued that loyalty
encompasses attitudinal elements that translate
into behavioral outcomes like repeat purchases
and advocacy. Arif et al. (2025) linked loyalty
directly to engagement and digital brand
experiences, while Garrod (2025) incorporated
cultural and emotional influences in loyalty
formation. Kapanen (2025) examined loyalty
through brand equity and retention metrics.
Natalia et al. (2025) added that loyalty also
involves resisting competitor influence. Finally,
Zhong (2024) suggested that brand loyalty can
serve as a strategic lever for marketing
differentiation. For this study, brand loyalty is

defined as the extent to which customers
consistently repurchase from a brand,
recommend it to others, and resist switching,
operationalized through retention, repeat
purchase intention, and advocacy measures.
Concept of Social Media Advertising
Spend
Social media advertising has become a
strategic pillar in modern marketing, offering
measurable, interactive, and highly targeted
promotional capabilities. Scholars have defined
it as a form of digital marketing where brands
pay to display content to users on platforms like
Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, and Twitter.
Ciocodeică, Barghier, and Mihălcescu (2025)
define social media advertising as a strategic
investment that targets user engagement
through algorithmically driven paid content.
Manurung and Ningsi (2025) emphasize the use
of sponsored ads to drive brand visibility and
influence purchasing behavior. Hassan (2026)
views social media ads as paid digital tools
optimized by user behavior and real-time
analytics. Saputra and Rochman (2025)
describe social media ads as personalized visual
content aimed at triggering immediate audience
responses. Zhong (2024) and Rousta (2025)
argue that advertising spend reflects a brand’s
commitment to visibility and brand recall.
Nguyen Thanh (2025) noted that ad spend
effectiveness depends heavily on platform
dynamics and consumer attention. Baranovska
(2025) linked ad spend with conversion
optimization and ROI. Nassar and Abdijabar
(2025) addressed the regulatory and ethical
dimensions of ad targeting. Wang (2025)
extended the view to short-form content
advertising, especially in video-based
platforms. For this study, social media
advertising is conceptualized as the total
financial expenditure made by firms to promote
branded content across digital platforms,
measured in South African Rand on a monthly
or quarterly basis.

Concept of Customer Engagement
Customer engagement has gained
prominence as a moderating factor in
understanding marketing outcomes,
particularly in the realm of digital advertising.
Researchers conceptualize customer
engagement as a multidimensional construct
encompassing cognitive, emotional, and
behavioral investment in brand interactions
(Bhale, Bedi, Ray, & PV , 2025). It is often
described as the depth of a customer’s brand-
related interactions across various digital
platforms, including social media (Xiao &
Chen, 2025). According to Nguyen Thanh
(2025), customer engagement significantly
affects brand loyalty and purchasing behavior,
especially in high-involvement environments.
Danaei (2025) proposes that engagement
includes observable metrics such as likes,
shares, and comments, which serve as proxies
for customer-brand connection. Mursalin
(2025) positions customer engagement as a
strategic orientation that drives firm
performance through sustained attention and
advocacy. Rahman, Carlson, and Chowdhury
(2025) argue for engagement as a time-based
behavior, reflecting how frequently and deeply
customers interact with brand content. Camelis,
Marchat, and Maunier (2025) stress the
emotional resonance of customer engagement,
seeing it as a tool for co-creation and brand
trust. Arif, Saeed, and Asghar (2025) measure
engagement through customer behavioral
outcomes like loyalty intention. In this study,
customer engagement is conceptualized as a
moderating variable, operationalized through
social media interaction metrics (likes, shares,
comments, CTR), as it influences the strength
of the relationship between social media
advertising and brand loyalty.

2.1 Empirical Review
Social Media Advertising Spend and
Brand Loyalty
Recent empirical studies have increasingly
emphasized the influence of social media
advertising on brand loyalty, particularly within
the retail sector. A study by Rahman and
Chowdhury (2025) found that consistent social
media campaigns significantly improve brand
loyalty through increased visibility and
emotional connection with audiences.
Similarly, Nguyen Thanh (2025) noted that
retail firms that invest in targeted social media
promotions experience higher repeat purchase
intentions. According to Mehta and
Krishnamurthy (2025), brand loyalty is fostered
when customers perceive value and trust
through interactive ad formats like stories and
reels. Evangelin (2025) argued that
engagement-driven social content, such as polls
and live sessions, helps elevate loyalty levels in
competitive markets. In a comparative study of
retail firms, Ridzki (2025) confirmed that social
media advertising, especially on Instagram,
creates more loyal customer segments than
traditional advertising. Lazaro and Marin
(2025) demonstrated that consumer interaction
with sponsored content mediates the link
between digital ads and loyalty. Sahayaraj and
Balan (2025) discovered that real-time
responses to customer queries on social
platforms further strengthen loyalty
perceptions. According to Banerjee (2025),
social media’s visual appeal plays a critical role
in forming emotional bonds with retail brands.
In contrast, Antonia (2025) observed that
brands with low engagement metrics despite
heavy ad spend witnessed limited loyalty
growth. Lastly, Das (2025) stressed that
customer engagement moderates the ad-loyalty
relationship, suggesting that ad effectiveness
depends heavily on how audiences interact with
content.

Moderating Role of Customer
Engagement in Social Media Advertising
Spend and Brand Loyalty
The moderating role of customer
engagement in the relationship between social
media advertising and brand loyalty has gained
significant attention in digital marketing
research. Jiménez-Zarco et al. (2025) found that
customer engagement enhances the emotional
connection between brand messages on
platforms like Snapchat and consumer loyalty.
Hasibuan et al. (2025) argued that storytelling-
driven emotional branding sees its effect on
loyalty amplified when customers are highly
engaged. Similarly, Theocharis et al. (2025)
discovered that digital engagement, via
interactions, shares, and saves, strengthens the
conversion of ad exposure into brand trust.
Zakki and Fathorrahman (2025) confirmed that
consumer loyalty is more likely when
engagement moderates brand experience on
social platforms. Hanifa and Fitriyani (2025)
emphasized that influencer-driven content is
only effective in building loyalty when
customers are active participants. Dai et al.
(2025) demonstrated that anthropomorphic
social media ads gain higher loyalty outcomes
among engaged users. Prasetyo and Wiharso
(2025) noted that digital literacy enhances the
moderating impact of engagement, particularly
in the retail space. Divya and VB (2025)
showed that customer trust, a precursor to
loyalty, is strengthened by engagement during
social ad interactions. Jayapal (2025) also
underscored the importance of engagement in
turning AI-driven personalization into loyalty-
building mechanisms. Lastly, Cheung (2025)
indicated that involvement level directly
moderates the loyalty response to branded
content, validating engagement as a key
strategic lever.
2.2 Theoretical Framework
This study is anchored in Engagement
Theory, first conceptualized by Kearsley and
Shneiderman (1999), and further developed in

digital marketing contexts by Brodie,
Hollebeek, and others. Engagement Theory
posits that active participation, interaction, and
emotional involvement are essential for
creating meaningful outcomes in digital
environments. It assumes that users must not
only be exposed to content but must actively
engage with it for deeper cognitive and
affective effects to emerge—ultimately
influencing behavioral outcomes such as
loyalty.
Applied to this study, social media
advertising represents the stimulus that initiates
a brand-consumer interaction. However, the
theory asserts that the impact of this advertising
depends largely on customer engagement—the
moderating factor that transforms passive
content exposure into active brand
relationships. This makes customer
engagement a central construct in the
advertising–loyalty linkage. Supporting this
theoretical logic, Cheung (2025) and
Theocharis et al. (2025) found that highly
engaged users were more likely to develop
brand loyalty, even when exposed to similar
advertising content. Thus, this study draws on
Engagement Theory to explain how social
media advertising spend influences brand
loyalty, and how this relationship is
significantly conditioned by the level of
customer engagement. The theory offers a
robust foundation for analyzing the interactive
dynamics at play in digital retail environments.
3.0 Methodology
This study adopts a quantitative research
design, aimed at examining the effect of social
media advertising spend on brand loyalty, and
the moderating role of customer engagement.
The target population comprises 382 retail
companies listed in the South African Council
of Shopping Centres (SACSC) directory and
active on social media platforms. Using
purposive sampling, a sample of 120 firms was
selected based on consistent digital marketing
activity and availability of engagement and
loyalty metrics over the past three years.
Data was collected from secondary sources,
including brand reports, advertising budgets,
social media analytics, and customer retention
databases. Descriptive statistics were used to
summarize the central tendencies and
variability of the variables. A correlation matrix
was employed to assess the strength and
direction of relationships between the variables.
To ensure the reliability of regression
estimates, diagnostic tests including
multicollinearity (VIF), heteroskedasticity
(Breusch-Pagan), and normality (Jarque-Bera)
were conducted. The hypotheses were tested
using hierarchical multiple regression analysis,
where the interaction term between social
media ad spend and customer engagement was
included in the second model. All statistical
analyses were conducted using Stata 17, with
significance levels set at 5%.

5.0 Discussion, Conclusion and
Recommendations
This section discusses the empirical results
in relation to the hypotheses, previously
reviewed literature, and theoretical
foundations. It also presents the conclusion,
practical recommendations, limitations, and
suggestions for future research.
H₀₁: Social media advertising spend has no
significant effect on brand loyalty among
retail consumers in South Africa.
The findings of this study reject H₀₁,
confirming that social media advertising spend
has a statistically significant positive effect on
brand loyalty (p = 0.002). This aligns with the
Engagement Theory, which emphasizes the role
of marketing stimuli in forming consumer
relationships. It corroborates previous studies
by Nguyen Thanh (2025) and Mehta and
Krishnamurthy (2025), who found that social
media ads enhance repeat purchase intention
and trust. Ad spend was positively linked with
brand loyalty, reinforcing the strategic
importance of financial commitment in digital
promotions within the South African retail
sector.
H₀₂: Customer engagement has no
significant effect on brand loyalty among
retail consumers in South Africa.
The rejection of H₀₂ affirms that customer
engagement significantly influences brand
loyalty (p = 0.015). This is in agreement with
findings by Danaei (2025) and Xiao & Chen
(2025), which emphasize that interactive
behaviors such as likes and shares foster
emotional bonds with brands. Customer
engagement serves as a behavioral bridge
between advertising content and loyalty
formation. This is also consistent with the
assumptions of Engagement Theory, where
involvement and emotional resonance

strengthen consumer-brand relationships in
digital contexts.
H₀₃: Customer engagement does not
significantly moderate the relationship
between social media advertising spend and
brand loyalty among retail consumers in
South Africa.
The study rejects H₀₃, as the interaction term
between social media ad spend and customer
engagement was statistically significant (p =
0.003). This confirms that the effectiveness of
ad spend on loyalty is amplified when customer
engagement is high. This result resonates with
the findings of Cheung (2025) and Das (2025),
who emphasized engagement as a strategic

This interaction effect enhances the explanatory
power of the model (R² increased from 0.426 to
0.489), further validating the theoretical
proposition that engagement transforms mere
exposure into active brand loyalty.
Conclusion
In summary, this study establishes that both
social media advertising spend and customer
engagement individually and interactively
contribute to brand loyalty in South Africa’s
retail sector. The findings validate Engagement
Theory and provide practical insights into how
digital marketing strategies can be optimized
for stronger brand relationships.
Recommendations
i. Retail marketers should allocate
adequate budgets to social media
advertising as it directly boosts brand
loyalty.
ii. Firms must actively monitor and enhance
customer engagement metrics such as
likes, comments, and shares to build
lasting loyalty.
iii. Social media content should be designed
to not only inform but also encourage
interaction, maximizing the synergistic
effect between advertising and
engagement.
Limitations of the Study
The study is limited to a sample of 120 retail
firms in South Africa, and findings may not be
generalizable to other sectors or regions.
Moreover, engagement was measured using
platform-based metrics, which may not fully
capture emotional or cognitive involvement.
Frontiers for Further Research
Future studies could explore sectoral
comparisons, integrate qualitative measures of
engagement, or examine long-term causal
effects using panel data. Additionally, cross-
country comparisons within Sub-Saharan
Africa could reveal cultural or economic factors
influencing digital engagement and loyalty.
