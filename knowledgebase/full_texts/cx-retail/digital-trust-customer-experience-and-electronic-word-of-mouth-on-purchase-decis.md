ABSTRACT
The rapid growth of e-commerce in Indonesia has significantly transformed consumer behavior
in digital purchasing activities. In the increasingly competitive digital business environment,
companies are required to understand the factors influencing customer satisfaction and purchase
decisions. This study aims to analyze the effects of digital trust, customer experience, and E-WOM
on customer satisfaction and purchase decisions, as well as to examine the mediating role of customer
satisfaction. The research employed a quantitative approach with an explanatory research design.
Data were collected through an online questionnaire distributed to 250 active e-commerce users in
Indonesia who had completed at least two transactions within the last six months. Data analysis
was conducted using Structural Equation Modeling–Partial Least Squares (SEM-PLS) with
SmartPLS 4.1.1.8 software. The findings reveal that digital trust, customer experience, and E-WOM
have positive and significant effects on both customer satisfaction and purchase decisions. Customer
experience emerged as the most dominant factor influencing satisfaction and purchasing behavior.
Furthermore, customer satisfaction was found to mediate the effects of digital trust, customer
experience, and E-WOM on purchase decisions. These findings imply the importance of enhancing
platform security, improving customer experience quality, and effectively managing digital reviews
to strengthen consumer purchase decisions.

INTRODUCTION
The rapid advancement of information technology and internet penetration has
fundamentally restructured consumer behavior in digital purchasing activities. The
growth of e-commerce in Indonesia has escalated substantially, aligned with shifts in
consumer lifestyles that are increasingly reliant on digital platforms to satisfy daily
requirements (Kanimozhi, 2023; Shafiq et al., 2023). Within the context of intensifying
digital business competition, firms are compelled to identify the factors governing
customer satisfaction and purchase decisions to sustain customer loyalty. Digital trust has
emerged as a paramount factor, as consumers prioritize transaction security, personal
data protection, and platform credibility before engaging in online transactions  (Al-
Adwan et al., 2020; Rugati & Santoso, 2025). Furthermore, customer experience and
Electronic Word-of-Mouth (E-WOM) play instrumental roles in constructing customer
perceptions regarding digital service quality ( Wahyuningjati & Purwanto, 2024).
According to Becker and Jaakkola (2020), customer experience reflects the holistic
impression of a consumer during interactions with a firm, spanning both the pre- and post-
transaction phases. Meanwhile, Lemon and Verhoef (2020) argue that a positive customer
experience can enhance perceived value and solidify the emotional connection between
consumers and digital services.

In the digital business environment, customer satisfaction serves as a critical metric for
assessing a company’s capacity to meet consumer expectations. Satisfaction is realized
when the services rendered align with or surpass consumer expectations, thereby fostering
customer loyalty and repeat purchase behavior (Oliver, 1999; Irfan, 2024). In this study,
customer satisfaction is operationalized through overall satisfaction, expectation
fulfillment, service convenience, and positive customer encounters. The digital trust
variable is assessed through transaction security, privacy protection, platform reliability,
and the credibility of digital information. The customer experience variable is measured
via application usability, service quality, transaction convenience, and the quality of
customer interactions. Additionally, the E-WOM variable is evaluated through review
quality, recommendation credibility, communication intensity, and the impact of
consumer-generated content. The purchase decision, as the dependent variable, is
quantified using purchase confidence, product preference, purchase frequency, and
repurchase intention. Previous research has demonstrated that digital trust, customer
experience, and E-WOM exert a positive influence on customer satisfaction and purchase
decisions within digital platforms (Joudeh et al., 2024; Handoyo, 2024).
While numerous studies have examined the relationships among digital trust,
customer experience, e-WOM, customer satisfaction, and purchase decisions, several
research gaps warrant further exploration. Most existing literature focuses predominantly
on the direct impact of independent variables on purchase decisions, often overlooking
the comprehensive mediating role of customer satisfaction. For instance, Febriyanti and
Purwanto (2023) emphasized the influence of E-WOM and consumer trust on purchase
decisions within TikTok Shop, whereas Dewi and Padmantyo (2025) investigated the
relationship between e-trust and e-WOM on customer satisfaction, utilizing customer
loyalty as a mediator. Furthermore, research integrating digital trust, customer
experience, and E-WOM into a single, cohesive model in the Indonesian e-commerce
landscape remains relatively scarce. The novelty of this research lies in the simultaneous
examination of these three variables by incorporating customer satisfaction as a mediating
variable influencing purchase decisions on digital platforms. This framework is expected
to yield a more profound understanding of digital consumer behavior in Indonesia.
This study aims to analyze the effect of digital trust, customer experience, and
electronic word-of-mouth on customer satisfaction and purchase decisions among e -
commerce platform users in Indonesia. Moreover, this research seeks to evaluate the
mediating role of customer satisfaction in the relationship among digital trust, customer
experience, and E-WOM in relation to consumer purchase decisions. By mapping the
interrelationships between these variables, this study intends to provide empirical
evidence concerning the determinants of consumer behavior in digital business settings.
The contributions of this research are anticipated to offer both theoretical and practical
implications. This study enriches the digital marketing management literature by
synthesizing digital trust, customer experience, and E-WOM into a unified research
model that identifies customer satisfaction as a mediator. It also broadens the conceptual
understanding of digital consumer behavior, specifically within the Indonesian e -
commerce context. The findings are intended to serve as strategic benchmarks for e-
commerce managers in formulating more effective digital marketing strategies by
improving platform security, elevating the quality of the customer experience, and
optimizing the management of customer reviews. As a result, companies are expected to
enhance customer satisfaction and reinforce long-term consumer purchase decisions.

The Influence on Customer Satisfaction
Digital trust represents the degree of consumer confidence in the security,
transparency, and reliability of digital platforms during online service interactions. In the
digital business landscape, trust is a pivotal determinant as consumers rigorously evaluate
transaction safety, privacy safeguards, and informational credibility prior to purchasing
(Setiyawami et al., 2025). This construct is typically operationalized through metrics such

digital trust, which mitigates perceived risks and enhances service evaluations. Halim et
al. (2023) underscored that trust within digital services facilitates satisfaction by ensuring
reliable transactional processes and positive electronic service encounters. Consequently,
maintaining secure and transparent systems allows firms to deepen long-term consumer

Furthermore, customer experience encompasses the holistic emotional, cognitive, and
functional impressions formed during information searching, purchasing, and post -
purchase phases (Luthfi et al., 2025). Measured through application usability,
responsiveness, and transaction convenience, positive experiences are fundamental to
driving customer satisfaction. Parallel to this, E-WOM, comprising review quality,
credibility, and communication intensity, shapes favorable perceptions of service quality.
Research indicates that seamless digital interactions and positive E-WOM significantly
elevate consumer satisfaction levels. Shandra et al. (2025) asserted that experience and
trust are intrinsically linked in forming positive service perceptions, while Cheung et al.
(2009) contended that constructive E-WOM refines service evaluations and enhances
satisfaction. Thus, companies that prioritize superior customer experiences and foster
positive online discourse are better positioned to achieve competitive satisfaction levels in
the digital economy.

The Influence on Purchase Decision
The purchase decision represents a consumer’s strategic process of selecting, acquiring,
and utilizing products or services based on specific evaluative criteria. Within digital
landscapes, these decisions are fundamentally shaped by trust in online platforms,
interactive service encounters, and information synthesized from digital communication
channels. This construct is typically operationalized through purchase confidence,
product preference, transaction frequency, and repurchase intention. Digital trust emerges
as a critical antecedent, as consumers gravitate toward platforms perceived as secure and
credible. Key metrics for this variable include system security, privacy safeg uards,
platform credibility, and service consistency. Mukti and Isa (2024) observed that trust
significantly bolsters purchase decisions by instilling confidence in consumers interacting
with high-reputation platforms. Similarly, Murtiasih (2025) argued th at brand trust
reinforces decision-making by augmenting consumer certainty regarding product quality
and security. Consequently, elevated digital trust levels facilitate more consistent
purchasing behavior in e-commerce environments, making trust maintenance a strategic
imperative for firms seeking sustainable competitive advantages.
Beyond digital trust, the synergy of customer experience, E-WOM, and customer
satisfaction significantly dictates purchasing outcomes. Customer experience assessed
through interaction quality, application usability, and emotional engagement generates
favorable value perceptions that catalyze transactions. E-WOM is evaluated via review
quality, recommendation credibility, and the persuasive power of user-generated content.
Positive online discourse serves to validate consumer confidence in ser vice quality.
Furthermore, customer satisfaction, measured through expectation fulfillment and service
loyalty, acts as a pivotal driver for repeat behavior. Zahid and Ruswanti (2024) noted that
E-WOM exerts a profound impact on purchase decisions, as consumers often prioritize
peer recommendations over corporate promotions. Additionally, Sneha and Student
(2025) highlighted that the credibility of digital information and active E -WOM
engagement are vital for strengthening decision -making processes. Optimizing the

The Influence of Customer Satisfaction as a Mediator
Customer satisfaction serves as a pivotal mediator between digital trust and purchase
decisions, representing a comprehensive evaluation of service performance relative to
consumer expectations (Heda et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2025). Within the digital
ecosystem, satisfaction is operationalized through overall contentment, service
convenience, expectation fulfillment, and consumer loyalty. Digital trust is assessed via
transaction security, privacy safeguards, platform reliability, and information credibility
(Yum & Kim, 2024; Rahmawati & Puspita, 2024). Consumers possessing elevated trust
in digital services experience enhanced security and comfort during transactions, which
directly bolsters their satisfaction levels. This heightene d satisfaction subsequently
empowers consumers to execute purchase decisions with increased confidence and
consistency. Sudaryanto et al. (2025) elucidated that trust reinforces purchasing behavior
by augmenting consumer confidence within digital marketplaces through the lens of
satisfaction. Furthermore, Halim et al. (2023) underscored that satisfaction is
fundamentally derived from superior digital service quality and robust platform trust,
which collectively stabilize the consumer’s decision-making process.
Additionally, customer satisfaction functions as a mediating mechanism for the impact
of customer experience and E-WOM on purchase decisions (Özçelik, 2020; Hasyim et
al., 2025). Customer experience is defined by service quality, application usability, and
emotional engagement, while E-WOM is characterized by review quality, information
credibility, and the persuasive influence of recommendations. Positive digital encounters
and constructive peer reviews elevate customer satisfaction, thereby increasing purchase
propensity. Yunitasari et al. (2025) observed that E-WOM exerts an indirect influence on
purchasing outcomes by first shaping the level of consumer satisfaction. Moreover,
Shandra et al. (2025) contended that superior customer experiences foster a dual sense of
trust and satisfaction, which are essential for driving stronger adoption and definitive
purchasing actions within the competitive digital marketplace. Consequently, the
optimization of these digital antecedents is critical for facilitating satisfied consumers who
are more inclined toward long-term transactional commitment.

RESEARCH METHODS
This study adopts a quantitative approach utilizing an explanatory research design to
systematically analyze the impact of digital trust, customer experience, and E-WOM on
customer satisfaction and purchase decisions among e-commerce users in Indonesia. The
quantitative framework was specifically chosen to facilitate the examination of causal
relationships between variables through rigorous statistical inference. Using a survey
methodology, the study collected primary data from a targeted demographic that aligned
with predefined research parameters. Furthermore, a cross -sectional design was
employed, ensuring that data collection occurred within a focused timeframe to capture
a snapshot of current consumer behavior.
The research population comprises active participants within Indonesia’s e-commerce
ecosystem. To ensure data quality and relevance, a purposive sampling technique was
applied, necessitating that respondents had finalized a minimum of two transactions via
digital platforms within the preceding six months. A total of 250 respondents were
included in the final analysis. This sample size is deemed statistically sufficient for
Structural Equation Modeling–Partial Least Squares (SEM-PLS) analysis, as it surpasses
the minimum thresholds for multivariate modeling and provides a robust representation
of the broader population’s characteristics within the Indonesian digital market.
The conceptual framework integrates five primary variables: digital trust, customer
experience, E-WOM, customer satisfaction, and purchase decision. Digital trust is
operationalized through transaction security, privacy safeguards, platform reliability, and
the credibility of digital information. Customer experience is evaluated via application
usability, service quality, transactional ease, and the emotional resonance of the
interaction. The E-WOM construct is measured by assessing review quality, information
credibility, communication intensity, and the persuasive power of peer recommendations.
Customer satisfaction is measured by overall contentment, expectation fulfillment, service
convenience, and positive experiences. The purchase decision variable is captured by
indicators of purchase confidence, product preference, transaction frequency, repurchase
intention, and willingness to provide referrals.
Primary data were elicited via an online questionnaire utilizing a five-point Likert
scale, ranging from “strongly disagree” to “strongly agree.” The research instrument was
meticulously developed by adapting validated indicators from established literature to
ensure construct validity. For data processing, Structural Equation Modeling –Partial
Least Squares (SEM-PLS) was executed using SmartPLS 4.1.1.8 software. The analysis
stages included outer model testing consisting of convergent validity, discriminant
validity, and construct reliability. Hypothesis testing was conducted using the
bootstrapping method with a significance level of α = 0.05 or t-statistic > 1.96.

DISCUSSION
The empirical analysis reveals that digital trust plays a pivotal role in enhancing both
customer satisfaction and purchase decisions. These findings confirm that data
transparency, transaction security, and platform credibility constitute the primary
foundation for consumers within the e-commerce ecosystem. High levels of trust mitigate
perceived risks, a common barrier in digital transactions, thereby fostering a sense of
security that leads to satisfaction. This aligns with the research by Dewi and Padmantyo
(2025) and Fatmawati and Ulfa (2026), which posits that e-trust is a fundamental pillar in
establishing a positive emotional bond between consumers and service providers.
Furthermore, Murtiasih (2025) and Ashari and Syahputra (2025) emphasize that trust in
digital platform security directly reinforces consumer confidence to execute purchase
decisions without hesitation.
The customer experience variable emerged as the most dominant factor influencing
customer satisfaction and purchase decisions. A seamless experience, ranging from an
intuitive application interface to service responsiveness, creates a direct perceived value
for the user. These findings provide empirical evidence that positive functional and
emotional interactions transcend mere basic need fulfillment. Consistent with the
arguments of Lemon and Verhoef (2020), Christianti and Hasanah (2025), and Dyatmika
et al. (2025), a superior customer experience serves as a primary catalyst in creating
perceived value. The study by Shandra et al. (2025) further reinforces these results, stating
that high-quality digital interactions significantly increase consumer intentions to
continue using the platform and engage in repeat purchasing behavior.
The influence of E-WOM in this study underscores that user-generated information is
a critical determinant in shaping quality perceptions. Positive reviews, recommendation
credibility, and the intensity of digital discourse serve as social proof that validates
consumer choices. In an asymmetric digital market, E-WOM acts as an informational
bridge that mitigates uncertainty. This finding is supported by Febriyanti and Purwanto
(2023) and Simanjuntak et al. (2025), who highlighted that consumer reviews on social
platforms like TikTok Shop exert a stronger psychological impact than conventional
promotions. Additionally, Zahid and Ruswanti (2024) and Hamijaya et al. (2024)
explained that credible digital recommendations strengthen consumer confidence in
product selection, ultimately increasing purchase decisions.

Customer satisfaction has been shown to act as an effective mediator linking digital
trust, experience, and E-WOM to purchase decisions. This indicates that to achieve
consistent purchasing behavior, firms must not only provide a secure platform and
enjoyable experience but also ensure these elements culminate in user contentment.
Satisfaction functions as an evaluative filter that amplifies the impact of independent
variables on final consumer behavior. Sudaryanto et al. (2025) and Dewi et al. (2025)
assert that satisfaction is a vital intervening variable in converting trust into loyalty and
purchase intention. Research by Yunitasari et al. (2025) and Nasir (2025) also concludes
that without a sense of satisfaction, the stimuli from positive reviews or user experiences
are insufficient to drive sustainable purchase decisions.
The integration of systemic security, interaction quality, and the management of digital
public opinion represents a key strategy for winning competition in Indonesia’s digital
business sector. Companies capable of managing these three aspects simultaneously will
create an ecosystem conducive to the growth of purchase decisions. As suggested by
Nasution and Sari (2025) and Abadi et al. (2025), the synergy between reliable technology
and sustained customer satisfaction is a primary driver of long-term competitiveness.
These findings imply that digital marketing managers must focus on enhancing service
quality based on user experience data to optimize sales conversion and strengthen future
customer retention.

CONCLUSION
This study provides robust empirical evidence that digital trust, customer experience,
and E-WOM significantly enhance customer satisfaction and purchase decisions within
the Indonesian e-commerce landscape. The findings confirm that customer satisfaction
serves as a vital mediator, effectively bridging the gap between digital antecedents and
final transactional behavior. This research enriches digital marketing literature by
validating and extending established frameworks using localized empirical data. From a
managerial perspective, these results suggest that e -commerce professionals must
prioritize a tripartite strategy: bolstering digital trust through stringent security
certifications, optimizing the customer journey via intuitive interfaces, and proactively
managing digital reviews to foster social proof. By synergizing these elements, firms can
cultivate a high-satisfaction ecosystem that fundamentally strengthens long-term purchase
intentions and competitive advantage.
Despite these insights, several limitations must be acknowledged to guide future
inquiry. This study utilized a cross-sectional design, which captures consumer behavior
at a single point in time and precludes the analysis of longitudinal trends. Furthermore,
the sample was restricted to active e -commerce users, potentially limiting the
generalizability of the findings to broader or non-user populations. Future research should
address these gaps by adopting longitudinal or experimental approaches to provide a more
definitive explanation of causal relationships. Additionally, incorporating moderator
variables such as digital literacy, brand trust, or socio-economic status could offer a more
nuanced understanding of consumer diversity. Exploring the quality of user-generated
content and its specific impact on the mediation mechanism would also provide a valuable
extension to this comprehensive digital behavior model.
