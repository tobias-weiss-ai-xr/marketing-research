  Abstract. The digitalisation of the banking industry represents a major challenge, closely linked to the growth of
financial technologies and customer expectations. The purpose of this paper was to examine the main digital banking
services, the key financial technologies that support them and their benefits and challenges for banks and their customers.
This study was based on qualitative analysis, literature review and a systematic organisation of information related
to modern digital banking services. As a result, eight key digital services that fundamentally changed the customer
experience were identified, including online account management, contactless mobile payments, instant transfers,
e-wallets, chatbots, online investment and automated budgeting tools. Furthermore, main financial technologies such
as artificial intelligence, blockchain, cloud computing, big data and cybersecurity were shown to improve operational
efficiency, transaction security and service personalisation. The results showed that the digitalisation of banking services
improved accessibility, convenience, financial inclusion and budget optimisation for customers. However, this study also
emphasised major challenges related to data privacy, cybercrime, reliance on technology, reduced human interaction
and digital isolation. These challenges, combined with sociocultural barriers, prevent 1.3 billion unbanked adults from
accessing digital banking services. The practical value of this paper lies in demonstrating the need for banks and financial
institutions to invest in secure infrastructure, hybrid solutions combining digital services and human support, and data
protection policies that enhance customer trust and improve the stability of digital banking services

 INTRODUCTION
Digitalisation has become one of the main forces driving the
evolution of the global banking system. The rise of digital
technologies and platforms has greatly changed tradition -
al banking services. Banks are no longer focused solely on
traditional banking activities. They are trying to build in -
tegrated ecosystems that provide fast, personalised and
remotely accessible services. With globalisation and rapid
technological progress, modern banking is becoming a cus-
tomer-centric digital system. It is based on predictive ana-
lytics, process automation and smart banking services. The
COVID-19 pandemic also played a major role in accelerating
the digitalisation of banking services. Health restrictions
and the rapid shift to remote work prompted millions of
customers to use digital services, electronic payments and
mobile applications. These changes increased competition
between traditional banks and fintech companies. As a re -
sult, banks adopted more ambitious digital transformation
strategies to remain competitive and attractive. Banks are
investing heavily in advanced cybersecurity systems, ro -
botic process automation (RPA) and open banking solu -
tions. These investments contribute to improved customer
service and increased operational efficiency.

banking industry encompasses not only technological im -
provements but also changes in organisational structures,
management and customer relationships. The study by

of artificial intelligence and digital technologies into bank-
ing services is reshaping decision-making processes and risk

 MATERIALS AND METHODS
This study employed a descriptive qualitative approach
based on a literature review and comparative analysis of
secondary data. Its objective was to examine the digitalisa-
tion of the banking industry. The research was conducted
in several stages. First, relevant literature was identified
and analysed, enabling the development of a theoretical
framework. Second, digital banking services and the tech -
nologies that support them were systematically examined.
Third, comparative and qualitative analyses were carried
out to assess the benefits, challenges and impacts of digi -
talisation for banks and their customers. Finally, an analy-
sis of financial inclusion statistics was conducted to iden -
tify trends in regions of Africa and Asia regarding access to
banking services.
The study used secondary data derived from peer-re -
viewed journal articles, books, institutional reports, regu -
latory publications and statistical databases. The primary
academic sources were publications indexed in Scopus, Web
of Science, ScienceDirect and SpringerLink. The inclusion
criteria focused on publications directly addressing modern
banking services, the digitisation of the banking sector and
digital banking technologies, with preference given to the
most recent publications to ensure the relevance and reli -
ability of the analysis. Sources lacking academic or institu-
tional credibility, not directly relevant to the research topic
or containing outdated information were excluded. Statisti-
cal data were collected from reports and databases published
by the World Bank (Demirgüç-Kunt et al., 2022; Klapper et
al.,  2025), the International Monetary Fund (IMF,  2025),
and the European Banking Authority (EBA, 2025). Addi -
tional data were obtained from publications by ACI World-
wide  (2024), Juniper Research  (2024), Deloitte  (2022),
the IBM Institute for Business Value (IBM,  2026).
The data sources were selected for the period 2018-
2026. This period reflects the rapid growth of digital ser -
vices following the widespread adoption of technologies
and digital devices. The following keyword combinations

inclusion”, “unbanked population”, “customer experience”

conducted. The main digital banking services were first
identified and classified according to their functional pur -
pose. The technologies underlying these services were then
comparatively analysed using the following criteria: func -
tional role, practical application in banking, advantages,
limitations, and contribution to banking innovation. The
qualitative analysis was based on a thematic approach.

 RESULTS
In line with the purpose of this study, the results identi -
fied main patterns in the digitalisation of modern banking
services. In particular, they highlight the widespread use
of digital technologies that are changing how services are
delivered. To assess these changes, it is useful to consider
the most widely used digital banking services. Online ac -
count management enables remote access to core banking
functions, including balance and transaction monitoring,
personal data management, card control and automat -
ed spending notifications via mobile applications. Digital
channels handle more than 70% of regular banking ac -
tivities, which has significantly reduced branch visits and
service costs (Klapper  et al., 2025). Contactless payments
based on Near Field Communication (NFC) technology en-
able cardless transactions through smartphones and other
compatible devices. Mobile payment solutions, including
Apple Pay, Google Pay and Samsung Wallet, combine con-
tactless transaction processing with biometric authenti -
cation, contributing to shorter processing times and en -
hanced payment security. By 2024, contactless payments
accounted for more than 60% of in-store card transactions
in many developed countries. At the same time, the num -
ber of mobile wallet users worldwide has exceeded 4 billion
(Juniper Research, 2024).
Instant transfers enable funds to be transferred be -
tween accounts within seconds, including outside standard
banking hours. Compared with traditional transfer meth -
ods, which may require several hours or days for settle -
ment, real-time payment systems provide substantially
faster access to funds. In many such systems, transactions
are processed in less than 10 seconds. Global real-time
payment volume exceeded 266 billion transactions in 2023
(ACI Worldwide, 2024). Banks increasingly use AI-powered
chatbots to automate routine customer support functions.
These systems provide information on account-relat -
ed matters, assist with basic service requests and explain
banking procedures. Their continuous availability enables
banks to reduce customer waiting times and improve the
efficiency of service delivery. According to IBM  (2026),
AI-powered chatbots can resolve approximately 70-80% of
routine customer enquiries without human intervention,
potentially reducing customer service costs by up to 30%.
Online investment services provide customers with direct
access to financial markets through mobile applications
and web-based platforms. These services support trans -
actions involving stocks, bonds and other financial instru -
ments and may incorporate analytical tools and automated
advisory systems, including robo-advisors, to support in -
vestment decision-making.
Banking applications increasingly incorporate spend -
ing analysis and budgeting tools that support personal fi -
nancial management through automated transaction cate-
gorisation and expenditure monitoring. Some applications
also provide notifications when predefined spending limits
are exceeded or when substantial payments are anticipat -
ed. More broadly, the digitalisation of banking services has
transformed the ways in which customers manage financial
transactions and interact with financial institutions. Con -
tinued investment in digital technologies reflects banks’
efforts to improve operational efficiency, maintain com -
petitiveness and enhance the accessibility and quality of
service delivery. Artificial intelligence is one of the most
widely used technologies in the banking industry. By lev -
eraging AI, banks can automate various tasks, such as re -
sponding to customer inquiries, analysing financial data,
and detecting fraudulent activity (Horobets et al., 2025). AI
is also used to assess credit risk by predictively analysing
borrowers’ financial behaviour. Banks use algorithms that
can identify suspicious transactions in real time to prevent
bank fraud. According to EBA  (2025), AI-based fraud de -
tection systems can achieve accuracy rates exceeding 90%.

Digital financial technologies provide many benefits
to banking customers. One of the main benefits is acces -
sibility. Through banking mobile apps and websites, cus -
tomers can check their account balances, send money and
pay utility bills anytime, anywhere. This is especially con -
venient for residents in rural areas with few bank branches.
This eliminates the need for long trips to the nearest bank
branch. Furthermore, digitalisation makes banking servic -
es more accessible to people with mobility difficulties or
time constraints (Ananda  et al. ,  2020). Digital technolo-
gies make banking services far more convenient and faster.
Customers no longer have to wait in long lines for simple
banking tasks. Transactions can be completed in seconds,
even on weekends and public holidays. Studies show that
digital banking transactions typically take less than a
minute. Similar transactions completed at a bank branch
often take 15-30 minutes, including waiting times
(Deloitte,  2022). This enhances the customer experience
and saves time in daily life (Müller et al., 2024).
Digital banking services leverage data and artificial
intelligence to provide personalised services and products
to customers. Banking applications can analyse spending
habits and recommend solutions tailored to each user’s
needs. For example, they can send notifications if a cus -
tomer exceeds their monthly budget. They can also provide
personalised savings plans to help customers reach their
financial goals. Banking platforms such as Monzo and Bank
of America also offer automated advice on spending and
investing. This personalised approach helps customers
manage their finances more effectively and increases their
trust in banking services. Automated payments are another
important advantage of digital banking services. Custom -
ers can schedule recurring payments such as utilities, rent
and monthly fees. This eliminates the need to enter pay -
ment details manually every month. Automated payment
systems significantly reduce missed payments and improve
on-time bill payments for both households and businesses.
Businesses also benefit from this feature, processing pay -
roll and supplier payments quickly and securely (Daoud &
Anaya, 2025).

of people in Asia and Africa to make transactions, save
money and receive financial assistance. This convenience
has supported economic development and reduced so -
cial inequality. It has also facilitated access to loans and

Barriers to accessing digital banking services show
broad similarities across regions; however, the predomi -
nant constraint varies by regional context. These include
the limited availability of digital banking service providers
in African countries, socio-cultural restrictions on wom -
en’s access to mobile phones in Middle Eastern countries,
low-income levels in South Asian countries, and inade -
quate digital infrastructure in rural areas across these re -
gions. While digitalisation of banking services offers many
benefits, it also creates significant challenges for banks and
customers. Security is one of the biggest issues in digital
banking services. Cybercriminals use various methods, in -
cluding malware, phishing and hacking, to steal money and
personal information (Asmar & Tuqan, 2024).
Banks continue to report that phishing is one of the
main causes of digital banking fraud. Although the use of
multi-factor authentication has significantly reduced ac -
count takeover fraud, it still accounts for a large propor -
tion of reported cyber incidents. For example, custom -
ers may receive fake messages that appear to come from
their banks. These messages can lead to the theft of their
login information. Attacks on banking systems can cause
significant financial losses and disrupt operations. To re -
duce these risks, banks invest heavily in data encryption,
two-factor authentication and fraud detection systems.
Digital banking services collect vast amounts of personal
and financial data. This includes customer identity, spend-
ing habits and transaction records. Storing so much sen -
sitive information in one place increases the risk of data
misuse and leakage. Furthermore, some companies may
use customer data for commercial or advertising purpos -
es without sufficient transparency. Therefore, banks must
develop strong data protection policies and comply with
privacy regulations (Barjaktarović Rakočević et al., 2023).
Banking operations are becoming increasingly de -
pendent on digital infrastructure and IT systems. This
dependence can cause problems when technical failures,
network outages or software errors occur. For example, in
January 2025, Barclays experienced a major IT outage that
lasted for more than two days. Many customers were un -
able to access online banking services, make payments or
complete transactions. During the same period, customers
could not receive their salaries on time. This outage also
occurred during the UK tax return deadline and affect -
ed thousands of users. (Reuters, 2025). In February 2025,
Halifax and Bank of Scotland experienced another out -
age. This disrupted mobile banking and payment services
for several hours. As a result, customers were unable to
transfer money or access their accounts (Makortoff & Ban-
field-Nwachi, 2025). These incidents show that over-reli -
ance on technology can increase operational risks. It can
also expose banks to service disruptions and cyberattacks
targeting servers and payment systems (Sharma,  2025).
Therefore, investment in backup systems, regular updates
and business continuity plans is essential for banks and
financial institutions.
As digital banking services become more widespread,
direct contact between customers and bank employees is
gradually decreasing. While mobile banking applications
are convenient, they cannot always replace the human
support needed for complex transactions such as mortgag-
es and financial advice. For example, older adults and those
unfamiliar with digital tools may find it difficult to resolve
issues using only chatbots and automated services. The
reduction in human interaction can make some custom -
ers feel isolated and dissatisfied (Ghosh & Golder,  2026).
Therefore, banks need to maintain a balance between au -
tomation and personalised service. The digital divide is a
significant social challenge in the development of digital
banking services. In particular, the elderly, people living
in rural areas and low-income groups do not always have
access to the internet or the necessary digital devices. In
accordance with International Telecommunication Un -
ion (2024), an estimated 2.6 billion people worldwide still
did not have access to the internet in 2024. This has lim -
ited their ability to access digital banking services. Physi -
cal bank branches are gradually closing around the world,
forcing customers to use unfamiliar digital tools. This sit -
uation risks exacerbating social and economic inequalities.

 DISCUSSION
The results of this study demonstrate that digitalisation
has greatly altered modern banking services. It has im -
proved accessibility, increased transaction speeds and pro-
vided a more personalised customer experience. Analysis of
various banking and financial services reveals that digital
technologies have become central to financial institutions’
strategies. These findings support several recent studies on
banking innovation and the digitalisation of banking ser -
vices. A. Muduli & A. Choudhury (2026) found that the use
of digital technologies significantly improves the efficien -
cy and competitiveness of banks. Furthermore, the authors
emphasised that even small banks can compete with larger
banks through the digitalisation of their services. These
findings are consistent with the results of this study, which
show that digital services enable banks to provide faster
and more accessible services to a wider range of customers.
Similarly, S.  Kamboj et al.  (2022) investigated mobile
banking platforms. They found that always-on access to
banking services improves customer satisfaction. Their
study notably emphasised the convenience of mobile apps
and the speed of banking transactions. This is especial -
ly evident when looking at services that make everyday
banking easier, including instant transfers and payments.
A study by S.E. Lolemo & H.B. Pandya (2025) focused on
the quality of digital banking services from a customer per-
spective. The authors showed that ease of use, security and
customisation are the most important factors in assessing
digital banking services. The results of this study support
these findings. They show that banks can use artificial in -
telligence and big data tools to provide personalised ser -
vices tailored to individual customer needs.
Furthermore, D. Kang & N. Shin (2026) examined how
customers view digital banking services. Their research re-
vealed that customers particularly value the stable availa -
bility, ease of use and speed of online services. However, the
researchers emphasised that concerns about cybersecurity
and limited digital skills are major barriers to the adoption
of digital services. These observations are consistent with
the findings of this study regarding risks associated with
security, privacy and the digital divide. A study by N. Del
Sarto et al. (2025) focused on the relationship between tra-
ditional banks and fintech companies. The authors revealed
that banks are collaborating with technology companies to
accelerate digitalisation and improve banking services. The
study also confirmed that technologies such as e-wallets,
online platforms and mobile applications are essential ele-
ments of the modern banking industry.
M.  Shabir et al .  (2023) conducted a study during the
COVID-19 pandemic. Their study revealed that this health
crisis increased the use of mobile banking applications
and digital payments. The authors highlighted reliability,
speed and performance as the main reasons for customers
to adopt digital banking services. This analysis confirms the
findings of this study that customers prefer services that
are fast, accessible and always available. From a technical
perspective, T. Felipe et al. (2026) investigated the impact of
main digital innovations on the banking industry. Their re-
search revealed that these technologies improve data man-
agement, security and overall efficiency within banks. The
results of this study are follow the same pattern, highlight-
ing the role of AI in fraud detection, blockchain in ensuring
security and cloud computing in developing digital services.
Z. Yu & J. Liu (2025) explored the digitalisation of bank-
ing services from a risk management perspective. Their
research revealed that digital technologies help reduce
fraud, improve regulatory compliance and make financial
transactions efficient. These results are consistent with
the findings of this study, particularly regarding the im -
portance of cybersecurity, biometrics and fraud detection
systems. Finally, the study of G. Osuma (2025) on financial
inclusion in African countries revealed that digitalisation
improves access to banking services for people in rural
areas and previously excluded from the banking system.
This study supports the important role of technologies in
reducing financial inequality. However, as many research -
ers have reported, limited internet access, low literacy and
economic constraints continue to hinder full financial in -
clusion. Overall, the reviewed research confirms that dig -
italisation is an important milestone in the development
of the global banking industry. Digital technologies signif-
icantly improve operational efficiency, service quality and
customer experience. At the same time, this evolution also
creates considerable challenges such as cybersecurity, data
protection, technology dependency and digital exclusion.
Therefore, banks must continue to invest in technological
infrastructure, security and digital education programs.
These efforts are necessary to achieve a secure, sustainable
and inclusive digital transformation.
 CONCLUSIONS
Digitalisation has made banking services faster, more ac -
cessible and more customer-centric. The findings of this
study showed that main digital banking services included
online account management, mobile banking applications,
contactless payments, instant money transfers, digital wal-
lets, AI-powered chatbots, online investment platforms
and automated budgeting tools. They allowed customers
to access banking services anytime, complete transactions
faster, manage their finances more easily and improve ac -
cess to banking services for both individuals and business-
es. The study identified AI, blockchain, big data analytics,
cloud computing and biometric authentication as key tech-
nologies driving digitalisation in banking services. AI im -
proves customer service, helps detect fraud and supports
credit risk assessment. Blockchain makes transactions
more secure and transparent. Big data analytics help banks
deliver personalised banking services. Cloud computing
gives banks greater operational agility. It also allows them
to develop digital banking services more quickly. Biometric
authentication increases security by providing a more se -
cure way to verify customer identity.
While digital banking offered many benefits, the study
also identified several important challenges. The main
risks included cybersecurity threats such as phishing, mal-
ware, hacking and fraud. There were also privacy concerns
associated with banks collecting and processing large
