The recent rapid entry of GenAI into financial services is transforming traditional
personalization into contextual, adaptive and conversational decision support at the
same time that limited theory -grounded empirical evidence explains how such
capabilities translate into customer - and / or inclusion -oriented effects. In this
regard, this study motivate s and develops one GenAI -Enabled Hyper -
Personalization Decision Framework (GEHPDF), comprising elements between
GenAI-enabled hyper -personalization to customer experience, customer
engagement, and financial inclusion respectively, with perceived explainability as
a boundary condition focused on trust or privacy concern or perceived algorithmic
fairness depending on responsible -AI behavior. Methods: This was a cross -
sectional survey using data from 550 users of digital financial services. By using
PLS-SEM, b ootstrapped mediation and moderation analysis and out -of-sample
predictive performance evaluation with competing machine -learning-based
models to validate the proposed framework. The model provides conclusive
positive effects of hyper -personalisation throu gh GenAI on the customer
experience (β = 0.612), of experience on engagement (β = 0.684) and of
engagement on financial inclusion (β = 0.571). Sequential mediation analysis
shows hyper -personalisation mediates financial inclusion (β=0.239) through the
customer experience and engagement. Trust, explainability and algorithmic
fairness significantly strengthen and privacy concerns only weakens the
relationship between personalisation and experience. From the observations, we
can see that with some features, th e model predicts reasonably well but in an
overall comparison Gradient Boosting is giving better comparative predictive
performance of 87.8% accuracy and AUC = 0.934 The next step consists of a
unified explanatory -predictive architecture on GenAI, not as m erely a financial
automation technology, but rather an adaptive financial decision -support
mechanism in this study. Results showed the inclusive value of GenAI is based on
designing personalised intelligence, customer -centric interaction and responsible
AI governance together.

1. Introduction
Artificial Intelligence (AI), machine learning, predictive analytics and natural language processing are
all integrated into customer facing systems and decision -support processes by the digitalisation of
financial services which have intimately transformed banking, fintech, lending, investment, insurance
and payment systems. These data-hungry AI applications in Banking, Financial Services and Insurance
(BFSI) have progressed from fraud detection, credit assessment, risk modelling and algorithmic
trading to automated advisory services and customer relationship management [1]. As part of this
evolution, banks and other financial institutions are heading well beyond the shores of classic
personalisation (solely via customer record segmentation, demographic dat a and historical product
use) to hyper -personalisation that map transactional behaviour patterns, behavioural signals,
contextual insights and extrinsic financial goals aggregated in near time along digital property into
recommendations or responses at point of service for individual(s). Generative Artificial Intelligence
(GenAI) evolves this capability, by combining data -driven insight with natural -language generation
and conversational interaction to provide a contextual explanation of a complex financial system that
allows for tailored financial guidance; personalised product recommendation; budgeting assistance;
and interactive decision support. Research regarding this new area of study remains highly immature
from a methodological and application-based perspective [2], but numerous studies are showing that
GenAI is an important emerging subfield in finance. Consequently, it is a minor shift from simply back-
end AI as a prediction to GenAI -enabled hyper -personalisation acting as the intelligent and
conversational self-learned interfaces between firms providing financial services and consumers.
The hyper-personalisation which is made possible by GenAI is something which has a value in terms
of doing operations better than today. AI -augmented customer experience is thus a function of the
interplay of personalisation, convenience, service quality a nd customer trust suggesting that clearly
intelligent systems is not merely about tech efficacy in driving outcome [3]. In this way, for instance,
in financial services customers' circumstances and needs can be scanned and analysed by GenAI
systems that th en use that analysis to provide real -time context-specific interactions that mitigate
information overload, and improve the relevance of investment or terms recommendations. These
potential features might improve engagement by promoting continual interacti on and use of the
service, as well as creating stronger relationships with digital finance platforms. Conversational AI
may also support financial inclusion by breaking down financial jargon, giving 'just -in-time' literacy
guidance, guiding consumers throu gh complicated products and delivering personalised assistance
to underserved groups in the existing advisory model. Some recent studies have suggested that AI
could address some of the limitations of existing technologies for financial inclusion, especially when
access alone does not translate into real engagement in formal financial markets [4]. But that comes
with a serious hurdle. You can get into privacy issues from data -heavy personalisation, replicate
existing financial inequalities through algorithm ic bias, destroy trust with non -transparent AI -
generated recommendations and establish paths for new exclusions in the form of divergent digital
literacy and infrastructure. Afterall, studies have begun to indicate that even if AI is applied equally,
its effect on financial inclusion depends more on good governance and inclusive system design [5].

customer intelligence layer (GenAI hyper -personalisation as a backbone of this), one driving the
customer experience, and others leading to engagement, financial inclusion, and responsible -AI

6. Conclusion
This study offered a theoretical contribution in the form of an empirical GenAI -enabled hyper -
personalisation decision framework that elucidates how smart personalisation capabilities are
frequently translated and delivered into customer experience, custom er engagement and greater
financial inclusion across the digital finance service landscape. The findings highlight that GenAI -
enabled hyper-personalization vastly enhances customer experience, thereby promoting customer
engagement and helping to arise stat istical general financial inclusion, while the pronounced
sequential mediation pathway emphasizes that technological capacity does not bestow inclusive
woven-financial gains freely with possession, rather its value is uncovered through an accumulative
experiential and behavioral mechanism. The moderation results support our first argument that
responsible-AI conditions include necessary conditions for hyper -personalised financial systems to
operate, in which perceived trust and explainability of algorithms (and algorithmic fairness) are
reinforcing influences on the personalisation -experience link and privacy concerns are undermining
influences. Such out-of-sample relevance is confirmed by the predictive assessment and the superior
performance of ensemble ma chine-learning models, especially Gradient Boosting and Random
Forest, suggests complex nonlinearities in customer outcomes and inclusion. The study further
extends the traditional ceiling underlying technology adoption and chatbot -acceptance model by
positioning GenAI as an adaptive dualism -driven financial decision -support mechanism while
ordinally integrating GenAI, hyper-personalisation, customer experience (CX), engagement, financial
inclusion under one adopted framework of decision architecture with acknowledged commercial and
societal repercussions along with responsible AI governance and predictive analytics aspects.
