Abstract
This study examines whether social media marketing, social -network engagement, and customer
value creation are associated with perceived marketing -organizational outcomes in a
multichannel environment. The manuscript was restructured to align its claims with the
variables and statistical output contained in the original dataset. A cross-sectional questionnaire
survey produced 120 usable responses, which were analyzed using descriptive statistics, Pearson
correlations, and multiple linear regression. The combined regression model was not
statistically significant, F(3, 116) = 0.458, p = .712, and explained 1.2% of the variance in the
outcome (R² = .012; adjusted R² = -.014). None of the individual predictors reached statistical
significance: social media marketing (B = .103, p = .260), social networks (B = .019, p = .846),
and customer value creation (B = .033, p = .712). Zero -order correlations with the outcome
were also weak and non -significant. These findings do not establish that digital channels are
unimportant; instead, they suggest that the measures, sample, context, or model used in this
exploratory study did not capture a reliable relationship. The study therefore cautions against
treating platform activity as evidence of organizational impact without validated constructs,
channel-integration measures, and outcome indicators. Practical recommendations emphasize
coordinated channel strategy, customer -journey measurement, and outcome -based evaluation.
Because the source file does not document the sampling frame, scale items, reliability statistics,
location, or ethics procedures sufficiently, the findings should be interpreted as preliminary until
those details are verified.

Introduction
Marketing communication now operates across interconnected physical, broadcast, web, mobile,
and social touchpoints. Customers may discover a brand through traditional advertising, compare
alternatives through search and social media, consult reviews, communicate with other users, and
complete a transaction through a different channel. This movement has shifted managerial
attention from the performance of isolated media to the coordination of the entire customer
journey. Multichannel marketing denotes the use of multiple channels, whereas an omnichannel
orientation places stronger emphasis on integration, continuity, and a coherent customer
experience across those channels (Rahman et al., 2022; Mansurali et al., 2024).
Digital and social media have expanded the speed, reach, interactivity, and measurability of
marketing communication. They allow organizations to distribute content, respond to customers,
support peer-to-peer interaction, and observe behavioral signals at comparatively low marginal
cost. At the same time, digital visibility does not automatically translate into customer value or
organizational performance. Likes, followers, comments, and traffic are intermediate indicators
whose business meaning depends on the platform, content, customer segment, and connection to
downstream outcomes. Recent work shows that engagement can carry over within a platform and
spill over across platforms, reinforcing the need to evaluate a coordinated portfolio rather than a
single social channel (Unnava & Aravindakshan, 2021).
Traditional media also remain relevant. Television, radio, print, outdoor advertising, and
personal contact can provide reach, legitimacy, repeated exposure, and access to audiences who
do not engage consistently through digital platforms. The practical challenge is therefore not a
simple choice between traditional and new media. It is the design of an integrated channel
system in which each touchpoint performs an appropriate role and produces a consistent  brand
experience. Contemporary research similarly treats channel integration, information consistency,
fulfilment, personalization, and customer experience as linked dimensions of multichannel
effectiveness (Balbín Buckley & Marquina Feldman, 2024).
The original study sought to assess traditional and new media but empirically measured three
digital-era constructs: social media marketing, social networks, and customer value creation.
Traditional-media exposure and channel -integration quality were not included in the displayed
statistical model. The present revision therefore narrows the empirical claim to the variables that
were actually analyzed . It asks whether these three predictors are associated with a reported
marketing-organizational outcome and interprets the results conservatively.
Research Problem and Gap
Organizations often adopt multiple media without a clear framework for connecting channel
activity to customer value and organizational outcomes. The literature increasingly recognizes
seamless customer experience and integrated touchpoints, but the evidence remains context -
dependent, and many studies rely on measures that cannot distinguish visibility from value

Aim, Objectives, and Research Questions
The revised aim is to examine the relationship of social media marketing, social -network
engagement, and customer value creation with perceived marketing-organizational outcomes in a
multichannel environment.
1. To explain the complementary roles of traditional and digital media in multichannel
marketing.
2. To test whether social media marketing predicts the reported marketing -organizational
outcome.
3. To test whether social -network engagement predicts the reported marketing -
organizational outcome.
4. To test whether customer value creation predicts the reported marketing -organizational
outcome.
5. To develop evidence -based recommendations for channel coordination and future
measurement.
The central research question is: To what extent do social media marketing, social networks, and
customer value creation explain variation in perceived marketing-organizational outcomes?

Literature Review
From Multichannel Presence to Channel Integration
Early multichannel strategies concentrated on adding channels and distributing promotional
resources among them. Current perspectives focus more strongly on how those channels work
together. A customer may experience an organization as one system even when the organization
manages its channels through separate departments, databases, and performance indicators.
Fragmentation can therefore create inconsistent pricing, messages, service, and fulfilment. In
contrast, integration can reduce friction and strengthen cognitive and affective evaluations of the
customer journey (Balbín Buckley & Marquina Feldman, 2024).

Rahman et al. (2022) conceptualize perceived omnichannel customer experience as customers'
evaluations of seamless experiences as they move across a retailer's channels. Their work
highlights that experience is broader than simple channel availability. It includes the customer's
perception of consistency, freedom of channel choice, synchronization, and continuity.
Mansurali et al. (2024), in a systematic review, similarly organize omnichannel research around
customer experience, strategy, and organizational benefits. These perspectives imply that
channel effectiveness should be assessed at the journey and outcome levels rather than through
isolated exposure or activity counts.
Social Media Marketing in a Cross-Platform System
Social media marketing allows firms to communicate, advertise, provide service, and stimulate
customer participation. Its value arises partly from interactivity: audiences can react, share,
create content, and communicate with other users. However, the same interactivity gives
customers greater influence over brand meaning and exposes organizations to negative word of
mouth, privacy concerns, and rapidly changing platform norms (Dwivedi et al., 2021).
Platform effects are not uniform. Unnava and Aravindakshan (2021) show that brand posts can
create direct engagement, carryover over time, and spillovers across multiple social -media
platforms. Their findings challenge the assumption that the visible response on the posting
platform captures the full value of an activity. For managers, this means that a low immediate
reaction on one platform may coexist with indirect effects elsewhere, but those effects require
suitable longitudinal and cross -platform measurement. A one -time perception survey cannot
fully capture these dynamics.
Social Networks and Customer Value Creation
Social networks are not merely distribution channels; they are structures through which
customers exchange information, opinions, recommendations, and support. Network
participation can amplify organizational messages, but it can also generate meanings that the
organization does not control. The managerial value of a network therefore depends on the
quality and relevance of interaction, not simply the number of connections.
Customer value creation refers to the benefits customers perceive or help create through
interactions with an organization and other customers. In multichannel settings, value can
emerge from convenience, accessible information, personalization, service continuity, social
support, and reduced effort. Yet value is not identical to engagement. A customer may view or
react to content without experiencing meaningful benefit, changing behavior, or improving the
firm's outcomes. Theoretical links between participation, value, and performance consequently
require explicit constructs and validated measures.

Research Methodology
Research Design
The study used a quantitative, cross -sectional survey design. This classification replaces the
source manuscript's inconsistent description of the project as qualitative and inductive. A
structured questionnaire was used to obtain respondents' perceptions of social media marketing,
social networks, customer value creation, and a variable labelled 'Marketing Organisation' in the
statistical output. Because the exact wording and validation of the outcome scale are not
supplied, this revision refers to it cautiously as the marketing-organizational outcome.
Population, Sampling, and Participants
The source manuscript reports non -probability sampling and 120 completed questionnaires. It
does not identify the sampling frame, recruitment setting, participant eligibility criteria, response
rate, industry, country of data collection, or fieldwork dates. These details must be confirmed
before journal submission because they determine the population to which the findings may
reasonably be transferred.

Data Analysis
The displayed output was generated in SPSS and includes descriptive statistics, Pearson
correlations, and standard multiple linear regression. The regression simultaneously entered all
three predictors. Statistical significance was evaluated at alpha = .05. This revision distinguishes
correlation coefficients (r), unstandardized regression coefficients (B), and significance
probabilities (p), which were conflated in the source narrative.
Ethical and Data-Management Information
No ethics approval number, consent procedure, confidentiality protocol, data -storage
arrangement, or permission statement appears in the source manuscript. These matters cannot be
reconstructed from the statistical tables and must be supplied by the authors. Until verified, the
manuscript should not claim institutional ethical approval or a specific consent process.

Discussion and Conclusion
Discussion
The study expected social media marketing, social networks, and customer value creation to
predict a marketing -organizational outcome, but the displayed evidence  did not support those
expectations. All relationships were weak, and the combined model explained little variance.
This result should not be converted into a claim that social or digital media have no role in
multichannel marketing. It indicates only that the variables, as operationalized in this sample, did
not provide reliable statistical prediction of the reported outcome.
One possible explanation is measurement misalignment. The theoretical discussion concerns
integrated traditional and digital media, customer journeys, and channel coordination, whereas
the model contains no measure of traditional-media exposure, cross-channel consistency, channel
integration quality, or actual customer behavior. Contemporary omnichannel research treats
integration and seamless experience as multidimensional constructs (Rahman et al., 2022; Balbín
Buckley & Marquina Feldman, 2024). A broad or ambiguous outcome labelled 'Marketing
Organisation' may not be sufficiently proximal to platform activity to show an association.
A second explanation concerns the distinction between activity and impact. Social media metrics
often capture immediate engagement rather than sales, retention, loyalty, service efficiency, or
long-term customer value. Unnava and Aravindakshan (2021) further demonstrate that social -
platform effects can be direct, carried over through time, or spilled over to another platform.
Cross-sectional perceptions may miss these dynamic effects. The non -significant findings
therefore reinforce the need to specify the causal sequence and match each construct to an
appropriate time horizon and level of analysis.

A third explanation is limited statistical and contextual information. Non -probability sampling,
an undocumented sampling frame, and missing reliability and validity evidence restrict
confidence in the estimates. The gender inconsistency also signals the need to reconcile the raw
data and manuscript. The weak predictor intercorrelations do not indicate an obvious
multicollinearity problem, but a rigorous reanalysis should calculate variance inflation factors,
residual diagnostics, reliability, descriptive scale statistics, missing -data patterns, and confidence
intervals.
From a managerial perspective, the results caution against allocating budgets on the assumption
that social activity alone generates organizational benefit. Organizations should map the
customer journey, define the intended role of each traditional and digital channel, and connect
intermediate metrics to outcomes. Channel -level indicators may include reach, attention,
engagement, and service response, while outcome indicators may include conversion, retention,
satisfaction, customer lifetime value, and cost -to-serve. A coordinated dashboard should show
how these levels relate rather than reporting them as interchangeable.
Conclusion
This revised study provides a more accurate account of the evidence contained in the original
manuscript. Social media marketing, social -network engagement, and customer value creation
did not significantly predict the reported marketing -organizational outcome in the sample of 120
respondents. The appropriate conclusion is not that digital media are ineffective, but that the
present measures and design do not demonstrate the proposed relationships. Multichannel
effectiveness requires conceptual alignment, validated measurement, integrated customer -
journey data, and transparent statistical interpretation. Correcting the hypothesis decisions and
narrowing the empirical claims turns the non -significant findings into a useful methodological
and managerial lesson: organizations and researchers must distinguish channel activity from
customer value and organizational impact.
Practical Recommendations
• Define the strategic function of every channel —awareness, evaluation, conversion,
service, retention, or advocacy—before comparing its performance.
• Measure integration quality, including consistency of information, pricing, service,
fulfilment, and customer records across channels.
• Link platform engagement to downstream indicators instead of treating likes, followers,
or traffic as final evidence of organizational impact.
• Use validated multi -item scales and report reliability, validity, missing -data treatment,
and complete regression diagnostics.
• Combine cross -sectional surveys with behavioral, longitudinal, experimental, or
campaign-level data to capture direct, carryover, and spillover effects.

Limitations and Future Research
The study is limited by non -probability sampling, incomplete reporting of the population and
recruitment process, an unresolved demographic discrepancy, and missing documentation for the
questionnaire scales. Its cross -sectional design does not establish causal direction. The outcome
variable is insufficiently defined, and the model does not directly measure traditional media or
channel integration even though those concepts motivated the original paper. The absence of
reliability, validity, and diagnostic statistics further limits inference.
Future research should use a clearly defined population and validated constructs for traditional -
media exposure, digital -media engagement, integration quality, customer experience, value
creation, and organizational performance. A larger probability -based or carefully stratified
sample would improve generalizability. Longitudinal campaign data could examine whether
engagement carries over or spills across channels, while mediation and moderation models could
test whether customer experience or value creation links channel integration with loyalty and
performance. Qualitative interviews could also clarify how customers interpret consistency and
value across physical and digital touchpoints.
