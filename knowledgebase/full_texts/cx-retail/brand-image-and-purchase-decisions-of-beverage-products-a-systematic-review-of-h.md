Abstract. Consumers in the beverage market face increasingly diverse alternatives differentiated by health
benefits, quality, price, packaging, promotion, reputation, and brand identity. Brand image is frequently employed
to explain purchase decisions, although empirical findings across beverage categories remain heterogeneous.
This study systematically synthesizes the relationship between brand image and purchase decisions, examines the
mechanisms and complementary variables involved, and identifies factors explaining variations across studies. A
Systematic Literature Review with narrative-thematic synthesis was conducted using 27 verified empirical studies
covering herbal beverages, functional drinks, milk, yogurt, juice, collagen drinks, isotonic beverages, ready -to-
drink tea, and bottled drinking water. Most studies report a positive and significant association between brand
image and purchase decisions. However, the direct relationship is not universal. Several studies indicate that
brand image operates through brand trust or purchase intention, while others find an insignificant direct effect
when price perception or other marketing variables are incorporated into the model. Product quality, price,
promotion, brand trust, customer experience, reference groups, halal labeling, and digital communication
frequently interact with brand image. Brand image therefore functions as an importan t informational and risk -
reducing cue, but it is not the sole determinant of beverage purchase decisions .

Keywords: Brand Image; Brand Trust; Functional Beverages; Purchase Decisions; Systematic Literature Review.

1. INTRODUCTION
The beverage industry develops through increasingly broad product diversification.
Consumers are no longer faced only with drinking water, milk, or tea, but with herbal drinks,
fermented milk, yogurt drinks, collagen drinks, cold-pressed juices, isotonic drinks, functional
drinks, and ready -to-drink products carrying specific claims regarding health and lifestyle
(Yıldırım et al., 2024). The increase in alternatives makes the product selection process
increasingly complex because consumers need to compare benefits, quality, price, composition,
safety, packaging, producer reputation, and previous experience before making a choice
(Alalwan, 2025). Consumer behavior studies show that the higher the level of product
diversification, the greater the role of psychological factors such as brand image in the decision-
making process (Zhou, 2025).
Brand image becomes relevant in such situations because consumers are not always able
to evaluate all product attributes directly prior to purchase (Zou, 2023). Keller (1993) explains
brand image as perceptions regarding a brand reflected in associations stored in consumer
memory. Such associations can be formed through experience, marketing communications,
information from other parties, perceived quality, symbols, and compan y reputation (Shaikh,
2024). A strong image can make it easier for consumers to recognize and differentiate products

selecting products within competitive markets (Upadhyay, 2016).
In beverage products, brand reputation can possess greater significance when consumers
consider aspects of safety, content, and benefits. Rakhmawati and Sudarwanto (2021) find that

Putri and Damayanti (2026) even find brand image to be a relatively dominant predictor

A positive pattern is also found in fermented and milk products. Abidin (2023)
demonstrates that product quality, brand image, and price possess positive relationships with
purchase decisions for Cimory Yogurt Drink. Sulastri and Puspasari (2022) find that brand
image and reference groups exert positive effects on Bear Brand purchase decisions, although
reference groups become the more dominant factor. Maulidah and Ekaningsih (2026) show that
brand image and halal labels possess important contributions in purchasing Yakult within
Islamic boarding school environments. Such findings reveal that brand image can operate
alongside quality guarantees, social norms, and trust attributes.
Bottled drinking water products constitute the most widely researched group. Ramadani
and Sudrajat (2022) , Handayani et al. (2024), Mashlihah and Hapsari (2025), Wulandari and
Kurniawan (2025), Hutami et al. (2025), as well as Suryaningsih and Dwianto (2026) obtain
positive relationships between brand image and purchase decisions. The multitude of studies in
this category indicates that brands possess a substantial differentiation function when basic
product attributes are relatively homogeneous.
Although the majority of findings indicate positive relationships, differing results exist.
Krisnandiva et al. (2024) find that the brand image of Larutan Penyegar Cap Badak possesses
no significant direct relationship with purchase decisions. Brand image instead relates to
increases in brand trust, whereas brand trust relates to purchase decisions. Wijaya and Anni sa
(2020) obtain a similar pattern for packaged herbal medicine, wherein brand image does not
directly drive purchase decisions but operates through brand trust.

when promotion is incorporated into the model. Kerans and Dewi (2026) find the direction of
the relationship between brand image and Le Minerale purchase decisions to be positive yet

The literature also reveals differences between purchase decisions and constructs
preceding them. Simbolon et al. (2024) show that brand image positively relates to purchase
intention which subsequently drives purchase decisions. Results like this must not be equated
with studies testing direct relationships. Purchase intention represents readiness or inclination
prior to transactions, whereas purchase decisions indicate choice stages closer to consum er
actions.
Systematic reviews are necessary to assess finding patterns cross-product-wise rather than
merely stating that the better the brand image, the higher the purchase decision. Synthesis is
required to ascertain when brand image possesses direct re lationships, when its influence
operates through trust or intention, and what conditions make price, promotion, or reference
groups more decisive. This research is formulated through three questions:
RQ1: What is the pattern of the relationship between brand image and purchase decisions across
various beverage product categories?
RQ2: What mechanisms and variables explain the relationship between brand image and
beverage product purchase decisions?
RQ3: What factors explain the variations in research findin gs and what gaps still exist in the
literature?

2. RESEARCH METHOD
The research employs a Systematic Literature Review approach with narrative -thematic
synthesis. SLR is used to identify, select, evaluate, and integrate the results of empirical
research regarding brand image and beverage product purchase decisions. The arrangement of
the review refers to the literature review principles as a research method put forward by Snyder

(2019) as well as the systematic review framework in management rese arch from Tranfield et

principles (Page et al., 2021).
The search was conducted through Google Scholar, Garuda, DOI, original journal
websites, and publisher portals. Ke ywords include "brand image", "citra merek", "purchase
decision", "keputusan pembelian", "health drink", "functional beverage", "herbal drink",
"yogurt drink", "milk", "juice", "isotonic drink", "bottled water", "ready to drink", as well as
product names found during the search process. Priority is given to articles from 2021 –2026.
Two older studies, namely Atidira et al. (2019) and Wijaya and Annisa (2020), were retained
because they provide important evidence regarding nonsignificant results and brand tru st
mediation relevant to the synthesis.
Inclusion criteria encompass empirical research on beverage products; brand image
placed as a construct tested against purchase decisions directly or through mediation
mechanisms; methods and results traceable; and b ibliographic identities verifiable through
journal pages, DOIs, or official academic indices. Conceptual articles and literature reviews are
not counted as empirical studies. Research measuring only satisfaction, preference, brand
awareness, or purchase in tention without pathways toward purchase decisions is not included
in the main corpus. The systematic principle in SLR emphasizes that only empirical research
with clear outcome variables can be utilized to produce valid synthesis (Nyoni et al., 2025).
Following audits and additional searches, the final corpus consists of 27 unique empirical
studies. Additional research was selected because it possesses purchase decision outcomes and
verifiable metadata. Data were extracted according to author, year, produc t category, sample
size, analytical method, brand image position, companion variables, and direction of findings.
Consistent data extraction methods constitute an important prerequisite so that synthesis results
can be compared reliably across studies (Choudhary & Sahu, 2023).
Synthesis was conducted by comparing significant and nonsignificant findings, then
grouping patterns into direct relationships, mediation pathways, product factors, economic
factors, consumer experience, social influences, and marketi ng communications. Research
utilizing regression and cross-sectional surveys is not treated as absolute causal evidence. The
term "influences" is retained when citing original article terminology, whereas synthesis
interpretations utilize the terms "relate s", "serves as a predictor", or "pertains" when research
designs fail to support strong causal inferences. Methodological literature asserts that
interpretive terminology differences are required to maintain scientific accuracy between
statistical relationships and causal evidence (Thapa et al., 2020).

3. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
The audit results yield 27 empirical studies with quite diverse characteristics. The
majority employ quantitative approaches with linear regression, while several studies utilize
SEM-PLS to test indirect relationships through brand trust or purchase intent ion. The studied
products encompass Herbalife, Re.juve, Bear Brand, Le Minerale, Cap Badak, Cimory Yogurt
Drink, Tolak Angin, Pocari Sweat, Tahesta and Bragg Apple Cider Vinegar, Noera Collagen
Drink, Buahvita, AQUA, Boost Juice, Yakult, Sunfresh, and a number of herbal drinks.
In general, brand image demonstrates a positive relationship with purchase decisions in
the majority of studies. Such results still need to be read alongside contribution magnitudes and
companion variables. Statistical significance d oes not always indicate that brand image
constitutes the most dominant factor. In several studies, price, reference groups, experience, or
trust possess equal or greater positions.
Nonsignificant findings also provide important information. Studies on Cap Badak,
packaged herbal medicine, Frestea, and one of the Le Minerale studies show that brand image
does not always operate directly. The following table maps out all studies used as the basis for

different categories, ranging from herbal drinks, fermented products, milk,  juices, isotonic
drinks, collagen drinks, to bottled drinking water. Cross -category patterns indicate that brand
image possesses a general function in helping consumers recognize and evaluate alternatives.
In herbal drinks, brand image appears to relate t o consumers' need to reduce uncertainty
regarding products. Rakhmawati and Sudarwanto (2021) obtain positive relationships for
Herbalife, Lorinda and Amron (2023) for Tolak Angin, Mardiana et al. (2026) for Si Rempah,

A similar pattern is found in fermented products. Abidin (2023) shows that brand image

Bottled drinking water produces the largest collection of evidence. Ramadani and
Sudrajat (2022), Handayani et al. (2024), Mashlihah and Hapsari (2025), Wulandari and

products. When basic benefits are relatively homogeneous, brands can become differentiation
tools that make it easier for consumers to determine choices.
The magnitude of brand image contribution still varies. Mashlihah and Hapsari (2025)
find that around 11.1% of Le Minerale purchase decision variation is explained by brand image.

These results indicate that statistical significance does not mean brand ima ge is always the
primary factor.

yet nonsignificant direction, whereas price perception exerts a significant effect. Consumers
can possess good brand perceptions yet still choose based on price considerations. Brand image
functions within a configuration of decision factors, rather than replacing entire economic
evaluations.

Mechanisms Explaining the Relationship Between Brand Image and Purchase Decisions
Brand trust constitutes the clearest mechanism within the corpus. Wijaya and Annisa
(2020) find that brand image possesses no direct significant relationship with purchase
decisions for packaged herbal medicine, yet operates through the full mediation of brand trust.
Krisnandiva et al. (2024) find an almost identical pattern for Larutan Penyegar Cap Badak.
Brand image enhances brand trust, whereupon brand trust relates positi vely to purchase
decisions.
Such findings indicate that consumers can build positive images without immediately
executing transactions. Image functions as a source of associations and reputation, whereas trust
converts such reputation into the conviction t hat a product can deliver expected quality and
benefits. This mechanism holds special significance in herbal and health drinks because
consumers face uncertainty regarding product safety and efficacy.
Suryaningsih and Dwianto (2026) also find brand image a nd brand trust to relate
positively to Le Minerale purchase decisions. Evidence from the bottled drinking water
category indicates that trust is not only relevant for herbal products. Producer reliability, safety
perceptions, quality consistency, and compa ny reputation can serve as the foundation of
decisions for daily consumption products.
Purchase intention serves as another pathway bridging image and decisions. Simbolon et
al. (2024) show that Le Minerale brand image enhances purchase intention, whereupo n
purchase intention drives purchase decisions. This pattern aligns with consumer behavior stages
that distinguish positive evaluations and purchase inclinations from the act of selecting a
product actually.
Brand image also relates to perceived quality. H asanah and Khoiri (2024) find brand
image and product quality to be equally significant for Noera Collagen Drink. Abidin (2023),
Afna and Khoiri (2023), Hardianti et al. (2024), Hutami et al. (2025), as well as Putri and
Damayanti (2026) present quality within models alongside brand image. Results vary, signaling
that actual quality or perceived quality remains a construct distinct from brand reputation.

Customer experience provides a mechanism based on direct usage. Hasanah and Khoiri
(2024) find customer experience to possess a slightly higher contribution than brand image for

solely by marketing messages. Real experiences regarding taste, benefits, service, packaging,
and consistency can strengthen or correct the image previously held by consumers.
Reference groups provide a social dimension. Rakhmawati and Sudarwanto (2021) find
reference groups alongside brand image to explain Herbalife purchase decisions. Sulastri and
Puspasari (2022) even find reference groups to be more dominant for Bear Brand. In products
possessing health associations, recommendations from family, friends, and communities can
function as validation toward brand reputation.
Marketing communication constitutes a source of image formation simult aneously with
decision stimulation. Widjaya and Indudewi (2023), Afna and Khoiri (2023), Krisnandiva et al.
(2024), Ilmi and Kusuma (2024), as well as Mardiana et al. (2026) demonstrate that promotion,
social media marketing, and green marketing operate si de by side with brand image. Brand
image is not fixed, but rather continually shaped through interactions between corporate
communications and consumer experiences.
Halal labels also indicate that brand reputation may require certain external guarantees.
Maulidah and Ekaningsih (2026) find halal labels and brand image to be significant toward
Yakult purchase decisions among female Islamic boarding school students. Positive images
gain additional support when product attributes align with consumer values.
Brand image relates to decisions through brand trust, purchase intention, perceived
quality, experience, and perceived risk reduction. Price, promotion, reference groups, halal
labels, and digital communications determine whether brand associations are strong enough to
be translated into purchase choices.

water, brands function more as differentiators among products with nearly identical basic

differentiation in the health beverage category acts as a psychological mechanism strengthening
consumer value perception (Murugan et al., 2025).
Respondent characteristics also differ. Studies involve tee nagers, college students,
housewives, female Islamic boarding school students, social media users, and general
consumers. Differences in age, purchasing power, social environment, experience, and values
can alter the weight of each attribute. The findings of Maulidah and Ekaningsih (2026), for
instance, indicate that religiosity makes halal labels and brand image more relevant than health
awareness. Consumer behavior research affirms that demographic variables and personal values
frequently serve as important moderators in the relationship between brand image and purchase
decisions (Joseph, 2015).
Sample sizes also vary widely. Several studies employ fewer than 100 respondents,
whereas Afna and Khoiri (2023) utilize 385 respondents. This difference affects e stimation
precision and research capability in detecting statistical relationships. Findings from small
samples need to be read with greater caution. Quantitative methodology emphasizes that the
larger the sample size, the higher the reliability of researc h result generalizability toward the
target population (Sharma & Bhattarai, 2024).
The majority of research employs cross-sectional designs. Such designs allow the testing
of statistical relationships, but are less robust in ensuring causal sequence. Consu mers who
already possess positive experiences can provide higher brand image scores post-purchase. The
relationship between brand image and decisions can be bidirectional in measurements
conducted post-consumption. Methodology reviews assert that longitudi nal designs are more
appropriate for identifying causal directions compared to cross -sectional designs (Critcher &
Siegel, 2022).
Research models are also non-uniform. Linear regression is utilized in most studies, while
Krisnandiva et al. (2024) and Simbo lon et al. (2024) use SEM -PLS to differentiate direct and
indirect effects. Studies featuring mediation models provide clearer mechanism depictions than
simple regression. Quantitative literature shows that SEM-PLS possesses advantages in testing
latent variables and complex relationships compared to traditional linear regression (Sarstedt &
Liu, 2023).
The literature is also dominated by research on single brands and single regions. Le
Minerale receives very large attention compared to other categories. Su ch concentration can
generate the impression that findings apply to all beverage products, whereas mineral water

heterogeneity and consumer behavior differences across segments (Krefeld -Schwalb &

The subsequent gap lies in brand image measurement which is frequently treated as an
aggregate score. Future research can separate favorability, strength, and uniqueness from brand
associations, or distinguish functional, symbolic, health, and environmental images. Such an
approach will clarify which image dimensions truly relate to decisions. Strategic marketing
reviews indicate that separating brand image dimensions enables the identification of specific
attributes most influencing consumer behavior (Plumeyer et al., 2019).
Experimental designs remain extremely limited. Experiments can test changes in brand
names, health i nformation, certifications, packaging, or communication to ascertain whether
image changes yield decision differences. Longitudinal studies are also required to observe
image development following consumption experiences and repeat purchases. Experimental
methods in consumer research are proven more effective for testing causal relationships
compared to observational surveys (Ashursky, 2023).
Result heterogeneity is explained by beverage types, consumer characters, price levels,
companion variables, marketing channels, sample sizes, and analytical methods. The clearest
research gaps are the dominance of single-brand surveys, the scarcity of longitudinal tests and
experiments, and the limited number of direct comparisons across beverage types. Meta -
analysis s hows that research result differences frequently originate from study design
differences and sample characteristics utilized (Mennander, 2025).

4. CONCLUSION AND SUGGESTION
Systematic review of the 27 empirical studies indicates that brand image is generally
positively related to beverage product purchase decisions. Such patterns are found in herbal,
functional beverages, milk, yogurt, cold-pressed juices, collagen drinks, isotonic drinks, ready-
to-drink tea, as well as bottled drinking water. Such relationships are not universal. Brand image
can possess direct influence, operate through trust or purchase intention, or become
nonsignificant when other variables like price and promotion are more dominant. Findings from
Cap Badak, packaged herbal medicine, Frestea, and one of the Le Minerale studies demonstrate
the importance of maintaining contradictory evidence within synthesis. Brand trust constitutes
the most consistent mechanism explaining how brand image is translated into decisions.

digital communication serve as important factors operating alongside brand image.
The main conclusion of this review is that brand image constitutes an important source
of information and differentiation, yet is not a single determinant of purchase decisions. Its
effectiveness depends on product contexts, consumers, as well as other attributes utilized in
evaluation processes.
Theoretically, simple models of brand image towa rd purchase decisions need to be
developed into models involving brand trust, purchase intention, perceived quality, perceived
risk, and customer experience. Nonsignificant results in several studies indicate that
intermediary mechanisms possess important roles. For beverage companies, image building
should not be limited to visual communication and popularity. Reputation needs to be supported
by consistent quality, reliable information, positive usage experience, prices matching
perceived value, and commun ication relevant to segment characteristics. For beverages
carrying health benefits, brand reputation needs to be supported by product information,
relevant certifications, and accountable claims. Consumers can utilize brands to reduce
uncertainty, but final decisions remain influenced by assessments of benefits and risks.
Subsequent research needs to compare multiple brands within a single design so that the
influence of brand characteristics can be separated from sample characteristics. Cross-category
studies are also required to ascertain whether the role of image is stronger in herbal and
functional beverages than in routine consumption products. Longitudinal models and
experiments need to be developed to strengthen causal inferences. Brand trust, percei ved
healthiness, perceived risk, customer experience, e-WOM, and price fairness deserve testing as
mediators or moderators. Purchase decision measurements also need to be clearly distinguished
from purchase intention, preference, and satisfaction. Such sep aration will enhance synthesis
consistency and reduce tendencies to conclude different constructs as the same phenomenon.
