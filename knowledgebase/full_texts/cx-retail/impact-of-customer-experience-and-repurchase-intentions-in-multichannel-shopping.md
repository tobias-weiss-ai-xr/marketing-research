Abstract
This research examines the impact of customer’s past experience on repurchase intentions
in multichannel shopping with the moderating role of sales promotion. Additionally, it examines
the mediating roles of online shopping behavior and physical store shopping behavior.
The study adopts a deductive approach with a cross-sectional design, using self-administered
questionnaires distributed online and in person. Measurement scales from prior studies assessed
customer experiences, shopping behaviors, and repurchase intentions, with reliability coefficients
between 0.728 and 0.972. Convenience sampling yielded 300 responses, analyzed through
SPSS 21.0 for reliability and demographic profiling. Mixed methods enhanced response rates
and provided a comprehensive view of online and offline customer experiences.
All the hypotheses of this study are accepted. The results of Smart-PLS indicate that online
shopping behavior and physical store shopping behavior play key roles in mediating the connection
between customer past experience and repurchase intentions. The influence of these mediators
is amplified when sales promotions are applied.
Retailers can improve customer experience by providing convenience and usefulness in their
multichannel offerings. Marketers can leverage sales promotion to increase customer loyalty and
encourage repurchase intentions. This study will add to the existing knowledge in the field of
multichannel shopping and offer practical insights for marketers and retailers.
This research aims to bridge the existing knowledge gap by examining the relationship
between customer’s past experience, repurchase intention, and sales promotion in the context
of multichannel shopping in Pakistan. By investigating the moderating role of sales promotion,

1. Introduction
As the retail sector continues to expand globally and online channel businesses
pose a challenge to physical channel businesses, the mode of shopping becomes a
fundamental issue for customers whenever they intend to make a purchase. Both
online and physical stores are available to fulfill their buying interests (Sarkar &
Das, 2017). This study aims to examine the impact of customer’s past experience on
online and physical store shopping behavior, which ultimately results in repurchase
intentions. The relational characteristics of brick-and-mortar stores offer benefits
that influence customers’ choices between online and offline purchasing (Wang et
al., 2023). Customer behavior is crucial for retail businesses operating in competi -
tive markets (Panjaitan &Yuliati, 2016). For many businesses, a key strategic goal is
to improve customer experience. Customer experience is defined as the interaction
between customers and their environment and their connection with the service
provider (Gentile et al., 2007). Customer experience is influenced by the customer’s
location, time, and context (KIM & Myung-Seong, 2020). It can be described as the
interactions that customers have with a product, staff members, the physical store,
and the purchasing process (Pei et al., 2020). These findings emphasize the complex
relationship between customer experience and shopping behavior in today’s highly
competitive retail landscape. As consumers evaluate the ease of online shopping
alongside the personal and sensory benefits offered by physical stores, prioritizing and
improving customer experience becomes essential for businesses. Striking this balance
is expected to shape future customer preferences and determine the effectiveness of
multichannel retail strategies.
In order to meet customer demand, the internet channel today has greater com-
petitive advantages than the offline channel. The advantages of using the internet
for shopping are that it is more convenient, useful, easy to use, and can even trace
which specific customer bought what by using their identification while making an
online purchase (Katawetawaraks & Wang, 2011). In recent years, online shopping
has dominated. Despite a decline in total retail sales, online sales have increased.
E-commerce accounts for a modest portion of total retail sales (Kumar et al., 2021).
This demonstrates that physical stores are vital, and businesses must continue to invest
in them (Akram et al., 2021). It becomes very challenging for companies to select the

2. Theoretical Background
The study of customer behavior in online and physical retail contexts has gained
significant attention in academia and industry. This research explores the relationships
between past customer experiences, shopping behaviors, repurchase intentions, and
the moderating role of sales promotions. Grounded in the Stimulus-Organism-Re -
sponse (S-O-R) Theory and the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), the study applies
Mehrabian and Russell’s (1974) framework to examine how stimuli (past experiences)
influence organisms (shopping behaviors) and responses (repurchase intentions), with
sales promotions as external modifiers. The S-O-R theory has been extensively used
to analyze consumer behavior in retail settings. Recent studies, such as Banerjee et al.
(2024), demonstrate its applicability in online retail by linking website experiences to
emotional responses and behavioral outcomes. Similarly, the Technology Acceptance
Model (TAM), introduced by Davis (1989), has been instrumental in examining fac-
tors influencing consumer technology adoption. Venkatesh and Davis (2000) further
refined TAM by integrating additional constructs to provide deeper insights into
behavioral intentions and consumer decision-making. TAM emphasizes the rational
and utilitarian aspects of consumer behavior, while S-O-R addresses the emotional
and psychological dimensions. Combined, these theories form a robust framework
for the study, offering a comprehensive perspective on retail consumer behavior. By
aligning with these established models, the research gains credibility and adopts a
structured approach to explore the relationships among prior customer experience,
online and in-store shopping behaviors, repurchase intentions, and the moderating
impact of sales promotions.

2.1 Customer’s past experience and repurchase intentions
Customer experience is a key marketing element, reflecting how well a company’s
products or services meet consumer expectations (Sari & Padmantyo, 2023). To stay
competitive, businesses focus on understanding consumer needs and fostering strong
relationships. They aim to deliver exceptional experiences that exceed expectations
throughout the entire purchase process (Sulistyo, 2020). The quality of these experi-
ences significantly shapes their overall impact on customers (Kandampully et al., 2018).
Repurchase intention refers to a consumer’s decision to buy a product again based
on their evaluation of a prior purchase and various influencing factors (Wafiyyah et
al., 2021). It reflects a positive response to a company’s product or service when the
experience exceeds expectations, encouraging future purchases. This intention is
driven by consumer loyalty to certain products and a preference for repeat purchases
(Dewi & Ekawati, 2019). Repurchase intention is influenced by various factors, such
as customer experience, trust, and satisfaction. Among these, customer experience is a
key predictor of future consumer behavior, as the quality of the experience during the
purchase and use of a product or service plays a critical role in shaping a customer’s
decision to make repeat purchases (Otivia & Sukaatmadja, 2020). According to a
recent study conducted by Ari Putra and Pramudana (2021), there is a strong positive
correlation between customer’s past experience and repurchase intentions.
Although existing research emphasizes the positive link between customer expe-
rience and repurchase intentions, the majority examines consumer behavior within
single-channel contexts. This study advances the field by investigating this relationship
in a multichannel environment, specifically focusing on the interplay between online
and physical store behaviors, an area that has received limited attention.

intention.
2.2 Customer’s past experience and online shopping
Whenever a consumer interacts with a business, whether formally or informally,
their experience is considered part of the company’s bottom line (Chen et al., 2022).
Another study confirms that providing a positive customer experience requires put-
ting the consumer in the driver’s seat throughout the buying process. The emotional
and intellectual goals of both traditional and digital shopping are captured (Barari
et al., 2020). According to Jaakkola et al. (2015), customer experience refers to how a
customer personally perceives or interprets any interaction, whether direct or indirect,
with various aspects of a service. These aspects may include the service provider, the

2.3 Customer’s past experience and physical store shopping:

shopping malls to take advantage of their more comfortable shopping atmosphere,
expanded product selection, and lower prices (Shi et al., 2019). Consumers are moti-
vated to make in-store purchases by their gender and personality features, including
enjoyment of shopping (Wong et al., 2012). Customers have the ability to authenticate
items in physical stores by touching, feeling, and smelling them (Spangenberg et al.,

2.4 Online Shopping and repurchase intentions
Repurchase intention refers to a customer’s plan to purchase a product or service
again based on their prior experience with its quality and benefits (Ilyas et al., 2020).
It reflects an individual’s evaluation of whether to revisit the same business for a
specific service, considering their current situation and expected future conditions
(Pandiangan et al., 2021). Serving new customers is generally more expensive than
retaining loyal ones (Hadjikhani & LaPlaca, 2013). Repurchase intention reflects a
consumer’s positive perception of an e-retailer, increasing the likelihood of repeat
purchases and demonstrating their willingness to shop from the same online store
(Zhou et al., 2009). However, the absence of face-to-face interaction makes maintaining
online customer loyalty more complex and challenging for vendors (Hsu et al., 2015).
Additionally, it becomes challenging to judge the seller’s credibility (Pavlou et al.,
2007). According to Kim et al., (2012), repurchase intention refers to the inclination
of consumers to engage in online shopping, make future purchases through online
platforms, and recommend online shopping to others because they themselves are
regular users of online shopping. Although the link between online shopping and
repurchase intentions has been well-explored, further research is needed to understand
how past experiences, particularly in terms of customer satisfaction and trust, shape
repeat online shopping behaviors, particularly in varied market settings.

The positivist approach focuses on objective reality, using empirical data and
structured methodologies to test hypotheses and identify generalizable patterns. On
the other hand, interpretivism emphasizes understanding subjective experiences
through qualitative methods to gain insights into specific contexts. Existing research
often overlooks the unique cultural and economic conditions of emerging markets,
primarily focusing on developed ones, and uses small or homogeneous samples
that limit external validity. Furthermore, limited analytical methods fail to capture
complex relationships, such as the mediating impact of shopping behaviors. To over-
come these limitations, this study employs a causal research design, which is suited
for exploring cause-and-effect relationships between customer experience, shopping
behaviors, and repurchase intentions, while also examining the moderating effects
of sales promotions. The self-administered questionnaire was distributed to partici -
pants both in face-to-face interactions and via online. Participants were selected using
a convenience sampling approach within a cross-sectional research design. Prior to
questionnaire distribution, the study’s purpose and potential benefits were explained,
and questionnaires were only provided to willing participants. Initially, the minimum
sample size was determined in accordance with the recommendation of Hair et al.,
(2014), which suggests a minimum of ten responses per questionnaire item.  A total
of 300 completed questionnaires were collected. The study enhanced response rate
and reliability by using mixed data collection methods, briefing participants to build
trust, and ensuring a concise yet comprehensive questionnaire. By combining mea -
surement scales for both online and offline shopping environments, the study adopts
a cohesive approach to understanding customer experiences and their influence on
repurchase intentions. The use of diverse data collection methods and comprehen -

5. Discussion and Implications
This study aimed to explore the impact of customers’ previous experiences (CE)
on their repurchase intentions (RI) in both online and physical store settings. Further-
more, the research investigated the moderating effect of sales promotions on these
shopping behaviors. To achieve this, an integrated model was developed to offer a
comprehensive analysis of the study’s empirical findings. The results in Table 5 reveal
that all hypotheses are supported, consistent with previous research. The findings
demonstrate that customers’ past experiences (CE) significantly influence repurchase
intentions (RI), aligning with the work of Mustikasari et al. (2021). Specifically, the
study highlights a strong positive direct effect of past experiences on repurchase
intentions, as well as on both online and physical store shopping behaviors. Addi -
tionally, a notable positive direct effect of online shopping behavior on repurchase
intentions was observed. In other words, customers who engage in online shopping
are more likely to have repurchase intentions. This indicates that activities in online

actions, significantly and positively affect the likelihood of customers returning for
future purchases. Additionally, there is a significant positive direct effect of physical
store shopping behavior on repurchase intentions. The experiences within physical
stores, encompassing aspects like customer service, product availability, and overall
satisfaction with the brick-and-mortar establishment, hold significant importance
in motivating customers to return for subsequent purchases. Retailers with physical
locations should heed this discovery and allocate resources to elevate the in-store

Interestingly, online and in-store buying behaviors served as mediators between
past consumer experience and repurchase intentions. Customers who had a pleasant
previous experience were more likely to engage in favorable online and physical buying
behaviors, resulting in higher repurchase intentions. These findings underscore the
need for merchants to integrate digital and physical interactions in order to maximize
consumer experience. When a buyer decides to make a purchase, the primary question
that arises is which mode of purchasing they should use to fulfill their needs (Sarkar

experiences on repurchase intentions via physical store shopping behavior. Similarly, a
significant positive indirect effect was found through online shopping behavior. These

The findings of this study indicate that the effect of sales promotions as a mod-

may be more affected by sales promotion than the relationship between customer’s
past experience and online shopping behavior, as indicated by a higher beta value,
which also suggests a stronger moderating impact. This implies that the effectiveness
of sales promotions depends on the setting, necessitating a customized strategy for
both online and physical stores (Ittaqullah et al., 2020).

5.2 Conclusion and limitations
The main objective of this study was to investigate the connection between cus-
tomers’ past experiences and their intention to repurchase, particularly focusing on
the effects of both online and physical store shopping behaviors. The study also aimed
to explore the moderating role of sales promotions in this relationship. Despite the
extensive discussion of multichannel shopping in marketing literature, there remains a
gap in research specifically addressing its impact. Therefore, the study sought to analyze
how past customer experiences influence repurchase intentions in a multichannel
shopping context. This study provides a comprehensive framework for examining how
past customer experiences affect future repurchase intentions, both online and in
physical stores. It emphasizes the significant role of sales promotions in shaping this
dynamic. To enhance customer loyalty, retailers should focus on offering outstanding
experiences in both online and offline shopping environments.
The moderating effect of sales promotions introduces an additional level of
complexity to the model. The study found that sales promotions can strengthen the
connection between customer past experiences and shopping behaviors, both online
and in physical stores. This indicates that well-implemented sales promotions can
amplify the influence of positive past experiences, encouraging more customers to
make purchases across both channels.
The study highlights the significant mediating role of online shopping behavior.

Likewise, physical store shopping behavior is also identified as a key mediator. Cus-

The study has several limitations. First, establishing causality in this complex
model is challenging, as it is difficult to determine whether past customer experiences
directly influence repurchase intentions or if other unmeasured factors are involved.
Second, the research was conducted in a specific region of Pakistan, which may limit
the generalizability of the results to other populations. Additionally, the study may not
fully capture shifts in consumer behavior and attitudes over time, given the evolving
nature of consumer preferences and market conditions. This study examines both
financial and behavioral factors affecting purchase intentions, yet it has some lim -

regions with different consumer behaviors. Furthermore, the cross-sectional design
makes it challenging to establish causal relationships. Despite these limitations, the
study offers valuable insights into the relationships between key consumer behavior
traits, which can inform marketing strategies and guide future research in this field.
