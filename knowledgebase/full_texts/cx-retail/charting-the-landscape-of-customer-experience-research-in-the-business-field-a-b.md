Abstract
Objective: Customer experience is now widely acknowledged as a key factor for achieving
sustainable competitive advantage. However, comprehensive research providing a global
picture of the scientific knowledge on this topic is limited. This study provides a systematic
review of the evolution and structure of customer experience research in business through
bibliometric and network analysis.
Methodology: A total of 2,035 articles, published between 1984 and 2024, were analysed
using the Web of Science (WoS) database. The Bibliometrix R package within the Bibli -
oshiny version was used to analyse the data.
Results: The findings reveal a significant increase in publications over time, with emerg -
ing topics such as the metaverse, artificial intelligence and omnichannel retailing gaining
attention in recent years. Moreover, keyword co-occurrence and co-citation networks
identified five and three distinct research clusters, respectively.

1. Introduction
Customers seek more than just a service; they desire satisfaction and excitement
through active involvement in the service process (Rageh et al., 2013). Their needs
vary throughout the lifecycle, requiring businesses to consider all interactions from the
first encounter to ensure an excellent customer experience, including services provided,
customer demographics and interactions with employees, whether in person or digitally
(Bolton et al., 2014; Prahalad & Ramaswamy, 2004). This experience comprises all inter-
actions, conveying tangible messages (Ibrahim Mohamad Ibrahim & Bayram, 2024).
Emphasising customer experience is key to the process of value co-creation
between customers and businesses, allowing for the creation of unique, strate -
gic interactions (Ford & Heaton, 2001). This co-creation meets customer needs,
builds strong business relationships, offers a sustainable competitive advantage and
enhances service development (Ayunia, 2013). In the digital age, businesses must
tailor unique experiences for each customer, considering various factors.
A superior customer experience is crucial for gaining a competitive advantage
(Dhaigude & Mohan, 2023). Despite growing interest, existing review studies lack
a comprehensive overview of progress in customer experience research (Arici et al.,
2022). Retrospective studies often focus on specific contexts like retail (Bascur &
Rusu, 2020), social commerce (Dhaigude & Mohan, 2023) and tourism and hospi -
tality (Kim & So, 2022). Some reviews explore the impact of technologies such as
augmented reality (Kim et al., 2020) and conversational agents (Tubin et al., 2022)
on customer experience. Ghesh et al. (2024) reviewed AI-enabled customer experi -
ence. However, these studies are typically systematic reviews with a limited number
of articles (Dhaigude & Mohan, 2023; Kacprzak & Hensel, 2023).
Unlike structured literature reviews with limited coverage, bibliometric analysis
handles numerous publications, providing a comprehensive overview and revealing
trends over time (Sindhu & Bharti, 2020). Therefore, we recognise the need for bibli-
ometric analysis to comprehensively review the expansive domain of customer expe-
rience. This study examines customer experience research using bibliometric analysis
to identify emerging trends within its broad intellectual structure. Our objectives are:
a) to analyse the development and scientific output of the field; b) to evaluate research
constituents through performance analysis; and c) to determine the knowledge

analysing customer experience literature. Kim and So (2022) examined the evolu -
tion and structure of the field in hospitality and tourism, using 1,248 articles from
13 leading journals. Kumar et al. (2023) analysed the knowledge structure through
co-citation analysis from 1997 to 2021. Gallardo-Garcia et al. (2022) reviewed
customer experience literature from 1996 to 2021 using bibliometric methods. Arici
et al. (2022) explored its development in service literature using bibliometric and
content analysis. While these studies offer valuable insights, they are limited by time
frame, industry focus or specific aspects of customer experience. They conclude with
articles up to 2022 and miss recent developments. To address these gaps, our study
offers more comprehensive insights by analysing 2,035 articles on customer expe -
rience in the business field, published between 1984 and 2024. Covering a 40-year
period, this is the most extensive bibliometric analysis of customer experience in the
business context to date. We also aim to identify past and current research clusters,
providing a richer example of science mapping through thematic maps and trend
topics by year. The large amount of data and advanced science mapping techniques
justify using bibliometric analysis over other retrospective tools like systematic
reviews and meta-analyses (Mukherjee et al., 2022).
This study offers a significant threefold contribution. Firstly, stemming from the
importance of customer experience for businesses (Jain et al., 2017), this study eluci-
dates the conceptual and intellectual structure of customer experience. In doing so, it
goes beyond just providing a general overview of the field and instead identifies key
themes for future research. Secondly, unlike previous reviews, this study enhances
understanding by incorporating a larger dataset, providing a more comprehensive
and updated perspective on customer experience research. Finally, using science
mapping techniques, it guides future research in the field by identifying key themes
and prominent topics by year, thus contributing to the guidance for future investiga-
tions in the knowledge domain.

2. Literature review on customer experience
Customer experience is crucial in influencing purchasing decisions, recommen -
dations and customer loyalty. Businesses must invest in creating a positive customer
experience to gain a competitive advantage and maintain profitability (Patil & Rane,
2023). A positive experience increases repeat purchases and reduces customer churn,
while a negative experience can harm revenue (Vijayakumar, 2023). Holbrook and
Hirschman (1982) linked experience with consumption, initiating extensive research,
while Pine and Gilmore (1998) revolutionised the field by distinguishing experiences
from goods and services. Unlike quality, which focuses on the business, experience
emphasises dynamic customer-business interactions and varies with customer per -
ception (Klaus & Maklan, 2013; Palmer, 2010). This uniqueness impacts every
phase of the customer journey, from sales to post-consumption (Jain et al., 2017).
Various models have been proposed to describe these stages. Shaw and Ivens
(2002) outlined a five-stage process: expectations, pre-purchase, purchase, consump-
tion, and post-consumption. Frow and Payne (2007) identified three key stages: pur-
chase, relationship strengthening and development. Tynan and McKechnie (2009)
viewed it as a continuum of before, during and after stages, involving activities like
searching and social interactions, with outcomes such as pleasure and learning.
Several authors have also explored the dimensions of customer experience.
Schmitt (1999) identified five strategic experience modules: sensory, emotional,
cognitive, behavioural and relational, each corresponding to different aspects of the
customer journey. Later, Gentile et al. (2007) added a utilitarian dimension. Similar-
ly, Verhoef et al. (2009) outlined eight dimensions of a holistic customer experience,
covering social environment, service interface, store atmosphere, product variety,
brand, price, alternative channels and past experiences. Kim and Choi (2013)
simplified these into three dimensions: output quality, interaction quality and inter-
equal quality, encompassing elements identified by Verhoef et al. (2009). Klaus and
Maklan (2013) also emphasised product experience, output orientation, decision
moments and internal comfort.

the Business category. Each document was manually verified for topical relevance,
resulting in a refined set of 2,035 articles spanning from 1984 to 2024. These arti -
cles were organised into five BibTeX files and consolidated using the Bibliometrix
package for further analysis.

3.2. Research method
Bibliometric analysis utilises quantitative and statistical methods to examine
bibliometric data, including scholarly works and citation metrics (Mukherjee et al.,
2022). It systematically examines scholarly publications (Gallardo-Garcia et al.,
2022), using quantification, aggregation and ranking to assess the contributions of
authors, institutions and journals in a given field (Kumar et al., 2023). Additionally,
bibliometric analysis uncovers past and present trends through visualisation tech -
niques, offering insights into recent research trends in a domain (Sindhu & Bharti,
2020). Its strengths lie in managing large volumes of documents, enhancing analytical
rigour and reducing researcher subjectivity (Bakır et al., 2022). Recently, marketing
and consumer behaviour researchers have embraced bibliometric analysis to explore
topics such as consumer vulnerability (Khare & Jain, 2022), service quality (Bakır
et al., 2022) and electronic word-of-mouth (Donthu, Kumar, Pandey, et al., 2021).
Bibliometric analysis sheds light on the knowledge structure of a field, represent-
ing intellectual connections within an evolving scientific system (Mukherjee et al.,
2022). There are three types of knowledge structures: conceptual, intellectual and
social. The conceptual structure reveals themes and sub-themes within a field, while
the intellectual structure identifies influential authors, documents and sources that
facilitate knowledge transfer. Finally, the social structure maps collaborations among
researchers and countries. This study analyses the conceptual and intellectual struc -
tures through trend topic analysis, co-occurrence analysis, thematic analysis and
co-citation analysis (Mukherjee et al., 2022).
Bibliometrics uses performance analysis and science mapping to examine research
domains (Cobo et al., 2015; Mukherjee et al., 2022). Performance analysis evaluates
research productivity and influence (Donthu, Kumar, Mukherjee, et al., 2021), while

4.7. Co-citation network
When two or more documents, sources or authors are cited together in refer -
ence lists of previous works, they are referred to as co-citations, and the resulting
networks are called co-citation networks (Dharmani et al., 2021; Kim & So, 2022).
Co-citation analysis is commonly used in bibliometrics to reveal the intellectual
structure of a specific domain, identifying key authors, papers and sources. In this
study, co-citation analysis was conducted for papers as a research constituent (Bakır
et al., 2022). Figure 6 illustrates the co-citation network based on the 50 most fre -
quently cited references ( n = 50). The analysis, conducted using the Louvain algo -
rithm, identified four research clusters.
Cluster 1 (#green) focuses on the experiential, hedonic, symbolic and emotional
drivers of consumption, beyond functional benefits, providing early conceptual
foundations for customer experience. This cluster examines the psychological mech-
anisms behind customer experience. Recognising the symbolic, hedonic and aesthetic
aspects of consumption, Holbrook and Hirschman (1982) argued for the importance
of consumer fantasies and feelings and the pursuit of enjoyment as key experiential
dimensions. Scales for measuring consumers’ perceptions of hedonic and utilitarian
shopping value, as well as dimensions of the consumption experience, help to better
understand consumer behaviour (Babin et al., 1994). Bitner (1992) explored how the
servicescape (physical surroundings) influences consumption experiences.
Cluster 2 (#red) is primarily focused on methodology, with key studies addressing
quantitative research methods. Fornell and Larcker (1981) analysed the limitations
of common statistical tests in structural equation models, proposing a new approach
based on shared variance measures. Podsakoff et al. (2003) reviewed common

5. Discussion
In response to the first research question ( RQ1), the field has seen a notable
increase in scientific output, with an annual growth rate of 13.88% over four dec -
ades (1984–2024). Early research was sparse, with few articles published before
2002, but the number of publications has steadily risen. In 2023, 256 articles were
published, reflecting growing interest, and by mid-2024, 181 articles had already
been published, with expectations of further growth by year-end. This upward trend
shows the increasing significance of customer experience in the competitive business
environment. A total of 4,478 authors have contributed to this field, with interna -
tional collaborations accounting for 34.3% of the studies.
Regarding the second research question (RQ2), we focus on the key contributors
to customer experience research, including authors, institutions and countries. The
findings indicate that Klaus leads the way with 32 articles and 1,519 citations, fol -
lowed by Holbrook with 16 articles and 1,982 citations. In terms of institutions, the

When examining the most cited studies in the field, it is evident that post-2016

cluster focuses on experiential, hedonic, symbolic and emotional drivers, beyond the

The second cluster consists of methodology-focused studies on customer experience

final cluster emphasises the strategic importance of customer experience manage -

Our keyword co-occurrence analysis identified five distinct research clusters. The
largest focuses on customer satisfaction and loyalty, emphasising customer involve -
ment in the service process. Another cluster highlights branding and customer value,
with value co-creation emerging as a key topic influenced by interactions among
tech-savvy customers, businesses and other consumers. Researchers explore the
effects of contemporary topics like omnichannel (Dastane et al., 2024), augmented
reality (Xu et al., 2024), AI (Swan et al., 2024) and service innovation (Kandampul-
ly et al., 2023) on value co-creation. With the rise of digital technologies, customer
experiences through these platforms have gained attention, focusing on chatbots
and machine learning functions like personalisation and facial recognition, aligning
with Peruchini et al. (2024). Another cluster addresses improving customer experi -
ence measurements, highlighted by research methods studies. Emotional experiences
and well-being are analysed through netnography (Sari et al., 2024) and qualitative
research (Goyal & Deshwal, 2023), given their inherent nature. The final cluster
emphasises customer engagement techniques, including relationship marketing and
the role of social media platforms. As Lim et al. (2022) noted, customer engagement

A thematic map analysis based on authors’ keywords in customer experience
research offers valuable insights for businesses to refine their customer experience
strategies. Thematic maps, organised into four quadrants, classify themes as motor
(well-developed and important), basic (important but underdeveloped), niche (unique
or specific) and emerging or declining (gaining or losing importance). Thematic analysis
determined that AI and technology were positioned as motor themes, highlighting their
central role in driving innovation and improvement in customer experience. These are
crucial due to their impact on automation, personalisation and efficiency in customer
interactions. It is unsurprising that the analyses point to AI and digital technologies.
AI has revolutionised how customers interact with brands, and AI-powered customer
experiences have shown positive effects (Ameen et al., 2021). Emerging technologies
like virtual reality (VR), augmented reality (AR), mixed reality (MR), Internet of Things
(IoT), chatbots and robotics have significantly transformed the customer experience
(Hoyer et al., 2020). In this regard, the “digitalisation experience” mentioned by Gallar-
do-Garcia et al. (2022) is likely to be a topic of increasing research in the coming years.
Moreover, customer satisfaction, loyalty and customer engagement are impor -
tant for customer experience and represent basic themes. Customer satisfaction
and loyalty are key indicators of customer experience and are crucial for building
long-term relationships with customers. Customer engagement, on the other hand,
is linked to the co-creation of value, where both businesses and customers mutually
benefit. Social media, innovation and experiential marketing are the main drivers of
customer experience and represent niche themes. Meanwhile, e-commerce and retail
are emerging themes, indicating a shift in customer experience priorities. The iden -
tification of retail and e-commerce as emerging themes reflects a transitional phase
regarding how these sectors are perceived or utilised within customer experience
strategies. In conclusion, this study serves as a guiding tool for future research by
providing an overview of the accumulated knowledge on customer experience and
presenting past and present themes.
6. Conclusion
This study conducted a comprehensive bibliometric analysis of customer expe -
rience research in the field of business over four decades (1984–2024), reviewing
2,035 articles. Notably, there has been a steady increase in publications since 2002,
reflecting the growing importance of customer experience in the competitive business
environment. Key contributors include leading authors such as Klaus and Holbrook,
institutions such as the International University of Monaco and Griffith University,
and countries such as the United States and the United Kingdom. The most cited
works highlight foundational contributions to the field, with Babin et al. (1994)

al. (2009) focusing on brand experience and its impact on loyalty. Co-citation analy-
sis identified three main clusters: experiential and emotional drivers of consumption,
methodology-focused studies and the strategic importance of customer experience
management. Keyword co-occurrence analysis revealed five distinct research clus -
ters, including customer satisfaction and loyalty, branding and customer value, tech-

6.1. Theoretical implications
This study presents several theoretical contributions. First, it offers a current,
comprehensive overview of customer experience in the business field. Second, it
provides a framework showing how customer experience research has evolved, with
a significant surge in studies post-2000, peaking in 2022-2023. Third, it identifies
the most productive, cited and influential authors, institutions and countries, helping
researchers understand the existing knowledge base and potential collaborations.
Fourth, the study examines trending topics in customer experience to guide future
research. In particular, the conceptual and intellectual structures within customer
experience research have been shaped by the growing influence of AI and technol -
ogy. Increasingly, studies focus on AI-driven applications such as chatbots, robots,
virtual reality and virtual assistants, which are transforming customer experiences.
Moreover, methodological issues in studies have also been subject to debate over
time and, in addition to quantitative methods, qualitative methods have started to
be explored.
6.2. Practical implications
This study aims to guide managers in identifying resources that could provide
them with significant practical insights. For instance, a review of studies on custom-
er experience reveals that one of the most intensely researched aspects associated
with customer experience is the co-creation of value with customers. Particularly in
fiercely competitive markets, for successful value co-creation, it is essential that man-
agers develop appropriate collaborations with their customers and actively engage
in programmes where they play a more active role within the process. Managers
understand the significance of co-creation in attaining competitive advantage and
consider different strategic approaches by addressing criteria within the business
that might impede this behaviour. On the other hand, the power of social media to
address service failures is undeniable. The thematic mapping and clustering analysis
in the study revealed the importance of social media in customer interaction.
Our findings demonstrate that customer experience is evolving in the digital
age we are currently in. Customers are focused on discovering new experiences in

technology-related areas such as AI, omnichannel retailing and the metaverse. This shift
has led researchers to direct their studies on customer experience towards technology.
Moreover, the increasing focus on online customer experience in the retail sector is seen
as a rising trend in this field. The studies conducted support this claim (Chen et al.,
2021). These innovations, emerging with the changing times, enrich research on cus-
tomer experience.
6.3. Social implications
Understanding the evolution of customer experience research may have social
implications, particularly in terms of understanding consumer behaviours and
improving customer well-being. As customer experience becomes increasingly tech -
nology-driven, it reflects a societal shift towards greater digitalisation. Indeed, by
embracing technologies such as AI and augmented and virtual reality, businesses are
reshaping how consumers interact with brands and with each other. These changes
influence social norms, behaviours and expectations, ultimately reshaping consump-
tion patterns and lifestyle choices. Furthermore, shedding light on the relationship
between customer experience and critical marketing outcomes such as customer
satisfaction, loyalty and customer engagement could support strategies aimed at
addressing contemporary issues like sustainable consumption (Yan et al., 2024).
6.4. Limitations and future research
Despite the significant findings and results obtained, this study has a few limitations.
First, the data used in the study were obtained from WoS. Although the analysis of
2,035 articles published between 1984 and 2024 provides a broad perspective, the liter-
ature can be enriched by including other data sources such as Google Scholar, SCOPUS,
PubMed and different types of studies such as books, theses, etc. Second, it should be
noted that this study focused solely on articles published in the English language. Future
investigations could encompass a broader range of sources, such as book chapters and
conference papers. Finally, future studies could employ various retrospective tools, such
as meta-analyses and systematic reviews, to evaluate customer experience across differ-
ent industries, especially considering the widespread adoption of emerging technologies.
