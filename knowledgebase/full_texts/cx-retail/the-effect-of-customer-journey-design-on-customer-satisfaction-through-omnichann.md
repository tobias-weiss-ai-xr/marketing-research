Abstract
This study aims to examine the effect of Customer Journey Design (CJD) on Customer Satisfaction
(CS), both directly and indirectly through Omnichannel Customer Experience (OCE) and Customer
Engagement (CE) among consumers of H&M Gaia Mall Pontianak. This research employs a
quantitative approach with an explanatory design. The sample consists of 210 respondents selected
through purposive sampling with strict criteria as omnichannel users, namely consumers residing in
Pontianak who have made cross-channel purchases (online and offline) of H&M within the last six
months (July-December 2025). Data were analyzed using Structural Equation Modeling based on
Partial Least Squares (PLS-SEM) with WarpPLS. The results indicate that CJD has a positive and
significant effect on OCE, CE, and CS. Furthermore, OCE and CE are proven to have a positive and
significant effect on CS. Mediation testing reveals that OCE and CE act as partial mediators, where
the indirect effect of CJD on CS operates significantly through the enhan cement of omnichannel
experience and customer engagement mechanisms. These findings emphasize that optimizing
customer satisfaction requires the integration of customer journey design supported by consistent
cross-channel experiences and strengthened emotional customer bonds.

Keywords: Customer Journey Design, Omnichannel Customer Experience, Customer
Engagement, Customer Satisfaction
1. INTRODUCTION
The acceleration of global digitalization has fundamentally transformed the fashion
retail landscape, creating complex and non -linear consumer behavior patterns through the
phenomenon of webrooming, where consumers explore products online before purchasing
offline. Chung et al. (2021) and Moliner & Tortosa-Edo (2024) emphasize that if this cross-
channel integration is not managed properly, it can negatively affect product evaluation and
purchase intention. Therefore, Shankar (2021) states that global fashion brands are required
to seamlessly integrate online and offline experiences to maintain customer engagement and
satisfaction amid increasingly intense competition.

Global industry data confirms that omnichannel strategy is no longer optional but a
necessity. Shah et al. (2024) report that 83% of consumers conduct online product research
before making in -store purchases (webrooming), while Truong (2021) finds that 73% of
fashion transactions involve cross-channel interactions. In the case of the H&M brand, the
H&M Group Annual Report (2024) shows that although online sales contribute significantly
(approximately 30%), physical stores remain the backbone of revenue (70% ) and function
as fulfillment hubs. This synergy is crucial; however, its implementation in Indonesia faces
serious challenges. Preliminary empirical data indicate an expectation gap: the H&M
application rating on the Google Play Store in Indonesia averages 3.6 out of 5 stars (accessed
in January 2026), with dominant complaints related to application bugs, inaccurate stock
synchronization, and discrepancies in promotions between online and offline channels
(H&M App Reviews, 2025). This suggests that technical integration has not fully translated
into value for local consumers (Kar, 2021; Ruiz-Molina et al. 2021).
Customer dissatisfaction is also reflected in sentiment analysis of customer reviews.
Based on preliminary observations of open reviews on Trustpilot and local social media
platforms (2024–2025), recurring complaints were identified regarding glitches in g ift card
features and inconsistencies in promotional information between the application and in-store
cashier systems. This phenomenon is exacerbated by the unique characteristics of digital
consumer behavior in West Kalimantan. Data from the Central Bureau of Statistics (BPS) of
West Kalimantan Province (2024), in the “E-Commerce Statistics” table, shows that 95.17%
of e -commerce transactions in the region are conducted through instant messaging
(WhatsApp/Line), far exceeding the use of official marketplaces (11.59%) or brand websites
(0.56%). The dominance of personal communication demands high responsiveness, which
is often not fulfilled by global brand automation systems. Furthermore, the same BPS data
records stagnation and even a decline in the proportion of fashion purchases through purely
digital channels, from 16.25% (2021) to 12.95% (2024), indicating that local consumers tend
to return to physical or hybrid shopping experiences due to a lack of trust in the consistency
of purely digital services.
This gap between digital promises and physical reality creates significant friction in
the Customer Journey Design (CJD) of H&M consumers at Gaia Mall Pontianak. A
preliminary survey of 30 active visitors to H&M Gaia Mall (December 2025) reveals that
65% of respondents have experienced disappointment due to discrepancies such as products
marked as “available” in the application being out of stock in -store, or member promotions
that cannot be claimed at the cashier. This misalignment of touchpoints has the p otential to
disrupt the customer journey flow and reduce perceived value. As emphasized by Reitsamer
& Becker (2024), disruption at a single critical touchpoint can reduce overall satisfaction
evaluation, regardless of the quality of the core product. Therefore, this study is essential to
examine how improvements in customer journey design can restore trust through enhanced
Omnichannel Customer Experience (OCE) and Customer Engagement (CE).
Disruptions at a single touchpoint can reduce overall satisfaction even when product
quality is adequate (Reitsamer & Becker, 2024), making a deeper understanding of
Omnichannel Customer Experience (OCE) and Customer Engagement (CE) essential. Massi
et al. (2022) and Jiang et al. (2024) state that integrated OCE can build a tangible brand
image, while Roy et al. (2023), Zhang et al. (2021), and Yan et al. (2024) highlight that
emotional engagement and system flexibility significantly influence customer satisfaction.

Despite these insights, this study identifies three critical research gaps. First, the
conceptual gap lies in the inconsistent findings regarding the mediating mechanisms between
Customer Journey Design (CJD) and Customer Satisfaction (CS). While previous studies
acknowledge the importance of CJD, the specific pathways through which OCE and CE
mediate this relationship remain underexplored, particularly in clarifying the directional
relationship between CE and CS. Second, the contextual gap is evident in the unique digital
consumer behavior in West Kalimantan, where personal communication platforms dominate
over official brand applications. This creates a distinct mismatch between global brand
automation and local consumer expectations, a context rarely exam ined in mainstream
omnichannel literature. Third, the empirical gap is reflected in the technical and operational
frictions experienced by H&M consumers, such as real -time inventory discrepancies and
promotional inconsistencies, which highlight that techno logical integration does not
automatically translate into perceived value.  To address these gaps, this study aims to
examine the effect of Customer Journey Design on Customer Satisfaction, both directly and
indirectly through Omnichannel Customer Experience and Customer Engagement among
consumers of H&M Gaia Mall Pontianak.
Hypothesis Development
The Effect of Customer Journey Design on Omnichannel Customer Experience
The quality of the omnichannel customer experience is strongly influenced by how
firms design the configuration of interactions throughout the shopping journey. Mele et al.
(2025) found that consumers’ perceptions of omnichannel journey quality are shaped by
their evaluation of self-efficacy and their mindset toward the experience design they receive.
Furthermore, Van Nguyen et al. (2024) identified that customer behavior in omnichannel
retail is dynamic and segmented ( digital switchers , webroomers, showroomers), thus
requiring a journey design that is both integrated and personalized. This indicates that an
adaptive Customer Journey Design (CJD), which considers behavioral characteristics at
various touchpoints, can enhance the overall experience quality. There fore, CJD serves as
the primary foundation for creating consistency and value across all channels. Based on this
reasoning, the first hypothesis is formulated:
H1: Customer Journey Design has a positive and significant effect on Omnichannel
Customer Experience.

The Effect of Customer Journey Design on Customer Engagement
A well -structured and experience -oriented customer journey design can create
meaningful touchpoints that encourage customer engagement cognitively, affectively, and
behaviorally. Ahmad et al.  (2022) explain that customer experience designed through
aspects such as the shopping environment, purchasing procedures, and staff services can
enhance engagement, especially when strengthened by value co-creation processes. This
finding aligns with Shoukat & Ramkissoon (2022), who emphasize that interactions
designed through enjoyable experiences and place identity  can strengthen customers’
emotional engagement with a brand. Therefore, an effective CJD will trigger psychological
and active behavioral responses from consumers toward the H&M brand. Based on this
reasoning, the second hypothesis is formulated:

H2: Customer Journey Design has a positive and significant effect on Customer

The Effect of Customer Journey Design on Customer Satisfaction
A well-designed customer experience throughout service interactions can enhance
positive perceptions that lead to satisfaction. Manyanga et al.  (2022) demonstrate that
effective management of each interaction point leads to higher customer satisfaction and
encourages word-of-mouth recommendations. In addition, Vo-Thanh et al. (2024) found that
perceived innovation throughout the service journey has a strong relationship with overall
customer satisfaction. These findings confirm that an integrated CJD is an important factor
in creating satisfying service experiences at every stage. Although its effect may not be as
strong as the mediating variables, a well -designed journey still contributes directly to post -
purchase evaluation. Based on this reasoning, the third hypothesis is formulated:
H3: Customer Journey Design has a positive and significant effect on Customer Satisfaction.

The Effect of Omnichannel Customer Experience on Customer Satisfaction
The integration of physical and digital channels plays a vital role in shaping a
seamless phygital experience. Mishra et al. (2023) found that when customers perceive a
well-integrated omnichannel experience, they feel more empowered and satisfied, which
contributes to customer retention. Pires et al. (2025) reinforce this by showing that customer
experience is a key determinant of customer satisfaction, where attributes such as
technological interactivity, security, and personalization significantly shape satisfaction
when expectations are met. In the context of H&M Pontianak, consistency across
experiential dimensions (affective, cognitive, relational) is a prerequisite for satisfaction.
Based on this reasoning, the fourth hypothesis is formulated:
H4: Omnichannel Customer Experience has a positive and significant effect on Customer
Satisfaction.

The Effect of Customer Engagement on Customer Satisfaction
While some literature posits satisfaction as a precursor to engagement, this study
adopts the perspective that Customer Engagement (CE) acts as a critical antecedent to
Customer Satisfaction (CS). This positioning is grounded in the Cognitive -Affective-
Behavioral framework, where engagement represents a heightened state of psychological
readiness and resource investment (cognitive, emotional, and behavioral) toward the brand
(Hollebeek et al., 2014; Vivek et al., 2012). When customers are deeply engaged, th ey
develop stronger self-brand connections and higher levels of trust, which serve as cognitive
filters that reduce perceived risk and enhance the interpretation of service encounters.
Tran and Nguyen (2022) argue that authentic brand experiences foster emotional
engagement, which subsequently drives satisfaction by aligning customer expectations with
perceived performance. Engaged customers are more likely to participate in value co -
creation activities, leading to a sense of ownership and fulfillment that directly translates
into higher satisfaction ratings (Ahmad et al., 2022). Furthermore, high engagement
mitigates the impact of minor service failures because engaged customers possess greater
resilience and commitment to the brand relationship (Rather et al., 2022). Thus, rather than
viewing satisfaction solely as a post -purchase evaluation, this study argues that the pre -

The Mediating Role of Omnichannel Customer Experience
Customer Journey Design not only has a direct effect but also operates through the
mechanism of forming high -quality experiences. Blom et al. (2021) show that integrating
touchpoints aligned with customer shopping goals creates service excellence , which
enhances satisfaction. Furthermore, Massi et al.  (2022) emphasize that authentic and
seamless omnichannel experiences play a crucial role in creating customer value through
consistent channel integration, acting as a bridge between journey design and positiv e
evaluation. This implies that an effective CJD must first be translated into a coherent OCE
to maximize customer satisfaction. Based on this reasoning, the sixth hypothesis is
formulated:
H6: Customer Journey Design has a positive and significant effect on Customer Satisfaction
mediated by Omnichannel Customer Experience.

The Mediating Role of Customer Engagement
An effective customer journey design builds engagement, which in turn strengthens
positive evaluations of the service. Chen et al. (2021) assert that positive brand experiences
encourage engagement behaviors such as repeat usage and word-of-mouth, all of which lead
to satisfaction. Similarly, Rather  et al.  (2022) found that customer engagement formed
through satisfying service experiences directly enhances behavioral intentions and customer
satisfaction. In this model, Customer Engagement acts as a partial mediator that translates
journey design into emotional and behavioral responses, which are subsequently converted
into holistic satisfaction. Unlike prior literature that positions satisfaction as a mediator
toward engagement, this study examines the pathway from CJD to CE to CS as a value -
creation mechanism based on engagement. Based on this reasoning, the seventh hypothesis
is formulated:
H7: Customer Journey Design has a positive and significant effect on Customer Satisfaction
mediated by Customer Engagement.

2. RESEARCH METHOD
This study employs a quantitative approach with an explanatory design to examine
the structural relationships among variables in a measurable manner. Although a cross -
sectional design with purposive sampling does not allow for definitive causal inference, this
approach is adequate for testing predictive and explanatory relationships among constructs
based on a strong theoretical foundation.
The target population consists of consumers who have interacted across channels
(omnichannel) with H&M at Gaia Mall Pontianak. The determination of sample size no
longer relies on the heuristic “10 -times indicator rule,” but instead uses the inverse square
root method and minimum sample size calculation based on statistical power in WarpPLS
version 8.0. The results indicate that to achieve a statistical power of 0.80 with a medium

effect size, a minimum of 195 respondents is required. However, to anticipate potential drop-

purposive sampling with the following criteria: (1) Aged ≥  17 years and residing/active in
Pontianak; (2) Have made purchases at the physical H&M Gaia Mall store at least twice in
the last six months (July –December 2025 period); (3) Have conducted transactions via the
H&M app/website during the same period (July –December 2025); (4) Possess an active
H&M membership account; and (5) Follow H&M social media channels. These criteria
ensure that respondents have experienced a complete omnichannel customer journey, rather
than being single-channel users.
The research instrument is a structured questionnaire using a 5 -point Likert scale (1
= Strongly Disagree to 5 = Strongly Agree). Primary data collection was conducted from
March to May 2026. Questionnaires were distributed both online via Google Forms and
offline through direct intercepts at the H&M Gaia Mall Pontianak store to reach eligi ble
respondents. The six-month transaction criterion specifically refers to the shopping activity
period from July to December 2025, ensuring that all reported experiences occurred prior to
the data collection phase to minimize  recall bias. Data collection was carried out through:
(1) Distribution of Google Form links via H&M Pontianak fan communities and social media
platforms (Instagram/TikTok); and (2) Direct intercepts at the exit area of the H&M Gaia
Mall store. To minimize Common Method Bias (CMB) due to the use of a single data source,
the study applied respondent anonymity and randomized item order.  Data analysis was
conducted using Structural Equation Modeling base d on Partial Least Squares (PLS -SEM)
with WarpPLS version 8.0. The algorithm applied was Warp3 PLS Regression, with a
resampling method using bootstrapping of 500 sub -samples to test path significance.
Hypothesis testing employed a two -tailed test with a s ignificance level of α = 0.05. The
analysis procedure includes three main stages:
1. Measurement Model Evaluation (Outer Model):  Assessing convergent validity (loading
factor > 0.70, AVE > 0.50), discriminant validity (Fornell -Larcker criterion, cross -
loading, and HTMT < 0.90), and reliability (Cronbach’s Alpha & Composite Reliability
> 0.70).
2. Structural Model Evaluation (Inner Model): Assessing model fit (Goodness of Fit: APC,
ARS, AVIF, AFVIF, GoF), coefficient of determination (R²), predictive relevance (Q²),
and effect size (f²).
3. Hypothesis Testing & Mediation Analysis:  Examining direct effects, indirect effects
(specific indirect effect), and total effects to determine the type of mediation (partial or
full).

DISCUSSION
The Effect of Customer Journey Design on Omnichannel Customer Experience
The results of this study indicate that Customer Journey Design (CJD) has a positive
and significant effect on Omnichannel Customer Experience (OCE), with a path coefficient
of 0.745. This finding is consistent with Wu et al. (2025), who found that the quality of
customer journeys in an omnichannel environment is largely determined by consumers’
mindset and perceptions of experience configuration throughout the journey. Furthermore,
Van Nguyen et al. (2024) emphasize that journey design that considers consumer behavioral
characteristics across various touchpoints can enhance the overall quality of the omnichannel
experience. In the context of H&M Pontianak, the design of a cohesive shopping journey
across physical stores, mobile appl ications, and social media provides a strong foundation
for a more consistent and valuable omnichannel experience. Thus, integration across
touchpoints becomes the key factor in shaping OCE.

The Effect of Customer Journey Design on Customer Engagement
The second hypothesis is supported with a path coefficient of 0.686, indicating that
CJD has a strong influence on Customer Engagement (CE). This result supports Ahmad et
al. (2022), who explain that customer experiences designed through aspects such as the
shopping environment, purchasing procedures, and staff services can enhance engagement
through value co-creation processes. Shoukat and Ramkissoon (2022) further reinforce that

themes and connectivity across touchpoints within the customer journey serve as key drivers
of deeper engagement, particularly through positive affective responses a nd behavioral
activation toward the brand.

The Effect of Customer Journey Design on Customer Satisfaction
Although the direct effect of CJD on Customer Satisfaction (CS) is moderate (path
coefficient = 0.249), the finding remains statistically significant. Manyanga et al. (2022)
explain that effective management of each customer interaction point leads to higher
satisfaction levels and encourages word-of-mouth recommendations. Additionally, Tran and
Nguyen (2022) found that perceived innovation throughout the service journey is strongly
associated with customer satisfaction. In the context of H&M Pontianak, the company’s
ability to manage interaction stages in a structured manner directly contributes to the
formation of satisfaction. However, the strongest influence of CJD on CS occurs through
mediation pathways via OCE and CE, indicating that journey design works optimally when
mediated by experience and engagement.

The Effect of Omnichannel Customer Experience on Customer Satisfaction
The acceptance of the fourth hypothesis with a path coefficient of 0.379 confirms
that OCE is an important determinant of CS. This finding aligns with Mishra et al. (2023),
who demonstrate that when customers perceive a well -integrated omnichannel experience,
they feel more empowered and satisfied with the service received. Pires et al. (2025) further
strengthen this argument by showing that digital interface attributes such as interactivity,
security, and personalization significantly shape customer satisfaction. For H&M consumers
in Pontianak, consistency of experience across affective, cognitive, physical, relational,
sensory, and symbolic dimensions within the omnichannel system contributes significantly
to satisfaction, confirming that seamless cross -channel integration is a key prerequisite for
satisfaction in modern retail.

The Effect of Customer Engagement on Customer Satisfaction
The results confirm that Customer Engagement (CE) has a positive and significant
direct effect on Customer Satisfaction (CS) with a path coefficient of 0.373. This finding
supports the perspective that engagement acts as an antecedent to satisfaction, aligning with
Tran and Nguyen (2022). Unlike studies that position satisfaction as a precursor to
engagement (Agyei et al., 2021), this study argues that actively engaged customers develop
stronger self-brand connections and higher psychological investment. This heightened state
of engagement creates a "halo effect" where customers are more tolerant of minor service

aspects of journey design into holistic satisfaction , demonstrating that investing in
engagement through personalization and social interaction is an effective strategy in the

4. CONCLUSION
Based on the structural analysis results using WarpPLS v8.0, this study concludes
that Customer Journey Design (CJD) has a positive and significant effect on Omnichannel
Customer Experience (OCE; β = 0.745), Customer Engagement (CE; β = 0.686), and
Customer Satisfaction (CS; β = 0.249). Although the direct effect of CJD on CS is moderate,
its total effect reaches 0.787 when mediated by OCE and CE. This highlights that the
effectiveness of journey design relies heavily on its translation into seamless experi ences
and emotional bonds. The mediation tests confirm that OCE (indirect effect = 0.282) and CE
(indirect effect = 0.256) act as significant partial mediators, proving that technical integration
alone is insufficient without fostering psychological engagement and coherent omnichannel
experiences.
This study offers three key theoretical contributions. First, it clarifies the conceptual
gap by positioning Customer Engagement not merely as an outcome, but as a critical

Managerial implications for H&M Gaia Mall Pontianak emphasize the need to

customers who use the app for in -store checks (webrooming). Specifically,  addressing the
pain point of out -of-stock discrepancies will reduce cognitive friction and enhance the
relational dimension of the Omnichannel Customer Experience.
At the digital system level (IT and app integration), the H&M application requires
technical upgrades to ensure promotional consistency. Bugs related to gift card features and
promo code validity must be resolved to align seamlessly with in -store cashier s ystems.
Furthermore, the user interface should be optimized for faster loading speeds and clearer
stock visibility indicators. These technical improvements support the process consistency
aspect of Customer Journey Design, thereby reducing consumer frustration and ensuring that
digital touchpoints effectively drive engagement.
Finally, at the Customer Relationship Management (CRM) level, H&M should shift
from purely transactional marketing to relational engagement. Leveraging the finding that
Customer Engagement mediates satisfaction, the brand can create personalized loyalty
rewards based on cross -channel purchase history rather than single -channel spending.
Additionally, integrating responsive customer service via popular local platforms, such as
the WhatsApp Business API, can bridge the gap between global automation and local
consumer expectations. These comprehensive efforts across the store, digital, and CRM
levels aim to minimize cognitive friction caused by information inconsistencies, ensuring
that investments in Customer Journey Design are optimally converted into sustain ed
Customer Satisfaction.
This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. First, the use of
purposive sampling with specific criteria limits the generalizability of the findings to a
broader population. Second, data collection was conducted at a single H&M store in Gaia
Mall Pontianak, which may not fully reflect conditions in other locations with different
consumer characteristics. Third, the cross -sectional research design captures respondent
perceptions at only one point in time, preventing the observation of chan ges in customer
experience over time. Fourth, the use of self-reported questionnaires may introduce response
