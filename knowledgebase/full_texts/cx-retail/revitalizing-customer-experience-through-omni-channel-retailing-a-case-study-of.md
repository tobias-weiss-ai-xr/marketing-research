Background:
Omnichannel retailing is intended to provide seamless customer experiences.
However, the proliferation of channels can result in inconsistent service,
inventory discrepancies, and short-term deterioration in brand experience.
Objective:
This study examined how Nike transformed its customer experience through
membership programmes, mobile applications, digital commerce, experiential
retail, community-created content, and retail partnerships.
Methods:
A case-study approach was adopted using publicly available evidence collected
from 2018 to June 2026. The evidence was analysed through temporal
bracketing, pattern matching, and channel-integration analysis.
Results:
The findings indicated that membership created a sense of shared identity,
transactional applications also supported inspiration, and localised stores
translated customer data into relevant physical experiences. However, an
excessive direct-to-consumer focus reduced marketplace coverage and
increased dependence on promotional activity. The analysis also identified
tensions involving marketplace relationships, exclusivity, convenience,
and customer privacy. Nike’s subsequent rebalancing towards wholesale
partnerships demonstrated that omnichannel advantage depends on the

4.1 Membership as the identity spine
Nike’s scheme of membership created an identity
that was sustained in trading, exercise, product and
to a lesser degree some services in the stores. This
architecture ensured a less fragmented approach
for the retailers, where each channel would “believe
there is a new visitor”. Preferences, community access,
purchase history, product access, to name a few of
the many customizations were provided through
membership. With increase in digital engagement,
Nike’s membership in their database increased by
approximately 55 million in 2020 [22]. The strategic
outreach was far more than transactions: both Nike
Run Club and Nike Training Club will be ingrained
into the culture of exercise, goal setting and self-
improvement.
If a design was recognized, then it had convenience
and symbolic values. One brand identity might be
reached by the customers during trainings, through
running, by product launches, and retail services. A
greater receptivity towards the relationship program
was previously correlated to engagement and loyalty
(Manjunath et al., 2025). However, it was the same
identity-backbone with its emphasis on sensitive
behavioral and location data. As such, membership
value was dependent upon reciprocity – customers
require beneficial training, relevant access, and
service benefits as opposed to over-targeting. As the
data collection became more apparent than the data
control it became intrusive.

4.6 The direct-to-consumer paradox
This approach by Nike directly contributed to a greater
brand, data, margin and membership. During this
fairly rapid pace of Covid-19 days, these advantages
appeared to be serving to bolster one another.

The strategy proved to be incorrect, by 2024. It was
about restoring “bringing back the health in the

5. Discussion
They discovered that while Nike had many new and
advanced Omnichannel assets, it was not necessarily
one for the customer. The first was movement
invisibilised through the move of membership, the
second was the move of familiarity and continuity
through the move of App, the third, the sensory and
relational move through the move of stores and the
fourth was the move of outreach through the move
of partnership. Weakened by the optimization of one
capability at the cost/expense of the other. In the
first case, the enhancements made to the customer
data segment to support Direct Channel growth had
a negative impact on access to the marketplace while
the enhancements made to the conversion through
promotion made the even more premium content
offerings feel Samey and privacy incompatible.
These tensions were solved by a five-part
revitalization model. Ensure through Owned Digital,
Owned Physical, Strategic Wholesale, Community. For
recognition to take place, customers had to choose
to name themselves with a preference or access to a
benefit... The whole problem with recognition is it’s
voluntary and doesn’t put all customers in the same
trajectory (Vinoth & Srivastava, 2024). Responsiveness
required the right assortment, inventory and
empowered the service, with fulfillment flexibility.
Importance of reassurance in recovery, authenticity,
privacy, fairness and consistency. Renewal needed to
give feedback based on returns, search, community,
partner and FHE feedback to re-influence products,
product content, allocation and channel policy.
The model was based on the notion of channel-
integration research and advanced further one step
to include a new concept the notion of marketplace
governance. The vast majority of integration builds
(layouts) are driven by the retailer being the channel
that is integrated. For a global brand, a number of
possibilities exist to operate such as partial control,
contracts, shared launches, partner systems and
customer propositions. When it comes to seamless, it
doesn’t necessarily mean same (Vinoth & Srivastava,
2024). For a retailer running a retailer, there should
be retailer expertise, retailer comparisons and there
shouldn’t be an owned application that is matching
that. The challenge for the brand is to make sure
there’s no break in the “truth of the product”,
availability of the product and responsibility of the

Another point raised in the case was on the
relationship between Customer Experience and
Product Innovation. Omnichannel can assist in seeing
behavior and dissipating friction but it can’t get people
to truly want it if the products in the product portfolio
don’t provide novelty or are irrelevant. Information
can even provide you with confirmation of current

exploring. For Nike, however, the message was clear:
A product-strategy, combined with an understanding
of the customer journey, was required for experience
revitalization as part of the new sport/athlete focus
and new product development. Omnichannel should
not replace the attitude of innovation, it should bring
the innovation to the life of the customer (Wang,

7. Limitations and Future
Research
8. Conclusion
The case does not provide access to quantitative data
at Nike level or to their individual level satisfaction or
proprietary decision rules. Market competition, tariff,
macro-economic factors as well as product cycles
and geographic variations had an impact on financial
and traffic indicators. As a result, the mechanisms
were identified that might just be possible, but not
necessarily causative. Store descriptions to the public
also sometimes may be developed to emphasize
planned design instead of operation.
It is possible to conduct more research as it compares
the Nike journeys in different countries, sports and
types of partners, using customer diary, a series of
transactions, service records and experiments. It
would be interesting to study the possibility of seamless
wholesale-to-membership benefits, fairness issues
with algorithmic scarcity and any potential positive
effect on member’s levels of engagement due to a
purchase. In longitudinal analysis the effects of the
innovation and integration of product channels have
to be taken into account separately. The customer-
effort, customer-trust, customer-fulfillment and
customer-commercial outcome elements of journey-
level metrics should also be research-tested.
With Nike, it has been quite apparent, that the
experience of omnichannel customers was not solely
one of digital growth, but orchestration too. Each
one of those items — membership, mobile apps,
experiential retail, local content, community content
and fulfillment services and wholesale partners —
were unique with value. The ecosystem weakened
with the shift to direct channel as being the headwind,
promotion as the new fuel and with data driven
personalization turning into the new product renewal
and industry trust.
