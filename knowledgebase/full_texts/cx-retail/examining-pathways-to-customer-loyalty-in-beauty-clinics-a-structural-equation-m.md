ABSTRACT

Customer loyalty is a critical determinant of long-term competitiveness in beauty clinics and
aesthetic healthcare services. Despite a growing body of research, the structural pathways
through which service quality and customer experience influence custome r loyalty remain
theoretically fragmented. The psychological mechanisms that link these antecedents to loyal
behavior, particularly the mediating role of customer satisfaction and the moderating role of
service intimacy, have not been simultaneously examin ed in an integrated framework
specific to beauty clinic settings. This systematic literature review addresses that gap by
synthesizing 19 peer-reviewed studies selected through the PRISMA 2020 framework from
Scopus-indexed publications between 2020 and 202 6. Thematic synthesis revealed three
principal findings. First, customer satisfaction consistently and strongly mediates the effects
of both service quality and customer experience on customer loyalty. Second, customer
experience is a multidimensional cons truct encompassing cognitive, emotional, sensory,
relational, and digital dimensions of the patient journey. Third, service intimacy amplifies
the satisfaction -to-loyalty conversion by deepening trust, emotional attachment, and
personalized care between pa tients and practitioners. The novelty of this review lies in its
proposal of an integrated moderated mediation framework that positions service intimacy as
a relational boundary condition within a unified loyalty model for aesthetic healthcare, a
configuration that has not previously appeared in the literature.

INTRODUCTION

The beauty and aesthetic healthcare industry has grown substantially over the past
decade. Rising consumer awareness of skincare, personal appearance, and preventive
aesthetic care has positioned beauty clinic services as a significant segment within the
broader personal healthcare service sector. In Indonesia, this growth has been accelerated
by the expansion of the urban middle class, increasing household incomes, and the pervasive
influence of social media and digital beauty content (Fakhfakh et al., 2025; Elfi et al., 2025).
West Java, as Indonesia's most populous province with approximately 50.35 million
residents, represents a particularly large and fiercely competitive market for beauty clinic
services (BPS, 2024).
As the number of competing clinics increases, the ability to retain existing patients has
become more strategically important than acquiring new ones. Customer loyalty, understood

Research consistently shows that loyal patients generate higher lifetime value, lower

Prior studies have examined individual determinants of customer loyalty in beauty
clinic and skincare contexts, including service quality (Naung, 2024; Lienata and Berlianto,

Ribeiro, 2023; Comlek, 2025), brand trust (Traiyarach et al., 2025; Yanti et al., 2026 ), and
relational constructs such as brand love ( Muhajir et al., 2026). However, these antecedents
have largely been investigated in isolation. The psychological mechanisms that translate
service quality and customer experience into loyal behavior, and the relational conditions
that determine how strongly that conversion oc curs, remain inadequately theorized in an
integrated framework for beauty clinic research (Fitriani et al., 2024).
A particularly important and unresolved issue is the well -documented satisfaction -
loyalty gap in high-contact service environments. Jones and Sasser (1995) demonstrated that
even highly satisfied customers may defect to competitors under the right conditions. In
beauty clinic settings, satisfied patients sometimes switch clinics due to competitor
promotions, influencer recommendations, or the appeal of newer  aesthetic technologies.
This gap suggests that customer satisfaction, while necessary, is insufficient on its own to
generate durable loyalty. An additional relational mechanism is needed to explain why some
satisfied patients become loyal advocates while  others remain transient. This review
proposes that service intimacy, defined as the depth of emotional closeness, mutual
understanding, and personalized care between a patient and a specific practitioner, functions
as that mechanism by moderating the stre ngth of the satisfaction -to-loyalty relationship
(Yim et al., 2008; Mansour, 2021; Treacy and Wiersema, 1993).

Research Questions
This systematic literature review is guided by four research questions that together
cover the full range of structural pathways proposed in the conceptual framework.
RQ1: What constitutes customer loyalty in beauty clinic and aesthetic healthcare service
contexts, and what are its primary behavioral and attitudinal dimensions?
RQ2: Through what pathways do service quality and customer experience independently
and jointly influence customer loyalty in beauty clinic settings, and to what extent
does customer satisfaction mediate those relationships?
RQ3: Does service intimacy moderate the relationship between customer satisfaction and
customer loyalty in beauty clinic and related high-contact service environments, and
if so, through what relational mechanisms does that moderation operate?
RQ4: What theoretical gaps and empirical limitations exist in the current body of literature,
and what integrated framework would best address those gaps for future empirical
research in Indonesian beauty clinics?

Novelty of This Study
The novelty of this study is established along three dimensions. First, this is the first
systematic literature review to simultaneously integrate service quality, customer
experience, customer satisfaction, and service intimacy within a single theoretical
framework for the beauty clinic and aesthetic healthcare context. Prior reviews and empirical
studies have examined subsets of these constructs in isolation or in pairs, but no study has

fifteen years of customer loyalty research and found that relational and emotional variables
consistently produced stronger and more durable loyalty effects than functional service
quality alone, particularly in contexts characterized by rep eated personal interactions. The
proposed framework directly responds to this finding by positioning service intimacy as the
relational amplifier that transforms transient satisfaction into enduring loyalty.

Theoretical Framework
This review is grounded in two complementary theoretical traditions that together
explain how loyalty is formed and sustained in high-contact personal service environments.
Service Dominant Logic (SDL), developed by Prahalad, C. K., & Ramaswamy, V  (2016),
holds that value is not embedded in a product or service output but is co-created through the
ongoing interactions between the provider and the customer. Applied to beauty clinics, this
means that the loyalty a patient develops toward a specific clinic i s not determined by
clinical outcomes alone but emerges from the cumulative quality of relational exchanges
with doctors, therapists, and support staff across multiple service touchpoints and visits.
SDL therefore supports a model in which both service qua lity and customer experience
contribute to loyalty as dimensions of co -created value, and in which relational depth,
operationalized as service intimacy, strengthens that loyalty over time.
Relationship Marketing Theory (Palmatier et al., 2020) provides the second theoretical
foundation. This theory argues that durable loyalty in service contexts requires more than
momentary satisfaction with individual transactions. It depends on the development of trust,
affective commitment, and long-term relational bonds between the customer and the service
provider. Relationship marketing research has consistently shown that customers who feel
personally known, genuinely cared for, and individually valued by their service provider
exhibit significantly high er retention rates and advocacy behaviors ( de Waal and van der
Heijden, 2016; Tabrani et al., 2018 ). Service intimacy is the construct that operationalizes
this relational depth within the proposed framework.
Five constructs constitute the proposed conceptual model. Service quality is defined
as the patient's overall assessment of how well clinic performance meets or exceeds
expectations across five dimensions: reliability, responsiveness, assurance, empathy, a nd
tangibles (Parasuraman et al., 1988; Lee et al., 2000). Customer experience encompasses the

customer experience function as complementary antecedents that each influence both
customer satisfaction and customer loyalty. Customer satisfaction in turn mediates those
antecedent effects, acting as the psychological gateway through which perceived service
quality and positive experiences are converted into loyal behavioral intentions. Service
intimacy moderates the relationship between customer satisfaction and customer loya lty,
such that the same level of satisfaction produces significantly stronger loyalty outcomes
when the patient has developed a deep relational bond with their practitioner.

RESEARCH METHODS

This study adopts a Systematic Literature Review methodology following the
PRISMA 2020 framework (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta -
Analyses). The PRISMA 2020 framework was selected because it provides a transparent
and replicable procedure for identifying, screening, and synthesizing the evidence base in a
way that minimizes selection bias and supports methodological rigor ( Mishra et al., 2023).
Scopus was chosen as the primary database because of its broad coverage of peer-reviewed
journals across business, management, medicine, and social sciences, and its rigorous
indexing standards.
The search string was applied to title, abstract, and keyword fields and systematically
combined terms from four conceptual domains: the service context domain (beauty clinic,
skincare clinic, aesthetic clinic, aesthetic healthcare), the antecedent domain (service
quality, SERVQUAL, customer experience, patient experience), the mediator domain
(customer satisfaction, patient satisfaction), and the moderator and outcome domain (service
intimacy, customer intimacy, customer loyalty, repurchase intention, revisit intention). The
search was restricted to English -language, peer-reviewed journal articles and conference
papers published between 2020 and 2026. Studies using qualitative methods alone or relying
entirely on secondary data were excluded.

Theme 1: Service Quality as a Foundational Driver of Satisfaction and Loyalty (RQ2)
Across the reviewed studies, service quality emerges as the most consistently
examined and most broadly supported antecedent of customer satisfaction and loyalty in
beauty clinic and aesthetic healthcare settings. In these high -contact service environments,
patients evaluate not only the clinical outcomes of their treatments but also the full range of
human interaction quality, including the technical proficiency and communication style of
doctors and therapists, the responsiveness of administrative staff, the reliability of
appointment systems, and the physical environment of the clinic (Chang et al., 2020; Naung,
2024; Traiyarach et al., 2025).
Empirical evidence from Taiwan, Myanmar, Indonesia, and Thailand consistently
confirms that SERVQUAL dimensions, particularly empathy, responsiveness, and
assurance, are significantly and positively associated with both customer satisfaction and
revisit in tention. Chang et al. (2020) found that service quality in Taiwanese medical
cosmetology clinics had a strong direct effect on experiential value and repurchase intention,
with satisfaction functioning as a critical mediating pathway. Lienata and Berlianto (2023)
demonstrated in Indonesian beauty clinics that service quality operated primarily through
customer satisfaction to influence loyalty, with clinic image serving as a complementary
predictor. Gulo and Sabar (2024)  confirmed this pattern at Natasha Skin Care Clinic in
Jakarta, showing that service quality and price fairness jointly determined customer
satisfaction, which in turn drove repurchase intention. Naung (2024) extended this finding
to Myanmar by showing that service quality combined with customer relationship
management predicted satisfaction and revisit intention at an aesthetic clinic.
A consistent observation across these studies is that service quality, while necessary,
is rarely sufficient on its own to produce durable loyalty. Its effect on loyalty becomes
significantly stronger when it acts in conjunction with positive experiential and relational
variables. Yusria et al. (2024)  confirmed in the Indonesian beauty clinic context that the
influence of service quality on repurchase interest was substantially amplified by patient
trust, suggesting that relational conditions moderate the service quality-to-loyalty pathway.
This observation provides strong support for treating service quality as one foundational
antecedent within a more comprehensive integrated model rather than as a standalone
predictor.

of a treatment to encompass the full range of cognitive, emotional, sensory, social, and
behavioral responses that patients accumulate throughout the entire service journey (Becker
and Jaakkola, 2020; Lemon and Verhoef, 2016; Pine and Gilmore, 1998 ). This journey

contemporary beauty clinic landscape. Elfi et al. (2025)  demonstrated that social media

similar cognitive and emotional pathways. Fakhfakh et al. (2025) further demonstrated that
content marketing and strategic digital engagement in beauty clinic settings mediated the
relationship between customer engagement and purchase intention. These findings
collectively confirm that customer experience must be conce ptualized and managed across
both physical and digital dimensions to fully account for its influence on loyalty.

Theme 3: Customer Satisfaction as the Central Mediating Mechanism (RQ2 and RQ3)
Across all 19 reviewed studies, customer satisfaction emerges as the most consistently
confirmed psychological mechanism that links service quality and customer experience to
loyal patient behavior. Satisfaction functions as the central cognitive and affective gateway
through which positive perceptions of service inputs are converted into actual loyalty
outcomes including repeat visits, positive word -of-mouth, and resistance to switching
(Oliver, 1980; Hamzah and Shamsudin, 2020; Khan et al., 2022).
The mediation evidence from beauty clinic and aesthetic healthcare contexts is
particularly clear. Lienata and Berlianto (2023)  found that satisfaction fully mediated the
effect of service quality and clinic image on loyalty and revisit intention in Indonesian
beauty clinics, with no significant direct path remaining between service quality and loyalty
once satisfaction was includ ed as a mediator. Gulo and Sabar (2024) demonstrated that
satisfaction mediated the combined effects of service quality and price fairness on
repurchase intention. Brandao and Ribeiro (2023)  confirmed that patient experience
influenced loyalty through its effect on satisfaction. Dam and Dam (2021)  in a general
service context showed that both service quality and brand image affected loyalty primarily
through their prior effects on satisfaction, a pattern highly consistent with the beauty clinic
evidence. Yum and Yoo (2023) confirmed the same mediation structure in the mobile social
media service context, further supporting the generalizability of satisfaction as the central
mediating pathway.
A critical and practically significant observation, however, is that satisfied customers
do not inevitably become loyal ones. Jones and Sasser (1995) documented this satisfaction-

Theme 4: Service Intimacy as a Relational Moderator of the Satisfaction -to-Loyalty
Conversion (RQ3)
Service intimacy refers to the depth of emotional closeness, mutual understanding, and
individualized care that develops between a patient and a specific practitioner through
repeated service encounters (Yim et al., 2008; Treacy and Wiersema, 1993). In beauty clinic
settings, the conditions for intimacy development are naturally present. Aesthetic treatments
are physically close, personally sensitive, and often recurring. Patients disclose personal
information about their skin conditions, health hi stories, and aesthetic insecurities. Over
time, a patient who consistently receives treatment from the same doctor or therapist can
develop a relationship characterized by genuine emotional connection, mutual recognition,
and deeply personalized care.
Yim et al. (2008)  established the foundational evidence for service intimacy as a
loyalty amplifier by demonstrating that customer -firm affection and customer -staff
relational closeness significantly strengthened the impact of satisfaction on loyalty across
multiple service categories. Their structural model showed that the satisfaction -to-loyalty
path was substantially stronger for customers who experienced high levels of intimacy with
their service provider. Mansour (2021) confirmed that customer intimacy moderated the
satisfaction-to-loyalty relationship in Islamic banking, with the moderating effect being
most pronounced for customers who maintained long -term relational bonds with specific
service advisors. Tabrani et al. (2018) showed that customer intimacy mediated the effect of
trust and commitment on loyalty in banking, while Yusfiarto et al. (2023) demonstrated that
service quality influenced loyalty partly through its effect on customer intimacy in Islamic
banking contexts.
In the beauty service context, Park (2023) provides the most directly relevant evidence
by demonstrating that relationship benefits, including special treatment, social interaction,
and recognition benefits, significantly predicted relational commitment and loyalty beyond
the effects of transactio nal satisfaction alone. Jeon and Kim (2016) further confirmed that
service intimacy moderated customer responses to service failures, with high -intimacy
customers exhibiting significantly greater loyalty restoration after negative service
experiences compa red to low -intimacy customers. De Waal and van der Heijden (2016)
showed that employee relational behavior, including attentiveness, personalization, and
emotional engagement, was a primary driver of both customer intimacy and loyalty, a
finding with clear implications for staff training in beauty clinics.
The emotional switching cost mechanism is the primary pathway through which
service intimacy amplifies loyalty beyond what satisfaction alone can achieve. A patient
who has built genuine intimacy with a specific practitioner faces a meaningful relational loss
if they switch clinics, not merely an inconvenience but the loss of a trusted personal
relationship in which they feel genuinely understood. This emotional barrier to switching is
what transforms a satisfied patient into a truly loyal one. The research evidence thus strongly
supports the moderating role of service intimacy in the proposed framework.

DISCUSSION

The synthesis of 19 peer -reviewed studies reveals that customer loyalty in beauty
clinics is the cumulative outcome of an interconnected set of structural relationships rather
than the product of any single driver. The thematic analysis provides clear and consistent
answers to all four research questions.
In response to RQ1, customer loyalty in beauty clinic and aesthetic healthcare settings
is a multidimensional construct that encompasses revisit intention, repurchase behavior,
positive word -of-mouth advocacy, and resistance to switching to competing provi ders
(Oliver, 1999; Bourdeau et al., 2024 ). The reviewed evidence suggests that behavioral
loyalty dimensions such as revisit frequency and recommendation rates are often preceded
by attitudinal loyalty dimensions such as emotional commitment and perceived superiority
of the clinic, which is consistent with Oliver's (1999) four-stage loyalty model.
In response to RQ2, both service quality and customer experience emerge as
significant and complementary antecedents of customer loyalty, operating through two
distinct pathways. The indirect pathway, in which service quality and customer experience
raise satisfaction and satisfaction in turn raises loyalty, is the more consistently confirmed
route across all reviewed contexts. The direct pathway also receives support, particularly for
customer experience, where emotional and sensory dimensions appear to pr oduce loyalty-
relevant affective states that do not require satisfaction as an intermediate step. Customer
satisfaction functions as the central and most powerful mediating mechanism, converting
positive service perceptions and experiential responses into committed loyal behavior. This
finding is consistent with the theoretical expectation of Service Dominant Logic, in which
co-created value accumulates into relational commitment through repeated positive
interactions.
In response to RQ3, service intimacy moderates the satisfaction-to-loyalty relationship
in a theoretically and practically meaningful way. The evidence from beauty service,
banking, and general service contexts consistently shows that the conversion of satisfaction
into loyalty is significantly stronger when patients experience high levels of emotional
closeness and personalized care from their practitioners. The emotional switching cost
mechanism explains this moderating effect: patients with deep relation al bonds with a
specific practitioner must sacrifice not only functional convenience but genuine personal
connection if they switch providers. This makes service intimacy a critical amplifier of
loyalty that operates above and beyond what functional servic e quality or satisfying
experiences alone can produce.
In response to RQ4, the principal theoretical gap identified in the literature is the
absence of an integrated framework that simultaneously models service quality and
customer experience as complementary antecedents, customer satisfaction as the central
mediator, and service intimacy as the relational boundary condition that moderates the
critical satisfaction -to-loyalty conversion. Most existing studies test one or two of these
relationships in isolation, limiting the explanatory power of individual model s and
preventing a coherent understanding of how loyalty is formed in beauty clinic settings. The
proposed integrated moderated mediation framework addresses this gap directly.

CONCLUSION

This systematic literature review synthesized 19 peer-reviewed studies to examine the
structural pathways through which service quality and customer experience influence
customer loyalty in beauty clinics and aesthetic healthcare services, with customer
satisfaction as the central mediating variable and service intimacy as the moderating
boundary condition. The review followed the PRISMA 2020 framework and was grounded
in Service Dominant Logic and Relationship Marketing Theory.
Three principal conclusions emerge from the synthesis. First, service quality and
customer experience are complementary and mutually reinforcing antecedents of customer
loyalty. Service quality provides the functional and professional foundation that patients rely
on, while customer experience adds emotional, sensory, and relational value that elevates
satisfaction and loyalty beyond what functional delivery alone can achieve. Together, these
two variables address the full spectrum of patient needs across the entire service journey.
Second, customer satisfaction is the central mediating mechanism in the customer
loyalty formation process across all reviewed contexts. Improvements in service quality and
customer experience consistently produce stronger loyalty outcomes primarily by fir st
elevating patient satisfaction. The satisfaction -to-loyalty conversion, however, is not

Third, service intimacy is the relational mechanism that closes the gap between
satisfaction and loyalty. When patients develop emotionally close, personalized, and trusting
relationships with specific practitioners through repeated service interactions, the emotional
cost of switching increases substantially. This transforms transient satisfaction into genuine
long-term relational commitment. The moderating effect of service intimacy represents the
most theoretically novel contribution of this review, and it has direct practical implications
for how beauty clinic operators invest in staff development, patient relationship management
systems, and personalized service protocols.
The primary theoretical contribution is the development of an integrated moderated
mediation framework that positions service intimacy as a relational boundary condition
amplifying the satisfaction -to-loyalty conversion within a unified model. This framewo rk

coherent, testable architecture for future empirical research. Scholars are encouraged to

technical service quality, rich and personalized customer experiences, and the development
of deep patient -practitioner intimacy as the three interconnected pillar s of a sustainable
loyalty strategy.
