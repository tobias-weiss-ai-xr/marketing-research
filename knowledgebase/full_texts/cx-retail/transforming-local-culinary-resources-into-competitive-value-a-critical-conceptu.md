ABSTRACT: The growing strategic role of local cuisine in hospitality requires an understanding of competitiveness that extends
beyond food attributes alone. Although existing research has extensively examined Food Quality, Authenticity, Service Qu ality,
Customer Experience, and Customer Loyalty, these perspectives remain conceptually fragmented in explaining how local culinary
resources acquire competitive significance from the customer’s perspective. This critical literature review aims to integra te these
perspectives and develop a coherent conceptual explanation of the transformation of local culinary resources into customer -
perceived competitive value. The review employed a Critical/Conceptual Literature Review approach based on purposive and
concept-driven literat ure identification, critical analysis, thematic synthesis, and conceptual integration. The synthesis indicates
that Food Quality, Authenticity, and Service Quality represent complementary sources of culinary value whose strategic
significance is realized through their integration within Customer Experience. Customer Experience functions as the central value -
transformation process, while Customer Loyalty represents the continuity of experienced value within customer relationships.
Building on this synthesis,  the review develops the Value Transformation Perspective, through which Local Menu
Competitiveness is conceptually expressed in Value Superiority, Differentiation Advantage, Market Preference, and Competitive
Sustainability. These manif estations are conceptual expressions rather than predetermined empirical dimensions. The synthesis
further proposes experiential -relational competitiveness of local menus as its principal conceptual implication, reframing local -
menu competitiveness as cust omer-oriented strat egic value shaped through experience and relational continuity. The proposed
perspective is interpretive rather than deterministic and provides a conceptual basis for future empirical validation across diverse
hospitality settings, customer segments, and local culinary contexts.
KEYWORDS: Local Menu Competitiveness; Food Quality; Authenticity; Service Quality; Customer Experience; Customer
Loyalty.
I. INTRODUCTION
The hospitality industry has increasingly shifted from a predominantly se rvice-based orientation toward an experience -based
economy, changing the basis of value creation from the fulfillment of functional needs to the ability of hospitality provider s to
orchestrate meaningful, memorable, and contextually relevant experiences. T his transformation has repositioned food and
beverage from a supporting function of accommodation into an increasingly strategic component of the hospitality experience.
Food is no longer evaluated solely according to its functional attributes but also acc ording to the sensory, emotional, cultural, and
relational meanings attached to its consumption. Within this changing competitive landscape, local cuisine offers a distincti ve
opportunity because its value may derive from the combination of product quality , cultural identity, authenticity, service
interaction, and the experience created around consumption. From a Resource -Based View (RBV), such resources may constitute
a basis for competitive advantage when they are valuable, distinctive, and difficult for competitors to replicate, particularly when
competitive value extends beyond the physical product to the cultural and experiential resources surrounding it (Barney, 1995 ;
Barney et al., 2023; Porter, 1997).
The growing importance of culinary experience is closely associated with changing tourist and consumer expectations. Local
food increasingly forms part of the motivation for experiencing a destination rather than merely satisfying physiological nee ds.
Bagaihing and Mantolas (2021), for example, highlight  the importance of local culinary experiences in destination -related travel
decisions. This development suggests that local cuisine can function as an experiential resource through which destinations a nd
hospitality businesses communicate identity and crea te differentiation. Experiential marketing and Customer Experience
perspectives further indicate that consumption experiences emerge through the integration of sensory, emotional, cognitive, a nd
relational responses rather than through isolated evaluations  of individual attributes (Schmitt, 1999; Gentile et al., 2007; Verhoef
et al., 2009; Ou et al., 2017). In hospitality settings, therefore, the significance of local food cannot be adequately under stood

The Indonesian hospitality context provides a particularly relevant setting for this discussion because the country's culinar y

within the hospitality offering. Local wisdom may consequently remain a supplementary offering when it is not connected to a
broader hospitality value-creation strategy (Dinihayati et al., 2024).
Existing literature provides substa ntial knowledge concerning the individual components involved in local culinary value
creation. Food Quality research has examined sensory, aesthetic, functional, and health -related evaluations; Authenticity research
has emphasized cultural legitimacy, per ceived genuineness, identity, and meaningfulness; Service Quality research has addressed
reliability, responsiveness, interaction, and the service environment; Customer Experience research has examined holistic sen sory,
emotional, cognitive, and relational  responses; and Customer Loyalty research has considered the continuation of customer
relationships through behavioral, attitudinal, and advocacy responses (Majid et al., 2018; Prayag et al., 2022; Jeaheng et al ., 2020;
Verhoef et al., 2009; Naini et al., 2022). Recent studies further demonstrate the continuing relevance of these perspectives across
hospitality, tourism, culinary experience, authenticity, service quality, and customer loyalty (Ling et al., 2024; Ahmed et a l., 2023;
Cankül et al., 2024; Croitoru et al., 2024; Purnami & Nurcaya, 2025; Hurriyati & Dirgantari, 2026).
However, these literature streams have largely developed around different analytical concerns. Food Quality emphasizes the
value of the culinary product; Authenticity emphasizes cul tural and symbolic meaning; Service Quality emphasizes the conditions
and interactions surrounding service delivery; Customer Experience emphasizes customers' holistic interpretation of consumption;
and Customer Loyalty emphasizes the continuation of custo mer relationships. Each perspective explains an important aspect of
local culinary value, but their combined implications for the competitive significance of local menus remain insufficiently
integrated. The unresolved issue is therefore not simply the abs ence of research on these concepts, but the limited conceptual
integration of how different forms of culinary value can be understood collectively from the customer's perspective.
This gap is particularly relevant because competitive advantage in local cui sine cannot be adequately understood as an
objective characteristic inherent in a dish. Competitive strategy emphasizes value creation and differentiation, while RBV
highlights the  strategic importance of valuable and difficult -to-replicate resources (Port er, 1997; Barney, 1995; Barney et al.,
2023). In local culinary contexts, cultural identity, authenticity, culinary knowledge, local resources, service capabilities , and
experiential capabilities may possess strategic relevance when customers recognize the m as meaningful and distinctive. The
resulting conceptual challenge is to clarify how these different forms of value can be considered together in explaining the
competitive significance of local menus without reducing the explanation to any single culinar y or service attribute.
Against this background, this critical literature review aims to interpretively synthesize fragmented hospitality and tourism
literature concerning Food Quality, Authenticity, Service Quality, Customer Experience, Customer Loyalty, and Local Menu
Competitiveness. The review does not seek to establish or test causal relationships among predefined variables. Instead, it
critically examines the complementary explanatory roles of these perspectives and identifies the conceptual connectio ns through
which functional, cultural, interactional, experiential, and relational forms of value can be understood across the customer journey.
The ultimate objective is to develop a theoretically coherent explanation of how local culinary resources can a cquire customer -
perceived strategic significance within hospitality.
The review therefore addresses the following central question: How can existing hospitality and tourism literature be
conceptually integrated to explain the transformation of local culina ry resources into customer -perceived competitive value?
Addressing th is question provides a basis for reconsidering local cuisine not merely as an additional menu category, but as a
potentially strategic hospitality resource whose competitive significance depends on the ways in which culinary, cultural, service,
experiential, and relational value are connected.

II. LITERATURE REVIEW
A. Food Quality as a Foundation of Culinary Value
Food Quality represents a fundamental component of culinary value because customers  initially encounter a local menu
through the characteristics of the food itself. Conventional approaches emphasize sensory properties such as taste, aroma, te xture,
freshness, temperature, and presentation. Contemporary hospitality and tourism literature,  however, indicates that customers
evaluate food through broader forms of consumption value. Consumption Value Theory recognizes functional, emotional, and
epistemic dimensions in consumer evaluation, while the Experience Economy perspective emphasizes the  sensory, emotional, and
symbolic dimensions of consumption (Liu & Tse, 2018; Liu et al., 2019; Kukanja et al., 2017).
Food Quality can consequently be understood across several interconnected domains. Sensory -hedonic quality concerns taste,
aroma, texture , freshness, and the pleasure derived from consumption. Aesthetic -symbolic quality concerns visual presentation
and the meanings communicated through the appearance of food. Functional -utilitarian value concerns portion, variety, price, and
value for money. Health-related value concerns perceptions of nutritional benefits and well -being (Zhang et al., 2019; Majid et al.,
2018; Brewer & Sebby, 2021; Kim et al., 2013; Faulkner et al., 2014). Recent evidence continues to demonstrate the relevance o f
Food Quality to customer evaluations in hospitality and culinary settings (Ling et al., 2024).
The literature therefore positions Food Quality as an important foundation of culinary value and customer acceptance. At the
same time, quality should not be treated as sy nonymous with competitive distinctiveness. Taste, presentation, portion, freshness,
and other technical attributes can potentially be reproduced by competing establishments. Food Quality consequently remains
essential to the value of a local menu, while di fferentiation may also depend on characteristics that connect the culinary offering
with place, identity, and customer interpretation.
B. Authenticity as Cultural and Symbolic Value
Authenticity introduces a form of culinary value that is closely connected to  place, identity, tradition, and cultural meaning.
Contemporary authenticity literature has moved beyond a strictly objective understanding toward a multidimensional perspectiv e
encompassing objective-cultural, constructive or perceived, and existential fo rms of authenticity (Zhang et al., 2019; Prayag et al.,
2022; Hsu et al., 2021; Eck et al., 2026).
Objective-cultural authenticity concerns the relationship between a culinary offering and its traditional recipes, ingredients,
preparation methods, cultural  representation, and continuity of identity. Constructed or perceived authenticity concerns whether
customers recognize an offering as genuine, original, credible, and appropriately representative of a particular place or cul ture.
Existential authenticity refers to emotional engagement, self -connection, and the personal meaningfulness generated through
consumption. These perspectives demonstrate that authenticity is simultaneously related to the culinary product, cultural
representation, and individual experience.
The interpretive nature of authenticity is particularly important for local cuisine. Cultural identity cannot simply be place d on a
menu and assumed to generate value. Customers must be able to recognize and interpret the cultural significance repr esented by
the offering. Cultural storytelling can therefore function as a reinforcing mechanism through which the origins of a dish, lo cal
ingredients, preparation traditions, cultural values, and relationships with local communities are communicated (Che ng, 2023;
Jantsch et al., 2024; Kaur, 2022). Storytelling is not a separate form of authenticity; rather, it can strengthen the interpr etation of
authenticity by connecting culinary attributes with cultural meaning. Through such interpretation, food may be come associated
with identity, memory, place attachment, and experiential value (Prayag et al., 2022; Leksana et al., 2025; Cankül et al., 20 24).
The literature also reveals a tension between cultural continuity and contemporary relevance. Excessive emphas is on
preserving traditional characteristics may constrain adaptation to contemporary customer preferences, whereas excessive
modification may wea ken perceptions of cultural legitimacy. Perceived authenticity therefore depends not only on historical
fidelity but also on how a dish is presented, explained, served, and experienced in a contemporary hospitality setting (Hsu et al.,
2021; Eck et al., 2026; Leksana et al., 2025; Bakr et al., 2025). Authenticity consequently involves a balance between mainta ining
recognizable cultural identity and remaining meaningful within contemporary consumption contexts.
C. Service Quality as Interactional and Experiential Infrastructure
Service Quality provides the interactional and environmental context within which culinary o fferings are encountered.
Traditional service -quality research, particularly the SERVQUAL perspective, conceptualizes quality through reliability,
responsiveness, assurance, empathy, and tangibles. Contemporary service perspectives broaden this understandi ng by emphasizing
interaction, resource integration, and value creation within particular consumption contexts.
Service-Dominant Logic (SDL) conceptualizes value as emerging through resource integration, use, and interaction rather than
being embedded excl usively in a provider's output (Vargo & Lusch, 2008; Vargo et al., 2020; Nariswari & Vargo, 2024). In
hospitality, this perspective suggests that the value of a local culinary product may be influenced by the manner in which
employees communicate, respond, personalize service, resolve problems, and create an appropriate physical environment. Service

experienced differently depending on the reliability, responsiveness,  communication, and interpersonal quality of the surrounding
service.
D. Customer Experience as Holistic Consumption Experience
Customer Experience has developed from a predominantly transactional concept toward a holistic and dynamic perspective
encompassing the customer journey (Verhoef et al., 2009). Hospitality experiences emerge through interactions with products,
employees, physical environments, social relationships, and cultural contexts (Schmitt, 1999; Gentile et al., 2007; Verhoef et al.,
2009). Rece nt hospitality research similarly positions Customer Experience as an important perspective for understanding
customer responses within hospitality encounters (Veloso & Gomez-Suarez, 2023; Kacprzak & Hensel, 2023).
From an SDL perspective, experience also involves customer participation in value creation through the integration of
available resources with personal knowledge, expectations, emotions, and social context (Vargo & Lusch, 2008; Vargo et al.,
2020). Consequently, the same local menu may be experie nced differently by different customers depending on how they perceive
its quality, authenticity, presentation, service encounter, cultural relevance, and surrounding atmosphere.
Customer Experience can be understood through interconnected sensory, emotion al, cognitive, and relational dimensions.
Sensory experience emerges from taste, aroma, texture, and visual appearance; emotional experience encompasses pleasure,
comfort, excitement, and affective responses; cognitive experience involves interpretation, e valuation, expectations, and perceived
value; and relational experience connects consumption with identity, lifestyle, social relationships, and cultural meaning (Z hang et
al., 2019; Kukanja et al., 2017; Ling et al., 2024; Prayag et al., 2022; Hsu et al., 2021).
The holistic character of Customer Experience is particularly relevant to local cuisine because customers encounter food
attributes, cultural meanings, service interactions, and environmental conditions within the same consumption context. Their
responses therefore cannot always be understood by examining each element independently. Stimulus –Organism–Response
reasoning provides a supporting perspective by suggesting that environmental and consumption stimuli are internally processed
before generatin g behavioral responses (Mehrabian & Russell, 1974). Within hospitality, culinary characteristics, cultural cues,
service encounters, and environmental conditions may contribute to customers' experiential evaluations, which can be reflecte d in
satisfaction, emotional attachment, revisit intention, recommendation, willingness to pay, and other post -consumption responses
(Cetin & Dincer, 2014; Veloso & Gomez-Suarez, 2023; Kacprzak & Hensel, 2023; Croitoru et al., 2024).
The literature therefore positions Customer Experience as a holistic customer-level perspective for understanding how multiple
elements of a hospitality encounter are perceived together. Its importance for local cuisine lies in its capacity to account for the
fact that culinary quality, cultural  meaning, service interaction, and consumption context may acquire significance through their
combined interpretation rather than through isolated attributes.
E. Customer Loyalty as Relational Continuity
Customer Loyalty has increasingly moved beyond a narrow  behavioral definition based on repeat purchase or revisit toward a
multidimensional understanding encompassing behavioral, attitudinal, and advocacy dimensions (Naini et al., 2022; Pekovic &
Rolland, 2020; Purnami & Nurcaya, 2025; Hurriyati & Dirgantari, 2026). Behavioral loyalty concerns repeat consumption and
revisitation; attitudinal loyalty reflects preference, attachment, and psychological commitment; while advocacy loyalty is
manifested through recommendations and positive word of mouth.
This distinc tion is important in hospitality because repeat behavior alone does not necessarily indicate strong relational
attachment. Customers may return because of convenience, location, price, or limited alternatives, whereas stronger forms of
loyalty involve more durable preference and willingness to maintain a relationship with the hospitality provider.
The literature associates perceived value, gastronomic experience, product quality, service quality, and authenticity with
customer responses extending beyond imm ediate satisfaction toward continued patronage and advocacy (Rafdinal & Suhartanto,
2020; Croitoru et al., 2024; Cankül et al., 2024; Majid et al., 2018). Customer Experience and Customer Loyalty nevertheless

F. Customer Relevance and Culinary Adaptability
Customer preference provides an important contextual consideration because local cuisine is encountered by customers with
heterogeneous expectations, cultural backgrounds, motivations, and consumption preferences. Preference to ward local food is not
determined exclusively by taste but may also involve cognitive, affective, demographic, cultural, and psychographic
considerations. Customer evaluations may encompass visual presentation, ingredient quality, cultural storytelling, se rvice context,
and consumption atmosphere (Anam, 2024; Gunawan et al., 2024).
Such heterogeneity creates a tension between cultural specificity and contemporary relevance. A local menu may preserve
recognizable cultural characteristics while requiring adap tation in presentation, communication, service processes, or value
proposition to remain attractive to different customer groups (Ahani, 2019; Oktaviana et al., 2024; Baek et al., 2020). The
objective is not necessarily to remove cultural specificity but t o ensure that distinctive characteristics remain accessible and
meaningful within changing consumption contexts.
Culinary innovation can contribute to this adaptability. The Diffusion of Innovations perspective identifies relative advanta ge,
compatibility, and perceived value as important considerations in the adoption of innovations (Rogers, 2003). Applied to local
culinary offerings, innovation may involve redesigning presentation, communicating cultural meaning, adapting service process es,
improving valu e for money, strengthening local sourcing, or incorporating sustainability while retaining recognizable cultural
characteristics. Recent hospitality and culinary research similarly highlights innovation as relevant to the competitiveness and
sustainability of local food offerings (Firmansyah et al., 2025; Mau et al., 2024).
Thus, innovation is best understood here as a supporting contextual consideration rather than as a separate core component of
local culinary value. Its significance lies in the ability t o maintain the relevance of culturally distinctive offerings without
necessarily compromising their recognizable identity.
G. Local Menu Competitiveness
Local Menu Competitiveness refers to the competitive significance of a local culinary offering in relation  to available
alternatives. It is more appropriately understood as a customer -based form of competitive advantage than as an objective
characteristic inherent in a dish. Competitive strategy emphasizes value creation and differentiation as fundamental sour ces of
competitive advantage, while RBV emphasizes the strategic significance of resources that are valuable and difficult to replic ate
(Porter, 1997; Barney, 1995; Barney et al., 2023).
In the culinary sector, competitiveness may be influenced by quality,  innovation, market conditions, and consumer response
(Hamulczuk & Pawlak, 2022; Abdumuminov, 2021). Local identity and authenticity may provide additional differentiation when
customers recognize them as meaningful and distinctive (Shahrin et al., 2025; E ck et al., 2026). Recent work on food and culinary
competitiveness likewise emphasizes differentiation, innovation, sustainability, and market relevance (Fathiyah & Nuvriasari,
2024; Seo & Lee, 2025; Shahrin et al., 2025).
From this perspective, the compet itive significance of a local menu cannot be inferred solely from the existence of cultural
characteristics or technical product quality. It must be considered in relation to the value customers perceive, the distinct iveness
they recognize, the preferences  they develop, and the continued relevance of the offering in relation to alternatives. These
considerations establish the conceptual boundaries of Local Menu Competitiveness without presupposing a fixed set of empirica l
dimensions.
H. Theoretical Foundations and Complementary Perspectives
Three theoretical perspectives provide the principal foundation for interpreting the literature on local culinary value. Serv ice-
Dominant Logic provides the value -creation perspective by emphasizing resource integration, use , and interaction. Customer
Experience and Experience Economy perspectives provide the experiential perspective by emphasizing holistic sensory,
emotional, cognitive, and relational engagement across the consumption context. Resource -Based View provides th e strategic
perspective by explaining why valuable, distinctive, and difficult -to-replicate culinary, cultural, and organizational resources may
acquire competitive significance (Vargo & Lusch, 2008; Vargo et al., 2020; Verhoef et al., 2009; Barney, 1995; Barney et al.,
2023).

III.  METHODOLOGY
I. Research Approach
This study employs a Critical/Conceptual Literature Review approach. This approa ch is used to identify, evaluate, compare,
and synthesize the literature in order to develop a more coherent conceptual understanding of a phenomenon (Snyder, 2019).
Unlike a systematic review, which is oriented toward the comprehensive and protocol -based search and synthesis of evidence, a
critical review emphasizes critical analysis, synthesis, and the development of conceptual understanding (Grant & Booth, 2009 ).
This approach also follows the principles of an integrative literature review, which integra tes various bodies of thought and
research findings to generate a broader conceptual interpretation (Torraco, 2005).
J. Literature Identification and Selection
The literature was identified purposively and concept -driven, based on its direct relevance to the focus of the review.
Literature identification was directed toward the key concepts that constitute the focus of the analysis, namely food quality ,
authenticity, service quality, customer experience, customer loyalty, and local menu competitiveness. The su bsequent selection of
sources considered conceptual relevance, theoretical contribution, academic quality, and relevance to the analytical focus.
The literature includes foundational scholarly works to establish the conceptual foundation, as well as more r ecent peer -
reviewed studies to enrich, develop, and critically examine existing understanding. Literature was retained when it made a cl ear
contribution to conceptual understanding, the development of theoretical perspectives, relevant findings, or critica l interpretations
of the issues under review. Sources lacking substantive relevance to the focus of the review were excluded from the synthesis .
Accordingly, literature selection prioritized relevance and conceptual contribution to the objectives of the re view, consistent with
the characteristics of a critical and integrative review (Grant & Booth, 2009; Torraco, 2005).
K. Analytical Procedure
The analysis was conducted through three main stages:
1. Critical Analysis
The literature was critically analyzed to iden tify key concepts, assumptions, similarities, differences, interrelationships, and
limitations in the arguments developed in previous research (Grant & Booth, 2009).
2. Thematic Synthesis
Conceptual findings from different sources were grouped according to th emes with substantive relevance. The synthesis was
conducted thematically to identify patterns, convergences, differences, and relationships among ideas, rather than presenting
the literature on an article-by-article basis (Snyder, 2019; Torraco, 2005).
3. Conceptual Integration
The identified themes were subsequently integrated through critical comparison and theoretical interpretation to produce a
coherent conceptual understanding. This process enabled relevant perspectives to be reorganized into an integrat ed
conceptual synthesis (Torraco, 2005).
L. Rigor and Transparency
The rigor of the review was maintained through transparency in literature selection, consistency in analysis, traceability
between the literature and the synthesis, and coherence in conceptual  integration. The key concepts used in the review were
grounded in relevant literature and assessed based on their contribution to the conceptual argument. The analysis also considered
the alignment, differences, and limitations of perspectives identified in the literature so that the synthesis did not remain at the
level of descriptive summary (Snyder, 2019; Grant & Booth, 2009).

IV.  RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
A. Critical Synthesis of the Literature
The critical synthesis indicates that local menu competitiveness in the hospitality context cannot be explained solely throug h
the superiority of food attributes. Recent literature shows that gastronomic value is formed through the interconnectedness o f
product quality, cultural authenticity, service quality, consumption experience, and customer relationships. Studies on food qu ality
demonstrate its association with customer satisfaction and responses, while research on gastronomic experiences indicates that
consumption value develops through a combination of experience, perceived value, satisfaction, and loyalty (Ling et al., 2024 ;
Cankül et al., 2024; Rahmayoga & Wiwoho, 2025). However, the literature also indicates that these elements do not perform
identical functions. The value of a menu is not determined solely by the presence of superior attributes, but by how product
attributes, cultural meaning, and service interactions are integrated within the consumption experience. Accordingly, a local  menu
needs to be understood not merely as a food product, but as part of a value -creation system involving customers, service, the
consumption environment, and cultural context.
The synthesis indicates that these elements have different but complementary value -creation functions. Food Quality provides
the foundation of product value through taste, freshness, ingredient quality, and presentation quality. Recent research
demonstrates that food quality remains relevant to customer evaluations and responses, but its s trategic strength does not always
operate independently and may depend on the value and experience accompanying consumption (Ling et al., 2024; Siregar et al.,
2023; Rahmayoga & Wiwoho, 2025). Therefore, Food Quality is more appropriately understood as a p roduct-value foundation
that provides value potential. This position does not diminish the importance of food quality but places it proportionately w ithin
the value -creation process: quality provides the basis for acceptance and the formation of product va lue, while competitive
differentiation requires a broader configuration of value.
Authenticity serves a different function because it provides cultural and symbolic content that enables local menus to repres ent
the identity, traditions, history, and charac ter of a place. Recent research indicates that food authenticity is associated with
perceived value, purchase intention, authentic experience, and customer loyalty ( Shin et al., 2026). Research on gastronomic
experiences also indicates that the value of au thenticity becomes more meaningful when customers can connect f ood with cultural
meanings and personally relevant experiences (Cankül et al., 2024; Belly et al., 2024). Thus, authenticity should not be unde rstood
merely as the presence of traditional eleme nts in a menu. Its value depends on how cultural identity is recognized, interpreted, and
presented in a manner that is relevant to the consumption experience. This involves an important conceptual tension: the stro nger
the effort to preserve local identit y, the more important it becomes for hospitality to translate t hat identity into a form of
experience that customers can understand without losing its cultural meaning.
Meanwhile, Service Quality provides the relational dimension of value creation. Recent literature indicates that service quality
is associated with satisfaction, perceived value, and loyalty in restaurant and hotel contexts (Ahmed et al., 2023; Gulam et al.,
2023; Pandiangan et al., 2024; Soerjanto et al., 2024). In hotel restaurant contexts , service quality is not limited to the accuracy of
service delivery but also encompasses interaction, responsiveness, environmental comfort, and staff members' ability to creat e a
convincing experience. This indicates that service is not merely a compleme nt to the food product. Rather, service provides the
interactional context through which product quality and cultural meaning can be received, interpreted, and experienc ed by
customers more fully.
Accordingly, the literature reveals a consistent pattern wh ile also establishing limitations on approaches that focus excessively
on a single attribute. Food Quality primarily provides product value, Authenticity enriches cultural and symbolic value, whil e
Service Quality provides the context for interaction. Conc eptually, none of these three elements alone is sufficient to explain the
entire process through which local menu competitiveness is formed. The limitations of an attribute -based approach create the need
to explain how different value resources are transfo rmed into customer experience and subsequently sustained within customer
relationships.

B. Customer Experience as the Central Value-Transformation Process
The synthesis of the literature positions Customer Experience as the central value -transformation proces s. Literature on
customer experience in hospitality indicates that experience is multidimensional, dynamic, subjective, and develops throughou t
the customer journey (Becker & Jaakkola, 2020; Kacprzak & Hensel, 2023; Veloso & Gomez -Suarez, 2023). In the gas tronomic
context, experience is also associated with restaurant image, customer perceived value, customer satisfaction, and customer
loyalty, indicating that food consumption cannot be separated from the overall experience surrounding it (Cankül et al., 20 24).
This central position is important because the literature on Food Quality, Authenticity, and Service Quality essentially
addresses different sources of value, whereas the Customer Experience literature explains how these sources come together wit hin
a single consumption process experienced by customers. Developments in customer experience research in the hotel industry
further reinforce the position of experience as a space in which multiple touchpoints, social interactions, the service envir onment,
and customer engagement are integrated (Veloso & Gomez -Suarez, 2023; Kacprzak & Hensel, 2023). Therefore, Food Quality,
Authenticity, and Service Quality do not need to be viewed as independent sources of value. Food Quality provides product
quality, Authenticity provides cultural meaning, while Service Quality provides the context for interaction. Their strategic value
develops when customers integrate these elements into a coherent gastronomic experience.
This perspective also helps explain why good product  quality is not always identical to the strongest experience. Recent
research indicates that gastronomic experiences can shape perceived value, satisfaction, and loyalty through a broader
configuration of experience than food attributes alone (Cankül et al ., 2024; Belly et al., 2024). In the hotel context, customer
experience is also influenced by interactions with staff, atmosphere, the service environment, and the overall customer journ ey
(Veloso & Gomez-Suarez, 2023). Thus, the issue is not to contrast p roduct quality with experience, but to understand that product
quality acquires different strategic value when situated within a broader experience. Customer Experience becomes the concept ual
space in which product attributes, cultural content, and interaction quality acquire broader meaning for customers.
From the perspective of Service -Dominant Logic, this process can be understood as the integration of resources within value -
in-use. Developments in SDL increasingly emphasize human -centered value creation , relationships, interactions, and context of
use (Vargo et al., 2020; Nariswari & Vargo, 2024). Accordingly, Customer Experience is more appropriately understood as a
value-transformation process rather than merely an evaluation of a product or service. E xperience represents the point that
connects value r esources with relational and strategic consequences. In other words, gastronomic resources do not automatically
carry their full competitive value; part of that value is formed when customers experience t hem and assign meaning to the
combination of product, culture, service, and consumption context.
C. Customer Loyalty as Value Retention and Relational Continuity
The subsequent synthesis indicates that customer experience acquires relational consequences when  the experience is
perceived as valuable, relevant, and meaningful. Recent literature shows that Customer Loyalty develops as a multidimensional
construct enco mpassing repeat purchase behavior, preference, attachment, recommendation, and continuity of the customer
relationship (Croitoru et al., 2024; Purnami & Nurcaya, 2025; Hurriyati & Dirgantari, 2026). In the restaurant context, the
relationship among perceived value, gastronomic experience, and loyalty increasingly indicates that customers maintain
relationships when the value received is perceived as relevant and worth continuing (Croitoru et al., 2024; Cankül et al., 2024).
On this basis, Customer Loyalty can be understood as value retention, referring to the continuity of experiential value withi n
the customer relationship after the consumption episode has taken place. Loyalty does not end with the act of returning but may
develop into preference, recommendation, advocacy, and a tendency to maintain the relationship. Recent research on restaurant
loyalty also indicates the importance of the combination of value equity, brand equity, and relationship equity in building more
sustainable customer relationships (Hurriyati & Dirgantari, 2026). This understanding extends the meaning of loyalty from an
immediate behavioral response to a form of value continuity with a relational dimension.
This perspective indicates that loyalty extends beyond a mere behavioral outcome. Loyalty represents a relational
manifestation of experienced value because value experienced  by customers is retained through preference, repeat visits,
recommendations, and connectedness with the service provider. Purnami and Nurcaya (2025) position loyalty as a strategic issue
shaped by various drivers of experience and value, while Hurriyati a nd Dirgantari (2026) demonstrate the importance of
relationship equity in explaining restaurant customer loyalty.
The distinction between experience and loyalty is important in this synthesis. Customer Experience represents the process
through which custom ers experience, interpret, and integrate various sources of value, whereas Customer Loyalty represents the
continuity of that value within the customer relationship. Thus, loyalty is not simply a repetition of the same experience bu t an
indication that the  value formed through experience has acquired relational relevance. This perspective enables the relationship
between experience and competitiveness to be understood through the continuity of customer relationships, rather than through
the assumption that every positive experience automatically produces loyalty.

D. From Customer Value to Local Menu Competitiveness
Based on the cross -literature synthesis, local menu competitiveness needs to be understood from the perspective of customer -
validated competitiveness. Competitiveness is determined not only by the quality or uniqueness of a menu but also by the extent
to w hich the value offered gains customer acceptance, preference, and sustained support. Recent research on heritage food
competitiveness emphasizes the  relationship between cultural value, sustainability, and the competitive position of local food
(Shahrin et al., 2025). Literature on competitive advantage in culinary businesses also indicates that product and service qu ality
can contribute to competitiv e advantage, while competitive strength remains related to how these elements are perceived and
evaluated in the market (Fathiyah & Nuvriasari, 2024).
Within this framework, loyalty functions as a conceptual link between individual experience and market co nsequences. Repeat
choice, recommendations, preference, and advocacy provide more tangible expressions of customer acceptance. Literature on
restaurant loyalty indicates relationships among perceived value, gastronomic experience, and the continuity of cus tomer
relationships (Croitoru et al., 2024; Cankül et al., 2024; Hurriyati & Dirgantari, 2026). Loyalty can therefore be understood  as a
form of relational value that helps retain the value formed through the consumption experience and extend it into the c ustomer
relationship.
The Resource-Based View reinforces the understanding that competitive advantage is not derived solely from physical assets
but also from configurations of valuable and difficult -to-replicate resources. Recent discussions of RBV and co mpetitive
advantage emphasize the importance of understanding how resources are configured into advantage rather than merely identifyin g
the existence of a resource (Barney et al., 2023). In local gastronomy, cultural identity, interaction quality, context ual experience,
and customer relationships can form a more complex value configuration than standalone product attributes.
On the basis of this synthesis, Local Menu Competitiveness can be conceptually formulated through four interrelated
manifestations: Value Superiority, Differentiation Advantage, Market Preference, and Competitive Sustainability. These are not
dimensions treated as established empirical constructs but rather conceptual manifestations formulated through the integration of
the literature. Value Superiority represents the ability of a local menu to offer value perceived as more meaningful or more
valuable than available alternatives. Differentiation Advantage refers to the ability to present distinctive characteristics through a
combination of quality, cultural identity, service, and experience. Market Preference is reflected in customer acceptance, choice,
and tendency to maintain a preference for the menu. Meanwhile, Competitive Sustainability indicates the ability to maintain
relevance and competitive value through customer relationships and the sustainable management of gastronomic resources.
These four manifestations demonstrate that competitiveness is not merely a matter of possessing a differentiated product, but
the ability to transfor m that difference into value recognized by customers and capable of being sustained within market
relationships. Accordingly, local menu competitiveness can be understood as a strategic consequence of customer -recognized
value, rather than as an objective characteristic automatically inherent in a food offering.

F. Experiential-Relational Competitiveness of Local Menus
The final synthesis gives rise to the concept of experiential -relational competitiveness, defined here as a conceptual
proposition in which the competitive significance of local menu s is understood through the strategic role of customer experience
and relationship continuity in sustaining competitive value. The concept emerges from the integration of the literature rather than
from a previously established construct or category of com petitiveness. Gastronomic research indicates that culinary experiences
are associated with perceived value, restaurant image, satisfaction, loyalty, and customer behavior (Cankül et al., 2024), wh ile
research on local food competitiveness increasingly conn ects heritage identity, sustainability, and market strength (Shahrin et al .,
2025).
This perspective extends local -menu competitiveness beyond product -based differentiation. Food Quality remains an essential
foundation of menu acceptance, but its competiti ve significance is not exhausted by product attributes alone. Similarly, cultural
authenticity and service interaction contribute to the distinctiveness of a local offering by shaping how its value is encounte red
within the hospitality context. Competitive  significance therefore depends not only on the attributes a local menu possesses but
also on the value customers recognize in experiencing those attributes.
The central implication is that local culinary resources acquire stronger competitive significance  when their distinctive value
becomes meaningful to customers and remains relevant within customer relationships. In this sense, experiential -relational
competitiveness does not constitute a new empirical construct or an additional stage in the value trans formation chain. Rather, it
characterizes the form of competitiveness that emerges when the strategic significance of local culinary resources is recogni zed
through customer experience and sustained through customer relationships.
Accordingly, experiential-relational competitiveness represents the principal conceptual implication of the synthesis developed
in this review. It reframes local -menu competitiveness as a customer -oriented strategic condition, in which culinary resources
become competitively meani ngful not simply because of their inherent attributes or cultural distinctiveness, but because their
value is recognized through experience and sustained within customer relationships. Local gastronomy can consequently be
understood not merely as a menu of fering, but as a potential source of competitive value grounded in experience and relational
continuity.

V. CONCLUSIONS
This critical literature review concludes that local menu competitiveness in the hospitality context is better understood as a
customer-validated form of strategic value rather than as an inherent property of food quality, cultural distinctiveness, or any
individual menu attribute. The critical synthesis shows that the competitive significance of local cuisine depends on the ext ent to
which its distinctive culinary resources become meaningful and valuable from the customer’s perspective. This understanding
shifts attention from the mere possession of local culinary resources toward their capacity to generate recognized value with in
hospitality consumption and to remain relevant in relation to available alternatives.
The review contributes the Value Transformation Perspective as an interpretive conceptual synthesis that brings together
Service-Dominant Logic, Customer Experience and Experience Economy perspectives, and the Resource -Based View to explain
the strategic significance of local culinary resources. Within this perspective, Local Menu Competitiveness is conceptually
manifested through Value Superiority, Differentiation Advantage, Market  Preference, and Competitive Sustainability. These
manifestations provide co mplementary expressions of customer -validated competitiveness rather than predetermined empirical
dimensions, emphasizing that competitive value becomes meaningful when distinctive  culinary resources achieve recognition,
preference, and continuity within market relationships.
The principal conceptual implication is the proposition of experiential -relational competitiveness of local menus, which
extends the understanding of culinary competitiveness beyond product -based differentiation. This perspective positions local
cuisine as a potential strategic hospitality resource whose competitive significance is strengthened when distinctive culinar y value
acquires customer relevance and is s ustained through relational continuity. The proposed perspective remains interpretive rather
than deterministic, and its conceptual connections may vary according to customer characteristics, destination context, service
form, and the context in which valu e is constructed. Future empirical research should therefore examine and validate the proposed
perspective across different hospitality settings, customer segments, and local culinary contexts.
