ABSTRACT
This study examined the perception and use of digital technologies in fresh tomato marketing in Jalingo  Local
Government Area of Taraba State, Nigeria. Specifically, it described the socio -economic characteristics of tomato
marketers, identified the digital technologies used in marketing activities, examined marketers' perceptions towards
digital technology  adoption, and tested whether perception differed among retailers, wholesalers, and marketers
engaged in both wholesale and retail activities. A multistage sampling technique was employed to select 174 tomato
marketers from a population of 307 marketers ac ross eight major tomato markets. Primary data were collected using
questionnaires and analysed using descriptive statistics, a five -point Likert scale, one -way Analysis of Variance
(ANOVA), and Tukey's Honestly Significant Difference (HSD) post hoc test. T he findings revealed that retailers earned
an average monthly income of ₦165,800, compared with ₦278,600 for wholesalers and ₦316,400 for marketers engaged
in both wholesale and retail activities, while access to credit remained relatively low, particularl y among retailers
(31.7%). Mobile phone calls (94.3%), WhatsApp (82.8%), and mobile money/bank transfer services were the most
widely used digital technologies, whereas digital record -keeping (37.4%), social media advertising (29.9%), and e -
commerce platforms (16.7%) recorded low adoption rates. Tomato marketers generally had a positive perception of
digital technology adoption, with an overall grand mean of 4.04. Respondents agreed that digital technologies improve
business transactions (4.19), communicati on (4.16), access to market information (4.14), and sales and profitability
(4.07), but perceived digital technologies to be relatively unaffordable (3.01) and internet services unreliable (2.93).
The ANOVA results showed a significant difference in percep tion among the three categories of marketers (F = 12.864,
p < 0.001), with Tukey HSD indicating that retailers differed significantly from wholesalers and marketers engaged in
both wholesale and retail activities. The study concluded that although basic di gital technologies are widely adopted,
advanced digital marketing tools remain underutilised due to financial and infrastructural constraints. The study
recommends strengthening digital capacity -building programmes, improving access to affordable credit fo r digital
technology acquisition, and enhancing internet infrastructure in major tomato markets.

1 INTRODUCTION
Tomato ( Solanum lycopersicum ), the second largest fruit vegetable, is one of the most important horticultural crops
cultivated in Nigeria and serves as a major source of income for dry-season farmers. It is widely regarded as a "poverty
reduction crop" because of its contribution to household income, employment generation, food security, and nutrition
(Oyesuntimi, 2023). Tomato is also valued for its high content of vitamins, minerals, antioxidants, and bioactive
compounds such as lycopene, which promote human health (Ali et al., 2020). Beyond its nutritional importance, tomato
production and marketing provide livelihoods for millions of farmers, traders, transporters, processors, and other
actors along the value chain (Thompson et al., 2018). However, the crop is highly perishable, with a shelf life of only four
to seven days under normal storage conditions, making efficient marketing essential to minimise losses.
Nigeria is one of the largest producers of tomatoes in Africa and ranks among the leading producers globally. According
to the Food and Agriculture Organization Corporate Statistical Database (FAOSTAT), Nigeria produced approximately
3.73 million metric to nnes of tomatoes from about 824,000 hectares of cultivated land in 2024. Despite this high
production, productivity remains constrained by poor-quality seeds, pest and disease infestations, inadequate irrigation
facilities, and inefficient post -harvest handling practices (FAOSTAT, 2024; Food and Agriculture Organization [FAO],
2025). Tomatoes are among the most widely consumed vegetables in Nigeria, with annual domestic consumption
estimated at about 2.3 million metric tonnes, driven by rapid population gro wth, urbanisation, and increasing demand
for fresh and processed tomato products (Yamauchi et al., 2024).
Tomato marketing plays a vital role in food supply, employment creation, and income generation in Nigeria. However,
the marketing system is characterised by perishability, seasonal price fluctuations, weak market organisation, and
inefficient supply chain management (Uzomo et al., 2026). During peak production periods, excess supply often results
in market glut, low prices, and high wastage, whereas scarcity during the off -season leads to sharp price increases. In
addition, tomato marketing is constrained b y poor transportation systems, inadequate storage facilities, lack of
standard grading and packaging, weak market information systems, and the dominance of intermediaries in price
determination (Adeoye et al., 2019; Osunmakinde et al., 2024).
Post-harvest losses remain one of the greatest challenges facing the Nigerian tomato value chain. The Food and
Agriculture Organization estimated that about 50% of fresh tomatoes produced in Nigeria are lost before reaching
consumers because of poor harves ting practices, inadequate packaging, poor transportation, insufficient cold storage
facilities, and weak market linkages (Yamauchi et al., 2024). Similarly, the Federal Ministry of Agriculture and Food
Security reported that Nigeria loses approximately 45 % of its annual tomato harvest, equivalent to about 1.76 million
metric tonnes, due to spoilage and supply chain inefficiencies (Federal Ministry of Agriculture and Food Security, 2025).
These losses reduce farmers' and marketers' incomes, increase seasonal price fluctuations, limit the availability of fresh
tomatoes, and increase dependence on imported processed tomato products. Consequently, improving tomato
marketing through better information flow, market coordination, transportation, storage, and digit al innovations has
become increasingly important (Yami et al., 2025).
Recent advances in digital technologies present new opportunities for improving agricultural marketing systems.
Digital technologies, including mobile phones, internet services, social media platforms, electronic payment systems, e-
commerce platforms, and digital record-keeping applications, facilitate communication, access to market information,
financial transactions, and business management (Ajibola et al., 2025). These technologies enable producers and
marketers to communicate directly with buyers and s uppliers, advertise products, obtain current market information,
coordinate logistics, receive electronic payments, and reduce transaction costs. The rapid growth in mobile phone
ownership and internet access across developing countries has further enhance d opportunities for digital
transformation in agricultural value chains.
Digital technologies also have considerable potential to reduce post -harvest losses by improving market coordination
and facilitating faster movement of produce from producers to consumers. Through mobile applications, WhatsApp,
Facebook, e-commerce platforms, and digital payment systems, marketers can identify buyers, negotiate prices, arrange
transportation, and complete transactions more efficiently (Morepje et al., 2024). Improved access to timely market
information enables marketers to make informed bu siness decisions, respond quickly to market changes, improve
profitability, and enhance market competitiveness. In addition, digital communication channels such as social media,
Short Message Service (SMS), mobile applications, and interactive voice respon se systems provide opportunities for
market information dissemination, business advisory services, training, and extension support. However, the successful
use of these technologies depends not only on their availability but also on users' awareness, skill s, perceptions, and
willingness to adopt them.
Although several studies have examined digital agriculture and technology adoption among farmers in Nigeria, limited
attention has been given to the perception and use of digital technologies among fresh tomato marketers, particularly

2 METODOLOGY
2.1 The Study Area
The study was conducted in Jalingo Local Government Area of Taraba State, Nigeria. Jalingo, located in the North -East
geopolitical zone of the country, serves as the administrative headquarters and capital of Taraba State. Historically, it
was a predominan tly agrarian settlement inhabited by ethnic groups such as the Jukun -Kona, Fulani, Mumuye, and
Yandang, whose major occupations included farming, livestock rearing, fishing, and trading. The Local Government Area
lies between latitude 8°47′N and longitude 11°09′E and has favourable climatic conditions for agricultural production,
particularly the cultivation of tomatoes and other vegetables. According to the 2006 National Population Census, Jalingo
Local Government Area had a population of 139,845 persons. Using the annual population growth rate of 3.2% reported
by the National Bureau of Statistics, the projected population for 2025 is approximately 254,200 persons. Agriculture
remains the principal economic activity, while the area's strategic location has made it an important assembly and
distribution centre for fresh agricultural produce (National Population Commission, 2006; National Bureau of Statistics,
2023).
Jalingo hosts several major markets where fresh tomatoes produced within Taraba State and neighbouring states are
assembled, distributed, and sold to wholesalers, retailers, and consumers. The interactions among farmers, traders,
transporters, and buyers make the area an active centre for agricultural marketing and price formation. In recent years,
the increasing availability of mobile phones, internet services, and other information and communication technologies
(ICTs) have gradually transformed marketing  activities in the area. Tomato marketers increasingly utilise digital
technologies such as mobile phone calls, WhatsApp, SMS, mobile money, bank transfers, Point -of-Sale (POS) terminals,
and social media platforms to communicate with customers, obtain mar ket information, negotiate prices, and facilitate
business transactions (Food and Agriculture Organization, 2022).
2.2 Sampling Procedure and Sample Size
A multistage sampling technique was employed for the selection of respondents for this study. The first stage involved
the purposive selection of eight (8) major fresh tomato markets in Jalingo Local Government Area of Taraba State. The
markets selected were Tashan Lau, Mayo-Goi Bus Stop, Nukkai Market, Mile Six Market, Sabon Gari Market, Kona Area,
Jalingo Central Market, and Gindin Doruwa. These markets were purposively selected because they constitute the major
centres for fresh tomato marketing in the study area and account for a high concentration of tomato marketing activities.
A reconnaissance survey was conducted in the selected markets to identify and compile a sampling frame of tomato
marketers. The survey identified a total of 307 tomato marketers  comprising retailers, wholesalers, and marketers
engaged in both wholesale and retail activities. The sample size for the study was determined using the Yamane (1967)
sample size determination formula: Therefore, a sample size of 174 tomato marketers was used for the study. The
determined sample size was proportionately allocated to each selected market using Cochran's (1977) proportionate
allocation formula. The proportionate allocation of the sample is presented in Table 1. In the final stage, random
sampling was used to select the required number of respondents from each market based on the proportionately
allocated sample. The final sample consisted of 104 retailers, 35 wholesalers, and 35 marketers engaged in both
wholesale and retail activities, giving a total of 174 respondents.

2.3 Sources and Type of Data
The study utilised both primary and secondary data. Primary data were collected from fresh tomato marketers in the
study area using questionnaire, while secondary data were obtained from relevant journals, government publications,
and reports to provide background information and support the analysis and discussion of the findings.
2.4 Methods of Data Analysis
The data were analysed using both descriptive and inferential statistics. Descriptive statistics, including frequencies,
percentages and means, were used to summarise the socio -economic characteristics of the respondents, identify the
types of digital technologies used in tomato marketing, and examine marketers' perceptions towards digital technology
adoption using a five-point Likert scale. Inferential statistics involved the use of one-way Analysis of Variance (ANOVA)
to test the formulated hypothesis, wh ile Tukey's Honestly Significant Difference (HSD) post hoc test was employed to
separate the group means where significant differences existed .

3 RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
3.1 Socio-economic Characteristics of the Respondents
The results in Table 2 show that tomato marketing in the study area was undertaken by both men and women, although
participation differed across marketing categories. Retailing was equally dominated by males and females (50.0% each),
while wholesalers (80.0%) and marketers engaged in both wholesale and retail activities (65.7%) were predominantly
male, indicating that digital technology interventions should target both male and female marketers. This finding is
consistent with Wahab et al. (2025), who repor ted that tomato marketing in Kano State was dominated by male
marketers because of the larger capital requirements and the demanding nature of wholesale marketing
The respondents were generally within the economically active age group, with mean ages of 39 years for retailers and
47 years for wholesalers and combined marketers. The largest proportion of retailers (37.5%) were aged 30 –39 years,
while 40.0% of wholesalers were aged 40–49 years, suggesting that most marketers possess the experience and capacity
to adopt digital technologies. Similar findings were reported by Tafida et al. (2025), who found a mean age of 39 years
among tomato farmers in Katsina State, in dicating that economically active individuals are more likely to adopt
agricultural innovations and digital technologies.
Most respondents had formal education, with 41.3% of retailers and 40.0% of combined marketers attaining secondary
education, while 28.6% of wholesalers had tertiary education, indicating adequate literacy to utilise digital technologies.
This agrees with the findings of Beeior et al. (2024 ), who reported that educational attainment significantly enhances
the adoption of tomato post -harvest technologies among farmers in Benue State by improving their ability to
understand and use new technologies. The major ity were married, accounting for 65.4% of retailers, 82.9% of
wholesalers, and 71.4% of combined marketers. The average household size was 6 persons for retailers and 8 persons
for wholesalers and combined marketers, with 54.3% of wholesalers and 51.4% of combined marketers having
households of 6–10 persons, providing family labour that may support business operations.
Membership in marketing associations was relatively high, particularly among wholesalers (80.0%), followed by
combined marketers (71.4%) and retailers (60.6%), highlighting the potential of these associations as channels for
digital technology training and  information dissemination. Access to credit was generally limited, with only 31.7% of
retailers, 51.4% of wholesalers, and 48.6% of combined marketers reporting access, which may constrain investment
in digital technologies. This finding supports Oladejo (2025), who reported that access to credit among tomato
marketers in Osun State was generally low and significantly influenced marketing performance, recommending
cooperative financing to improve investment capacity.  Monthly income differed across the mar keting groups, with
retailers earning an average of ₦165,800, wholesalers ₦278,600, and combined marketers ₦316,400. Furthermore,

although the level of adoption varied across retailers, wholesalers, and marketers engaged in both wholesale and retail
activities. Mobile  phone calls were the most widely used technology, with 94.3% of respondents reporting usage,
including 100.0% of wholesalers and combined marketers and 90.4% of retailers, confirming their importance for
business communication. WhatsApp was the second mos t commonly used technology (82.8%), with adoption rates of

while tomato marketers in northeastern Nigeria widely adopted basic digital communicatio n tools, the use of more
advanced digital marketing practices remained limited due to infrastructure, skills, and market -related constraints.

The findings in Table 4 revealed that tomato marketers generally had a positive perception of digital technology
adoption, with an overall grand mean of 4.04. Perceptions were more favourable among marketers engaged in both
wholesale and retail activities (grand mean = 4.35) and wholesalers (4.18) than retailers (3.58), indicating greater
appreciation of digital technologies among larger-scale marketers.
Respondents agreed that digital technologies improve business transactions (grand mean = 4.19), enhance
communication with customers and suppliers (4.16), improve access to market information (4.14), increase sales and

losses (3.79) and that they possessed adequate skills to use digital technologies (3.87). Across these indicators,

3.4 Test of Hypothesis
• Ho: There is no significant difference in the mean perception scores of tomato marketers towards digital
technology adoption among retailers, wholesalers, and marketers engaged in both wholesale and retail
activities.
The result of the One-Way ANOVA presented in Table 5 shows that there was a statistically significant difference in the
mean perception scores of tomato marketers towards digital technology adoption among retailers, wholesalers, and
marketers engaged in both wholesale and retail activities (F = 12.864, p = 0.000). Since the p-value is less than the 5%

4 CONCLUSION
The study concluded that fresh tomato marketers in Jalingo Local Government Area generally had a positive perception
of digital technology adoption and widely used basic digital tools such as mobile phone calls, WhatsApp, and digital
payment platforms. However, the adoption of advanced digital technologies, inclu ding digital record -keeping, social
media marketing, and e-commerce platforms, remained low, particularly among retailers. Limited access to credit, the
high cost of digital technologies, and unreliable internet services were identified as the major constr aints to adoption.
The study also revealed significant differences in perceptions towards digital technology adoption among the three
categories of marketers, with retailers exhibiting less favourable perceptions than wholesalers and marketers engaged
in both wholesale and retail activities.
Recommendations
• Government, extension agencies, and marketing associations should provide regular training and capacity -
building programmes to improve marketers' knowledge and skills in the use of digital marketing tools, with
particular emphasis on retailers.
• Government and financial institutions should improve access to affordable credit and digital financing to enable
marketers to acquire smartphones, digital payment systems, internet services, and other technologies required
for efficient marketing.
