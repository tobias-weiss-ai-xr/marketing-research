Abstract
Purpose: The rapid digital transformation of the banking industry has fundamentally changed
the way financial institutions interact with customers. While service quality and technology
acceptance have been widely recognized as critical determinants of customer satisfaction and
loyalty, existing studies have predominantly examined these constructs in isolation.
Consequently, understanding of how traditional service quality dimensions and digital
technology acceptance factors jointly influence customer satisfaction a nd loyalty remains
limited, particularly in the Malaysian banking industry. This conceptual paper proposes an
integrated research framework that combines the Service Quality (SERVQUAL) model and the
Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT ) to explain customer
satisfaction and loyalty in the Malaysian banking sector.  Design/methodology/approach:
This study adopts a conceptual research design based on a comprehensive review and
synthesis of contemporary literature relating to service quality, technology acceptance,
customer satisfaction, and customer loyalty. The proposed framework integrates four
SERVQUAL dimensions (reliability, responsiveness, assurance, and tangibles) with four UTAUT
constructs (performance expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence, and facilitating
conditions). Customer satisfaction is conceptualized as a mediating varia ble linking these
antecedents to customer loyalty. The framework is developed through a critical analysis of
prior empirical and theoretical studies published in leading banking, marketing, information
systems, and service management journals. Findings: The proposed framework suggests that
both service quality and technology acceptance are fundamental antecedents of customer
satisfaction, which subsequently influences customer loyalty. Specifically, reliability,
responsiveness, assurance, tangibles, perf ormance expectancy, effort expectancy, social
influence, and facilitating conditions are expected to influence customer satisfaction
positively. Customer satisfaction is further proposed to mediate the relationships between
the service quality and technolo gy acceptance antecedents and customer loyalty. By

Introduction
The global banking industry has undergone significant transformation over the past two
decades, driven by rapid technological advancements, increasing competition, changing
customer expectations, and evolving regulatory requirements. Digitalization has
fundamentally altered how banking products and services are delivered, compelling financial
institutions to adopt innovative technologies to remain competitive and relevant in an
increasingly dynamic marketplace. Consequently, customer satisfaction and loyalt y have
emerged as critical strategic priorities for banking institutions seeking sustainable competitive
advantage and long-term profitability.

Traditionally, banks competed primarily through product offerings, branch networks, pricing
strategies, and customer service excellence. However, the emergence of internet banking,
mobile banking applications, digital payment platforms, artificial intellig ence (AI), and
financial technology (FinTech) solutions has transformed the banking landscape. Modern
banking customers increasingly demand convenient, secure, reliable, and efficient banking
experiences accessible anytime, anywhere. As a result, banks are  no longer evaluated solely
on the quality of their face-to-face services but also on the effectiveness and usability of their
digital platforms.

Malaysia is among the leading countries in Southeast Asia for digital banking adoption and
financial technology development. The Malaysian government’s digital economy initiatives,
together with regulatory support from the central bank, have accelerated th e adoption of
digital banking services. The introduction of digital banking licenses, increasing smartphone
penetration, and growing consumer familiarity with online financial transactions have
encouraged banks to invest heavily in digital transformation i nitiatives. Consequently,
Malaysian banking customers now interact with financial institutions through multiple
channels, including branch banking, internet banking, mobile applications, automated teller
machines (ATMs), and digital payment ecosystems.

The increasing reliance on digital banking services has created new challenges and
opportunities for banking institutions. While technological innovations have enhanced service
accessibility and operational efficiency, they have also raised customer expect ations for
service quality and the digital experience. Customers expect banking systems to provide
accurate transactions, seamless functionality, secure environments, responsive customer
support, and user -friendly interfaces. Failure to meet these expectat ions may result in
customer dissatisfaction, negative word -of-mouth, and customer switching behavior.
Therefore, understanding the factors that influence customer satisfaction and customer
loyalty has become increasingly important in the digital banking era.

Customer satisfaction is widely recognized as one of the most important determinants of
organizational success in service industries. According to (Oliver 1999)Customer satisfaction
refers to the overall evaluation of a product or service based on the extent to which customer
expectations are fulfilled. Satisfied customers are more likely to maintain long -term
relationships with service providers, engage in repea t purchases, recommend services to
others, and exhibit stronger loyalty behaviors. In the banking industry, customer satisfaction
has been consistently associated with higher retention rates, increased profitability, and
stronger competitive positioning.

Similarly, customer loyalty has become a strategic asset for financial institutions. Customer
loyalty reflects a customer’s commitment to continue using a bank’s products and services
despite the availability of alternative providers. Loyal customers are g enerally less price -
sensitive, more receptive to cross -selling opportunities, and more likely to advocate for the
organization through positive recommendations. Previous studies have consistently
demonstrated that acquiring new customers is significantly m ore expensive than retaining
existing customers, making customer loyalty an essential component of long -term business
sustainability.

One of the most widely adopted frameworks for examining customer satisfaction and loyalty
in service industries is the Service Quality (SERVQUAL) model developed by (Parasuraman,
Zeithaml et al. 1988) . The SERVQUAL model posits that customers evaluate service quality
across several dimensions, including reliability, responsiveness, assurance, empathy, and
tangibles. Numerous studies have applied SERVQUAL in the banking sector and found that
service qua lity significantly influences customer satisfaction and loyalty. In particular,
reliability, responsiveness, assurance, and tangibles have been identified as critical
determinants of customer perceptions in both traditional and digital banking environments.

Reliability refers to a bank's ability to perform promised services accurately and dependably.
Customers expect banking transactions to be processed correctly, account information to be

reflects banks' willingness and ability to provide prompt service and effective assistance to

professionalism, and security measures. Tangibles encompass the physical and visual aspects
of service delivery, including branch facilities, websites, mobile applications, and
technological infrastructure. Collectively, these dimensions contribute to customers’ overall
assessment of service quality and influence their satisfaction levels.

While service quality remains an important determinant of customer satisfaction, the
increasing digitalization of banking services has highlighted the importance of technology
acceptance factors. The Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAU T),
developed by (Venkatesh, Morris et al. 2003) is one of the most influential frameworks for
understanding technology adoption behavior. The UTAUT model proposes that technology
acceptance is influenced by four primary constructs: performance expectancy, effort
expectancy, social influence, and facilitating conditions.

Performance expectancy refers to the degree to which customers believe that using digital
banking technologies will improve their banking performance and efficiency. Effort
expectancy reflects the perceived ease of using digital banking systems. Social inf luence
represents the extent to which customers perceive that important others encourage or
expect them to use digital banking services. Facilitating conditions refer to the availability of
organizational and technical support that enables technology use. These constructs have
been widely applied in studies examining mobile banking, internet banking, and financial
technology adoption.

Despite the extensive literature on service quality and technology acceptance, existing
research has generally examined these constructs independently. Many studies have focused
exclusively on service quality as a predictor of customer satisfaction and loyalty, while others
have concentrated solely on technology acceptance factors influencing digital banking
adoption. Such fragmented approaches may not adequately reflect the realities of
contemporary banking environments, where customers simultaneously evaluate both service
quality and digital technology performance. Consequently, a more comprehensive framework
is needed to integrate traditional service quality dimensions and technology acceptance
constructs to explain customer satisfaction and customer loyalty.

Furthermore, few studies have examined the combined effects of SERVQUAL and UTAUT in
the Malaysian banking context. Given Malaysia’s rapidly evolving digital banking ecosystem
and unique dual banking system comprising conventional and Islamic financial ins titutions, a
context-specific framework is needed to capture the factors influencing customer behavior in
the local banking environment. Additionally, the mediating role of customer satisfaction in
the relationship between service quality, technology accep tance, and customer loyalty
remains underexplored.

To address these gaps, this study proposes an integrated conceptual framework that
combines the SERVQUAL and UTAUT dimensions to explain customer satisfaction and loyalty

Literature Review and Hypotheses Development
Service Quality in Banking Services
Service quality has been widely acknowledged as a critical determinant of organizational
performance, customer satisfaction, and customer loyalty in service -oriented industries.
Within the banking sector, service quality plays a fundamental role in shaping  customer
perceptions and influencing behavioral intentions. (Parasuraman, Zeithaml et al. 1988)  The
SERVQUAL model, one of the most influential frameworks for measuring service quality, was
introduced. The model proposes that customers evaluate service quality based on the gap
between expectations and perceived performance.

As banking services increasingly migrate toward digital platforms, service quality remains a
crucial factor in determining customer experiences. While traditional banking interactions
focused on face-to-face encounters, modern customers evaluate the quality of both physical
and electronic services. Consequently, dimensions such as reliability, responsiveness,
assurance, and tangibles continue to influence customer satisfaction and loyalty in both
traditional and digital banking environments.

Reliability and Customer Satisfaction
Reliability refers to a banking institution's ability to deliver promised services accurately,
dependably, and consistently. It represents the extent to which customers can trust a bank to
perform transactions correctly, maintain accurate records, and prov ide uninterrupted
services. Reliability is particularly important in banking because customers entrust financial
institutions with sensitive financial information and assets.

According to Parasuraman, Zeithaml, and Malhotra, reliability is often regarded as the most
important dimension of service quality because it directly influences customer trust and
confidence. In digital banking environments, reliability encompasses system  availability,
transaction accuracy, real -time account updates, and uninterrupted access to banking
services. Customers expect online banking platforms to function consistently without
technical disruptions or transaction errors.

maintain high levels of professionalism. As digital banking transactions continue to increase,
concerns regarding cybersecurity, fraud, and privacy protection have bec ome increasingly

Theoretical Foundation and Research Gap Analysis
Theoretical Foundation
Service Quality Theory (SERVQUAL)
The SERVQUAL model, developed by Parasuraman, Zeithaml, and Berry, remains one of the
most influential theories for evaluating service quality across various industries. The theory
suggests that customers assess service quality by comparing their expectati ons with their
perceptions of actual service performance. When perceived performance exceeds
expectations, customers experience satisfaction; conversely, when it falls short, they
experience dissatisfaction.

Originally, SERVQUAL consisted of five dimensions: reliability, responsiveness, assurance,
empathy, and tangibles. However, within the context of contemporary banking services,
particularly digital banking, reliability, responsiveness, assurance, and tangi bles remain the
most relevant dimensions for evaluating customer experiences. Together, these dimensions
represent customers’ perceptions of a bank’s ability to deliver dependable, responsive,
secure, and user-friendly services.

The SERVQUAL framework has been extensively applied within the banking sector and
consistently demonstrates significant relationships with customer satisfaction and loyalty.
Studies conducted by (Parasuraman, Zeithaml et al. 1988), (Kaura, Durga Prasad et al. 2015),
(Amin 2016) , and (Ananda, Kumar et al. 2023)  provide strong evidence supporting the
importance of service quality in shaping customer attitudes and behavioral intentions.

In the Malaysian banking industry, where competition among conventional banks, Islamic
banks, and digital banking providers continues to intensify, service quality remains a crucial
determinant of customer satisfaction and long-term customer retention.

Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT)
Venkatesh and Morris introduced the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology
(UTAUT) to explain users’ acceptance and utilization of technological innovations. UTAUT
integrates elements from eight prominent technology adoption theories, including  the
Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), the Theory of Planned Behavior (TPB), the Innovation
Diffusion Theory (IDT), and the Social Cognitive Theory (SCT).

The theory proposes four primary determinants of technology acceptance:
1. Performance Expectancy
2. Effort Expectancy
3. Social Influence
4. Facilitating Conditions

Performance expectancy refers to the extent to which users believe technology enhances
performance. Effort expectancy reflects how easy the technology is to use. Social influence
captures the effect of social networks and significant others on technology a doption.
Facilitating conditions refer to the availability of resources and support structures necessary
for technology utilization.

As banking services become increasingly digitalized, understanding customers’ perceptions of

Integration of SERVQUAL and UTAUT
While SERVQUAL focuses on customers’ evaluations of service quality, UTAUT explains
customers’ acceptance and utilization of technology. Historically, banking research has
examined these theories independently. However, contemporary banking customers
simultaneously evaluate both service quality and technological capabilities when interacting
with financial institutions.

For example, customers expect online banking platforms not only to function efficiently but
also to provide secure transactions, responsive support services, reliable performance, and
visually appealing interfaces. Therefore, relying solely on service quality theory or technology
acceptance theory may not fully explain customer behavior.

Integrating SERVQUAL and UTAUT offers a more comprehensive explanation of customer
satisfaction and loyalty in digital banking environments. Service quality dimensions capture
customers’ evaluations of banking services, while technology acceptance construc ts explain
customers’ perceptions of digital banking platforms. Together, these frameworks provide a
holistic perspective on customer experiences.

The proposed framework, therefore, integrates four SERVQUAL dimensions (reliability,
responsiveness, assurance, and tangibles) and four UTAUT constructs (performance
expectancy, effort expectancy, social influence, and facilitating conditions) as antecedents of
customer satisfaction. Customer satisfaction is subsequently proposed to influence customer
loyalty.

This integrated perspective aligns with the realities of modern banking, where service delivery
and technology adoption are increasingly inseparable.

Research Gap Analysis
Despite substantial research on customer satisfaction and loyalty in the banking industry,
several important gaps remain in the literature.

Gap 1: Fragmented Theoretical Perspectives
Most previous studies have focused either on service quality or technology acceptance
independently. Studies employing SERVQUAL primarily investigate the influence of service
quality dimensions on customer satisfaction and loyalty, whereas studies utilizin g UTAUT
predominantly examine technology adoption and usage behavior.

As a result, limited research has integrated both frameworks within a single model capable of
explaining customer satisfaction and loyalty in contemporary banking environments.

Gap 2: Limited Integration of Traditional and Digital Banking Factors
The banking industry has undergone a significant digital transformation. Consequently,
customers evaluate both traditional service encounters and digital banking experiences
simultaneously. However, many existing studies continue to examine these factors
separately.

This creates a theoretical gap because customers no longer clearly distinguish between
service and technological quality when evaluating banking services.

Gap 3: Insufficient Malaysian Banking Evidence
Although numerous studies have been conducted in developed economies such as the United
States, the United Kingdom, China, and other European countries, limited evidence exists on
the combined effects of the SERVQUAL and UTAUT constructs in the Malaysian b anking
sector.

Malaysia’s unique banking environment, comprising conventional and Islamic banks, as well
as newly established digital banks, provides a valuable context for investigating customer
behavior.

Gap 4: Limited Examination of Customer Satisfaction as a Mediator
Previous studies frequently treat customer satisfaction as a direct outcome variable rather
than examining its mediating role between service quality, technology acceptance, and
customer loyalty.
Understanding customer satisfaction as a mediating mechanism may provide deeper insights
into how customer perceptions translate into loyalty behaviors.

Gap 5: Lack of Comprehensive Customer Loyalty Models
Many customer loyalty studies focus on either behavioral intentions or technology adoption
outcomes, without incorporating both the service quality and technology acceptance
perspectives.

Consequently, existing models may not adequately capture the multidimensional nature of
customer loyalty within digital banking environments.

recognizes that contemporary banking customers evaluate both service quality attributes and
technology-related characteristics when interacting with banking institutions.

The proposed model positions reliability, responsiveness, assurance, tangibles, performance

Theoretical Contributions
The proposed framework contributes to the literature in several important ways.
First, the study advances research on customer loyalty by integrating SERVQUAL and UTAUT
into a single conceptual framework. While previous studies have generally treated service
quality and technology acceptance separately, this research recognizes their
interconnectedness in modern banking environments.

Second, the framework extends service quality theory by demonstrating that traditional
service quality dimensions remain relevant despite increasing digitalization. Although
technology has transformed banking operations, customers continue to evaluate reli ability,
responsiveness, assurance, and tangibles when assessing banking services.

Third, the framework extends UTAUT by examining technology acceptance constructs beyond
initial adoption behavior. Rather than focusing solely on technology usage intentions, the
proposed model investigates how technology acceptance contributes to customer
satisfaction and customer loyalty.

Fourth, the study contributes to relationship marketing theory by positioning customer
satisfaction as a mediating mechanism linking customer perceptions and loyalty outcomes.
Finally, the proposed framework contributes to the Malaysian banking literature by providing
a context-specific model that explains customer behavior in a rapidly evolving digital banking
ecosystem.

Conclusion
This conceptual paper develops an integrated SERVQUAL -UTAUT framework to explain how
service quality and technology acceptance may jointly shape customer satisfaction and,
subsequently, customer loyalty in the Malaysian banking industry. Importantly, the
relationships advanced in this paper are conceptual propositions derived from the reviewed
literature; they should not be interpreted as empirically established findings. Reliability,
responsiveness, assurance, tangibles, performance expectancy, effort expe ctancy, social
influence, and facilitating conditions are therefore proposed as antecedents of customer
satisfaction. In contrast, customer satisfaction is proposed to influence customer loyalty and
to mediate the effects of these antecedents on loyalty.

The framework's principal theoretical contribution is its direct response to the five research
gaps identified in this paper. First, it addresses fragmented theoretical perspectives by
integrating SERVQUAL and UTAUT within a single model rather than treati ng service quality
and technology acceptance independently. Second, it addresses the limited integration of

traditional and digital banking factors by modeling service -quality evaluations and
technology-acceptance perceptions simultaneously. Third, it responds to insufficient
Malaysian banking evidence by providing a context -specific framework for a banking
environment that includes conventional, Islamic, and increasingly digital banking services.
Fourth, it addresses the limited examination of customer satisfaction as a mediator by
positioning satisfaction as the mechanism through which service -quality and techn ology-
acceptance perceptions are proposed to translate into loyalty. Fifth, it responds to the lack of
comprehensive customer-loyalty models by linking these two theoretical perspectives to a
common loyalty outcome.

Through these five contributions, the framework extends the application of SERVQUAL
beyond conventional service evaluation and extends UTAUT beyond its predominant focus on
technology adoption and usage intention. Their integration provides a theoretical b ridge
between service marketing and technology -acceptance research, while the mediating role
assigned to customer satisfaction connects these antecedent evaluations with relationship -
marketing outcomes. The paper's contribution is therefore not evidence th at the proposed
effects occur, but the development of a theoretically grounded and testable explanation of
how they may operate together in the Malaysian banking context.

From a managerial perspective, the framework suggests that banks should consider service
delivery and digital technology as interconnected components of the customer experience.
Reliability, responsiveness, assurance, interface quality, perceived usefulnes s, ease of use,
social influence, and enabling support may collectively shape satisfaction and, through
satisfaction, loyalty. These implications remain provisional until the proposed relationships
are empirically tested.

Future research should empirically validate the propositions using appropriate quantitative
methods, including structural equation modeling, and assess the proposed mediating effects
of customer satisfaction. Comparative testing across conventional, Islamic, and digital banks,
as well as across different customer segments and ASEAN markets, would further establish
the framework's explanatory power, boundary conditions, and generalisability . Such
empirical validation would determine whether the integrated framework robustly explains
customer satisfaction and loyalty in increasingly digitalized banking environments.
