A BSTRACT
This paper develops a conceptual framework examining how AI-enabled banking may contribute to
customer satisfaction, sustained digital usage and digital financial inclusion in India, and how these out-
comes relate to the vision of Viksit Bharat 2047. The study adopts a conceptual approach based on a
structured synthesis of secondary sources, including peer-reviewed literature, Reserve Bank of India
publications, regulatory and policy documents, and official statistics on digital payments and financial
inclusion. Expectation-confirmation and information-systems success perspectives inform the sequenc-
ing of the proposed constructs and relationships, articulated through five testable propositions. Trust,
data privacy and protection, digital literacy, and responsible AI governance are positioned as enabling
conditions that may shape the strength of each relationship. The reviewed evidence provides stronger
support for the customer experience and usage links, conditional support for the move from usage to in-
clusion, and mainly indirect support for the link between inclusion and national development. By ex-
tending technology adoption and continuance perspectives beyond individual user outcomes, the study
offers a basis for future moderation and multigroup analysis, while informing banks, regulators and pol-
icymakers on service quality, privacy protection, governance and digital literacy. The proposed relation-
ships are conceptual pathways rather than established causal effects and require empirical validation.
Keywords: Artificial Intelligence, AI-enabled banking, Customer satisfaction, Digital financial inclu-
sion, FREE-AI, Viksit Bharat 2047
1. Introduction
India's financial system has been reshaped by digital public infrastructure and a sustained policy emphasis
on inclusion. By 19 August 2026, the Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana had opened 59.09 crore accounts
holding deposits of Rs 3,16,514 crore; women held 55.7 per cent of these accounts and 77.8 per cent were in
rural and semi-urban areas (Ministry of Finance, 2026). The Unified Payments Interface processed a record
24.51 billion transactions worth Rs 29.82 lakh crore in August 2026, 22 per cent more than a year earlier
(NPCI, 2026). The Reserve Bank of India's composite Financial Inclusion Index rose to 70.0 in March 2026
from 67.0 a year earlier, with the improvement attributed mainly to higher usage (RBI, 2026). Within this
setting, banks are extending AI from back-office analytics into customer-facing services. Bank of Baroda,
for example, introduced Aditi, a generative AI virtual relationship manager offering round-the-clock, multi-
lingual conversational banking through audio, video and chat (Bank of Baroda, 2024). These developments
sit within Viksit Bharat 2047, the Government of India's vision of a developed nation by the centenary of in-
dependence, in which financial inclusion supported by digital public infrastructure is a recurring foundation
(Ministry of Finance, 2024).
Greater AI deployment does not by itself guarantee better outcomes for customers. The rise in the Financial
Inclusion Index shows that inclusion now depends increasingly on how far people use financial services and
not only on whether they hold accounts. A first-time user who has a poor experience with a digital channel
may stop using it, whatever the number of accounts opened. AI could ease some barriers, and the RBI's
FREE-AI committee noted that multimodal, multilingual AI can help deliver financial services to people
who have so far been excluded (RBI, 2025). AI can also introduce opaque decisions, bias, misuse of per-
sonal data and the exclusion of people unfamiliar with digital interfaces. The practical problem for banks and
regulators is therefore whether, and under what conditions, AI-enabled banking raises customer satisfaction
and sustains use among newly included customers.

mercial bank whose chatbot handled about 400 million interactions in 2021, against 17 million in 2019. It re-
solved 92.1 per cent of queries without transfer to a human, and users gave it an average satisfaction rating
of 3.9 out of 5. Their single-case study centers on service efficiency in an emerging economy and says little

Meanwhile, regulation has moved quickly. The RBI's FREE-AI com-

principles and 26 recommendations across six pillars for the responsi-

Protection Rules were notified on 13 November 2025, with consent
manager provisions taking effect from 13 November 2026 and the re-
maining substantive obligations from 13 May 2027 (MeitY, 2025).
Despite this growing body of work, a gap remains. Evidence on AI-
enabled service quality and customer satisfaction typically rests on
customer surveys or single-institution cases, whereas evidence on fi-
nancial inclusion rests on national access and usage statistics. These
two strands are rarely examined together, and still less often alongside
the regulatory conditions that now govern how banks may deploy AI.
As a result, there is limited synthesis of how AI-enabled banking, cus-
tomer satisfaction and digital inclusion connect, and of what the
FREE-AI recommendations and data protection obligations imply for
that connection.
This paper addresses the gap with four objectives: (1) to describe
trends in AI-enabled banking and digital financial inclusion in India
using secondary data; (2) to analyze what the FREE-AI framework
and the data protection regime imply for banks that deploy AI; (3) to
synthesize published evidence on how AI-enabled services relate to
customer satisfaction; and (4) to propose a conceptual framework,
with propositions for future testing, linking AI-enabled banking, cus-
tomer satisfaction and digital inclusion in support of Viksit Bharat
2047.
The paper makes three contributions. Conceptually, it integrates the
three themes into one framework in which trust, data protection and
digital literacy condition whether AI-enabled banking translates into
satisfaction and inclusion. Empirically, it consolidates scattered offi-
cial statistics, regulatory documents and published evidence into a
single account of current trends in India. For policy and practice, it
offers banks and regulators a basis for aligning AI deployment with
customer-centric and inclusion-oriented goals. Because the study re-
lies entirely on secondary sources, it describes trends and plausible
pathways without claiming causal effects, and the proposed relation-
ships are left for empirical testing in future research.
2. Critical review of literature
This section reviews the literature on AI-enabled banking, customer
satisfaction and digital financial inclusion, together with the regula-
tory context that shapes them. It begins with the evidence on AI-en-
abled banking and digital transformation.
2.1 AI-enabled banking and digital transformation
Artificial Intelligence (AI) has become an important component of
digital transformation in banking, extending technology adoption
from back-office operations to customer-facing services. De Andrade
and Tumelero (2022), in a case study of a large Brazilian commercial
bank, demonstrated the scalability of AI-enabled chatbots. Chatbot in-
teractions increased from 17 million in 2019 to 400 million in 2021,
while the proportion of queries resolved without human intervention
increased from 77% to 92%. However, the single-institution design
and reliance on bank-reported customer data limit the generalizability
of these findings and provide limited evidence on financial inclusion.
In India, research has traditionally emphasized the operational and ef-
ficiency implications of AI. Mor and Gupta (2021), examining 47
Indian commercial banks using a stochastic frontier approach, re-
ported an association between AI deployment and lower technical in-
efficiency. Similarly, Sharma, Sharma and Barua (2013) identified a
strong efficiency-oriented tradition in Indian banking research. While
this literature supports the potential organizational benefits of AI, it
provides comparatively limited evidence on customer experience and
inclusion.
Recent evidence suggests that AI adoption in Indian banking remains
uneven. Singh and Singh (2019) identified limitations in the function-
ality and awareness of chatbots and virtual assistants, while the RBI's
FREE-AI Committee highlighted differences in AI adoption and insti-
tutional readiness across supervised entities (RBI, 2025). Banks have
nevertheless begun introducing advanced customer-facing applica-
tions, such as Bank of Baroda's Aditi generative-AI virtual relation-
ship manager (Bank of Baroda, 2024). Overall, existing literature in-
dicates that AI can support service scalability and operational effi-
ciency, but evidence concerning customer outcomes remains compar-
atively limited. More importantly, AI-enabled banking, customer sat-
isfaction and digital financial inclusion have rarely been examined
within an integrated framework in the Indian context. This gap pro-
vides the basis for examining these relationships in the subsequent
sections.
2.2 AI-enabled banking and customer satisfaction
Customer satisfaction is an important indicator of the effectiveness of
AI-enabled banking services. AI applications such as chatbots, virtual
assistants, personalized services and automated customer support can
improve convenience, responsiveness, service availability and inter-
action quality. De Andrade and Tumelero (2022) reported an average
satisfaction rating of 3.9 out of 5 among users of a banking chatbot,
although the evidence was largely based on the bank's internal data.
In India, Othayoth and Khanna (2024) found that chatbot service
quality was significantly associated with customer satisfaction and
perceived value, with assurance, interface design and personalization
emerging as important factors.
However, technological functionality alone may not ensure customer
satisfaction. Trust, privacy, transparency, reliability and the availabil-
ity of human support can influence how customers perceive and use
AI-enabled services. Bansal et al. (2024) identified concerns relating
to transparency, privacy, trust and reduced human interaction as im-
portant barriers to AI-enabled chatbot adoption in Indian banking.
Overall, existing evidence suggests that AI-enabled banking can con-
tribute to customer satisfaction through greater convenience, person-
alization, responsiveness and service availability. However, the
strength of this relationship may vary according to customer charac-
teristics and enabling conditions such as trust, digital literacy, privacy
protection and responsible AI governance. Moreover, customer satis-
faction has rarely been examined alongside digital financial inclusion
in the Indian context. This provides a basis for considering satisfac-
tion as a potential link between AI-enabled banking and sustained
digital engagement.
2.3 AI-enabled banking and digital financial inclusion
Digital financial inclusion increasingly depends not only on access to
banking services but also on customers' ability and willingness to use
them effectively. AI-enabled banking may support this process by im-
proving service availability, personalizing interactions, providing
multilingual and conversational assistance, and simplifying access to
digital financial services. The RBI's FREE-AI Committee specifically
recognized the potential of multimodal and multilingual AI to im-
prove financial-service delivery to underserved and excluded popula-
tions (RBI, 2025).
India's expanding digital financial infrastructure provides an impor-
tant context for this potential. The growth of PMJDY accounts, UPI

2.4 Trust, responsible AI, sustained digital usage and Viksit
Bharat 2047
Trust, data privacy and responsible AI governance are important con-
ditions for the sustainable use of AI-enabled banking services.
Although AI can improve convenience and service accessibility, con-
cerns regarding privacy, cybersecurity, transparency, algorithmic bias
and the loss of human interaction may influence customers' willing-
ness to continue using AI-based services. Bansal et al. (2024) identi-
fied transparency, privacy, trust and limited human interaction as im-
portant barriers to AI-enabled chatbot adoption in Indian banking.
The RBI's FREE-AI framework further emphasizes responsible,
transparent and secure AI adoption in the financial sector (RBI,
2025).
Customer satisfaction may therefore play an important role in sus-
tained digital engagement. A positive experience with AI-enabled ser-
vices may encourage customers to continue using digital banking
channels, while dissatisfaction or lack of trust may discourage contin-
ued usage. However, because the present study relies on secondary
evidence, this pathway is treated as a conceptual proposition rather
than a tested causal relationship. These issues are particularly relevant
to India's financial inclusion agenda and the broader vision of Viksit
Bharat 2047. Digital public infrastructure such as PMJDY and UPI
has substantially expanded access and usage of formal financial ser-
vices (Ministry of Finance, 2026; NPCI, 2026). AI-enabled banking
can potentially complement this infrastructure by improving accessi-
bility, personalization and service availability, particularly for under-
served customers. However, inclusive digital transformation requires
more than technological expansion; it also depends on trust, digital
literacy, privacy protection, accessibility and responsible governance.
Thus, the literature suggests a potential pathway in which AI-enabled
banking may enhance customer satisfaction, satisfaction may support
sustained digital usage, and sustained usage may contribute to deeper
digital financial inclusion. Trust, data protection, digital literacy and
responsible AI governance can be viewed as important enabling con-
ditions within this pathway. Examining these interconnected dimen-
sions provides a basis for developing the conceptual framework of the
present study in the context of Viksit Bharat 2047.

3.3 Theoretical positioning and contribution
The proposed model builds on established technology and service
frameworks and extends them in one important direction. Technology
acceptance models such as TAM and UTAUT explain why users
adopt a technology, but they focus mainly on initial adoption (Davis,
1989; Venkatesh et al., 2003) [1, 2]. The expectation confirmation
model moves the focus to post adoption, showing how confirmation
and satisfaction shape continued use (Bhattacherjee, 2001) [3]. The in-
formation systems success model links system quality to satisfaction,
use and net benefits (DeLone et al., 2003) [4]. Each of these frame-
works ends at the user, at adoption, satisfaction or continued use. The
present model carries the chain forward from sustained usage to digi-
tal financial inclusion and national development, and it treats trust,
data privacy and protection, digital literacy and responsible AI gover-
nance as conditions that shape every link. This joins a user level path-
way with a societal outcome that these frameworks study separately.

4. Methodological approach
4.1 Research design
This study adopts a conceptual, literature-based approach to develop
the proposed model. Published research on AI enabled banking, cus-
tomer satisfaction, sustained digital usage, digital financial inclusion
and national development was reviewed and synthesized. The aim is
to derive a sequential model and testable propositions (P1 to P5) from
existing evidence and theory.

4.3 Analytical approach
The selected literature was analyzed through content analysis
(Krippendorff, 2018) [5]. Each study was read, and its content was
categorized according to the constructs examined, the relationships
reported and the conditions under which those relationships held.
Categories were compared across studies to identify where evidence
converged, for example satisfaction leading to continued use, and
where it pointed to factors that strengthen or weaken a relationship.
Established theory, including the expectation confirmation model,
guided the ordering of categories into a sequential pathway from AI
enabled banking to Viksit Bharat 2047. Categories that shaped the
strength of links, rather than forming a step in the chain, were treated
as enabling conditions: trust, data privacy and protection, digital liter-
acy and responsible AI governance. This process produced the model
in Figure 1 and propositions P1 to P5.

6. Discussions
The literature review supports the direction of the proposed frame-
work while highlighting important gaps in the existing evidence. AI-
enabled banking may contribute to customer satisfaction, while posi-
tive customer experiences may encourage sustained use of digital
banking services. The subsequent pathway from sustained usage to
digital financial inclusion is likely to depend on enabling conditions
such as digital literacy, accessibility and connectivity. The relation-
ship between digital inclusion and Viksit Bharat 2047 is more indirect
and should therefore be interpreted as a broader policy implication
rather than a directly established outcome. Trust is positioned as an
enabling condition across the proposed relationships rather than as a
sequential stage in the model.
Conceptually, the framework brings together insights from technol-
ogy adoption, expectation-confirmation and information-systems lit-
erature to extend the discussion beyond individual technology use to-
wards sustained digital engagement and financial inclusion. It also in-
corporates trust, data privacy, digital literacy and responsible AI gov-
ernance as conditions that may shape the proposed relationships.
From a practical perspective, the framework suggests that banks
should focus not only on deploying AI-enabled services but also on
service quality, transparency and continued availability of human sup-
port. Regulators can strengthen privacy protection and responsible AI
governance in line with the RBI's FREE-AI framework (Reserve
Bank of India, 2025) [6], while policymakers can support digital liter-
acy and connectivity to facilitate more inclusive use of digital finan-
cial services.
The framework is conceptual and is based on a synthesis of selected
secondary evidence rather than primary empirical testing. Future re-
search can therefore examine the proposed relationships using struc-
tural equation modelling, test the role of enabling factors through
moderation analysis, and investigate the broader relationship between
digital financial inclusion and the objectives of Viksit Bharat 2047 us-
ing appropriate empirical indicators.

7. Limitations and future scope of work
This study has limitations that point to opportunities for further work.
The model is conceptual and draws on a selected body of literature, so
its propositions remain untested with primary data. Evidence on the
link between digital financial inclusion and Viksit Bharat 2047 is in-
direct, and empirical studies of data privacy and responsible AI gover-
nance as conditioning factors are scarce. The model is also set in the
Indian context, which limits generalization to other markets. Future
research can test the propositions through structural equation model-
ling, examine the enabling factors through moderation analysis, and
use longitudinal and multigroup designs to capture change over time
and across rural and urban users. Studies that gather direct evidence
on the inclusion to national development link, and that compare the
model across countries, would extend its reach further.
8. Conclusion
This paper examined the potential role of AI-enabled banking in sup-
porting customer satisfaction, digital financial inclusion and the
broader objectives of Viksit Bharat 2047. Based on a synthesis of sec-
ondary evidence, the study proposes a sequential conceptual pathway
in which AI-enabled banking may enhance the service experience and
contribute to customer satisfaction. Positive customer experiences
may, in turn, encourage sustained use of digital banking channels,
while continued digital engagement may contribute to deeper digital
financial inclusion. The broader relationship between digital financial
inclusion and Viksit Bharat 2047 is interpreted as a policy-oriented
pathway rather than a directly established causal relationship. Trust,
data privacy and protection, digital literacy, and responsible AI gover-
nance are positioned as enabling conditions that may shape the pro-
posed relationships.
The review indicates that the strength of evidence varies across the
proposed pathway. The relationship between AI-enabled banking and
customer satisfaction is supported by existing studies, while evidence
concerning sustained digital usage is also suggestive of the impor-
tance of positive customer experiences. The transition from digital us-
age to financial inclusion, however, depends on conditions such as
digital literacy, accessibility and connectivity, since access or adop-
tion alone does not necessarily ensure meaningful inclusion. The con-
nection between digital financial inclusion and Viksit Bharat 2047 re-
mains more indirect, as the reviewed literature does not directly estab-
lish this relationship. Similarly, evidence concerning trust is mixed
across studies, supporting its treatment as an enabling condition rather
than a sequential stage in the proposed model.
The study makes three conceptual contributions. First, it extends the
discussion of technology adoption, expectation confirmation and in-
formation-systems success beyond individual technology use towards
sustained digital engagement and financial inclusion. Second, it incor-
porates trust, data privacy and protection, digital literacy and respon-
sible AI governance as conditions that may shape the proposed rela-
tionships. Third, it provides five conceptual propositions that estab-
lish a structured agenda for future empirical research.
The framework also has practical implications. Banks may need to fo-
cus not only on deploying AI-enabled services but also on service
quality, transparency, reliability and the continued availability of hu-
man support. Regulators can strengthen privacy protection and re-
sponsible AI governance in alignment with the RBI's FREE-AI
framework (Reserve Bank of India, 2025) [6]. Policymakers and de-
velopment agencies can further support digital literacy, accessibility
and connectivity to facilitate meaningful and sustained use of digital
financial services, particularly among first-time or less digitally expe-
rienced users.
The study is conceptual and relies on a selected body of secondary lit-
erature and documentary evidence rather than primary empirical test-
ing. Accordingly, the proposed relationships should not be interpreted
as causal findings. Future research can test the propositions using
structural equation modelling, examine the enabling factors through
moderation analysis, and employ longitudinal and multigroup designs
to investigate changes over time and differences across user groups
and geographical contexts. Further research can also examine the re-
lationship between measurable digital financial inclusion outcomes
and the objectives of Viksit Bharat 2047 using appropriate indicators.
Overall, the framework provides a basis for examining how responsi-
ble and customer-centred AI-enabled banking may contribute to more
inclusive digital financial development in India.
