 The rapid development of digital technology has significantly transformed
the tourism industry, including homestay services that increasingly rely on
digital platforms to improve customer experiences. However, the adoption
of digital services among communi ty-based homestays remains uneven,
particularly in rural tourism destinations, creating challenges in
maintaining tourist satisfaction, trust, and loyalty. This study aims to
examine the effects of homestay service digitalization and tourist
satisfaction on tourist loyalty through the mediating role of trust at Telaga
Sarangan Tourism Destination, Magetan, Indonesia. A quantitative
approach was employed using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation
Modeling (PLS-SEM). Data were collected from tourists who  had stayed in
homestays around Telaga Sarangan through structured questionnaires
measured using a five -point Likert scale. The measurement model was
evaluated through convergent validity, discriminant validity, composite
reliability, and Cronbach's alpha,  while the structural model was assessed
using path coefficient analysis and bootstrapping. The findings indicate that
homestay service digitalization significantly enhances tourist trust and
loyalty. Tourist satisfaction also exerts significant positive e ffects on trust
and loyalty. Furthermore, trust plays a significant mediating role in
strengthening the relationships between homestay service digitalization
and tourist loyalty as well as between tourist satisfaction and tourist loyalty.
These findings suggest that improving digital service quality and enhancing
tourists' satisfaction are essential strategies for increasing trust and fostering
long-term tourist loyalty. The study contributes to the literature on digital
tourism by highlighting the strategi c role of trust in strengthening loyalty
within community-based homestay services in rural tourism destinations.

INTRODUCTION
The rapid advancement of information and communication technology has
significantly transformed the global tourism industry by changing the way tourists
search for information, compare destinations, make reservations, complete
transactions, and share trave l experiences. Digital transformation has become one of
the primary drivers of competitiveness in tourism because it enables tourism
businesses to improve operational efficiency, expand market reach, and provide better
customer experiences. The integration of online travel agencies (OTAs), social media,
digital payment systems, and mobile applications has fundamentally changed
tourists' expectations regarding tourism services, making digitalization an essential

Community-based accommodation providers, particularly homestays, are also
experiencing increasing pressure to adopt digital technology in response to these
changes. Unlike hotels that generally possess more sophisticated technological
infrastructure and fi nancial resources, most homestays are operated by local
communities with limited technological capabilities. Consequently, the
implementation of digital services among homestay businesses remains uneven,
particularly in rural tourism destinations. This condition affects service accessibility,
reservation efficiency, promotional effectiveness, and communication between service
providers and tourists, ultimately influencing tourists' overall experiences (Rasim et
al., 2026; Wijaya, 2024; Sarma et al., 2026; Yang et al., 2025).
Digitalization in homestay services encompasses various technological
applications, including online reservation systems, electronic payment facilities, social
media promotion, online customer communication, and digital review management.
These technologie s simplify tourists' decision -making processes by providing
transparent information regarding accommodation facilities, prices, locations, and
previous customer experiences. Previous studies have demonstrated that digital
marketing and digital service quality significantly improve customer satisfaction
because tourists perceive digital platforms as providing convenience, efficiency, and
transparency throughout the service proc ess (Ardhi et al., 2025; Ichsan et al., 2024;
Octanisa et al., 2024; Liu & Roehl, 2025; Xishihui et al., 2024).
Tourist satisfaction has long been recognized as one of the most important
indicators of tourism service performance. Satisfaction emerges when the actual
service experience meets or exceeds tourists' expectations regarding accommodation
quality, facilitie s, cleanliness, hospitality, responsiveness, and overall service
performance. Within the homestay industry, positive interactions between tourists
and local hosts contribute significantly to memorable travel experiences that
strengthen favorable perception s toward the destination. Consequently, satisfied
tourists are more likely to revisit the destination and recommend their experiences to
others through both traditional word -of-mouth and digital platforms (Pratama &
Ramadhan, 2022; Anggani, 2022; Lam et al., 2024; Quang et al., 202 5; Jasrotia et al.,
2024).
In today's digital tourism environment, tourist loyalty has become increasingly
important because loyal visitors not only generate repeat purchases but also
contribute to destination promotion through online reviews, ratings, and
recommendations shared on digital platforms. Electronic word-of-mouth has become

Besides satisfaction, trust represents another critical determinant of tourist

without direct face -to-face interaction. Trust reflects tourists' confidence that
accommodation pr oviders are capable of delivering services consistent with the
information presented through digital platforms. Accurate information, secure

tourists' online booking intentions and strengthens customer loyalty in digital tourism
services (Chouykaew et al., 2024; Ghosh et al., 2025; Suryana, 2023).
The relationship among digital service quality, satisfaction, trust, and loyalty
has received considerable attention in tourism literature. The Service Quality
framework proposed by Parasuraman, Zeithaml, and Berry (1988) explains that
superior service qua lity increases customer satisfaction, which subsequently
encourages customer loyalty. Furthermore, the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM)
argues that users are more willing to adopt digital technologies when they perceive
them as useful and easy to use, the reby increasing trust toward technology -based
services (Parasuraman et al., 1988; Lisha et al., 2025; Chouykaew et al., 2024). These
theoretical perspectives suggest that digitalization may influence tourist loyalty both
directly and indirectly through psychological factors such as satisfaction and trust.
Although previous studies have investigated digital tourism services
extensively, several important research gaps remain. Most existing studies focus on
hotels, online travel agencies, or general e-tourism platforms rather than community-
based homestays operating in rural destinations. In addition, many empirical studies
examine only the direct relationships between digital service quality and customer
loyalty or investigate satisfaction as the sole mediating variable. Limited research
simultaneously evaluates the mediating role of trust in explaining how homestay
service digitalization and tourist satisfaction influence tourist loyalty, particularly
within Indonesian rural tourism destinations. Recent international stud ies have
examined homestay service quality, tourist satisfaction, revisit intention, and digital
experiences; however, most focus on direct relationships or are conducted outside the

community-based homestays rather than conventional hotels or online travel
platforms. Second, it integrates digitalization, tourist satisfaction, trust, and loyalty

Modeling (PLS -SEM). Third, this study provides empirical evidence from Telaga

management within rural  tourism contexts in Indonesia. Therefore, this research is
expected to contribute theoretically to tourism marketing literature and practically to
homestay operators and tourism policymakers in developing effective digital service
strategies that strengthen tourists' trust and long-term loyalty.

METHODS
This study employed a quantitative research approach with an explanatory
design to examine the causal relationships among homestay service digitalization,
tourist satisfaction, trust, and tourist loyalty at Telaga Sarangan Tourism Destination,
Magetan Regency, East Java, Indonesia. A quantitative approach was selected because
the research aims to test the proposed hypotheses empirically and to measure the
magnitude of relationships among the latent variables using statistical analysis.
The population of this study consisted of tourists who had stayed at homestays
located in the Telaga Sarangan tourism area. Since the exact number of homestay
visitors could not be accurately identified, the sample was determined using a

Sarangan tourism area at least once, and (2) they were at least 20 years old. These
criteria ensured  that respondents possessed sufficient experience to evaluate the
quality of digital services, their satisfaction, trust, and loyalty toward the homestay

Primary data were collected using a structured questionnaire distributed
directly and through online platforms. The questionnaire employed a five-point Likert
scale ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). The research instrument
consisted of four latent constructs: Homestay Service Digitalization, Tourist
Satisfaction, Trust, and Tourist Loyalty. Homestay Service Digitalization was
measured using four indicators representing online reservation services, digital
payment facilities, accessib ility of information, and responsiveness of digital
communication. Tourist Satisfaction was measured through four indicators reflecting
tourists' overall satisfaction with facilities, service quality, accommodation experience,
and value received. Trust was  assessed using four indicators related to honesty,
reliability, transaction security, and confidence in the homestay provider. Tourist
Loyalty was measured using four indicators representing revisit intention,
recommendation intention, positive word -of-mouth, and commitment to reuse the
homestay services.
Prior to testing the structural relationships, the measurement model was
evaluated to ensure the validity and reliability of all constructs. Convergent validity
was assessed using outer loading values and Average Variance Extracted (AVE),
where outer loadi ng values greater than 0.70 and AVE values above 0.50 indicate
satisfactory convergent validity (Hair et al., 2022). Internal consistency reliability was
examined using Cronbach's Alpha and Composite Reliability, with recommended
threshold values exceeding  0.70. Discriminant validity was evaluated using the
Heterotrait–Monotrait Ratio (HTMT) and cross -loading criteria to confirm that each
construct adequately differed from the others.
The structural model was subsequently evaluated using Partial Least Squares
Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -SEM) implemented through SmartPLS  software.
PLS-SEM was selected because it is appropriate for prediction -oriented research,
accommodates complex structural relationships involving mediation effects, and does
not require multivariate normality assumptions. Furthermore, this method perform s
effectively when analyzing latent constructs measured by multiple indicators and is
widely applied in tourism and hospitality research.

To evaluate the predictive capability of the proposed model, several structural

Discussion
The Effect of Homestay Service Digitalization on Trust
The empirical findings demonstrate that homestay service digitalization has a
positive and statistically significant effect on tourist trust (β = 0.452; t = 7.539; p < 0.001).
This result indicates that improving digital services, including online reservat ion
systems, digital payment facilities, accessible information, and responsive online
communication, significantly enhances tourists' confidence in homestay providers.
This finding is consistent with previous studies emphasizing that digital
transformation improves service accessibility and strengthens customer trust in
tourism services (Singgalen et al., 2025; Rasim et al., 2026; Ichsan et al., 2024).
These findings support the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), which
argues that technologies perceived as useful and easy to use encourage users to
develop positive beliefs toward service providers (Lisha et al., 2025). In the tourism
industry, digital platforms reduce information asymmetry by providing transparent
information regarding accommodation quality, prices, facilities, and customer
reviews. Consequently, tourists perceive lower uncertainty when making
accommodation decisions, thereby strengthening their trust (Chouykaew et al., 2024).
The result is also consistent with Relationship Marketing Theory, which
emphasizes that trust develops when service providers consistently deliver reliable
information and fulfill customer expectations. Digital platforms facilitate transparent
interactions between homestay operators and tourists, enabling visitors to evaluate
service quality before making reservations. Such transparency reduces perceived risk

Empirically, this finding supports previous studies reporting that digital

business performance (Octanisa et al., 2024; Wijaya, 2024).

The Effect of Homestay Service Digitalization on Tourist Loyalty
The results indicate that homestay service digitalization positively affects
tourist loyalty (β = 0.346; t = 4.826; p < 0.001). This finding suggests that tourists are
more likely to revisit and recommend homestays when digital services provide
convenience, efficiency, and reliable information throughout their travel experience.
Similar findings have been reported by Wisnawa  (2024), who argued that
digitalization has become an important driver of tourist loyalty in modern tourism
destinations.
Digital technology simplifies various stages of the customer journey, beginning
with information searching, reservation, payment, communication, and post -visit
interaction. Tourists who experience seamless digital services tend to perceive greater
value fr om the accommodation, increasing their willingness to revisit the same
homestay and recommend it to others through electronic word -of-mouth (Enrique &
Luna, 2022; Fongiman et al., 2025).
Interestingly, the effect size analysis indicates a moderate contribution (f² =
0.199), suggesting that although digitalization directly improves loyalty, its influence
is further strengthened through psychological mechanisms such as trust. This finding
illustrates that digital innovation alone is insufficient to create long-term loyalty unless
accompanied by tourists' confidence in the reliability of the service provider.

The Effect of Trust on Tourist Loyalty
The analysis further reveals that trust significantly influences tourist loyalty (β
= 0.400; t = 5.312; p < 0.001). This result confirms that tourists who trust homestay
providers are more willing to revisit the accommodation and recommend it to other
potential visitors. This finding supports previous studies indicating that trust is one
of the strongest determinants of customer loyalty within digital tourism services
(Suryana, 2023; Ibrahim et al., 2025).
Trust reduces tourists' perceived uncertainty regarding accommodation
quality, transaction security, and service consistency. When tourists believe that the
information provided by homestay operators accurately reflects actual service
performance, they dev elop stronger emotional attachment and commitment toward
the accommodation (Chouykaew et al., 2024).
This finding supports Relationship Marketing Theory, which considers trust as
one of the primary determinants of long-term customer relationships. Within tourism
services, trust functions as the psychological foundation that transforms positive
service experiences into enduring loyalty. These findings are also consistent with the
SERVQUAL framework proposed by Parasuraman et al. (1988), which suggests that
high-quality services strengthen customer trust and eventually encourage customer
loyalty.
The moderate effect size (f² = 0.200) indicates that trust contributes substantially
to explaining tourist loyalty. Accordingly, homestay managers should prioritize
maintaining service consistency, honesty, responsiveness, and transparency to
preserve tourists' confidence over time. Strengthening trust through reliable digital
services will ultimately encourage repeat visits and positive electronic word-of-mouth
among tourists (Lam et al., 2024; Ghosh et al., 2025).

Tourist Loyalty, as well as between Tourist Satisfaction and Tourist Loyalty. These
findings indicate that trust serves as a crucial psychological mechanism through
which digital services and tourist satisfaction are translated into long -term tourist
loyalty.
From a theoretical perspective, this study extends the literature on digital
tourism and relationship marketing by confirming the complementary roles of
digitalization, satisfaction, and trust in fostering tourist loyalty within community -
based homestay se rvices. Unlike many previous studies that primarily focused on
hotels or online travel platforms, this research provides empirical evidence from
homestay businesses operating in a rural tourism destination. The results highlight
that digital transformation  alone is insufficient to strengthen tourist loyalty unless
accompanied by the development of tourists' trust. Therefore, trust should be
considered a key mediating construct in future tourism loyalty models.
From a practical perspective, the findings suggest that homestay operators
should prioritize the development of user-friendly digital reservation systems, secure
digital payment facilities, responsive online communication, and transparent service
information. Tourism managers and local governments are also encouraged to
strengthen the digital capabilities of community -based accommodation providers
through training programs, technological support, and digital marketing initiatives.
Such efforts are expected to improve tourists' trust and satisfaction, thereby increasing
their loyalty and contributing to the long -term competitiveness of rural tourism
destinations.
This study has several limitations. First, the research was conducted only in the
Telaga Sarangan Tourism Destination, limiting the generalizability of the findings to
other tourism destinations. Second, the model included only four constructs, whereas
other variables such as destination image, service quality, perceived value, electronic
word-of-mouth, or tourist experience may also influence tourist loyalty. Future
studies are therefore encouraged to examine broader geographical contexts, employ
longitudinal research designs, and incorporate additional variables to provide a more
comprehensive understanding of tourist loyalty in the context of digital tourism.
