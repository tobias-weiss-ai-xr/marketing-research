ARTICLE INFO ABSTRACT
Received:  May 19th, 2026 This study examines factors associated with individual customers’ decisions to make
online savings deposits at SHB Dong Nai Branch. The model includes perceived
usefulness, perceived ease of use, trust, security and perceived risk, social influence,
and in centive policies and interest rates. A mixed -methods design combined
qualitative discussions with seven banking officers and a survey of 475 customers
who had used online savings deposits. Data were analyzed using Cronbach’s Alpha,
Exploratory Factor Analy sis, and multiple linear regression. All six factors were
positively and statistically significantly associated with online savings deposit
decisions. In this sample, incentive policies and interest rates (β = 0.428) and
security/perceived risk (β = 0.425)  had the two largest standardized coefficients.
Because the difference between these coefficients was not formally tested, the study
does not claim that the former is significantly stronger than the latter. The findings
inform managerial implications conce rning financial attractiveness, transaction
security, trust, and digital service experience.

1. INTRODUCTION
In the context of the Fourth Industrial Revolution, digital
transformation has become an inevitable trend and a key
driver of development in the banking sector [1, 2]. In
Vietnam, commercial banks have increasingly applied
digital technologies to improve s ervice quality, reduce
operating costs, and enhance customer experience. Among
digital banking products, online savings deposits have
attracted growing attention because they allow customers
to open, manage, renew, and close savings accounts
through Intern et Banking or Mobile Banking without
visiting a physical branch [4].
For individual customers, online savings deposits offer
several advantages, such as convenience, time savings,
flexible transactions, and potentially more attractive
interest rates and promotional policies. However, the
adoption of this service remains uneven, especially in local
markets outside major urban centers [5-6]. Many customers
still prefer traditional over-the-counter savings deposits due
to concerns about security risks, limited trust in online
transactions, unfamiliarity with digital banking
applications, or habits formed through long -term use of
conventional banking services.
At Saigon – Hanoi Commercial Joint Stock Bank, Dong
Nai Branch, online savings deposits are considered an
important product in the bank’s digital transformation and
retail banking development strategy. Although the branch
has made efforts to promote digita l banking services, the
proportion of customers choosing online savings deposits
has not fully matched its potential. Therefore, identifying
the factors that influence customers’ decisions to use online
savings deposit services is both theoretically and practically
significant.
This study aims to examine the factors affecting
individual customers’ decisions to make online savings
deposits at SHB Dong Nai Branch. The findings provide
empirical evidence for understanding customer behavior in
digital banking and suggest managerial implications to help
the branch improve service quality, strengthen customer
trust, enhance security, and design more attractive incentive
and interest rate policies.
The academic contribution of this study lies in integrating
technology-acceptance determinants (perceived usefulness
and perceived ease of use) with trust, security/risk
perception, social influence, and financial incentives in a
single empirical framework  for online savings deposits.
Unlike studies that focus on general Internet/Mobile
Banking adoption, this study examines a deposit product in
which customers simultaneously evaluate digital usability,
protection of accumulated financial assets, and expecte d
financial returns. The evidence from SHB Dong Nai
Branch therefore adds context -specific insight for an
emerging provincial market in Vietnam and identifies the
relative importance of these determinants for online savings
deposit decisions.
2. LITERATURE REVIEW AND RESEARCH
MODEL
2.1 Literature review
An online savings deposit is a specific form of digital
banking service that allows customers to conduct term
deposit transactions through electronic channels such as
Internet Banking or Mobile Banking. Instead of visiting a
physical branch, customers can open, monitor, renew, or
close savings accounts directly on the bank’s digital
platform [7]. In essence, online savings deposits still
represent a traditional capital mobilization activity of
commercial banks, but the service delivery process is
digitalized to improve convenience, reduce transaction
time, and enhance customer experience [8-9].
The decision to make online savings deposits can be
viewed as both a financial behavior and a technology
adoption behavior. From the perspective of consumer
behavior, customers make this decision after evaluating
expected benefits, perceived costs, possibl e risks, and the
suitability of the service for their personal needs  [9]. From
the technology adoption perspective, customers are more
likely to use online savings services when they perceive the
service as useful, easy to use, secure, and reliable.
Therefore, studies on digital banking adoption provide an
important theoretical foundation for examining customers’
online savings deposit decisions [10].
Several theoretical models have been widely applied to
explain customers’ acceptance of digital financial services.
The Technology Acceptance Model emphasizes perceived
usefulness and perceived ease of use as key determinants of
technology adoption [11]. In the context of online savings
deposits, perceived usefulness reflects the extent to which
customers believe that the service helps them save time,
manage deposits conveniently, and gain better financial
benefits. Perceived ease of use refers to the degree to which
customers believe that the online savings process is simple,
understandable, and effortless. In addition, the Theory of
Planned Behavior suggests that behavioral decisions are
influenced by attitudes, subjective norms, and perceived
behavioral control [12-13]. This theory is relevant because
customers’ decisions to deposit money online may be
affected not only by personal evaluations but also by social
pressure and confidence in using digital banking platforms.
The Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of
Technology further expands the explanation of technology
adoption by incorporating performance expectancy, effort
expectancy, social influence, and facilitating conditions
[14]. For online savings deposits, social influence may
come from family members, friends, colleagues, media, or
bank staff. Positive recommendations and successful
experiences from others can encourage customers to adopt
the service, while negative informatio n about online risks
may discourage them [15].
Previous studies on Internet Banking, Mobile Banking,
and digital banking have also highlighted the importance of
trust, security, perceived risk, and financial incentives [16].
Trust is especially important in online financial transactions
because customers need to believe that the bank can protect
their money, personal information, and transaction records.
Security and perceived risk directly affect customers’
willingness to c onduct financial transactions on digital

2.2 Research model and hypothesis development
Based on the literature review and the theoretical
foundations of the Technology Acceptance Model, the
Theory of Planned Behavior, and the Unified Theory of
Acceptance and Use of Technology, this study proposes a
research model to examine the factors affec ting individual
customers’ decisions to make online savings deposits at
SHB Dong Nai Branch. The dependent variable of the
model is the decision to make online savings deposits. The
independent variables include perceived usefulness,
perceived ease of use,  trust, security and perceived risk,
social influence, and incentive policies and interest rates.
These factors are expected to have positive effects on
customers’ online savings deposit decisions [18].
Perceived usefulness refers to the extent to which
customers believe that using online savings deposit services
brings practical benefits. In the banking context, customers
may perceive online savings deposits as useful when the
service helps them save tim e, conduct transactions
conveniently, manage savings accounts more flexibly, and
access deposit services anytime and anywhere. Compared
with traditional counter -based savings deposits, online
savings services reduce the need to visit a bank branch and
allow customers to perform transactions through digital
channels [10, 19]. When customers clearly recognize these
benefits, they are more likely to choose online savings
deposits. Therefore, the first hypothesis is proposed as
follows:
H1: Perceived usefulness has a positive effect on
individual customers’ decisions to make online savings
deposits at SHB Dong Nai Branch.
Perceived ease of use reflects the degree to which
customers believe that using online savings deposit services
is simple and does not require much effort [3, 20]. For
digital banking services, ease of use is demonstrated
through a user-friendly interface, clear instructions, simple
procedures, quick processing, and limited technical
difficulties. If customers find the online savings deposit
process easy to understand and easy to perform, they may
feel more confident and less hesitant to use the service.
Conversely, complicated procedures or unclear instructions
may reduce customers’ willingness to adopt online savings
deposits. Thus, the following hypothesis is suggested:
H2: Perceived ease of use has a positive effect on
individual customers’ decisions to make online savings
deposits at SHB Dong Nai Branch.
Trust is considered a key factor in online financial
transactions. It represents customers’ confidence in the
bank’s reputation, service quality, transaction accuracy,
and commitment to protecting customer rights [5, 21].
Since savings deposits are directl y related to customers’
financial assets, customers tend to be cautious when
deciding whether to deposit money through digital
channels. They are more likely to use online savings
services when they trust that the bank can process
transactions correctly, p rotect their money, and provide
timely support if problems occur. Therefore, trust is
expected to encourage customers’ online savings deposit
decisions. The third hypothesis is stated as follows:
H3: Trust has a positive effect on individual customers’
decisions to make online savings deposits at SHB Dong Nai
Branch.
Security and perceived risk are especially important in
digital banking because customers often worry about fraud,
information leakage, system errors, and financial loss. In
this study, the factor is understood in terms of customers’
positive perception of  the bank’s security measures and
their belief that risks are well controlled [7, 21]. When
customers feel that the online savings deposit system is
secure, transactions are protected, and personal information
is safeguarded, their concerns about online ri sks will
decrease. As a result, they may be more willing to make
savings deposits through digital banking platforms. Hence,
the fourth hypothesis is proposed:
H4: Security and perceived risk have a positive effect on
individual customers’ decisions to make online savings
deposits at SHB Dong Nai Branch.
Social influence refers to the extent to which customers
are affected by people around them or by information from
the social environment when making decisions. In the
context of online savings deposits, customers may be
influenced by family members, frien ds, colleagues, bank
staff, media, and social networks [8, 22]. Positive
recommendations, successful user experiences, or
promotional communication from reliable sources can
encourage customers to try and continue using online
savings services. In contrast, negative opinions or concerns
shared by others may reduce customers’ confidence.
Therefore, social influence is expected to play a role in
shaping customers’ online savings deposit decisions. The
fifth hypothesis is formulated as follows:
H5: Social influence has a positive effect on individual
customers’ decisions to make online savings deposits at
SHB Dong Nai Branch.
Incentive policies and interest rates represent the
financial attractiveness that customers perceive when
choosing online savings deposits. In the Vietnamese
banking market, individual customers often compare
interest rates, promotional programs, deposit t erms, and
related benefits before making savings decisions [14 -16,
23]. Online savings deposits may become more attractive
when banks offer higher interest rates than counter -based
deposits, promotional rewards, flexible renewal options, or

3. RESEARCH METHODS
3.1 Qualitative research
Qualitative research was conducted as the first stage of
the study to explore, adjust, and complete the research
model, measurement scales, and questionnaire items before
implementing the official quantitative survey  [24]. This
stage was necessary because online savings deposits are
associated with both financial behavior and technology
adoption behavior; therefore, the measurement scales
needed to be reviewed carefully to ensure their suitability
for the practical context of SHB Dong Nai Branch.
The qualitative research was carried out through group
discussion with seven banking officers currently working
at SHB Dong Nai Branch. These participants included
managers and staff directly involved in capital mobilization
activities, customer service, a nd the implementation of
digital banking products. The discussion focused on several
key contents, including the current situation of online
savings deposit services at the branch, factors that may
influence individual customers’ decisions to use online
savings deposits, and the relevance of the proposed
variables and observed items inherited from previous
studies.
The results of the qualitative discussion confirmed the
appropriateness of six factors in the proposed research
model: perceived usefulness, perceived ease of use, trust,
security and perceived risk, social influence, and incentive
policies and interest rates. In addition, several questionnaire
items were adjusted in terms of wording to make them
clearer, more understandable, and more suitable for
individual customers at SHB Dong Nai Branch. The
findings from this stage provided an important basis for
finalizing the official questionnaire and preparing for the
quantitative research stage.

dependent variable in the research model. The quantitative
method was considered appropriate because the study

3.2.1 Sampling and data collection
The target respondents of the quantitative survey were
individual customers who had used or were using online
savings deposit services at SHB Dong Nai Branch. These
customers were selected because they had practical
experience with the service and could pr ovide reliable
assessments of the factors affecting their decisions. The
questionnaire was designed based on the research model
and the measurement scales developed from previous
studies, then adjusted through the qualitative research
stage. The official q uestionnaire consisted of two main
parts. The first part collected demographic information,
including gender, age, education level, occupation, income,
and other relevant characteristics. The second part
measured the observed variables of the research mode l,
including perceived usefulness, perceived ease of use, trust,
security, and perceived risk, social influence, incentive
policies and interest rates, and online savings deposit
decision.
The observed items were measured using a five -point
Likert scale, ranging from 1 “strongly disagree” to 5
“strongly agree”. This scale was chosen because it is widely
used in studies on customer behavior and technology
adoption. It allows respondents to ex press their level of
agreement with each statement and provides suitable data
for reliability testing, exploratory factor analysis, and
regression analysis. Before conducting the official survey,
the questionnaire was reviewed to ensure that the wording
was clear, understandable, and appropriate for individual
customers in the banking context.
The study distributed 500 questionnaires to individual
customers at SHB Dong Nai Branch. After screening the
collected responses, 475 valid questionnaires were retained
for data analysis. Invalid responses, such as incomplete
answers or responses showing insufficient reliability, were
removed from the dataset. The final sample size of 475 was
considered appropriate for the multivariate statistical
analyses used, especially Exploratory Factor Analysis and
multiple linear regression. A non -probability convenience
sampling approach was used within the eligible customer
population because a complete sampling frame of online -
savings users was not available for random selection.
Eligibility required respondents to be individual customers
of SHB Dong Nai Branch who  had used or were using the
online savings deposit service and who completed the
questionnaire sufficiently for analysis. Of 500
questionnaires distributed, 475 valid responses were
retained (95.0%). Accordingly, the sample supports
analysis of relationshi ps within the surveyed branch
context, but convenience sampling does not provide
probability-based representativeness of all SHB customers
or the wider Vietnamese banking population.
The collected data were coded, cleaned, and analyzed
using statistical software. The data analysis process
included several main steps. First, descriptive statistics
were used to summarize the demographic characteristics of
respondents and provide an overv iew of the sample
structure. This step helped identify the distribution of
customers by gender, age, income, education level, and
occupation, thereby offering an initial understanding of the
surveyed customer group.
3.2.2 Measurement and reliability testing
The reliability of the measurement scales was evaluated
using Cronbach’s Alpha coefficient. This test was
conducted to determine the internal consistency of the
observed items within each factor. Items with low item -
total correlation or items that reduced the reliability of the
scale were considered for removal. A scale was considered
reliable when its Cronbach’s Alpha coefficient met the
commonly accepted threshold. This step ensured that the
observed items used to measure each construct were
consistent and suitable for further analysis [24].
Exploratory Factor Analysis was applied to examine the
convergent and discriminant validity of the measurement
scales. The purpose of EFA was to identify the underlying
factor structure among the observed variables and
determine whether the observed items were grouped
consistently with the proposed research model. The Kaiser-
Meyer-Olkin coefficient and Bartlett’s test of sphericity
were used to assess the suitability of the data for factor
analysis. Factor loadings were examined to determine
whether each ob served item had sufficient explanatory
value for its corresponding factor. Items with weak factor
loadings or cross-loadings could be removed to improve the
validity of the scales [25].
3.2.3 Data analysis techniques
Multiple linear regression analysis was conducted to
test the proposed hypotheses and estimate the association
of each independent variable with the online savings
deposit decision. The dependent variable was individual
customers’ decision to make online savings deposits, while
the independent variables were perceived usefulness,
perceived ease of use, trust, security and perceived risk,
social influence, and incentive policies and interest rates.
The regression results were used to assess the direction,
magnitude, and statistical significance of these
associations. Standardized regression coefficients were
compared descriptively within the sample; no formal test of
differences between coefficients was conducted. Multiple
linear regression was selected rather than SEM because the
primary objective was to estimate direct associations of six
predictors with one outcome after scale reliability and
factor structure had been established by Cronbach’s Alpha
and EFA. The study did not specify mediation, moderation,
or a simultaneous measurement –structural model that
would require SEM. Composite construct scores were
therefore used in regression, providing a parsimonious and
readily interpretable test of H1 –H6; SEM remains an
appropriate extension for future confirmatory research.
In addition, the study examined the assumptions of the
multiple regression model to ensure the reliability of the
estimated results. Multicollinearity was assessed through
tolerance values and variance inflation factors. The
normality of residuals was chec ked through graphical
methods, while the independence of errors and the
suitability of the regression model were also considered.
These diagnostic tests helped confirm whether the
regression model was appropriate and whether the

4. RESEARCH RESULTS
4.1 Data analysis on the demographic variables
The demographic characteristics of the survey sample

Discussion of findings
The research findings show that all six proposed factors
are positively and statistically significantly associated with
individual customers’ decisions to make online savings
deposits at SHB Dong Nai Branch. The pattern suggests
that online savings deposit  decisions are related to a
combination of financial, technological, psychological, and
social factors. Incentive policies and interest rates (β =
0.428) and security/perceived risk (β = 0.425) have the two
largest standardized coefficients in the sample. Because
their difference was not formally tested, the results do not
establish that the first factor is significantly stronger than
the second.
Security and perceived risk show a positive association
with online savings deposit decisions. This is substantively
relevant because online savings deposits involve
customers’ financial assets and perceptions of transaction
safety. Customers who report st ronger confidence in
system security and risk control also report higher
willingness to use online savings services; however, the
cross-sectional data do not establish a causal effect.
Trust is also positively associated with customers’
decisions. This pattern is consistent with the relevance of
bank reputation, accurate transaction records, transparent
information, and timely customer support to customer
confidence in online savings services. Perceived ease of use
and perceived usefulness are likewise positively associated
with decision scores, consistent with technology -
acceptance theory.
Social influence has the smallest standardized
coefficient in the fitted model but remains statistically
significant. Recommendations from relatives, friends,
colleagues, bank staff, and media may accompany
customers’ decisions, although the coefficient or dering
alone should not be interpreted as a causal hierarchy.
Overall, the findings provide empirical support for the
proposed model within the surveyed sample and inform,
rather than mechanically determine, managerial priorities.
5. CONCLUSIONS AND MANAGERIAL
 RECOMMENDATIONS
5.1 Conclusions
This study examined the factors affecting individual
customers’ decisions to make online savings deposits at
SHB Dong Nai Branch in the context of digital
transformation in the banking sector. Based on the
Technology Acceptance Model, the Theory of Planned
Behavior, and the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use
of Technology, the study proposed and tested a research
model with six independent variables: perceived
usefulness, perceived ease of use, trust, security and
perceived risk, social influence, and inc entive policies and
interest rates.
Using data collected from 475 valid questionnaires, the
study applied reliability testing, Exploratory Factor
Analysis, and multiple linear regression analysis. All six
factors show positive and statistically significant
associations with customers’ online  savings deposit
decisions in the surveyed sample. Incentive policies and
interest rates (β = 0.428) and security/perceived risk (β =
0.425) have the two largest standardized coefficients; no
formal test was conducted to determine whether the
difference be tween these two coefficients is statistically
significant.These findings indicate that customers’ online
savings deposit decisions are associated with a combination
of financial benefits, perceived safety, trust in the bank,
convenience, usefulness, and so cial encouragement.

5.2 Managerial recommendations
The standardized regression coefficients provide a
descriptive view of the associations observed in this
sample: incentive policies and interest rates (β = 0.428),
security and perceived risk (β = 0.425), trust (β = 0.303),
perceived ease of use (β = 0.205 ), perceived usefulness (β
= 0.158), and social influence (β = 0.098). Because
differences between coefficients were not formally tested,
this ordering should not be treated as a statistically
established hierarchy or as the sole basis for allocating
managerial resources. The recommendations below
therefore combine the regression evidence with practical
considerations of customer value, security, trust, usability,
implementation feasibility, and risk.
Financial attractiveness should be considered an
important managerial area because incentive policies and
interest rates have one of the two largest standardized
coefficients in the sample (β = 0.428). Where commercially
feasible, the branch can clearly co mmunicate online -rate
advantages, design targeted promotions by customer
segment, and use renewal - or tenure -based incentives.
Effectiveness should be monitored through conversion to
online deposits, renewal rates, and incremental balances
rather than inferred from the coefficient alone.
Security/risk control (β = 0.425) and trust (β = 0.303)
should also receive sustained managerial attention.
Practical actions include multi -layer authentication,
immediate transaction confirmation, plain -language safety
guidance, transparent display of deposit terms and maturity
conditions, and a fast -response channel for digital
incidents. Branch staff should be trained to explain how
online deposits are recorded, protected, and supported so
that technical security is translated into customer-perceived
security and trust.
The branch should also simplify the online -savings
customer journey because perceived ease of use (β = 0.205)
and perceived usefulness (β = 0.158) are positively
associated with decision scores. Relevant actions include
reducing unnecessary steps, making r ates/terms/renewal
options visible before confirmation, providing short
QR/video guides, and offering hands -on assistance for
customers less familiar with mobile banking.
Communication can emphasize concrete benefits such as
time savings, remote access, deposit monitoring, and
flexible management of idle funds.
Social influence (β = 0.098) can be used as a
complementary communication channel. Staff
recommendations, customer referrals, testimonials, and
concise messages through SHB Mobile and other permitted
channels may reinforce adoption. Resource decisions
across these actions should be based on expected benefits,
implementation costs, operational and security risks,
customer feedback, and further testing —not solely on the
numerical ordering of standardized regression coefficients.
5.3 Limitations and future research
Although this study achieved its research objectives,
several limitations remain. First, the research was
conducted only at SHB Dong Nai Branch, so the findings
may not fully represent the online savings deposit behavior
of individual customers at other SH B branches or other
commercial banks. Differences in customer characteristics,
local market conditions, service quality, and digital banking
strategies may lead to different results in other contexts.
Second, the quantitative survey used non -probability
convenience sampling among eligible customers at one
branch. Although the final sample of 475 is sufficient for
the statistical analyses used, the absence of probability
sampling limits population -level representativeness and
may introduce selection bias. Future studies should use
stratified or probability -based sampling across multiple
branches where a sampling frame is available.
Third, the study used cross -sectional survey data
collected at one point in time. The reported coefficients
therefore represent statistical associations within the survey
period and do not establish causal effects or the temporal
direction of relationships . Customer perceptions and
decisions may change as interest rates, promotional
policies, technology, regulations, or cybersecurity risks
evolve. Longitudinal or repeated -measure designs are
recommended to assess temporal changes and strengthen
causal interpretation.
Fourth, the independent and dependent variables were
measured from the same self -reported questionnaire at the
same time, so common method bias cannot be ruled out.
The present analysis did not include a dedicated common -
method-bias diagnostic. Future rese arch should apply
procedural remedies (e.g., temporal or source separation
and improved item design) together with appropriate
statistical diagnostics to assess and reduce this risk.
Fifth, the research model focused on six main factors:
perceived usefulness, perceived ease of use, trust, security
and perceived risk, social influence, and incentive policies
and interest rates. Although these factors explained a
relatively high proporti on of the variation in customers’
decisions, other possible factors were not included, such as
service quality, financial literacy, customer experience,
brand image, digital banking habit, and personal risk
preference.
Future research should expand the survey scope to other
SHB branches or compare different commercial banks to
increase the generalizability of the findings. In addition,
