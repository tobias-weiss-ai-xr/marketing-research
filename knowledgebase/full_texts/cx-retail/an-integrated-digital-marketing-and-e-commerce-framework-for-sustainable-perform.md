ABSTRACT
Background: Small and medium enterprises (SMEs) face a critical gap in understanding how
to systematically integrate digital marketing and e-commerce capabilities to create competitive
and sustainable value. While digital transformation presents significant opportunities for SMEs,
a comprehensive theoretical framework addressing both operational integration mechanisms
and strategic performance outcomes within a sustainability lens remains underdeveloped in the
literature.
Purpose: This study develops and proposes a comprehensive theoretical framework for
integrating digital marketing and e-commerce capabilities within SMEs to achieve sustainable
performance, specifically addressing how these capabilities can be coordinated to influence
customer experience and marketing performance among both business-to-business and business-
to-consumer SMEs.
Design/Methodology/Approach: This study employs a systematic literature review and
conceptual synthesis (PRISMA 2020), drawing from digital business strategy, dynamic
capabilities theory, and sustainable business model literature. The framework development follows
a structured theoretical grounding process, integrating insights from 82 sources, comprising a
core corpus of peer-reviewed journal articles and a smaller set of background references (books,
seminal texts, and one standards document) across digital marketing, e-commerce, and SME
strategy domains.
Findings/Results: This study develops an integrated framework comprising five core
components: digital marketing, e-commerce integration, technology infrastructure, organizational
capabilities, and strategic capabilities, all converging toward sustainable performance. The
framework identifies four integration mechanisms, namely data integration, process integration,
technology integration, and customer journey integration, as proposed enablers of SME digital
transformation. These mechanisms are proposed, from synthesis of the reviewed literature,
to shape customer perceptions of value, trust, and journey consistency in ways that plausibly
support conversion and retention.
Conclusions: The framework is conceptual and requires empirical validation across different
SME contexts and industries. Nevertheless, it offers SME managers, consultants, and
policymakers a systematic roadmap for digital transformation, intended to guide capability
development, resource allocation, and performance measurement for sustainable digital growth.
It is also intended to inform SME resilience and competitiveness in digital economies.
Originality/value (State of the art): Existing frameworks address single capability domains
in isolation without integrating them. Prior work has identified this integration gap. This
framework's distinct contribution is specifying the mechanisms through which digital marketing
and e-commerce capabilities jointly shape outcomes for resource-constrained SMEs. This
contribution is theoretical and, comprises propositions pending empirical testing.
Keywords: digital marketing, e-commerce integration, SME strategy, sustainable performance,
digital transformation, dynamic capabilities

INTRODuCTION
Small and medium enterprises (SMEs) face
unprecedented challenges in navigating digital
transformation while simultaneously managing
resource constraints and competitive pressures (Kraus
et al. 2021; Verhoef et al. 2021). The rapid acceleration
of digitalization, particularly following the COVID-19
pandemic, has made the integration of digital marketing
and e-commerce capabilities a critical survival
mechanism for SMEs (Donthu & Gustafsson, 2020;
Pantano et al. 2020). This integration extends beyond
technology adoption to include strategic alignment,
organizational capability development, and sustainable
value creation (Matt et al. 2015; Vial, 2019). The
global digital economy has fundamentally altered the
competitive landscape for SMEs, compelling these
organizations to review their strategic orientations and
operational models. SMEs collectively represent the
backbone of most national economies, accounting for
most of the employment and a significant share of GDP
across both developed and emerging markets (Kraus
et al. 2021; OECD, 2023). However, their capacity to
harness digital technologies for competitive advantage
remains constrained by structural limitations, including
capital scarcity, limited managerial bandwidth, and
inadequate digital expertise (Ramdani et al. 2022).
These limitations make it imperative to develop
purpose-built frameworks that address SME-specific
needs rather than simply downscaling large-enterprise
models.
Digital marketing and e-commerce represent two of the
most transformative domains in contemporary business
strategy, yet their integration within the SME context
has received insufficient scholarly attention (Kwarteng-
Amaniampong & Tait, 2026). Digital marketing
encompasses the full spectrum of online customer
engagement activities, from content creation and social
media management to search engine optimization
and data-driven campaign analytics. E-commerce,
by contrast, refers to the infrastructure and processes
that enable online commercial transactions, including
platform architecture, secure payment systems, supply
chain management, and post-purchase customer service
(Laudon & Traver, 2016). When aligned strategically,
these two domains create compounding value by
enhancing customer reach, reducing transaction costs,
and enabling data-informed decision-making across
the customer lifecycle.
Existing literature predominantly focuses on large
enterprises or treats digital marketing and e-commerce
as separate domains, leaving a significant gap in
understanding how SMEs can systematically integrate
these capabilities for sustainable performance (Hanelt et
al. 2021; Ramdani et al. 2022; Kwarteng-Amaniampong
& Tait, 2026). The fragmented nature of prior studies
has produced limited theoretical frameworks addressing
SME-specific needs. More specifically, Kannan and Li
(2017) model digital marketing capabilities without
addressing e-commerce infrastructure; Trainor et
al.  (2014) examine social CRM as a relationship-
building capability rather than a cross-functional
integration mechanism; and Lemon and Verhoef
(2016) map the customer journey without specifying
the underlying digital marketing and e-commerce
capabilities that develop it. Kwarteng-Amaniampong
and Tait (2026) identify the resulting integration gap
but do not construct a framework to fill the gap. This
study addresses this specific, unresolved boundary:
how digital marketing and e-commerce capabilities,
once integrated through defined mechanisms, translate
into customer-level responses and SME performance.
This study’s novelty lies in repositioning digital
integration as a strategic imperative rather than a
purely technological undertaking, within a resource-
constrained SME context. A critical review of extant
literature reveals that digital transformation studies in
the SME domain have largely adopted a technology-
centric perspective, prioritizing adoption rates and tool
utilization over strategic integration and capability
development (Vial, 2019; Kraus et al. 2021). While
studies such as Bharadwaj et al.  (2013) and Nambisan
et al.  (2019) have advanced digital business strategy
theory at the enterprise level, their applicability to
SMEs, which are characterized by flat organizational
structures, owner-managed operations, and limited
slack resources, remains theoretically underdeveloped.
The present study addresses this deficit by synthesizing
insights from dynamic capabilities theory, digital
business strategy literature, and sustainable business
model research to construct an SME-specific integrative
framework. Furthermore, existing frameworks have
tended to evaluate digital performance through narrow
financial metrics, neglecting the broader sustainability
dimensions that increasingly shape stakeholder
expectations and regulatory requirements (Elkington,
1997; Porter & Kramer, 2011). This study proposes
embedding economic, social, and environmental
performance indicators into its framework architecture,
positioning sustainability not as a peripheral

METHODS
This study relies exclusively on secondary data;
specifically, academic and scholarly literature sourced
from internationally recognized scholarly databases.
Given its theoretical and conceptual orientation, no
primary data were collected. The study draws on 82
sources in total, comprising a core review corpus
of 60 peer-reviewed journal articles, from which
the framework’s components and mechanisms are
derived, and 22 background references that provide
conceptual grounding but were not subject to the
same screening protocol as the core corpus. The
background set comprises 15 books, one book chapter,
three practitioner-oriented Harvard Business Review
articles, two policy reports (African Union, 2020;
OECD, 2023), and one technical standards document
(Mell & Grance, 2011). The literature was retrieved
from Scopus, Web of Science, Google Scholar, and
EBSCOhost. Sources were selected across three
core domains: digital marketing, e-commerce, and
SME strategy, supplemented by foundational works
in dynamic capabilities theory, sustainable business
models, and digital business strategy. The temporal
scope spans foundational contributions from the 1990s
consideration but as a central evaluative dimension of

This study addresses the identified gaps by developing
a comprehensive framework that integrates digital
marketing and e-commerce capabilities in an SME
context, highlighting clear pathways to sustainable
organizational performance. The approach draws on
three theoretical traditions: digital business strategy
literature, which provides the architecture for capability
alignment; dynamic capabilities theory, which
explains how SMEs reconfigure digital competencies
in changing environments (Bharadwaj et al. 2013;
Nambisan et al. 2019); and sustainable business
model thinking, which balances operational efficiency
with long-term value creation (Soto-Acosta, 2020;
Soluk & Kammerlander, 2021). Methodologically,
the study employs a systematic literature review
informed by the PRISMA 2020 reporting guidelines,
analyzing 82 sources to identify convergent themes,
theoretical gaps, and practical integration pathways.
This approach enables rigorous theoretical synthesis
while maintaining contextual sensitivity to the
distinctive challenges and opportunities confronting
SMEs in varied digital environments. The framework
development process adopts a deductive-inductive
logic: grounded in established theoretical traditions
while remaining responsive to emergent patterns and
SME-specific dynamics identified through thematic
analysis (Thomas & Harden, 2008). The problem-
solving approach adopted in this study recognizes that
SME digital transformation is not a single discrete event
but a continuous, iterative process requiring sustained
capability investment and adaptive management (Matt
et al. 2015; Warner & Wäger, 2019). The IDM-EC
framework presented herein provides a structured
yet flexible architecture that accommodates this
complexity, guiding SME managers through phased
capability development while offering policymakers
and consultants a theoretically grounded basis for a
diagnostic tool for contextualized digital transformation
support.
This study advances an SME-specific integration

E-Commerce Capabilities and SME Performance
The integration of e-commerce capabilities has emerged
as a key driver of performance and competitiveness
among SMEs, enabling expanded market reach, reduced
transaction costs, and new revenue streams (Zhu &
Kraemer, 2005; Oliveira & Martins, 2011). E-commerce
adoption accelerated significantly in the wake of
COVID-19 (Pantano et al. 2020; Donthu & Gustafsson,
2020), underlining the strategic imperative for SMEs
to develop robust digital commerce infrastructures.
Critical capability areas identified in the literature
include platform capabilities (DeLone & McLean,
2003), payment and security systems (Kim et al. 2010),
supply chain and logistics (Gunasekaran et al. 2004),
customer service (Chang et al. 2009), and analytics and
business intelligence (Chen et al. 2012). Many SMEs
face challenges in developing robust analytics functions
due to limited resources and personnel shortages
(Mikalef et al. 2019; Raguseo, 2018). These findings
directly inform the E-Commerce Integration component
of the proposed framework, which consolidates
platform development, payment processing, inventory
management, order fulfilment, customer service, and
business intelligence into a coherent capability cluster
oriented toward seamless online selling.
Conceptual foundation
The theoretical framework proposed in this study
is informed by an extensive review of the extant
literature spanning digital transformation in SME
contexts, digital marketing capabilities, e-commerce
integration, and sustainable performance. This body of
literature collectively establishes the conceptual terrain
from which the framework derives its architecture,
components, and integration logic. The following
synthesis organizes the literature thematically to
illuminate the theoretical lineage underpinning each
framework dimension.
Digital Transformation in SME Contexts
Digital transformation has become a pivotal strategic
priority for organizations of all scales, fundamentally
redefining the mechanisms through which value is
created, delivered, and captured (Bharadwaj et al. 2013;
Vial, 2019). SMEs often possess inherent advantages
in navigating digital transformation, including
organizational agility, expedited decision-making, and
close customer proximity, which enhance their capacity
to adapt and innovate in dynamic digital environments
(Nambisan et al. 2019; Soluk & Kammerlander, 2021).
Nevertheless, SMEs encounter distinct challenges,
including limited resources, digital skill gaps, and the
need for tailored strategic approaches aligned with
their operational contexts (Soto-Acosta, 2020; Verhoef
et al. 2021). The existing literature identifies several
critical success factors for SME digital transformation.
Foremost among these is digital leadership, where
managerial roles are pivotal in fostering cultures of
innovation and digital creativity (Matt et al. 2015; Hanelt
et al. 2021). Equally important is strategic alignment
— ensuring that digital investments are closely linked
to overarching business objectives and complement
core business models (Sebastian et al. 2017; Warner
& Wäger, 2019). These findings directly inform the
organizational and strategic capability components
of the proposed framework, foregrounding digital
leadership and strategic alignment as foundational
determinants of integration effectiveness.

Dynamic Capabilities Foundation
The proposed framework is grounded in dynamic
capabilities theory, which emphasizes an organization
‘s ability to sense opportunities and threats, seize
strategic initiatives, and reconfigure resources to sustain
competitive advantage in changing environments
(Teece, 2007; Helfat & Peteraf, 2009). This theoretical
lens offers a robust foundation for examining how
SMEs can develop, deploy, and integrate digital
marketing and e-commerce capabilities to remain agile
and competitive in rapidly evolving digital landscapes.
The dynamic capabilities framework operates across
three interdependent dimensions, each of which maps
directly onto specific framework components and
integration mechanisms:
• Sensing dimension: Includes SMEs’ ability to
identify and evaluate digital opportunities and
threats through market intelligence, customer
analytics, and competitive monitoring (Teece, 2007;
Kindström et al. 2013). These capabilities enable
SMEs to detect evolving customer needs, emerging
market trends, and technological advancements,
informing proactive strategic adaptation. Within
the proposed framework, the sensing dimension is
operationalized through the Strategic Capabilities
component, which incorporates strategic foresight,
market intelligence, and competitive benchmarking,
and is supported by the Data Integration Mechanism,
which provides the informational infrastructure for
continuous environmental scanning.
• Seizing dimension: Reflects SMEs’ capacity to
mobilize resources and capabilities in response to
identified digital opportunities through strategic
investments, partnerships, and organizational
Integration Challenges and Opportunities
Integration opportunities for SMEs include enhanced
customer experience through consistent touchpoint
management, improved operational efficiency
through shared data and processes, and increased
revenue potential through cross-selling and upselling
(Verhoef et al. 2009; Lemon & Verhoef, 2016).
Integration challenges are equally extensive, spanning
technical dimensions such as system compatibility
and data synchronization (Chen & Popovich, 2003),
organizational dimensions including skills development
and change management (Kotter, 2012; Sebastian et al.
2017), and strategic dimensions involving alignment of
digital capabilities with business objectives and channel
conflict management (Brynjolfsson & Hitt, 2000; Porter,
2001). These challenges are particularly acute for SMEs
given their limited resources and need for rapid return on
investment (Laudon & Traver, 2016; Turban et al. 2018).
This body of evidence provides the theoretical rationale
for the framework’s four integration mechanisms (data
integration, process integration, technology integration,
and customer journey integration), each of which is
specifically designed to address identified integration
barriers while capitalizing on the documented
opportunities for synergistic value creation.
Sustainable Performance in the Digital Context
Sustainable performance in an SME context refers to an
organization ‘s ability to sustain economic, social, and
environmental outcomes over time in ways that contribute
to long-term organizational viability and stakeholder
value creation (Elkington, 1997; Porter & Kramer, 2011).
This study distinguishes sustained commercial success
(the durability of financial and customer performance
over time, corresponding to the economic pillar) from
social and environmental sustainability, which are
additional, not automatic, outcomes. Digital marketing
and e-commerce integration can plausibly sustain
commercial performance without generating comparable
social or environmental benefits, and the outcomes may
occasionally trade off against one another. Economic
sustainability involves achieving positive returns on
digital investments while maintaining financial stability
(Melville et al. 2004). Social sustainability encompasses
employee development and community engagement,
drawing on a stakeholder perspective (Freeman et al.
2010). Environmental sustainability involves the energy
consumption, electronic waste, and carbon footprint
implications of digital operations (Melville, 2010;

merely operate, digital marketing and e-commerce
routines is what the seizing dimension specifically
requires. The seizing dimension is enacted
through the Digital Marketing component and the
E-Commerce Integration component, which provide
the operational capabilities through which identified
opportunities are intended to be converted into
market actions, with the integration mechanisms
linking the two.
• Reconfiguring dimension: pertains to SMEs’ ability
to continuously adapt and restructure their resource

The PRISMA 2020 systematic literature review and
theoretical synthesis yielded a comprehensive integrated
framework, the IDM-EC (Integrated Digital Marketing
and E-Commerce) Framework, comprising five core
capability components, four integration mechanisms,
and a multi-dimensional performance measurement

around these three structural elements, elaborated in
the sub-sections that follow. First, the review identified
five interdependent capability components as central to
SME digital transformation success: digital marketing,
e-commerce integration, technology infrastructure,
organizational capabilities, and strategic capabilities.

but mutually reinforcing dimensions of a coherent
digital capability architecture. Second, four integration
mechanisms were identified as enabling the coordination

proposed to translate individual capability investments

Technology Integration Requirements
Technology integration facilitates automated
workflows, synchronized data exchange, and operational
optimization through unified systems (Ross et al. 2006;
Bharadwaj et al. 2013). A well-structured integration
architecture emphasizes three elements: API-based
integration supporting flexible, modular connectivity
between heterogeneous platforms while preserving
system independence; real-time data synchronization
ensuring that customer profiles, inventory levels,
and performance metrics are consistently updated
across platforms, minimizing errors, and enhancing
efficiency; and scalable infrastructure design
enabling SMEs to expand digital operations without
substantial technology reinvestment, with cloud-based
infrastructure offering cost-efficiency, adaptability, and
operational resilience.
Technology Infrastructure Requirements
Technology infrastructure underpins all digital
marketing and e-commerce activities, ensuring
operational scalability, reliability, and efficiency. Cloud
computing enables SMEs to access enterprise-grade
resources without substantial upfront capital, offering

(Armbrust et al. 2010; Mell & Grance, 2011). Data
management and analytics platforms support evidence-

improvement, narrowing the competitiveness gap with
larger enterprises (Chen et al. 2012; Wamba et al. 2015).

operational fragmentation and supporting automated
workflows (Ross et al. 2006; Bharadwaj et al. 2013).
Cybersecurity and data protection measures are
indispensable for regulatory compliance and consumer
trust (Bulgurcu et al. 2010; Crossler et al. 2013).

digital capabilities across data, process, technology,

Recommendations
This study has several limitations, which point to
opportunities for future research. As a review of the
secondary literature, its conclusions are bounded by the
coverage of the retained corpus, by coding conducted
by a single reviewer without an inter-coder reliability
check, and by the heterogeneity of measures used across
the original studies synthesized here. The framework
may therefore not transfer uniformly across the
customer and SME contexts represented by the studies.
It also requires empirical validation across diverse
SME contexts, industries, and geographic regions
using quantitative methods, for which the constructs,
proposed relationships, and level of analysis must first
be specified. Customer-level constructs (perceived
value, trust, and journey consistency) and firm-level
constructs (financial and operational performance)
require different measures and respondents; therefore,
a single SME manager survey would not adequately
capture both. A multi-level or multi-source design,
for example, combining manager-reported firm data
with customer-reported perceptions, is recommended
over a single-source instrument. Once the constructs
and relationships are specified, structural equation
modelling can be used to test the proposed relationships.
Longitudinal case study approaches would better
capture implementation dynamics and provide more
actionable practitioner guidance. Future research should
develop and psychometrically validate measurement
instruments for assessing the relationships between
digital integration efforts and sustainable performance
outcomes. Finally, the framework’s applicability across
varying cultural, regulatory, and market environments
requires systematic examination, particularly in
emerging markets and diverse regulatory contexts.
framework highlights the central role of organizational
and strategic capabilities, particularly digital leadership
and change management, in determining transformation
outcomes. SME owner-managers and senior leaders
should invest in their own digital literacy and cultivate
innovation-oriented organizational cultures that
support experimentation, learning, and continuous
improvement. Engagement with external partners,
technology providers, and digital transformation
consultants is also recommended as a mechanism for
augmenting internal capability gaps, particularly in the
early phases of the roadmap.
CONCLuSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS
Conclusions
This  study  developed  a   theoretical   framework
integrating  digital  marketing and e-commerce
capabilities within SMEs and proposed how such
integration might contribute to sustainable performance
outcomes. These are   propositions  derived from
synthesis, not tested relationships. The IDM-EC
framework agrees with Kannan and Li (2017), Trainor
et al.  (2014), and Lemon and Verhoef (2016) that
digital marketing, social CRM, and customer journey
management each contribute distinct capabilities.
It extends this work by specifying how these
capabilities interact across the digital marketing/e-
commerce boundary for resource-constrained SMEs.
Its incremental contribution beyond Kwarteng-
Amaniampong and Tait (2026) is the structural
framework and integration mechanisms that address
the gap that earlier study identified but did not itself
resolve. This should not be read as implying that
all channels, customer segments, or SMEs benefit
equally from integration. The reviewed corpus reports
considerable variation by sector and resource base, and
the framework’s applicability is bounded accordingly
pending empirical testing. The framework comprises
five core components (digital marketing, e-commerce
integration, technology infrastructure, organizational
capabilities, and strategic capabilities) alongside
four integration mechanisms. These are proposed to
support, rather than guarantee, value creation and
competitive positioning as capabilities are developed
systematically. The central boundary condition is that
these benefits depend on the integration mechanisms
being enacted, which the review corpus does not itself
