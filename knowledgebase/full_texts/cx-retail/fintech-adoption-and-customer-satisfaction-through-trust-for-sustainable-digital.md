Abstract
FinTech is transforming the banking sector by enabling more accessible, eﬃcient and digitally delivered
ﬁnancial services, while also creating challenges related to usability, security, service quality and
customer trust. These factors are important for the development of sustainable digital ﬁnancial services
because technological innovation alone does not ensure continued customer acceptance and
satisfactory service experiences. This study examines the relationships between perceived ease of use,
perceived security and service quality and customer satisfaction among FinTech users in the Malaysian
banking sector, with trust as a mediating variable. The study adopts a quantitative, cross-sectional
correlational design and focuses on Malaysian banking customers aged 18 years and above who have
used at least one FinTech-enabled service offered by their main commercial bank. Data were collected
through a self-administered online questionnaire using Qualtrics, with 300 valid responses obtained.
Established measurement scales were adapted from Davis (1989), Abdullah and Hisamudin (2024),
Parasuraman, et al., (1988), Oliver, (1980) and Đặng and Boratyńska (2026), using a ﬁve-point Likert
scale. Data were analysed using descriptive statistics, Cronbach's alpha, Pearson correlation, multiple
regression and mediation analysis using Hayes' PROCESS approach in SPSS version 29. The ﬁndings
indicate that perceived ease of use, perceived security and service quality are positively associated with
customer satisfaction, while trust signiﬁcantly mediates the relationships between each of these factors
and customer satisfaction. The ﬁndings highlight that trusted, secure, user-centred and high-quality
FinTech services can support sustainable digital transformation in banking by strengthening positive and
enduring customer experiences. The study contributes to the sustainable development context,
particularly Sustainable Development Goals 8 and 9, by demonstrating the importance of customer-
centred and trustworthy digital ﬁnancial services in supporting sustainable ﬁnancial-service innovation.
The ﬁndings provide practical implications for Malaysian banks, FinTech developers and regulators in
designing secure, reliable and sustainable digital banking services.
1. INTRODUCTION
1.1 Background of the Study
The banking sector is undergoing substantial digital transformation, driven by the rapid development and
adoption of ﬁnancial technology (FinTech). FinTech encompasses a wide range of technology-enabled
ﬁnancial innovations, including mobile banking applications, online payment solutions, digital banking
platforms and artiﬁcial intelligence-enabled customer service systems (Hamid et al., 2024). These
innovations have changed how ﬁnancial services are delivered and accessed, while increasing customer
expectations regarding convenience, speed, security and service quality. As banks increasingly integrate
FinTech into their service delivery, understanding the factors that contribute to positive and sustainable
digital banking experiences has become increasingly important.
The development of digital ﬁnancial services is also relevant to the broader sustainable development
agenda. Technological innovation can contribute to more eﬃcient and accessible ﬁnancial-service
Page 2/31

delivery, while sustainable digital transformation requires ﬁnancial institutions to develop services that
customers can use conﬁdently and continuously. In this context, sustainable digital ﬁnancial services
extend beyond the introduction of new technologies to include usability, security, service quality and
trust. When digital banking services are diﬃcult to use, perceived as insecure or provide inconsistent
service quality, technological investment may not translate into satisfactory and sustainable customer
experiences. Thus, understanding the customer-related conditions that support the effective use of
FinTech-enabled banking services is relevant to the long-term sustainability of digital ﬁnancial-service
innovation.
This perspective is particularly relevant to Sustainable Development Goal 8 (SDG 8), which promotes
sustained and sustainable economic growth and productive economic activity, and Sustainable
Development Goal 9 (SDG 9), which emphasises innovation and resilient infrastructure. Within the
banking sector, FinTech-enabled digital services represent an important component of technological
innovation and the transformation of ﬁnancial-service infrastructure. However, the sustainability of this
transformation depends not only on technological development but also on whether customers perceive
digital ﬁnancial services as usable, secure, reliable and trustworthy. Consequently, examining customer
responses to FinTech-enabled banking services provides an important customer-centred perspective on
sustainable digital ﬁnancial transformation.
From the customer perspective, the increasing availability of FinTech services has changed expectations
regarding the quality and convenience of banking services. Existing research indicates that factors such
as perceived ease of use, security and service quality are important in shaping customers' experiences
with digital ﬁnancial services. However, positive perceptions of these characteristics may not
automatically result in customer satisfaction. Trust may provide an important psychological mechanism
through which customers evaluate and respond to FinTech-enabled banking services. Customers who
perceive a digital banking service as easy to use, secure and reliable may be more likely to develop
conﬁdence in the service provider, which can contribute to higher satisfaction.
Therefore, examining the relationships among perceived ease of use, perceived security, service quality,
trust and customer satisfaction can provide a more comprehensive understanding of customer
responses to FinTech-enabled banking. In particular, investigating trust as a mediating mechanism can
clarify how technological and service-related characteristics are translated into customer satisfaction.
Such understanding is relevant not only to customer experience but also to the development of trusted
and sustainable digital ﬁnancial services.

The present study focuses on the Malaysian commercial banking sector, comprising licensed
commercial banks regulated by Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM). Malaysian banks have increasingly
implemented digital banking systems and FinTech-enabled channels as part of their service delivery.
Malaysia provides an appropriate context for examining sustainable digital ﬁnancial services because of
its growing FinTech ecosystem and continuing policy emphasis on digitalisation within the ﬁnancial

sector. Bank Negara Malaysia's Financial Sector Blueprint 2022–2026 also emphasises the continued

The study focuses on Malaysian banking customers who have used at least one FinTech-enabled service

activities. The increasing integration of such technologies makes customer perceptions of usability,

The scale of investment in FinTech further demonstrates the importance of understanding whether

supports technological development, sustainable digital transformation requires ﬁnancial institutions to
ensure that the resulting services provide value to customers and maintain their conﬁdence in digital
channels.
1.3 Problem Statement
Despite substantial investment in FinTech and digital banking, technological advancement does not
necessarily guarantee customer satisfaction or sustained positive experiences with digital ﬁnancial
services. Previous evidence has identiﬁed security and service quality as important concerns among
digital banking customers. For example, the J.D. Power (2023) U.S. Direct Banking Satisfaction Study
identiﬁed mobile application security as a major concern among dissatisﬁed digital banking customers,
while Capgemini (2023) reported that a substantial proportion of global banking customers were
dissatisﬁed with at least one dimension of digital banking services. These concerns indicate that the
successful digitalisation of banking depends not only on technological investment but also on
customers' perceptions of the quality, security and usability of digital services.
From a sustainable development perspective, this issue is important because the long-term value of
digital ﬁnancial innovation depends on whether customers can use and trust the services provided.
Digital banking systems that are technologically advanced but diﬃcult to use, perceived as insecure or
characterised by poor service quality may not generate sustainable customer value. Consequently, there
is a need to understand the factors that contribute to positive customer experiences and trust in
FinTech-enabled banking services.
From an academic perspective, existing research has examined FinTech adoption and customer
satisfaction using frameworks such as the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM). However, previous
studies have often examined individual determinants rather than considering perceived ease of use,
perceived security and service quality simultaneously. Examining these factors together can provide a
more comprehensive understanding of the determinants of customer satisfaction in digital banking.

Furthermore, trust represents an important mechanism that may explain how customers' perceptions of
FinTech-enabled services translate into satisfaction. While existing research has examined trust in
relation to FinTech adoption and customer satisfaction, fewer studies have incorporated trust as a
mediator between multiple FinTech-related service characteristics and customer satisfaction within a
single model. This creates an opportunity to examine whether trust explains the relationships between
perceived ease of use, perceived security, service quality and customer satisfaction.
The Malaysian context provides an important setting for addressing this gap. Malaysia has experienced
substantial growth in digital banking and FinTech services, while the country's ﬁnancial-sector
development continues to emphasise digitalisation. However, the existing literature remains
comparatively limited in integrating multiple FinTech-related factors and trust within a single mediation
framework among Malaysian banking customers. The changing digital banking environment following
the COVID-19 pandemic further highlights the need for contemporary evidence on customer experiences
with digital ﬁnancial services.
Accordingly, this study examines the relationships between perceived ease of use, perceived security
and service quality and customer satisfaction among Malaysian banking customers, while investigating
the mediating role of trust. By identifying the customer and service characteristics associated with
trusted and satisfactory FinTech-enabled banking experiences, the study contributes to understanding
the customer-centred dimension of sustainable digital ﬁnancial-service development and its broader
relevance to SDG 8 and SDG 9.

2. LITERATURE REVIEW
In this chapter the theoretical and empirical literature on the adoption of FinTech and customer
satisfaction in banking is critically and comprehensively reviewed. It outlines the theoretical background,
provides literature review on the key variables, identiﬁes the research gap, and proposes conceptual
framework and hypotheses of this study. The review also considers the relevance of these factors to the
development of trusted and sustainable digital ﬁnancial services, thereby positioning the study within the
broader sustainable development context.
2.1 Theoretical Framework
2.1.1 Technology Acceptance Model (TAM)
The Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) has been developed to understand and forecast the
acceptance of technology (Davis, 1989). TAM proposes 2 factors for the adoption: Perceived Usefulness
(PU) - the extent to which the user believes that the technology will help him/her do his/her job better
and Perceived Ease of Use (PEOU) - the extent to which the user believes using the technology will be
easy. (Davis, 1989) showed that PEOU has a direct effect on PU, with both affecting attitudes to
technology use. For the context of FinTech banking, ease of use and customer satisfaction share a
theoretical relationship: When customers' perception of ease of use of FinTech banking services is high,
they will also perceive the overall service experience as good, which will lead to a high level of overall
customer satisfaction (Mamatha & Dev R, 2024).
From a sustainable digital ﬁnancial-service perspective, ease of use is important because the
sustainability of digital banking innovation depends partly on customers' ability to use and continue
engaging with the services provided. Technological innovation that is diﬃcult to understand or operate
may limit the value generated from digital transformation. Therefore, user-friendly FinTech services can
support more positive and sustainable digital banking experiences.
2.1.2 Expectation-Conﬁrmation Theory (ECT)
Expectation-Conﬁrmation Theory (ECT) by (Oliver, 1980), explains customer satisfaction through the
comparison between prior expectations and perceived service performance. According to ECT,
customers are satisﬁed when the performance of a service meets or exceeds their expectations,
Page 7/31

whereas dissatisfaction may occur when performance falls below expectations. The theory therefore
provides a useful basis for understanding satisfaction with FinTech-enabled banking services.
In digital banking, customers develop expectations regarding usability, security, reliability and service
quality based on their previous experiences and interactions with technology. As far as FinTech banking
is concerned, ECT gives insights into the expectations of customers with regard to security, usability and
service quality, based on their previous experience (Saunders, et al., 2023). When FinTech services
perform according to or above customers' expectations, positive evaluations and satisfaction are more
likely to occur.
ECT is also relevant to sustainable digital ﬁnancial services because continued customer satisfaction is
important for the ongoing value of digital transformation. While TAM helps explain customers'
acceptance of technology, ECT provides an explanation of how customers evaluate the performance of
that technology after use. The combination of TAM and ECT therefore provides an appropriate
theoretical foundation for examining how FinTech-related service characteristics contribute to customer
satisfaction.
2.2 Key Concepts and Variables Review
2.2.1 Customer Satisfaction (Dependent Variable)
Customer satisfaction in terms of FinTech (Oliver, 1980) is the overall satisfaction of a customer with a
speciﬁc FinTech service compared to his or her expectations. The ﬁndings of the meta-analysis suggest
that customer satisfaction and repurchase intention in service sector are strongly and positively related
and that customer satisfaction is one of the most important factors that inﬂuences customer loyalty,
customer retention and word-of-mouth. In the literature it has been shown that customer satisfaction is
a good predictor of customer loyalty, customer retention and word-of-mouth and the metanalytical
results in this study have shown that there is a strong positive relationship between customer
satisfaction and repurchase intention in the service sector. In the context of FinTech banking,(Oladapo,
et al., 2022) pointed out that customer satisfaction with mobile banking is one of the key predictors of
intention to continue using and brand advocacy, which necessitates the understanding of the factors
that drive customer satisfaction within FinTech banking.
From a sustainability perspective, customer satisfaction can be viewed as an important indicator of the
ability of digital ﬁnancial services to generate continuing value for customers. Although satisfaction
itself does not directly measure sustainable development outcomes, positive and consistent customer
experiences can support the continued use and development of digital ﬁnancial services.
2.2.2 FinTech Ease of Use (Independent Variable 1)
The ease of use in FinTech is related to ease of understanding, ease of navigation and lack of fatigue for
customers in using FinTech banking applications (Davis, 1989). A factor that many think is a key
determinant of satisfaction with the adoption of FinTech is ‘user-friendliness'. In the banking sector,
Page 8/31

(Oladapo, et al., 2022) found ease of use to be one of the main factors determining customers
satisfaction which contributed approximately 24 per cent of the unique variance. Previous studies by
(Ryu, 2018 ) also indicated that ease of use was one of the leading three factors contributing to
satisfaction of 278 FinTech users in South Korea. This means that banks which focus on simplicity of
the user interface and UX design are going to gain considerable satisfaction beneﬁts.
Ease of use is also relevant to sustainable digital ﬁnancial-service development. As ﬁnancial institutions
increasingly depend on digital channels, services need to remain accessible and manageable for
customers. User-centred design can therefore contribute to positive and continuing digital banking
experiences, supporting the broader sustainability of technology-enabled ﬁnancial-service delivery.
2.2.3 Perceived Security (Independent Variable 2)
Perceived Security: It is a perception of the security of FinTech banking services from unauthorized
access, fraud and other cybersecurity issues (Kim, et al., 2008). Perceived security has been
documented in the empirical literature as one of the most important factors of FinTech customer
satisfaction. Perceived security was found to have the greatest direct impact on customer satisfaction
among all TAM constructs by (Đặng & Boratyńska, 2026) who studied mobile banking adoption among
352 banking customers in Southeast Asia. In a similar fashion, (Nurrela, et al., 2025)established that
trust, which is closely associated with perceived security acted as a mediating variable between the
security practices of the banks and satisfaction, with banks that performed well on the security practices
having much greater satisfaction levels.
Security is also central to sustainable digital ﬁnancial-service development. Digital ﬁnancial innovation
cannot generate sustained customer value if customers perceive the services as vulnerable to fraud,
unauthorised access or cybersecurity threats. Therefore, maintaining customers' perceptions of security
can contribute to trust and support the continued development of reliable digital banking services.
2.2.4 Service Quality (IV3)
Dimensions of digital banking service quality are as perceived by the customers, using the SERVQUAL
model is: Reliability, Responsiveness, Assurance, Empathy and Tangibles, in FinTech banking. The
sample consisted of 523 South Koreans who participated in a study by (Nawawi, et al., 2024) on the
mobile banking service quality and its effect on customer satisfaction. They discovered that the four
dimensions of service quality accounted for 47 per cent of the variance in customer satisfaction, with
reliability and responsiveness being the two most important dimensions. Likewise, (Zeitham,
2026)validated the positive and signiﬁcant relationship between digital service quality and customer
satisfaction in online banking and the dimensions of SERVQUAL were found to explain the concept of
quality in digital service.
High-quality digital services can therefore support sustainable digital ﬁnancial-service delivery by
ensuring that technological innovation provides consistent and dependable value to customers. In this
Page 9/31

sense, service quality represents an important customer-centred component of sustainable digital
transformation in banking.
2.2.5 Trust (Mediating Variable)
The level of trust that customers have in the ﬁnancial service providers in FinTech that offer ﬁnancial
services that are secure, reliable and transparent, according to (Đặng & Boratyńska, 2026). In the
literature, trust is a psychological mechanism that has been found linked to the perceptions of
technology and customer satisfaction in the FinTech ﬁeld. Trust is a psychological process that has
been identiﬁed in the literature as a key factor connecting technology perceptions to customer
satisfaction in the FinTech arena. If customers feel that FinTech services are user-friendly, secure and
high-quality, it will strengthen their conﬁdence in the service provider, thereby increasing the level of
satisfaction (Nurrela, et al., 2025).
The role of trust is also important from the perspective of sustainable digital ﬁnancial services.
Sustained digital transformation requires customers to have suﬃcient conﬁdence to continue engaging
with technology-enabled ﬁnancial services. Therefore, examining trust as a mediator can provide greater
insight into how characteristics of FinTech services translate into customer satisfaction. This approach
provides an integrated explanation of how customer perceptions of FinTech-enabled banking services
may contribute to positive and sustainable digital banking experiences.
2.3 Previous Empirical Research
There are many empirical studies that have examined the factors affecting customers' satisfaction in
FinTech banking. (Oladapo, et al., 2022) adopted a quantitative research approach, sampling 343
Jordanian customers who use mobile banking, and identiﬁed the ease of use (beta = 0.2) and perceived
usefulness (beta = 0.29) as drivers of customer satisfaction with mobile banking, with service quality
being the most signiﬁcant factor (beta = 0.42), accounting for 61 per cent of the variance.
(Đặng & Boratyńska, 2026) conducted the study to analyze the adoption of FinTech in the Southeast
Asian region and added the perceived security and trust in the TAM model. The perceived security was
identiﬁed as the most dominant predictor of satisfaction (beta = 0.48) with both direct and indirect
effects on satisfaction through bolstering trust. The results are in line with the proposed framework, with
perceived security being dominant (beta = 0.51), service quality (beta = 0.38), and ease of use (beta = 
0.29) being the subsequent factors, respectively, in satisfaction among the users of FinTech banking in
Pakistan, as examined by (Ahmed, et al., 2026).
In a study, (Nawawi, et al., 2024) explored the variance in customer satisfaction that could be explained
by ease of use and service quality, while trust could explain unique variance in satisfaction among
customers of digital banking in Malaysia. The ﬁndings from these studies give suﬃcient support to the
integrated mediation model used in this study, and the importance of conducting more local studies
about Malaysia is highlighted.

Although these studies provide valuable evidence, the existing literature has not suﬃciently integrated
perceived ease of use, perceived security and service quality with trust as a mediating mechanism within
a single framework focused on Malaysian banking customers. Furthermore, relatively limited attention
has been given to how these customer-centred factors can support the development of trusted and
sustainable digital ﬁnancial services. The present study addresses this issue by examining the direct
relationships between the three FinTech-related factors and customer satisfaction and the indirect
relationships through trust.
2.4 Research Gap
Four gaps are identiﬁed from the existing literature.
First, although previous studies have examined individual determinants of FinTech satisfaction, limited
research has integrated perceived ease of use, perceived security and service quality with trust as a
mediator within a single empirical framework. Existing evidence indicates that these factors are
individually associated with customer satisfaction (Oladapo et al., 2022; Đặng & Boratyńska, 2026;
Nawawi et al., 2024), but their combined relationships through trust require further investigation.
Second, the Malaysian context remains relatively underrepresented compared with other markets.
Although research has examined digital banking in Malaysia, further empirical evidence is needed to
understand the mechanisms through which customers' perceptions of FinTech-enabled banking services
contribute to satisfaction, particularly across multiple banking providers (Nawawi et al., 2024).
Third, the rapid expansion of digital banking following the COVID-19 pandemic has changed customers'
interaction with digital ﬁnancial services. Consequently, contemporary evidence is required to
understand customer expectations and experiences in the current FinTech environment (Nawawi et al.,
2024).
Fourth, the sustainability dimension of FinTech-enabled banking requires greater attention. Existing
research has largely focused on technology adoption and customer satisfaction, while less attention has
been given to the customer-centred conditions that support trusted, reliable and sustainable digital
ﬁnancial-service delivery. In particular, understanding the roles of usability, security, service quality and
trust can provide insight into how digital banking innovation can generate continuing value for
customers.
Therefore, this study addresses these gaps by examining perceived ease of use, perceived security and
service quality as predictors of customer satisfaction, with trust as a mediating variable, among
Malaysian banking customers. By integrating these relationships within a single framework, the study
contributes to understanding the customer-centred foundations of sustainable digital ﬁnancial services
and their relevance to the broader objectives of SDG 8 and SDG 9.

2.6 Hypotheses Development
Based on the conceptual framework and literature review the following hypotheses are set:
H1: Ease of use of FinTech is positively related to customer satisfaction.
The ﬁndings of the many empirical studies (Oladapo, et al., 2022) support the link between the usability
of FinTech applications and positive user experiences of the service.
H2: Perceived security is positively related to customer satisfaction.
The research ﬁndings by (Abdullah & Hisamudin, 2024), conﬁrmed that security perceptions play a
crucial role in affecting FinTech satisfaction.
H3: Service quality is positively linked to customer satisfaction.
(Nawawi, et al., 2024) indicated that a number of literatures are available to support the notion of service
quality as a determinant of service satisfaction.
The result indicates that the ease of use of FinTech has a direct positive correlation with customer
satisfaction among Malaysian bank customers, while trust is a mediating variable. H4: Trust mediates
the relationship between ease of use of FinTech and customer satisfaction.
In Malaysian bank customers, so that the higher the ease of use of FinTech perceived by customers, the
higher the level of trust they have, the higher customer satisfaction they will have.
H5: Trust mediates the relationship between perceived security and customer satisfaction.
Among Malaysian bank customers such that when perceived security increases, then Trust increases,
which further leads to customer satisfaction.
H6: Trust mediates the relationship between service quality and customer satisfaction.
In the context of Malaysian bank customers, such that the increase in perceptions of service quality
leads to an increase in Trust, which ultimately leads to higher customer satisfaction.
3. RESEARCH METHODOLOGY
Page 12/31

3.1 Research Philosophy
In this research, the approach adopted is positivist research, where it is believed that social phenomena
can be observed, measured and explained by empirical propositions which are derived from theory
(Saunders, et al., 2023). Ontologically, positivism is a realist one and epistemologically is an approach of
empirical observation. The positivist philosophy can be applied in this study because it aims to test the
hypotheses that have been formulated based on TAM and ECT, uses quantitative measurement tools,
and aims to generalize from statistical analysis.
3.2 Research Approach
The approach of this study is based on positivist philosophy that is deductive; general to speciﬁc. This
study tests the hypotheses generated from TAM and ECT using the data of the bank customers, and the
statistical analysis will be employed to support, modify or disprove the hypothesized relationship
between the variables (Saunders, et al., 2023). The deductive approach is suitable for this study because
of the theoretically solid nature of the study constructs and the presence of validated measuring tools
for all constructs in this study.
3.3 Research Strategy
The survey approach is used in this study – this involves gathering standardized information
systematically from a sample of individuals over the internet by way of structured questionnaires
(Saunders, et al., 2023). O. A survey method can be used to collect data from Malaysian bank customers,
test the hypothesis by statistical analysis then generalize the results in the Malaysian banking system.
The main data collection instrument will be a Web-based self-administered questionnaire, thus providing
geographic dispersion, anonymity of respondents and eﬃcient data collection.
3.4 Research Design
The research design used in this research is quantitative research and type of research is cross sectional
and correlational research. The quantitative design can be quantitative measurement and hypothesis
testing (Creswell & Creswell, 2018). Use of the cross-sectional design means that data is gathered at one
time, and the relationships that are observed are those present at the time of data collection. The cross-
sectional design does not allow causal inferences to be drawn but is appropriate for the correlational
and predictive hypotheses. The correlational component of the study reveals strength and the direction
of the relationship between IV and DVs.
3.5 Population and Sampling
3.5.1 Target Population
The target population is banking customers aged 18 + that utilize, in the past three months, at least one
FinTech service from their major commercial bank (deﬁned as customers that use at least one FinTech
service in the last three months). Purposive sampling is used in this study, which involves recruiting
Page 13/31

participants based on their suitability to meet the criteria: adults, active users of commercial banks and
recent users of FinTech services. All others are not included if they do not meet these criteria. The
respondents for Malaysia are sourced from the online survey tool and LinkedIn groups/customer
communities of Malaysian banks on social media such as Facebook and Telegram.
3.5.2 Sample Size
The sample size is calculated using Cochran's (1977) formula: n = (Z² x p x q) / e², in which n = the
required sample size, Z = 1.96 (95% conﬁdence level), p = q = 0.5 (maximum variance), and e = 0.05 (5%
margin of error), giving n = 384. It is assumed that 20% of the questionnaires will not be returned, with a
total of 380 questionnaires being sent out, it is hoped to receive 300 valid responses. This is more than
the minimum number of cases (300) versus number of predictors (3 cases) that is recommended for
multiple regression analysis (Hair, et al., 2019).
3.6 Research Instrument
The criteria have been incorporated into the questions and sections of a questionnaire.
Questionnaire will be used to collect data through 6 parts (A, B, C, D, E and F) which will be self-
administered. The items on the attitudinal and evaluative scales will be assessed on a 5-point Likert
scale, ranging from 1 (Strongly Disagree) to 5 (Strongly Agree) which is common in many studies
pertaining to the adoption of FinTech (Oladapo, et al., 2022). The sections are as follows: A)
Demographic Questions (6 items) gender, age, education, country, bank type, how often do you use
FinTech); B) FinTech Ease of Use (6 items) adapted from (Davis, 1989), updated by (Wong, et al., 2026);
C) Perceived Security (5 items) adapted from Abdullah and (Hamid, et al., 2024); D) Service Quality (10
items) adapted from SERVQUAL, (Parasuraman, et al., 1988); E) Trust (5 items) adapted from (Đặng &
Boratyńska, 2026); F) Customer Satisfaction (5 items) adapted from (Oliver, 1980).
3.6.2 Validity and Reliability
Content validity will be ensured by pre-testing with the three academic experts in digital banking studies
to test the relevance, clarity and comprehensiveness of each of the questions in the questionnaire. The
preliminary reliability will be determined from a pilot survey of 30 customers for items that are not
understood. Reliability will be assessed using Cronbach's Alpha coeﬃcient which will be at 0.70 and
above (Hair, et al., 2022). To test construct validity, conﬁrmatory factor analysis (CFA) will be conducted
before moving on to hypothesis testing.
Data was gathered using a method of data collection.
Data will be collected on an online survey tool (Qualtrics) for 6 weeks. The online survey information will
be sent via professional social media (LinkedIn), banking customer social media groups, and emails to
individuals who meet the inclusion criteria. Participants will be provided with an informed consent
message at the beginning of the questionnaire which will explain the purpose of the study, the nature of
Page 14/31

the task, that they are free to take part, and anonymity and right to withdraw. Information will be
encrypted and kept on the University server (password protected). Responses will be added in SPSS
version 29 for cleaning and questionnaires will be omitted if > 10% of the answers are missing.
3.8 Data Analysis Techniques
There will be ﬁve analytical techniques. Descriptive statistics (frequency, mean, standard deviation) will
be used to analyze all the demographic variables and constructs initially. Secondly, reliability analysis
(Cronbach's Alpha) will be carried out on each construct (Cronbach's Alpha 0.70). Thirdly, Pearson
correlation analysis will be used and following (Cochran, 1977) guidelines, the bivariate relationships will
be examined between each independent variable and customer satisfaction. In addition, the Variance
Inﬂation Factor (VIF) diagnostics of correlation matrix of the independent variables will be checked for
possible multicollinearity with VIF value > 10 indicating multicollinearity. The fourth is to use multiple
regression with the Enter method to test the overall signiﬁcance of the three independent variables, and
to obtain the standardized Beta coeﬃcients to determine which is the strongest independent variable to
predict the level of customer satisfaction. Fifth, the indirect effects of the three dimensions of FinTech
adoption on customer satisfaction will be tested and bootstrapped conﬁdence intervals (5000 samples)
will be computed in the mediation analysis using (Hayes, 2018) PROCESS macro for SPSS (Model 4) to
test the mediation effect.
3.9 Ethical Considerations
Research in this study is conducted in accordance with the highest ethical standards in conducting
research with human participants. The participants will have to sign an informed consent statement at
the start of the questionnaire. There is no coercion in giving consent, no consequences if not consented.
The participants' anonymity is maintained as personal information is not collected apart from
demographic information. The lead researcher and his supervisor will keep research data on university
computers that are secure and protected. There will be an age requirement of 18 or older. Only within the
aggregated level will respondents be reported, in order to guarantee conﬁdentiality.

5. DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION
5.1 Discussions of ﬁndings
The ﬁndings of the data analysis that has been obtained regarding the hypotheses will be discussed in
the following sections:
H1: Ease of use of FinTech is positively related to customer satisfaction.
The ﬁndings support H1, indicating a statistically signiﬁcant positive relationship between perceived
ease of use and customer satisfaction. The Pearson correlation coeﬃcient of r = 0.412 indicates a
Page 21/31

moderate positive relationship between the two variables. This ﬁnding suggests that customers who
perceive FinTech banking applications as easy to navigate and use, tend to report higher levels of
satisfaction. That is, as the ease of use becomes high, customer satisfaction becomes high as well,
aligning with the trend of ease of use becoming high (Mamatha & Dev R, 2024).
The ﬁnding also has relevance to sustainable digital ﬁnancial-service development. As banking services
become increasingly digital, customers need to be able to interact with digital platforms eﬃciently and
conﬁdently. User-friendly interfaces and simple navigation can therefore contribute to positive customer
experiences and support the continued use of digital banking services. In this sense, usability represents
an important customer-centred element of sustainable digital transformation in banking.
H2: Perceived security is positively related to customer satisfaction.
The ﬁndings support H2, with a Pearson correlation coeﬃcient of r = 0.531, indicating a moderate to
strong positive relationship between perceived security and customer satisfaction. The statistically
signiﬁcant result suggests that customers who perceive FinTech banking services as secure tend to
report higher levels of satisfaction. This ﬁnding is consistent with (Đặng & Boratyńska, 2026), who
identiﬁed perceived security as an important predictor of satisfaction among FinTech and mobile
banking users.
The result highlights the importance of security for the sustainability of digital ﬁnancial services.
Customers are required to provide sensitive ﬁnancial and personal information when using digital
banking platforms. Consequently, perceptions of protection against unauthorised access, fraud and
other cybersecurity risks are important for maintaining conﬁdence in digital ﬁnancial services. The
ﬁnding therefore suggests that security is not only a technical requirement but also an important
customer-centred condition for sustainable digital banking.
H3: Service quality is positively linked to customer satisfaction.
The ﬁndings support H3. Service quality demonstrated the strongest Pearson correlation with customer
satisfaction among the three FinTech-related factors, with r = 0.587, indicating a strong positive
relationship. This ﬁnding suggests that customers' evaluations of the reliability, responsiveness,
assurance and other service-quality characteristics of FinTech-enabled banking services are closely
associated with their satisfaction.
The ﬁnding is consistent with (Nawawi, et al., 2024), who reported the importance of service quality in
explaining customer satisfaction in digital banking. It is also consistent with the SERVQUAL perspective
of (Parasuraman, et al., 1988), which emphasises the importance of service reliability, responsiveness,
assurance, empathy and tangibles in customers' evaluations of service quality.
From a sustainable development perspective, the result indicates that technological innovation needs to
be supported by reliable and responsive service delivery. Digital banking services that function
effectively and consistently can provide continuing value to customers and support the long-term
Page 22/31

development of digital ﬁnancial services. Therefore, service quality represents an important component
of customer-centred sustainable digital transformation.
H4: Trust mediates the relationship between ease of use of FinTech and customer satisfaction.
The mediation analysis results show that the ease of use of FinTech strongly inﬂuences customer
satisfaction through the mediation of Trust. The bootstrapped 95% conﬁdence interval for the indirect
effect of Ease of Use on Customer Satisfaction through Trust is [0.073, 0.223] and does not contain zero,
indicating that the indirect effect is statistically signiﬁcant. This means that if consumers consider that
FinTech banking apps are user friendly, they trust the banking platform more and this boosts their overall
satisfaction. This is in line with the previous studies that found ease of use as one of the strong
antecedents to trust in digital ﬁnancial services (DFS) (Karim, et al., 2026), and trust is a crucial
psychological link between the positive perception of a technology and the satisfaction outcomes
(Wong, et al., 2026).
The ﬁnding is particularly relevant to sustainable digital ﬁnancial services because ease of use alone
may not be suﬃcient to sustain positive customer experiences. Customers need to feel conﬁdent when
interacting with digital ﬁnancial platforms. Thus, designing accessible and user-friendly systems while
simultaneously strengthening customer trust may support more sustainable engagement with digital
banking services.
H5: Trust mediates the relationship between perceived security and customer satisfaction.
Perceived security also has a strong interaction with customer satisfaction through trust. The indirect
effect (β = 0.198, 95% CI [0.119, 0.291]) also indicates that customers who feel secure on FinTech
banking platforms have a higher trust in the service provider and then have higher satisfaction levels.
This result is in line with (Nurrela, et al., 2025), who found that perceived security is one of the most
signiﬁcant factors in determining the formation of trust in mobile banking environments. Moreover, the
relationship between adoption of FinTech and sustainable outcomes of ﬁnancial development is found
to be positive and signiﬁcant if security is associated with it, as concluded by (Karim, et al., 2026), which
highlights the overall signiﬁcance of the trust-security linkages in the context of digital banking.
The ﬁnding also strengthens the sustainability argument of the study. Sustainable digital ﬁnancial-
service development requires customers to have conﬁdence that their ﬁnancial information and
transactions are adequately protected. Therefore, security practices that strengthen trust may support
the continued acceptance and use of digital ﬁnancial services. The result highlights that sustainable
digital transformation is not simply a matter of implementing new technologies but also of developing
secure and trustworthy services.
H6: Trust mediates the relationship between service quality and customer satisfaction.
As for the mediated effect of service quality on customer satisfaction, the result shows that Trust fully
supports H6 (β = 0.231, 95% CI [0.141, 0.335]). This ﬁnding suggests that the relationship between high
Page 23/31

quality delivery of FinTech services and trust in the banking platform becomes strong, which further
leads to high customer satisfaction, as guided by the SERVQUAL model developed by Parasuraman,
Zeithaml and Berry (1988) that includes reliability, responsiveness, assurance, empathy and tangibles.
This aligns with the ﬁndings of (Nawawi, et al., 2024), which revealed that service quality is the most
important factor that contributes to satisfaction for digital banking. In addition, (Wong, et al., 2026)
found that satisfaction with service quality and trust in digital banking have a signiﬁcant impact on
behavioral intentions to use digital banking in Malaysia, which further validates the mediating role of the
present study. Overall, the results of H4, H5, and H6 conﬁrm that Trust is not only a result of adopting any
of these three dimensions of FinTech, but also a psychological mechanism that transforms each of
these adoption dimension into customer satisfaction (Nurrela, et al., 2025).
The ﬁnding has an important implication for sustainable digital ﬁnancial services. High-quality digital
services need to be reliable, responsive and capable of consistently meeting customer expectations.
When such service characteristics strengthen trust, they may support more positive and continuing
customer experiences. Therefore, service quality and trust should be considered together when ﬁnancial
institutions seek to develop sustainable digital banking services.
Overall Discussion
These ﬁndings extend the existing literature by demonstrating that customer satisfaction with FinTech-
enabled banking services is associated not only with technological and service characteristics but also
with the development of customer trust. The integration of TAM and ECT therefore provides a useful
basis for understanding both technology-related perceptions and satisfaction formation in digital
banking (Davis, 1989; Oliver, 1980).
Importantly, the ﬁndings provide a customer-centred perspective on sustainable digital ﬁnancial
services. The results suggest that sustainable digital transformation in banking requires more than
technological innovation. Digital services need to be usable, secure and of suﬃcient quality to generate
trust and positive customer experiences. These ﬁndings therefore provide a basis for understanding how
customer-oriented FinTech development can support the broader objectives of SDG 8 and SDG 9,
particularly in relation to sustainable ﬁnancial-service innovation and digital technological development.
5.2 Implications of the study
5.2.1 Theoretical implications
The theoretical contribution of this study is undeniable as it explores the effect of FinTech adoption on
customer satisfaction with the mediation of Trust, which has many implications. The use of both TAM
and ECT in the same mediation model provides a more holistic explanations for how satisfaction is
formed in the context of FinTech, as compared to the single-predictor studies (Davis, F. D., 1989). The
conﬁrmation of Trust as an important mediator, strengthens theory as the dimensions of FinTech
adoption do not only directly impact on customer satisfaction, but they do so through a path of trust
Page 24/31

building. Furthermore, one can gain insight from analyzing ease of use and perceived security/service
quality simultaneously, in one model, as to how different aspects of the adoption process affect the
satisfaction outcomes. However, other factors and challenges need to be included into variables to
further enhance the statistical analysis and support the theoretical framework (Oladapo, et al., 2022).
5.2.2 Practical Implications
The ﬁndings provide practical implications for Malaysian banks and FinTech developers. Since service
quality demonstrated the strongest indirect relationship with customer satisfaction through trust, banks
should prioritise reliable, responsive and consistent digital services. At the same time, user-friendly
interfaces and strong security measures should be maintained to strengthen customers' conﬁdence in
digital banking platforms. These ﬁndings are consistent with previous research highlighting the
importance of ease of use, security and service quality in digital banking satisfaction (Nawawi, et al.,
2024), (Đặng & Boratyńska, 2026).
For ﬁnancial-sector regulators, the ﬁndings highlight the importance of supporting responsible digital
innovation through appropriate security, consumer protection and service-quality standards.
Strengthening customer conﬁdence in digital ﬁnancial services can help create an environment in which
FinTech innovation delivers sustained value to banking customers.
5.2.3 Sustainable Development Implications
The ﬁndings provide a customer-centred perspective on the sustainability of digital ﬁnancial-service
development. The results indicate that sustainable digital transformation in banking requires more than
technological innovation. FinTech services need to be easy to use, secure and reliable, while trust is
important in translating these characteristics into positive customer satisfaction.
These ﬁndings are relevant to SDG 8, particularly the broader objective of supporting sustainable
economic activity and effective ﬁnancial-service development, and SDG 9, which emphasises innovation
and resilient infrastructure. Although the present study does not directly measure SDG outcomes, it
demonstrates that customer usability, security, service quality and trust are important conditions for
developing digital ﬁnancial services that can provide continuing value to customers.
Therefore, banks and FinTech providers can support sustainable digital banking by adopting user-
centred design, robust security practices, consistent service quality and trust-building mechanisms.
Such practices can help ensure that digital ﬁnancial innovation is not limited to technological
advancement but also delivers reliable and sustainable customer experiences. This interpretation is
consistent with previous research highlighting the role of FinTech in improving banking eﬃciency, service
delivery and customer satisfaction (Oladapo et al., 2022; Nawawi et al., 2024).
5.3 Limitation of the study
A limitation of the study is the cross-sectional design, which only captures the data at one point in time,
without providing the possibility to analyse changes in satisfaction with FinTech over time. The 5-point
Page 25/31

Likert scale can yield any number of positive and negative responses, but also has the potential to lead
to central tendency bias if the respondents select the middle category to avoid extreme responses.
There may also be response bias with a Likert scale, as they might be responding the way that they think
is "socially correct. In addition, the results of this survey should not be used to represent the overall
picture of global FinTech adoption or directly applicable to other countries or regions that may have
different regulatory frameworks and/or levels of digital maturity. Moreover, this survey was based on a
quantitative research method, and has only delivered quantitative data, and perhaps not presented a
detailed understanding of the customer experiences which might be provided by qualitative research
methods by FinTech.
Finally, although the study discusses the relevance of its ﬁndings to sustainable digital ﬁnancial services
and SDG 8 and SDG 9, sustainability outcomes were not directly measured in the present study.
Therefore, the ﬁndings should be interpreted as providing a customer-centred perspective on conditions
that may support sustainable digital ﬁnancial-service development rather than as direct evidence of SDG
achievement.
5.4 Recommendations for future research
Recommendation: A longitudinal research method should be used to understand the satisfaction of the
customers regarding the FinTech over the period of time, especially when new technologies and
regulatory changes come into existence. Moreover, the quantitative research technique should be used
in conjunction with qualitative research techniques to gain deeper insights into why respondents were
experiencing the results, and their subjective experiences of using the FinTech banking service
(Saunders, et al., 2023). In addition, future research should use other statistical procedures such as
Structural Equation Modelling (SEM) that might be more appropriate to evaluate more complex
mediation models. In addition, the moderating factors affecting the relationship between the dimensions
of FinTech adoption and satisfaction of consumers could be analyzed in future research, such as digital
literacy, age and ﬁnancial inclusion. Explore the potential impact of implementing Environmental, Social,
and Governance (ESG) principles on FinTech banking strategies on the level of customer satisfaction
and business sustainability. The above will contribute towards closing the identiﬁed gap and better
understanding of the adoption of FinTech and customer satisfaction in the banking industry for
Malaysian customers.
5.5 Conclusion
FinTech is transforming the banking industry, paving the way for new innovations like mobile banking
apps, digital payment systems, and AI-powered customer service, creating a more eﬃcient and engaging
banking experience as per (Nawawi, et al., 2024). The advantages of taking the plunge into FinTech also
encompass sustainable banking practice "to ensure optimal services and reduce ineﬃciencies in
operations," they added. Furthermore, it would help improve collaborations among all the stakeholders
and banking customers through the real-time connection and centralized into banking networking
platforms for seamless banking services and communications (Nurrela, et al., 2025).
Page 26/31

Moreover, as stated by (Oladapo, et al., 2022), the use of FinTech in banking can lead to the boosting of
customer satisfaction, higher accuracy in ﬁnancial reporting, higher eﬃciency in managing time and cost
factors and also mitigate any operational risks that can be encountered during the service delivery life
cycle. But there are hurdles to cross, including a boost in awareness of the customers about how secure
and reliable online banking instruments are. There are training and digital literacy programs to enable
effective and conﬁdent use of FinTech services, as well as gaps in customers. Apart from this, FinTech
platforms in Malaysia do not have the consistency of the quality of their services (Ahmed, et al., 2026).
To surmount the challenges and harness the digital transformation for banking industry transformation,
Malaysia needs to develop an ecosystem of user-centred design, strong security measures, consistent
service quality, and trust building mechanisms to achieve sustainable customer satisfaction in FinTech
banking, especially in a way that caters to today's customers while keeping pace with the rest of the
world's FinTech leader (Wong, et al., 2026).
The ﬁndings also provide a customer-centred perspective on sustainable digital ﬁnancial-service
development. Sustainable digital transformation requires more than technological innovation; digital
banking services need to be usable, secure, reliable and capable of generating customer trust. By
highlighting these conditions, the study provides insights relevant to the broader objectives of SDG 8 and
SDG 9, particularly in relation to sustainable ﬁnancial-service development and technological innovation.
For Malaysian banks, FinTech developers and ﬁnancial-sector regulators, the ﬁndings emphasise the
importance of developing user-centred, secure, reliable and trustworthy digital banking services.
Strengthening these characteristics can help support positive customer experiences and the continued
development of sustainable digital ﬁnancial services.
Overall, the study demonstrates that trust is a central mechanism linking key characteristics of
FinTech-enabled banking services with customer satisfaction. The ﬁndings therefore contribute to a
better understanding of how customer-centred FinTech development can support sustainable digital
transformation in the Malaysian banking sector.
