Abstract
This study examined the relationship between entertainment marketing strategies and customer
patronage of fast food firms in Port Harcourt, Rivers State. Specifically, the study investigated
how entertainment attractiveness and brand image transfer relate with customer engagement and
positive word -of-mouth among customers of fast food firms. The study was anchored on the
Meaning Transfer Theory and adopted a cross -sectional survey research design. The target
population comprised customers of thirty -four reg istered fast food firms in Port Harcourt as
documented by the Rivers State Ministry of Commerce and Industry and the Association of Fast
Food and Confectionary of Nigeria (AFCON) Rivers State Branch. Due to the absence of an
updated customer database, the population was treated as infinite, and a sample size of 249
respondents was determined using William’s (2009) formula for infinite populations. Convenience
sampling technique was adopted in selecting respondents available during questionnaire
administration. Primary data were collected through a structured questionnaire measured on a
five-point Likert scale. Validity was established through content and construct validity procedures,
while reliability was confirmed using Cronbach’s alpha coefficients which exceeded the
acceptable threshold of 0.70. Data were analyzed using the Statistical Package for Social Sciences
(SPSS) Version 26.0, employing Pearson Product Moment Correlation Coefficient and partial
correlation analysis. The findings revealed that enter tainment attractiveness had strong positive
and significant relationships with customer engagement and positive word -of-mouth. Similarly,
brand image transfer demonstrated strong positive relationships with customer engagement and
positive word-of-mouth among customers of fast food firms in Port Harcourt. The study concluded
that entertainment marketing strategies significantly enhance customer patronage by
strengthening customers’ emotional attachment, interaction, and advocacy behaviours toward fast
food brands. The study recommended that fast food firms should invest in attractive entertainment
content, strengthen entertainment-based digital engagement, partner with relevant entertainment
personalities and events, and create memorable customer experiences  capable of stimulating
positive word-of-mouth and sustained customer patronage.

Keywords: Entertainment Marketing Strategies; Entertainment Attractiveness; Brand Image
Transfer; Customer Patronage.

1.1 Background of the Study
The fast food sector occupies a strategically central position within the Nigerian economy,
contributing meaningfully to gross domestic product, generating substantial employment
opportunities across formal and informal value chains, and indirectly supporting foreign exchange
earnings through the operations of both indigenous and international franchise brands (Adiele et
al., 2015; Enyia & Emelah, 2018; Uchenna, 2020). Despite this economic significance, the sector
has encountered persistent challenges that  directly undermine customer patronage, including
intense competitive rivalry, shifting consumer expectations, economic volatility, and the lingering
behavioural effects of the post -pandemic era, which have collectively reduced repeat visits and
dampened positive word -of-mouth communication (Adiele & Grend, 2016; Baker et al., 2002;
Nwulu & Asiegbu, 2015; Pan & Zinkhan, 2006). Customer patronage is the lifeblood of any fast
food firm; it is defined as the frequency, depth, and loyalty of consumer engagement with a brand,
encompassing store choice, repeat purchase behaviour, and the willingness to recommend the firm
to others (Moye, 2020; Seock, 2019; Westbrook, 1987). When patronage declines, fast food firms
experience reduced sales volume, lower profit marg ins, and erosion of market share, making the
understanding of patronage drivers not merely a marketing objective but an imperative for
organisational survival (Cengiz & Yayla, 2007; Jones et al., 2006; Njelita & Anyasor, 2020).
Entertainment marketing strategies have been identified in this study, as a potential driver capable
of enhancing customer patronage by transforming the dining experience from a functional
transaction into a memorable, emotionally engaging event (Barnouw &  Kirkland, 2022;
Finkelstein, 2019; Kaser & Oelkers, 2021; Singhal & Rogers, 2019). Unlike conventional
advertising, entertainment marketing integrates brand identity into enjoyable content so naturally
that the entertainment itself becomes the vehicle of persuasion, transmitting cultural meanings and
emotional values to consumers (Lehu, 2023; Lieberman & Esgate, 2022; Regueira, 2022). In the
fast food context, where consumers increasingly seek hedonic and experiential gratification
alongside nutritional va lue, entertainment marketing strategies such as attractive celebrity
endorsements, lively in -store music, sponsored events, and engaging social media content can
significantly influence how customers perceive and emotionally connect with a brand (Cooper -
Chen, 2015; Hernandez, 2023; Turner, 2017). Two theoretically grounded dimensions of
entertainment marketing are particularly relevant: entertainment attractiveness, which refers to the
appealing qualities of an endorser or brand representative that shape co nsumer attitudes and
behavioural intentions (Langmeyer & Shank, 2012; Ohanian, 1991; Shimp, 2020), and brand
image transfer, which describes the process by which positive associations from entertainment
content or celebrities are transferred to the sponsor ing brand, thereby enriching its perceived
identity and differentiation (Aaker, 1991; Chien et al., 2005; Grohs, 2004; Keller, 1993; Pitts &
Slattery, 2004). These dimensions are conceptually anchored in Meaning Transfer Theory
(McCracken, 1989), which pos its that culturally constituted meanings move from entertainment
figures to brands and subsequently to consumers, shaping their engagement and advocacy
behaviours. Despite the acknowledged importance of customer patronage and the theoretical
promise of ent ertainment marketing strategies, a notable research gap persists in the fast food
literature. Previous empirical studies have examined various predictors of customer patronage,
including service quality, price, convenience, and physical environment (Baker et al., 2002; Nwulu
& Asiegbu, 2015), yet limited attention has been devoted to entertainment marketing strategies as
systematic drivers of patronage outcomes, particularly in the Nigerian fast food sector. Studies
such as Patel (2015) and Giridhar (2012) investigated entertainment attractiveness in celebrity
endorsement contexts but did so in India and without linking the construct to customer engagement

2.0 Literature Review
2.1 Conceptual Review
2.1.1 Entertainment Marketing Strategies : Entertainment marketing strategies represent a
multidimensional approach to brand communication in which the marketing message is
deliberately designed to entertain audiences while simultaneously achieving commercial
objectives such as awareness, engagemen t, and behavioral change (Barnouw & Kirkland, 2022;
Singhal & Rogers, 2019). At its conceptual core, entertainment marketing strategies treat the
entertainment experience itself as a marketable product, one that is consumed and valued by large,
heterogeneous audiences who allocate their limited time and money in exchange for pleasure,
stimulation, and emotional gratification (Kaser & Oelkers, 2021). Unlike conventional marketing
communications, which rely on direct product messaging, entertainment marketing strategies
integrate brand identity into content so naturally that the entertainment itself becomes the vehicle
of persuasion, a distinction that Lehu (2023) captures in arguing that the brand must coexist
harmoniously within the content while transmitting its values and emotional dimension to the
consumer. The theoretical depth of this construct is further illuminated by Lieberman and Esgate
(2022), who propose the entertainment marketing mix consisting of content, conduit, convergence,
and consumption, as a framework that parallels the conventional 4Ps of marketing but is uniquely
calibrated to the dynamics of entertainment production and distribution. This mix is particularly
relevant to the fast food sector, whe re experiences are multi -sensory, emotionally charged, and
increasingly competitive with broader entertainment alternatives (Finkelstein, 2019). The conduit
dimension of this mix is especially significant in the digital age, as it determines how entertainment
content reaches consumers across platforms; Hernandez (2023) demonstrates that real -time
analytics tools such as Facebook Insights and Twitter Analytics enable marketers to monitor the
effectiveness of entertainment campaigns and make data-driven adjustments to content strategies.
The distinctiveness of entertainment marketing also lies in its ability to address cognitive and

2.1.2 Dimensions of Entertainment Marketing Strategies
Entertainment Attractiveness : Entertainment attractiveness is a construct that extends well
beyond physical appearance to encompass the totality of qualities that make an endorser or brand
representative appealing, credible, and persuasive to a target audience (Langmeyer & Shank, 2012;
Shimp, 2020). Ohanian (1991) established the seminal framework for entertainment attractiveness,
positioning it as one of three dimensions of source credibility alongside expertise and
trustworthiness, and demonstrating that attractive communicators are more successful at altering
beliefs and shaping consumer attitudes toward endorsed brands. Building on this, Sallam and
Abdslfattah (2017) confirm that physical attractiveness functions as a significant initial judgment
cue that influences consumer evaluations of advertisements, and that the added credibility derived
from an attractive endorser is expected to enhance consumers' attitude toward the advertisement.
The mechanism by which entertainment attractiveness operates is partly explained by the concept
of identification: when audiences perceive a shared similarity between themselves and the source,
they are more likely to adopt the source's attitudes and behaviors (Santi et al., 2022). This
identification effect is particular ly pronounced among micro -influencers, who are perceived as
more relatable and approachable than mainstream celebrities, thereby generating stronger
parasocial bonds with their followers (Djafarova & Rushworth, 2017). The match -up hypothesis
provides an additional theoretical lens, arguing that entertainment attractiveness is most effective
when the endorser's image is congruent with the product being promoted, such that physically
attractive endorsers are particularly persuasive for products associated wit h personal appearance
or lifestyle aspirations (Teng et al., 2014). Critically, however, the literature reveals some
inconsistency regarding the universal efficacy of entertainment attractiveness: Klaus -Peter et al.
(2021) found that while trustworthiness is the most important endorser attribute, entertainment
attractiveness ranks second, but expertise contributes negligibly to brand outcomes. Furthermore,
Lim et al. (2017) highlight that physical attractiveness interacts with consumer attitudes toward
the product category, meaning that attractiveness effects are context -dependent rather than
invariant. In the fast food industry, where consumers form rapid evaluations of brands based on
visual and experiential cues, entertainment attractiveness serves as a p owerful determinant of
customer engagement and brand preference (Cohen & Golden, 2022; Mowen & Mowen, 2002).
These theoretical perspectives collectively justify the inclusion of entertainment attractiveness as
a central dimension of entertainment marketing strategies in this study.

entertainment property are transmitted to and absorbed by the sponsoring brand in the perceptions
of consumers (Grohs, 2004; Pitts & Slattery, 2004). Keller (1993) provided the foundational

entertainment property's persona. Critically, Yong et al. (2008) caution that measuring brand
image transfer correctly requires attention to the specific qualitative characteristics of a company's

differentiation is increasingly driven by experiential associations rather than product quality alone,
brand image transfer through entertainment partnerships offers a compelling strategic pathway to
enhanced consumer patronage (Basil & Basil, 2003; Irwin et al., 2002).

2.1.3 Customer Patronage: Customer patronage is a multidimensional behavioral construct that
encompasses the frequency, depth, and loyalty of consumer engagement with a firm, representing
not only repeat purchase transactions but also the broader set of attitudes and behaviors thro ugh
which customers signal their preference for a particular establishment over competitors (Adiele et
al., 2015; Moye, 2020). Seock (2019) defines customer patronage specifically as the repeat
purchase behavior that takes place at a particular store for a previously purchased or new product,
while Uchenna (2020) emphasizes that the importance of repeated customer patronage lies in its
direct and significant impact on a firm's sales volume and profitability levels. The conceptual
richness of customer patron age is further demonstrated by Nwulu and Asiegbu (2015), who
identify multiple behavioral indicators of patronage including patronage intention, shopping
enjoyment, satisfaction, time spent, repeat purchase, money spent, and share of wallet. Pan and
Zinkhan (2006) operationalize the construct through two primary dimensions: store choice, which
captures a customer's preference for a particular establishment, and frequency of visit, which
captures the regularity of patronage,  together providing a parsimonious yet comprehensive
framework for measurement. The antecedents of customer patronage are diverse and include not
only product quality and service but also atmospheric and experiential factors; Baker et al. (2002)
demonstrate that patronage in retailing invo lves customers' willingness to spend more time in a
store, engage in positive word -of-mouth, increase future purchases, and make repeated
acquisitions, all of which are influenced by the total in -store experience. In the Nigerian context,
Adiele and Grend (2016) explain that patronage arises when an individual makes a conscious effort
to seek a rewarding solution to a need amidst competing alternatives, s uggesting that fast food

2.1.4 Measures of Customer Patronage
Customer Engagement: Customer engagement is a psychological and behavioral construct that
describes the depth of a customer's active participation, emotional investment, and interactive
relationship with a brand or firm across multiple channels and touchpoints (Calder et al., 2019;
Vivek et al., 2010). Unlike more superficial indicators of consumer -brand relationships such as
satisfaction or awareness, customer engagement captures the sustained involvement that customers
maintain wit h a brand, encompassing emotional, cognitive, and behavioral dimensions that
collectively determine the quality and durability of the customer-brand bond (Bendapudi & Leone,
2013; Stringer, 2016). Vivek et al. (2010) argue that in order to increase customer loyalty,
companies must engage customers both offline and online, and that the marketer's goal should
extend beyond brand preference to ensuring that the consumer remains associated, interested, and
genuinely involved with the brand over time. The rise of social media has funda mentally
transformed the landscape of customer engagement: Calder et al. (2019) document that the
interactivity of social media platforms significantly increased academic and practitioner interest in
customer engagement as a distinct construct, moving it b eyond mere website metrics toward a
richer conceptualization that includes buying behavior, administration interactions, online
conduct, and referral intention. Peppers and Rogers (2016) further clarify that engagement occurs
at multiple levels such as brand, product, and individual service provider, and that high customer
engagement, analogous to the concept of co-production, requires customers to present themselves
physically, cognitively, and emotionally during service encounters. In the context of fast food
establishments, where the consumption ex perience is inherently social and multisensory, Ashley
and Tuten (2015) establish that brand-related social media activities, including interactive content
and visual entertainment, can build meaningful relationships with consumers and elevate levels of
engagement. Godey et al. (2016) add that visually appealing social media content is a significant
antecedent of consumer engagement, with consumers showing heightened involvement when
content is aesthetically stimulating and entertaining. Seo and Park (2018)  further establish that
entertainment attractiveness is a vital component of social media engagement in building brand
equity, suggesting a direct pathway from entertaining brand content to customer engagement.
From a managerial standpoint, Aggarwal and Mittal (2022) demonstrate that brand content that is
interactive, likable, and entertaining generates high brand value, underscoring the strategic
importance of investing in entertaining customer experiences as a driver of engagement in
competitive fast food markets.

formed, communicated, and diffused across social networks, and its significance has only
intensified i n the digital age (Chaniotakis & Lymperopoulos, 2009; Westbrook, 1987). In its
classical formulation, positive WOM is defined as informal, person -to-person communication

risk-reduction function of positive WOM is equally well documented: Chaniotakis and

associated with specific purchasing decisions. In the fast food industry, where product
differentiation is often subtle and consumer choices are frequency-based, positive WOM serves as
a critical driver of trial, repeat patronage, and reputation management (Dichter, 1966; Jones et al.,
2006). Furthermore, positive WOM has important implications for brand equity: when customers
voluntarily and enthusiastically share positive testimonials about a fast food firm's entertainment
offerings, atmosphere, and service quality, they generate organic brand visibility and social proof
that no paid advertising can replicate (Weiner, 2019). This dynamic is particularly salient in the
Port Harcourt fast food market, where consumers are embedded in dense social networks and peer
recommendations powerfully shape patronage decisions, making the cultivation of positive word-
of-mouth an indispensable component of any entertainment marketing strategy.

2.2 Theoretical Framework
Meaning Transfer Theory: Meaning Transfer Theory was propounded by Grant McCracken in
1989 to explain how culturally constituted meanings move from society to consumer goods and
subsequently to consumers through symbolic consumption processes. The theory posits that
celebrities, e ntertainers, and other culturally significant figures carry socially constructed
meanings such as status, lifestyle, attractiveness, prestige, and personality traits, which are
transferred to brands through endorsement or entertain ment associations and eventually
internalized by consumers through product usage (McCracken, 1989). The theory departs from
earlier communication models that emphasized only source credibility or attractiveness by arguing
that endorsers function as carrier s of cultural meaning capable of shaping consumer perceptions
and behavioural outcomes. In marketing contexts, meaning transfer occurs in three stages: from
culture to the entertainment figure, from the entertainment figure to the brand, and from the brand
to the consumer (McCracken, 1989). The theory has been widely applied in advertising, celebrity
endorsement, hospitality branding, and entertainment marketing studies because it explains how
symbolic associations influence customer attitudes and purchase behaviour. However, critics argue
that the theory pays insufficient attention to individual differences in consumers’ interpretation of
meanings and assumes that symbolic meanings are transferred uniformly across audiences and
cultures (Erdogan, 1999). Oth ers contend that the theory is more interpretive than predictive

The Meaning Transfer Theory underpins the present study because entertainment marketing
strategies in fast food firms are fundamentally designed to create symbolic and emotional
associations capable of influencing customer patronage behaviours. Entertainment attractiveness,
which reflects the appeal, excitement, and emotional value embedded in entertainment -based
marketing activities, can shape how consumers perceive and emotionally connect with fast food
brands. Similarly, brand image transfer occurs when the symbolic meanings associated with
entertainment content, celebrities, music, or events are transferred to the fast food brand and
subsequently influence consumer evaluations and behavioural responses. In the context of fast
food firms in Port Harcourt, consumers are likely to associate entertaining brand experiences with
modernity, excitement, social relevance, and lifestyle identity, thereby strengthening customer
engagement and stimulating positive word -of-mouth communication. The theory therefore
provides a logical explanation for how entertainment-oriented promotional activities move beyond
functional advertising to create deeper psychological and symbolic attachments that encourage
repeated patronage and customer advocacy. By emphasizing the transfe r of meanings from
entertainment stimuli to brands and ultimately to consumers, the theory offers a strong conceptual
foundation for explaining the relationship between entertainment marketing strategies and
customer patronage within the fast food industry.

3.0 Methodology: The study employed a cross -sectional survey design. The target population
comprised customers of thirty-four (34) registered fast food firms in Port Harcourt, as documented
by the Rivers State Ministry of Commerce & Industry and the Association of Fast Foo d and
Confectionary of Nigeria (AFCON) Rivers State Branch. Due to the absence of an updated
customer database resulting in an infinite population, a non -probability convenience sampling
technique was adopted. The sample size of 249 respondents was determi ned using William's
(2009) formula for infinite populations, and this sample was drawn from customers present at the
fast food firms during the questionnaire distribution period (Anastasi & Urbina, 1997; Cronbach
& Mechi, 1955; William, 2009).
Data were collected using a structured questionnaire comprising four sections: respondent
demographics, entertainment marketing strategies items (entertainment attractiveness and brand
image transfer), and customer patronage items (customer engagement and positive word -of-
mouth), all rated on a five -point Likert scale. Validity was established through content and
construct validity procedures, involving systematic analysis of the questionnaire items to ensure
appropriate measurement of the study variables ( Anastasi & Urbina, 1997; Cronbach & Mechi,
1955). Reliability was assessed using Cronbach's alpha test, which yielded acceptable coefficients
exceeding the 0.70 threshold: entertainment attractiveness (α = 0.76), brand image transfer (α =
0.82), customer engagement (α = 0.87), and positive word-of-mouth (α = 0.86). Data analysis was
conducted using the Statistical Package for Social Sciences (SPSS) Version 26.0, employing
Pearson Product Moment Correlation Coefficient to test the hypotheses, with partial co rrelation
used to assess the moderating effect of corporate policy (Anastasi & Urbina, 1997; Cronbach &
Mechi, 1955).

5.0 Conclusion
The study concluded that entertainment marketing strategies significantly enhance customer
patronage among fast food firms in Port Harcourt, Rivers State. Specifically, entertainment
attractiveness and brand image transfer demonstrated strong positive rela tionships with customer
engagement and positive word-of-mouth, indicating that customers are more likely to interact with,

activities are attractive, relatable, a nd capable of creating favourable brand associations. The
findings further validate the Meaning Transfer Theory by demonstrating that symbolic meanings
embedded in entertainment experiences and endorsers can be successfully transferred to fast food
brands, thereby shaping customer behavioural outcomes and strengthening patronage intentions.

5.1 Recommendations
Given the outcome of the study and the conclusions, the following recommendations were made:
i. Fast food firms in Port Harcourt should consistently invest in attractive and relatable
entertainment content capable of sustaining customer engagement. Management should move
beyond static advertising by organizing live music nights, comedy sessions, sports viewing
experiences, celebrity appearances, dance contests, and social media challenges that resonate with
young consumers who dominate the fast food market. Firms should also collaborate with relatable
local influencers, comedians, musicians, and on -air personalities rather than relying solely on
expensive national celebrities, since local personalities are often perceived as more authentic and
culturally connected to customers.
ii. Management of fast food firms should deliberately integrate entertainment attractiveness
into their digital customer engagement strategies in order to stimulate positive word -of-mouth.
Practical actions may include creating short entertaining videos on TikTok, Instagram, and
Facebook, rewarding customers who post entertaining customer experien ces online, and
introducing customer-generated content competitions where winners receive free meals, discounts,
or branded merchandise. Since Nigerian consumers are highly influenced by peer
recommendations and online trends, firms should encourage customers to share pictures, reviews,
and testimonials through hashtags, referral incentives, and interactive online campaigns that can
increase brand visibility organically.
iii. Fast food firms should strengthen brand image transfer by partnering with entertainment
properties, events, and personalities whose values align with the identity of the brand. Management
should sponsor local concerts, campus events, football viewing centres, fashion shows, and
cultural festivals that attract their target market. These partne rships should consistently
communicate excitement, quality, social relevance, and youthful lifestyle values capable of
making customers emotionally attached to the brand. Firms should also ensure consistency in
ambience, music selection, customer service behaviour, and visual branding across outlets so that
the entertainment experience aligns with the image customers associate with the brand.
iv. Management should strategically leverage entertainment-based brand associations to
stimulate stronger positive word -of-mouth among customers. Practical steps include creating
memorable in-store experiences through themed décor, engaging playlists, branded selfie spots,
and customer appreciation events that customers naturally want to dis cuss with others. Firms
