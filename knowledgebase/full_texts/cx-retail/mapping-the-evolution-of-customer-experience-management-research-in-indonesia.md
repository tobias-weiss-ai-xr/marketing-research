 A B S T R A C T

Customer Experience Management (CEM) has emerged as a critical strategic
paradigm for enhancing customer satisfaction, strengthening loyalty, and
sustaining competitive advantage in increasingly digital and experience -
driven markets. This study conducts a systematic literature review (SLR) to
map the evolution, intellectual structure, and thematic development of CEM
research in the Indonesian context. Following the PRISMA framework, 210
records were initially retrieved from the Scopus database, yielding  a final
sample of 67 peer -reviewed journal articles published between 2010 and
2025. Bibliometric and keyword co -occurrence analyses reveal four
dominant research clusters: customer experience and relationship
management; brand management and brand equity development; digital and
technology-enabled marketing;  and sales performance and market -driven
strategy. The findings indicate that Indonesian CEM research has evolved
from traditional service quality and satisfaction perspectives toward more
integrated, experience -centric, and digitally enabled frameworks. D espite
this progress, several areas remain underexplored, including emotional and
psychological dimensions of customer experience, advanced analytics and
artificial intelligence integration, ethical and cultural considerations, and the
development of robus t, context -sensitive experience measurement
frameworks. By synthesizing fragmented research streams into a coherent
thematic structure, this study offers theoretical insights into the maturation
of CEM research. It provides a foundation for advancing future scholarship
and managerial practice in Indonesia and similar emerging market contexts.

INTRODUCTION
Customer Experience Management (CEM) has emerged as a central theoretical and strategic
paradigm in contemporary marketing and service research. As competition intensifies and customer
expectations evolve in increasingly digital and hybrid service environm ents, organizations are no longer
differentiated solely by functional value or service efficiency, but by their ability to design, manage, and
sustain superior customer experiences across the entire customer journey. CEM represents a strategic
approach that aligns organizational processes, technologies, and human interactions around customer needs
and experiences, aiming to enhance customer satisfaction, loyalty, and advocacy through consistent, high-
quality touchpoints (Gupta, 2016; Kim et al., 2025).
Theoretically, CEM builds upon and extends foundational perspectives in service-dominant logic,
relationship marketing, customer engagement theory, and experience -based value creation. Recent
conceptual frameworks emphasize that customer experience is multidimensional, dynamic, and co-created
through continuous interactions between customers, firms, technologies, and contextual factors (Kim et al.,
2025). Advances in digital technologies , particularly the integration of Internet of Things (IoT), artificial

361

intelligence (AI), cloud computing, and large language models , have further transformed CEM from a
descriptive construct into a data-driven, context-aware, and adaptive management system (Benzarti et al.,
2022; Shareef et al., 2024). These developments enable firms to personalize interactions, anticipate
customer needs, and deliver emotionally resonant experiences in real time.
Effective CEM requires the orchestration of all customer touchpoints, encompassing both physical
and digital interactions, and ensuring consistency across channels and service encounters (Kim et al., 2025;
Choudhary & Chauhan, 2023). From a theoretical sta ndpoint, this reinforces the view of CEM as an
ecosystem-level construct rather than a firm-centric or transaction-based outcome. Understanding customer
experience, therefore, necessitates systematic analysis of customer behaviors, perceptions, and emotions.
Recent studies highlight the growing importance of advanced data analytics, sentiment analysis, and text
mining in uncovering experiential insights from customer feedback a nd user-generated content, enabling
organizations to recalibrate their experience strategies continuously (Gallagher et al., 2022).
Empirical research  demonstrates that effective CEM yields substantial performance benefits.
Superior experience management has been linked to increased customer satisfaction, loyalty, advocacy, and
long-term profitability (Kim et al., 2025; Mandagi et al., 2025; Walean et a l., 2023; Pongoh & Mandagi,
2025; Rantung et al., 2025; Walean et al., 2025). Personalized and context-aware interactions, in particular,
enhance customer engagement, increase visit frequency, expand wallet share, and strengthen relational
bonds, ultimately translating into improved financial performance (Shareef et al., 2024; Mandagi et al.,
2024; Walean et al., 2024). Recent studies further illustrate how experiential constructs such as brand
gestalt, brand trust, and customer citizenship behavior interac t with CEM -related mechanisms across
sectors, including healthcare, tourism, telecommunications, retail, and emerging digital markets (Walean et
al., 2023; Mandagi et al., 2024; Mandagi et al., 2025; Walean et al., 2025).
Despite these advances, the theoretical development of CEM remains fragmented. Existing models
often focus on isolated outcomes (e.g., satisfaction or loyalty), specific technologies, or single industries,
without integrating these into a coherent,  cumulative theoretical framework. Moreover, much of the
prevailing CEM literature is grounded in developed economies, implicitly assuming homogeneous
customer expectations, advanced digital infrastructure, and stable institutional settings. This limits th e
explanatory power and contextual validity of existing CEM theories when applied to emerging markets
such as Indonesia, where cultural diversity, relational consumption patterns, uneven digital maturity, and
hybrid service ecosystems play a critical role in shaping customer experiences. While recent Indonesian
studies have examined related constructs such as brand gestalt, brand equity, social media marketing, and
customer loyalty (Walean et al., 2023; Mandagi et al., 2024; Pongoh & Mandagi, 2025; Walean e t al.,
2025), these insights remain theoretically dispersed and insufficiently positioned within an explicit CEM
framework.
This study addresses this critical gap by advancing a theory-oriented synthesis of CEM research in
the Indonesian context. Rather than introducing another fragmented empirical model, this study adopts a
knowledge-structuring and theory -building perspective. It systematically maps the intellectual structure,
dominant themes, and conceptual linkages that underpin CEM research, thereby clarifying the theoretical
connections among antecedents, experiential processes, and outcomes. In doing so, the study contributes to
CEM theory in three key ways. First, it consolidates fragmented research streams into an integrative
conceptual framework that explicates the core dimensions of CEM. Second, it extends existing CEM
models by embeddin g them within an emerging -market context, highlighting how sociocultural and
technological conditions reshape experience management processes. Third, it positions CEM as an

362

ecosystem-level construct that bridges marketing, branding, digital strategy, and customer behavior
theories.
The originality of this study lies in its meta-theoretical contribution to CEM. By synthesizing prior
empirical findings and theoretical perspectives, the study moves beyond outcome -driven explanations. It
reframes CEM as a dynamic system of value co-creation involving customers, organizations, technologies,
and market environments. This reconceptualization not only advances theoretical clarity but also enhances
the practical relevance of CEM by offering a transferable analytical framework for scholars, managers, and
policymakers operating in complex and resource-variable contexts. Accordingly, this study seeks to address
the following research questions:
RQ1. How has CEM research evolved within the Indonesian context?
RQ2. What is the underlying thematic structure of CEM research in Indonesia?
RQ3. What theoretical gaps and underexplored relationships characterize the current CEM literature?

METHODOLOGY
This study employs a systematic literature review (SLR) to ensure a transparent, rigorous, and
replicable synthesis of CEM research in the Indonesian context. The review protocol is summarized in
Table 1, while the step-by-step article screening and selection procedure is illustrated in Figure 1, following
a PRISMA-inspired approach. The initial search was conducted using the Scopus database, which is known
for its extensive coverage of peer -reviewed journals in business and management research. Applying the
search string (customer AND experience AND management) to titles, abstracts, and keywords within the
period 2010–2025 yielded an initial pool of 210 records.

Publication trends
Figure 2 depicts the annual distribution of publications and citations on CEM from 2010 to 2025.
During the early period between 2010 and 2016, research activity was minimal, with almost no publications
and negligible citation counts, indicating that CEM had not yet gained strong academic traction. A gradual
increase in scholarly output began in 2017, marking the initial emergence of systematic academic interest
in CEM. This upward trend became more visible in 2018, with a modest rise in publications, follo wed by
a substantial increase in 2019, when the number of documents grew sharply, signaling a turning point in
the field’s development.

higher than in the pre-2018 period, reflecting sustained research attention. A significant surge occurred in

robust. Citation trends closely mirrored and amplified this pattern, with a marked acceleration beginning

cumulative knowledge-building, and the rising theoretical relevance of CEM research, particularly in the

illustrating both the thematic maturity and contextual specificity of CEM research in Indonesia.

Cluster 1 (Red) consists of 18 high-frequency keywords that collectively represent the core domain

Cluster 2 (Green) comprises 16 keywords related to brand management and brand equity

brand commitment further emphasizes the experiential foundations of brand relationships. This thematic
focus is strongly supported by studies such as Maduretno and Junaedi (2022), which examine how brand
experience influences loyalty through emotional and trust-based mechanisms in the coffee shop industry.

confirm that customer experience serves not only as a perceptual construct but also as a strategic lever to

Direction for Future Research
The density map shown in Figure 6 highlights a maturing yet unevenly developed research landscape
in CEM within the Indonesian context. High-density areas cluster around well-established themes such as
customer experience, customer satisfaction, service qu ality, brand experience, and customer loyalty,
indicating that these topics form the core of existing CEM scholarship. In contrast, several lower -density
but emerging themes suggest promising avenues for future research. These include customer engagement
management, data analytics, artificial intelligence, omnichannel strategy, emotional value, and customer
journey analysis, which remain comparatively underexplored despite their growing relevance. The spatial
distribution of keywords reflects a transition f rom traditional service- and satisfaction-based perspectives
toward more technology-enabled, strategic, and value-co-creation–oriented approaches. Addressing these
emerging themes through integrative , context -sensitive research can broaden CEM's theoretical scope,
enhance its practical relevance, and support the development of more adaptive and sustainable customer
experience strategies in Indonesia and similar emerging markets.

Several emerging yet underdeveloped themes can be identified from the density visualization of
CEM research in Indonesia. First, although customer experience research emphasizes personalization,
demographic-based experience differentiation, such as gender or generational differences, remains weakly
developed. The peripheral presence of demographic -related keywords suggests opportunities to examine
how customer characteristics shape experience perception, emotional responses, and loyalty formation
across service contexts. Second, emotional and psychological dimensions of customer experience appear
underrepresented. While satisfaction and motivation -related terms are visible, deeper constructs such as
emotional well-being, anxiety, and affective engagement receive limited attention. This indicates scope for
advancing CEM research beyond cognitive evaluations toward more emotion -centered and experiential
models that capture stress, trust, and emotional value, particularly in high-involvement services. Third, the
density map reveals relatively low emphasis on advanced digital and analytical capabilities, despite the
growing relevance of data analytics, artificial intelligence, and omnichannel technologies. This gap
highlights the need for research on dig ital readiness, technology acceptance, and the role of intelligent
systems in enhancing experience design and real -time personalization.  Fourth, ethical and cultural
sensitivity in experience management emerges as a marginal yet important theme. Keywords related to
ethics, values, and quality of life suggest that moral and cultural considerations influence customer
experience but  remain insufficiently theorized. Integrating ethical and intercultural perspectives could
strengthen CEM frameworks in diverse and emerging market settings. Finally, although customer feedback
tools are widely used, customer-reported experience measures remain primarily descriptive. Future research
should prioritize the development of robust, context -specific experience measurement frameworks to
capture long-term, subjective, and relational aspects of customer experience. Collectively, these themes
point toward a more holistic, technology-enabled, and human-centered CEM research agenda.

CONCLUSIONS
This study has examined the thematic landscape of CEM research in Indonesia, identifying four
major clusters of high -frequency keywords that represent the core areas of scholarly focus: customer
experience and relationship management ; brand management and brand equity development; digital and
technology-enabled marketing; and sales performance and market-driven strategy. Together, these clusters
reflect the evolution of CEM from traditional service quality perspectives toward more integrated,
experience-centric, and outcome-oriented frameworks. While the literature demonstrates growing thematic
diversity and increasing scholarly engagement, the distribution of research across clusters remains uneven,
with certain strategically important areas receiving comparatively limited attention.
The keyword co -occurrence and bibliometric analyses reveal several underdeveloped yet critical
research directions. First, despite the centrality of personalization in CEM, demographic -based and
experiential heterogeneity, such as differences in customer characteristics and emotional responses, remains
insufficiently explored. Second, although satisfaction and engagement are widely studied, the emotional
and psychological dimensions of customer experience are still under -theorized, limiting understanding of
affective value creation and long -term relational outcomes. Third, while digital transformation features
prominently, advanced analytics, artificial intelligence, and real -time experience orchestration appear as
emerging rather than fully integrated theme s, indicating a gap between technological potential and
theoretical development. Fourth, ethical and cultural considerations in experience design remain peripheral,
despite their relevance in diverse and emerging market contexts. Finally, existing studies rely heavily on
descriptive customer feedback measures, underscoring the need for more robust, context -sensitive
experience measurement frameworks that capture longitudinal and relational aspects of customer
experience.
The implications of these findings are twofold. From a theoretical perspective, they call for more
integrative and interdisciplinary CEM research that bridges marketing, psychology, data analytics, and
ethics to advance holistic experience models. From a m anagerial standpoint, the results highlight the
importance of developing digitally enabled, emotionally intelligent, and ethically grounded CEM strategies
that are responsive to contextual and cultural nuances.
Several limitations should be noted. The analysis relies primarily on keyword co -occurrence and
bibliometric techniques, which may not fully capture the conceptual depth of individual studies.
Additionally, the focus on Indonesian -based publications may limit the generalizability of the findings to
other contexts, though it enhances  contextual relevance. Future research could complement bibliometric
mapping with qualitative synthesis or theory -driven reviews to further enrich the understanding of CEM
development.
