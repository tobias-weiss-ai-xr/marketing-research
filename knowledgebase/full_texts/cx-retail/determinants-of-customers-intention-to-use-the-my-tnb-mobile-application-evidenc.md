Abstract
Leveraging mobile application features, the utility industry chooses such an advantageous
platform to enhance customer value, customer satisfaction, and long -term engagement
through continuance usage intention. Tenaga Nasional Berhad (TNB), as the main ele ctricity
utility company in Malaysia, considers customer satisfaction one of its main concerns as an
electricity energy provider, which led it to explore and adopt digitalization in its business
strategy. MyTNB mobile applications, categorized under smart retail technologies, have been
commercialized as a medium to provide excellent customer experiences by providing
superior, personalized retail services. Continuous improvement and exceeding customers’
expectations by providing mobile solutions lead to the necessity of this study, as it explores
the status of the myTNB  mobile applications and aligns with the company’s profit and
sustainability strategy. In addition, business strategy and business performance could be
supported by understanding the driving factors of customers’ mobile app use, including how
they affect myTNB’s usage intention. Hence, investigates the relationships among theory of
Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), consist of Perceived Ease of Use (PEOU), Perceived
Usefulness (PU) and Technological Readiness (TR) theory with customers’ Attitude (ATT) and
Intention to Use (ITU) myTNB mobile applications are the study focus. The data were collected
from registered customers of TNB, and SmartPLS  4.0 was used for the analysis. PU and TR
results positively influence the customer’s ATT, which in turn influences their ITU of the
myTNB mobile application. PEOU affects the customer’s positive ATT. However, PEOU has no
direct influence on the ITU of the myTNB mobile application.

Literature Review
Underpinning Theory
TAM is one of the most common models used to examine the development of information
technologies and the potential for end users to employ them (Davis, 1989, 1993; Davis et al.,
1989). A cognitive model was refined by Davis (1989) through the Theory of Reasoned Action
(TRA), from which TAM was proposed in the domain of information systems and shares
similarities with the TRA (Fishbein and Ajzen, 1975). To evaluate an individual’s acceptance of
technology, including innovations such as electronic commerce, m obile commerce, social
commerce, and electronic banking, TAM is commonly used based on TRA (Dash & Mohanty,
2023). Perceived ease of use and perceived usefulness are the two core cognitive components
in TAM. Ferreira et al. (2023) identified the intention to use technology as determined by an

Relationship between Perceived Ease of Use and Perceived Usefulness
The relationship between perceived ease of use and perceived usefulness is significant
(Herzallah et al., 2022). Given their conceptual closeness yet distinct theoretical terms, an
argument always arises over how to differentiate between perceived ease of use and
perceived usefulness. Perceived ease of use refers to the effort required to use the syst em,
whereas the benefits gained from using it relate to perceived usefulness. Herzallah  et al.
(2022) mentioned that the availability of easy -to-use websites on mobile devices attracts
customers to frequently used ones, which customers readily understand. Consequently,
perceived usefulness is enhanced whenever the technology or new system is found to be easy
to use and understandable (Kim et al., 2023). Moreover, they found it helpful and
demonstrated a positive attitude towards mobile application use when no extra effort was
experienced by customers upon using the information system (S. ur  Rahman et al., 2024).
Hence, the following hypothesis was proposed for this study:
H1: Perceived ease of use has a positive and significant influence on perceived usefulness and
intention to use the myTNB mobile application.

Relationship between Perceived Ease of Use, Attitude, and Intention to Use
Customers’ attitude fundamentally shaped the relationship between perceived ease of use
and intention to use mobile applications through its mediating role (Soodan et al., 2023).
Furthermore, Soodan et al. (2023) specifically found that in agro-advisory mobile applications,
attitude fully mediates the relationship between perceived ease of use and usage intention.
By helping users stay focused on the transaction, prevent confusion, and reduce cognitive
load, Anwar (2024) found that this results from a seamless experience, with smooth browsing
of relevant products without transaction-related obstacles. Expanding the ease of use enables
easier transactions while also resulting in a positive experience, which fosters a positive
attitude towards mobile applications (Hasan 2022, Rahman et al. 2024). Moreover, in mobile
applications, attitude plays a crucial role in usage intention (Soodan et al., 2023). By providing
easy and less complicated features for customers, the intent to use them may increase. The
following hypotheses were proposed for this study:
H3: Perceived ease of use has a positive and significant influence on intention to use the
myTNB mobile application.
H7: Perceived ease of use has a positive and significant influence on the attitude of intention
to use the myTNB mobile application.
H10: Attitudes positively and significantly mediate the influence of perceived ease of use on
intention to use the myTNB mobile application.

Relationship between Perceived Usefulness, Attitude, and Intention to Use
Beyond the mediating role of attitude between perceived ease of use and intention to use,
ElSayad (2024) noted the significant mediating role of customers’ attitude between perceived
usefulness and intention to use a mobile application, which forms individual intention
towards actual usage. Prior to actual use, a positive attitude indicates that users  are more
likely to believe their lives improve, performance and usefulness through the technology
(Khor et al., 2023). The perceived usefulness comes in various forms, such as through
providing faster services at a lower price as compared to conventional methods (Almajal et
al., 2023), health improvement through mobile health applications (Khor et al., 2023) and the
structure, design and content associated with mobile applications offered (Kampa, 2023).
Furthermore, Cimbaljević et al. (2023) have identified perceived usefulness as a key driver of
attitudes toward technology use among individuals who feel insecure about using it. For
individuals who do not perceive mobile applications or other technologies as useful, Khor et
al. (2023) mentioned that they can still demonstrate a positive attitude toward them
depending on the objective. Hence, a positive attitude from customers toward the usage of
technology acts as an indicator of actual usage and acceptability of technology (Khor  et al.,
2023). Thus, the following hypotheses were proposed for this study:
H2: Perceived usefulness has a positive and significant influence on intention to use myTNB
mobile application.
H6: Perceived usefulness has a positive and significant influence on the attitude of intention
to use myTNB mobile application.
H9: Attitudes positively and significantly mediate the influence of perceived usefulness on
intention to use the attitude of myTNB mobile application.

Relationship between Technological Readiness, Attitude, and Intention to Use
Customers’ attitude further strengthens the relationship between technological readiness
and intention to use mobile applications (Alhammadi et al., 2023; Baltaci et al., 2024;
Gopinathan, 2024). Cultural shifts in digitalization habits associated with technologies such as
mobile devices and applications are cited as an effort to expand smart learning
implementation (Alhammadi  et al., 2023). Furthermore, behavior and attitudes are
influenced by the cultural shifts towards digital culture, which, in turn, shape the adoption
and acceptance of new technologies (Alhammadi et al., 2023). In addition, Ofosu-Ampong et
al. (2023) stated that technology readiness acts as a driver of intention to use technology, and
individuals are more likely to adopt technology when their level of readiness is high. It is also
highlighted that managers need to deliver the essential tasks of earlier training to the required
individuals for technology deployment, as it reflects the significance of technology readiness
for users’ behavioral intention (Ofosu -Ampong et al., 2023). Consequently, a ro bust
theoretical framework indicates that TR alone does not directly translate into intention to
use. Rather, a favorable attitude mediates this relationship. Hence, the following hypotheses
were proposed for this study:
H4: Technological readiness has a positive and significant influence on intention to use the
myTNB mobile application.
H8: Technological readiness has a positive and significant influence on the attitude of
intention to use myTNB mobile application.
H5: Attitude has a positive and significant influence on intention to use the myTNB mobile
application.

Methodology
The study investigated the direct relationships between perceived usefulness, perceived ease
of use, technological readiness, and intention to use, with attitude serving as a mediator
among customers of TNB. Data were collected using survey questionnaires developed to
measure the constructs. The items were adapted from established scales to ensure reliability.
A purposive sampling technique was employed due to the absence of a complete sampling
frame. Surveys were distributed electronically via WhatsApp, email, and the Quick Response
Code (QR Code) medium. A cross -sectional design was used for this study as it provides a
snapshot of the population or phenomenon at a given time and offers the benefit of being
time- and resource -efficient (Creswell, 2014). The  target population comprises registered
residential customers of TNB, 8.9 million as per TNB’s annual report (TNB Annual Report,
2023). All constructs were measured using a 5 -point Likert scale, which is a reliable
measurement item suggested in existing literature (Jiang et al., 2021), ranging from “strongly
disagree” (1) to “strongly agree” (5). Of the 533 questionnaires, 415 usable responses were
obtained, yielding a high response rate. PLS -SEM using SmartPLS 4 was employed following
recommended procedures for predictive modeling and mediation analysis (Hair et al., 2022;
Ringle et al., 2022).

Discussion & Conclusion
Discussion
Based on the empirical findings of this study, Tenaga Nasional Berhad (TNB), as a dominant
institution in the electricity industry in Malaysia, must adopt a comprehensive strategy to
enhance the intention to use of myTNB mobile applications. This aligns with its business and
marketing strategy objectives by leveraging the significant mediating role of attitude, which
demonstrated the strongest direct effect on intention to use (β = 0.507). This indicates that
targeting attitude among Tenaga Nasional Berhad customers will yield the most substantial
improvements in their intention to use myTNB  mobile applications. The results confirm that
both perceived usefulness and technological readiness significantly support the intention to
use myTNB mobile applications, either directly or indirectly through customers’ attitudes.

Customers are more willing to use the application when they perceive that it improves their
performance through convenient features, including bill payment, consumption monitoring,
outage notifications, and other features that support their daily activitie s. Furthermore,
individuals with higher technological readiness tend to embrace digital utility services more
readily due to their mental preparedness and comfort with new technologies. Emerged as the
strongest predictor of intention to use, indicating tha t favorable evaluations and positive
feelings toward myTNB will substantially increase adoption intentions. Institutions like TNB
must establish robust, supportive frameworks to amplify technological readiness among
individuals in Malaysia, specifically by considering collaborations with the government and
other business players in the electrical industry. This is evidenced by the strongest direct
effect on attitude (β =0.357) alongside a significant direct effect on intention to use (β =
0.169). Furthermore, although perceived ease of use did not directly influen ce the intention
to use (β = 0.041), it significantly boosted attitude (β = 0.300), which subsequently enhanced
intention to use through mediation. Theoretically, the evolving role of usability in technology
usage can explain the insignificance of perceive d ease of use towards intention to use. Ease
of use could be one of the critical barriers in earlier stages of digital adoption, where it
determines whether engagement exists within the system. However, aligning with the
increase in smartphone penetration,  customers are more familiar with mobile applications,
thus reducing the ease of use as a differentiating factor. Therefore, TNB should systematically
demonstrate the digital tools that are compatible with usage to reduce the complexity
customers might exp erience when considering using their mobile application platform,
myTNB. The lack of a direct relationship between perceived ease of use and intention to use
may also indicate that perceived ease of use operates indirectly through perceived usefulness
to shape usage intention. This supports the context of myTNB that ease  of use alone is not
sufficient to secure intention, but that the benefits behind it should be highlighted as the
primary reason for usage.

Theoretical Implications
This study offers significant theoretical implications by successfully integrating the
Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) (Davis, 1989) with the Technology Readiness Theory to
create a more nuanced understanding of intention to use in mobile application settings. While
both theories have been applied independently, their synthesis provides novel insights into
how technology perceptions and individuals’ readiness toward technology function within
broader utility industry frameworks. The findings demonstrate that the integration addresses
an important theoretical gap by offering a more comprehensive understanding of how

technology acceptance factors act as psychological mechanisms that interact to influence
behavioral intention through attitude as a mediating variable. The non -significant direct

technologies are perceived as easy to use, hidden cognitive demands may simultaneously
emerge and mitigate behavioral intention. Despite the existing integration of both models in
the Technology Readiness and Acceptance Model (TRAM), this study broadens the theoretical
understanding of how technologically ready individuals develop stronger intentions to use

readiness resources as critical components in mediated settings for future theoretical

Practical Implications
This section provides important practical implications for Tenaga Nasional Berhad (TNB). It
strengthens the adoption and utilization of the myTNB mobile application among its
customers. Throughout the study, the findings suggest that understanding the dete rminants
of customers’ intention to use the application is crucial in designing more effective digital
engagement strategies. As a key channel for customer interaction, TNB can use the study’s
insights to improve the myTNB  mobile application experience, increase adoption, and
strengthen long-term customer relationships. Furthermore, the study findings align directly
with TNB’s digitalization agenda, supporting its commitment to achieving Net Zero 2050 goals
by encouraging c ustomers to actively monitor and manage their electricity consumption
through digital platforms. In addition, such promotion of greater usage of myTNB  and
paperless transactions can be facilitated by TNB to align with energy awareness improvement
and encourage customers to be responsible for their consumption behavior, which could
contribute to environmental sustainability and carbon footprint reduction . Additionally,
several opportunities for functionality enhancement and the attractiveness of the myTNB
mobile application were also highlighted through the study findings. Customer attitude was
identified as a key factor influencing usage intention. Prioritizing user -centric innovations
could improve customer perceptions and experiences. Gamification, rewa rd mechanisms,
energy-saving challenges, and personalized consumption insight features can encourage
greater engagement and behavioral change among users. An increase in myTNB  mobile
application adoption could generate significant operational benefits for TNB, which come
from reducing physical billing dependency, manual meter reading, and conventional
customer service channels through operational cost reduction, service delivery improvement,

initiatives, while enabling customers, including smart meter users and solar energy
prosumers, to effectively manage their energy usage, achieving greater value for both
customers and TNB.

In a broader view, the findings of this study provide implications for the electricity utility
industry and other service sectors undergoing digital transformation. The findings emphasize
the need for organizations to adopt a customer -centric approach when  developing and
implementing mobile applications as part of their business strategy. Instead of focusing only

Suggestions for Future Studies
Based on this study, several directions for future research are proposed. First, the adoption
of longitudinal research designs is encouraged to overcome the limitation of cross -sectional
studies, as the examination of behavioral changes over time is possib le with a longitudinal
approach. It provides stronger evidence of causal relationships and a more comprehensive
understanding of the evolving nature of mobile application adoption. Second, future
researchers should adopt mixed -method or qualitative approac hes, such as interviews and
focus group discussions, to gain deeper insights into customers’ perceptions, emotions,
experiences, and motivations for mobile application adoption. Third, the existing research
model should be extended for future research by incorporating other relevant variables, such
as subjective norms, trust, loyalty, enjoyment, rewards and organizational perceptions of
innovation, to achieve a more comprehensive understanding of related factors influencing
attitude, behavior, and intention  toward adoption. Fourth, investigating continuance usage
behavior is the next suggestion for future research, as it allows identification of factors that
drive long-term engagement with mobile applications. This suggested future study will further
contribute to customer satisfaction and loyalty. Finally, a comparative study involving other
utility providers will provide broader practical insights for such industries, particularly those
operating in more competitive environments, as well as different application domains.

Conclusion
This study successfully establishes that perceived usefulness and technological readiness
significantly influence both attitude and customers’ intention to use myTNB  mobile
applications. Integrating TAM and TR theory provides a robust framework showing that
attitude emerged as a strong predictor of intention to use and a significant mediator linking
perceived ease of use and intention to use. While perceived ease of u se boosts attitude, its
lack of direct effect on intention to use underscores the complex nature of technology
