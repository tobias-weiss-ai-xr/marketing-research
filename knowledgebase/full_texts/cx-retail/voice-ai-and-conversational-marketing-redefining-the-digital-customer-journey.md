Abstract
This study investigates the influence of Voice AI capabilities and conversational
marketing features on customer experience (CX), trust, satisfaction, and loyalty. Although prior
research has emphasized the role of CX in shaping consumer outcomes, limited attention has
been given to the combined effects of Voice AI and conversational marketing within a trust -
centered framework. This research addresses this gap by clarifying when and how trust matters
most in AI -mediated interactions. A quantitative, cross -sectional survey design was employed,
collecting data from consumers familiar with AI -driven services. Using PLS -SEM in SmartPLS
4.0, the study tested direct, mediating, and moderating effects across a sample representative of
digitally engaged users. Resul ts reveal that Voice AI (β = 0.426, p < 0.001) and conversational
marketing (β = 0.395, p < 0.001) significantly improve CX. CX strongly predicts satisfaction (β
= 0.699, p < 0.001) and moderately predicts loyalty (β = 0.216, p = 0.006). Trust directly
influences loyalty (β = 0.625, p < 0.001) but does not significantly affect satisfaction (p =
0.250). Moderation tests show trust strengthens CX → satisfaction but not CX → loyalty. The
study contributes by extending Customer Experience Theory, showing that C X drives
satisfaction, while trust sustains loyalty. Practically, it highlights the need for empathetic,
transparent AI design to balance short-term and long-term consumer outcomes.

Introduction
oice Artificial Intelligence (Voice AI) has emerged as a transformative force in shaping
digital customer journeys by enabling natural, human -like interactions between brands and
consumers. Unlike traditional task -based Chabot’s, voice -enabled systems comb ine
conversational, functional, and emotional capabilities to create meaningful engagement and
personalized experiences (Chaturvedi, Verma, & Srivastava, 2024). Within conversational
marketing, these technologies allow firms to deliver real -time recommenda tions, enhance
interactivity, and build companionship -driven relationships that extend beyond simple
transactions. This shift reflects a growing emphasis on dialogue -based strategies, where
customer-brand interactions a re mediated by intelligent systems capable of understanding
context and emotions (Alam et al., 2023). As a result, organizations increasingly integrate AI -
driven touchpoints to improve customer decision -making, reduce friction across channels, and
enhance brand engagement (Dong et al., 2024).
That said, much like any emerging technology there is still a host of important subtleties to
navigate in designing tryly more personalized and independent AI CX paths that are also deemed
trustworthy. Particularly, ov er-personalization might decrease autonomy and result in privacy
concerns (Wertenbroch et al., 2020; Nersessian & Mancha, 2021). Chabots and virtual agents, as
‘Brand Voice of a company’ on the other hand offer opportunity to enhance satisfaction and
loyalty, if they are transparent, context -aware and trustworthy in their interactions ( Rana et al.,
2022). Furthermore, voice -based AI assistants can serve as relationship partners (versus purely
servicers) and have an impact on customers ’ cognitive and emotional journey experiences
(Chaturvedi et al., 2024; Lemon & Verhoef, 2016), the study found. This is what makes the need
to understand how Voice AI and conversational marketing redefine digital customer journeys in
real time through an experience-trusted moderation of trust, which drives satisfaction and
ongoing loyalty so crucial now.
Voice AI and conversational marketing is also gaining pace, disrupting sectors racing to
reimagine their digital customer journeys. However, these advancements also make possible easy
adaptable and emotionally interactive experiences, which in turn results in customer satisfaction
and loyalty (Chaturvedi et al., 2024). Conversational -AI platforms used to support events
provide for new and established custom ers during purchase, point of sale and post -purchase
scenarios with model -and-context-specific retrains and in -the-moment help . However, the
increase in use of algorithmic personalization raises significant concerns regarding auto nomy,
perceived surveillance and excessive reliance on machine -mediated judgments (Wertenbroch et
al., 2020). Furthermore, industries face barriers to establish trust and transparency, as poor
design of AI or algorithmic bias could lead to bad customer exp eriences that destroy brand
relationships (Nersessian & Mancha, 2021; Huang & Rust, 2021). These obstacles underline the
importance of win-win positioning due to technological breakthrough and demand -driven-based
movements: Opportunities on how VAI can be leveraged for competitive advantage.

Some recent work on Voice AI leverages conversational, task -based and emotional capabilities

Theoretical Framework
Customer Experience Theory and AI-enabled journeys
Customer Experience (CX) theory positions experience as the core mediator linking firm -
provided stimuli to customer evaluations and behaviors. In the context of AI, CX is shaped by
the seamlessness, personalization, and emotional resonance of digital touchpoints ( Dong, Liu, &
Wu, 2024 ). Voice AI capabilities and conversational marketing tools exemplify such stimuli ,
offering human -like dialogue, emotional support, and functional assistance that enrich

1803

experiences beyond transactional exchanges . Earlier foundational work stressed that customer
journeys involve pre -purchase, purchas e, and post -purchase interactions, each contributing
uniquely to CX (Lemon & Verhoef, 2016; Dwivedi et al., 2021). Thus, grounding this research
in CX theory allows the study to explain how conversational AI technologies drive perceived
value, satisfaction, and eventual loyalty by first enhancing the lived experience.
Building on CX theory, recent scholarship highlights the critical role of trust in ensuring that
technologically mediated experiences convert into satisfaction and loyalty (Weidig, Weippert, &
Kuehnl, 2024; Sussman, Bright, & Wilcox, 2023 ). Trust is not automatically granted to AI -
driven touchpoints; it must be earned through transparency, fairness, and reliability (Alam et al.,
2023). If trust is present, CX is more li kely to produce favorable outcomes such as repeat
purchase and positive word of mouth. Conversely, when consumers perceive surveillance,
manipulation, or bias, trust erodes, weakening the CX –outcome link (Ostrom et al., 2021;
Wertenbroch, Vosgerau, & Bruyn eel, 2020). Positioning trust within CX theory therefore
enriches the model by showing that AI -enabled experiences have both direct effects (on
satisfaction and loyalty) and conditional effects (strengthened or weakened by trust).
Research Purpose and Objectives
The purpose of this research is to extend Customer Experience Theory into the era of
conversational AI, demonstrating how Voice AI capabilities and conversational marketing
features reshape consumer –brand relationships. The main objective is to test  how these AI -
enabled inputs influence customer experience, and in turn, trust, satisfaction, and loyalty within
digital customer journeys. By embedding trust into the CX framework, this study offers a more
holistic theoretical explanation of how customer outcomes emerge in AI-mediated environments
(Dong et al., 2024). Prior studies provide the foundation for this extension by underscoring CX
as a nonlinear and multi-dimensional process (Lemon & Verhoef, 2016;  De Keyser et al., 2020).
In doing so, the research not only contributes to CX theory but also guides managers on
designing transparent, empathetic, and context -aware AI strategies that foster both short -term
satisfaction and long-term loyalty.
Supporting and Negating Perspectives
Scholars increasingly agree that Voice AI and conversational marketing can significantly
enhance customer journeys by improving personalization, efficiency, and emotional connection
(Dong, Liu, & Wu, 2024). By embedding natural dialogue, empathy, and functional assistance,
AI systems elevate the quality of customer experience, which in turn fosters trust, satisfaction,
and loyalty (Rana et al., 2022). Earlier re search emphasized that customer experience is a multi -
stage, nonlinear process where touchpoints collectively influence long -term loyalty, reinforcing
the idea that Voice AI capabilities are central enablers of value ( De Keyser et al., 2020). These
supporting views establish Voice AI and conversational marketing as positive drivers in creating
sustainable consumer–brand relationships when aligned with CX theory.

Conceptualization
Existing research on AI -enabled customer j ourneys has largely conceptualized relationships
through Customer Experience (CX) theory, highlighting how AI technologies such as chatbots,
voice assistants, and personalized recommendation systems enhance perceived value and
engagement (Dong, Liu, & Wu, 2024). Prior studies have shown that CX mediates the impact of
technology on satisfaction and loyalty, but much of this work remains fragmented, often
focusing on single variables like personalization or automation without integrating trust as a
central factor. Earlier frameworks established that customer journeys are nonlinear and multi -
touchpoint, requiring coherence and transparency for long -term success (Lemon & Verhoef,
2016; Dwived i et al., 2021). What has been done is the identification of CX as the mediator
between technology and outcomes, but what needs to be done is to extend these models by
embedding trust as both a mediator and moderator, explaining how ethical design, transpa rency,
and fairness condition the pathways from Voice AI and conversational marketing features to
satisfaction and loyalty. This conceptualization strengthens theory by integrating CX and trust
into a unified framework, while guiding practitioners toward h uman-centric AI strategies that
balance efficiency with autonomy.

Methodology
Research Design
This study adopts a quantitative research type since it aims to examine hypothesized
relationships among Voice AI capabilities, conversational marketing features,  customer
experience, trust, satisfaction, and loyalty using measurable constructs. Quantitative approaches
are widely recognized for their ability to test causal paths and validate conceptual models in
technology adoption and marketing contexts ( Dong, Liu, & Wu, 2024). Such approaches allow
for statistical rigor, reliability testing, and model validation, making them suitable for studies
grounded in theories such as customer experience and relationship marketing. Prior research in
digitally driven customer journeys has also demonstrated that a survey -based quantitative
methodology allows researchers to collect consumer perceptions at the aggregate level and make
generalizable inferences (Dwivedi et al., 2021). Therefore, the use of quantitative methodology is
so relevant in this research since it provides procedures to be able to test hypotheses within an
ordered procedure.
The reason for choosing a quantitative design  was the capability of examining hypothesis based
on theory tested by measurable constructs ensuring objectivity and reliability. Recent research in
AI-enabled marketing has shown that quantitative approaches successfully capture people’s
perceptions of pe rsonalization, trust, and satisfaction, allowing researchers to statistically
validate conceptual models ( Dong et al., 2024). This direct and mediated but also moderating
relationship can be tested by researc hers in statistical softwares like PLS -SEM even with
complex models (Henseler et al., 2016) and that is crucial when we need to understand how
Voice AI or conversational marketing are affecting the consumer experience. It is also important
to bear in mind,  as these previous studies do, that quantitative research generates generalizable
knowledge and not population -based evidence i.e. scientific understanding across wider
population, which is a condition necessary for any paper concerned with consumer adopti on and
loyalty (Dwivedi et al., 2021; Lemon & Verhoef, 2016). In this sense, it is empirically robust and
theoretically novel.
A cross-sectional survey design is used given its capacity for data collection over a relatively
large sample and at one point in  time, which makes it efficient for testing structural relationships
in dynamic digital environments. Facing this, current literature advocates cross -sectional designs
to seize consumer’s reactions when being exposed to AI -based applications since adoption
actions and perceived values are captures in real -time (Weidig et al., 2024 ). These are especially
useful in investigations of new technologies, when long -term data may be unavailable bu t short-
term trends warrant analysis for practical implications. As previous researches also confirm the
appropriateness of cross -sectional studies for analyzing mediation and moderation effects, and
providing strong tests for multi -value relationships in marketing and IS (Rana et al., 2022;
Dwivedi et al., 2021). Thus, such a design is justified as being methodologically valid and
practically efficient for the purposes of the present study.
Methodologically, the study adopts a cross -sectional survey design  and a quantitative approach

Results and Discussion
The results of this study demonstrate that Voice AI capabilities and conversational marketing
features are strong predictors of customer experience, confirming their role in shaping digital
customer journeys (; ; Dong, Liu, & Wu, 2024). Customer experience was found to significantly
influence both satisfaction and loyalty, with the relationship to satisfaction being especially
strong, aligning with prior research that highlights CX as the primary mediator of consumer
outcomes. However, customer trust showed only a weak and non -significant direct effect on
loyalty, indicating that while trust contributes marginally, it is not the domina nt pathway in AI -
enabled contexts. These findings echo earlier studies, which suggested that trust may not always
guarantee loyalty without consistent reinforcement across touchpoints (Lemon & Verhoef, 2016;
Dwivedi et al., 2021). Overall, the results stre ngthen the argument that technology -driven CX is
the most critical driver of satisfaction and loyalty, while the role of trust requires further
theoretical and empirical refinement.

Discussion
The results of this study make notable theoretical contributions to the understanding of AI -
enabled customer journeys by positioning customer experience (CX) as the central mediator
between technological capabilities and consumer outcomes. The finding that Voice AI
capabilities (VAC → CE) and C onversational Marketing Features (CMF → CE) significantly
influence customer experience reinforces Customer Experience Theory, which posits that
customer evaluations are constructed from multi -touchpoint interactions ( Dong, Liu, & Wu,
2024). This aligns with the theoretical proposition that technological innovations do not create
value in isolation but are filtered through the lived experiences of customers (Lemon & Verhoef,
2016). Furthermore, the strong positive relationship between CX and Customer Satisfaction (β =
0.699) highlights CX as a primary pathway in technology adoption models, extending theoretical
frameworks by confirming the dominance of experience over trust in immediate satisfaction.
However, the weak direct effect of Customer Trust on Satisfaction (β = 0.093, ns) diverges from
some earlier models that considered trust an essential antecedent of satisfaction (Dwivedi et al.,
2021). This suggests that in AI -mediated contexts, trust may be better conceptualized as a long -
term relational construct rather than an immediate determinant, extending theoretical
perspectives on the sequencing of CX and trust.

This study also contributes to existing literature by empirically validating and extend ing prior
findings on AI in marketing. The significant pathways VAC → CE and CMF → CE confirm that
conversational technologies enhance experience, supporting recent studies that demonstrated
how AI -driven interactions improve relevance, efficiency, and emo tional connection (Weidig,
Weippert, & Kuehnl, 2024; Sussman, Bright, & Wilcox, 2023; ). The strong mediating role of
CX in driving Satisfaction corroborates earlier literature that consistently positioned CX as the
foundation of posit ive consumer outcomes (De Keyser et al., 2020; Lemon & Verhoef, 2016).
Yet, the study diverges in showing that Customer Trust strongly drives Loyalty (β = 0.625) but
does not significantly influence Satisfaction, challenging prior studies where trust was f ound to
shape both satisfaction and loyalty simultaneously (Rana et al., 2022; Dwivedi et al., 2021).
Moreover, the finding that the interaction effect of CX × Trust on Loyalty is non -significant (β =
–0.005) contradicts studies suggesting trust amplifies loyalty outcomes (Dong et al., 2024; ).
Instead, trust’s moderating role was significant for CX → Satisfaction, indicating that trust
strengthens immediate experiential evaluations rather than long -term loyalty in AI -driven
settings. This nuanced result contributes to the literature by refining our understanding of when
and where trust matters most in AI-mediated journeys.
From a managerial perspective, the findings hold important practical implications for firms
deploying Voice AI an d conversational marketing tools. The evidence that both VAC and CMF
significantly improve CX suggests that organizations should invest in designing empathetic,
transparent, and context -aware conversational systems to maximize consumer engagement ( ; ;
Dong et al., 2024). The strong effect of CX on Satisfaction indicates that managers must
prioritize seamless, multi -channel integration to deliver consistent experiences that generate
immediate consumer fulfillment ( Weidig et al., 2024; De Keyser et al., 2020). Furthermore, the
robust impact of Trust on Loyalty highlights that cultivating long -term customer relationships
depends heavily on building and maintaining trust in AI systems. This finding resonates with
literature showing that trust reinforces repeat purchases and advocacy, but it also cautions
managers that satisfaction alone may not secure loyalty (Dwivedi et al., 2021; Lemon &
Verhoef, 2016). However, the non-significant moderation of trust on loyalty suggests that simply
adding transparency features may not be sufficient; trust must be nurtured through continuous
reinforcement across multiple interactions, rather than assumed as an automatic loyalty driver.
Thus, businesses must strategically balance short -term satisfaction gains through CX with long -
term trust-building practices to secure customer loyalty in AI-mediated environments.
Integrating theoretical, literature, and practical contributions, this study underscores the primacy
of customer experience as the linchpin of AI-driven customer relationships, while also clarifying
the conditional role of trust. The findings support prior studies showing that experience
consistently predicts satisfaction and loyalty ( ; Dong et al., 2024; ), yet they also highlight
divergences by showing trust influences loyalty directly but not satisfaction, contrasting with
studies where trust was essential for both (Dwivedi et al., 2021; Rana et al., 2022). This suggests
that in prac tice, firms must treat CX and trust as complementary rather than interchangeable
assets: CX drives short -term satisfaction, while trust builds long -term loyalty. The study also
demonstrates that trust moderates the CX → Satisfaction link but not CX → Loyal ty, refining
existing literature by specifying that trust amplifies perceptions of fairness and transparency in
experiences, but loyalty is more resistant to single -episode interventions. Collectively, these

insights contribute to advancing theory by refin ing trust’s placement within CX models, extend
literature by offering nuanced evidence of mediation and moderation, and provide actionable
guidance for firms seeking to leverage AI technologies for sustainable customer relationships.
Conclusion
This study demonstrated that Voice AI capabilities and conversational marketing features
significantly shape customer experience, which in turn strongly influences satisfaction and
moderately predicts loyalty. The findings confirm that CX is the most critical driver of short -
term outcomes, while trust plays a stronger role in long -term loyalty but has little direct impact
on satisfaction ( ; ; Dong, Liu, & Wu, 2024). This aligns with prior work emphasiz ing CX as a
mediator of consumer value but diverges from studies that placed trust as a universal antecedent
of both satisfaction and loyalty (Dwivedi et al., 2021; Lemon & Verhoef, 2016). Theoretically,
the study extends Customer Experience Theory by clar ifying that trust enhances satisfaction
when paired with transparent CX but does not always amplify loyalty, suggesting that trust must
be conceptualized as a relational construct that matures over time (Weidig, Weippert, & Kuehnl,
2024; Rana et al., 2022).
From a practical perspective, the findings urge managers to prioritize seamless and transparent
CX design, integrating personalization, empathy, and omni -channel consistency to maximize
satisfaction ( ; ). At th e same time, trust -building remains essential for loyalty, requiring
organizations to invest in ethical AI practices, fairness, and data transparency to reinforce long -
term relationships (Dong et al., 2024; Dwivedi et al., 2021). The non -significant moderation of
trust on loyalty suggests that single -episode experiences may not be enough to secure lasting
commitment; rather, loyalty requires ongoing reinforcement across multiple interactions
(Sussman, Bright, & Wilcox, 2023; Lemon & Verhoef, 2016). Thus, th is study contributes by
showing that CX is indispensable for satisfaction, while loyalty depends on the interplay of CX
and sustained trust, providing both theoretical refinement and practical guidance for leveraging
AI technologies in customer engagement.
Future Research Directions
While this study contributes significantly to understanding the role of Voice AI and
conversational marketing in shaping customer experience, satisfaction, trust, and loyalty, it is not
without limitations. Methodologically, the use of a cross -sectional su rvey design limits causal
inference, as relationships were tested at one point in time rather than across multiple
touchpoints or contexts (; Dong, Liu, & Wu, 2024; ). Future research should employ longitudinal
designs or experimental approaches to capture the evolution of trust and loyalty over repeated
interactions. Additionally, the study focused on a single -country sample (Pakistan), which may
restrict generalizability, as cultural diffe rences shape perceptions of AI, trust, and satisfaction
(Dwivedi et al., 2021; Lemon & Verhoef, 2016). Expanding this research across multiple
countries could enhance cross -cultural insights and refine theoretical models. Theoretically, the
study applied C ustomer Experience Theory with trust as a mediator/moderator, but future work
could integrate complementary frameworks such as Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) or
Relationship Marketing Theory to capture additional drivers like perceived ease of use or

emotional attachment (Rana et al., 2022; De Keyser et al., 2020). Finally, while SmartPLS -SEM
was appropriate for testing mediation and moderation, future work may benefit from fsQCA or
multi-group analysis to capture configurational and demographic nuances that SEM alone may
not detect.
For practitioners, the results highlight the need to prioritize customer experience design as the
strongest predictor of satisfaction and a key enabler of loyalty. Firms should focus on delivering
empathetic, context-aware, and transparent AI-driven interactions that reduce friction and foster
immediate satisfaction (Weidig, Weippert, & Kuehnl, 2024; Sussman, Bright, & Wilcox, 2023; ).
The finding that trust significantly predicts loyalty implies that bu sinesses must go beyond
functional efficiency to invest in ethical AI governance, privacy protection, and fairness,
ensuring that long -term consumer confidence is maintained (Dong et al., 2024; Dwivedi et al.,
2021). Managers should recognize that satisfac tion does not automatically translate into loyalty;
loyalty requires consistent reinforcement across multiple interactions and is more dependent on
building sustained trust than one -time experiences (Rana et al., 2022; Lemon & Verhoef, 2016).
This suggests  practical strategies such as ongoing transparency reports, user control over
personalization, and robust feedback loops to strengthen relational bonds. Moreover, as AI
technologies are globally deployed, managers must adapt AI strategies to cultural and m arket
contexts, ensuring that conversational systems resonate with diverse customer expectations.
Collectively, these implications guide organizations toward balancing short -term satisfaction
with long -term loyalty, demonstrating that customer experience a nd trust are complementary
levers for competitive advantage in AI-driven marketing.
