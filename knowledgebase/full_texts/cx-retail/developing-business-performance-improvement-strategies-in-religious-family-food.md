ABSTRACT

Improving business performance has become increasingly challenging for established service
firms operating in highly competitive and digitally mediated markets. This study explores how
business performance improvement strategies can be formulated in the co ntext of religious
family food services, where operational excellence, trust, and customer experience jointly
influence competitive advantage. A qualitative single -case study design was employed
involving six purposively selected informants representing ke y organizational functions and
the industry. Data were collected through semi-structured interviews, company documents,
secondary data, and a management focus group discussion. Internal organizational conditions
were analyzed using the Resource -Based View, while external pressures were examined
through PESTEL and Porter's Five Forces. The findings were integrated into a SWOT analysis,
translated into strategic alternatives using the TOWS matrix, and prioritized through
management scoring. The study identifies valuable organizational resources, including brand
reputation, operational experience, halal and legal compliance, service quality, and customer
trust. However, these strengths are constrained by coordination gaps, uneven operational
support, underutili zed assets, limited capability development, and ineffective value
communication. T welve strategic alternatives were generated, with three implementation
priorities emerging: strengthening response speed and lead management, integrating the
digital customer journey, and enhancing marketing communication targeting young Muslim
families. The findings demonstrate that sustainable business performance in religious family
food services depends not only on organizational resources but also on the firm's ability to
integrate digital capabilities, stakeholder trust, and customer experience into a coherent
strategic framework. The study contributes to strategic management literature by illustrating
how integrated SWOT –TOWS analysis can support evidence -based strategy formulation in
trust-intensive service industries.

INTRODUCTION
Business performance has long been recognized as a central determinant of
organizational sustainability and competitive advantage. From the Resource -Based View
(RBV), differences in organizational performance arise because firms possess
heterogeneous resources and capabilities that vary in their ability to create and sustain
value (Barney, 1991; Wernerfelt, 1984). However, contemporary research argues that
valuable resources alone are insufficient to maintain superior performance. Organizations
must continuously adapt their capabilities to changing market conditions, technological
developments, and evolving customer expectations, particularly in service industries
experiencing rapid digital transformation (Abrokwah-Larbi, 2024; Behl et al., 2022; Barney
& He sterly, 2015; Kusa et al., 2024; Teece, 2007). Consequently, improving business
performance has become a strategic challenge that extends beyond resource ownership
to the organization's ability to coordinate, communicate, and deliver value effectively.
Recent strategic management research has increasingly emphasized that business
performance is shaped through the interaction between internal organizational
capabilities and external competitive environments. Studies have shown that customer -
value analytics, information management, and digital capabilities enhance organizational
resilience and competitive advantage by enabling firms to respond more effectively to
market dynamics (Abrokwah-Larbi, 2024; Behl et al., 2022; Kusa et al., 2024). Likewise,
customer experience research demonstrates that purchasing decisions emerge from a
series of interconnected touchpoints in which response speed, information quality, service
consistency, and organizational credibility jointly influence customer evaluation
(Homburg et al., 2017; Lemon & Verhoef, 2016; Verhoef et al., 2021). These developments
indicate that sustainable performance increasingly depends on an organization's capability
to integrate operational resources with customer-facing processes throughout the service
journey.
These strategic challenges become even more complex in trust -intensive service
industries, where customers evaluate not only functional performance but also
organizational legitimacy and perceived credibility. Religious family food services
represent a distinctive example because the service simultaneously involves food quality,
religious compliance, emotional family experiences, and ceremonial significance.
Customers therefore assess multiple dimensions before making purchasing decisions,
including halal a ssurance, Islamic compliance, product quality, service responsiveness,
convenience, delivery reliability, and organizational trust. Unlike conventional catering
businesses, competitive advantage in this context depends on the firm's ability to combine
operational excellence with credible religious assurance and meaningful customer
experiences throughout the purchasing process (Wilson & Liu, 2011).
Previous studies have employed strategic management tools such as SWOT, TOWS,
PESTEL, the Business Model Canvas, the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP), and the
Quantitative Strategic Planning Matrix (QSPM) to formulate business development
strategies across  various organizational settings, including catering businesses, coffee
shops, manufacturing firms, and small and medium -sized enterprises (Duratulhikmah &
Wijaya, 2024; Pradana & Hermawan, 2025; Pratama & Wandebori, 2024; Sumara, 2024;
Wahmil et al., 2025 ). These studies consistently demonstrate that systematic strategic
analysis can support organizational decision-making by identifying internal strengths and
weaknesses together with external opportunities and threats. Nevertheless, the majority
of these studies focus on conventional commercial contexts where functional,
operational, or economic considerations primarily determine customer value.
Furthermore, recent studies highlight the growing importance of digital capabilities,
organizational learning, customer -value management, and information systems in

strengthening business performance (Abrokwah-Larbi, 2024; Behl et al., 2022; Kusa et al.,
2024). Despite these advances, relatively limited attention has been devoted to explaining
how business performance improvement strategies should be formulated in rel igious
family food services, where organizational legitimacy, halal compliance, emotional value,
and customer experience simultaneously influence strategic competitiveness. More
importantly, previous SWOT -based studies generally present strategic recommend ations
as the final analytical outcome without clearly demonstrating how internal resources,
macro-environmental conditions, industry competition, and managerial judgment are
systematically integrated into an evidence -based strategy formulation process.
Consequently, the analytical traceability between organizational diagnosis, strategic
alternatives, and implementation priorities remains insufficiently developed.
To address these limitations, this study adopts an integrated strategic formulation
framework combining the Resource -Based View, PESTEL analysis, Porter's Five Forces,
SWOT analysis, TOWS strategy formulation, and management prioritization through a
structured focus group discussion. Rather than treating these analytical tools as
independent approaches, this study demonstrates how they collectively support
transparent and evidence -based strategic decision -making by linking organizational
resources, external  environmental conditions, competitive pressures, strategic
alternatives, and managerial evaluation within a single analytical framework.
This study makes two contributions to the strategic management literature. First, it
extends existing knowledge on business performance improvement by examining a trust -
intensive service context in which organizational resources, religious legitimacy, customer
experience, and competitive dynamics jointly shape strategic outcomes. Second, it
demonstrates how integrating multiple strategic analysis frameworks into a coherent
input–matching–decision process strengthens the analytical transparency of strategy
formulation by providing explicit connections between empirical evidence, strategic
diagnosis, managerial prioritization, and implementation recommendations. Accordingly,
this study aims to identify the internal and external factors influencing business
performance, formulate strategic alternatives through an integrated SWOT –TOWS
framework, and determine strategic implementation priorities within an Indonesian
religious family food service organization.

LITERATURE REVIEW
Business Performance as a Strategic Outcome
Business performance represents the extent to which an organization achieves its
strategic and operational objectives through sustainable value creation. Performance is
reflected not only in financial indicators such as revenue growth, profitability, and
operating margins but also in customer acquisition, service quality, operational efficiency,
and organizational competitiveness. From a strategic management perspective, business
performance should therefore be understood as the outcome of interactions betw een
organizational resources, managerial capabilities, and environmental conditions rather
than as a purely financial consequence (Barney, 1991; Wernerfelt, 1984).
The Resource -Based View (RBV) explains that firms achieve superior performance
because they possess heterogeneous resources and capabilities that competitors cannot
easily imitate (Barney, 1991; Wernerfelt, 1984). However, sustainable competitive
advantage depends not merely on resource ownership but also on the organization's
capability to organize, coordinate, and continuously reconfigure those resources in
response to environmental changes (Barney & Hesterly, 2015; Teece, 2007). Recent
studies likewise e mphasize that digital capabilities, customer -value analytics, and

organizational knowledge management increasingly determine how effectively firms
transform resources into competitive performance (Abrokwah -Larbi, 2024; Behl et al.,
2022; Kusa et al., 2024). Consequently, strategic analysis should simultaneously examine
organizational resources and the capabilities through which those resources create value.

External Environment and Strategic Diagnosis
Organizational resources operate within external environments that continuously shape
strategic opportunities and constraints. PESTEL analysis provides a systematic framework
for examining macro -environmental influences, including political, economic, soci al,
technological, environmental, and legal conditions affecting organizational performance
(Sammut-Bonnici & Galea, 2015). Complementing this macro perspective, Porter's Five
Forces explains how competitive rivalry, new market entrants, substitute product s,
supplier bargaining power, and buyer bargaining power influence industry attractiveness
and competitive behavior (Porter, 2008).
Although both frameworks are widely applied independently, their combined use
enables a more comprehensive understanding of external strategic conditions by
distinguishing broader environmental changes from industry -specific competitive
pressures. Such int egration reduces the risk of limiting external analysis solely to
competitor observation while recognizing the influence of regulations, technological
developments, customer purchasing behavior, and supply conditions on organizational
competitiveness.

Strategy Formulation through SWOT–TOWS
Strategic formulation requires translating organizational diagnosis into actionable
managerial decisions. SWOT analysis synthesizes internal strengths and weaknesses with
external opportunities and threats to provide an integrated strategic overview (Gurel  &
Tat, 2017). However, SWOT primarily functions as a diagnostic framework rather than a
decision-making tool. Accordingly, Weihrich (1982) proposed the TOWS matrix to
systematically transform SWOT findings into strategic alternatives by matching internal
organizational conditions with external environmental factors.
Within the strategic management framework proposed by David and David (2017),
organizational resources and external environmental analyses constitute the input stage
of strategic planning, while SWOT–TOWS represents the matching stage where evidence is
converted into feasible strategic alternatives. Decision making subsequently requires
managerial evaluation to determine implementation priorities based on organizational
relevance, feasibility, resource availability, and expected strategic impact. This
sequential process enhances analytical transparency by establishing explicit relationships
between empirical diagnosis and strategic recommendations.

Customer Experience and Trust in Religious Family Food Services
Business performance within service industries is increasingly influenced by customer
experiences accumulated across multiple organizational touchpoints. Rather than being
determined by isolated transactions, customer evaluations emerge throughout
interconnected stages including information search, consultation, purchasing, service
delivery, and post-purchase interaction (Homburg et al., 2017; Lemon & Verhoef, 2016).
Digital transformation has intensified this process by expanding customer access to
organizational information, online reviews, social media interactions, and digital
communication platforms, thereby requiring organizations to integrate technological
capabilities with operational processes and customer relationship management (Verhoef
et al., 2021).

Within religious family food services, customer experience extends beyond functional
service quality because purchasing decisions involve both credence attributes and
emotional value. Islamic branding research suggests that halal -conscious customers
evaluate organizational authenticity and credibility rather than relying solely on formal
certification or product characteristics (Wilson & Liu, 2011). Consequently, organizational
legitimacy, halal assurance, service reliability, family experience, and operati onal
convenience collectively shape customer trust and influence business performance. These
characteristics distinguish religious family food services from conventional food -service
industries by positioning trust and experiential value as complementary s ources of
competitive advantage.

Analytical Perspective
This study adopts an integrated strategic management perspective in which business
performance is viewed as the outcome of interactions among organizational resources,
external environmental conditions, competitive pressures, and managerial decision -
making. The Resource -Based View provides the analytical foundation for identifying
internal strengths and capability limitations, while PESTEL and Porter's Five Forces explain
macro-environmental influences and industry competition. SWOT analysis synthesiz es
these internal and external conditions into a coherent strategic diagnosis, whereas the
TOWS matrix systematically translates that diagnosis into alternative strategic responses.
Finally, management evaluation through a structured focus group discussion deter mines
implementation priorities by considering organizational relevance, urgency, feasibility,
resource readiness, and expected strategic impact.
Rather than functioning as independent analytical tools, these frameworks collectively
constitute an integrated input –matching–decision process that enables transparent,
evidence-based strategy formulation. Accordingly, the conceptual framework developed
in this study illustrates how organizational diagnosis is transformed into strategic priorities
capable of improving business performance within trust -intensive religious family food
services.

RESEARCH METHOD
This study employed a qualitative interpretive case study design to examine how
business performance improvement strategies can be formulated within a religious family
food service organization. A qualitative case study was considered appropriate because
the research sought to understand a contemporary strategic management problem within
its real -life organizational context, where organizational resources, competitive
pressures, and managerial decision -making interact dynamically (Creswell & Creswell,
2018; Yin, 2018). The unit of analysis was a single Indonesian religious family food service
organization that had experienced declining business performance despite possessing an
established market reputation and formal operational systems.
Purposive sampling was employed to obtain informants possessing direct knowledge of
organizational strategy, operational performance, customer management, financial
conditions, and industry dynamics. Six informants participated in the study, representing
branch coordination, branch operations, telesales, human resources and legal affairs,
finance, and an external industry association. Collectively, these participants provided
comprehensive functional perspectives regarding organizational resources, competit ive
conditions, operational challenges, and strategic priorities. Details of the participants are
presented in Table 1.

Primary data were collected through semi-structured interviews lasting approximately
45–60 minutes. Interview questions were designed to explore organizational performance,
internal resources and capabilities, external environmental conditions, competitive
pressures, customer behavior, operational constraints, and strategic responses. To
enhance contextual understanding, interview findings were complemented by company
documents, operational reports, sales and financial records, standard operating
procedures, legal documentation, and relevant secondary industry information. The use
of multiple data sources enabled methodological triangulation and strengthened the
credibility of the findings.
Data analysis followed an iterative qualitative procedure. Interview transcripts were
repeatedly reviewed to develop an initial understanding of the data before meaningful
statements were assigned preliminary codes. Similar codes were subsequently grouped
into broader categories corresponding to the Resource -Based View, PESTEL analysis, and
Porter's Five Forces. These analytical categories were then synthesized into organizational
strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats, providing the basis for SWOT analysis.
Finally, the identified SWOT factors were systematically matched through the TOWS
matrix to generate strategic alternatives. This analytical sequence follows the integrated
strategy formulation logic proposed by David and David (2017), in whic h organizational
diagnosis provides the foundation for strategic decision-making.
To maintain analytical rigor, only strategic factors satisfying four criteria were retained
for the SWOT matrix: direct relevance to business performance, recurrence across
interview participants or corroboration by documentary evidence, strategic importan ce
for organizational competitiveness, and conceptual distinctiveness from other factors.
Although several strategic alternatives addressed related organizational issues, all
alternatives were retained to preserve analytical traceability between empirical evidence
and strategic recommendations before being grouped into broader strategic themes.
At the prioritization stage, five members of the organization's management team
participated in a structured focus group discussion to evaluate the proposed TOWS
strategies. Each alternative was independently assessed according to five criteria:
strategic relevance, urgency, implementation feasibility, resource readiness, and
expected organizational impact. Each criterion was scored on a five -point scale ranging
from one (very low) to five (very high). Individual scores were subsequently aggregated to
establish implementation priorities, while discussion among participants was used to
interpret close ranking results and reach consensus where necessary.

Trustworthiness was established through source triangulation, methodological
triangulation, member confirmation, and the maintenance of a transparent chain of
evidence linking interview quotations, coding decisions, analytical categories, and final
strategic recommendations (Braun & Clarke, 2006). Documentary evidence was used to
verify factual organizational information whenever available, whereas interview data
were primarily employed to interpret organizational conditions and explain the strategic
meaning underlying observed performance patterns. This approach enhanced the
credibility, dependability, and transparency of the analytical process.

RESULTS
Business Performance Challenges and Internal Strategic Conditions
The findings indicate that the focal organization has experienced declining business
performance despite possessing substantial organizational resources accumulated over
more than two decades of operation. Documentary evidence and participant interviews
consistently reveal fluctuations in sales, revenue, customer acquisition, and operating
margins following a period of stronger performance. Rather than representing a temporary
marketing issue, participants described the decline as reflecting broader challen ges in
aligning organizational capabilities with increasingly competitive and digitally mediated
market conditions.
Participants consistently identified organizational trust as one of the company's most
valuable strategic resources. Brand reputation, accumulated operational experience,
customer referrals, positive service evaluations, halal compliance, legal certification, and
standardized operating procedures were repeatedly described as factors reducing
customer uncertainty when purchasing services associated with important family religious
occasions. These findings indicate that intangible organizational resources rem ain the
primary source of competitive differentiation within religious family food services.
However, the analysis also reveals that these valuable resources are not consistently
transformed into superior organizational performance. Interview participants described
uneven branch capabilities, inconsistent coordination among organizational units,
underutilized operational assets, limited employee capability development, and weak
communication of organizational value propositions as significant internal constraints.
Consequently, although the organization possesses strong reputational resources,
limitations in organizational capability reduce its ability to convert those assets into
customer acquisition, sales growth, and sustainable profitability.

External Environment and Competitive Pressures
The external analysis demonstrates that macro-environmental conditions and industry
competition simultaneously influence business performance . Economic factors emerged
as the most immediate source of strategic pressure, particularly declining purchasing
power, increasing customer price sensitivity, rising operating costs, and fluctuations in
livestock prices. Participants consistently reported that customers increasingly compare
competing providers before making purchasing decisions, intensifying competition beyond
traditional price considerations.
Social conditions, however, continue to create favorable market opportunities.
Participants emphasized that aqiqah remains an important religious and family tradition,
while younger Muslim families increasingly value practical, integrated, and emotionally
meaningful service experiences. This trend suggests that organizations capable of
combining religious legitimacy with convenience and service quality may strengthen their
competitive position without relying primarily on price competition.
Technological developments present both opportunities and challenges. Digital
communication platforms, customer relationship management systems, websites, and
online payment technologies provide opportunities to improve customer experience
throughout the purchasing journey. Nevertheless, participants acknowledged that digital
integration remains incomplete, limiting organizational responsiveness and reducing the
effectiveness of customer engagement.
Industry analysis further reveals intense competitive rivalry characterized by relatively
low entry barriers, strong buyer bargaining power, and numerous competing providers
offering comparable services. Although substitution at the product level remains relatively
limited because aqiqah follows specific religious requirements, substitution at the service
level remains possible through informal providers and self -managed alternatives. These
conditions collectively increase pressure on organizations to compe te through
differentiated value rather than price alone.

Prioritization of Strategic Alternatives
To determine implementation priorities, management participants evaluated each
strategic alternative using five assessment criteria: strategic relevance, urgency,
implementation feasibility, resource readiness, and expected organizational impact.
Aggregate scores indicate relatively small differences among several alternatives,
suggesting that participants considered multiple strategies important for improving
organizational performance. Consequently, the evaluation process established an
implementation sequence rather than identifying mutually exclusive strategic choices.
The highest -ranked strategy emphasizes strengthening response speed, lead
management, follow-up discipline, and after-sales coordination throughout the customer
journey. Participants considered this strategy the most immediate response to increasing
customer comparison behavior and competitive pressure because delayed responses
frequently resulted in lost business opportunities.
The second -ranked strategy focuses on integrating digital customer touchpoints,
including websites, social media, customer relationship management systems,
communication platforms, payment processes, and order management. Participants
viewed digital integr ation as essential for improving customer convenience and
strengthening organizational competitiveness.
The third -ranked strategy emphasizes improving marketing communication by
strengthening the organization's capability to communicate religious legitimacy, service
quality, convenience, and customer value more effectively to younger Muslim families.
Management considered this strategy essential because existing organizational strengths
remain insufficiently communicated throughout the customer decision-making process.

DISCUSSION
Business Performance as the Outcome of Organizational Resources and Capabilities
The findings demonstrate that business performance within the focal organization is
influenced not only by the possession of valuable organizational resources but also by the
organization's capability to deploy those resources effectively in increasingly competitive
market conditions. Although participants consistently identified brand reputation,
operational experience, halal certification, standardized operating procedures, and
positive customer evaluations as major organizational strengths, these resources have not
been fully translated into sustainable performance improvement because of limitations in
coordination, digital integration, and organizational capability.  These findings are
consistent with the Resource -Based View proposed by Barney (1991), which argues that
valuable organizational resources constitute the foundation of competitive advantage only
when they are effectively organized and strategically utilized. Similarly, Wernerfelt (1984)
emphasized that competitive superiority derives from the s trategic deployment of
resources rather than their mere possession. The present findings therefore extend this
perspective by demonstrating that, within religious family food services, intangible
resources such as institutional trust, religious legitimacy,  and accumulated service
reputation represent strategic assets whose value depends heavily on managerial
capability and organizational integration.
The results also support the argument of Teece (2007) that dynamic capabilities
determine an organization's capacity to adapt its resources to environmental change.
Participants repeatedly described inconsistencies in branch coordination, underutilized
operational assets, and uneven employee capabilities as barriers preventing valuable
organizational resources from generating superior business performance. Consequently,
performance improvement requires strengthening organizational capabilities alongside

preserving existing strategic assets. These findings reinforce recent studies suggesting that
organizational performance increasingly depends on the interaction between valuable
resources and managerial capability rather than resource ownership alone (Abrokwah -
Larbi, 2024; Behl et al., 2022; K usa et al., 2024). In the context of religious family food
services, customer trust emerges as a strategic resource whose effectiveness depends on
organizational responsiveness, operational consistency, and service quality throughout the
customer journey.

Environmental Dynamics Require Value-Based Rather than Price-Based Competition
The external analysis indicates that business performance is increasingly shaped by
economic uncertainty, technological transformation, changing customer expectations,
and intensifying market competition. Participants consistently described increasing
customer price sensitivity and stronger competitive rivalry while simultaneously
recognizing growing opportunities created by digital technologies and the continued social
importance of aqiqah services.  These findings correspond with Porter's competitive
strategy framework, which suggests that sustainable competitive advantage cannot be
maintained through price competition alone when buyers possess strong bargaining power
and market rivalry is intense. Instead, organizations must differentiate themselves
through superior value propositions that competitors cannot easily imitate. Within the
present study, participants emphasized trust, halal assurance, service quality, and
customer convenience as the primary dimensions supporting organizational
differentiation.
The findings similarly align with previous studies demonstrating that digital
transformation has fundamentally altered customer expectations regarding
responsiveness, convenience, and purchasing experience (Homburg et al., 2017; Lemon &
Verhoef, 2016; Verh oef et al., 2021). Rather than replacing traditional service quality,
digital interaction increasingly complements organizational trust by facilitating faster
communication, transparent information exchange, and more efficient customer
relationship managem ent. Furthermore, the persistence of aqiqah as an important
religious and family practice indicates that market demand remains relatively resilient
despite increasing economic pressure. Consistent with Wilson and Liu (2011), religious
legitimacy continues to in fluence consumer decision -making within Islamic service
industries. Consequently, organizations capable of combining religious credibility with
operational convenience are better positioned to sustain competitive advantage than
organizations relying primarily on pricing strategies.

Integrating SWOT–TOWS into Strategic Decision-Making
One important contribution of this study lies in demonstrating how multiple strategic
analysis tools can be integrated into a coherent decision -making process. Rather than
employing SWOT solely as a descriptive classification technique, the present study
combines Resource -Based View analysis, PESTEL, Porter's Five Forces, SWOT, and the
TOWS matrix to establish a systematic sequence from empirical diagnosis to strategic
formulation. This integrated analytical process addresses a limitation observed in previous
strategic management studies, where SWOT frequently concludes with descriptive
strategic recommendations without clearly explaining how empirical evidence informs
strategic priorities. By incorporating internal organizational resources, external
environmental conditions, and managerial evaluation into a unified framework, the
present study provides greater transparency regarding the logical relationship between
empirical findings and strategic recommendations.

Practical Implications for Religious Family Food Service Organizations
The prioritization process provides practical evidence regarding managerial
preferences under conditions of increasing environmental uncertainty. Management
consistently assigned the highest priority to strategies associated with customer
responsiveness, d igital customer journey integration, and communication of
organizational trust, while physical resource expansion received comparatively lower
priority. These priorities suggest that business performance improvement is increasingly
viewed as a function of organizational capability rather than physical capacity. Faster
response times, integrated digital communication, structured customer relationship
management, and consistent service delivery were considered more influential than
expansion of operational assets alone.
For organizations operating within trust -intensive religious service industries, these
findings indicate that sustainable competitive advantage depends on combining
institutional credibility with organizational agility. Investments in digital capability,
employee competence, customer experience management, and operational coordination
therefore represent strategic priorities that reinforce existing organizational resources
while improving long-term organizational resilience. Overall, the study demonstrates that
organizational resources remain valuable only when supported by adaptive managerial
capabilities capable of responding to changing customer expectations and competitive
dynamics. This conclusion reinforces the strategic relevance of integrating intern al
capability development with customer -centered value creation to improve business
performance.

THEORETICAL CONTRIBUTIONS
This study offers several theoretical contributions to the strategic management and
service management literature by extending the application of established strategic
frameworks within the context of religious family food services, a setting that has received
relatively limited scholarly attention despite its growing economic and social significance.
First, the findings reinforce the central proposition of the Resource-Based View (RBV) that
valuable organizational resources alone are insufficient to genera te superior business
performance. Although the focal organization possesses strategic assets such as a well -
established reputation, halal certification, standardized operating procedures, and
accumulated operational experience, these resources do not autom atically produce
competitive advantage. Instead, the findings demonstrate that their strategic value
depends on the organization's capability to coordinate internal functions, integrate digital
technologies, and consistently deliver high-quality customer experiences. This observation
provides additional empirical support for the complementary relationship between
valuable resources and organizational capabilities proposed by Barney (1991) and Teece
(2007).

Second, this study contributes to strategic management literature by illustrating how
internal and external strategic analyses can be integrated into a coherent analytical
process rather than being applied as isolated managerial tools. Previous studies frequently
employ Resource -Based View analysis, PESTEL, Porter's Five Forces, SWOT, or TOWS
independently, often resulting in fragmented strategic recommendations. In contrast, the
present study demonstrates an integrated analytical sequence in which internal
organizational resources, external environmental conditions, and managerial evaluations
collectively inform the formulation and prioritization of strategic alternatives. This
sequential approach enhances analytical transparency by explicitly linking empir ical
evidence to strategic decision-making.
Third, the findings broaden current understanding of competitive advantage within
trust-intensive service industries. While traditional strategic management research often
emphasizes tangible resources or cost efficiency as primary drivers of organizationa l
performance, the present study demonstrates that intangible organizational resources —
including institutional trust, religious legitimacy, service consistency, and customer
experience—function as critical strategic assets. These findings suggest that
organizational credibility should be understood not merely as a marketing outcome but as
a strategic capability that supports long -term competitiveness in service organizations
where purchasing decisions involve significant emotional, social, and religious
considerations.
This study extends existing discussions on business performance improvement by
highlighting the growing strategic importance of customer -centered organizational
capabilities. Rather than prioritizing physical expansion or price -based competition, the
findings indicate that digital integration, rapid customer responsiveness, structured
customer relationship management, and effective communication of organizational value
represent the primary mechanisms through which strategic resources are transformed into
superior organizational performance. Consequently, this study contributes to strategic
management literature by demonstrating that sustainable competitive advantage
increasingly depends on an organization's ability to integrate valuable resources, adaptive
capabilities, and customer-oriented value creation within a unified strategic framework.

PRACTICAL IMPLICATIONS
The findings of this study provide several practical implications for managers operating
in religious family food service organizations. First, the results suggest that improving
business performance should not rely solely on expanding physical resources or increasing
promotional activities. Instead, managers should prioritize strengthening organizational
capabilities that enable existing strategic resources to be utilized more effectively.
Investments in employee competency development, cross -functional co ordination,
standardized operational practices, and digital integration are likely to produce greater
long-term organizational benefits than resource expansion alone.
Second, the prioritization of strategic alternatives demonstrates that customer
responsiveness has become a critical determinant of organizational competitiveness.
Management should therefore establish structured customer relationship management
practices that ensure rapid response to customer inquiries, systematic lead follow -up,
effective after -sales communication, and continuous customer engagement. Such
initiatives can improve conversion rates while reinforcing customer trust throughout the
purchasing process.
Third, the study highlights the importance of digital transformation beyond the simple
adoption of online marketing channels. Organizations should integrate websites, customer

systems, and order management processes into a unified customer journey. This
integrated approach enables customers to experience greater convenience while allowing
organizations to improve operational efficiency and managerial decision -making through
more consistent information flows.
Fourth, the findings indicate that trust represents one of the most valuable strategic
assets within religious family food service organizations. Managers should therefore
communicate organizational credibility through consistent service quality, halal
compliance, transparent business practices, customer testimonials, and standardized
service delivery. Strengthening these dimensions can enhance customer confidence and
reduce reliance on price-based competition in increasingly crowded markets.
The findings also provide practical implications for industry practitioners beyond the
focal organization. Service providers operating in trust-intensive sectors may benefit from
adopting integrated strategic planning processes that simultaneously evaluate  internal
organizational capabilities and external environmental conditions before determining
strategic priorities. Rather than implementing isolated improvement initiatives,
organizations are encouraged to align resource development, digital capability, customer
experience management, and operational excellence within a unified strategic
framework.
This study offers useful insights for policymakers and business development institutions
responsible for supporting small and medium -sized enterprises in religious service
industries. Capacity -building programs should extend beyond financial assistance by
emphasizing managerial capability development, digital transformation, quality
assurance, halal governance, and customer relationship management. Such initiatives may
strengthen organizational competitiveness while supporting the long -term sustainability
of religious service enterprises operating in increasingly dynamic market environments.

CONCLUSION
This study examined how business performance improvement strategies can be
formulated within a religious family food service organization by integrating the Resource-
Based View, PESTEL analysis, Porter's Five Forces, SWOT analysis, the TOWS matrix, and
management evaluation into a unified strategic decision -making process. Using a
qualitative case study approach, the research identified the internal organizational
resources, external environmental conditions, strategic alternatives, and implementation
priorities that collectively influence organizational performance.
The findings indicate that the organization possesses valuable strategic resources,
including institutional reputation, halal compliance, standardized operating procedures,
accumulated operational experience, and customer trust. However, these resources alone
are insufficient to sustain superior business performance without corresponding
organizational capabilities. Limitations in coordination, digital integration, operational
consistency, and capability development reduce the organization's ability to tran sform
valuable resources into sustained competitive advantage. At the same time, changing
customer expectations, increasing digitalization, and intensifying market competition
require organizations to compete through customer value, organizational agility,  and
service quality rather than through price alone.
The study further demonstrates that integrating multiple strategic management
frameworks provides a transparent and systematic basis for strategic decision -making.
Rather than producing isolated recommendations, the sequential integration of internal
analysis, external analysis, SWOT –TOWS formulation, and managerial prioritization

enables strategic alternatives to be developed directly from empirical evidence. The
prioritization process further indicates that strengthening customer responsiveness,
digital integration, organizational capability, and trust -based value communication
represents the most effective pathway for improving business performance within trust -
intensive service industries.
Overall, this study contributes to strategic management literature by demonstrating
how valuable organizational resources, adaptive capabilities, and customer -centered
value creation interact to support sustainable business performance. The findings also
provide a practical strategic framework that may assist managers and decision-makers in
designing evidence -based performance improvement strategies within religious service
organizations and other trust -intensive service sectors operating in increasingly dy namic
competitive environments.

Limitations And Future Research
This study should be interpreted in light of several limitations that also provide
opportunities for future research. First, the study employed a qualitative single-case study
design, enabling an in -depth understanding of strategic decision -making within o ne
religious family food service organization. While this approach generated rich contextual
insights, the findings are analytically rather than statistically generalizable. Future studies
may adopt multiple -case designs involving organizations of differen t sizes, ownership
structures, or geographical locations to examine the transferability of the proposed
strategic framework across diverse organizational contexts.
Second, the present study focused primarily on managerial perspectives regarding
organizational resources, environmental conditions, and strategic priorities. Although
these perspectives are essential for strategic planning, future research could incorporate
additional stakeholder groups, including customers, suppliers, strategic partners, and
employees, to develop a more comprehensive understanding of how strategic resources
and competitive advantage are perceived across the organizational ecosystem. Third, the
strategic priorities identified in this study reflect organizational conditions at the time
the research was conducted. Because competitive environments, customer expectations,
and digital technologies continue to evolve, future studies may employ lo ngitudinal
research designs to investigate how strategic priorities change over time and how
organizations continuously adapt their capabilities in response to environmental
dynamics.
The integrated analytical framework developed in this study was examined within the
context of a trust -intensive religious family food service organization. Future research
may extend this framework to other service industries where customer trust,
organizational credibility, and service experience play similarly important roles, such as
healthcare services, educational institutions, Islamic financial services, hospitality, and
other faith -based or experience -oriented service organizations. Comparative stud ies
across different service sectors may further refine the theoretical understanding of how
organizational resources, environmental conditions, and customer -centered capabilities
interact to influence sustainable business performance.
