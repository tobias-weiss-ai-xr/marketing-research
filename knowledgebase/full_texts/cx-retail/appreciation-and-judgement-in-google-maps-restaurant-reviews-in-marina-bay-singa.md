 Online restaurant reviews have become an important source of information in
shaping customer perceptions and influencing dining decisions in digital
platforms such as Google Maps. However, there is still limited linguistic
analysis focusing on how evaluati ve meanings are constructed within these
reviews, particularly in terms of Appreciation and Judgement. This study aims
to analyze how Appreciation and Judgement are realized in Google Maps
restaurant reviews in the Marina Bay Area, Singapore. This research employs a
qualitative descriptive design using qualitative content analysis guided by
Systemic Functional Linguistics, specifically the Appraisal Theory proposed by
Martin and White (2005), to interpret evaluative meanings in naturally
occurring restaurant reviews. The data consist of 16 selected restaurant reviews
collected from Google Maps and analyzed based on attitudinal resources. The
findings show that Appreciation is predominantly used to evaluate food quality,
ambience, and overall dining experience, while J udgement is mainly directed
toward staff behavior and service performance. Positive evaluations are more
frequently expressed compared to negative ones, indicating generally favorable
customer experiences. The findings contribute to the application of Appraisal
Theory in digital tourism discourse by demonstrating how evaluative language
systematically constructs customer perceptions of food, service, and dining
experiences in location-specific restaurant reviews.

1. Introduction

The rapid development of digital technology has significantly transformed how people access
and share information in everyday life. Digital platforms are no longer limited to communication
tools but have become spaces where users actively exchange experiences and opinions. Saridah et
al. (2024) explain that digital platforms enable users to interact and share information easily
through mobile devices, encouraging the increasing use of online platforms as sources of
information, including in the contexts of food and tourism. One of the most widely used platforms
is Google Maps, which has evolved into a global digital platform that facilitates social and
economic interactions (McQuire, 2019). Beyond  its navigation purposes, Google Maps enables
users to share personal experiences through ratings and written feedback, making it a significant
source of user-generated content, particularly restaurant reviews. The global tourism industry has
simultaneously undergone rapid transformation driven by technological advancements and socio-
cultural shifts, with digital innovations such as smart tourism platforms significantly redefining
tourist behavior and destination management (Ahmed, 2025). In this context, onlin e restaurant

reviews on platforms such as Google Maps have emerged as one of the most prominent forms of
digital consumer expression in tourism destinations worldwide.
Online consumer reviews refer to evaluations provided by customers based on their personal
experiences with products or services (Alzate et al., 2022). According to Amalia et al. (2021), such
reviews function as a medium for expressing consumer opinions wh ile simultaneously providing
feedback for service improvement. In the restaurant industry, online reviews play an important
role in influencing customer decision -making and serve as rich data sources for understanding
customer experiences (Mathayomchan & T aecharungroj, 2020). Written comments are
particularly valuable because they provide deeper insights than numerical ratings alone (Abdullah
et al., 2024). As a form of electronic word of mouth (eWOM), restaurant reviews are closely linked
to consumer behavior in the hospitality sector (Mukhopadhyay et al., 2023).
From a linguistic perspective, restaurant reviews are inherently evaluative rather than purely
descriptive. Halliday and Matthiessen (2014) state that language is a resource for making meaning
in context Systemic Functional Linguistics (SFL) explains language through three metafunctions
of language: ideational, interpersonal, and textual (Banks, 2024), with the interpersonal
metafunction focusing on the expression of attitudes, evaluations, and interperson al meanings.
Young and Harrison (2004) further emphasize that the relationship between Systemic Functional
Linguistics and Critical Discourse Analysis is particularly useful for revealing how language
reflects and contributes to social change across different discourse settings.Within this framework,
Appraisal Theory (Martin & White, 2005) provides a systematic approach to analyzing evaluative
meanings through three subsystems: Attitude, Engagement, and Graduation. Sun (2024) further
argues that the Appraisal system functions not only at the level of individual evaluative expressions
but also as a textual mechanism that organizes evaluative meaning coherently across an entire text,
involving contextual, discourse -semantic, and lexico -grammatical levels. The Attitude system is
further divided into Affect, Judgement, and Appreciation. Judgement evaluates human behavior
based on social norms, while Appreciation evaluates objects, products, and experiences based on
aesthetic and evaluative value (Liu et al., 2022). This study specifically focuses on Judgement and
Appreciation rather than Affect or Engagement because restaurant review discourse is
fundamentally oriented toward evaluating two primary dimensions: the behavior and performance
of service staff (Jud gement) and the quality of food, ambience, and overall dining experience
(Appreciation). Affect, which concerns personal emotional reactions, and Engagement, which
deals with dialogic positioning, are less central to the evaluative genre of restaurant reviews.
Appraisal Theory has been widely applied across various discourse contexts; for instance,
Aljuraywi and Alyousef (2022) employed Martin and White's (2005) framework to examine
evaluative language in political discourse, analyzing the three subsystems of At titude including
Affect, Judgement, and Appreciation in political interviews, demonstrating that each subsystem
realizes distinct argumentative and ideological functions. Similarly, Mirzaaghabeyk (2022) applied
Appraisal Theory to news texts and found that  Judgement emerged as the most dominant
subsystem, followed by Affect and Appreciation, indicating that written texts across different
genres consistently foreground evaluative meaning through attitude realization.  Haryudin,
Gunawan, and Musthafa (2025) further demonstrate that the Appraisal system is not limited to
written discourse, showing that Affect, Judgement, and Appreciation are also realized in spoken
classroom interactions between teachers and students, wh ich contributes to the construction of
shared knowledge.
Specifically within the context of restaurant reviews, however, significant research gaps
remain despite the broad application of Appraisal Theory described above.  Chaves et al. (2014)
analyzed 1,100 restaurant reviews in Portugal using Appraisal Theory and found that Appreciation
was the most dominant category; however, their study relied on non -digital, offline data and did
not involve Google Maps as a platform, n or did it situate the analysis within a Southeast Asian
tourism context. Shin et al. (2022) analyzed Google Maps restaurant reviews using computational
methods such as TF -IDF and machine learning; however, their study focused solely on
quantitative sentiment classification without applying any discourse -based linguistic framework
such as Appraisal Theory. The contrast between these two studies reveals a clear and persistent
gap: existing research either applies Appraisal Theory without using Google Maps dat a, or uses

Google Maps data without applying Appraisal Theory. No previous study has integrated both
approaches within a geographically specific and internationally recognized tourism destination.
Furthermore, discourse analysis has been acknowledged as a powerful methodological approach
to uncovering textual meaning and evaluative constructions across various social and educational
contexts (Awaludin et al., 2025). Therefore, this study is novel because it simultaneously combines
Appraisal Theory as the linguistic framework, Google Maps as the source of user -generated

tourism setting, which is a combination that has not been examined in any previous research.
Fundamentally, online restaurant reviews represent not only personal opinions but also
evaluative discourse through which consumers construct meanings about service quality, food,
and social interaction. This study focuses on the Marina Bay Area, Singapore , for two main
reasons. First, Marina Bay Sands, an integrated tourism and hospitality complex covering
approximately 570,000 square meters, is one of Singapore's most iconic landmarks (Szolomicki &
Golasz-Szolomicka, 2020). The area consistently attracts a massive proportion of international
visitors. In 2023, 48% of all international visitors to Singapore visited the Integrated Resorts
including Marina Bay Sands, making it the most visited paid tourist attraction in the country
(Singapore Tourism Board, 2024). This figure is further supported by Singapore's record-breaking
tourism performance in 2024, with 16.5 million international visitor arrivals representing a 21%
increase from 2023, and total tourism receipts reaching an all-time high (Singapore Tourism Board,
2025). These statistics establish Marina Bay as an exceptionally active and globally significant site
of tourism consumption. Second, the area receives visitors from diverse cultural and linguistic
backgrounds, with top source markets including China, Indonesia, India, Malaysia, and Australia
(Singapore Tourism Board, 2025), which makes it a highly relevant and representative context for
examining how evaluative meanings are constructed across different user perspectives in online
restaurant reviews. A preliminary observation of Google Maps restaurant reviews in the Marina
Bay Area was conducted prior to this study to support this justification. From an initial scan of 50
reviews across five restaurants in the Marina Bay precinct, it was found that the majority of reviews
contained simultaneous evaluations of both food quality and service performance within a single
written text. Expressions related to food quality and ambience such as "amazing dining
experience," "absolutely delicious food," and "beautiful atmosphere" frequently co-occurred with
evaluations of staff behavior such as "excellent service," "friendly staff," and "attentive and
professional." This co -occurrence pattern indicates that Appreciation and Judgement are not
produced in isolation but are consistently realized together within the same review, reflecting the
dual evaluative nature of restaurant discourse. These observations provide the empirical basis for
the analytical focus of this study.
This study is considered urgent due to the increasing reliance on user -generated restaurant
reviews in tourism decision-making, particularly in globally recognized destinations. Despite this
trend, limited research has examined how evaluative meanings are constructed linguistically in
such reviews using Appraisal Theory within specific tourism contexts. This study contributes
theoretically by extending the application of Appraisal Theory to digital tourism discourse,
specifically to Google Maps restaurant reviews, filling the gap left by previous studies that treated
linguistic analysis and platform-based data separately. Practically, this study provides insights for
restaurant owners, hospitality managers, and tourism stakeholders in understanding how
international customers linguistically construct their evaluations of service quality and dining
experiences, which can inform service improvement strategies and destination branding.
This study aims to analyze how Judgement and Appreciation are realized in Google Maps
restaurant reviews in the Marina Bay Area, Singapore, and to identify their dominant types. The
findings are expected to contribute to the development of Appraisal Theory  studies in digital
discourse and provide empirical evidence of evaluative language use in location-specific restaurant
reviews within the hospitality and tourism context.

2. Methods

2.1 Research Design

 This research utilizes a qualitative descriptive method through a discourse analysis lens to
explore how Judgement and Appreciation are expressed in restaurant evaluations found on
Google Maps. The goal of this method is to systematically describe and inte rpret evaluative
language found within naturally occurring examples.
 The theoretical basis for this research is Systemic Functional Linguistics (SFL), which
highlights the connection between linguistic structures and their social implications. As stated by
Halliday and Matthiessen (2014), an analysis of language should take into account its functionality
in social environments. Consistent with this viewpoint, Appraisal Theory is employed as the
primary framework for analysis to recognize and classify evaluative statements, especially those
pertaining to Judgement and Appreciation.
 A descriptive qualitative approach is considered appropriate because this study aims to
provide a systematic description and interpretation of evaluative language in its natural context
rather than to test hypotheses. Through this approach, the researcher s are able to explore how
linguistic resources of Judgement and Appreciation are realized in Google Maps restaurant
reviews and how these evaluative meanings contribute to the construction of customer
experiences.

2.2  Data Collection
2.2.1 Instrument of Collecting Data
This study employed document analysis as the primary research instrument. The data source
consisted of user-generated restaurant reviews collected from Google Maps. These reviews served
as the primary data because they contain evaluative expressions reflecting customers’ experiences.
To analyze the data, this study applied the Appraisal Framework proposed by Martin and
White (2005) as the analytical framework to identify and classify the use of Appreciation and
Judgement. The analysis involved selecting relevant reviews, identifying ev aluative expressions,
and categorizing them according to the Appraisal system. Through this framework, the study
examined how evaluative meanings are constructed in restaurant reviews.

2.2.2 Techniques for Collecting Data
The data used in this study consist of textual restaurant reviews containing evaluative
expressions categorized as Appreciation and Judgement based on the Appraisal Theory proposed
by Martin and White (2005). The data were obtained from Google Maps, focusi ng on restaurant
reviews of restaurants located in the Marina Bay Area, Singapore.
This study employed purposive sampling by selecting reviews that explicitly contain
evaluative language related to food, ambience, and service. The data collection process involved
documenting and extracting selected reviews from Google Maps to maintain th e originality and
authenticity of the data source.
During the selection process, the researcher identified evaluative expressions and classified
them based on the Appraisal categories. Appreciation was analyzed through reaction,
composition, and valuation, while Judgement was examined through normality, ca pacity,
tenacity, and propriety. The selected data were then prepared for further analysis to distinguish
evaluations of things from evaluations of human behavior.

2.3 Data Analysis
The data analysis in this study is conducted using qualitative content analysis guided by the
Appraisal Framework proposed by Martin and White (2005). The analysis is carried out
systematically through several detailed stages to ensure that evaluative mean ings in the data are
accurately identified, classified, and interpreted.
First, all collected restaurant reviews from Google Maps in the Marina Bay Area are read
repeatedly to obtain a comprehensive understanding of the context and overall meaning of each
review. This step is conducted to familiarize the researcher with the dat a and to identify initial
patterns of evaluative language.
Second, the reviews are segmented into meaningful clauses or phrases. Each clause is
examined to identify lexical items that indicate evaluation, particularly adjectives, adverbs, and

evaluative verbs such as delicious, amazing, excellent, friendly, rude, and worth it. These lexical
items are highlighted and extracted as units of analysis.
Third, the identified evaluative expressions are coded and classified into the Attitude system
of Appraisal Theory, specifically into Judgement and Appreciation. Judgement is used when the
evaluation refers to human behavior, character, or service performa nce (e.g., staff friendliness,
attentiveness, professionalism), while Appreciation is applied when the evaluation refers to
objects, food, ambience, or overall dining experience. The classification is carefully determined
based on contextual meaning within each review to avoid misinterpretation.
Fourth, each category is further analyzed into its subtypes. Appreciation is categorized into
Reaction, Composition, and Valuation, while Judgement is categorized into Normality, Capacity,
Tenacity, and Propriety. Each evaluative expression is assigned to a specific subtype based on its
linguistic realization and contextual meaning in the review.
Fifth, the coded data are organized into tables to facilitate comparison and to identify patterns
of frequency and dominance between Judgement and Appreciation. This step allows the researcher
to systematically observe which types of evaluation appear most frequently in the dataset.
Finally, the findings are interpreted by relating the classified data to the theoretical framework
of Appraisal Theory. The interpretation focuses on how evaluative meanings are constructed in
restaurant reviews and how these meanings reflect customers’ perceptions of food quality, service
performance, and dining experience in the Marina Bay Area. All analytical procedures are
conducted to ensure consistency, transparency, and alignment with the research objectives.

3. Results

An analysis of Google Maps restaurant reviews in the Marina Bay Area shows that reviewers
frequently express their evaluations through Judgement and Appreciation. The data consist of
evaluative expressions identified based on Appraisal Theory proposed by M artin and White
(2005). These expressions reflect how reviewers assess aspects such as service, food, and ambience.
For clarity, the findings are organized into two main categories: Appreciation and Judgement.

3.1 The Realization of Appreciation in Google Maps Restaurant Reviews
Appreciation in this study focuses on the evaluation of things, including food, ambience, and
overall dining experience. The findings indicate that reviewers commonly express Appreciation
through descriptive language that reflects their impressions and fee lings toward the restaurant.
These expressions are categorized into reaction, composition, and valuation. In general,
Appreciation is used to highlight the quality of food, the atmosphere, and the overall experience
perceived by the reviewers.

3.2 The Realization of Judgement in Google maps Restaurant Reviews
Judgement in this study is used to evaluate human behavior, particularly the performance

The findings of this study demonstrate that evaluative language in Google Maps restaurant
reviews in the Marina Bay Area is consistently realized through the Attitude system of Appraisal
Theory, particularly Appreciation and Judgement. Across the dataset, reviewers repeatedly
construct their evaluations by separating experiential assessment of restaurants (food, ambience,
and overall experience) and interpersonal assessment of service staff behavior. This pattern
confirms that evaluative meaning in digital restaurant reviews is systematically organized and not
random, but shaped by specific linguistic resources within Appraisal Theory (Martin & White,
2005).
In the Appreciation system, the data show a consistent dominance of Reaction and Valuation.
Expressions such as amazing dining experience, absolutely delicious, stunning rooftop view,
beautiful views, absolute pleasure, and delicious (Data 1, 2, 5, 6, 7, and 10) are repeatedly used to
express reviewers’ sensory, emotional, and aesthetic responses toward food, ambience, and overall
dining experiences. Meanwhile, Valuation is realized through expressions such as worth the
money, must -have experience, memorabl e dining experience, thoughtfully executed, and
definitely worth to visit, which evaluate the significance, quality, and perceived value of the dining
experience. In addition, Composition appears through expressions such as ambience, innovative,
and artistic, which evaluate the arrangement, presentation, and aesthetic qualities of the restaurant
environment and food. This pattern indicates that reviewers tend to evaluate restaurants not only
through direct sensory reactions but also through judgments of exp eriential value and aesthetic
quality. This finding supports Martin and Rose (2007), who explain that Appreciation functions
to evaluate objects, products, and experiences according to their perceived quality, composition,
and value.
In contrast, Judgement is consistently realized through Capacity and Propriety across the
dataset. Reviews in Data 1 –13 frequently evaluate staff performance using expressions such as
excellent service, superb service, attentive staff, and very bad custome r service, which reflect
Capacity. At the same time, Propriety is realized through expressions such as friendly staff, warm
service, rude staff, and disinterested staff, which assess interpersonal behavior and attitude. These
findings show that customers consistently evaluate both competence and behavior of service staff
as key components of their dining experience. According to Martin and White (2005), Judgement
is concerned with evaluating human behavior based on social norms, which is clearly reflected in
the dataset.
A key and consistent finding in this study is that Judgement appears more frequently and with
stronger evaluative intensity than Appreciation. This indicates that interpersonal service
experience plays a more dominant role in shaping customer evaluations t han food or ambience
alone. Even when Appreciation is positive, negative Judgement tends to strongly influence the
overall tone of the review. This pattern suggests that service interaction is a critical determinant of
customer satisfaction in the Marina Bay restaurant context.
Another consistent pattern in the data is the co -occurrence of Appreciation and Judgement
within single reviews. For example, in Data 1, 4, 6, and 10, reviewers simultaneously evaluate
food and ambience (Appreciation) while also evaluating staff performanc e (Judgement). This
supports the view that restaurant reviews function as multidimensional evaluative texts, where
users integrate both experiential and interpersonal evaluations in a single discourse. This aligns
with Thomas and Kristina (2024), who found that Appreciation and Judgement often co-occur in
online review discourse to construct a complete evaluative meaning.
Furthermore, the dominance of Judgement in this study may be influenced by the tourism -
oriented context of Marina Bay, Singapore. As an internationally recognized destination, service
expectations in this area are high, leading reviewers to pay greater att ention to staff behavior,
responsiveness, and professionalism. This explains why evaluative expressions related to
friendliness, attentiveness, and service quality are consistently prominent across the dataset.
The findings also reflect the function of online reviews as electronic word of mouth (eWOM),
where evaluative language is used not only to describe experiences but also to influence potential
consumers. Across the dataset, positive expressions such as must  try, highly recommended, and
worth to visit, as well as negative expressions such as worst service ever and horrible ambience,
demonstrate the persuasive function of review writing. According to Haro-Sosa et al. (2024), such

evaluations are strongly influenced by actual customer experiences, which is clearly reflected in
the present data.
From a broader linguistic perspective, the findings support the view that language carries
social meaning. As stated by Halliday and Matthiessen (2014), language functions as a resource
for meaning -making in social context. In this study, evaluative expres sions such as friendly,
attentive, rude, and professional do not only describe service quality but also construct social
judgments about acceptable behavior in hospitality contexts. This demonstrates how linguistic
choices reflect broader social values and expectations (Pride, 1971).
Overall, the findings confirm that evaluative language in Marina Bay restaurant reviews is
systematically structured through Appreciation and Judgement, with clear dominance of
experiential evaluation (Reaction and Valuation) and interpersonal evaluation ( Capacity and
Propriety). As Martin and Rose (2007) note, evaluative meanings accumulate across discourse,
and this pattern is clearly evident in how reviewers construct their overall judgments through
multiple evaluative expressions in a single review.

5. Conclusions

This study concludes that evaluative language in Google Maps restaurant reviews in the
Marina Bay Area, Singapore, is consistently realized through the Attitude system of Appraisal
Theory, particularly Appreciation and Judgement (Martin & White, 2005). The findings show that
reviewers systematically use evaluative lexical items to express their perceptions of food quality,
service performance, and restaurant ambience.
The results indicate that Appreciation is mainly used to evaluate food, atmosphere, and
overall dining experience through Reaction and Valuation, as reflected in expressions such as
delicious, amazing experience, stunning view, and worth the money. Meanwhi le, Judgement is
used to evaluate staff behavior and service performance through Capacity and Propriety, as shown
in expressions such as excellent service, friendly staff, attentive staff, and rude service. These
findings demonstrate that restaurant review s construct evaluative meanings through both
experiential and interpersonal dimensions.
Furthermore, the findings reveal that Judgement is slightly more dominant than Appreciation
in the dataset. This suggests that evaluations of service performance and staff interaction represent
prominent aspects of restaurant review discourse, while food a nd ambience remain important
elements in constructing customer experiences. The co-occurrence of Appreciation and Judgement
also shows that reviewers evaluate restaurants through a combination of tangible experiences and
human interactions.
Overall, this study contributes to the application of Appraisal Theory in digital tourism
discourse by demonstrating how evaluative meanings are constructed in location-specific Google
Maps restaurant reviews. However, this study is limited to reviews from the Marina Bay Area and
focuses only on Appreciation and Judgement within the Attitude system. Future research may
explore other appraisal resources or different digital review platforms to provide broader insights
into online evaluative discourse.
