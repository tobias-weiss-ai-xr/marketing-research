Abstract: Artificial Intelligence (AI) is playing an increasingly important
role in transforming the banking sector. AI is being leveraged in the
banking industry to enable robotic process automation, enhance
customer experience, offer faster response times, and digitize banking
services. One of the most prominent applications of AI in the banking
sector is chatbots. Chatbots are software platforms that offer virtual
assistance to customers. The banking chatbots can either take the
form of text -based or voice -based v irtual assistants. The adoption of
digital banking services in India has prompted several banks to invest
in AI-powered chatbots systems. This paper focuses on SBI’s SIA/Ask
Sia, HDFC’s EVA, ICICI’s iPal, Axis’s AHA and Kotak Mahindra’s Keya
chatbots. The paper utilizes a qualitative secondary research approach
to examine the features, application, benefits and limitations of the five
chatbots. Five chatbots have been reviewed individually in terms of
their key features, functions, and effectiveness. The pa per also
explores how AI -powered chatbots can help in enhancing customer
convenience, access to information, speed, customer satisfaction and
trust. The paper reveals that AI chatbots have evolved from simple
Q&A to providing product information, account related queries, service
requests, card payment services, loan -related information and select
transaction banking services. The paper also reveals that features
such as voice, multilingual and app -integrated support are increasingly
being adopted by the five chatbots. The paper concludes by noting that
while AI chatbots can transform banking services, the success of their
implementation depends on their trustworthiness, accuracy, security,
and learning capabilities.

Introduction
 Technological advancements have brought about significant changes in the ways organizations
operate and interact with their customers. The changes have been particularly profound in the banking
sector as the banking activities involve high volumes of routine customer service, transactions and
requests. In the past, customers used to access bank services through physical branches, telephone

banking and customer service representatives. However, the emergence of internet and mobile banking
has changed the way customers access and use banking services and products. In addition, customers
have become more demanding and want services to be accessible at their convenience.
Artificial Intelligence (AI) is among the emerging technologies being adopted by the banking
sector to satisfy the changing demands of customers. AI empowers computers to carry out activities that
would otherwise require human intellect. AI can be applied in various aspects of the banking sector
including customer service, fraud detection, credit risk modeling, predictive analytics, robo -advice, and
robotic process automation. As described in the uploaded reference paper, the banking sector is
currently witnessing a wave of innovation and disruption by leveraging AI -enabled technologies such as
chatbots, robo -advice, predictive analytics, cybersecurity solutions and credit scoring models among
others.
 Chatbots are computer programs that engage in one -to-one conversations with their users.
Natural Language Processing (NLP) enables chatbots to converse with humans in natural languages and
respond appropriately to their questions. The uploaded reference paper observes that chatbots are
designed to interact with human beings and can entertain questions while also learning from them
through algorithms.
 Indian banks have been leveraging chatbots to offer enhanced digital customer experience.
Some of the major Indian banks offering chatbot services include State Bank of India, HDFC Bank, ICICI
Bank, Axis Bank and Kotak Mahindra Bank. SBI offers Ask Sia in their mobile and online banking
platforms. HDFC Bank offers EVA – an intelligent 24×7 virtual assistant that provides information on
products and services and offers customer support including search options and Hindi language support.
ICICI Bank offers iPal – a digital banking assistant that offers chatbot features and transaction banking
chatbots. Axis Bank offers AHA – an AI enabled virtual assistant that delivers chatbot -based banking
services through the adoption of machine learning algorithms and natural language processing. The
virtual assistant also supports multiple languages. Kotak Mahindra Bank offers Keya – an AI -powered
virtual assistant accessible through the bank’s website, Net Banking and mobile application.
 The applications of chatbots in the banking sector present several advantages. First, the use of
chatbots eliminates the need to visit a physical bank branch or wait for a customer service representative
to address a query. The chatbots can answer customer questions instantly and help with several banking
transactions. The use of chatbots also allows banks to automate routine customer requests and free up
human resources to handle more complex customer service issues. However, the banking sector has
several distinctive features that must be considered when deploying AI -powered chatbots. For instance,
the banking sector requires a high level of assurance and confidence between the banks and their
customers. Thus, chatbots must be accurate and reliable to avoid dissatisfying customer experience.
 The objective of this paper is to examine how five major Indian banking chatbots leverage
artificial intelligence to assist the banks in supporting their customers and the key benefits and limitations
associated with the chatbots.
Literature Review
 Artificial Intelligence has become an integral part of the modern banking system. AI has allowed
banks to transform and automate cognitive and repetitive banking operations while also improving the
analytical capabilities of banks. The reference paper observes that AI can improve customer experience,
enable predictive analytics, automate cognitive functions, enhance decision making and robotic process
automation among other applications.
 Chatbots have become increasingly important virtual tools in the banking sector as they can
offer a direct interface between AI and customers. Chatbots can be used in providing customer support,
information dissemination, addressing customer inquiries and complaints among other applications.
 Studies conducted on the application of chatbots in the Indian banking sector reveal that chatbot
service quality plays a crucial role in determining customer satisfaction and intention to continue using
the chatbot services. Mehrolia et al. carried out a study on the influence of service quality on the intention
to adopt chatbots among Indian banking customers. The study focused on the five service quality
dimensions, namely: reliability, interactivity, assurance, responsiveness and understandability. The
results revealed that the five dimensions influenced post -adoption confirmation, satisfaction and
likelihood to continue using chatbot services. The study also revealed that perceived risk had a negative
influence on customers’ responses. Another study that explored the influence of AI chatbots in building

the banks and their customers. First, chatbots can increase efficiency and productivity by being able to
automate most of the functions that would have been performed by human employees. Second, chatbots
can improve customer convenience and satisfaction by providing a round the clock digital customer
service. Lastly, chatbots can build customer trust if implemented effectively. However, there are several
limitations associated with implementing chatbots in the banking sector. For instance, customers may be
skeptical of using AI systems to carry out their banking activities due to data privacy and security
concerns. Some of the limitations associated with chatbots include the inability to address complex
queries and lack of empathy when responding to customer inquiries.
 This paper undertakes a comparative study of five Indian banking chatbots to examine how
each chatbot can be used to benefit the bank and its customers.
Research Methodology
Research Design
 This paper focuses on the application of AI in the banking sector through chatbots. Specifically,
the paper examines the role of chatbots in improving banking operations, customer convenience,
satisfaction and trust. This paper utilizes a qualitative, descriptive and comparative research design in
order to focus on the features, application, benefits and limitations of chatbots in the Indian banking
sector. This paper does not utilize primary data collected through questionnaires and interviews but relies
on secondary data.
 This study has been inspired by the research design of the uploaded reference paper. Like the
reference paper, this paper utilizes qualitative research methods, case study and document analysis to
examine the five different banking chatbots.
 The five chatbots that have been selected for this paper include:
• SBI – SIA / Ask Sia
• HDFC Bank – EVA
• ICICI Bank – iPal
• Axis Bank – AHA
• Kotak Mahindra Bank – Keya

Five Chatbot Models Implemented in Indian Banks
• SBI – SIA / Ask Sia
 State Bank of India is one of India’s largest banks with an extensive digital presence. One of the
applications developed by the bank is SIA, available on the bank’s digital platforms as Ask Sia.
The current website for SBI offers the Ask Sia interface where users can type their questions
and receive answers. Additionally, the system asks users to rate their experience with SIA.
 SIA represents one of the most significant implementations of conversational AI in banking due
to the scale of the banking institution. SBI has a very large customer base, and thus, even small
improvements in automating customer service can have a major impact on the bank’s operations.
 The main purpose of SIA is to offer information and assistance to the bank’s customers in a
conversational manner. Instead of browsing the bank’s website, a user can simply ask SIA to answer
their questions.
Major benefits of SIA
 The first benefit is accessibility. Users can access SIA from the bank’s digital ecosystem without
having to visit a physical branch.
Second, SIA offers speed. Straightforward questions can be answered instantly, while more
complex ones might require minutes, as opposed to hours or days when using conventional customer -
support methods.
 Third, SIA offers scalability. Since it is a computer program, it can theoretically handle millions of
requests at once.
 Fourth, the bank can use feedback from users to continuously improve the system’s
performance. The feedback mechanism is currently available on the Ask Sia website.
 However, SIA, similarly to other chatbots, represents a fundamental limitation in terms of the
scope of problems it can handle. The system is useful when the user asks a question or makes a request
that the chatbot can handle. However, more complex issues usually require human intervention.
• HDFC Bank – EVA
 HDFC Bank’s EVA (Electronic Virtual Assistant) is one of India’s most established examples of
a banking chatbot. The system was launched in 2017 as an AI -powered customer-service chatbot. At the

channels and provide answers in real-time.
 The current website for HDFC Bank describes EVA as a personal banking assistant that offers
round-the-clock assistance. The chatbot can provide information about the bank’s products and services
and supports Hindi and voice search.
 EVA can assist users with a wide range of banking -related queries. It covers savings accounts,

 An important feature of EVA is that it can be used for voice searches. Additionally, the system
can switch to the Hindi language. These two features increase the accessibility of the chatbot.
 HDFC Bank has also leveraged the capabilities of EVA to target new markets. In 2021, the bank
announced the launch of EVA on the Digital Seva Portal, in partnership with Common Services Centres.
On the portal, EVA helps Village Level Entrepreneurs to serve rural and semi-urban customers.

• ICICI Bank – iPal
 ICICI Bank’s iPal is another major example of a banking chatbot. The system is described by
the bank as a digital assistant, with chatbot functionality available through the bank’s online -banking
system.
 ICICI’s website shows that iPal has different functions – the chatbot can provide customer
support, while its transactional capabilities are available through separate services. This shows that
conversational banking is not necessarily limited to answering simple questions.
 The bank also offers Ask iPal  for NRI (Non -Resident Indians) customers. The chatbot can
provide 24-hour customer support, instant responses, and escalation to customer -support executives. It
is available to Net Banking and iMobile users.
 An important feature of iPal is its voice-based capabilities. The bank announced the launch of its
AI-powered multi-channel chatbot on Amazon Alexa and Google Assistant. With this feature, customers
can access some of iPal’s services through voice commands.
 This is a major benefit, as it shows that ICICI Bank is working towards making conversational
banking more accessible to all customers. Voice-based chatbots can be a convenient tool for people who
are not comfortable with digital banking.
Major benefits of iPal
 The first benefit is the use of multiple channels. iPal is available to users through different digital
channels.
 Second, the chatbot offers instant responses. The availability of Ask iPal for NRIs shows that
the system can offer round-the-clock support.

• Axis Bank – AHA
 Axis Bank’s AHA is an AI-powered virtual banking assistant. The bank’s website shows that Axis
AHA 2.0 is a chatbot that uses machine learning, artificial intelligence, and natural language processing
to offer assistance. Additionally, the system offers English and Hindi language options and a microphone
facility for voice-based queries.
 Axis AHA can assist users with a range of banking -related tasks. According to the bank’s
website, the chatbot can help check savings -account balances, view or download mini statements, and
manage debit - and credit -card services. The latter includes tracking card delivery, changing PINs,
assessing limits, and deactivating cards.
 AHA can be accessed through the Axis Bank website, as well as through the Axis Mobile App.
 This feature is important because it shows that Axis Bank is working towards providing
conversational assistance through the channels that customers use to access the bank’s digital services.
Benefits of Axis AHA
 One of the most significant benefits of Axis AHA is convenience. Users do not have to navigate
through multiple digital menus to find the information or services they need.
 A second benefit is multilingual support. The chatbot can be used by a larger audience due to
the availability of English and Hindi language options.
 A third benefit is voice interaction. Users can speak with the chatbot and ask questions using
the microphone facility.
 A fourth benefit is transactional assistance. AHA is not limited to providing information; it can
also help with routine banking transactions.
 A fifth benefit is 24 -hour access to the chatbot. The bank’s website shows that AHA is always
available to answer questions and assist with basic transactions.
 The main limitation of Axis AHA is that it cannot address all of the bank’s customers’ needs.
Some queries and transactions can only be handled by human representatives. This is an inevitable
limitation due to the complexity of banking operations.
Kotak Mahindra Bank – Keya
 Keya is Kotak Mahindra Bank’s AI-powered virtual assistant. Kotak’s website shows that Keya is
an AI chatbot that can answer banking -related queries and learn through interaction. The current Keya
service is available on Kotak’s website, after Net Banking login, and the Kotak Mobile Banking App.
 Keya can assist customers with a range of queries. According to the bank’s website, Keya can
help with inquiries about Kotak 811 accounts, full KYC procedures, CRN, MPIN, charges, virtual and
physical debit cards, and modes of loading money into an account.
 Keya has a long history in Kotak Mahindra’s conversational -banking services. According to
Kotak’s website, Keya was launched in 2018 as an AI-powered bilingual voicebot that was integrated with
the bank’s phone -banking system. The bank described Keya as India’s first AI -powered voicebot in the
banking sector, capable of understanding English and Hindi.
 Keya’s development shows the evolution of chatbots in the Indian banking sector, from
traditional phone-based IVR systems to advanced conversational assistants.
 Kotak’s website shows that Keya can provide 24×7 conversational banking support and
assistance with account balances, transaction history, statements, service requests, and selected
financial transactions.

Benefits of Chatbots in Indian Banking
• Advanced Communication
 The first major benefit of banking chatbots is advanced communication. Customers can speak to
the chatbot in natural language, as opposed to navigating through a series of predefined menus.
The reference paper also mentions that chatbots can fundamentally change the way customers
communicate with a bank. The system can respond to customer queries in natural language, as opposed
to requiring them to understand complex menus.
• 24/7 Support
 Banking chatbots can provide continuous support to customers. This is a major benefit,
considering that traditional customer -support channels such as phone lines and email have limited
availability.
HDFC EVA, ICICI Ask iPal, Axis AHA, and Kotak Keya all offer 24 -hour customer support
through their chatbots.
• Increased Speed
 A chatbot can respond to customer queries in real -time. This is a major advantage over
traditional customer-support channels that require customers to wait on the phone or send an email and
wait for a reply.
 The original research paper notes that chatbots can fundamentally change the way transactions
are made, with faster speeds.
• Improved Customer Satisfaction
 Customer satisfaction can be improved by reducing the amount of time and effort required to
complete a task.
 If a customer can receive the required information or assistance without waiting, the interaction
with the bank is more likely to be positive. The reference paper notes that satisfaction with service
delivery is a major determinant of customer loyalty.
• Reduced Workload for Employees
 Chatbots can reduce the amount of repetitive work done by bank employees.
By automating routine tasks, chatbots can free up employees to focus on more complex issues.
This can be particularly beneficial in areas such as complex complaint resolution, financial planning,
fraud detection, and relationship management.
 As such, chatbots should not be considered as a replacement for human employees. Instead,
they should be used as an enabler to make employees more productive.
• Wider Accessibility
 Voice and multilingual capabilities can make banking more accessible to a larger audience.

HDFC EVA, Axis AHA, and Kotak Keya all offer Hindi language support, while ICICI iPal and

• Customer Trust and Security
 Trust is a major factor that determines whether a customer will use a particular banking service.
As such, it is essential for chatbot developers to consider the factors that can increase or decrease
customer trust.
 Customers must feel that:
▪ They are interacting with the correct entity
▪ The information provided by the chatbot is accurate
▪ Their personal information is secure
▪ Transactions are not compromised
▪ The chatbot cannot misuse their personal information
▪ They can speak to a human representative if needed
 ICICI’s Ask iPal service, for instance, offers encryption and two -factor authentication for all
transactions, while also offering the option to speak to a customer-support executive.
 HDFC Bank also takes customer security and privacy into consideration when designing EVA.
For instance, for services that require card details, the system asks for the customer’s registered mobile
number and the last four digits of their card.
 As such, it is essential for banks to design chatbot systems with security and privacy in mind.
Customers are likely to be more receptive to chatbot services that offer additional security features, such
as two-factor authentication.
 Customers are also more likely to trust chatbots that offer human support, particularly for
complex transactions. As such, banks should consider offering an option for customers to speak to a
human representative if needed.
It is also worth noting that customers’ trust can be undermined if the chatbot fails to answer their
questions correctly or understand their intent.
Challenges of AI Chatbots in Indian Banking
• Accuracy
 A chatbot must be able to provide accurate information, particularly on topics such as interest
rates, transaction fees, eligibility criteria, and other quantitative factors.
• Privacy
 Chatbots may process personal and sensitive information, which means that banks must ensure
that appropriate data-protection measures are in place.
• Security
 Banking systems are major targets for cyber -attacks, which means that chatbots must have
stringent security measures.
• Lack of Human Empathy
 Chatbots are not capable of understanding human emotions. As such, they are not suitable for
handling sensitive requests, such as fraud-related inquiries or complaints.
• Complex Queries
 Chatbots may struggle to understand complex queries, particularly if the customer does not
follow the predefined instructions.
• Digital Literacy
 Not all customers are comfortable with digital technologies. As such, chatbots may not be the
most appropriate channel for all customers.

Discussion
 The comparative analysis shows that AI chatbots are becoming an essential part of India’s
digital-banking ecosystem. While the five chatbots have different features, they all represent a transition
from traditional customer-support channels to conversational interfaces.
 SBI’s SIA shows how a large public-sector bank can provide conversational assistance within its
digital ecosystem. HDFC EVA is a great example of how AI can be used to provide extensive information
about a bank’s products and services. ICICI iPal is notable for its multichannel and voice-based approach
to conversational banking. Axis AHA combines traditional digital -banking services with AI -based
assistance, while Kotak Keya represents the evolution of voice -based banking into a more
comprehensive digital assistant.
 It is evident that chatbots can offer major benefits to Indian banks, their employees, and
customers.
 For customers, the main benefit is convenience.
 For employees, chatbots can reduce the amount of repetitive work, allowing them to focus on
more complex tasks.
 For banks, conversational AI can fundamentally change the way they operate, providing
significant cost-reduction opportunities while improving customer satisfaction.
 However, it is essential for banks to understand that the success of chatbots depends on
customers’ trust and satisfaction. The reference paper notes that the quality of service, reliability,
responsiveness, and understandability all contribute to customer satisfaction with chatbots.
 As such, future research on banking chatbots should focus on how these factors can be
improved.
Findings
 The secondary analysis conducted for this paper has yielded several major findings. First, AI
chatbots are firmly established in India’s digital-banking ecosystem. The five major banks all use chatbots
to provide assistance to their customers. Second, the capabilities of banking chatbots go far beyond the
capacity of traditional FAQ chatbots.
 Modern banking chatbots can assist with a wide range of tasks, including product information,
account and card -related services, transaction support, and more. Third, chatbots can provide 24 -hour
support, which is particularly beneficial for customers who need assistance outside of regular business
hours.
 Fourth, voice-based assistance and multilingual support are becoming major features of banking
chatbots.
 Finally, it is evident that customer satisfaction is not solely driven by the speed of service.
 While faster speeds are certainly beneficial, the reliability and convenience of chatbots also play
a major role in the overall customer experience. Additionally, human support is essential, particularly for
complex transactions.
Conclusion
Artificial Intelligence is transforming India’s banking sector, with chatbots being among the most
prominent applications of the technology. This paper provides an analysis of five major banking chatbots
used by India’s largest banks: SBI SIA, HDFC EVA, ICICI iPal, Axis AHA, and Kotak Keya. The analysis
focuses on the capabilities of these chatbots, their benefits, and their limitations.
 The paper shows that these chatbots represent major advancements in conversational banking.
They can all provide assistance to customers in natural language, answer questions, provide product
information, and help with account and card -related services. Some chatbots also offer 24 -hour support,
voice-based assistance, and multilingual support.
