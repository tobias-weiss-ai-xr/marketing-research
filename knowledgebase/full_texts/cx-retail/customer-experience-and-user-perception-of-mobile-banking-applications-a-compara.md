Abstract
This study examines customer experience and user perceptions of SBI YONO and HDFC Mobile Banking applications by integrating
the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) and SERVQUAL framework. Using a comparative quantitative approach, 2,000 user
reviews (1,000 reviews per application) collected from the Google Play Store between 1 January 2023 and 31 December 2025 were
analysed using frequency analysis, chi-square tests, and ordinal logistic regression. The findings revealed that Attitude Toward
Use (ATU) was the most frequently occurring construct, while Reliability (REL) emerged as the most prominent service quality
dimension. HDFC Mobile Banking performed significantly better than SBI YONO in terms of Attitude Toward Use and Perceived
Ease of Use. However , no significant difference was found between the two applications regarding Reliability. The regression
results indicated that Attitude Toward Use and Reliability significantly influenced user ratings, whereas Perceived Ease of Use and
application type did not. The study concludes that customer evaluations of mobile banking applications are primarily driven by
users’ overall experiences and perceptions of reliability. The findings provide practical insights for banks seeking to enhance user
experience and improve service quality in mobile banking applications.

The rapid growth of mobile banking has transformed the manner in

security, responsiveness, and service quality significantly influence
customers’ evaluations of mobile banking services (Davis, 1989;

relied on survey-based methodologies and structured questionnaires
toexaminecustomerperceptionsandbehaviouralintentions(Shaikh
& Karjaluoto, 2015; Alalwan et al., 2017). While these approaches
provide valuable insights, they may not fully capture customers’
spontaneous experiences and actual concerns encountered during
the use of mobile banking applications. Moreover, studies comparing

ing App using customer-generated review data remains limited.
Furthermore, previous studies have generally examined technology
acceptanceandservicequalityindependently,leavingagapinthelit-
erature regarding the integrated application of TAM and SERVQUAL

Existing studies have largely focused on adoption intentions rather
than actual user experiences expressed through review platforms.
As a result, important insights relating to customers’ satisfaction,
dissatisfaction, usability concerns, service quality perceptions, and
technical challenges remain insufficiently explored.
Therefore, there exists a need for a comprehensive comparative
investigation of customer experience and user perception regard-
ing SBI YONO and HDFC Mobile Banking App using customer-
generated review data. By integrating the Technology Acceptance
Model (TAM) and SERVQUAL framework, the present study seeks
to address this gap by examining how technological factors and
service-quality dimensions influence customer experiences and
perceptions. The findings of the study are expected to contribute
to the existing literature on digital banking and provide practical
insights for improving mobile banking applications and enhancing
customer satisfaction.

3.1.2. Technology Acceptance model and Mobile Banking Usage
The Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), introduced by Davis
(1989), is one of the most widely used frameworks for understanding
technology adoption. The model explains that perceived useful-
ness and perceived ease of use are major determinants of users’
acceptance of a technological system. Perceived usefulness refers
to the extent to which users believe that a system improves their
performance, while perceived ease of use refers to the extent to
which the system is free from effort (Davis, 1989). In mobile banking,
these constructs are highly relevant because customers are more
likely to use applications that help them complete banking tasks
quickly and easily. Venkatesh and Davis (2000) extended TAM and
demonstrated that perceived usefulness and ease of use continue
to influence users’ behavioural intention and technology usage. In
mobilebankingstudies,TAMhasbeenwidelyappliedtoexplainwhy
customers adopt and continue using digital banking applications
(Shaikh & Karjaluoto, 2015). For the present study, TAM is useful
for interpreting review themes such as ease of use, convenience,
interface simplicity, app functionality, and overall usefulness of SBI
YONO and HDFC Mobile Banking App.
3.1.3. Service quality in Mobile Banking
Service quality is an important determinant of customer satisfac-
tion in banking services. Parasuraman et al. (1988) developed the
SERVQUAL model, which identifies five dimensions of service qual-
ity: reliability, responsiveness, assurance, empathy, and tangibility.
Although the model was originally developed for traditional service
settings, its dimensions remain relevant in mobile banking. In digital
banking applications, reliability refers to successful and error-free
transactions, responsiveness refers to speed of service and issue
resolution, assurance refers to security and trust, empathy refers
to customer support, and tangibility may be reflected in the design
and interface of the application. Jun and Palacios (2016) found that
mobile banking service quality is influenced by factors such as
reliability, convenience, ease of use, security, accuracy, and continu-
ous improvement. Kaura et al. (2015) also emphasized that service
quality and service convenience significantly influence customer
satisfaction and loyalty in banking services. Therefore, customer
reviews mentioning transaction failures, poor support, slow service,
app crashes, or security concerns can be meaningfully interpreted
through the SERVQUAL framework.
3.1.4. Customer Experience and User Perception in Mobile Bank-
ing
Customer experience in mobile banking refers to users’ overall
perceptions and responses arising from their interaction with bank-
ing applications. Positive experiences may result from fast transac-
tions, easy navigation, secure payments, and reliable services, while
negative experiences may result from login failures, transaction
delays, app crashes, poor customer support, or security concerns.
Previous studies have shown that customer satisfaction and loyalty
are strongly influenced by service quality, convenience, and trust
(Jun & Palacios, 2016; Kaura et al., 2015).
User perception is particularly important in mobile banking
because customers directly interact with the application rather than
with bank employees. As a result, the application itself becomes a
major representation of the bank’s service quality. If the application
isdifficulttouse,unreliable,orslow,customersmaydevelopnegative
perceptionsofthebank’sdigitalservicequality.Therefore,analysing
customer reviews provides an opportunity to understand how users
perceive SBI YONO and HDFC Mobile Banking App based on their
actual experiences.
3.1.5. Security, Trust, and Reliability in Mobile Banking
Security and trust are central to mobile banking because users
conduct sensitive financial transactions through digital platforms.
Customers are more likely to adopt and continue using mobile
banking applications when they believe that their personal data,
account details, and financial transactions are safe. Alalwan et al.
(2017) found that perceived risk influences customers’ adoption of
digital banking services. Similarly, Shaikh and Karjaluoto (2015)
identified trust and security as important factors affecting mobile
banking adoption. Reliability is also essential because customers
expect mobile banking applications to perform transactions accu-
rately and consistently. Frequent transaction failures, login issues,
server problems, or technical glitches may reduce customer trust
and satisfaction. Within the SERVQUAL framework, reliability and
assurance are especially relevant for analysing mobile banking
reviews because they capture users’ concerns about transaction
success, system stability, safety, and confidence in digital banking
services.
3.1.6. Online Reviews and Customer Experience Analytics
Customer-generated online reviews have become an important
source of understanding customer experience and service quality
perceptions. Unlike survey responses, online reviews often capture
spontaneous and experience-based opinions expressed by actual
users. Oh and Kim (2021) demonstrated that mobile banking app
reviews can reveal important factors affecting customer satisfaction,
including ease of use, convenience, security, customer support, and
technical problems. Their study shows that app review analysis can
provide valuable insights into both positive and negative customer
experiences.
For the present study, online reviews are particularly useful
because they allow a comparative analysis of SBI YONO and HDFC
Mobile Banking App based on actual user feedback. Review-based
analysis can help identify recurring themes such as usability, trans-
action failure, login issues, app performance, security concerns, re-
sponsiveness, and customer support. Therefore, customer-generated
reviews provide a suitable empirical basis for examining customer
experience and user perception in mobile banking applications.

are expected to occur with equal frequency in customer reviews

modelling. App-store reviews are spontaneous, unstructured, and
user-driven; consequently, users may discuss certain dimensions
extensively while making little or no reference to others. All iden-

the descriptive analysis to preserve the theoretical breadth of the
framework. However, inferential analysis is restricted to constructs
with sufficient empirical occurrence to support meaningful and
stable statistical estimation. This distinction allows the integrated
framework to provide comprehensive theoretical coverage while
ensuring that the statistical analysis remains appropriate to the
characteristics of the observed review data.

on a five-point ordinal scale ranging from one to five stars, serves as
the dependent variable. The mobile banking application (SBI YONO

4. Methodology Adopted
4.1. Research design
This study adopted a comparative quantitative research design using
secondary data obtained from user-generated reviews of mobile
banking applications. A content analysis approach was employed to
examine customer experiences and user perceptions of SBI YONO
and HDFC Mobile Banking applications. The study integrated the
Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) and SERVQUAL framework
to identify the key factors influencing user evaluations and ratings.
4.2. Data Source
The data for this study consists of public user reviews extracted from
the Google play store. Android reviews were chosen exclusively
because Android holds the majority of the market share in India,
providing large volumes of publicly available user feedback and
serve as important channels through which customers express
their experiences, opinions, and concerns regarding mobile banking
applications.
4.3. Sampling Framework and Time-frame
Thisstudyemploysastratifiedsamplingapproachtoensurebalanced
representation across different rating categories. Reviews were
stratified according to their star ratings (1-star to 5-star), and an
equal number of reviews were selected from each category. The
final sample comprised 2,000 reviews, including 1,000 reviews for
SBI YONO and 1,000 reviews for HDFC Mobile Banking. For each
application, 200 reviews were selected from each rating category,
resulting in an equal distribution of ratings across the sample.
The study analysed user reviews posted between 1 January 2023
and 31 December 2025. A three-year sampling period was selected to
capture recent customer experiences and perceptions of SBI YONO
and HDFC Mobile Banking applications. The selected period was
considered appropriate because it captures recent developments
in mobile banking while providing a sufficiently large and diverse
sample of user reviews. A shorter period could limit data availability,
whereas a longer period might include outdated reviews that no
longer reflect current application features and user experiences.

The TAM constructs considered in the study include Perceived
Usefulness (PU), Perceived Ease of Use (PEOU), Attitude Toward Use
(ATU), and Behavioural Intention (BI). The SERVQUAL dimensions
include Reliability (REL), Responsiveness (RES), Assurance (ASS),
Empathy (EMP), and Tangibles (TAN). A content analysis approach
was employed to code the user reviews according to the constructs
of the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) and SERVQUAL
framework. Keywords and phrases associated with each construct
were identified from prior literature and refined through a manual
review of the selected app-store reviews. The coding process
involved examining each review to determine the presence or
absence of construct-related expressions. Each TAM and SERVQUAL
construct was operationalised as a binary variable, where a value of
1 indicated the presence of a construct-related expression in a review
and a value of 0 indicated its absence. The dependent variable of
the study is the user rating score, measured on a five-point ordinal
scale ranging from 1 star to 5 stars. The type of mobile banking
application was included as a control variable and coded as 1 for SBI
YONO and 2 for HDFC Mobile Banking.
To improve coding validity, the identification of TAM and
SERVQUAL constructs was not based solely on isolated keyword
matching. Each review was examined in its contextual form, and
negations and syntactic qualifications were considered during
manual coding. For example, expressions such as “not easy to
use” were not coded as positive evidence of Perceived Ease of Use
merely because the phrase contained the keyword “easy.” Similarly,
statements such as “never useful” were interpreted according to
their complete contextual meaning rather than the presence of
the word “useful.” Where the meaning of a phrase was ambiguous,
the broader sentence context was considered before assigning the
constructcode.Thecodingprocedurethereforecombineddictionary-
assisted identification of relevant terms with manual contextual
verification. This approach was adopted to reduce the risk of
false-positive classification arising from simple keyword matching.
However,becausethecodingwasconductedbytheresearcherrather
than through independent multiple-coder assessment, a formal
inter-coder reliability coefficient such as Cohen’s Kappa was not
calculated. This is acknowledged as a methodological limitation of
the study.

ity dimensions relevant to digital banking services. The dimensions
include Reliability (REL), Responsiveness (RES), Assurance (ASS),
Empathy (EMP), and Tangibles (TAN). These constructs assess users'
perceptions of system performance, customer support, security,
personalised services, and interface design.

6. Findings
The study found that Attitude Toward Use (ATU) was the most
frequently identified TAM construct, while Reliability (REL), re-
flected primarily through technical failures and unreliability, was
the most prominent SERVQUAL dimension in the customer reviews.
In contrast, other SERVQUAL dimensions, including Responsive-
ness, Assurance, Empathy, and Tangibles, occurred relatively in-
frequently. However, their low frequency should be interpreted
as a characteristic of the spontaneous and technology-mediated
nature of app-store reviews rather than as evidence that these
dimensions are theoretically irrelevant to mobile banking services.
The comparative analysis revealed that HDFC Mobile Banking had
a significantly higher frequency of reviews containing Attitude
Toward Use and Perceived Ease of Use content than SBI YONO.
However, no statistically significant difference was found between
the two applications in the occurrence of reliability-related content.
These results indicate differences in the frequency with which
users expressed particular experiences and perceptions in their
reviews and should not be interpreted as direct evidence that one
application performed better overall than the other. The ordinal
logistic regression analysis further showed that Attitude Toward
Use and technical failures/unreliability were significantly associated
withuserratings,whereasPerceivedEaseofUseandapplicationtype
werenotstatisticallysignificant.Specifically,theabsenceoftechnical
failures or unreliability was associated with higher odds of being in a
higher user-rating category. Overall, the findings suggest that users’
overall attitudes and experiences with the technical dependability
of mobile banking services are closely associated with the ratings
they assign, while application type and Perceived Ease of Use did
not independently show significant associations with user ratings
in the estimated model.
7. Implications of the study
The findings of this study have important theoretical, managerial,
and methodological implications. Theoretically, the study demon-
strates the usefulness of integrating the Technology Acceptance
Model (TAM) and SERVQUAL framework to analyse customer
experiences in mobile banking environments. The findings indi-
cate that Attitude Toward Use and reliability-related factors play
important roles in shaping user ratings. In particular, the findings
provide an integrated interpretation of the relationship between
technical reliability and user attitude in digital banking. In a mo-
bile banking environment, the application itself constitutes the
primary interface through which customers experience banking
services. Consequently, technical stability, successful transaction
processing, dependable login functionality, and uninterrupted access
may form basic conditions for positive user evaluations. When
these functional expectations are not met, users are more likely
to express dissatisfaction through negative attitudes and lower app
ratings. The significant association between Attitude Toward Use
and user ratings, together with the importance of reliability-related
problems, therefore suggests that technical performance and overall
user attitude are closely interconnected in app-based banking ex-
periences. This relationship also helps explain why interpersonal
SERVQUAL dimensions such as empathy and responsiveness oc-
curred less frequently in the present review data. In traditional
branch banking, customers interact directly with employees and
can evaluate interpersonal service behaviours. Mobile banking, in
contrast, is predominantly a self-service environment in which
users interact directly with the technological interface. As a result,
customer-generatedappreviewsmaynaturallyemphasisefunctional
dimensions such as system reliability, transaction success, usability,
and application performance rather than interpersonal aspects of
service delivery. The low frequency of empathy, responsiveness, and
related constructs should therefore be interpreted cautiously as a
characteristic of the review context rather than as evidence that
these service-quality dimensions are theoretically irrelevant. From a
managerial perspective, the findings suggest that banks should pri-
oritise enhancing the overall user experience and improving system
reliability by reducing application crashes, transaction failures, login
issues, and other technical problems. Since technical performance is
closely associated with users’ overall evaluations of mobile banking
applications, maintaining a stable and dependable application is
important for improving customer experience. The results also show

8. Limitations and Direction for Future study
This study has several limitations that should be considered when
interpreting the findings. First, the study relied exclusively on cus-
tomer reviews obtained from the Google Play Store. Consequently,
the findings primarily represent the experiences and perceptions of
Android users and may not fully capture those of customers using
iOS-based devices. Differences in device characteristics, operating-
system environments, application interfaces, update cycles, and
user interaction patterns may influence how customers experience
and evaluate mobile banking applications. Therefore, the findings
should not be generalised to the entire population of SBI YONO and
HDFC Mobile Banking users across both Android and iOS platforms.
Future studies could incorporate reviews from both the Google Play
Store and Apple App Store to examine whether platform-specific
differences influence customer experience, usability perceptions,
technical problems, and service-quality evaluations. Second, the
study employed a stratified sampling approach with equal allocation
across the five rating categories, with 200 reviews selected from
each rating category for each application. Although this procedure
ensured balanced representation of ratings from one to five stars, the
resulting sample does not reflect the natural distribution of ratings
availableontheGooglePlayStore.Consequently,thesampledrating
distribution should not be interpreted as representing the actual
proportion of positive, neutral, or negative evaluations among the
overall population of app users. Third, several theoretically relevant
TAM and SERVQUAL constructs, including Behavioural Intention,
Responsiveness, Assurance, Empathy, and Tangibles, occurred rela-
tively infrequently in the sampled reviews and were therefore not
includedintheinferentialanalysis.Theirlowoccurrencemayreflect
the spontaneous and unstructured nature of app-store reviews, in
which users tend to discuss issues that are most salient to their
immediate experiences. It may also partly reflect the sensitivity of
the coding dictionary used to identify the constructs. Accordingly,
the low frequencies should not be interpreted as evidence that these
constructs are theoretically unimportant. They were retained in
the descriptive analysis, while inferential analysis was restricted
to constructs with sufficient empirical occurrence to support more
stable statistical estimation. Fourth, although dictionary-assisted
coding was supplemented by contextual verification to account
for expressions involving negation and contextual meaning, the
coding procedure may not fully capture complex linguistic features
such as sarcasm, implicit meanings, mixed emotions, and nuanced
expressions. In addition, the coding was undertaken by a single
researcher; therefore, formal inter-coder reliability statistics such as
Cohen’s Kappa were not calculated. This should be recognised as a
methodological limitation of the study. Future research could em-
ploy multiple independent coders and assess inter-coder agreement
using appropriate reliability measures. Future studies may further
address these limitations by incorporating reviews from multiple
app platforms, using larger and naturally distributed review datasets,
employing multiple independent coders, and applying advanced
text-analytic approaches such as machine-learning-based sentiment
analysis and natural language processing. From a statistical per-
spective, future research may also consider alternative modelling
approaches, including multinomial logistic regression or generalised
ordered logit models, where appropriate, to provide deeper and more
robust insights into customer experiences and perceptions of mobile
banking applications.
9. Conclusion
This study examined customer experience and user perceptions
of mobile banking applications through a comparative analysis of
SBI YONO and HDFC Mobile Banking reviews using an integrated
Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) and SERVQUAL framework.

Google Play Store between 1 January 2023 and 31 December 2025,
comprising 1,000 reviews from each application. The integrated
framework enabled customer-generated review content to be exam-
ined from both technology-acceptance and service-quality perspec-
tives. The descriptive findings revealed that Attitude Toward Use
(ATU) was the most frequently identified TAM construct, while
Reliability (REL), operationalised primarily through expressions
relating to technical failures and unreliability, was the most fre-
quently identified SERVQUAL dimension. The comparative analysis
further showed that HDFC Mobile Banking had a significantly
higher frequency of reviews containing ATU and Perceived Ease
of Use (PEOU) content than SBI YONO. However, no statistically
significant difference was observed between the two applications in
the frequency of reliability-related review content. These findings
should be interpreted as differences in the occurrence of coded

the overall performance of the two applications. The ordinal logistic
regression analysis further demonstrated that Attitude Toward Use
and technical failures/unreliability were significantly associated
with user ratings, whereas Perceived Ease of Use and application
type were not statistically significant. In particular, the absence of
technical failures or unreliability was associated with higher odds
of being in a higher user-rating category. These results indicate
that users’ overall attitudes and experiences with the technical
dependability of mobile banking services are closely associated with
the ratings they assign, while application type alone does not sig-
nificantly explain differences in user ratings. The study contributes
to the growing literature on digital banking by demonstrating the
usefulness of integrating TAM and SERVQUAL as complementary
theoretical frameworks for classifying and interpreting customer-
generated app-review content. The findings also indicate that some
interpersonal SERVQUAL dimensions, such as responsiveness and
empathy, are less frequently expressed in app-store reviews. How-

of theoretical irrelevance. Rather, it may reflect the predominantly
