Abstract. In the context of Russia's full-scale aggression against Ukraine and the ongoing decolonization of
the national socio-cultural space, the study of intangible asset capitalization mechanisms through strategic
communications has gained particular resonance. Investigating craft production as a representative of local identity
and cognitive resistance is critical for understanding the contemporary model of Ukrainian economic resilience.
The subject of the study  is the socio-communicative technologies of intangible heritage capitalization and the
formation of decolonial narratives within Ukraine's craft sector under martial law. The aim of the research  is to
analyze integrated communication strategies that ensure the conversion of cultural codes and historical memory
into reputational and symbolic brand capital (based on the nationwide project “Craft Christmas” and the regional
initiative “Craft Zaporizhzhia”). The methodology is based on a combination of systemic and decolonial approaches,
the theory of identity claims, as well as elements of digital content discourse analysis and case studies. The analytics
of the implemented cases indicate that decolonial communication creates a Unique Selling Proposition (USP),
significantly increasing the product's added value through a “narrative of search” and the legitimation of local
knowledge. The application of Google Ads and SE Ranking tools allowed for the verification of public demand
for craft content, confirming a high correlation between strategic brand communications and increased digital
audience engagement (specifically within the “Craft Christmas” project). It is proven that the transformation of
social media from advertising platforms into socio-communicative institutions facilitates the formation of “digital
clusters” , where intangible assets (trust, solidarity) are converted into the financial stability of a region. The study
of memorializing lost heritage (e.g., the Chateau Trubetskoi case) and social rehabilitation through fair activities
demonstrates that craft serves as a tool for the “decolonization of the mind” and the restoration of community
agency. The analysis of the “Craft Christmas” case identified a series of terminological definitions describing new
socio-communicative phenomena in the Ukrainian business environment: “Craft Banking” – a strategic model of
financial communication where a banking institution transforms from a technocratic service into an active mediator
of cultural meanings, investing in “humanity” and unique customer experience; “Investment Authentication” – the
process of verifying a local producer by large capital, granting the craft product the status of a national heritage
and increasing its reputational value; “Digital Identity Incubation” – the use of large-scale media capacities (mobile
apps, bank social networks) to bring local micro-brands out of “information shadow” into the broad public space.
Special significance in the context of the craft sector's survival is attributed to the strategy of collective agency.
Our research confirms the hypothesis that in a Wartime Economy, the “solo survival” model is the least effective.
It is critically important for craft producers to integrate into clusters, initiatives, and digital platforms. Joining

1. Introduction
In economics, reputation is not just a concept
of “people think well of us” , it is the difference between
the market price of a company and the cost of its
equipment or raw materials. Craft production without
a legend is just a workshop. With a legend and decolonial
optics it is an expensive brand. In the conditions of
informational, territorial, and armed resistance to the
occupying forces, decolonial communication creates
a unique selling proposition (USP), which significantly
increases the added value of the product. Craft brands
that have built strong communities through socio-
communication technologies demonstrate a higher
level of survival: the community supports “their”
manufacturer even in critical conditions, transforming
social capital into financial stability.
In modern conditions, the capitalization of intangible
assets directly depends on the quality of strategic
communications. As noted by Perez-Cornejo and
co-author (Pérez-Cornejo et al., 2019), high-quality
reporting and transparency not only increase the
visibility of socially responsible business actions, but
also convert them into reputational capital through the
trust mechanism. For craft enterprises whose activities
are based on ethical production, such a communication
strategy becomes a tool for overcoming information
asymmetry and strengthening market positions. Studies
of international practices confirm that all dimensions
of corporate activity – social, environmental and
economic – have a sustainable positive impact on the
reputation of the company (Pérez-Cornejo et al., 2019).
In the context of decolonial discourse, this influence
is amplified as craft enterprises become representatives
of local knowledge and authenticity. However,
successful capitalization of such assets requires a radical
revision of traditional management approaches. As
S.B. Banerjee argues (Banerjee, 2022), decolonizing
management theory is a necessary condition for
overcoming dominant Eurocentric paradigms
that often marginalize local contexts. Introducing
a decolonial perspective into communication policies
allows craft brands to focus on reviving authentic
narratives.
This process is implemented through active
“authenticity work” . In particular, А. Koontz and
N. G. Chapman (Koontz & Chapman, 2019) prove,
that digital resources of craft producers (e.g., sections
“ About Us”) serve as key platforms for legitimation
of their identities through narratives of “search” . For
example, when a craft producer in Zaporizhzhia or
Drohobych writes: “We are restoring an ancient
Ukrainian recipe that was forgotten due to Soviet
unification” – this is simultaneously: a Search Narrative
(“we were looking for our roots”); legitimation (“we
are genuine because we care for culture” , “the right to
recognition”); decolonization (“we are abandoning
the imposed “Soviet” standard in favor of our own”).
So, the website is a place where the brand tells the
“legend” of its journey, so that the consumer wants
to buy this product as a symbol of their identity. This
allows brands to maintain opposition to the mass
market, while remaining economically viable. Thus,
social communication technologies become a tool for
“decolonization of the mind” of both the producer and
the consumer, which turns local agency into a powerful
strategic factor of market capitalization and strengthens
the internal resilience of the community.
2. Integrated Communications Strategy in the
Process of Capitalizing of Intangible Heritage:
Case Studies of the “Craft Christmas” Project
The transformation of the “Craft Christmas” project
demonstrates the transition from a small-scale family
tradition to a large-scale public communication strategy
that performs the function of social consolidation,
implementing the concept of “cultural branding” ,
where a commercial brand becomes part of the national
cultural landscape. The first stages of the project,
implemented through craft fairs and festivals, witnessed
the revitalization of public space as a platform for
direct interaction between producers (craftsmen) and
communities provides several strategic advantages: distribution of communication costs, the “umbrella trust” effect,
and narrative synergy. Thus, the transition from an individualistic model to network collaboration is not merely
a business recommendation but a necessary condition for the decolonization of the Ukrainian market, where
fragmented producers become a single powerful economic front. Conclusions. The research highlights the priority
of the relational communication model (co-creation of meanings) over the transactional one; the effectiveness of
cross-sectoral collaborations between large capital and micro-entrepreneurship as a Win-Win-Win model; and the
necessity of forming a Ukrainian craft narrative as a component of national security and cognitive resistance that
dismantles imperial stereotypes regarding the “peripherality” of local production.
Keywords: capitalization of intangible assets, decolonial discourse, craft production, strategic communications,
social resilience, reputation capital, authenticity work, Ukrainian wartime economy, regional branding, cognitive
security.

The intellectual capitalization of the project was
achieved due to multimedia support, in particular
through a series of podcasts (in collaboration with the
magazine “Local History” and Nadiyka Gerbish), where
each year new stories of Christmas rituals, symbols,
and regional differences are revealed to listeners.
The involvement of opinion leaders and intellectuals
(historian Ya. Hrytsak, musicians and public figures
P . Gudimov, the ONUKA band) made it possible
to verify the voice of tradition in a modern context,
transforming ethnographic knowledge into relevant
content that forms the reputational asset of partners
(in particular, Kredobank), and to perform the function
of an “intellectual verifier” that transforms marketing
content into a scientific and educational asset.
Within the framework of the implementation of the
project's cultural and educational initiatives, objects of
applied art and ritual attributes are transformed into
powerful communication mediators of national heritage.
In particular, the case of the exhibition of Christmas
stars in the Kyiv Art Gallery (supported by Kredobank)
demonstrates the process of actualizing a traditional
symbol as a recognizable visual marker. The evolution
of this element from an authentic ritual attribute to an
object of exhibition and stage space (nativity scenes,
concerts) indicates the scaling of the decolonial
discourse and its integration into a living socio-
economic context. A perfect example of capitalization
of intangible heritage is the cross-sectoral collaboration
between the Grono brand and the Kurbasy ethno-group.
Within the framework of this special project, there
was a convergence of audio folklore (unique regional
Christmas songs) and applied design (craft Christmas
tree decorations). Such integration of ethno-music into
a physical product not only expands the market niche of
craft production, but also serves as a tool for actualizing
the regional diversity of Ukraine, turning the cultural
code into a significant brand asset. The evolution of
symbolic capital gains a particular importance: from
exhibition objects (a Christmas star in the Kyiv Art
Gallery) and cross-industry collaborations (the Grono
brand and the Kurbasy band) to instruments of social
responsibility. This is an example of “multisensory
capitalization” , where the consumer buys not only
a visual object (a toy), but also an audio code (a carol),
which creates the effect of immersion in identity. The
final stage of this strategy is the conversion of cultural
loyalty into real assistance: from caring for vulnerable
groups to raising funds for defense needs (a case of
collecting 100,000 UAH for FPV drones in the Sense
bookstore). This stage is critical because it closes the
cycle of capitalization: symbolic capital (tradition) ->
social capital (trust) -> real resource (security). Thus,
“living tradition” in the context of craft production
ceases to be just an object of consumption, but becomes
a tool of economic resilience and social mobilization,
where the human dimension and the ethic of care act as
key verifiers of brand authenticity.

project is conceptual and strategic. “This holiday has
been destroyed for decades by the Soviet authorities
along with the customs and traditions inherent in
Ukrainian Christmas. Ukraine has regained not only
the authentic date, but also the values of Christmas:
the family circle, home and traditions passed down
from generation to generation. Supporting artisans is
supporting people who preserve these meanings not in
words, but in real things: ceramics, textiles, toys, decor,
food. “Craft Christmas” has become a space where

A special place in the structure of the socio-

the context of the Wartime Economy is strategically
anti-crisis. The advent calendar format here acts not
as entertainment, but as a “digital incubation” of small
business, where the bank provides craftsmen with its
media power as an intangible resource for survival.
The advent calendar mechanics, integrated into the
bank’s mobile application, implements the function
of a digital showcase for craftsmen. Daily presentation
of the brand (history of creation, positioning, values)
allows converting emotional engagement of customers
into real transactions (activation of payments with
Mastercard cards). This transforms the social initiative
from a short-term image campaign into a sustainable
business model, where the banking institution focuses
on human capital and shared values. An example of
such integration is the straw constructor “Vytynanka”

value connotation – such as the silver pendant “Good”
(the “ Antidote” collection from the Katria jewelry

The integration of the vytynanka into the bank
branch is a “desacralization” of the official space, which

institution” in favor of a people-centered hub (Figure 1).

management (A. Melnyk), such support is critical in

challenges), as it allows local producers to maintain
product quality and ensure economic sustainability. The
scaling of the project – from raffles among premium
segments to attracting a wide range of customers –
indicates the democratization of craft consumption.
Thus, the systematic nature of the project (since 2023)
turns it into an institutional tradition that generates not
only marketing indicators, but also reputational capital
based on trust and systemic support of the national
manufacturer. Communication support for the winners
and strict adherence to banking secrecy during the
implementation of the campaign reinforce the ethical
component of the brand, making “Craft Christmas”
an example of the transformation of corporate social

the Case of “Craft Zaporizhzhia”
If the project “Craft Christmas” (Kredobank) is an
example of the macro level (big capital + national idea),
then “Craft Zaporizhzhia” is a micro level (grassroots
initiative + local resilience). The experience of the
“Craft Zaporizhzhia” initiative allows us to analyze
the mechanisms of forming local identity as a form
of cognitive resistance . If nationwide projects focus
on symbols, then local communities capitalize on
territorial affiliation (place-based identity). The
transition from a “home workshop” to a professional
community (156 craftsmen) indicates the creation of
a horizontal network that replaces the Soviet model of
vertical management (Demianova, 2026).
The Craft Zaporizhzhia community acts as
a communication mediator, helping small producers
go through the process of legitimation (legal,
psychological and marketing support). “ A separate
direction is the creation of content for project pages,
advertising materials and support programs for new
community members” (Demianova, 2026). This
is the implementation of the strategy of economic
decolonization, where the local producer ceases to
be “artisanal” and becomes a market entity, and the
direction is planned to be supported by the Zaporizhzhia
Regional Military Administration. The organization of
fairs in the format of “mini-stores” in large shopping
and entertainment centres (CITY MALL, Amstor) is
not just trade, but the appropriation (development)
of space. In the conditions of war, when public space
is constantly under threat, craft fairs serve as a social
stabilizer. Direct producer-consumer contact creates
an “economy of trust” , which is much more sustainable
than an anonymous mass market. It is worth mentioning
that “before the full-scale war, the team also held large
festivals with tastings, culinary shows, and raffles. This
format is currently on pause” (Demianova, 2026).
The singling out craft food (cheeses, honey, sauces,
drinks) into an independent format is an act of the so-
called “gastronomic decolonization”. The rejection
of unified “Soviet” or global brands in favor of the local
tastes of the Zaporizhzhia region is a return to the “taste
of home”. This is the capitalization of local knowledge,
which S. Banerjee wrote about, where authenticity
becomes the main competitive advantage.
Conducting educational hackathons and regular
meetings (marketing, law, psychology) turns the
NGO “Craft City” into an educational hub. This is an
example of the intensification of socio-communication
mechanisms, where knowledge is transferred through
the Peer-to-Peer model. This strengthens the internal
resilience of the community, turning the “hobby” into
a strategic factor in the market capitalization of the
region.
Their Instagram, as a tool for the visual narrative of
local resilience, acts as a meaningful and thoughtful
“social archive of resilience”  (310 posts and
1102 readers as of March 16, 2026) (Craft Zaporizhzhia,
2026), because the profile clearly traces the transition
from pure marketing to military-civilian synthesis .
The visual content of the page implements the strategy
of “normalizing life”  in a frontline city. Photos of
products (ceramics, textiles) against the backdrop of
urban locations in Zaporizhzhia mark the space as “their
own” , controlled and alive.
This is an act of visual decolonization, where the
Ukrainian city is represented through creation, not just
destruction. Unlike the mass market, the Instagram of
“Craft Zaporizhzhia” focuses on the faces of craftsmen.
Each post is a case of “individual agency” : when the
main attention span is not just on a product, but on the
story of a person who continues to work under fire. In
scientific discourse, this can be described as “relational
capital”: the consumer does not buy a thing, but
sympathizes the position of the craftsman. The page
also is also used as a tool of horizontal mobilization,
because Instagram is a platform for coordination
of assistance to the Armed Forces of Ukraine and
announcing “masters’ meetings” , which demonstrates the
transformation of the social network from an advertising
platform to a socio-communication institution. Thus,
the project forms a “digital cluster” where intangible
assets (trust, mutual assistance) are converted into the
social sustainability of the region. An important stage
in the institutionalization of this process is cooperation
with grant platforms, in particular, the involvement of
craftsmen in the “Permanent Competition of Creators”
from the “This is Craft” fund (post from October 20,
2023). Such a mechanism for financing the development
of prototypes of creative products on an irrevocable
basis is a tool for investment authentication of local
craft. This allows microentrepreneurs to overcome the
barrier of transition from amateur hobby to professional
production, while preserving copyright and subjectivity
of the creator. In the context of decolonization, this
demonstrates the transition from resource survival
to the formation of intellectual property within the
regional cluster. In general, posts often emphasize
the authenticity of the ingredients (Zaporizhzhіa
honey, local herbs, clay), which within the decolonial
process performs the function of “geographic
branding”, legitimizing local resources as unique,
nationally identifiable, which destroys the stereotype
of the “peripherality” of regional products. A separate
vector of socio-communication activity of the “Craft
Zaporizhzhia” project is the educational function of
memorializing lost heritage . An example of “digital
commemoration” is the dissemination of the narrative
about the Chateau named after Prince T rubetskoy
(Kherson region) which is a unique object with a
126-year history and was completely plundered during

This case represents the collision of two civilizational
paradigms: creative (craft) , based on generational
inheritance and integration into the global context
(recognition of vintage wines “Velvet of Ukraine” ,
“Pearl of the Steppe” at the international level, supplies
to the British royal court); destructive (imperial) ,
the goal of which is not only the physical destruction
of assets (the blowing up of a warehouse in Gostomel
with 40 thousand bottles), but also the annihilation of
historical memory (the theft of a rare collection that
has been kept since the 19th century). Highlighting
such stories within the Zaporizhzhia craft community
transforms the Instagram page from a trading platform
into a platform of solidarity and cognitive resistance.
Emphasizing that “the winery survived the First and
Second World Wars” creates a historical perspective of
invincibility. This forms the recipient’s belief that the
restoration of the winery is not just a business plan, but
part of a nationwide process of deoccupation and
decolonization of the symbolic space of Ukraine .
Thus, craft production is marked not as private property,
but as a national heritage for which the struggle
continues. In addition, it is worth mentioning that
Zaporizhzhia and Kherson are adjacent regions that are
in a single modern military-historical context, and such
a narrative demonstrates regional unity.
The particular importance belongs to the analysis of
the motivational factors of craftsmen's participation in
public events (fairs) in the first months of the full-scale
invasion (post dated June 23, 2022). The theses put in
the motivational post allow us to distinguish several
levels of socio-communicative rehabilitation through
craft:
Sensory and emotional authentication . The thesis
that “goods want to be touched” in a scientific context
means a return to a tactile, “living” reality that has been
destroyed by the digital isolation of the epidemic period
and the stress of war. It is a mechanism for restoring
trust in the world around us through material objects.
T ransformation from consumer to “accomplice”.
The offline meeting of the craftsman and the buyer at
the fair is defined as a process of co-creation of demand.
Unlike algorithmic online sales, a “live” dialogue arises
here, where the awakening of curiosity about the local
product becomes an act of solidarity.
The formation of a “professional caste” as a tool
for survival. Post emphasizes the emergence of a “sense
of caste” and horizontal connections between craftsmen
(information sharing, joint trade). Within the framework
of a decolonial approach, this can be interpreted as the
creation of “local networks of resilience” that replace the
destroyed global logistical and corporate chains.
Collective visual representation . The involvement
of professional photographers into creating content at
the fair (“someone is photographing you with love”)
demonstrates a collective effort to create a positive
image of the frontline city. This turns the fair into
a media event that broadcasts a signal to the world
about the subjectivity and indomitability of the local
community.
Thus, the fair in June 2022 at the CITY MALL
Zaporizhzhia shopping mall became not only a trading
platform, but a form of social therapy and institutional
self-affirmation of the city's craft community. In fact,
the theory of A. Koontz and N. G. Chapman (Koontz
& Chapman, 2019), mentioned above, highlights in
this case the ideas of “ Authenticating Identity Claims”:
the craftsman at the fair “authenticates” himself to
the buyer, and the buyer accepts his belonging to the
community through a purchase.
A comparative analysis of the dynamics of queries for
the marker “Craft Zaporizhzhia” revealed significant
differences in the perception of the local initiative
compared to nationwide projects. In contrast to the
stable interest in the concept of “Craft Christmas” , the
local case currently does not demonstrate a formed target
query. The results of the Keyword Research tool (Figure
8) recorded the presence of semantic asymmetry: the
associative range of users is limited to narrow-profile
categories of goods (for example, “craft beer” , “barrel
of Zaporizhzhia”), which does not correspond to the
socio-cultural content of the initiative itself.
This situation indicates a lack of institutional
communication at the local level and the need to
develop a “consumer education” strategy. The lack of
a direct correlation between the name of the initiative
and search intent highlights the problem of brand-
category blurring. This highlights the need to intensify
media presence and use additional storytelling tools
to separate the cultural craft product from the mass
consumption segment.
4. Conclusions
Empirical research using SEO metrics has revealed an
uneven formation of the craft narrative: while national
initiatives demonstrate sustainable reputational
capitalization, local projects (the “Craft Zaporizhzhia”
case) face semantic asymmetry  and a deficit in
categorical identification . This underscores the
necessity of transitioning from individual advertising
strategies to the formation of network clusters, which
provide “umbrella trust” and the collective promotion
of authentic meanings.
The analysis of socio-communicative technologies for
the capitalization of craft production within the context
of wartime and decolonial discourse allows for the
formulation of the following key theses:
1. T ransformation of the Communication
Paradigm. In conditions of crisis and war, the
reputational capital of craft brands is based not on
transactional benefits but on a relational model – the
co-creation of meanings and identity. Decolonial optics

2. Effectiveness of Cross-Sectoral Collabo-

The use of “digital incubation” tools (advent

capitalization of intangible heritage, transforming it
into a contemporary market asset.
3. Clustering as a Survival T ool . The experience
of the “Craft Zaporizhzhia” initiative confirms that the
network integration of local producers is an effective
innovation that replaces outdated vertical management
models. The formation of craft clusters ensures an
“umbrella trust effect”  and the distribution of
communication costs, which is critical for preserving
the economic agency  of territories in frontline
zones.
4. Cognitive and Memorial Functions of Craft .
Contemporary Ukrainian craft serves as a “social
archive of resilience”. Narratives of lost heritage (the
Chateau T rubetskoi case) and the return to offline
interaction through fairs (the CITY MALL case)
mark local spaces as “ours” , controlled, and vibrant.
This implements a strategy of visual and gastronomic
decolonization, dismantling the imperial myth of the
“peripherality” of Ukrainian culture and economy.
5. Development Prospects . The capitalization
of intangible assets through craft requires the further
implementation of systemic approaches to  investment
authentication. Creating platforms that combine
grant support for prototypes (the “T se Kraft” case)
with professional SMM support is a strategic task for
strengthening national security and integrating Ukraine
into the global market as a subject of unique intellectual
and cultural products.
