Abstract
Aim/Purpose: This study addresse d the intensifying competition faced by Thai cosmetics retailers
from price-driven online platforms with variable product reliability. It examine d how service quality,
trust, store atmosphere, location, pricing, and promotion influence customer satisfaction and loyalty,
informing strategies for sustaining competitive advantage in Thailand’s cosmetics retail sector.

Introduction/Background: This study examine d how the 7Ps marketing mix influences customer
satisfaction and customer loyalty in Thailand’s cosmetics retail sector, with satisfaction serving as a
mediating mechanism. In the context of sustained industry growth and increasing competition from
e-commerce and omnichannel platforms, understanding how product quality, service delivery, s tore
environment, accessibility, and value perception shape satisfaction and loyalty is critical for sustaining
competitive advantage.

Methodology: This study utilized a quantitative, cross -sectional survey design to empirically test the
impact of the 7Ps marketing mix on customer satisfaction and customer loyalty. Data were collected
from walk-in customers at Casual Beauty, a boutique cosmetics retailer in suburban Bangkok, using
structured, self-administered questionnaires, with the sample size determine d by Cochran’s (1977)
formula. The instrument applied Likert-scale measures, and data were analyzed using SPSS through
descriptive statistics, reliability testing (Cronbach’s alpha), correlation analysis, multicollinearity
assessment (VIF),  Heterotrait-Monotrait Ratio (HTMT), Fornell-Larcker criterion , and multiple
regression analysis, including mediation testing based on the approach of Baron and Kenny (1986).

Findings: The 7Ps marketing mix and customer satisfaction significantly influence d customer loyalty
in the cosmetics retail context, with customer satisfaction serving as a mediating mechanism.  The
measurement scale demonstrate d high reliability ; no mul ticollinearity was found. Customer
satisfaction exhibited a strong positive effect on loyalty. Among the 7Ps, product quality emerged as
the most influential determinant of customer satisfaction, while price and process were not significant
predictors. Direct effects reveal ed that price, promotion, and physical evidence significantly
influenced customer loyalty. Overall, the results underscore d customer satisfaction as the primary
pathway through which marketing mix strategies translated into sustained customer loyalty.

Contribution/Impact on Society: These findings contribute to the marketing and retail literature by
empirically demonstrating the central mediating role of customer satisfaction in translating the 7Ps
marketing mix into sustained customer loyalty. The findings provide practical guidance for retail
managers to prioritize product quality, store atmosphere, staff competence, accessibility, and
targeted promotional and pricing strategies in strengthening long -term customer relationships.
Additionally, the results highlight societal relevance  by reinforcing consumer trust through product
safety, transparent pricing, and service standards, while supporting policymakers in enhancing retail
competitiveness and consumer protection.

Recommendations: The empirical findings suggest that cosmetics retailers should adopt an integrated
marketing approach that prioritizes product quality, store atmosphere, and staff competence , while

Introduction
This study aimed to examine the effect of customer satisfaction on customer loyalty ; assess the
influence of the 7Ps marketing mix as antecedents of customer satisfaction; analyze the mediating
role of customer satisfaction in the relationship between the 7Ps and customer  loyalty; and provide
recommendations for improving satisfaction and loyalty in the cosmetics retail sector. The study is
expected to contribute to a better understanding of customer behavior in Thailand’s cosmetics retail
market by explaining how marketing mix strategies shape satisfaction and loyalty outcomes, thereby
offering insights that are relevant for both academics and practitioners

Background
Thailand’s cosmetics industry has grown steadily and become a significant player in international
trade. In 2021, the industry generated approximately USD 4.2 billion in revenue, driven by a growing
middle class, rising disposable incomes, and shifting consumer preferences (International Trade
Administration, 2021). The market is dominated by skincare products (42%), followed by hair products
(15%), soaps and hygiene (14%), oral and dental care (12%), and makeup (12%). It has grown faster
than the overall economy, highlighting its importance in the retail sector (Krungthai COMPASS, 2023).
The Thai cosmetics market is highly competitive, with both physical stores and rapidly expanding
digital platforms. E-commerce and omnichannel strategies have reshaped consumer purchasing
behavior on physical retailers (Grewal et al., 2013). As post-pandemic recovery and tourism demand
rise, competition has intensified further (Krungthai COMPASS, 2023). Retailing has shifted from a
transactional to an experience-driven approach, where customer satisfaction and loyalty depend on
service quality and in-store experiences (Grewal et al., 2013; Hameli, 2018).
In this context, physical cosmetics retailers in Thailand must differentiate themselves through
effective marketing mix strategies. Understanding ho w f actors such as product quality, store
environment, service quality, accessibility, and perceived value influence customer satisfaction and
repeat purchase is therefore essential for cosmetics retailers seeking to maintain competitiveness and
achieve sus tainable growth. However, it remains unclear which factors strongly drive customer
satisfaction and loyalty in the Thai cosmetics retail context. The mediating role of customer
satisfaction remains underexplored, creating challenges for prioritizing strategy.

Objectives
1. To ascertain the effect of customer satisfaction on customer loyalty.
2. To assess the influence of the antecedents of the 7Ps on customer satisfaction.
3. To study the mediation effect of customer satisfaction on the 7Ps and customer loyalty.
4. To provide recommendations for improving satisfaction and loyalty in cosmetics retail.

Literature Review
The 7Ps marketing mix and the SERVQUAL model served as theoretical foundations for explaining
how marketing strategies and service quality affect customer perceptions.

Customer Satisfaction
Customer satisfaction reflects customers’ evaluations and emotions following the comparison of
perceived performance with expectations (Oliver, 1980; Day, 1984). It is also defined as a judgment of
performance and feelings of pleasure or disappointment ( Stank et al., 1997 ; Zondiros et al., 2007;
Kotler & Keller, 2018 ). Satisfaction influences future behavior and firm performance, as satisfied
customers are more likely to repurchase, engage in positive word -of-mouth, and resist switching
(Fornell et al., 1996; Kaura, 2013). It is also associated with  increased loyalty, willingness to pay
premium prices, and opportunities for upselling and cross-selling (Cronin & Taylor, 1992; Zeithaml et
al., 1996; Bachmann & Jodlbauer, 2023; Vo-Thanh et al., 2024). Overall, customer satisfaction is both
an outcome of service performance and a driver of loyalty and business sustainability.

Process as Service Quality
Process is the structured delivery of services that shapes customer experiences and service quality
(Hirankitti et al., 2009; Saidani & Sudiarditha, 2019; Xie, 20 20; Alwinie et al., 2024). Service quality
reflects how well performance meets customer expectations (Parasuraman et al., 1988; Zeithaml et
al., 1996; Saleh et al., 2021). In the input-process-output view, customers evaluate quality through the
service process itself  (Dobrowolska & Ossowska, 2016 ; Kowalik & Klimecka -Tatar, 2018). Process
evaluations depend on expectations and prior experiences ( Anwar & Abdullah , 2021). Effective
processes improve efficiency, trust, satisfaction, loyalty, repeat patronage, and word -of-mouth
(Cronin & Taylor, 1992; Chana et al., 2021). In Thai retail and emerging markets, speed, accuracy, and
attentiveness influence purchasing decisions and loyalty ( Chotivanich, 2014; Ngo & Nguyen, 2016;
Romyanan, 2021 ; Worapaknapis, 2023). However, in standardized or premium settings, process is
often a baseline expectation (Romyanan, 2021).

People Assume the Role of Trust
People in the services marketing mix refer to employees involved in service delivery who influence
customer perceptions and evaluations (Kotler, 1997). Trust is the belief that service providers act with
competence, integrity, and care under uncertainty (Gefen & Straub, 2004). In service settings, trust
develops through employee –customer interactions that shape the service expe rience (Ko, 2024).
Based on the trust paradigm, customers evaluate employees through ability, benevolence, and
integrity, making trust an outcome of employee behavior (Mayer et al., 1995). Employee interactions
strongly affect satisfaction and loyalty (Khu mnualthong, 2015), while trust strengthens emotional
bonds, repeat purchasing, and long -term relationships ( Putsom et al., 2019 ; Suwannarat, 2023). In
cosmetics retail, trust also influences loyalty and customer perceptions (Pappas et al., 2014; Widdo &
Murwatiningsih, 2019). Thai studies have further show n that politeness, product knowledge,
enthusiasm, and attentiveness shape customer decisions and brand perceptions (Romyanan, 2021).
Overall, people contribute to satisfaction and loyalty through trust in service interactions.

Location Plays the Role of Place
Place refers to physical and virtual locations where customers access goods or services, including
accessibility, visibility, and distribution channels (Kotler & Armstrong, 2008). Its purpose is to maximize
convenience through stores and online channels to  increase engagement (Kotler & Keller, 2006).
Research shows that location influences customer traffic, satisfaction, and purchasing behavior,
particularly through accessibility, visibility, and proximity to transportation (Iqbal et al., 2022; Rashid
et al., 2022; Channamallu et al., 2024). Strategic location also requires consideration of competition,
regulation, and environmental quality (Heizer & Render, 2013). In cosmetics retail, accessible stores
and online channels enhance convenience and broaden customer reach, though the impact of place
varies by product type and consumer behavior (Worapaknapis, 2023).

Product Quality
Product quality is the ability of a product to meet or exceed customer expectations in design,
performance, reliability, and value (Kotler, 2013). It is assessed by how effectively the product satisfies
customer needs, performs its functions, and delivers perceived value, thereby shaping customer
evaluations, satisfaction, and purchase decisions. Product quality is a key determinant of satisfaction
and loyalty, as high -quality products enhance trust, strengthen brand perception, and reduce
switching in a competitive market (Rashid et al., 2022). In the service context, including cosmetics
retail, product quality extends to service quality, where professional and reliable delivery shapes
customers’ evaluations and experience s (Alwinie et al., 2024).  Correspondingly, product q uality in
cosmetics retail includes effectiveness, ingredient safety, brand credibility, packaging, and suitability
for consumer needs, all of which influence purchase decisions and satisfaction (Worapaknapis, 2023).

Price
Price represents the value customers exchange for a product or service, including monetary costs
and non-monetary sacrifices such as time, effort, and perceived risk (Kotler & Armstrong, 2008). It also
serves as a signal of quality and value, shaping perceptions, satisfaction, and loyalty (Kotler & Keller,
2016). From a value-perception perspective, fair and value-aligned pricing enhances satisfaction and
trust, whereas perceived unfairness reduces confidence and encourages switching (Xia et al., 2004;
Ting, 2013). Accordingly, v alue-oriented pricing  enhances satisfaction and is  effective for price -
sensitive segments (Rashid et al., 2022; Fajrin & Aprianingsih, 2024). However, low prices alone do not
guarantee satisfaction if product quality is insufficient, highlighting the need to align price with
perceived value (Muhtarom et al., 2024).

Promotion
Promotion refers to communication activities such as advertis ing, sales promotion, public
relations, personal selling, direct marketing, and digital channels used to inform and persuade
customers about a brand’s value (Kotler & Keller, 2016). It shapes customer perceptions, stimulates
interest, and influences purcha se behavior and satisfaction (Kotler & Keller, 2016). Effective
promotion increases brand visibility, strengthens brand identity, and enhances engagement and
purchase intention (Romyanan, 2021). In cosmetics retail, mass media, social media, influencers, and
loyalty programs improve perceived value and reinforce brand positioning (Fajrin & Aprianingsih,
2024). However, promotional effectiveness depends on context, as service quality, price, and location
may be more influential in premium or service-oriented settings (Chana et al., 2021; Romyanan, 2021).
Therefore, promotion supports customer attraction and retention when integrated into broader
value-creation strategies.

Physical Evidence as the Role of Store Atmosphere
Physical evidence refers to tangible , sensory elements in service environments,  such as layout,
décor, lighting, cleanliness, music, and aroma (Kotler & Keller, 2006). In cosmetics retail, these
elements shape first impressions, purchase decisions, brand identity, and service delivery (Oliver ,
1980; Booms & Bitner, 1981). According to Servicescape Theory, customers interpret physical
environments as stimuli that influence cognitive, emotional, and behavioral responses (Bitner, 1992;
Mari & Poggesi, 2013). Thus, effectiveness depends on custome r perception. Well -designed
atmospheres enhance perceived value, satisfaction, engagement, retention, and support premium
pricing (Khumnualthong, 2015; Munawaroh & Simon, 2023). In cosmetics retail, layout, décor, and
ambiance influence perceived quality, purchase behavior, and repeat patronage (Chana et al., 2021;
Romyanan, 2021; Worapaknapis, 2023; Alwinie et al., 2024).

despite alternatives (Oliver, 1980). Loyal customers show consistent repurchase behavior, lower price

Adopted Framework
In this study, a conceptual framework based on the 7Ps marketing mix model was adopted (Booms
& Bitner, 1981) to examine the influence of marketing and service elements on Customer Satisfaction
and Customer Loyalty  in Thailand’s cosmetics retail sector. The seven dimensions of the 7Ps are
Product Quality, Price, Promotion, Place, People (as trust), Process (as service quality), and Physical
Evidence. The 7Ps function as independent variables affecting both satisfaction and loyalty, with
Customer Satisfaction serving as a mediating variable. The framework explains how marketing
strategies and service delivery mechanisms collectively drive loyalty outcomes (Oliver, 1980; Kotler &
Keller, 2016). The conceptual model is presented in Figure 1.

Research Design
In the study, a quantitative research design was used to test hypotheses and examine relationships
among measurable variables (Creswell & Creswell, 2018). Structured data were collected and
statistically analysed to assess relationships between the 7Ps, customer satisfac tion, and loyalty. A
cross-sectional survey captured perceptions at a single point in time (Bryman & Bell, 2015), a common
approach in consumer and service research on satisfaction and loyalty (Parasuraman et al., 1988).

Population and Sampling
The target  population consist ed of walk -in customers at Casual Beauty, a boutique cosmetics
retailer in suburban Bangkok. Given the store’s diverse clientele, the study included participants aged
17 years or above across various income levels. Thus, the size of this study's population was indefinite.
Hence, to determine an appropriate sample size, Cochran’s formula (1977) for estimating proportions
in large populations was applied.

Data Collection Method
Data were gathered through structured, self-administered surveys conducted on-site using iPads.
To improve response rates, participants received small incentives such as product samples or discount
coupons, a method proven effective in survey research (Dillman  et al., 2014). The surveys were
conducted over one month, with sessions scheduled on various days and times to ensure a
representative sample of customers.

Data Analysis
Data were analyzed using SPSS. Descriptive statistics summarized respondent characteristics,
while reliability was a ssessed using Cronbach’s alpha (α > .70) (Nunnally & Bernstein, 1994).
Discriminant validity was evaluated using the Fornell –Larcker criterion (Fornell & Larcker, 1981) and
the HTMT ratio, with a threshold of .90 indicating acceptable validity (Henseler et al., 2015). Pearson
correlations and Variance Inflation Factor (VIF) v alues were examined to assess relationships and
multicollinearity (Hair et al., 2006). Multiple regression analysis was conducted to test the hypotheses
and evaluate the mediating role of customer satisfaction using the Baron and Kenny (1986) approach.

Discussion
This discussion links the findings to marketing theory, identifying both expected and unexpected
patterns. Product quality emerged as the strongest determinant of customer satisfaction, followed by
physical evidence and store  experience, reinforcing the importance of core performance and retail
environment. Promotion and price exerted significant direct effects on customer loyalty beyond
satisfaction, suggesting that value perceptions and incentives influence loyalty independently. The
non-significant effect of process and the asymmetric role of price indicated that 7Ps elements operate
through differentiated mechanisms rather than uniform pathways in shaping satisfaction and loyalty
within cosmetics retail.
Although several HTMT values exceed ed the recommended thresholds, this was theoretically
consistent with the 7Ps framework, in which elements function as interdependent components of an
integrated service system rather than as independent constructs (Booms & Bitner, 1981). Service
marketing theory suggests that customers evaluate offerings holistically, simultaneously processing
product attributes, environmental cues, and marketing comm unications (Kotler & Keller, 2016). This
reinforces Servicescape Theory, which posits that physical environment dimensions are perceived as
a unified configuration, making constructs such as physical evidence and place empirically difficult to
distinguish (Bitner, 1992; Baker et al., 1994). The strong association between product quality and
customer satisfaction reflects the well-established idea that perceived quality is a primary antecedent
of satisfaction and is often evaluated as a global judgment (Parasuraman, 1988; Cronin & Taylor, 1992;
Fornell et al., 1996). Similarly, place influences perceived product quality through environmental cues,
while place and promotion jointly shape customer perceptions as part of an integrated retail
experience (Baker et  al., 1994; Grewal et al., 20 13). In this study, the salon –retail hybrid context
further intensified these effects, as customers experience d product performance, environment, and
promotional activities simultaneously, leading to expected empirical overlap among constructs.

repurchase intentions and lowers switching tendencies, reinforcing its central role in sustaining loyalty

Customer satisfaction drives positive word-of-mouth, increased spending, and sustained retailer
commitment across retail contexts (Mittal et al., 2023). Its strong mediating role further supports its
function as the key mechanism linking marketing and service factors to loyalty (Elgarhy & Mohamed, 2023).
Collectively, these findings reaffirm satisfaction as the primary pathway to loyalty in cosmetics retail.

7Ps Marketing Mix and Customer Satisfaction (H2)
These results confirmed partial support for H2 (H2a–H2g), demonstrating that only certain 7Ps
dimensions significantly influence customer satisfaction. Product quality  (H2a) was the strongest
predictor (β = .417), followed by physical evidence (H2g), place (H2c), promotion (H2d), and people (H2e).
This highlights the dominance of outcome -related and experiential factors in sha ping satisfaction,
consistent with earlier findings showing that product performance drives perceived value and trust
(Rashid et al., 2022; Alwinie et al., 2024; Muhtarom et al., 2024). The significance of physical evidence,
place, promotion, and people further underscores the roles o f Servicescape, accessibility,
communication, and interpersonal interaction in forming positive satisfaction judgments
(Khumnualthong, 2015; Fajrin & Aprianingsih, 2024).
In contrast, the non-significant effects of price (H2b) and process (H2f) suggest that these elements
function as baseline expectations rather than drivers of satisfaction. Prior studies have indicated that
price affects satisfaction mainly when fairness is uncertain, while standardized processes are typically
taken for granted (Chana et al., 2021; Romyanan, 2021; Muhtarom et al., 2024). This aligns with the
study context, where shops have transitioned into a hybrid retail-salon model with frequent discounts,
making price an expected , rather than a differentiating attribute. Consistent with discount retail
research, anticipated low prices do not enhance satisfaction (Powers et al., 2018)
In the Thai cosmetics market, intense competition and widespread information-search behavior
encourage active price comparison, thereby reinforcing price transparency and reducing its role as a
driver of satisfaction  (Thanasrichatthon, 2023). At the same time, Thai consumers are highly value -
conscious and promotion -driven, further positioning price as an expected baseline rather than a
source of additional satisfaction ( Milieu Insight , 202 5). Empirical evidence has also shown that
experiential factors such as packaging and perceived quality exert a stronger effect on satisfaction
than price (Taghipour & Loh, 2017)
However, price remains significant for customer loyalty, indicating that while it does not elevate
satisfaction, it shapes perceived value, and influences repeat purchase and switching behavior. This
suggests price operates more strongly at the behavioral level, while satisfaction is driven primarily by
core performance and experiential factors.

Customer Satisfaction as a Mediator between the 7Ps and Customer Loyalty (H 3)
The findings demonstrate that customer satisfaction mediates the association between the 7Ps
marketing mix and customer loyalty. After introducing satisfaction, the direct effects of product
quality and physical evidence were substantially reduced, with pr oduct quality becoming a non -
significant and physical evidence  weakening, suggesting their influence operates primarily through
satisfaction. This supports prior studies identifying satisfaction as the key mechanism linking service
attributes to loyalty outcomes (Molinillo et al., 2022).
Nevertheless, the non-significant effects of price and process suggest that these elements serve
as baseline expectations rather than drivers of customer satisfaction, particularly when pricing is
acceptable and service procedures are standardized. Prior research has indicated that price enhances
satisfaction primarily when it signals fairness or value; however, its impact diminishes when it is
aligned with expected quality (Muhtarom et al., 2024). Consistent with studies in beauty, retail, and

Conclusion
This study confirmed that the 7Ps marketing mix and customer satisfaction significantly influence
customer loyalty in the cosmetics retail context. Key elements, particularly product quality,
promotion, price, and physical evidence, shape loyalty outcomes. Among these, customer satisfaction
serves as the primary driver,  reinforcing repeat purchases, positive word-of-mouth, and long -term
relationships.
Customer satisfaction plays a key mediating role, translating  7Ps marketing mix  strategies into
sustained customer loyalty . While some elements directly influence loyalty, their effects are
significantly strengthened through satisfaction. The reduction in  direct effect , after including
satisfaction, indicates that it functions as the central mechanism translating marketing efforts into
sustained loyalty.
In conclusion, cosmetics retailers should adopt an integrated 7Ps approach that prioritizes product
quality and customer experience, while strategically pricing and promoting. By aligning these elements
to enhance customer satisfaction, firms can strengthen emotional connections and achieve
sustainable competitive advantage. These findings offer practical guidance for developing effective
strategies in highly competitive retail environments.
