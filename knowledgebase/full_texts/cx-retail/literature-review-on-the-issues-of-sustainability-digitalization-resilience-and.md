Abstract
Background: The sphere of tourism and hospitality is essential for job creation, income
generation, cultural exchange, and development in South Asia, although it is susceptible to
risks of climate, economic, health, infrastructural, and technological nature.
Methods: The systematic literature review reviews peer-reviewed literature with DOI numbers
mostly published during 2021 -2025 years and centers on the South Asian countries and the
sphere of tourism and hospitality management in South Asia. Literature searches using
scholarly se arch engine and database search engines were performed to select appropriate

Introduction
Tourism and hospitality ha ve been gaining importance in the socio -economic wellbeing of
South Asia. The region contains different types of tourism systems: large Indian market,
mountain tourism in Nepal and Bhutan, coastal and cultural tourism in Sri Lanka and
Bangladesh, luxury is land tourism in Maldives, heritage, nature, and adventure tourism in
Pakistan. They all differ greatly; nevertheless, they share common problems in management.
Firstly, it is necessary to attract tourists, to provide high service quality, to protect the resources,
to manage human resources, to develop technologies, and to benefit the community from the
activities of tourism. Moreover, recently, scholars have found that after the crisis, there could
be rapid changes in the demand for tourism and that the tou rism companies require better
methods of managing uncertainties (Kaushal & Srivastava, 2021; Bandara et al., 2021; Radha
et al., 2026).
COVID-19 crisis has increased the problem. Indian researchers found the high need in human
resource management, hygiene,  continuity, and crisis preparedness in the hospitality and
tourism organizations (Kaushal & Srivastava, 2021; Rajnarayanan, et al., 2026; Shrestha et al.,
2024). In Sri Lanka, the stakeholders declared the pandemic an additional shock to the industry
that suffered from the consequences of war, the 2004 tsunami, and the 2019 Easter attacks
(Bandara et al., 2021). Pakistani scientists concentrated on learning the lessons from the
pandemic and improving the preparedness to the possible disruptions in the futu re (Baloch et
al., 2022). Consequently, it is clear that the management of tourism should not focus only on
tourists anymore. The focus should be put on the questions of resilience, risks, human
resources, and continuity of operation during the disruption.

The digital transformation has affected greatly the tourists' search for, comparison of, booking,
paying for, and assessment of the tourism and hospitality services. Indian scholars point to the
growing role of technologies, online reviews, influencers, a nd customer engagement and
emphasize the significance of human interaction in these processes (Singh & Munjal, 2021).
The research of the hotel visitors of South Asia showed the positive effect of awareness, brand,
digital information, price, and variety on hotel decisions made through digital platforms (Faizal
et al., 2024). The researchers of Bangladesh demonstrated the effects of new payment
technologies on tourist loyalty due to privacy, security, and customer satisfaction (Al Karim et
al., 2023). Therefore, the digitalization affects marketing of tourism services and management
of hospitality services simultaneously.
Another issue, which is getting popular in the research, is the question of sustainability.
Tourism resources in South Asia are natural an d cultural; however, most of the destinations
suffer from the problems of waste, water shortage, energy consumption, biodiversity loss,
congestion, and community concerns (Karki et al., 2024). In a panel study of South Asian
countries, the authors have sho wn the correlation between tourism, urbanization, and carbon
emissions; thus, tourism development cannot be analyzed separately from other environmental
and urban processes (Bin Amin & Aftabi Atique, 2021). In Sri Lanka, the sustainable practices
of hotels  and tourism companies receive more and more attention from researchers.
Meanwhile, in Bangladesh, the influence of environment value and employee service quality
on tourists' satisfaction and environmentally responsible behavior is investigated (Iddawala et
al., 2024; Sahabuddin et al., 2024). In India, the scientists conduct the researches concerning
green hotels, sustainable tourism practices, and the factors of tourists' sustainable behavior
(Wani et al., 2025; Naik & Chanda, 2025).
Also, community participation is going to be important for the future of the region. Tourism is
able to generate job opportunities and markets for local entrepreneurs but not all parts of the
society benefit from it equally. Literature in Bangladesh underlines the significanc e of social
and local development aspects of sustainable tourism while the literature in Nepal shows that
community-based homestays may promote rural development and cultural education provided
that there are certain local conditions and policy supporting such initiatives (Kumar et al., 2023;
Can et al., 2024). Community participation was also identified as an approach to strengthen
sustainable tourism and marketing in India (Sharma & Hasti, 2024). All these facts point at a
management approach where local community is seen as a partner in development of the
destination.
It is necessary to maintain service quality despite the growing importance of technology and
sustainability (Anas et al., 2025; Radha et al., 2025). Hospitality sector creates an experience
and the guest evaluates such aspects as cleanliness, reliability, responsiveness, communication,
facilities, safety, value and emotional experience (Mahat et al., 2024; Shrestha et al., 2025;
Adhikari et al., 2026). In Nepal, researchers developed a contex t-specific SERVQUAL scale
to measure service quality of tourist -standard resorts, finding out that generic measures of
service quality should be adapted to a specific context (Adhikari et al., 2023). In Bangladesh,
the perception of service quality among t he guests in star hotels was found to be generally

and policymakers. The purpose of this paper is to analyze how recent research explains the
main management priorities of tourism and hospitality in South Asia where the evidence base
is the strongest and identify the gaps in literature.
Research Objective
The main objective of this systematic literature review is the synthesis of recent empirical and
review literature on tourism and hospitality management in South Asia. There are four specific
objectives of this paper: (1) to determine the key themes in recent tourism and hospitality
management research; (2) to analyze the way how sustainability, digital tran sformation,
resilience, service quality and community participation are studied in South Asia; (3) to
compare the focus of the research in different countries and destination types and (4) to identify
gaps and further directions of the research and management.

Review of Literature
In the modern research, the interconnection of the different aspects of tourism and hospitality
management is evident. Demand forecasting, destination marketing, hotel operations,
employees' management, digital technologies and s ustainability practices and community
development influence each other. This section analyzes the literature on the themes that are
identified in the selected studies.
Tourism demand, destination competitiveness, and market management
The concept of demand management is an integral element of tourism management. In Nepal,
Bam (2023) stated that the number of days tourists spent in Pokhara was associated with visit
frequency, nationality, age, education and expenses. This point is extremely important because
the number of tourists is not an indicator of the economic value of tourism. Therefore, even if
there are many tourists in a destination, its local economic value may be low because tourists
spend few days in a destination and do not spend much money (Adh ikari & Neupane, 2025).
Thus, it is necessary to consider the visitor profile, duration of visit, expenses, seasonality and
tourism benefits distribution.

Another element of management is forecasting, which allows making reasonable decisions.
According to the findings of Paudel et al. (2024), who examined the seasonal forecasting
models based on Nepal's monthly tourists' arrivals for the period of 1992 -2023, these models
may be helpful in predicting the tourist flow. The results are valuable for hotels, tra nsport
companies, attractions, destination authorities and other stakeholders because the significant
change in the flow of visitors will affect the number of employees, inventories, the capacity of
hotels, food supplies, etc.
Another topic related to tour ism management is the connection between demand and
destination image. As it has been proved in Pakistan, destination attributes affect tourists'
intentions through destination image (Rauf et al., 2024). In another Pakistani study, destination
image, service quality, electronic word of mouth and social media usage were recognized as
factors that affect tourist loyalty (Rahman et al., 2023). Consequently, the competitiveness of
the destination depends not only on the natural beauty and other physical charact eristics of
destinations, but on the tourists' perception of the destination safety, service, information,
authenticity and other features.
Sustainability and environmental management
One of the topics discussed by South Asian tourism literature is sustain ability, which is
considered frequently. The panel analysis by Bin Amin and Aftabi Atique (2021) indicates that
tourism is connected with urbanization and carbon emissions in South Asia. Thus, the results
confirm the idea that the development of tourism sh ould be managed within urban and
environmental systems. Besides consideration of hotels and tourist attractions, destination
authorities should take into account transport, energy consumption, waste production, land use,
water consumption and other elements.
The similar conclusion may be drawn from hotel -level researches. For example, according to
the findings of Iddawala et al. (2024) about sustainability practices of chain hotels in Sri Lanka,
hotels adopted practices in all three spheres (economic, socia l and environmental), but
managers considered sustainability from the environmental perspective only. Chain hotels had
more opportunities than small hotels to implement sustainability practices and integrate them
into the SDGs because of their additional resources and knowledge.
Green practices and ecological conscious consumption were examined by Jayanetti et al.
(2024) in regard to their influence on hotel business in Sri Lanka. The study emphasizes the
importance of green practices as the factor that can  help to attract environmentally conscious
consumers and make them to choose responsible hotels. In India, similar results have been
obtained. Thus, Wani et al. (2025) analyzed the link between hotel green practices and decision
making, and Naik and Chanda  (2025) studied the factors which are related to the sustainable
tourism practices. As it follows from the above-mentioned facts, the sustainability becomes not
only the issue regulated by law but also the market-oriented.
Community and environment aspects are connected to sustainable development. For example,
Kumar et al. (2023) identified the factors which promote sustainable tourism and community
development. Moreover, Khan et al. (2024) found out that environment integrity, social equity,
economic prosperity, and technology adoption are positively associated with the post-pandemic

Digital transformation and technology
Tourism and hotel marketing and operations are affected by the digital transformation. Thus,
Singh and Munjal (2021) have considered the role of digital technology, influencers, reviews,
and customer engagement in tourism and hospitality in India. Furthermore, the authors
underlined that digital tools should not substitute human interaction with customers. Such an
approach is essential in hospitality since customers usually value communication and problem-
solving in hospitality.
From regional perspective, Faizal et al. (2024) analyzed 1,431 customers of hotels in the main
cities of South Asia. The  researchers found that awareness, brand, digital information, price,
and variety positively influence decision making about hotels in the digital space. Thus, it may
be concluded that digital information has become the part of service. Consequently, hotel s
require accurate digital information, reasonable prices, communication, and consistent brand
representation.
The payment technologies change as well. Thus, Al Karim et al. (2023) analyzed the use of the
blockchain-based mobile payment services in hospitality industry in Bangladesh. The findings
revealed that satisfaction and privacy and security mediate the relationship between blockchain
payment services and loyalty intention, but service quality does not mediate the relationship.
The management implicat ion is that the technology adoption should be connected to the
protection of customers' interests.
Service quality and customer experience
Service quality management is one of the management challenges. In Adhikari et al. (2023), a
contextualized SERVQUAL scale was developed to assess the service quality in Nepalese
resorts using modified Delphi. In the article, service quality is considered to be an employee
behavior, cleanliness, facilities, procedures, atmosphere and other components of the customer
experience. Contextualized scale is required since the expectations of the resort clients in the
mountain destination may be different from those of urban business hotel clients.
Customer perceptions towards star-ranking hotels in Bangladesh were analyzed by B howmik
et al. (2024). Positive perceptions were detected regarding the following factors: service
quality, food quality, location and modern facilities. The findings confirm the basic principle
of hotel management, which states that it is important to ensu re the harmony between several
service factors. The good quality room product cannot compensate the poor quality of
communication, cleanliness, slow service, etc.
It was revealed that the link between service quality and sustainability exists. In Sahabuddin et
al. (2024), it was shown that employee service quality and environmental value influenced the
tourist satisfaction in the Sundarbans, which contributed to the tourist loyalty, environmentally
responsible behavior and environmental commitment. Therefor e, sustainability and service

Resilience, crisis management, and human resources
It becomes clear that it is necessary to build the focus on resilience rather than on efficiency
during the pandemic. In Kaushal and Srivastava (2021), human resource management, health
and hygiene, continuity, and other problems were identified based on interviews wi th senior
managers of hospitality and tourism businesses in India. It is important for South Asia because
the majority of tourism companies there are small and have few employees. Multiskilled
professionals will be useful in case of disruptions.
The research in Sri Lanka offers an interesting point of view about employees. According to
Prayag and Dassanayake (2022), the relationships between employee resilience, organizational
resilience and financial performance were revealed among tourism and hospitality
organizations during the impact of COVID -19. Next, in Prayag et al. (2023), it was revealed
that the behaviors of resilient leaders helped to enhance organizational and employee resilience
during the pandemic. Thus, resilience is not only about an emergenc y plan. It is a human
resource and leadership problem.
However, resilience requires learning from previous shocks. Hence, Baloch et al. (2022) have
explored the lessons learned by the industry for recovery and recommend being ready to face
other pandemics in the future. In a similar vein, the experiences that have been gained due to
different shocks have helped in forming the resilience and vulnerabilities of the Sri Lankan
tourism industry (Bandara et al., 2021). Consequently, crisis management for South A sian
managers should consist of financial planning, health and safety planning, workforce
flexibility, communication, relations with suppliers, insurance (if possible) and coordination at
destination level.
Community participation and inclusive tourism
The participation of the community is particularly important for rural, cultural and nature-based
tourism. The experience of Nepal in this area can provide useful insights since there are
community-based homestays in this country. As stated by Can et al. (202 4), homestays were
heavily impacted by the pandemic but managed to redirect their efforts to serving domestic
tourists. The guests appreciated culture, food, possibility to learn something about it, and
hospitality. On the other hand, the owners encountered road, promotion, and attraction issues.
Domestic tourism plays an assisting role when the numbers of international tourists drop,
however, there are infrastructure and marketing challenges to be addressed by the local
companies.
In Bangladesh, Kumar et a l. (2023) have found out that socio -cultural and local development
aspects are vital for sustainable tourism and community development. Furthermore, Sharma
and Hasti (2024) have suggested engaging communities in sustainable tourism marketing and
policy in India. Such papers indicate that the participation of communities is not merely a social
task but also helps to build an authentic image of the destination, generate local support, create
jobs, and make tourism development sustainable in the long run.

However, the empirical management literature on Bhutan and the Maldives is somewhat
scarce. The topic of tourism in Bhutan can be analyzed through the framework of sustainability
and high-value-low-volume tourism. At the same time, the infrastructure and environment of
the Maldives are special in terms of tourism management. According to the findings of the
search, in the future, the research on South Asia should focus more on small tourism economies
and island/mountain destinations, not being biased towards big ones.

Methods
For this systematic review, the methodology of systematic literature review was applied. The
review was done in accordance with PRISMA guidelines, which means that the identification,
screening, selection, and synthesis of studies were done via defining clear inclusion and
exclusion criteria, structured searching and screening process, systematic data extraction and
thematic synthesis (Page et al., 2021). This is the interpretative review and not a meta-analysis
because of large differences in selected studies regarding research design, sample size,
variables, destination, outcomes, and so forth.
Search strategy
Keywords used in searching in cluded "South Asia" AND tourism, "South Asia" AND
hospitality, tourism management AND Nepal, tourism management AND India, tourism AND
Bangladesh, tourism AND Sri Lanka, tourism AND Pakistan, tourism AND Bhutan, tourism
AND Maldives, sustainability, digita l transformation, service quality, resilience, community
tourism, hotel management, tourist satisfaction, destination image, tourism demand, and other
major themes. Additional searches were carried out by visiting the publishers' websites to find
the DOI, publication date, article type, and other details.

The review period was defined as 2021 -2025 due to the necessity to use recent literature
according to the author guidelines and because the issue itself is significantly affected by the
latest developments  in tourism management, post -COVID recovery, climate change, and
technology. The previous studies were used only in order to illustrate a particular
methodological aspect or structure the review. As a result, 24 sources were identified as
relevant: 21 empi rical research articles, two literature/review articles, and one systematic
review reporting guideline. Thus, over 80% of the sources included in the review were
empirical articles with DOI.
Inclusion and exclusion criteria
• Published mainly in 2021-2025, with a preference for the last five years.
• Related to one or more South Asian countries or South Asia as a geographical region
specifically studied.
• Covered topics such as tourism, hospitality, hotels, destination management, tourism
demand, sustainability, digital transformation, resilience, service quality, and community
development.
• Published as peer-reviewed research paper in a journal or scholarly paper in a journal with
DOI.
• Had sufficient data on context of research, methodology and findings.
• Did not include papers without DOI, marketing material, news items, non -scholarly web-
based materials, duplicates, and studies outside South Asian context except for those used
for methodological purpose.
Screening and data extraction
At first, title screening and abstracts were done to determine their relevancy to this systematic
review. Papers that had some relevance were screened at the level of full text or publisher page.
For each paper that was selected based on the review, the following data were  identified:
country or region of research, context in the tourism/hospitality industry, research design,
research question, sample/source of data, main findings, management implications. The
obtained information was compared for all papers. At the same ti me, the application of
qualitative interviews, surveys, structural equation models, time -series models, Delphi
techniques, and case studies made statistical pooling impossible.
Quality and synthesis approach
The research quality was evaluated based on the following five practical questions: is the
research question formulated properly? Does the design of the paper meet the research
question? Is the description of data and sample used in the research proper? Is the method of
analysis used adequate? Are the r esults obtained in the paper able to support the conclusion?
No artificial numerical scores were assigned due to very high variability in designs. However,
the higher weight was given to papers that had a better method and more direct evidence from
South Asia.

The second theme that emerges is the theme of digital transformation. In the South Asian hotel

The next theme that recurs in the studies of the above -mentioned literature is the theme of
service quality. The contextualized SERVQUAL study conducted in Nepal demonstrates the
need to adapt measures of service quality to the local hospitality industry, and the Bangladesh
studies show that the quality of food, facilities, location, and employee service is still an
important factor of the customer satisfaction. As a result, it can be concluded that in spite of all
innovations, fundamental aspects of hospitality remain crucial.
Resilience is the fourth theme which is tightly connected with people. In the Indian and Sri
Lankan studies, employees, leaders and their routine activities become important aspects of
recovery and preparedness. Resilience, creative self-efficacy, leaders' behavior, multiskilling,
hygiene, and crisis procedure become the important components of tourism company
resilience. Thus, resilience should be integrated into human resources management.
Community participation is one of the conditions of inclusive tourism. The studies in Nepal,
Bangladesh, and India show that community participation can be useful for livelihoods, cultural
learning, destinations development, and sustainable tourism. However, it is necessary to
provide community participation with some infrastructure, marketing, access to the market,
training, and involvement in decision making. Otherwise, locals will just supply labor and
culture, but will not get a fair part of tourism profits.

Discussion
In conclusion, the results of the analysis have shown that the area of tourism and hospitality
management in South Asia goes through the stage of transition. While the previous
management strategy was oriented at growth in terms of the number of tourists,
accommodation capacity, and tourism revenues, the contemporary literature recognizes the
importance of growth but considers the environmental, digital, employees', crisis preparedness,
service and local aspects of management.
From growth to sustainable value
The research on sustainability notes that the metrics of destination success should be based on
the value created by them rather than on numbers related to arrivals and income. The research

The sustainable tourism is much broader than reporting practices. The res earch carried out in
Sri Lanka reveals that some managers consider the sustainability mostly as the environmental
issue. However, the approach to the sustainable tourism should be holistic and follow the logic
of triple bottom line approach: economic viabi lity, social value, and environment protection.
The small hotels and homestays need practical tools which would be affordable and user -
friendly. The destination authority should provide the tools for waste and water management,
renewable energy, training, local procurement, and sustainable communication.
Digitalization with a human touch
Digital transformation is necessary for the hotel industry but the literature indicates that
technology should supplement rather than substitute the hospitality industry. T he regional
analysis of hotel decision-making process reveals the importance of the digital information and
price while the research in India emphasizes the continuing importance of interpersonal
communication. The research in Bangladesh adds the privacy a nd security concerns into the
list of management concerns. Therefore, the strategy of digital transformation should cover all
the stages of customer journey: discovery, comparison, booking, payment, arrival, service,
feedback, and post-trip contacts.
The digital transformation of small South Asian businesses does not mean the use of costly
artificial intelligence systems. The use of such existing technologies as mapping, websites,
online booking and payment, fast response, review management, and quality photos could help
in increasing the market accessibility. The governments and tourism associations could help
small business in digital transformation via common platform, training, and standards. This
could reduce the difference between large hotel chains and the communities.
Resilience as a management capability
The resilience research demonstrates that the tourism businesses need to possess some
capabilities before crisis. The resilience of employees and leadership are important factors
because of great reliance of the tourism services on people. Pandemic showed the fragility of
the business models based on job description, poor cash flow, and concentrated source markets.
The hotels and destinations need to create basic crisis management plans, cross -trained
employees, communication strategies, alternative suppliers, and demand analysis.
Destination resilience should also be considered. A single hotel cannot deal with such problems
as traffic, air transport, natural disasters, or any kinds of travel limitations. Some coordination
mechanism is needed between DMOs, governments, transportation firms, hotels, attractions,

Service quality remains the foundation
Despite technological and sustainability innovations, the service quality remains key to
competitiveness. The N epal SERVQUAL study is important since it proves the necessity to
adapt service quality indicators to the local context of the hotel industry. The application of
international standards is not enough – the wishes of the guests regarding their destination
should be revealed.
The scope of the service quality is getting wider. A guest evaluates a hotel on the basis of its
virtual representation before arrival, digital payments at the time of booking, environmentally
friendly practices during his stay in the hotel and customer support when the client complains
about something afterwards. Therefore, service quality management should cover all these
aspects and it is the competitive advantage of hospitality businesses in South Asia which does
not need to cover infrastructure costs.
Community participation and inclusive development
Community tourism and homestays are the examples of the creation of the value outside regular
hotels. The cases of homestays in Nepal show the impact of culture, cuisine, educational
services, and hospitality on the attraction of domestic tourists. Also, community-based tourism
in Bangladesh and India confirms this trend of involving local people in tourism business.
However, it is not enough for community to take part in tourism projects – they should have
proper training, financing, market platform and decision-making mechanisms.
It is necessary for the tourism business model to be inclusive considering the distribution of
profits. Further research should pay more attention to gender issu es, youth employment,
informal economy, indigenous people and SMEs. Usually, the literature pays attention to
tourist satisfaction or business performance, however, the distribution of benefits of tourism
industry is rarely discussed. It would be desirable  to include both competitiveness and local
development into South Asian management model.

Conclusion and Recommendation
Literature review has shown the trends in the research on tourism and hospitality management
in South Asia. There is evidence of South Asian tourism sector moving towards integration in
its approach to tourism management. Sustainable development, digitization, quality of services,
resilience, and participation of the community are no longer separate issues; the y are
interconnected and make a destination competitive.
Sustainability became one of the key aspects of the tourism industry business management.
Companies need to deal with such aspects as energy consumption, water consumption, waste,
biodiversity, cultural resources, community impact and economic sustainability at the same
time. Secondly, the digital transformation influences the decisions of tourists and the process
of services delivery. However, the technology should support hospitality and trust of pe ople.
The third aspect is related to the issue of resilience that depends on such components as
employees, leaders, skills, and cooperation on the destination level. Quality of services is
important as well.
Only one notable gap in the current knowledge base can be observed in the systematic review
process. The academic field on tourism and hospitality management -related matters in South
Asia is quite restricted to certain countries and subject matter areas. While there are a multitude
of empirical studies conducted on the tourism and hospitality industry in India, fewer studies
exist regarding those in Bhutan and Maldives within the chosen literature. Majority of the
studies were mainly cross -sectional and destination -related studies. Also, there is a need for
longitudinal and comparative studies.
Specific recommendations
• Destinations' managers should develop an integrated plan in regard to destination that
includes all factors, such as economy, environment, technology, services, communities, etc.
• Tourism organizations should use sustainable systems that are not complicated in regard to
energy, water, waste, local sourcing, involvement of employees and customer
communication.
• Small and medium-sized tourism organizations should receive digital education concerning
online bookings, digital payments, social media management, review management,
cybersecurity, and data management.
• The hospitality organizations should consider the training of employees and skills
development as a part of crisis management and future preparation.
• The criteria of service quality assessment should be determined in terms of local features,
and not international ones.
