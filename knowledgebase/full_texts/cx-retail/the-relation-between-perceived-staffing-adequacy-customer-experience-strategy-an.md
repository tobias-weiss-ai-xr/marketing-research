Abstract:
Introduction: This study aimed to explore the association between perceived staffing adequacy, Customer Experience Strategy (CES), and
customer loyalty in the Saudi Arabian retail sector and evaluate the moderating influence of the Perceived Vision 2030 Consum er Economic
Agenda (PVCEA). This study uses the Social Exchange Theory (SET) and Institutional Theory as its theoretical framework.
Methodology: A structured survey questionnaire using a five-point Likert scale and purposive sampling method were used to collect data from
488 retail customers in Saudi Arabia, using purposive sampling approach. Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling (PLS-SEM) was
used to determine the relationships.
Results: The results showed a positive and significant association between customer loyalty, perceived staffing adequacy, and Customer
Experience Strategy in Saudi Arabia’s retail industry. Customer Experience Strategy showed a relatively strong association wi th customer
loyalty. Moreover, the Perceived Vision 2030 Consumer Economic Agenda (PVCEA) significantly moderated the association between
perceived staffing adequacy, Consumer Experience Strategy (CES), and customer loyalty.
Conclusion: This study’s novelty lies in its focus on the role of consumer perception of macro -level policy reform (PVCEA) as a boundary
condition in determining the association between staffing adequacy, Customer Experience Strategy and customer loyalty. It com bines social
exchange and institutional theories to provide a basis for understanding how perceptions of institutional reforms affect consumer judgments of
service inputs and differentiate between strategic service signals and generic measures of service qualit y in the changing environment of the
Saudi Arabian retail sector.
Keywords: Customer loyalty, perceived staffing adequacy, CES, Vision 2030, Saudi retail sector, institutional theory.
1. INTRODUCTION
Saudi Arabia’s retail industry is at the crossroads of radical
economic change and consumer market development in the
Middle East. In the last ten years, Saudi Arabia has grown to be
the biggest retail economy in the Gulf Cooperation Council,
with both online and offline consumer spend ing skyrocketing
as the country is urbanising, income levels are increasing, and
the population of young techno -savvy people alters the
consumption trend (Arab News 2026; Consultancy Middle East
2025). As reported by Knight Frank (2025), Saudi retail
expenditure reached SAR 1.41 trillion in 2024, and e -
commerce transaction volume is ra pidly increasing and is
projected to account for  almost 50% of the overall retail
transactions by 2030 as part of the digital economy push of the
Vision 2030 agenda.

This accelerated market growth is part of the larger
PVCEA, which aims to diversify  the Saudi Arabian economy
by focusing on developing the non-oil private sector, consumer
empowerment, and diversifying into a vibrant service
economy, as indicated by Kayani and Alzaid (2025). As
suggested by Saied et al., (2024), Vision 2030 includes a clear
intention to reinforce the Consumer Economy Programme,
become more competitive in the market, and offer enhanced
services in critical sectors such as retail. In the context of these
radical structural changes, there are critic al underexplored
concerns regarding how the adequacy of staffing and the
Consumer Experience Strategy (CES) result in customer
loyalty within this dynamic environment, and to what extent
customer relationships depend  on the perception of Saudi
consumers of PVCEA.
Although the literature, such as Mutambik (2023) and Sun
and Pan (2023) on service quality, agrees that the interaction
between workers has a profound impact on the degree of
customer satisfaction and loyalty, empirical support for the
topic of staffing adequacy in the Middle Eastern retail sector is
scarce and disjointed. Similarly, the Consumer Economy
Agenda of Vision 2030, as a perceived moderating variable in
consumer loyalty relations, has not been given significant
scholarly coverage, even though it has been central to the policy
of retail and consumer expectations , as  Saied et al.,  (2024)
highlighted.
In addition, although the relationship between service
quality and customer loyalty has been extensively studied, there
are several conceptual and theoretical gaps. Studies such as
Zaid and Patwayati (2021)  generally focused on staffing as a
component of overall service quality measures, such as
SERVQUAL, rather than conceptualising perceived staffing
levels as a separate consumer perception that reflects the
sufficiency, availability, and competence of the front line .
Existing studies, such as  Mutambik (2023) and Sun and Pan
(2023), have tended to consider staffing as one component of
broader service quality measures without conceptualising it as
an independent consumer perception that reflects the
sufficiency, availability , and competence of the front line .
Similarly, research such as Makudza (2021) and Pappas et al.,
(2023) on customer experience has analysed it as a
consumption outcome and not a deliberate organisational
ability called Customer Experience Strategy (CES)  that
involves how the organisation’s interactions and experiences
are designed with the customer throughout multiple customer
touchpoints. Hence, the unique value of strategic experience
management in customer loyalty is unclear. In addition, while
some studies have started to consider Vision 2030 as an
emerging theme in policy and economic development literature
(Saied et al.,  2024), prior investigations have not used
consumers perceptions of macro-level institutional reforms as
a contextual mechanism impacting the evaluation of firm-level
service practices in the customer loyalty model. Thus, the main
gap in the literature addressed in this research is conceptual,
focusing on explaining the effect of strategic service signals
and consumer -perceived institutional environments on
customer loyalty in the Saudi retail sector.
In this study, we assume that staffing adequacy is a distinct
construct which is not fully captured in broader service quality
frameworks. In the field of traditional service quality models,
the focus is mainly on consumers' overall assessment of service
performance in terms of reliability, responsiveness, assurance,
empathy, and tangibles. In comparison, perceived staffing
adequacy is a measure of consumers' ratings of the number of
available, competent, and sufficient employees in the front-line
service area of a retailer. Therefore, staffing adequacy is not a
judgement on the quality of service but a separate signal of the
capability and operational resources of the organisation.
Moreover, retailers could evaluate their service quality
similarly, even though the degree to which they felt they had an
adequate workforce was significantly different. Therefore, the
independent analysis of staffing adequacy allows for a more
detailed understanding of consumer perceptions of the level of
investment by the retailer in the frontline service capacity and
how these perceptions impact customer loyalty beyond the
focus of traditional service quality dimensions.
This study addresses these gaps by assessing the effects of
perceived staffing adequacy and Customer Experience Strategy
on customer loyalty among Saudi retail consumers and the
moderating effect of PVCEA on these relationships. This study
contributes to the body of research by reconfiguring the
construction of customer loyalty by providing a consumer
interpretation of macro -level policy reform as a conditioning
factor. By doing so, this study brings Institutional Theory out
of its conventional emphasis on organisational adherence to
regulatory and normative forces to a consumer -oriented
approach, whereby institutional reforms serve as interpretive
frames according to which customers make judgments and
respond behaviourally. Moreover, the conceptualisation of the
two strategic service signals, staffing adequacy and Customer
Experience Strategy , in the present study contrasts with
traditional service quality research, which regards service
attributes as generic SERVQUAL-type dimensions.
The remaining sections of the article are analysed as
follows: This section outlines the theoretical background,
conceptualisation of the constructs , and hypotheses
formulation for the proposed research model. Next, the
methodology section presents  the research design, research
methodology, measurement instruments, sample, and analysis
method. The empirical results are reported in the next section
and discussed in terms of the other literature and theories that
are relevant. Finally, this paper presents a summary of the main
results, theoretical and practical implications, limitations of the
study, and directions for future research.
2. LITERATURE REVIEW
2.1. Theoretical Framework
The theoretical basis of this study combines Social
Exchange Theory (SET) with Institutional Theory to illustrate
the process and situational dynamics that determine customer
loyalty. SET articulates the relationship between positive
service inputs and loyalty results because it is based on mutual
interactions between customers and companies. Customers
consider service encounters and base their choices on perceived
benefits and costs, as proposed by Liu et al., (2026), indicating
that they develop relational commitments when they realise

that the benefits are positive. Personal perceptions of staffing
sufficiency and Customer Experience Strategy  are strategic
service inputs in retail settings  that indicate organisational
investment in service quality. These inputs are perceived by
customers as valuable benefits, and they respond with loyalty
behaviours, as observed by Liu et al., (2026) and PhamThi and
Ho (2024). Similarly, Hsiao et al.,  (2023) highlighted that
frontline service interactions are an important exchange
mechanism that may affect customer loyalty.
However, SET cannot answer the question of when these
service inputs are perceived more favourably. In response to
this limitation, Institutional Theory was developed, where the
evaluations of stakeholders are influenced by the regulatory
and normative environments suggested by Buallay et al.,
(2022). The PVCEA is the conceptual framework of this study
in terms of the consumer-perceived institutional frame. Hsiao
et al.,  (2023) argue that national reform agendas are salient
institutional forces that condition consumer responses, and
Alqahtani et al.,  (2024) propose that alignment with
socioeconomic goals is important for evaluating  services.
Therefore, favourable attitudes toward Vision 2030 reinforce
the ways customers perceive the adequacy and experience
strategies in staffing and increase their correlation with loyalty.
Institutional theory implies that not only does
organisational behaviour change due to institutional
environments, but perceptions of organisational actions are also
highlighted in PhamThi and Ho (2024). In this study, as
signified by Galleli and Amaral (2026) regarding institutional
policies and frameworks, the PVCEA is viewed as a sub -
institutional context at the consumer level that influences
perceptions of retail service. From this perspective, Saied et al.,
(2024) also articulate that consumers who are positive
regarding their perceptions of Vision 2030 have shown higher
levels of optimism towards the reforms of the country, trust in
institutions, and confidence in the modernisation of the retail
sector. They assume that staffing levels prove that retailers are
working to deliver good service, as required by the goals of
national development. Likewise, strategies for improving
customer experience could be interpreted as trustworthy
indications of the company’s dedication to the  consumer-
centred change desired in Vision 2030. This psychological
mechanism, in terms of institutional trust and positive
evaluative framing, legitimises service initiatives and enhances
their perceived  value. Based on this, Institutional Theory
suggests that the moderating effect can be attributed to people's
different perceptions of the overall institutional context in
which they are engaged.
The moderating role of PVCEA is theoretically justified by
the assumption that institutional environments affect not only
organisational behaviour but also the cognitive frames that
enable stakeholders to understand the behaviour of the
organisation. The P VCEA among consumers could lead to
greater confidence in the path of retail modernisation, trust in
institutional reforms, and expectations of better levels of
service for customers (Saied et al., 2024). This also makes them
more likely to consider adequate staffing and strategically
designed customer experiences as indicators of a retailer ’s
commitment to broader national reform objectives and not just
a series of operational activities.
While the central theoretical perspective in this research is
that PVCEA should condition the relationships between
retailers’ service practices and customer loyalty, Institutional
Theory also offers a justification for a direct link between
PVCEA and loyalty. Due to the expected positive portrayal of
Vision 2030, consumers' confidence in the entire retail
environment is likely to increase, meaning that this change will
support improvements in service standards, market
competitiveness, and consumer protection (Saied et al., 2024).
Consumers' trust in individual service events can also
positively impact their general trust in retailers' institutions in
the altered market environment. In this context, customer
loyalty is a function of the assessment of staffing levels  and
customer experience practices, but also an indicator of general
trust and confidence in the sector's future and its
trustworthiness. Therefore, the moderating role of PVCEA is
considered an institutional spillover effect, whereas the direct
effect is a consumer's evaluative framing of retailer service
efforts. Complementing Institutional Theory, these roles are
extended to help explain the development and contextual
reinforcement of customer loyalty.
However, customers with fewer positive perceptions of
Vision 2030  are more inclined to assess similar service
practices in terms of their primary functional qualities, as found
by Suriansha et al.,  (2024). Therefore, institutional theory
predicts the moderating role not only that it does not predict the
absence of service investment but also that the perception of the
institution together with its perception of the services will
influence the interpretation of the service investment, not
whether it exists or not, as suggested by Dam and Dam (2021).
This study gives  Institutional Theory a new aspect beyond
organisational compliance and establishes consumer-perceived
institutional environments as evaluative frames that affect the
effectiveness of firm-level service initiatives.
2.2. Conceptualisation of Key Constructs
In this study, the constructs were conceptualised as separate
consumer perceptions that occur at various points in the service
evaluation process. Perceived Staffing Adequacy is the
consumer's evaluation of the retailer's staffing level,
availability, and competency as a means of providing timely
and effective service , as suggested by  Fisher et al.,  (2021).
According to Zaid and Patwayati (2021) , staffing is hidden in
the context of service quality and a broader strategic capacity.
Staffing is part of a larger service quality measure, but it is
overlooked in this broader context because it serves as a signal
of resource capacity. In this perspective, Makudza (2021)
argues that staffing adequacy is a measure of the adequacy of
staff that captures consumers' evaluation of the availability of
staff and staff performance in service encounters, compared to
general service quality which reflects the overall evaluation of
the quality of the staff performing the services. Consequently,
it can be considered a structural service input  rather than  a
service output, in line with the arguments presented by Haque
et al., (2024).

Perceived Staffing Adequacy can be conceptually separated
from the overall concept of service quality. Traditional service
quality measurement systems, such as SERVQUAL, measure
customer perceptions of overall service quality based on five
dimensions: reliability, responsiveness, assurance, empathy,
and tangibility (Mutambik, 2023; Sun & Pan, 2023). In
contrast, consumers' perception of staffing adequacy is limited
to their assessment of the availability, number, and competency
of the store's frontline employees to provide adequate and
effective service in a timely manner (Suriansha et al., 2024).
This construct does not assess the quality of the service
received but rather reflects the customer's perception of an
organisational capability that can be used in the provision of
service. As a result, it can be said that staffing is a strategic
operational capability that relates to customers' confidence in
the retailer to provide services that satisfy customers'
expectations at all times. By separating the construct of service
quality from customer loyalty, an important contribution of this
study lies in its ability to isolate the distinct contribution of the
front line in terms of their perceptions of the resources they
have on the job to the customer's loyalty towards the
organisation.
Customer Experience Strategy is the consumer
consciousness of how a retailer deliberately designs and
coordinates a consistent, engaging , and seamless experience
across points of contact the customer touchpoints throughout
the experience , as  specified by Bruckberger et al.,  (2023).
Unlike service quality , this construct looks at the conscious
management of interactions, not just the service performance,
nonetheless, before, during, and after the purchase. According
to Hsiao et al.,  (2023), Customer Experience is a strategic
process that results from several interactions and channels.
Customer Experience Strategy (CES)  is closely related to
customer experience but should not be used as an
interchangeable term. Customer experience is the consumer's
full cognitive and emotional appraisal formed upon the
completion of a retailer's service encounter, which is
considered a result of the service encounter (Hsiao et al., 2023).
However, the Customer Experience Strategy (CES) is true for
retailers' deliberate organisational ability to design, coordinate,
and proactively manage customer interactions before they are
evaluated in a multi-contacting manner (Pappas et al., 2023). It
is not about consumer responses but about strategic decisions
related to the journeys themselves, the uniformity of the service
offered, and the involvement of physical and digital channels.
Thus, Customer Experience Strategy (CES) is a managerial
capability that leads to customer experience rather than the
experience itself. By separating Customer Experience Strategy
from customer experience, this study allowed for a focus on the
impact of planned service initiatives on customer loyalty
through more strategic organisational action than evaluation
after the customer consumption of the product, thereby moving
beyond the outcome-oriented approaches of consumer -centric
customer experience research.
Finally, PVCEA reflects consumers' perceptions that the
national reform movement has raised the quality of retail,
customer orientation, and service standards (Suriansha et al.,
2024). This variable is an institutional frame, as opposed to the
previous constructs developed at the firm level, that is, a macro-
level definition that consumers use to define their retail
experience. Staffing adequacy is considered a measure of
service capacity, Customer Experience Strategy is a measure of
service design, and Vision 2030 perceptions are considered a
measure of institutional context and are independent within the
proposed model.
2.3. Hypotheses Development
2.3.1. Perceived Staffing Adequacy and Customer Loyalty
The relationship between staffing adequacy in retail settings
and customer loyalty has become increasingly important in
retail and service management studies. Frontline employees
have been the focus of  service experiences in retail
environments; hence, the display of availability,
responsiveness, and competence of employees strongly
influences how consumers rate service quality and consequent
loyalty behaviour (Alqahtani et al.,  2024). Dam and Dam
(2021) explored the connections between staffing adequacy,
customer satisfaction, and customer loyalty using survey data
from 299 customers of a supermarket in Vietnam, which was
analysed using PLS -SEM. Their results show that the quality
of services is a major boost to customer loyalty and
simultaneously reinforces brand image and customer
satisfaction. Although the study conceptualises service quality
as a multidimensional construct, the authors focus on employee
responsiveness and reliability , which are strongly linked to
staffing adequacy as a determinant of loyalty development .
Nevertheless, the application of convenience sampling and the
emphasis on a single urban retail setting restrict the
extrapolation of the results to other markets where consumers
and industries are undergoing radical changes.
Similar emphasis is placed in the operational research on
the significance of staffing capacity  to ensure the effective
delivery of retail services. Diaz et al.,  (2025) examined the
problem of operational constraints of 24 -hour retail stores in
the Philippines by qualitatively interviewing store owners and
employees in the Philippines. The results indicate that staffing
deficits, especially during peak times or night shifts, interfere
with service provision and compromise customer experiences
because they have to wait to be served and have inaccessible
service experiences, which eventually negates their retention.
However, the qualitative case study design is mainly indicative
of managerial and operational views as opposed to consumer
reviews and hence incapable of empirically estimating the
direct impact of staffing adequacy on loyalty outcomes. These
results are consistent with the Social Exchange Theory, which
assumes that loyalty is a relational consequence  of positive
interactions with customers in terms of service, as reflected in
the arguments of Suriansha et al., (2024). However, perceived
staffing adequacy as a predictor of consumer-based loyalty has
been investigated in previous studies. To address this gap, this
study proposes the following hypothesis:
H1: There is a statistically significant and positive
relationship between Perceived Staffing Adequacy and
Customer Loyalty in the retail sector of Saudi Arabia.

2.3.2. Customer Experience Strategy and Customer
Loyalty
The strategy of customer experience has been identified as
an important factor in customer loyalty, especially in service -
oriented industries, where value generation is realised at many
points of interaction. Makudza (2021)  proves that customer
loyalty in the banking industry is largely determined by
customer experience management, which involves virtual,
physical, and service interaction. These observations are
consistent with the Social Exchange Theory , which states that
such positive experiential benefits are returned by customers in
loyalty behaviours, as indicated by Dam and Dam (2021).
However, the research is more operationalised to the
dimensions of interaction instead of focusing on the experience
as the wider strategic organisational potential. In addition, the
industry-specific focus and cross -sectional survey design
restrict the applicability of the results to different retail settings,
where the elements of experience are more diverse.
Likewise, evidence is emerging in the e-retailing literature.
Zaid and Patwayati (2021) concluded that customer experience
has a positive effect on loyalty, both directly and indirectly, via
engagement and satisfaction processes , using PLS-SEM with
512 participants. Although their results support the relational
exchange perspective, the study uses a student sample and deals
with digital retail environments; therefore, the findings can be
questionable in terms of their applicability to the real-life retail
world, where face -to-face service interactions are still at the
centre. Specific information related to the retail sector is
presented by Pappas et al.,  (2023), who also affirm that
customer retention is greatly boosted when customer
experience is improved in the contemporary retail setting.
However, the authors’ conceptualisation of customer
experience is mostly a moderating product and not a strategic
initiative planned by organisations to influence loyalty. To
expand on this point of view, Kumar and Mokha (2021) state
that technological programmes such as  E-CRM can improve
customer experiences and loyalty results. However, their
review also found conceptual vagueness in the integration of
customer experience and strategic relationship management in
organisations. Taken together, these studies argue the
significance of experience in influencing loyalty, though they
lack empirical emphasis on the issue of customer experience as
a unified strategic capability in retail markets. To fill this gap,
this study hypothesises the following:
H2: There is a statistically significant and positive
association between Customer Experience Strategy and
Customer Loyalty in the retail sector of Saudi Arabia.
2.3.3. Moderating Role of Perceived Consumer Economic
Agenda
Emerging markets are characterised by greater consumer
expectations and relationships between firms and customers, as
increased by national economic agendas and institutional
reforms. According to  Institutional Theory, macro -level
policies establish regulatory and normative pressures which
impact the way consumers judge organisational practices and
service quality  (Olaopa & Alsuhaibany, 2023). The PVCEA
within the framework of Saudi Arabia is a significant
institutional change that helps enhance consumer markets,
boost the level of services, and promote competition in the
private sector. Kumar and Mokha (2021) claimed that Vision
2030 changes have transformed consumer expectations in the
Saudi retail industry, especially regarding service quality and
customer-focused innovation. This research mainly utilises
policy analysis and secondary economic information ; thus,
empirical knowledge of consumers’ perceptions of these
institutional alterations relative to loyalty patterns is restricted.
Similarly, Ha (2021) explored the overall economic
transformation in relation to Vision 2030 and found that
institutional changes in favour of service excellence and
workforce development prompt firms to enhance their
operational and customer engagement strategies. Although the
strategy of institutional alignment is emphasised in the study,
its macroeconomic approach does not consider the dimension
of consumer perception that accounts for behavioural responses
such as  loyalty. At the organisational level, Shibeika (2025)
discovered that Vision 2030 efforts have compelled Saudi
service organisations to enhance customer experience
strategies and employee competence building to keep them
competitive. Nevertheless, the qualitative design that considers
managerial views limits the capacity to study the direct impact
of such institutional initiatives on customers’ perceptions.
Similar arguments are made by Kumar and Venkatesan
(2021), who propose that consumer trust and service
expectations can be moulded by national transformation
programs and have an indirect influence on loyalty results.
However, they did not explicitly assess the moderating effects
within the given service interactions in their cross -national
comparative analysis.
Studies such as Ha (2021) , Manyanga et al., (2022), and
Shibeika (2025) have consistently shown that Vision 2030 has
brought about innovation in services, work skills,  and
customer-centric transformation in retail services. However,
most of these studies focus on organisational reactions to
reform agendas and not on consumer surveys of their
assessment. Similarly, Kumar and Venkatesan (2021) claim
that national transformation programs impact service
expectations and trust, but the impact of these perceptions on
service outcomes depends on firm-level service practices.
Based on the above, it can be concluded that while the literature
reviewed shows that institutional reforms impact organisations
and markets, their impact on the influence of perceived staffing
levels and the Customer Experience Strategy (CES) on
customer loyalty is limited.
Similarly, Manyanga et al., (2022) note that the consumer-
based reforms of Vision 2030 stimulate retail organisations to
promote workforce capacity and innovation in services,
although there is no empirical validation based on consumer
data. These studies show that institutional reforms affect the
strategies of organisations and consumer expectations;
however, few of them investigate how consumer perceptions of
such reforms moderate the relationship between service
practices and loyalty results. To fill this research gap, the

3. METHODOLOGY
3.1. Research Design and data Collection Instrument
This study followed a primary quantitative research design
to empirically investigate the interrelationships between
perceived staffing adequacy, Customer Experience Strategy,
and customer loyalty, and the moderating effect of PVCEA. A
survey questionnaire based on structured and closed-ended
questions was used to collect data based on measurement items
that obtained validity in the examination of the retail setting.
The measurement scale was based on a five -point Likert scale
ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree), which
is generally suggested to yield perceptions and behavioural
intentions in consumer research, as suggested by Abdallah
et al., (2021). The questionnaire was segmented into four parts
that stated the constructs of the study: PVCEA, Customer
Loyalty, Perceived Staffing Adequacy, and Customer
Experience Strategy.
3.2. Variable Construction and Operationalisation
The questionnaire comprised both adapted and newly
developed measures. Items related to Customer Experience
Strategy were adapted from Garg et al., (2014), and Customer
Loyalty items were adapted from Bobâlcă et al.,  (2012). In
contrast, PSA and PVCEA were developed for this study based
on the conceptual definition and vision of the study.
Specifically, a comprehensive literature review of Institutional
Theory, consumer-driven economic reforms, modernisation of
the service sector , and Vision 2030 policy initiatives
contributed to the development of PVCEA items. Academic
research, policy documents from the government, and reports
on retail transformation, service levels, consumer
empowerment, and competitiveness in the private sector in the
context of Vision 2030 were analysed to find common themes.
These themes served as a basis for drawing up an initial item
pool to assess consumer perceptions of the impact of Vision
2030 on enhancing the standards in retail, customer orientation,
and the overall environment of the consumer economy in Saudi
Arabia.
Moreover, the instrument underwent content validation by
a panel of three scholars who were knowledgeable and
experienced in the field of consumer behaviour and retail
management, which resulted in some minor changes to the
wording and context of the items. Moreover, when the items
were being evaluated by experts for consistency, items that
showed some overlap in content and ambiguity or poor
correspondence to the construct definition were either dropped
or rewritten to ensure that the remaining items would reflect a
coherent institutional perception of the attitude of the users and
not a general policy attitude. To test item clarity, reliability, and
preliminary validity, a pilot study was conducted with 75 retail
consumers. The full measurement instrument is provided in
Appendix A.
Four criteria were used to evaluate construct validity and
reliability: Cronbach's alpha, composite reliability, Average
Variance Extracted (AVE), and discriminant validity. The
values of Cronbach’s alpha and composite reliability were
higher than 0.7 which showed stronger reliability of the
construct. Further, the AVE values were higher than 0.5 which
showed robust convergent validity.

sampling method used in this study was purposive sampling,
which, as Ahmad and Wilkins (2025) explain, entails selecting
respondents based on specific criteria relevant to the research
purpose. This was deemed to be the right approach , as only

3.4. Addressing Selection Bias, Non -Response Bias and
Common Method Bias
The issue of potential selection bias was reduced by
dispersing the survey on various  social media platforms to
represent different population segments. As indicated by Wei
et al., (2023), independent t -tests were used to compare early
(n1 = 100) and late respondents (n2 = 100) to examine non -
response bias. In addition, multicollinearity was tested based
on the Variance Inflation Factor (VIF) method, in which lower
values than the criterion of 3.3 indicate that there is no
significant issue of multicollinearity, as suggested by Gaskin et
al., (2025). In addition, the issue of Common Method Bias
(CMB) was tested through Harman’s Single Factor Test, in
which the total variance explained was found to be below 50%
for the first factor, that is, 46.31%. This indicates that there is
no CMB issue in the model. A full collinearity VIF was also
used to assess common method bias, where a VIF below 3.3
indicated no CMB issue in the study (Table 5).
To reduce the likely impact of Common Method Bias
(CMB), certain procedural remedies were applied throughout
the questionnaire design and data collection  process.
Evaluation apprehension and socially desirable responses were
minimised by guaranteeing that the respondents would not be
personally identified and  that their participation was
anonymous and confidential. The questionnaire was clear and
concise to ensure comprehension and reduce the chances of
misunderstanding. Moreover, it was assessed by three
academic experts and pilot-tested with 75 store consumers to
enhance the clarity of the items and their context. The
constructs were introduced separately to minimise the
respondents’ responses to the study hypotheses . In addition to
these procedural remedies, common method bias was
statistically examined using the full -collinearity approach
Variance Inflation Factor  (VIF) results, which  showed no
significant common method problems , since all VIFs were
below the recommended values.
3.5. Data Analysis
Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling (PLS-
SEM) with SmartPLS software was used for data analysis
because it is appropriate for predictive models and complex
relationships with moderating effects (Haji-Othman & Yusuff,
2022). The measurement model was analysed by initially
testing the internal consistency reliability using Cronbach’s
alpha and composite reliability, and convergent  validity using
Average Variance Extracted (AVE). This was followed by a
path coefficient analysis to validate the hypothetical
relationships and moderating relationship between the
constructs.

5. DISCUSSION
The findings of this study affirm H1 that customer loyalty
in the Saudi retail industry is positively and significantly
predicted by perceived staffing adequacy. However, this
relationship was found to be modest. This finding is consistent
with the general service management literature on the
significance of frontline employees in influencing customer
perceptions and relationship results. For example, according to
the research conducted by Dam and Dam (2021), customer
loyalty in a retail setting is greatly boosted by the quality of the
services provided , which underscores the fact that both
responsiveness and reliability in customer communication
matter a great deal. Similarly, Alqahtani et al.,  (2024) cited
staffing shortages as a major issue in operations that influenced
the delivery of services and customer satisfaction in retail.
These revelations are expanded in the current research because
they prove that customers’ perceptions of sufficient staffing
directly influence their loyalty.
The relationship between Perceived Staffing Adequacy and
Customer Loyalty was statistically significant , but the effect
size analysis indicated that the relationship did not have a
severe impact. This suggests that as consumers rate the staffing
of retailers as a crucial factor in assessing how adequate and
competent they are, Staffing Adequacy alone may not be a key
factor in explaining the retail loyalty behaviour of consumers.
Instead, it is one of several other service -related factors that
influence consumer retention. Thus, the results should be
considered as indicating that it has made some contribution
towards loyalty formation, albeit modest. The level of
significance of the relationship should be considered, together
with its relatively low practical importance when making
managerial decisions, as recommended by contemporary SEM.
Nevertheless, the size of the relationship witnessed in this
study is relatively small compared to  the more significant
service quality impacts witnessed in some earlier studies, such
as Dam and Dam (2021). The first reason could be the brisk
modernisation and digitalisation of Saudi retail, as automated
service provision and omnichannel solutions partially lessen
the dependency on human relations, as in the traditional retail
environment. Nonetheless, human service encounters are
essential for  improving trust, solving problems, and
personalising experiences in the hospitality industry.
The results are also in line with the Social Exchange Theory
(SET), according to which customers respond to positive
service experiences with a positive behavioural response,
including loyalty (Alqahtani et al.,  2024). As indicated by
Makudza (2021), consumers who find that retail stores have
provided them with enough attentive staff who are  able to
deliver perceive this as an investment in the quality of service
by a given organisation, thus creating reciprocal commitment.
Hence, based on these arguments, it can be said that adequate
staffing is likely to fortify customer -retailer relationships and
increase the resultant loyalty in the Saudi ret ail environment,
where economic reforms and global competition in the retail

While Customer Experience Strategy was a more effective

suggests a moderate practical significance of Customer
Experience Strategy on the process of forming loyalty. This
means that Customer Experience Strategy is a critical element
of the process of building loyalty, but it is in addition to other
organisational and contextual factors. The findings thus add to
previous studies by showi ng that strategic customer
experiences continue to play a significant role in the Saudi
retail market despite swift digitalisation and market
transformation. As in modern times, customers are increasingly
choosing retailers based on the quality, consistency , and
coordination of their shopping experience. As a result,
investments in customer journey management and experience
design are more likely to explain loyalty results  than discrete
operational service inputs.
The current results support these inferences, although they
generalise them to the case of a fast -developing retail industry
in Saudi Arabia, where experiential differentiation is the key
competitive approach. In contrast to conventional retail
markets, where the variety of products is the key determinant
of consumer behaviour, Saudi retailers compete with an
increasingly high rate of immersive stores, customised
services, and online platforms  (Suriansha et al., 2024). These
changes can be used to justify why Customer Experience
Strategy is among the impacts that are stronger relative to
staffing adequacy in this study. Similarities with past studies
can also be attributed to cultural and industrial factors. Most of
the emerging markets studied by Makudza (2021)  also have
similar retail trends of rapid digitalisation and growing
consumer demands for convenience and customisation.
Nevertheless, the socio -economic changes peculiar to Saudi
Arabia, which are based on Vision 2030, have spurred the pace
of retail innovation and pushed organisations to invest in
customer-oriented service paradigms.
PVCEA has a comparatively strong relationship with
customer loyalty, which proves that consumers do not respond
only to firms ‘service interactions at the micro-level but also to
the macro-level institutional perception. This association can
be explained in terms of institutional spillover effects when
national reform efforts increase the level of confidence in
market regimes and service providers. The greater direct impact
of PVCEA than that of staffing adequacy indicates that the

Furthermore, the comparatively stronger influence of
PVCEA than that of staffing adequacy is that macro -level
confidence could be superior to micro -level customer service
assessments through adequate staffing, especially in fast -
changing economies, as supp orted by Makudza (2021). With
the active encouragement of service excellence, modernisation,
and consumer empowerment promoted by Vision 2030 in the
Saudi context, retailers can use legitimacy spillover, whereby
alignment with national transformation increases their
perceived credibility and appeal. This means that customer
loyalty can also be inspired by direct service encounters and
encompass more confidence in the retail ecosystem, which can,
in turn, support the significance of the institutional context in
consumer behaviour formation.
The results are aligned with the Social Exchange Theory
because improved customer experiences generate a perception
of relational values, which leads to  consumer loyalty
behaviours. Consequently, the findings imply that when
retailers streamline both physical and digital interactions,
customers feel the greater advantages of the relationship
between the retailer and customer. Therefore, they are likely to
make repeat purchases and spread positive word of mouth .
These findings have strategic implications for Saudi retailers ,
who must  invest in experiential innovation, omnichannel
integration, and customised services to remain competitive in a
fast-paced consumer economy.
The results reveal that PVCEA significantly moderates the
relationships between perceived Staffing Adequacy, Customer
Experience Strategy, And Customer Loyalty, supporting H3a
and H3b. However, the magnitude of these Moderation Effects
is rather small, which implies that national reform initiatives,
as perceived by consumers, affect the formation of loyalty, but
in practice , they have a relatively small effect. The small
moderation coefficients indicate that Vision 2030 does not
significantly affect consumer loyalty formation. Rather, such
perceptions seem to slightly validate their current assessment
of retailers' service levels. This discovery suggests that loyalty
is still largely a result of retail experiences  and no longer the
overall perception of the policy itself.
This is consistent with previous research suggesting that
national transformation programs influence consumer
expectations and market actions. Similarly, Olaopa and
Alsuhaibany (2023) established that economic diversification
policies lead to greater operational efficiency and engagement.

IMPLICATIONS
The findings have practical implications for policymakers,
retail managers, and researchers in Saudi Arabia. PVCEA is
positively correlated with customer loyalty which means that
consumer confidence in continuous reforms in the retail sector
could complement firms' stronger customer relations efforts.
The findings suggest that Customer Experience Strategy plays
a greater role in customer loyalty for retail managers, but
perceived staffing adequacy is also an important predictor of
customer loyalty. Therefore, retailers should focus on
providing uniform and properly managed customer experiences
and ensure that the store is sufficiently staffed to provide the
service. Managers should consider a positive perception of
national reforms as a positive contextual factor, not a
replacement for good service practices, given that the
moderating effects of the PVCEA were statistically significant
but relatively small. From a theoretical perspective, the results
provide a theoretical extension of social exchange and
institutional theories, as they provide evidence for various
factors that work to shape customer loyalty, not only based on
assessment of service experiences but also on perceptions of
the general institutional setting in which services are realised.
CONCLUSION
This study provides empirical evidence that customer
loyalty in the Saudi retail industry is not determined solely by
traditional service factors, although it is formed in the
environment of interaction of organisational service practices
with institutional changes. The results indicate that although
perceived staffing adequacy is relevant to loyalty, as it enables
the presence of responsive and reliable service experiences, it
has stronger effects when combined with strategically designed
customer experiences that can create value at multiple service
touchpoints. To this end, the establishment of loyalty in modern
retail settings seems to be more experiential than it is
transactional. In addition, the moderating role of PVCEA
suggests that macro -level economic reforms can be used to
change how consumers interpret and analyse organisational
service initiatives. This shows that consumer loyalty in Saudi
Arabia is part of a broader socio -economic discourse in which
institutional changes and reform rest on the expectations of the
quality of services, innovation, and the capacity of workforces.
Therefore, retailers who align their operational practices with
the Vision 2030 goals can build consumer loyalty more
efficiently.
Although these contributions exist, several limitations must
be considered. First, purposive sampling and self -reported
survey data were used in the study, which limits the
generalisability of the results and may cause bias in perception.
Second, a cross-sectional design does not allow for attention to
changes in customer perception in the long run. Future studies
can take the form of longitudinal or mixed methods to reflect
the changing attitudes of consumers over time due to changes
in Vision 2030 reforms. Moreover, considering other
moderating factors , such as  service innovation or digital
transformation, may help unravel the nature of loyalty in the
new retail market. Finally, as data were collected through social
media platforms, the sample mostly represents  digitally
accessible retail consumers in Saudi Arabia, which does not
capture less digitally active groups  in the population . This
could result in reduced representativeness and generalisation of
the overall findings to the entire retail population. Therefore,
further studies should consider stratified sampling methods that
will make the sample much more balanced in its demographic
composition and provide a comparison between the sample
features and national retail consumer profiles to increase
generalisability.
