# Generative AI in Digital Marketing: Its Impact on Consumer Engagement and Brand Trust

GENERATIVE AI IN DIGITAL MARKETING:
ITS IMPACT ON CONSUMER ENGAGEMENT
AND BRAND TRUST

BHAVANA GARG A

Abstract: The present research focused on the effect of Generative AI (GenAI) in digital marketing on
consumer engagement and brand trust. The ‘Quantitative cross -sectional approach’ was applied to survey
data collected from 151 participants. The results were analysed using ‘Descriptive statistics’, ‘Reliability
test’, ‘Pearson correlation’ and ‘Linear regression’. The findings r evealed that there are statistically
significant positive correlations between ‘GenAI and consumer engagement’, ‘GenAI and brand trust’, and
‘consumer engagement and brand trust’. Thus, all three formulated hypotheses have been tested positively.
The study showed how GenAI could be effectively applied in digital marketing to engage consumers and
build brand trust.

Keywords: Generative AI, Digital Marketing, Consumer Engagement, Brand Trust and Ad Personalisation

1. Introduction
‘Generative artificial intelligence (GenAI)’ is consistently revolutionising digital marketing through
automation and personalisation of advertisements, social -media content, product descriptions, and
conversations. Recent evidence has highlighted that Gen AI can improve behavioural engagement through
increased interactivity, attention, and personalisation. However, the effects of GenAI on affective outcomes,
including trust and emotional involvement, are inconsistent (Pokhrel & Somasiri, 2026). AI -enabled
advertising can also positively affect perceptions of relevance, transparency and credibility, further
influencing cognitive, emotional and behavioural engagement (Kolesnik, 2025). On the other hand, AI -
generated marketing raises issues related to authentic ity, transparency and credibility. Kirk & Givi (2024)
reported that AI -generated emotional marketing communications could be perceived as less authentic,
negatively influencing customer loyalty and word -of-mouth activities. Hence, understanding whether
GenAI increases consumer engagement without decreasing brand trust became an important contemporary
marketing question. Thus, this study investigates this issue by empirical analysis of consumers' reactions to
GenAI-based digital marketing.

Research Aim and Objectives
This research aims to evaluate the effect of Generative AI in digital marketing on consumer
engagement and brand trust. The main objectives of this research will be:
● To investigate consumers' perceptions about Generative AI in digital marketing.
● To measure the role of Generative AI in customizing digital marketing content for consumers.
● To analyse the relationship between Generative AI and consumer engagement.
● To investigate the relationship between Generative AI and brand trust.
● To underpin the relationship between consumer engagement and brand trust.
● To determine the reliability of the measurement scales used in the research.
● To test the research hypotheses through correlation and regression analysis techniques.
● To highlight the implications of Generative AI for increasing consumer engagement and brand trust.

2. Literature Review
Efficient optimisation of GenAI accelerates digital marketing capabilities in generating and
modifying various types of text and imagery and adapting consumers' experienc e for personalisation
purposes. Recently, a systematic review of GenAI concluded that GenAI holds greater potential for content
generation, personalisation, and consumer engagement, even though privacy, bias, governance, and ethical
implementation are some  of the challenges (Devadiga & Aithal, 2026). Consumer engagement is
particularly important since personalised and interactive AI advertising can induce various cognitive,

On the contrary, the association between GenAI and brand trust is quite complicated. Transparency

Therefore, it is evident that there is a conflict between the engagement potential of AI -enabled
personalisation and authenticity -related trust issues. While the existing literature on GenAI marketing is
extending, it is still dependent on specific contexts and lacks long-term trust, cultural diversity, and affective

consumers' perceptions of GenAI-based digital marketing and its effect on consumer engagement and brand
trust.
3. Methodology
This study utilised a ‘Quantitative cross -sectional research design’ to examine the effect of GenAI
in digital marketing  on consumer engagement and brand trust. Primary data were collected from 151
participants who had experience with digital platforms and exposure to AI -generated marketing content.
The data were gathered by means of a ‘Structured questionnaire’ containing demographic questions and
items measuring three constructs: GenAI in digital marketing, consumer engagement, and brand trust. Items
of the constructs were measured with a ‘Five -point Likert scale’, ranging from ‘1 (strongly disagree) to 5
(strongly agree)’ . The participants were recruited using the ‘Convenience sampling method’ to gather
information from the target population of digital consumers. The data analysis was carried out using ‘IBM
SPSS Statistics’. ‘Frequency’ and ‘Descriptive statistics’ were ap plied in order to characterise the sample
and describe the variables. The reliability of the scale was checked by means of ‘Cronbach's alpha’.
‘Correlation’ and ‘Regression analyses’ were used to test the hypotheses.
4. Findings
There were 151 respondents included in the analysis, with no cases being excluded. ‘Descriptive
statistics’ provided concise results which indicated generally moderate levels of perceptions regarding the
questionnaire items. Mean scores ranged between 2.62 and 3.20, with the highest  mean being reported for
confidence in brands that utilise AI in an efficient manner but with authentic communication (M = 3.20, SD
= 1.312). ‘Internal consistency reliability’ of the ‘15-item scale’ was extremely high (Cronbach’s α = .945),
exceeding the recommended 0.70 threshold.

The results of ‘Pearson’s correlations’ revealed significant positive associations among all three
constructs. Generative AI (GAI) had a positive association with consumer engagement (CE) (r = .666, p

have a positive impact on ‘Consumer engagement’ and ‘Brand trust’.

Overall, the findings indicate that GenAI -based digital marketing efforts can positively influence
brand trust and customer engagement. They confirm the literature stating that personalised and relevant AI
marketing experiences can contribute to higher engagement levels, and it  is important to make AI usage
responsible, transparent, and authentic for building consumer trust. Therefore, H1, H2, and H3 were
supported by the findings.
5. Discussion
The results are strongly consistent with existing literature on GenAI -based marketing. First, the
positive relation between GenAI and consumer engagement (β = .666) is supported by Mohammadkhani et
al. (2026), who found that personalisation, interactivity, and relevance in AI advertisements influence
consumer engagement. Second, the positive correlation between GenAI and brand trust (β = .671) is closely
connected with the results of Nizette et al. (2025) who stated the significance of the transparency in
influencing consumer reactions to AI -based communication. Third, the positive  effect of ‘Consumer
engagement’ on ‘Brand trust’ (β = .629) shows that good interaction with consumers using AI helps to build
consumer-brand relationship. However, Rahman et al. (2026) noted that AI authorship may negatively affect
the authenticity, suggesting responsible GenAI implementation in marketing.
6. Conclusion
It can be concluded that Generative AI in digital marketing can positively affect consumer
engagement and brand trust. All three hypotheses were supported in the study, showing the positiv e
predictive influence of GenAI on consumer engagement and brand trust, along with higher consumer
engagement positively predicting brand trust. It proved that brands need to use relevant, personalised and
interactive marketing experience. However, brands should use GenAI in a responsible way with
transparency, authenticity and credible communication in order to increase consumer engagement and brand
trust.

6. Future Scope
Future research might focus on collecting a larger and more diverse sample in terms o f countries,
industries, and demographics to make the study generalisable. The longitudinal design will help to further
identify the changes in consumer attitudes to GenAI over time. Further research can include such variables
as AI transparency, perceived authenticity, privacy concerns, and personalisation.
