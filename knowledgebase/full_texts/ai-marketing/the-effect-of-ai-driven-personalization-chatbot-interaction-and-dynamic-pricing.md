Abstract: The rapid adoption of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in digital marketing has changed
how consumers interact with brands, particularly through personalized recommendat ions,
chatbot services, and dynamic pricing. However, although these AI -based features are
widely used on digital platforms, consumers may still question their reliability, fairness, and
transparency. This study aims to examine the effects of AI -driven per sonalization, AI -
enabled chatbot interaction, and AI -based dynamic pricing on consumer purchase decisions
through trust in AI systems. This research employed a quantitative approach using a cross -
sectional survey of consumers who had experience using AI -based features on digital
platforms in Indonesia. Data was analyzed using PLS -SEM. The results show that AI -driven
personalization and chatbot interaction positively influence purchase decisions, while
dynamic pricing has no direct effect. Trust in AI system s significantly mediates the
relationship between AI -based marketing features and purchase decisions. This study
concludes that consumer trust is essential in converting AI -driven marketing strategies into
purchase behavior.

INTRODUCTION
Artificial Intelligence (AI) has rapidly transformed contemporary marketing practices by enabling firms
to personalize offerings, automate customer interactions, and optimize pricing strategies at an
unprecedented scale. Recent industry reports indicate that over 80% of companies worldwide have adopted
at least one form of AI -based marketing technology, with personalization and conversational AI being the
most widely implemented applications (Chui et al., 2023) . Moreover, empirical evidence shows that 71% of
consumers expect personalized interactions from brands, and 80% are more likely to make a purchase when
such personalization is provided (Deloitte, 2023). These figures highlight the growing strategic importance of
AI-driven marketing in influencing consumer purchase behavior.
One prominent application of AI in marketing is AI -driven personalization, which utilizes machine
learning algorithms to tailor product recommendations, promotional content, and user experiences based
on individual consumer data. Prior studies demonstrate that personalized recommendations significantly
enhance perceived relevance  and reduce information overload, leading to higher purchase intentions
(Grewal et al., 2020) . Firms implementing AI -based per sonalization have reported sales increases ranging
from 10% to 30%, underscoring its tangible impact on consumer decision-making (Chui et al., 2023).
In parallel, AI -enabled chatbot interactions have become a dominant channel for customer
engagement. Chatbots powered by natural language processing provide real -time assistan ce, streamline

Another increasingly prevalent AI application is dynamic pricing, where algorithms adjust prices in real
time based on demand, competition, and consumer behav ior. While dynamic pricing has been shown to
improve revenue performance by approximately 5 –15%, it also raises concerns regarding perceived fairness
and transparency (Kumar et al., 2022). Empirical findings suggest that consumers may react negatively to AI-
driven price discrimination when algorithmic decision processes are not clearly communicated, thereby
undermining trust in the system (Xia et al., 2004).
Across t hese AI -driven marketing practices, consumer trust in AI systems emerges as a critical
mediating mechanism. Trust reflects consumers’ beliefs regarding the reliability, fairness, and transparency
of AI technologies and has been identified as a key determin ant of technology acceptance and usage
behavior (Gefen et al., 2003) . Recent studies confirm that insufficient trust —particularly related to data
privacy and algorithmic opacity —can weaken the positive effects of AI personalization, chatbot interaction,
and dynamic pricing on purchase decisions.
Given the rapid diffusion of AI in marketing and the simultaneous rise of consumer concerns regarding
data usage and algorithmic decision -making, a deeper empirical investigation is required. Therefore, this
study aimed to examines the effect of AI-driven personalization, chatbot interaction, and dynamic pricing on
consumer purchase decisions, with trust in AI systems positioned as a mediating variable. By adopting a
quantitative approach, this research contributes to the growing l iterature on AI marketing by clarifying the
psychological mechanism through which AI influences consumer behavior.
Previous empirical studies have investigated the role of AI personalization, chatbot services, and
algorithmic pricing independently. However , limited studies have simultaneously examined these three AI -
driven marketing mechanisms within a single integrated framework while considering trust in AI systems as a
mediating variable, particularly in the context of digital consumers in emerging markets such as Indonesia.
RESEARCH METHOD
Research Design
This study adopts a quantitative research approach with a causal explanatory design to examine the
effects of AI -driven personalization, AI -enabled chatbot interaction, and AI -based dynamic pricing on
consumer purchase decisions, both directly and indirectly through trust in AI
systems. A quantitative approach is appropriate as this study aims to test theoretically grounded
hypotheses and measure relationships among latent constructs using statistical modeling techniques (Hair et
al., 2022). The research employs a cross-sectional survey design, in which data are collected at a single point
in time to capture consumers’ perceptions and behavioral responses toward AI -based marketing practices.
To ensure cl arity, respondents were instructed to evaluate their experiences with e -commerce or digital
platforms that utilize AI -based recommendation systems, AI -powered chatbots, and algorithmic pricing
mechanisms that specifically are used in Indonesia, such as e -commerce marketplace (e.g., Tokopedia,
Shopee, Lazada), supper-apps (e.g., Gojek, Grab), and fintech platforms (e.g., OVO, Dana).

Population and Sample
The population of this study comprises consumers who have prior experience using AI -based features
on d igital platforms, such as personalized product recommendations, AI -powered chatbots, and dynamic
pricing mechanisms. Given the specificity of the research context, this study applies a purposive sampling
technique, a form of non -probability sampling that a llows researchers to select respondents who meet
predefined criteria relevant to the study objectives (Etikan et al., 2016).
To ensure adequate statisti cal power for Structural Equation Modeling (SEM), the minimum sample
size follows the recommendation of (Hair et al., 2022), which suggests a sample size of at least ten times the
maximum number of structural paths directed at any endogenous construct. Consi dering the complexity of
the proposed model, a minimum sample size of 200 respondents is deemed sufficient for reliable estimation
and hypothesis testing.
Data Collection Procedure
Primary data are collected using a self -administered structured questionnai re distributed online
through digital channels (Google Form). Online surveys are considered effective for capturing consumer
perceptions in digital and AI -driven contexts, as they enable broad reach and efficient data collection
(Saunders et al., 2023).
Measurement of Variables
The research model consists of five latent constructs: three independent variables, one mediating
variable, and one dependent variable.
1. AI-Driven Personalization is measured th rough indicators reflecting perceived relevance, accuracy,
usefulness, and overall evaluation of personalized recommendations (Huang & Rust, 2021).
2. AI-Enabled Chatbot Interaction is measured by indicators related to responsiveness,
informativeness, interaction quality, and overall evaluation of chatbot services.
3. AI-Based Dynamic Pricing is measured using indicators capturing perceived price fairness,
transparency, consistency, and acceptance (Xia et al., 2004).
4. Trust in AI Systems is measured through perceptions of reliability, integrity, competence, and overall
trust of AI technologies (Shin, 2021).
5. Consumer Purchase Decision is measured by indicators reflecting purchase intention, confidence in
decision-making, likelihood, and overall willingness of completing a purchase.

Questionnaire Item Indicators
All items are measured using a five-point Likert scale, ranging from 1 (str ongly disagree) to 5 (strongly
agree).
Data Analysis Technique
Data analysis is conducted using Structural Equation Modeling with the Partial Least Squares approach
(PLS-SEM), employing statistical software such as SmartPLS 4. PLS -SEM is particularly suita ble for this study
due to its predictive orientation, ability to handle complex mediation models, and robustness with relatively
large numbers of indicators (Hair et al., 2022).
The analysis procedure consists of two main stages. First, the measurement model  (outer model) is
evaluated by assessing convergent validity, discriminant validity, and internal consistency reliability
(construct reliability). Second, the structural model (inner model) is assessed by examining path coefficients
using bootstrapping tec hniques, coefficient of determination (R²), effect size (f²), and predictive relevance
(Q²) (Hair et al., 2022).
Hypothesis Testing
Hypotheses H1 –H6 and H10 are tested through the evaluation of direct effects among constructs,
while hypotheses H7 –H9 are test ed using mediation analysis to assess the indirect effects of trust in AI
systems. A hypothesis is considered supported if the path coefficient is statistically significant at a p -value <
0.05, consistent with established SEM research standards (Hair et al., 2022).
Ethical Considerations
Ethical considerations are addressed by ensuring voluntary participation, informed consent, and
anonymity of respondents. Respondents are informed that their data will be used solely for academic
research purposes, and no personal identifiable information is collected.

generating pricing d ecisions (Xia et al., 2004) . In addition, the moderation analysis reveals a significant
negative interaction effect between Trust in AI Systems and AI-based dynamic pricing on Consumer Purchase
Decision (β = –0.267, p < 0.001). This result i ndicates that higher levels of trust may increase consumer
sensitivity toward perceived unfair pricing practices.
Discussion
Overall, the findings support the Stimulus–Organism–Response (S–O–R) framework, in which AI-driven
marketing features act as external stimuli, trust in AI systems represents an internal psychological state, and
consumer purchase decisions constitute behavioral responses  (Shin, 2021). The positive effect of AI -driven
personalization on consumer purchase decisions indica tes that consumers value relevant and customized
recommendations because they simplify information processing and improve decision efficiency. This finding
aligns with the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), which explains that perceived usefulness influenc es
consumer acceptance of technology -based features in digital purchasing contexts (Davis, 1989). The results
are also consistent with (Bhojwani et al., 2026) , who found that AI -enabled features such as
recommendation engines significantly enhance consumer confidence, satisfaction, and perceived usefulness
in product evaluation, ultimately supporting purchase intention. Their study furth er highlights that AI

CONCLUSION
This study concludes that AI -driven personalization and AI -enabled chatbot interaction positively
influence consumer purchase decisions, both directly and indirectly through trust in A I systems. While AI -
based dynamic pricing does not directly affect purchase decisions, it exerts an indirect influence by
enhancing consumers’ trust in AI technologies. These findings highlight the critical role of trust as a
psychological mechanism linking AI-driven marketing practices to consumer behavior. The study contributes
to the AI marketing literature by integrating personalization, chatbot interaction, and dynamic pricing within
a single framework and demonstrating the mediating role of trust in A I systems. Practically, the results
suggest that organizations should prioritize transparency, reliability, and user-centered AI implementation to
strengthen consumer trust and improve marketing effectiveness in increasingly digitalized business
environments.
