Abstract: Electronic marketing (eM) is a vital component of a successful sales
strategy having the ability to reach a gl obal audience, offer targeted marketing,
provide data-driven insights, and foster customer engagement makes it a more
powerful tool for driving sales perfo rmance and achieving business growth.
This study attempts to examine the ne xus among electronic marketing tools,
i.e., mobile marketing, search engine optimisation, chatbot , email marketing,
and social media marketing on organi sational e-sales performance (ESP) from
the perspectives business to consumer (B2C) and business-to-business (B2B).
A total of 648 questionnaires for B2C and a total of 625 for B2B perspective
were eventually considered for data an alysis using SEM and SmartPLS. First,
the study affirmed a positive connection between eM on e-sales performance
from a B2C perspective. Second, the study confirmed a positive connection
between eM on e-sales performance from the B2B domain. This study endows
with insights into electronic marketing on how they may optimise the e-sales
performance of the companies by uti lising various technologies both B2C and
B2B.
Keywords: electronic marketing; e-sales performance; business-to-consumer
perspective; business-to-business pers pective; empirical analysis; innovation
diffusion theory.

1 Introduction
Electronic marketing (eM) research emerged as  a systematic approach to understanding
customer or consumer behaviour, preferen ces, and trends in the online landscape
(El-Gohary, 2012). It encompasses data-driven analysis of digital marketing strategies,
social media impact, website analytics, and customer insights (Clarke and Flaherty, 2005;
Gratzer et al., 2004). The research on eM is  vital in guiding businesses to optimise their

electronic marketing efforts, improve sales performance, and stay competitive in the
rapidly changing digital landscape. However, eM also known as digital marketing utilises
online platforms and tools to promote products or services, connect with target audiences,
and drive sales. It encompasses various channels  such as social media, email, SEO, PPC,
and content marketing (Taherdoost and Jalaliyoon, 2014; Wen and Chen, 2010). Through
data-driven insights and personalised approaches, businesses can reach a global audience,
optimise marketing efforts, and build la sting customer relationships. Electronic
marketing’s cost-effectiveness, adaptability , and measurable results make it a vital
component of modern sales strategies, enabling businesses to thrive in the digital age (Eid
and El-Gohary, 2013; Taherdoost and Jalaliyoon, 2014).
It is asserted that conducting eM research is essential for businesses to stay
competitive, measure ROI, optimise campaigns, re duce risks, adjust to changes, foster
customer loyalty, and test new ideas, (Eid and El-Gohary, 2013). It lays the foundation
for successful and effective marketing strategi es in the digital ag e both from B2B and
B2C perspectives. EM is vital from a B2C perspective as it enables direct and
personalised communication with consumers (Eid and El-Gohary, 2013; El-Gohary,
2012). It offers a global reach, targeti ng specific demographics and interests. B2C
businesses can engage with customers thro ugh social media, email, and targeted
advertising, building brand loyalty and custom er relationships. EM’s data-driven insights
can optimise campaigns, can improve ROI, and can adapt to changing consumer
preferences swiftly. With e- commerce and online transactions, B2C companies can offer
convenience and accessibility to customers a ll the time. This digital strategy creates a
competitive edge in the quick-changing consumer market, improves customer
satisfaction, and increases sales performance – particularly e-sales. But eM is also
important from a business-to- business (B2B) standpoint becau se it facilitates effective
and focused communication with other companies (El-Gohary and Eid, 2012). It provides
a venue for showcasing goods, services, an d business know-how via email marketing,
content marketing, and websites. Social media and search engine optimisation are
valuable tools that B2B companies can use to connect with their target market and
produce quality leads. EM makes it easier to network and establish connections with
possible customers and business partners. B2B eM can improve competitive positioning,
brand visibility and credibility in the digita l age, which will ultimately spur business
growth and success (Sheth and Sharma, 2005).
It is claimed that research  on electronic marketing from various angles, including
B2B, B2C, and electronic sales perspectives is being done for a number of reasons. For
instance, firms need to con duct eM research to measure ROI, find high-performing

2 Literature review
The innovation diffusion theory (IDT) explains how new ideas, products, or technologies
spread through a social system (Shaikh et al., 2023; Wani and Ali, 2015). It categorises
adopters into innovators, early  adopters, early majority, late  majority, and laggards, each
adopting at different stages (Wani and Ali, 2015). IDT theory highlights factors that
influence adoption such as perceived advantages, compatibility, complexity,
observability and trialability (Lee et al., 2011). It  is advocated that a successful diffusion
depends on effective communication and the presence of opinion leaders. IDT is
frequently utilised in campaigns for social change, technology adoption, and marketing
(Ho, 2022). IDT theory facilitates the stra tegic implementation of new concepts and
technologies, which aids in understanding and forecasting  the patterns of innovation
adoption within a population (Ho, 2022). It is suggested that using IDT theory, businesses

and organisations can develop targeted strategies to promote adoption (Wani and Ali,
2015). It emphasises how important it is to  understand consumer attitudes, convictions,
and behaviours in order to create marketing campaigns and initiatives that are
compelling. Because of its practical insights, IDT theory is a useful tool for encouraging
successful innovation implementation and soci etal change (Ahire and Ravichandran,
2001). IDT encourages more study becaus e of its important ramifications across
numerous fields (Ahire and Ravichandran, 2001; Ho, 2022; Wani and Ali, 2015).
Businesses, whether B2C or B2B, must comprehend consumer adoption patterns in order
to properly launch and promote new products.
As such, academics recognise its importance in shaping creative strategies and
accelerating economic growth (H o, 2022). IDT theory’s app lication extends to public
health campaigns, policy-making, and social initiatives, urging further research to address
pressing societal challenges. As technology evolves, studying the theory helps uncover
new insights into innovation adoption trends, fostering a continuous cycle of research,
improvement, and innovation implementation to  better meet the needs of a dynamic and
ever-changing world (Ahire and Ravichandran, 2001; de Reuver et al., 2009; Feder and

hypotheses as mentioned in Figure 1, and explained thereafter to develop the
hypothesised relationship to ensure the connections between EM and e-sales performance
from both B2C and B2B perspectives.

2.1 Electronic marketing and e-sales performance: B2C perspective
Electronic marketing or e-marketing states to the strategic use of digital technologies and
online channels, such as social media, email, search engines, and websites, to promote
products or services, build brand awareness, engage with customers, and drive sales in
the digital marketplace (Eid and El-Gohary,  2013; El-Gohary, 2012; Taherdoost and
Jalaliyoon, 2014). In the past, eM has been widely discussed by academic scholars in
various ways across the globe (Ayanso et al., 2010). However, how eM influences the
e-sales performance of the organisation is a novel and interesting domain that needs to be
explored in the present instant by developing hypothesised relationships, especially the
B2C perspective (Mort and Drennan, 2002; Waheed and Jianhua, 2018). For example,
B2C relationships between eM and online sales performance can be derived from various

investment in eM leads to higher sales performance and e-sales performance (Ayanso
et al., 2010). Delivering personalised eM s’ content to consumers can enhance
engagement and purchase intent (Li et al., 2016). It is stated that tailoring messages,
product recommendations, and offers to individual preferences and behaviours can create
a more meaningful connection, leading to a higher likelihood of making a purchase (Li
et al., 2016). We are currently discussing five major tools of eM, i.e., mobile marketing,
search engine optimisation, ch atbot, email marketing, and social media marketing which
are most evolving and accepted  in the perspective of B2C (El-Gohary and Eid, 2012).
Effective social media engagement helps in optimising the e-sales performance of
organisations (Dwivedi et al., 2015). Likewise, mobile optimisation also enhances the
e-sales performance of the organisations which supports better participating the
consumers (Eid and El-Gohary, 2013; Waheed and Jianhua, 2018). Integrating tools such
as websites, social media, email, and m obile apps, can lead to improved e-sales

tested and validated through empirical research to establish their validity and significance

industry, target audience, and other contextual factors (Bolos et al., 2016).

encompasses online advertising, content ma rketing, social media campaigns, email
marketing, and other tactics to enhance brand visibility, customer engagement, and
ultimately boost sales and conversions (Ha, 2008). Although many studies have shown

performance is a key concern of this study (Gao et al., 2021). Hence, based on the
enormous worth of electronic marketing in the existing literature and supporting
arguments of IDT theory concerning organisational performance, we now assume the
following propositions to empirically validate the outcomes from China.
H1 Electronic marketing is positively correla ted with the e-sales performance of the
organisations from a B2C perspective.

5 Discussion and implications
A total of two hypotheses were proposed to  explore the nexus of electronic marketing
(eM) and e-sales performance (ESP) from both B2C and B2B perspectives. First, it was
implicit in H1 that eM positively linked with ESP. The results after SEM implementation
have shown a positive connection between eM and ESP at ( β = 0.242; 0.000;
SE = 0.044). Based on such calculations and findings, H1 is accepted. In addition, the
outcomes support past studies in which expe rts suggested a positive connection between
electronic marketing and the e-sales performance of corporates from B2C perspectives
within various domains and perspectives, worldwide (Clarke and Flaherty, 2005;
Dwivedi et al., 2015; Gao et al., 2021; Hawk, 2004; Henderson, 1984; Mort and Drennan,
2002; Taherdoost and Jalaliyoon, 2014; Taylor and Strutton, 2010; Waheed and Jianhua,
2018). Second, it was assumed in H2 that eM  is positively linked with ESP. The results
after SEM implementation have shown a pos itive connection between eM and ESP at
(β = 0.301; 0.000; SE = 0.035). Based on such calculati ons and findings, H2 is accepted.
Furthermore, the results are following previous findings where researchers proposed a
