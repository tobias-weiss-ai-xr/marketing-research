Abstract
With the rapid development of artificial intelligence (AI), digital marketing and brand management have
significantly influenced talent cultivation in the beauty and SPA industry. Traditional skill -oriented
vocational education is no longer sufficient to meet the demands of industrial digital transformation. This
study explored the innovative integration of AI marketing into a SPA brand management course and
examined students’ learning experiences, professional competency development, and AI literacy within a
five-year junior college cosmetics application and management program in Taiwan. An exploratory mixed-
methods teaching practice study with a single-group course implementation was adopted. During the first
half of the semester, traditional lecture -based instruction was implemented, followed by AI -integrated
Project-Based Learning (PBL) activities in the second half of the course. Students applied AI tools to virtual
SPA brand development, marketing copywriting, presentation design, visual content creation, and video
production. Participants included 29 students. Quantitative data were collected using a five -point Likert-
scale questionnaire consisting of th ree dimensions: AI tool learning, AI application scenarios, and future
expectations of AI. Qualitative data were obtained from stage -based student interviews and reflective
feedback collected at the beginning, middle, and final stages of the course. The findings indicated that all
three dimensions achieved mean scores above 4.0, reflecting highly positive student perceptions toward
AI-integrated SPA brand instruction. Among the dimensions, AI tool learning demonstrated the highest
performance, particularly in AI-assisted copywriting and presentation design. Reliability analysis showed
high internal consistency across all dimensions. Qualitative findings further revealed that students gradually
progressed from initial unfamiliarity with AI toward practical application, workplace imagination, and critical
reflection on AI ethics and information credibility.  This study highlights the potential of integrating AI
marketing into SPA brand courses to enhance students’ digital application abilities, contextual learni ng
experiences, and career-oriented thinking, while supporting the development of AI literacy and curriculum
innovation in vocational education.

1. INTRODUCTION
With the rapid advancement of information and communication technologies and the widespread
application of artificial intelligence (AI), global industries are accelerating toward a new phase of digital
transformation. AI has demonstrated substantial value not only in bus iness operations, administrative
management, and decision support, but also in educational contexts, where it has become an important
driving force for instructional innovation and the enhancement of learning effectiveness. Chen, Chen, and Lin
(2020) indic ated that through the integration of big data analytics and information technologies, AI can
support the development of personalized learning pathways, improve learning efficiency and engagement,
and consequently reshape traditional teaching and learning models. This suggests that AI is no longer merely
an auxiliary tool in education, but has become an important medium for transforming learning models and
cultivating competencies.
Under the rapid development of AI technologies, the beauty and SPA industry, characterized by strong
integration of service experiences, emotional value, and personalized demands, has also undergone
profound changes in its business models. Charllo (2022) argued that AI has become a critical engine for
innovation in personalized beauty products and services, prompting the industry to shift from a technology-
centered service model toward an integrated business approach combining brand image, digital interaction,
and precision marketing. Hua and Huang (2023) further pointed out that be auty industry operators must
continuously adjust their management strategies and innovate service models in response to market
changes. In other words, modern consumers no longer focus solely on the professionalism of beauty services
themselves; instead, they increasingly expect to establish emotional connections with brands through visual
design, digital content, and social media interaction, thereby enhancing brand loyalty and purchase intention.
In practical market applications, AI-driven beauty technology (Beauty Tech) has been developing rapidly and
demonstrates significant commercial potential. Many SPA brands and cosmetics companies have adopted
AI-based image and graphic design tools, as well as AI voice and music generation technologies, to reduce
marketing production costs while improving content quality. At the same time, self-media platforms such as
TikTok, Facebook, and Instagram are widely utilized for precision marketing and brand communication.
Huang and Chung (2023) noted that self-media word-of-mouth has become an important factor influencing
customer behavioral intention, while Chen and Ho (2024) further emphasized that optimizing self -media
online marketing strategies can effectively increase audience traffic and marketing effectiveness. In addition,
Letitia (2024), using green skincare products as an example, found that social media influencers significantly
affect consumers’ purchase intentions. These studies demonstrate that digital content, self-media operation,
and brand interaction have become essential strategies in the management of beauty and SPA brands.
However, compared with the rapid transformation occurring within the industry, technical and vocational
education programs related to beauty and cosmetics application in Taiwan remain relatively limited in terms
of curriculum transformation and instructional innovation. Tsai and Chang (2023) pointed out that most
beauty-related curricula still focus primarily on hands -on treatments, certification examinations, and
professional technical training, while systematic instruction in digital marketing, brand management, and AI
tool application remains insufficient. Jhuang (2019) also noted that beauty education teachers face increasing
pressure in adapting to curriculum transformation and ev olving workplace demands. This phenomenon
suggests that if technical and vocational education continues to emphasize traditional technical training
alone, it may not adequately respond to the growing demand for interdisciplinary talent with digital
marketing competencies in the beauty service industry.
Within educational settings, guiding students to effectively integrate AI technologies with marketing thinking
while maintaining professional beauty skills training has become an important issue that technica l and
vocational education must address. Hwang and Chiu (2021) suggested that the application of AI in skill-based

guiding students to use AI tools to conduct virtual SPA brand construction , brand positioning , marketing
copywriting, visual content design, video production, and brand proposal presentations. In this instructional
design, AI no longer functions merely as an auxiliary teaching tool, but rather serves as a learning partner
that supports creative ideation, facilitates decision-making, and provides immediate feedback.
Furthermore, the purpose of integrating AI into technical and vocational education should not be limited
solely to the cultivation of operational proficiency. Greater emphasis should also be placed on the
development of AI literacy, critical thinking, ethical awareness, and sustainable learning competencies. As AI
technologies become increasingly embedded in digital marketing and beauty industry practices, students
must not only learn how to operate AI tools, but also develop the ability to evaluate AI -generated content,
understand the ethical implications of AI -assisted creativity, avoid excessive dependence on technological
tools, and cultivate autonomous learning abilities in response to future industrial transformation. Therefore,
AI-assisted instruction should not merely be regarded as a short-term technological intervention, but rather
as an important opportunity for long -term curriculum transformation and interdisc iplinary competency
development within technical and vocational education.
In summary, this study targeted students enrolled in a five -year junior college program in Cosmetics
Application and Management to explore the innovative practice and learning effectiveness of integrating AI
marketing into SPA brand courses. To systematically evaluate this instructional model, students’ learning
performance was categorized into three dimensions based on the course content and measurement items:
tool learning, ap plication scenarios, and future expectations. These dimensions were used to analyze
students’ perceptions regarding AI marketing tool operation, contextual brand application, and future career
development. It is hoped that the findings of this study will p rovide concrete and practical pedagogical
references for technical and vocational education in the fields of beauty and cosmetics application, while also
serving as an exploratory study on integrating AI into beauty and SPA curricula and promoting the digi tal
transformation of vocational education.
2. LITERATURE REVIEW
2.1. Applications of Artificial Intelligence in Education and Marketing
Artificial intelligence (AI) refers to the capability of machines to adapt to new situations, handle unexpected
conditions, solve problems, formulate plans, and perform tasks and functions that traditionally require
human intelligence (Chen et al., 2020). With the rapid development of key technologies such as machine
learning and natural language processing (NLP), AI has evolved from an early automation-oriented tool into
a complex system capable of simulating human reasoning, judgment, and decision -making. This
transformation has exerted profound influences across multiple domains, including education, marketing,
and industrial development (Vaněček et al., 2025; Grájeda et al., 2024).

In educational contexts, the introduction of Artificial Intelligence in Education (AIEd) has triggered a paradigm
shift in teaching and learning models. Traditional teacher -centered and conten t-uniform instructional
approaches are gradually being replaced by learner -centered and personalized learning models that
emphasize individual needs and learning processes (Grájeda et al., 2024). By leveraging AI technologies to
collect and analyze learnin g data, instructors can more accurately monitor students’ learning progress and
provide adaptive learning support, thereby enhancing instructional effectiveness and learning quality.
As technology continues to advance, generative artificial intelligence (GenAI), as one of the most disruptive
innovations in recent years, has brought substantial and far -reaching impacts to higher education systems
(Bearman et al., 2023). In particular, since the official release of generative AI chatbots such as ChatGPT in
2022, academic discussions on AI have shifted from speculative considerations of “future potential” to
practical explorations of “current applications in instructional settings,” indicating that generative AI has
achieved a high level of feasibility and immediacy in educational practice (Jensen et al., 2024).
Generative AI is capable of rapidly processing large volumes of information, analyzing data in real time, and
producing feedback outputs, which has facilitated its swift integration into teaching and learning processes.
This integration has created both new opportunities and challenges for students in terms of knowledge
construction, problem -solving, and access to learning feedback. Prior research suggests that, when
generative AI –based feedback is implem ented within appropriate instructional designs and pedagogical
guidance, it can effectively enhance students’ learning motivation, learning performance, and overall
learning outcomes (Henderson et al., 2025).
Taken together, the literature indicates that A I is not only an important learning partner supporting
instructional innovation, but also an indispensable driving force in digital marketing and brand management.
For educational contexts such as five -year junior college programs in cosmetics application management,
which integrate both professional technical training and marketing orientation, cultivating students’ abilities
to operate AI marketing tools and to understand the data -driven decision-making logic underlying these
technologies has become a cri tical competency. Such competencies are essential for enhancing students’
career competitiveness and for responding effectively to the ongoing digital transformation of the industry.
2.2. The Relationship between AI Marketing and Brand Management in the SPA and Beauty Industry
With changes in lifestyles and the increasing awareness of personal appearance management, the beauty
and SPA industry has become an indispensable service sector in modern society. According to the Ministry of
Finance, Department of Statistics (2024), there were approximately 35,000 hairdressing and beauty-related
businesses in Taiwan in 2023, generating an annual sales revenue of NT$46.6 billion. However, as the industry
has gradually matured and market competition has intensified, the growth rates in both the number of firms
and total sales over the past five years have been only about 15.5% and 23.0%, respectively. These figures
indicate that the beauty and SPA industry is facing challenges of market saturation and brand
homogenization, highlighting an urgent need for innovative marketing approaches to enhance brand
differentiation and customer loyalty.
From the perspective of digital economic development, the Directorate General of Budget, Accounting and
Statistics, Executive Yuan (2025) reported that, driven by increasing demand for technologies such as artificial
intelligence and high-performance computing, as well as the continued expansion of domestic consumption,
Taiwan’s e-commerce transaction value in the industrial and servic e sectors reached NT$8.1097 trillion in
2024, representing a year -on-year growth rate of 17.8%. This trend suggests that digital platforms and AI
technologies have become fundamental infrastructures for business operations and brand marketing, and
also provides a favorable environment for the adoption of AI marketing tools in the beauty and SPA industry.
In the marketing domain, AI technologies —particularly generative artificial intelligence —have significantly

3. METHODOLOGY
3.1. Research Design
This study adopted an exploratory mixed-methods approach situated within a vocational education teaching
practice context. AI marketing was integrated into the course Introduction to the SPA Industry and Marketing
Management as an innovative instructional strategy aimed at cultivating students’ competencies in
professional practice, digital marketing, and AI-assisted brand management. Rather than employing a strictly
controlled experimental design, the study primarily focused on examining students’ learning experiences,
instructional responses, and application processes during the implementation  of AI -integrated teaching
activities. Therefore, the study may be regarded as both a course effectiveness evaluation and a practice -
oriented pedagogical investigation (see Figure 1).
The course was implemented over an 18 -week semester. The first half of t he course focused on traditional
theoretical instruction related to SPA brand management, while the second half incorporated AI marketing
tools and Project -Based Learning (PBL) activities. To comprehensively understand students’ learning
processes and inst ructional outcomes, this study employed quantitative questionnaires, classroom
observations, learning assessments, open -ended feedback, and stage -based qualitative interviews
conducted at the beginning, middle, and final stages of the course.
The study exa mined students’ learning performance and perceptions across three dimensions: AI Tool
Learning, AI Application Scenarios, and Future Expectations of AI. Quantitative data were used to analyze
students’ operational competencies, contextual application abili ties, and future learning intentions, while
qualitative data were employed to further explore students’ learning experiences, reflective processes, and
perceptions regarding AI-integrated vocational education.

3.2. Participants
The participants in this study were 29 students from the Department of Cosmetics Application and
Management at a technological university in northern Taiwan, all of whom enrolled in the elective course
Introduction to SPA Industry and Marketing Manageme nt and completed all instructional activities and the
final questionnaire survey. Five -year junior college programs in Taiwan generally adopt small -class and
profession-oriented teaching models, with approximately 50 students in each class. In this study, a total of
29 students selected the elective course. Since only one class was established at the corresponding grade
level, the study employed a census sampling approach (n = 29). Therefore, the findings were intended
primarily for course effectiveness eva luation and pedagogical reflection rather than for generalization to a
broader population.
This course primarily served as a career -oriented elective course designed to facilitate students’ transition
into off-campus internships. The researcher has long be en responsible for student internship coordination
and industry–academia collaboration. Through interactions with internship institutions and employers in the
beauty service industry, it was observed that current industry demands no longer focus solely on traditional
beauty-related technical skills, but increasingly emphasize interdisciplinary competencies such as AI-assisted
copywriting, self-media management, and digital marketing. Accordingly, this study sought to move beyond
conventional theory -oriented teaching approaches by integrating AI marketing tools into SPA brand
management instruction. Through innovative instructional design, the study aimed to enhance students’
learning motivation, digital application competencies, and career readiness, serving  as an exploratory
investigation into the integration of AI and digital marketing education within technical and vocational
education.

3.4. Research Instruments and Questionnaire Design
This study employed a self -developed questionnaire entitled “AI Marketing and SPA  Brand Development
Effectiveness Questionnaire” as the primary research instrument. The questionnaire items were jointly
developed by the researcher based on course objectives, departmental curriculum committee discussions,
and relevant literature. Since t his course represented an innovative teaching practice integrating AI
technology into SPA brand management, its primary purpose was to assist fourth-year students in a five-year
junior college program in developing AI marketing and digital branding competencies in preparation for fifth-

year off -campus internships and future workplace demands within the beauty industry. Therefore, the
questionnaire focused on students’ learning perceptions and cognitive experiences regarding AI tool
operation, contextual application, and future development.
The questionnaire dimensions were designed based on relevant literature. The “AI Tool Learning” dimension
was primarily informed by Hwang and Chiu’s (2021) perspectives on integrating artificial intelligence into
skills-based instruction, as well as studies by Tsai and Chang (2023) and Yeh (2023) regarding beauty
vocational education and digital learning effectiveness. The “AI Application Scenarios” dimension drew upon
studies related to digital marketing, self -media management, and beauty industry branding applications,
including Chen and Ho (2024), Huang and Chung (2023), Hua and Huang (2023), and Letitia (2024). Finally,
the “Future Expectations of AI” dimension was developed with reference to Jhuang (2019), Tsai and Chan g
(2023), and Yeh (2023), which explored vocational students’ professional competencies, career development,
and lifelong learning perspectives. These studies collectively served as the theoretical foundation for the
questionnaire structure and design of this research.
3.5 Data Analysis Procedures and Criteria
Quantitative data were analyzed using SPSS statistical software. First, descriptive statistical analysis was
conducted to calculate the mean, standard deviation, skewness, and kurtosis of each questionnaire item and
the three dimensions in order to examine students’ overall learning responses toward the AI-integrated SPA
brand course. Mean scores above 4.0 on the five -point Likert scale were interpreted as indicating positive
learning perceptions.
Second, reliability analysis was performed using Cronbach’s α to examine the internal consistency of the
questionnaire and its dimensions. Based on commonly accepted standards in social science research, α values
above .70 indicate acceptable reliability, above .80 indicate good reliability, and above .90 indicate excellent
reliability. The results showed that the Cronbach’s α values for “AI Tool Learning,” “AI Application Scenarios,”
and “Future Expectations of AI” were .971, .918, and .909, respectively, demo nstrating good to excellent
internal consistency.
Third, Pearson’s product -moment correlation analysis was employed to examine relationships among the
three dimensions. Correlation coefficients between .10 and .29 were interpreted as low, .30 to .49 as
moderate, and .50 or above as high correlations. The results revealed strong positive correlations among all
dimensions, including .981 between “AI Tool Learning” and “AI Application Scenarios,” .950 between “AI Tool
Learning” and “Future Expectations of AI,”  and .940 between “AI Application Scenarios” and “Future
Expectations of AI.”
In addition, open -ended responses and stage -based interview data were analyzed through keyword
frequency analysis, sentiment analysis, and qualitative thematic categorization to further understand
students’ learning experiences and reflections regarding AI-integrated instruction.
3.6. Research Ethics and Limitations
Prior to data collection, the study purpose and instructions were clearly explained to the students.
Participation was voluntary, and all questionnaires were completed anonymously. Data were used solely for
academic research purposes and did not affect course grades, in accordance with ethical research principles.
Given the small sample size (n = 29), the results primarily reflect learning outcomes within this specific course
and instructional context. As such, the study is practice-oriented, emphasizing in-depth understanding of the
instructional process and student feedback rather than generalizability to a broader population. Despite this
limitation, the findings offer valuable practical implications for curriculum design and pedagogical strategies
in similar technical and vocational education settings.

4. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
4.1. Mean Score Analysis
This study was conducted as a teaching practice and exploratory mixed-method study within the context of
technical and vocational education. The primary purpose was to explore the innovative instructional process,
students’ learning perceptions, and application experienc es associated with integrating AI marketing into a
SPA brand management course, rather than adopting a strictly controlled experimental research design.
Therefore, this study employed multiple sources of data for analysis, including classroom observations,
student learning outcomes, quantitative questionnaires, open-ended feedback, and stage-based qualitative
interviews, in order to present the learning processes and instructional effectiveness of AI -integrated
teaching practices.
Based on the questionnaire  content and course objectives, the questionnaire items were categorized into
three dimensions: “AI Tool Learning,” “AI Application Scenarios,” and “Future Expectations of AI.” The “AI
Tool Learning” dimension primarily measured students’ operational abili ties and learning perceptions
regarding AI-generated copywriting, presentation design, AI image generation, video production, and digital
human applications. The “AI Application Scenarios” dimension focused on evaluating students’
understanding of applying  AI technologies to SPA brand management, digital marketing, and future
workplace contexts. The “Future Expectations of AI” dimension explored students’ confidence in future AI
applications within the industry and their willingness to continue learning AI-related skills.
The results (Table 2) indicated that the mean scores of all questionnaire items were above 4.0, demonstrating
that students generally held positive attitudes toward the integration of AI marketing into the SPA brand
course. Among the items, “AI Copywriting,” “Understanding AI Applications,” and “AI Presentation Design”
achieved the highest scores, suggesting that students perceived significant learning benefits from the
immediate application of AI tools in content generation and brand planni ng tasks. However, the item
“Continued Exploration of AI Marketing and Brand Applications in the Future” received a relatively lower
score. This finding indicates that although students recognized the effectiveness of AI tools in classroom
learning and pra ctical applications, they remained relatively cautious regarding long -term autonomous
learning and sustained engagement. The results suggest that students acknowledged the short-term practical
value of AI marketing, while there remains room for further sup port in strengthening their motivation for
continuous learning and future career development.

5. CONCLUSION
This study investigated the innovative integration of AI marketing into a  SPA brand management course for
students in a five -year junior college program in Cosmetics Application and Management in Taiwan. An
exploratory mixed-methods approach was adopted to systematically examine students’ learning perceptions,
application experiences, and future development awareness in AI -integrated vocational education through
quantitative questionnaires, open -ended feedback, sentiment analysis, stage -based qualitative interviews,
and course performance assessments. Unlike strictly controlled experimental studies emphasizing causal
inference, this research focused primarily on teaching practice, curriculum innovation, and students’ learning
processes in order to explore the feasibility and educational value of integrating AI into beauty and SPA
vocational education.
The findings revealed that integrating AI marketing tools with Project -Based Learning (PBL) and situational
teaching strategies effectively enhanced students’ operational competencies, contextual application abilities,
learning engagement, and workplace-oriented thinking. Quantitative analysis demonstrated that the mean
scores for the three dimensions—“AI Tool Learning,” “AI Application Scenarios,” and “Future Expectations of
AI”—were all above 4.0, indicating highly positive student perceptions toward the AI -integrated course
design. Among these dimensions, “AI Tool Learning” achieved the highest performance, particularly in AI -
assisted copywriting, presentation design, and visual content creation. This suggests that students highly
valued AI tools capable of generating immediate and practical marketing outputs.
Furthermore, the reliability analysis demonstrated good to excellent internal consistency across all
dimensions, while the correlation analysis revealed strong positive relatio nships among “AI Tool Learning,”
“AI Application Scenarios,” and “Future Expectations of AI.” These findings suggest that as students gradually
developed proficiency in AI operational skills, they became better able to understand the practical value of
AI technologies in SPA brand management and future workplace contexts, which subsequently strengthened
their confidence and willingness to continue exploring AI marketing applications. Although the “Future
Expectations of AI” dimension received slightly lower scores than the other two dimensions, the results still
remained highly positive, indicating that while students recognized the short -term benefits of AI tools in
learning and practical applications, further support mechanisms and career -oriented instructional guidance
may still be necessary to strengthen students’ long-term motivation for autonomous learning and continuous
engagement.
Regarding objective learning outcomes, this study further incorporated a comparison between traditional
theoretical instruction and AI-integrated instructional assessment. Midterm paper-based examinations were
compared with final AI -integrated group project presentations. The results showed that students’ final
performance after the integration of AI marketing and PBL activities was generally higher than their midterm
paper-based assessment performance, and the passing rate of the final group presentations approached
100%. These findings supplement the limitations of relying solely on self-reported questionnaires and suggest
that AI -integrated instruction not only improves students’ subjective learning perceptions, but is also

Journal of AI
183
reflected in practical outputs such as brand proposals, presentation design, teamwork performance, and
applied project-based learning outcomes.
In terms of qualitative analysis, keyword frequency analysis indicated that students’ feedback was highly
concentrated on terms such as “AI,” “application,” “learning,” and “assignments,” reflecting the effectiveness
of the course’s practice -oriented and tool -based instructional design in promoting learning engagement.
Sentiment analysis further revealed that students’ responses were predominantly neutral and positive, with
nearly 40% of the responses reflecting positive emotional tendencies. Frequently occurring te rms included
“practical,” “beneficial,” and “helpful,” suggesting that students generally recognized the positive
contribution of AI marketing integration to their professional competency development. A small number of
negative responses were mainly associ ated with tool familiarity, operational complexity, and flexibility in
extended applications, indicating that future courses may continue to improve advanced operational
instruction and learning support systems.
In addition, this study employed stage-based qualitative interviews conducted at the beginning, middle, and
final stages of the course to further illustrate the transformation of students’ AI learning processes. Students
gradually progressed from unfamiliarity and limited awareness of AI technologie s during the initial stage of
the course toward practical application of AI tools for brand copywriting, visual design, and multimedia
production during the middle and final stages. By the end of the course, students further connected AI
applications to SP A brand management, off -campus internships, and future career development. Some
students reported that AI tools enhanced their creative thinking, marketing capabilities, and future
employability, while others noted that AI -generated information could occas ionally be inaccurate,
highlighting the importance of information evaluation and critical thinking skills. These findings suggest that
integrating AI into vocational education should not focus solely on operational skills, but should also
emphasize the cultivation of AI literacy, ethical awareness, and digital competencies.
From an educational perspective, this study further suggests that the value of integrating AI marketing into
SPA brand courses extends beyond improving students’ digital operational abil ities. More importantly, it
helps students establish meaningful connections between AI technologies and future workplace applications.
Current international educational trends increasingly emphasize the importance of AI literacy, AI ethics,
digital transformation, and interdisciplinary competencies. AI is no longer viewed merely as a technological
tool, but rather as an important learning medium that supports creativity, problem -solving abilities, and
autonomous learning. The findings of this study indicate  that integrating AI marketing, project -based
learning, and situational instructional strategies into beauty and SPA vocational education may help narrow
the gap between industry expectations and vocational training, while cultivating a new generation of
professionals equipped with both technical expertise and digital marketing competencies. This also reflects
an important direction for the digital transformation of vocational education curricula.
Nevertheless, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this study employed a single-group design,
and the research participants were drawn from only one five-year junior college institution, which may limit
the generalizability of the findings. Second, the study primarily focused on students’ learning per ceptions,
instructional experiences, and teaching practices rather than conducting long -term longitudinal tracking or
causal effect validation. In addition, due to the limited sample size, Exploratory Factor Analysis (EFA) and
Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA) were not conducted. Future studies should therefore employ larger
sample sizes and cross -institutional research designs to further examine the construct validity and stability
of the questionnaire dimensions.
Future research may expand the participant pool by conducting cross -institutional, cross-cultural, or cross-
industry comparisons, while also incorporating pretest -posttest designs, longitudinal studies, and
comparative analyses of different AI tools such as virtual spokespersons, AI-generated multimedia, and data

integration on students’ professional competencies, AI literacy, career development, and lifelon g learning
abilities in beauty and vocational education contexts.
Overall, this study demonstrates that integrating AI marketing into SPA brand management courses not only
enhances students’ digital application competencies and practical branding abilities, but also contributes to
the development of AI literacy, ethical awareness, and future workplace adaptability. The findings provide
important implications for curriculum innovation, AI -assisted learning, and the digital transformation of
vocational education in beauty and cosmetics-related fields.
