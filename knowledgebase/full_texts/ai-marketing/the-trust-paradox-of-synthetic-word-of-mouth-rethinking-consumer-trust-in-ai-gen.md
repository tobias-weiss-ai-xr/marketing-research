Abstract
The rapid development of generative artificial intelligence (AI) in the modern business world is
changing how brands create, interpret, personalize, and distribute their marketing communication. One
of the recent developments in AI is its growing use to ge nerate, summarize, personalize, and simulate
word-of-mouth (WOM)-like communication. Among the many emerging developments is the use of AI
to generate, summarize, personalize, and simulate word -of-mouth (WOM) -like communication.
Artificial intelligence is increasingly incorporated into consumer communication . From AI-generated
summaries of customer reviews on Amazon to AI-assisted brand communication and generative material
utilized by international brands like Coca -Cola AI is increasingly used worldwide. This development
raises an important concern.
This conceptual paper introduces the concept of synthetic word-of-mouth (synthetic WOM) to describe
AI-generated, AI-assisted, AI-augmented, or algorithmically mediated communication . It reproduces,
summarizes, amplifies, or mimics consumer-like opinions, recommendations, reviews, and brand -
related conversations. The paper develops the Trust Paradox of Synthetic WOM, which advocates that
AI can simultaneously strengthen and weaken consumer trust.
The paper draws on Source Credibility Theory, the Information Adoption Model, Persuasion Knowledge
Theory, and viewpoints on its authenticity. Based on this, it develops a conceptual framework to
elaborate how Synthetic WOM affects consumer responses. Synthetic WOM impacts on how consumers
perceive the authenticity and credibility of information. These perceptions can affect consumer trust and
intention to buy . Additionally, the framework considers factors such as AI disclosure, human
involvement, consumer AI literacy, perceived manipulation, product type, and brand reputation.
Examples from Amazon, Coca-Cola, and AI-based review and communication environments are used
to explain these relationships. The study makes the case that artificial word-of-mouth (WOM) is neither
intrinsically reliable nor unreliable. Instead, how customers view AI will determine whether it improves
or replaces real consumer information. The study contributes to marketing literature by conceptualizing
synthetic WOM as an emerging form of AI -mediated eWOM and by explaining the conditions under
which AI can become a trust-enhancing tool or a source of consumer distrust. The paper concludes with
theoretical propositions, manageria l implications, recommendations, limitations, and directions for
future empirical research.
Keywords: Synthetic word-of-mouth, artificial intelligence, generative AI, consumer trust, perceived
authenticity, eWOM, AI-generated reviews, brand communication, AI disclosure, purchase intention

1. Introduction
Traditionally, Word-of-mouth (WOM) has been regarded as one of the most influential forms of
marketing communication. Consumers often rely on the opinions and experiences of other consumers

when evaluating products and services. Compared to  conventional advertising, WOM is basically
perceived as more personal, experience-based, and less directly controlled by the firm. Consumers attach
considerable value to recommendations from friends, family members, peers, online reviewers,
influencers, and other consumers.
The digital transformation of marketing  has extended traditional WOM into electronic word -of-mouth
(eWOM). Online r eviews, social media comments, ratings, blogs, discussion forums, influencer
recommendations, and consumer -generated videos have grown to be essential elements of consumer
decision-making. Research on eWOM shows that consumers are more likely to trust and use information
when they find it useful, credible, reliable, and relevant, which can also influence their purchase
decisions. However, the rise of generative artificial intelligence is challenging a basic assumption of
WOM—that the opinions and experiences shared through it are authored by humans.
Generative AI systems can produce highly advanced text, images, videos, recommendations, reviews,
testimonials, and conversational responses. AI can also summarize thousands of genuine consumer
opinions and present them. Due to this, consumer communication environment contains messages that
are not created entirely by humans though information underlying those messages basically originates
from human consumers.
A useful real-world example is Amazon. Amazon introduced AI-generated customer-review highlights
and summarizes frequently mentioned product features and customer sentiment from customer reviews.
The company explains that these highlights are generated from its review data bank and are aimed to
help customers quickly understand those reviews. Amazon has also stated that the feature uses reviews
from verified purchases and that its AI -generated highlights are intended to represent community
opinions rather than create new consumer experiences. Therefore, the AI-generated paragraph is not
necessarily a new customer review, it may be an AI-mediated representation of current consumer WOM.
(Amazon News).
This poses a fundamental question: Does the consumer really trust the original human experiences, the
AI-generated interpretation of those experiences  and the platform that presents the  AI-generated
interpretation?
The issue becomes more complex when AI produces content that appears to represent an individual
consumer's experience which in reality is  not true. Though AI can increase information accessibility,
relevance, personalization, consistency, speed, and perceived usefulness, consumers may also question
whether AI -generated communication represents genuine human experience. This creates concerns
regarding authenticity, transparency, manipulation, and credibility. The effect was weaker when AI was
used only for editing rather than authorship.
Recent research gives evidence that consumers can respond negatively to AI authorship. Amos and
Zhang found that consumers evaluated reviews perceived as ChatGPT -generated as less useful,
trustworthy, and authentic than human-generated reviews in hotel and restaurant contexts. Additionally,
their results showed that the relationship between review source and trust was moderated by perceived
genuineness. (ScienceDirect)
These findings suggest that AI can simultaneously create greater communication efficiency and lower
perceived authenticity. This contradiction is the foundation of the present study. The paper refers to this
contradiction as the Trust Paradox of Synthetic Word-of-Mouth.
2. Background of the Study
2.1 Evolution of Word-of-Mouth (WOM)
WOM is an example of interpersonal communication . Here customers discuss their thoughts,
experiences, suggestions, and assessments of goods, services, companies, or brands. The influence of
traditional WOM can be explained by several characteristics:
 personal experience;

 perceived independence;
 social similarity;
 experiential information;
 credibility;
 trust;
 reduced perceived commercial intent.
For example, when a consumer tells a friend, "I used this smartphone for six months and the battery is
excellent," the recommendation is supported by perceived personal experience.
The communication therefore contains two types of information:
1. Product information, and
2. Experiential information.
The second element is particularly important because the reviewer is assumed to have actually used the
product.
2.2. Evolution from WOM to eWOM
The internet transformed WOM from a largely interpersonal phenomenon into a scalable communication
mechanism. Electronic Word -of-Mouth (eWOM) refers to opinions, reviews, recommendations, and
experiences shared by consumers through digital platforms. It helps consumers learn about products and
brands from the experiences and views of others.
Reviews, social media comments, ratings, blogs, and online recommendations are common forms of
eWOM.
Consumers can now access:
 Amazon reviews;
 Google reviews;
 TripAdvisor reviews;
 restaurant ratings;
 YouTube reviews;
 Instagram recommendations;
 Reddit discussions;
 influencer recommendations;
 consumer blogs;
 online community discussions.
As a result, eWOM has a far wider audience than traditional W OM. At the same time, digital eWOM
creates a credibility problem because consumers may not always know:
 who created the content;
 whether the reviewer actually used the product;
 whether the review has been sponsored;
 whether the review was edited;
 whether the review was generated by AI;
 whether the content has been manipulated.
2.3. Emergence of Synthetic Word-of-Mouth
Synthetic word-of-mouth as a distinct conceptual category within the broader eWOM environment.
Meaning
AI-generated, AI-assisted, AI-augmented, or algorithmically mediated communication that replicates,
summarizes, transforms, magnifies, or mimics customer opinions, recommendations, reviews,
testimonials, or experiences on a product, service, brand, or organization is referred to as synthetic word-
of-mouth (WOM). Synthetic WOM can occur at different levels.

 Level 1: AI-Assisted WOM
A real consumer has the experience and uses AI to improve the expression of the experience.
For example, a customer may write, “The phone is good, but the battery could be better.” If AI helps the
customer convert this simple feedback into a more structured and detailed review . The  original
experience still comes from the customer. In this case, AI only assists in presenting the experience, so
the risk to authenticity is relatively low.
Level 2: AI-Augmented WOM
AI can combine and summarize genuine consumer experiences to make reviews easier to understand.
For example, Amazon’s AI-generated review highlights. They summarize common themes and opinions
frequently mentioned by customers in their reviews. In this case, AI does not create the original
experiences it only organizes existing consumer feedback into a simpler summary.
For example, instead o f requiring consumers to read hundreds of reviews for a smartphone, AI may
summarize common opinions regarding:
 battery life;
 camera quality;
 performance;
 ease of use;
 display quality.
This is an important example because the underlying WOM is human -generated, while the
communication interface is AI-generated. (Amazon News)
Level 3: AI-Generated Brand WOM
AI can also generate recommendations or consumer-like communication using the information available
from different sources. For example, an AI shopping assistant may say, “Customers generally appreciate
this laptop for its battery life and lightw eight design.” Such a statement may appear to be a form of
aggregated word -of-mouth. However, no individual consumer may have actually used these exact
words. The AI has smartly combined available information and presented it to sound like a consumer-
oriented recommendation.
Level 4: Fully Synthetic WOM
AI may create a consumer-like testimonial or review without any genuine human experience behind it.
For example, an AI-generated statement might say, “I bought this skincare product last month and my
skin improved dramatically.” However, if no real consumer actually made this statement or experienced
the product, the message can be misleading. It may look like a genuine customer review even though it
is entirely AI-generated. This creates serious concerns abo ut authenticity and credibility. It represents
the strongest form of the Trust Paradox of Synthetic WOM.
3. AI in Contemporary Brand Communication
These days, AI is no longer limited to back-office automation. Brands are widely using AI in customer
interaction, advertising, content creation, personalization, recommendation systems, and social media.
3.1 Amazon: AI-Generated Review Highlights
Amazon provides a clear example of how AI can shape consumer communication. Its AI -generated
review highlights bring together common points from existing customer reviews. The aim is to help
customers quickly understand what other buyers are saying without reading every review individually.
Thus, AI makes consumer feedback easier and faster to understand while still drawing on real customer
experiences.
Positive trust pathway:
Human reviews → AI summary → Easier understanding → Greater usefulness → Enhanced confidence
Negative trust pathway:
Human reviews → AI summary → Doubts about AI’s interpretation → Declined confidence

3.7. The Central Problem: The Trust Paradox
The trust paradox emerges because AI-generated WOM possesses two opposing characteristics.
a) Positive side
AI can make communication:
 faster;
 more personalized;
 more accessible;
 more concise;
 more consistent;
 more comprehensive;
 easier to understand.
b) Negative side
AI may make communication:
 less personal;
 less experiential;
 less authentic;
 more commercially motivated;
 more difficult to verify;
 potentially manipulative.
AI can make information quicker, simpler, and more useful for consumers. However, when AI becomes
too involved, the human experience behind the information may feel doubtful. This balance between
convenience and authenticity is at the core of this study.

8. Conceptual Findings
a) Synthetic WOM can be conceptualized as a distinct form of digital word -of-mouth: Generative
AI creates, modifies, or amplifies consumer-like brand communication. Compared to traditional WOM,
it combines the scalability and efficiency of AI . C ommunication is designed to resemble human
recommendations.
b) Synthetic WOM may have a dual effect on perce ived authenticity : AI-generated
recommendations can appear authentic when they are relevant, personalized, and natural . But the
awareness that the content is machine-generated may decline perceptions of genuine human experience
and sincerity.
c) Perceived credibility emerges as an important factor in evaluating AI -mediated
communication: Consumers may judge AI -generated WOM based on the accuracy, consistency,
usefulness, and quality of the information. However, such high informational quality is not enough to
compensate for doubts about the source behind the message.
d) Synthetic WOM creates a trust paradox.  AI-generated recommendations can increase consumer
trust when they value efficiency, personalization, and informational usefulness, but they can also reduce
trust when consumers discover that the communication as artificial, manipulative, or lacking authentic
human experience.
e) Consumer trust is conceptually linked to purchase intention : When consumers perceive AI -
generated brand communication as authentic and trustworthy, they more develop favourable evaluations
and stronger intentions to purchase. Thus, trust can play an important psychological bridge between AI-
generated WOM and consumer behavioural intentions.
f) AI disclosure has a conditional role in consumer evaluation: Disclosure may strengthen trust and
demonstrate transparency and reduc e the idea of deception. At the same time, disclosure may lower
perceived authenticity when consumers find genuine WOM with human experience. Therefore, the
effect of disclosure is likely to be depending on how consumers interpret the role of AI.
g) Human involvement remains important in maintaining authenticity and trust : Ai generated
WOM lacks h uman oversight, personal experience, editorial judgment, and meaningful human
contribution can provide the emotional and experiential elements. Human –AI collaboration therefore
offers a more trustworthy communication approach than complete automation.
h) The Trust Paradox of Synthetic WOM can be explained through competing benefits and risks:
AI provides scalability, consistency, personalization, and efficiency  But artificial origin may create
concerns about authenticity, credibility, manipulation, and transparency. The paradox arises because the
same AI capability that makes synthetic WOM attractive later turn into a source of consumer distrust.
i) The conceptual framework suggests several relationships for future empirical testing: Synthetic
WOM created trust may subsequently influence purchase intention, while AI disclosure and human
involvement may either strengthen or weaken these relationships.

9. Recommendations
a) Brands need to use AI in WOM communication in a responsible way. Consumers should know when
AI is involved  in review and recommendation of the brand . This builds transparency and reduces the
feeling that they are being misled.
b) Human involvement is also important. AI can create the content, but human review and judgment
should be kept as a core of the process. This can make the communication more natural, relevant, and
trustworthy.
c) Brands should avoid creating fake consumer exper iences and fabricated testimonials  to impress
consumers. Such practices may create short -term attention. But they can negatively impact consumer
trust when discovered.
d) AI should not replace  human communication, It should support the experience of consumers. Its
strengths are speed, personalization, efficiency, and large-scale content creation. These benefits should
be combined with genuine human insights and experiences.
e) Companies should also check AI -generated content before publishing it. Accuracy , Relevance,
Transparency are the factors matter to be kept while creatin synthetic WOM
f) Regular monitoring of consumer responses can help brands understand whether AI-generated WOM
is accepted or creating doubts among the internet users. Employee training and clear ethical guidelines
are also necessary.
g) Brands should balance between technology and the human element. Synthetic WOM can be useful
and effective, but its long-term value depends on authenticity, responsible use of AI and trust  by the
consumers.
10. Limitations
The study is conceptual and does not empirically test the proposed relationships. Consumer responses
to Synthetic WOM may vary across cultures, product categories, age groups, and levels of AI
understanding. The framework does not cover other factors such as consumers’ AI knowledge, privacy
concerns, and distrust.
11. Scope for Future Research
Future studies can empirically test the proposed relationships using surveys or experiments. Researchers
can compare human-generated WOM, AI-generated WOM, and AI-assisted WOM as a further study.
Future research can also examine the effects of AI disclosure, human involvement, AI literacy, product
type, and cultural differences on consumer trust.
12. Conclusion
Synthetic WOM indicates an important development in AI-driven digital marketing. AI can make WOM
communication faster, personalized, and scalable . It  also creates concerns about authenticity and
genuineness. The Trust Paradox of Synthetic WOM arises because AI can improve the quality of
communication and create doubts about its human origin.
The study suggests that perceived authenticity plays an important role in developing consumer trust.
Perceived credibility also plays an important role in strengthening consumer trust. Once trust is
developed, it can influence the consumer ’s purchase intention. AI disclosure and human involvement
can further shape these relationships. Therefore, the future effectiveness of Synthetic WOM will depend
not only on technologi cal capability but also on transparency, human involvement, authenticity, and
responsible use of AI.
