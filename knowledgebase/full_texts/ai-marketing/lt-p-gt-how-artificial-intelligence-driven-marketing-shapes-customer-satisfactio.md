Abstract
The increasing use of artificial intelligence (AI) in digital marketing has revolutionized the way in which organizations
interact with their customers through personalization, automation, and data-based decision-making. AI-driven marketing
mechanisms, such as personalization algorithms, recommender systems, conversational AI, predictive customer
relationship management  analytics, and AI-based trust mechanisms, are currently embedded in the customer journey.
While previous research has been done on these technologies, the research can currently be described as fragmented
and does not consider customer satisfaction as a multidimensional result with cognitive, aﬀective, and behavioral
dimensions.
This study follows the methodology of a systematic literature review and is guided by the Preferred Reporting Items for
Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines for systematic literature reviews to synthesize existing research on AI-
driven marketing and customer satisfaction. Thirty-four peer-reviewed journal articles published between 2020 and 2025
in Scopus, IEEEXplore, and ScienceDirect were identified. A theory-based thematic analysis was conducted to synthesize
the findings across heterogeneous studies. The synthesis was informed by known theoretical frameworks, such as the
Stimulus-Organism-Response model.
The findings suggest that AI-driven marketing mechanisms may collectively influence customer satisfaction from
cognitive, aﬀective, and behavioral channels. Cognitive satisfaction is determined to a large extent by the perceived
usefulness, eﬃciency, and relevance of information made possible by the application of AI-based personalization,
recommender systems, and predictive analytics. Aﬀective satisfaction is generated through emotionally intelligent
interactions, trust, and transparency, particularly through the use of conversational and explainable AI mechanisms.
Behavioral satisfaction is manifested in the form of loyalty, repurchase intention, and advocacy when cognitive and
aﬀective needs are satisfied. The results further illustrate that these dimensions of satisfaction are not isolated results but
rather sequential and dependent on each other.
This review provides an integrated understanding of the role of AI-based marketing in customer satisfaction across
multiple dimensions. It highlights significant areas of research gaps, including the lack of direct measurement of
satisfaction, longitudinal and cross-country research, and consideration of the ethical and trust-related design of AI. By
bringing together fragmented evidence into a coherent picture, this study oﬀers useful information for future research
and the responsible design of systems to ensure customer satisfaction.

Introduction And Background
Artificial Intelligence (AI) has become a game-changing factor in reshaping marketing and consumer engagement
processes in the digital economy. Through capabilities such as predictive analytics, machine learning, natural language
processing, and recommendation algorithms, AI helps marketers process large amounts of data, anticipate consumer
behavior and provide highly personalized experiences in real time (Haleem et al., 2022) and (Mustak et al., 2021). Unlike
traditional marketing approaches, which are based on historical insights or general segmentation, AI-driven marketing is
based on continuous learning, contextual adaptation, and automated decision-making, and can be seen to be a
fundamental shift in the way organizations interact and fulfil the needs of their customers (Rabby et al., 2021). The
incorporation of AI into marketing has transformed the relationship between customers and brands, as consumers are
becoming less engaged with traditional marketing methods and more interested in interacting with intelligent systems
such as chatbots, virtual assistants, recommender systems, and automated content generators at every step of the
customer journey (Vebrianti et al., 2025) and (Akdemir and Bulut, 2024). As these technologies become more
autonomous and become integrated into the consumption process, customer satisfaction, for example which has long
been recognized as a cornerstone of marketing success, is increasingly recognized as a multidimensional construct
formed by both technological performance and experiential quality (Mittameedi et al., 2025).
AI is a computer-based system capable of performing tasks that usually require human intelligence, such as learning,
reasoning, perception, and interaction (Haleem et al., 2022). In marketing contexts, AI includes technologies such as
machine learning algorithms, natural language processing, recommender systems, and predictive analytics that help
firms automate decision-making, personalize content, and optimize customer interactions (Mustak et al., 2021) and
(Rabby et al., 2021). Accordingly, the application of AI in marketing can be defined as the strategic use of AI technologies
to optimize marketing functions such as targeting, personalization, communication, and customer relationship
management (Lakshmi et al., 2025).
Customer satisfaction represents a customer’s overall evaluative judgment of a product, service, or interaction; it is how
far expectations are achieved or exceeded (Mensah, 2022). In an AI-mediated context, satisfaction can only be
understood as a multidimensional construct, which involves cognitive satisfaction, which relates to perceptions of
usefulness, eﬃciency, and accuracy of decision-making; aﬀective satisfaction, which refers to emotional responses such
as trust, comfort, and enjoyment; and behavioral satisfaction, which manifests in outcomes such as loyalty, repurchase
intention, and advocacy (Hsu and Lin, 2023) and (Ngo et al., 2025). This multidimensional perspective provides a
comprehensive lens for examining customer experience in AI-driven marketing environments, where rational
evaluations, emotional engagement, and behavioral responses are closely intertwined (Pahrudin et al., 2025).
Despite the broad integration of AI into marketing practices, the academic literature is fragmented in terms of answering
the question of how AI-driven mechanisms interact to influence customer satisfaction throughout the customer journey.
Existing research tends to focus either on isolated technologies, such as recommender systems, chatbots, or
personalization engines without truly analyzing the combined impact on the outcomes of satisfaction (Alamdari et al.,
2020) and (Necula and Păvăloaia, 2023). Moreover, some studies focus on performance indicators, technology adoption,
or purchase intention and provide few empirical evaluations of satisfaction as a multidimensional psychological result
(Mustak et al., 2021) and (Rabby et al., 2021). This fragmented understanding restricts the development of integrative
frameworks capable of explaining how AI-driven marketing mechanisms jointly influence cognitive (belief-based),
aﬀective (emotion-based), and behavioral (action-based) satisfaction dimensions (Mittameedi et al., 2025). Recent
systematic reviews confirm that while AI-driven personalization and targeting dominate the marketing literature,
empirical investigation of long-term behavioral satisfaction and ethical implications remains limited (Abreu et al., 2025).
Therefore, this research aims to systematically review and synthesize available literature to answer the following research
question: How do various AI-driven marketing mechanisms such as personalization, recommender systems,
conversational AI, predictive customer relationship management (CRM) analytics and AI-based trust mechanisms jointly
determine the cognitive, aﬀective and behavioral dimensions of customer satisfaction? By analyzing peer-reviewed
studies published between 2020 and 2025, this review constructs an integrated and theory-informed understanding of

Review
This review was conducted by using a structured and transparent method of systematic review that was based on the
PRISMA framework. Four stages were included in the protocol: identification, screening, eligibility, and final inclusion. This
research sought to combine the latest findings from empirical and review research on the joint influence of various AI-
driven marketing mechanisms on the cognitive, aﬀective and behavioral dimensions of customer satisfaction, such as
personalization, recommender systems, conversational AI, predictive CRM analytics, and AI-based trust mechanisms.

Screening
Following the elimination of 74 duplicate and ineligible records, a total of 516 records proceeded to the screening phase.
During this stage, titles and abstracts were systematically reviewed to determine their preliminary relevance to the
research question for this study. The screening process was conducted by the authors following predefined inclusion and
exclusion criteria, listed in Table 2,  to ensure consistency in study selection. In case of ambiguities, decisions were
discussed and resolved carefully to ensure alignment with the study objectives.
Each record was assessed to determine whether it had explicit content of artificial intelligence in the context of digital
marketing and also whether it discussed customer satisfaction either in an empirical or conceptual manner. Studies that
did not adequately align with the research objectives, research studies that did not have a clear focus on AI-driven
marketing mechanisms, or research studies that did not meaningfully address customer satisfaction were excluded at this
stage of the study. As a result, 482 records were excluded using title and abstract screening. The remaining 34 articles
showed possible relevance and were therefore moved on to the full-text eligibility assessment stage. This structured
screening procedure ensured that the screening process was transparent from a methodological perspective and that the
chance of subjective inclusion decisions was limited. 
To enhance methodological transparency and reproducibility, the screening process followed a structured protocol
guided by the predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria presented in Table 2. Titles, abstracts, and full texts were
systematically assessed to determine their alignment with the research objective. Decisions made during each stage of
the review process were documented to maintain consistency in article selection. This structured approach ensured that
study inclusion was based on conceptual relevance and methodological suitability rather than subjective judgment.
Eligibility
Following the screening of the title and abstract, 34 articles were identified as potentially relevant and hence sought for
full-text retrieval. In addition, backward and forward citation tracking of these studies produced six additional studies
that satisfied the preliminary screening criteria. As a result, 40 full-text articles were considered for eligibility. During the
full-text evaluation stage, each article was carefully evaluated based on the predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria
presented in Table 2. The assessment was focused on whether the study explicitly discussed artificial intelligence in the
digital marketing context and empirically or conceptually analyzed customer satisfaction dimensions. Studies that were
not suﬃciently aligned with the research objectives, did not discuss the marketing mechanisms driven by AI in suﬃcient
detail, or did not have a meaningful discussion of customer satisfaction were excluded at this stage.
As a result of this eligibility assessment, six articles were excluded because they did not satisfy the final inclusion criteria.
The other 34 studies were considered suitable and included in the systematic review. This structured eligibility evaluation
ensured methodological rigor and increased the transparency and reliability of the study selection procedure.
Final inclusion criteria
This study was guided by the inclusion and exclusion criteria listed in Table 1. The search was conducted for peer-
reviewed journal articles and conference proceedings articles written in English that satisfied the criteria outlined in Table
2. The criteria were publications between 2020 and 2025 that were empirical in nature and related to artificial
intelligence, digital marketing, and customer satisfaction. Studies that fell outside of this scope were excluded from the
review.
Articles that did not address artificial intelligence, digital marketing, and customer satisfaction were removed from the
initial screening. However, owing to limited access to some of the articles and the apparent scarcity of relevant literature
in this field, a few summarized peer-reviewed articles were also considered because these articles provided insights into
customer satisfaction factors. While empirical studies formed the primary focus of this review, a limited number of
conceptual and review-based articles were also retained because they provided theoretical explanations of AI-driven
marketing mechanisms and their relationship with customer satisfaction. These studies were primarily used to support
conceptual interpretations rather than to draw empirical generalizations.

Reviewer bias
To minimize reviewer bias, the screening and selection processes were conducted systematically, following predefined
inclusion and exclusion criteria. Where ambiguity arose, decisions were resolved through discussion to ensure
consistency. The use of structured screening protocols and transparent documentation of each selection stage further
reduced subjective influence. Although systematic reviews inherently involve interpretative judgment, eﬀorts were made
to maintain objectivity and methodological rigor throughout the review process.
This review did not apply a formal risk of bias or quality appraisal tool. Instead, study credibility was ensured through the
application of strict inclusion criteria, focusing on peer-reviewed publications and conceptual relevance to AI-driven
marketing and customer satisfaction. While this approach ensures thematic relevance, future systematic reviews may
benefit from incorporating formal quality appraisal frameworks to further strengthen methodological rigor.
Findings
This systematic review synthesizes findings from 34 peer-reviewed studies published between 2020 and 2025 to examine
how diﬀerent AI-driven marketing mechanisms, including personalization, recommender systems, conversational AI,
predictive CRM analytics, and AI-based trust mechanisms, collectively influence the cognitive, aﬀective, and behavioral
dimensions of customer satisfaction. The analysis shows that although there is a consistent relationship between AI
mechanisms and higher satisfaction, the type and magnitude of the relationship depend upon the type of technology
and the dimension of satisfaction measured.

including generative AI and predictive analytics, in digital marketing ecosystems.
Identified Themes in AI-Driven Marketing Research
The thematic analysis of the reviewed studies showed five main AI-driven marketing mechanisms that reoccur in the
reviewed studies, as shown in Figure 5. These themes are the dominant ways in which artificial intelligence is being used
to influence customer experience and customer satisfaction in digital marketing contexts. The first theme, AI-based
personalization, refers to the use of machine learning algorithms to personalize content, recommendations, and oﬀers to
individual customer preferences and behaviors. The second theme, recommender systems, focuses on algorithmic tools
designed to support decision-making by suggesting relevant products or services based on user data. The third theme,
conversational AI, includes chatbots and voice-based assistants that allow real-time interactive communication between
firms and customers. The fourth theme, predictive CRM  analytics, is the use of AI to anticipate customer needs,
preferences, and behaviors, in order to enable proactive engagement and retention strategies. Finally, AI-driven trust and
transparency mechanisms, such as explainable AI (XAI), privacy assurances, and ethical governance practices, can help to
build confidence and trust among users regarding AI-mediated interactions. Together, these themes provide a basis for
analyzing the impact of AI-driven marketing mechanisms on customer satisfaction in terms of the cognitive, aﬀective,
and behavioral dimensions, as discussed in the following sections.

A broader examination of the reviewed studies also reveals a noticeable imbalance in the way customer satisfaction has
been investigated in AI-driven marketing contexts. Most empirical studies prioritize cognitive outcomes such as
perceived usefulness, eﬃciency, and decision accuracy, particularly in relation to personalization systems and
recommender algorithms. In contrast, comparatively fewer studies examine aﬀective responses such as emotional trust,
perceived empathy, and comfort in AI-mediated interactions, and even fewer empirically investigate behavioral
outcomes such as long-term loyalty or advocacy. This pattern suggests that the existing literature remains largely
technology-centric, focusing on performance eﬃciency rather than the emotional and relational dimensions of AI-
enabled customer experiences.
Cognitive Dimension: Eﬃciency, Relevance, and Decision Confidence
The cognitive dimension of customer satisfaction reflects rational evaluations related to the usefulness, accuracy, and
eﬃciency of AI-driven systems. Across the reviewed literature, cognitive satisfaction was the most widely studied
dimension, and it was included in most of the studies. AI-driven mechanisms generally foster improved perceptions of
usefulness, decision eﬃciency, and information relevance, which are well-established antecedents of cognitive
satisfaction.
Studies on AI-based personalization have shown that customers are more satisfied when AI technologies adapt
recommendations, contents and promotional oﬀers to their individual preferences (Mustak et al., 2021) and (Haleem et
al., 2022). Personalized marketing cuts down search eﬀort and cognitive overload allowing quicker and more confident
buying decisions to be made. Similarly, studies on recommender systems show that algorithmic recommendations
increase the accuracy of product discovery and improve the quality of decisions, therefore reducing choice-fatigue and
increasing the perceived-value of the decisions provided (Alamdari et al., 2020) and (Necula and Păvăloaia, 2023).
Predictive CRM analytics is another aspect of cognitive satisfaction by facilitating anticipation of engagement, where
firms proactively anticipate customer needs and provide solutions (Rabby et al., 2021). This type of responsiveness
promotes the feeling of control and convenience. However, several studies caution that excessive automation and limited
algorithmic transparency may undermine perceived autonomy and trust, highlighting the importance of user-centred
and XAI design (Haleem et al., 2022).
Aﬀective Dimension: Emotional Engagement, Trust, and Enjoyment
The aﬀective aspect of satisfaction involves the emotional reaction of customers to AI-mediated communication and is
impacted by conversational AI and trust mechanisms the most. One of the categories of satisfaction, aﬀective, which is
explored in a large percentage of the studies reviewed, includes feelings of trust, empathy, enjoyment, and comfort.
Empirical research on AI chatbots consistently shows that responsiveness, conversational tone, and perceived empathy
enhance emotional connections and perceived care (Hsu and Lin, 2023) and (Ngo et al., 2025). Users, who perceive
themselves as being understood by conversational agents, indicate a higher level of emotional comfort and satisfaction.
Equally, AI assistants, or voice AI, create aﬀective interactions through creating human-like interactions, which also help
to create emotional associations with the brand (Mustak et al., 2021).

Algorithmic transparency, XAI, and privacy assurance, which can be implemented with AI, are also important in aﬀective

compile proposals or solutions, emotional safety and readiness to use them increase (Haleem et al., 2022) and (Necula
and Păvăloaia, 2023). On the other hand, sentiment analysis and opaque AI systems studies have shown that the absence
of explainability may cause anxiety and feelings of manipulation, thus lowering emotional satisfaction (Alamdari et al.,
2020).
Behavioral Dimension: Loyalty, Repurchase, and Advocacy
Behavioral satisfaction is a manifestation of customer behavior resulting from positive  aﬀective and cognitive
experiences, such as loyalty, repurchase intention, and advocacy. Most studies have discussed this dimension as a result
of AI-driven customer experiences rather than as a separate construct.
Personalization and recommender systems fueled by AI are often related to high rates of repurchase behavior and
customer loyalty. Researchers exploring the role of AI-based brand experiences and AI-assisted try-on systems in
shopping and luxury settings discovered that emotionally relevant AI experiences were proven to have a strong influence
on brand attachment and intention to buy again (Mittameedi et al., 2025) and (Pahrudin et al., 2025). It has also been
reported that conversational AI can be used to positively impact post-purchase customer experience, specifically, by
providing easy access to issues and prompt assistance, promoting continued use of the platform and positive word of
mouth (Hsu and Lin, 2023).
Predictive CRM analytics is also useful in enhancing behavioral satisfaction by enabling specific loyalty programs and
being proactive in churn prevention (Rabby et al., 2021). Nonetheless, the prevalence of cross-sectional research designs,
multiple review studies observing, restricts the insight about how behavioral satisfaction changes over time, and it is why
longitudinal studies on the topic of sustained AI customer relationships should be conducted (Mustak et al., 2021) and
(Haleem et al., 2022). Recent qualitative evidence further suggests that AI integration across marketing and customer
service functions enhances customer journey continuity and long-term loyalty, while simultaneously raising ethical and
transparency considerations that may influence sustained behavioral satisfaction (Oanh, 2024).
Integrative Discussion
These results indicate that cognitive, aﬀective, and behavioral satisfaction are mutually dependent eﬀects of AI-based
marketing. Aﬀective satisfaction follows cognitive satisfaction, which is a result of informational relevance and eﬃciency,
promoting confidence and trust in AI systems. This emotional trust then leads to behavioral loyalty and advocacy,
creating a chain of pathways aligned with the SOR model (Mustak et al., 2021).
Personalization, conversational AI, and predictive CRM analytics combine to form a positive feedback loop of satisfaction,
in which precise assessments lead to increased emotional bonds, positive feelings, and entrenched behavioral retention.
Nevertheless, the ethical considerations and humanization of AI are critical for the long-term sustainability of satisfaction.
Consistent with recent integrative analyses, AI adoption not only improves personalization and eﬃciency but also
reshapes customer journeys holistically, linking technological optimization with the experiential and ethical dimensions
of satisfaction (Oanh, 2024).
The synthesis shows that the notion of customer satisfaction in AI-based marketing is a multidimensional and integrative
phenomenon formed under the influence of the collective eﬀect of personalization, recommender systems,
conversational AI, predictive CRM analytics, and trust practices. It has been shown that cognitive satisfaction, which is
based on eﬃciency, relevance, and decision support, is the basis of aﬀective satisfaction, whether it is in the form of trust,
enjoyment, or emotional engagement, which in turn converts to behavioral outcomes in the form of product loyalty and
intention to repurchase. Critically, papers that research the mechanism of AI in isolation provide incomplete explanations;
however, integrative approaches are more eﬀective in explaining sequential and reinforcing relationships between the
dimensions of satisfaction.  Despite generally positive findings, the reviewed studies show limited methodological
diversity, with a strong reliance on cross-sectional survey designs and self-reported measures. This restricts the ability to
validate causal relationships and highlights the need for more robust empirical approaches.

Theoretical Integration Using the SOR Framework
This review can be interpreted through the SOR framework, which explains how environmental stimuli influence internal
cognitive and emotional evaluations that subsequently shape behavioral responses (Mehrabian and Russell, 1974). In
that regard, AI-based marketing mechanisms, which comprise of personalization, recommender systems, conversational
AI, predictive CRM analytics, and AI-based trust mechanisms can be qualified as stimuli, which influence the internal
reflective and aﬀective assessment of customers, including usefulness perception, trust, and emotional involvement
(Haleem et al., 2022), (Mustak et al., 2021), and (Mittameedi et al., 2025). Such organism reactions in turn have behavioral
consequences, both in terms of loyalty, repurchase intention and advocacy as has been shown in research on AI-enabled
customer experience and chatbot interactions (Hsu and Lin, 2023), (Ngo et al., 2025), and (Vebrianti et al., 2025). This
paper supports the applicability of the SOR framework to the context of multidimensional customer satisfaction in AI-
based marketing environments by specifically mapping the mechanisms of AI to stimuli, the dimensions of satisfaction to
organismic state, and the loyalty-related outcomes to responses (Rabby et al., 2021) (Lakshmi et al., 2025). Consistent with
prior systematic analyses, this review underscores the need to integrate ethical AI and trust constructs into satisfaction
theory to capture the evolving dynamics of AI-driven marketing environments (Abreu et al., 2025).
Theoretical Implications
This review is a conceptual step toward marketing and consumer behavior theory since it conceptualizes customer
satisfaction as a multidimensional product of AI-assisted interactions. The results build on the current satisfaction models
by making an addition of technological intelligence (algorithmic decision-making), emotional intelligence (aﬀective
engagement), and ethical intelligence (trust and transparency) to the model. Accordingly, AI is not only the tool of
transactions, but the cognitive-aﬀective co-producer of customer experiences.
Furthermore, the XAI integration into the research of satisfaction is a conceptual avenue through which transparency,
accountability, and fairness can be studied as the key factors of satisfaction in automated settings (Haleem et al., 2022).
Practical Implications
From a managerial perspective, this review can provide practical recommendations to build AI-enabled marketing
systems that will prove sustainable in terms of increasing customer satisfaction. Another goal that firms are encouraged
to focus on is the algorithmic transparency, that is, by stating how AI-generated suggestions and personalization logic
work to users (Haleem et al., 2022). Emotionally intelligent and adaptive communication can also be used to humanize
conversational AI, which would enhance aﬀective satisfaction and decrease customer anxiety (Hsu and Lin, 2023). The
combination of real-time sentiment analysis and predictive analytics allows the companies to react empathetically to the
needs of their customers and anticipate them (Rabby et al., 2021). The customer satisfaction will never be compromised
by privacy issues because an ethical personalization practice should be reinforced with the help of well-developed data
governance and user consent systems (Mustak et al., 2021). Lastly, AI must be implemented as a whole, throughout the
pre-purchase, purchase, and post-purchase process to guarantee satisfaction continuation in the customer journey.
Conclusions
This systematic review summarizes current studies investigating the impact of AI-based marketing processes on
customer satisfaction across cognitive, aﬀective, and behavioral dimensions. Combining evidence from various empirical
and conceptual studies, this review reveals that AI-based personalization, recommender systems, conversational AI,
predictive CRM analytics, and trust-enhancing mechanisms influence customer satisfaction in interconnected and
sequential ways. These mechanisms appear to influence the eﬃciency of decision-making, emotional engagement, and
behavioral intentions, such as loyalty. However, given that many of the reviewed studies rely on cross-sectional designs
and short-term measures, these relationships should be interpreted as indicative patterns rather than confirmed causal
pathways.

In addition to uniting scattered evidence, this review has gone a step further to advance current knowledge base by
positioning customer satisfaction as a product of both technological and psychological activities mediated by the factors
of trust, transparency, and emotional intelligence. The synthesis shows that although the available literature has achieved
major strides in the explanation of eﬃciency- and performance-based results, the aﬀective and ethical aspects of
satisfaction are relatively under-researched. 
Consequently, subsequent AI-based marketing research needs to shift to eﬃciency-based results, but make it more
ethical, emotionally sensitive, and longitudinal analyses of customer satisfaction. To create sustainable and human-
centered AI-based marketing systems, more focus on the mechanisms of trust, XAI, and cross-cultural dynamics is
needed. Such activities will not only enhance theoretical frameworks but also help practitioners develop AI technologies
that promote long-term customer relationships in highly automated and digital marketplaces.
Limitations and future research directions
Although 34 studies were included in this review, several methodological and contextual weaknesses were noted. In
addition, although searches were conducted across major databases such as Scopus, ScienceDirect, and IEEEXplore, the
final inclusion of studies was partly influenced by institutional accessibility constraints, which may have limited the
inclusion of some relevant subscription-based articles. Most studies are cross-sectional and focus on advanced markets.
Longitudinal, cross-cultural, and experimental designs should be used in the future to gauge the long-term eﬀects of AI
on user satisfaction. Satisfaction with emotions, hybrid AI models, and negative outcomes of AI are underdeveloped.
Furthermore, the lack of standardized measurement instruments for AI-specific satisfaction warrants scale development
attempts that consider aﬀective, cognitive, and ethical aspects. Despite the systematic approach adopted in this review,
several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the review included only studies accessible through institutional
databases and open-access sources, which may introduce potential selection bias. Second, many of the studies included
in the review relied on cross-sectional research designs, limiting the ability to draw strong causal conclusions regarding
long-term behavioral outcomes. Future research should therefore employ longitudinal and experimental designs to
better understand the dynamic relationship between AI-driven marketing mechanisms and customer satisfaction.
New studies should also explore the mediation between customer satisfaction and trust in digital ecosystems through AI
governance models, such as explainability, accountability, and fairness.
