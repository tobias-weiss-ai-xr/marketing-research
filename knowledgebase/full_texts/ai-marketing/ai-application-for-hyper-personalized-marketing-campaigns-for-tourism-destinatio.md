Abstract: The integration of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in the domain of tourism marketing holds
considerable promise for the development of highly customized campaigns, thereby enhancing audience
engagement and reinforcing—or even creating—territorial narratives. The objective of this study is to
analyze how artificial intelligence (AI) is applied in tourism marketing campaigns for hyper -
personalization and how it influences territorial narratives, taking sustainability signals into account.
Therefore, two marketing campaigns are analysed: Portugal is Art and Bulgaria's digital campaign in
partnership with Google Ads. The two systems utilize artificial intelligence for the purposes of
algorithmic segmentation and media optimization. However, they adopt divergent approaches to
narrative and  sustainability signals. The methodology employed is comparative, encompassing
document analysis and observation of elements of personalization, territorial identity, and sustainable
practices. The findings indicate that the degree of integration between t echnology and territorial
positioning exerts a significant influence on the outcomes of AI, particularly in terms of perceived
personalization and the coherence of the destination narrative. The conclusion drawn from this analysis
is that the effectiveness  of hyper-personalization via AI depends on the existence of well -established
territorial narratives and the integration between technology, identity, and sustainability.

Keywords: AI Application; Tourism Destinations; Marketing Campaigns; immersive experiences.

1. Introduction

Contemporary tourism is undergoing a digital transformation characterized by the use of
artificial intelligence (AI) for the segmentation and personalization of marketing campaigns.
This technology facilitates the customization of messages based on various criteria, including
profile, behavior, and travel intentions, thereby exerting a substantial impact on consumers. The
efficacy of personalization is contingent upon the aggregation of pertinent data through the
aforementioned insights, which facilitate the identification of consumption patterns, thereby
enabling the creation of customized experiences that align with consumer preferences.
Consequently, companies are compelled to devise superior promotional strategies through the
implementation of targeted marketing initiatives, thereby ensuring customer satisfaction (Basu,
Aktar, and Kumar, 2024; Spais and Jain, 2025).
This integrated marketing technology fulfills consumers' needs, desires, and preferences while
fostering a long -term, personalized relationship with companies through programmed
interactions between the brand and the consumer. This shift toward marketing personalization
is particularly significant in  the context of consumers' demands for more impactful and
satisfying experiences that meet their needs, thereby creating differentiation amid the
information overload that exists in the competitive digital marketplace (Gao, Liu, and Zhang,
2023; Kumar, Ashraf, and Nadeem, 2024).
The integration of immersive technologies, including augmented reality and virtual reality, into
smart ecosystems has precipitated a transformation in the tourist experience across multiple
domains. This evolution has given rise to novel forms of consumption and interaction with the
tourism product, thereby reshaping the tourism landscape. The development of this technology

Notwithstanding this scenario, extant literature on the subject tends to address AI -driven
marketing effectiveness, personalization technologies, and destination branding as relatively
discrete domains. The interaction between AI -based hyper -personalization and territorial
narratives, particularly in destinations with varying degrees of narrative maturity, remains a
subject of limited understanding.
This discrepancy is of critical importance, as technology alone does not define destination
positioning; rather, it amplifies pre -existing symbolic structures and governance dynamics
embedded in territorial narratives (Almeida, 2018). From this perspective, the research problem
addressed in this study lies in understanding how AI -driven hyper-personalization effectively
interacts with territorial narrative maturity and sustainability positioning in tourism
destinations, and to what extent these relationships influence the coherence and differentiation
of destination marketing strategies. This problem can be addressed through a comparative case
study approach, which allows for the observation of distinct configurations of technology use,
narrative structure, and sustainability signaling in real-world campaigns.
Consequently, this study contributes to the advancement of the debate by examining the
operational dynamics of AI application in tourism marketing campaigns in conjunction with
territorial narrative maturity and sustainability positioning. This study offer s a more nuanced
understanding of the interplay between technological innovation, narrative structures, and
sustainability in contemporary destination marketing strategies.

2. Theoretical Reference

2.1. Tourism Destinations and Marketing

In the domain of tourism literature, tourist destinations are typically conceptualized as dynamic
entities, susceptible to geographical and economic transformations due to internal and external
forces. These destinations are frequently depicted as comprisi ng a vast network of entities,
activities, and processes. These entities can be regarded as a system that aims to preserve
territorial integrity, specialize in the services offered, and ensure the availability of
infrastructure, as well as the quality and variety of tourist attractions, hospitality, and safety.
The fundamental dynamic of this system is to stimulate the concentration of tourist activity by
offering a tourist experience that is both attractive and satisfying for visitors (Baggio, 2020;
Emmendoerfer and Mediotte, 2022; Dudnyk, Pestushko, and Saichuk, 2023; Bernaki and
Marso, 2023).
Local stakeholders in a destination —such as tourism companies, government authorities, and
the local community—play a pivotal role in creating realistic and attractive tourism offerings
that promote local sustainability and quality tourism experiences. Thi s objective is
accomplished by implementing a balanced management model that consistently surpasses
visitor expectations within the context of a highly competitive global market. In this sense, the
centrality of the destination and its resources to a struc tured framework at local, regional, and
global levels has the potential to facilitate the management and promotion of the tourism
interests of the various stakeholders in the destination through strategic planning. This strategic
planning facilitates the d evelopment of local and regional marketing initiatives, thereby
enhancing the economic, social, and cultural resources of tourism. This enhancement is
achieved through the continuous attraction and retention of visitors to the destination (Bernaki
and Marso, 2023).
In this context, it is imperative for destinations and companies to differentiate themselves, not
only by offering more competitive prices and creating unique experiences, but also through an
increasingly concrete commitment to integrating new technologies  that facilitate more

a paradigm shift from traditional demographic segmentation to dynamic "personalized
marketing" (Vinerean and Opreana , 2024). By leveraging machine learning and predictive
analytics, AI enables organizations to interpret substantial volumes of data in real time. This
facilitates the generation of highly customized offers that adapt to the unique behaviors of
individual consumers and the specific circumstances of their situation (Rolando, 2024). This
transition is imperative for contemporary destinations as they endeavor to meet the escalating

The present study employs a qualitative, mixed -methods research design based on a
comparative multiple-case study approach (Yin, 2018). This design was strategically selected
to facilitate an in-depth examination of the application of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in tourism
marketing campaigns. It also facilitates a cross -case comparison to identify qualitative
differences in narrative strategies and sustainability signals.

5. Discussion

A comparative analysis of case studies and GT data reveals significant structural disparities
between Portugal and Bulgaria in the way they construct their territorial narratives and integrate
artificial intelligence (AI) into their tourism marketing strat egies. Portugal exhibits a higher
degree of narrative sophistication, as evidenced by its symbolic coherence, prominent
international brand visibility, and search patterns indicative of advanced stages in the travel
decision-making process.
Conversely, Bulgaria exhibits comparatively limited narrative sophistication, as evidenced by
the predominance of exploratory searches, such as inquiries into the location, climate, and
safety of the destination. Additionally, Bulgaria exhibits a paucity o f symbolic consolidation
and modest global visibility. These elements suggest that the destination is still in the early
stages of narrative structuring, with less capacity for differentiation in the international tourism
market.
With regard to the employment of artificial intelligence (AI), both the subject campaigns under
scrutiny utilize segmentation and algorithmic optimization. However, Portugal employs AI in
a manner consistent with its identity narrative, thereby reinforcing the cultural and aesthetic
attributes of the destination. In contrast, Bulgaria employs a technical approach that prioritizes
digital performance, emphasizing reach and efficiency, while eschewing symbolic or distinctive
elements. The comparative analysis indicates tha t the use of AI alone does not guarantee
meaningful personalization or the strengthening of territorial identity. Technology has been
demonstrated to augment, rather than supplant, meticulously structured narrative
methodologies.
However, a more critical perspective must be taken regarding the use of artificial intelligence
in tourism marketing. While artificial intelligence (AI) facilitates more precise targeting and
engagement, it also raises significant ethical concerns related to the construction and potential
manipulation of territorial narratives, particularly when such narratives are understood as
strategic assets within broader processes of territorial development and governance (Almeida,
2018; Almeida et al., 2025). AI -driven personalization has the potential to contribute to the
creation of idealized or "programmed" representations of destinations (Yu and Meng, 2025),
where algorithmically generated content selectively amplifies certain attributes while omitting
others. Thi s process has the potential to result in the distortion of local identities, the

6. Conclusion

A comparative analysis of the "Portugal is Art" campaigns and Bulgaria's digital campaign
reveals that artificial intelligence (AI) plays a significant role in the hyper -personalization of
tourism marketing actions. However, the effectiveness of this integ ration of technology,
territorial narrative, and sustainability is crucial for achieving optimal results. The findings
indicate that AI does not operate in isolation; rather, it serves to reinforce preexisting narratives
and to unveil deficiencies in destinations where the symbolic positioning has not yet been fully
developed.

In the case of Portugal, the application of AI is aligned with a mature territorial narrative,

established narrative, AI frequently leads to hyper -personalization with limited strategic
significance, resulting in communication that is generic and undifferentiated.
With respect to sustainability, both campaigns exhibit deficiencies, primarily concerning the

a circumstance that presents a strategic opportunity in light of the escalating significance of
responsible tourism.

hyper-personalization possesses the capacity to both fortify and diminish territorial narratives.
Technology has the capacity to augment the presence and impact of de stinations that already
possess a well -established identity. Conversely, destinations with limited narrative maturity
may perpetuate generic or ambiguous perceptions. Consequently, the efficacy of AI should be
regarded as a complementary tool, the effectiveness of which is contingent upon its integration
with preexisting narrative, symbolic, and sustainable strategies.

the limited sample size precludes the possibility of statist ical generalization of the findings.
Instead, the results should be interpreted as analytically transferable insights, highlighting
patterns and relationships that may inform future research with broader empirical coverage.
