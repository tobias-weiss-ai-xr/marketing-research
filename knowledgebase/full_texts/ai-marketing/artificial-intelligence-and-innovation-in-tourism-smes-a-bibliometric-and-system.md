Abstract
While Artificial Intelligence (AI) has the potential to spur innovation
among tourism small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), the
mechanisms through which this is likely to occur under resource
constraints are less well specified. By synthesizing bibliometric
mapping with a systematic literature review (SLR), we examine
tourism research from 2020 to 2025 in Scopus-indexed sources,
comprising 71 studies identified from an initial search of 387 records.
The bibliometric analysis establishes the publication and thematic
landscape, while the SLR extracts the theorized and conceptual
framing from the academic literature. Combining the bibliometric and
SLR contributions, the study develops a constraint-enabled, theory-
based framework that hypothesizes how AI capability in tourism SMEs
might be associated with innovation outcomes via three proposed
mediators: digital self-efficacy, epistemic curiosity, and knowledge
integration, which are theorized to be enabled by institutional support
and moderated by environmental constraints. Significantly, the study
does not empirically test these propositions in this paper, thereby
positioning the contribution as a theory-based synthesis and research
framework rather than an empirical examination of AI and innovation.
The study concludes by outlining propositions and a methodological
research agenda to be tested in future tourism investigations in
developing and emerging economies.

Introduction
Radical architectural changes and innovations led by artificial intelligence (AI) are reshaping the global tourism system.
AI technologies are fundamentally transforming the way value is created, distributed, and consumed (Sjödin et al., 2023).
Through their unique capacity to handle huge amounts of data in real time, provide intelligent process automation
capabilities, and offer AI-enhanced predictive and personalization solutions (Inavolu, 2024). These advances represent a
profound change in the nature of the services provided from reactive to proactive. Within this environment, organizations
compete along the traditionally defined parameters of the tourist experience (e.g., price and quality) but increasingly
leverage the capabilities of their intelligent systems to drive customer engagement, enhance internal processes, and adapt
more quickly to environmental shifts ( Gupta et al., 2020 ).
However, these innovations and new perspectives have produced an increasingly limited, narrowly conceived discourse
on the impact of AI in tourism. For example, these discussions tend to concentrate solely on large enterprises and
technologically advanced destinations; they neglect the fact that in this particular context, SMEs dominate the tourism
system and tourism provides widespread employment, promotes regional development, and contributes to the compet-
itive edge that tourism destinations aim to possess, and at best fails to acknowledge that. Furthermore, innovation
mechanisms differ significantly between large-scale organizations and SMEs ( Maziliauske, 2024). Usually, a compre-
hensive policy with sub-schemes responsible for research and development, alongside specific schemes responsible for
resource availability in larger organizations. Due to other constraints such as scarce financial resources, existing
institutions, market infrastructure, and the available market, SMEs, especially in developing countries, adopt an informal,
gradualist innovation process that largely depends on the availability of new technology ( Tajeddin et al., 2025 ).
Consequently, there appears to be a pressing need to develop context-dependent frameworks to address the effects of
AI-led innovation in tourism.
A significant amount of recent research has examined the use of AI techniques, the impacts of digital transformation
initiatives, and the consequences of these technological advancements on tourism performance outcomes (e.g., Demir &
Demir, 2025; Zhang et al., 2025a ). This recent work spans several disciplines, including information systems, tourism,
management, innovation, and entrepreneurship. Li & Mahasuweerachai (2026) asserted that the present field is, both
between and across fields, philosophically highly diffuse. Similarly, only a small subgroup appears willing to consider the
context-specific effects of economic, social, and institutional conditions in developing-world contexts, and much prior
comparative work on similar topics in developed countries does seem context-dependent (Saito et al., 2026). The impact
of socio-economic context on Africa’s tourism development has apparently been severely hindered by the combination of
individual factors and the structural context that shapes creative behavior patterns ( Jjagwe et al., 2026 ). Additionally,
most prior work on innovation takes a purely structural approach, focusing on external factors and organizational capacity
while ignoring human processes of learning, cognition, and agency.
To address the theoretical gaps, this research argues for the development and integration of a more nuanced theoretical
framing that explains innovation with AI in tourism SMEs in developing countries, beyond a resource-based framework
that emphasizes resource accumulation and capacity development ( Holland, 2026 ). In fact, constraints in developing
countries are seen as catalysts for innovation, spurring experimentation and the recombination of existing technologies,
products, and organizational forms. Moreover, constraints are not seen simply as hindrances to innovation but as enablers
of new, context-deeply embedded modes of innovation ( Yesuf & Fields, 2026 ). With such framing, it can connect with
other emerging fields and highlight the creative power of resource constraint in explaining behavior.

The review addresses three issues: first, what is the theme of research on AI, innovation, and tourism-related firms in
2020-2025? Second, what evidence and theoretical explanations indicate that AI capability leads to innovation
performance, underpinned by human-centered and knowledge-based mechanisms? and third, how might institutional
support and environmental barriers condition these pathways? The integrative framework resulting from synthesis is a
series of theory-based propositions; this is not to imply that the associations the propositions express are already
established by the studies reviewed.
Methodology
This study adopted a sequential bibliometric analysis and SLR design to examine the relationship between AI and
innovation in SMEs in the tourism sector in emerging economies. Combining these approaches is appropriate for an
emerging and interdisciplinary research field, such as AI-enabled innovation in tourism SMEs, because bibliometric
analysis identifies the intellectual structure and thematic development of the literature, whereas an SLR enables a more
detailed synthesis of the theoretical arguments, empirical findings, and contextual conditions reported in individual
studies ( Jobstreibizer et al., 2025 ; Marzi et al., 2025 ). The two methods were therefore used sequentially and
complementarily: bibliometric analysis provided a macro-level overview of publication patterns and knowledge
networks, while the SLR examined the substantive contributions of the selected studies and informed the development
of the conceptual framework and future research directions.
A distinction between mapping and synthesis was retained during the analysis. Bibliometric indicators, including
keyword co-occurrences, co-authorship, and co-citations) delineated relationships between the retrieved literature, in
the absence of causal relationships between the theories. The SLR was used to evaluate the extent to which the records
incorporated the AI capabilities discussed, human-centered and knowledge-based mechanisms, contextual restrictions,
and innovation impacts. The relationships envisioned in the synthesis were therefore interpreted by considering the
strength of the evidence reported by the antecedent and/or consequent theoretical constructs in the retrieved papers,
differentiating empirically validated correlations from theoretical constructs and propositions built through synthesis.
The bibliographic database Scopus was chosen for its broad scope, as it covers many peer-reviewed scientific journals,
and for the availability of bibliographic and citation data suitable for systematic retrieval of information using biblio-
metric methods of analysis. The search was limited to the period 2020-2025 to capture the latest information on the
implementation of AI in tourism and hospitality. The Boolean search string used for the initial search was: ( “artificial
intelligence” OR “machine learning ”) AND innovation AND (tourism OR hospitality) AND SMEs AND “emerging
economies” AND (innovation OR creativity OR “business model innovation ” OR “digital innovation”).
The initial search returned 387 records. This search strategy was designed to identify studies explicitly connecting AI or
machine learning, innovation, tourism or hospitality, SMEs, and emerging economies. However, requiring these
concepts to appear together may exclude relevant studies that investigate related phenomena without explicitly using
the terms “SMEs” or “emerging economies.” The resulting evidence should therefore be interpreted as representing the
literature retrieved through the specified search strategy rather than the entirety of research on AI-enabled tourism
innovation. For methodological reproducibility, the final search record should specify the exact search date, complete
Scopus field syntax, document-type and language restrictions, and the bibliographic fields exported for analysis. These
details should be verified against the original Scopus search history and export files. Where such information was not
recorded, the limitation should be acknowledged rather than addressed through retrospective assumptions. The study
selection process complied with the PRISMA reporting guidelines ( Page et al., 2021). Study selection consisted of three
steps: deduplication, title and abstract screening, and full-text eligibility assessment. As shown inFigure 1, the first search
resulted in 387 hits; after deduplication, there were 302 remaining records; 124 full texts were retrieved based on the title
and abstract review; and 71 full texts made up the final sample of the present review.
Studies were eligible if they were about AI or machine learning and their relation to innovation in tourism or hospitality
SMEs, and if they provided empirical or theoretical evidence to contribute to the understanding of how AI-related
capabilities, processes of human cognition, or organizational knowledge, and contextual conditions interact with
innovation outcomes. To maintain a focus on the argument of this proposed framework, the eligible studies had to have
studied at least two of the following four analytical domains; (1) AI capabilities and applications; (2) human cognition,
creativity, learning, or organizational knowledge processes; (3) contextual constraints and institutional or organizational
conditions; and (4) innovation-related outcomes, including employee, organizational, digital, or business-model inno-
vation.

The SLR analyzed the theoretical reasoning, empirical results, and contextual factors of the 71 SLR articles. The SLR
analysis presents results across four analytical areas: AI capabilities, human-centered and knowledge-based mechanisms,
context factors, and innovation outcomes. Emphasis was placed on the linkages of those factors to tourism SMEs in
emerging economies. The synthesis differentiated empirically tested and interpreted relationships from all the proposi-
tions and interpretations made in the synthesis of study findings. Based on this differentiation, a mechanism or moderated
relationship was treated as empirically supported only if the original study(s) presented the construct as such. Frequency
of occurrence of a construct or participation in a bibliometric cluster was not treated as evidence of causal influence or
explanatory power. An evidence diagram of research contexts, theories, methods, constructs, mechanisms, and the main
findings should be presented to describe the evidential basis for the synthesis.
The bibliometric analysis and SLR were integrated to link the literature ’s intellectual structure to its substantive
theoretical and empirical contributions. While bibliometric mapping identified prominent themes and knowledge
networks, the SLR examined how the included studies explained relationships among AI capabilities, human-centered
mechanisms, contextual conditions, and innovation outcomes. The conceptual framework was consequently developed
through evidence-based synthesis rather than bibliometric frequency alone. Each proposed relationship should be
traceable to relevant studies and classified as empirically supported, indirectly informed, or theoretically proposed. This
distinction strengthens the conceptual consistency of the framework and supports the identification of research gaps in
accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) reporting
principles (Page et al., 2021 ).

Systematic literature review
SLR examined four interconnected domains: (1) AI capabilities, including AI-related knowledge, data quality, digital
infrastructure, and skills; (2) human-centered and knowledge mechanisms, including digital self-efficacy, epistemic
curiosity, and knowledge integration; (3) contextual conditions, particularly resource constraints and institutional
support; and (4) innovation outcomes. The reviewed literature draws on three prominent theoretical perspectives: the
Resource-Based View (RBV) ( Barney, 1991 ); Dynamic Capabilities Theory (DCT) ( Teece et al., 1997 ); and the
Technology-Organization-Environment (TOE) framework (Baker, 2011). As summarized in Table 1, these perspectives
offer complementary explanations of resource advantages, organizational adaptation, and technology adoption, respec-
tively, but may leave important aspects of innovation under resource-constrained conditions insufficiently explained.
The RBV proposes that competitive advantage is based on a firm ’s resources ( Barney, 1991 ). Within tourism SMEs,
AI capabilities could enhance processes such as innovation decision-making, customer service delivery, and operational
efficiency ( Kim et al., 2025 ). Yet, limited access to finance, digital infrastructure, technical skills, and institutional
support may constrain SME players’ scale-up and resource leverage (Alharethi et al., 2026). RBV is therefore limited to
providing a theoretical explanation of an organization ’s dynamic mobilization of resources through improvisation,
resource recombination, and collaboration.
DCT builds on this assertion by highlighting the potential for firms to sense and seize opportunities and reconfigure
resources in changing circumstances ( Teece et al., 1997 ). In the context of AI-enabled tourism, this may yield
organizational learning, service reconfiguration, and capability development ( Ameen et al., 2024 ), but with limited
resources, the SME might find it challenging to develop or leverage these capabilities. The TOE framework complements
the viewpoints above in grouping the technical, organizational, and environmental factors impacting the adoption and
implementation of technology ( Baker, 2011), such as managerial action, technological readiness, and environment and
policy pressures ( Horani et al., 2025 ; Shahzadi et al., 2024 ) but places less emphasis on the interpretative and action-
oriented processes that inform employees ’ responses to AI opportunities and constraints.
As Table 1 shows, the theories offer useful, though somewhat separate, analytical starting points. The SLR encourages
further exploration of a chain involving the firm ’s resources and environmental conditions, as well as human-centered
processes for leveraging AI-enabled innovation within resource-constrained tourism SMEs (see related research: Prayag
et al. (2024) ; Seow et al. (2024) . Lack of resources is not necessarily a trigger for innovation; it depends on the firm ’s
capabilities, knowledge, agency, and access to external support.
Thus, the current study constraint-enabled AI innovation framework in Table 2 incorporates AI capabilities, a human-
centered, knowledge-based approach, contextual constraints, and innovation outcomes. DCT guides the explanation of
how AI capabilities are being instantiated. TOE offers insights on how AI can be adopted and utilized within the present
technological, organizational, and environmental context. A human-centered perspective provides context for how
employees’ digital self-efficacy, curiosity, and knowledge integration would influence the translation of AI capability

Conceptual model
Building upon the bibliometric analysis and SLR, this research introduces a constraint-enabled AI innovation framework
to understand how tourism SMEs in developing countries may leverage AI capabilities for innovation. As summarized in
Table 4 and shown in Figure 5 , AI capability is identified as the driver of innovation performance, encompassing
AI-related knowledge, data quality, technological infrastructure, and employees ’ digital skills. Its effects are proposed
to be mediated in part by three complementary mechanisms: digital self-efficacy, epistemic curiosity, and knowledge
integration. Digital self-efficacy refers to the belief in one ’s competence to use digital technology ( Juita et al., 2026 ).
Epistemic curiosity is the desire to learn new things and to be exposed to new information, ideas, or methods ( Mutonyi
et al., 2026 ). Knowledge integration involves combining existing organizational knowledge with new information,
including AI-generated insights, to develop novel solutions ( Wang et al., 2025 ). These mechanisms represent distinct
pathways through which AI capabilities may be translated into innovation, although their mediating roles require
empirical validation in tourism SMEs.
The framework further specifies institutional support and environmental constraints as contextual moderators of the
relationships between these mechanisms and innovation performance. Institutional support encompasses relevant
policies, financial and technical resources, training, infrastructure, and ecosystem support that facilitate AI adoption
and implementation ( Arroyabe et al., 2024 ; Sangnak, 2025 ). Environmental constraints encompass resource scarcity,
infrastructure limitations, market turbulence, regulatory instability, and operational disruption, reflecting conditions that
may restrict organizational action ( Samur et al., 2026 ). Institutional support is expected to strengthen the translation of

Innovation performance is the principal outcome and encompasses product, service, process, and marketing innovation.
Although AI-enabled innovation may also generate financial and social benefits, these represent distinct performance

Therefore, the framework generates the following propositions for future empirical investigation. These propositions are
conceptual deductions from the review, not relationships tested by the present study.
P1: AI capability is positively associated with innovation performance in tourism SMEs, conditional on organizations ’
ability to mobilize and apply AI-related knowledge, infrastructure, data, and digital skills.
P2a: Digital self-efficacy mediates the relationship between AI capability and innovation performance.
P2b: Epistemic curiosity mediates the relationship between AI capability and innovation performance.
P2c: Knowledge integration mediates the relationship between AI capability and innovation performance.
P3: Institutional support positively moderates the relationships between each proposed mediating mechanism and
innovation performance, strengthening these relationships when support is greater.
P4: Environmental uncertainties moderate the relationships between each proposed mediating mechanism and innova-
tion performance. The direction and strength of these relationships depend on the severity of constraints and firms ’
capacity to adapt through learning, improvisation, and resource recombination.
The evidential basis of each proposition should be traceable to the studies included in the SLR. Direct empirical support
must be distinguished from indirect contextual evidence and conceptual arguments. Accordingly, the proposed frame-
work provides a theoretically informed explanation of AI-enabled innovation under resource constraints and identifies
relationships that warrant further empirical testing.
Discussion
The proposed framework for AI capability considers AI as a multidimensional organizational capability that integrates AI
knowledge, data quality, infrastructure, and digital skills. Complementarity among those dimensions facilitates the use of
technological and human resources that tourism SMEs can utilize to pursue innovation. In line with RBV and DCT,
technological resources do not automatically lead to innovation; their impact depends on how effectively organizations
combine, use, and complement them for specific business purposes. Having adequate AI knowledge enables the
realization of technological opportunities and the implementation of appropriate technological innovations ( Demir &
Demir, 2025), while data quality ensures that the insights derived from artificial intelligence are high-quality and usable
for organizational decision-making ( Yaiprasert & Hidayanto, 2026 ).
Infrastructure underpins the implementation of such technological innovations and digital skills, as well as the
management and utilization of AI in organizational activities ( Zeqiri et al., 2025 ). The complementarity of those
dimensions provides theoretical grounds for why investment in technology alone may not be sufficient for tourism
SMEs to boost innovation performance. The importance of each dimension, however, is subject to empirical testing.
Additionally, the framework hypothesizes that AI capability may lead to innovation performance through three distinct,
concurrent mediators: digital self-efficacy, epistemic curiosity, and knowledge integration. These mediators articulate
how technological opportunities can be translated into innovative practices, rather than treating AI resources and
innovation outcomes as givens.
First, digital self-efficacy refers to individuals’ confidence in their ability to use digital technologies effectively (Yu et al.,
2025). AI capabilities may provide employees with greater access to technological tools, training opportunities, and
practical experience, potentially strengthening their confidence in applying AI to work-related tasks ( Al-Romeedy,
Page 11 of 21
F1000Research 2026, 15:1559 Last updated: 29 OCT 2026

2026). Digital competence and experience may further support experimentation and problem-solving ( Ercik & Karda ş,
2024), while confidence in using AI may reduce perceived barriers to technological engagement and encourage
innovative applications ( Khan, 2024 ). Through this pathway, employees may become better positioned to explore
improvements in products, services, processes, and marketing practices. Nevertheless, the proposed mediating role of
digital self-efficacy remains a theoretical proposition requiring direct empirical testing.
The second one, epistemic curiosity, illustrates a cognitive way in which AI capabilities lead to innovation. By
continuously generating novel information, perspectives, and options through algorithms and data analytics, AI helps
individuals learn and become aware of opportunities to acquire new knowledge, explore new worlds, and question the
normal ways ( Florido-Benítez & del Alcázar Martínez, 2024 ). Employees high in epistemic curiosity are prone to
exploring the AI-driven information, experimenting with emerging digital tools, and seeking novel business opportu-
nities that are unlikely to be revealed through existing organizational practices and knowledge ( Islam, 2025). It offers an
opportunity to shift AI from an agent-based, passive innovation tool to an accelerator of organizational exploration, as
seen in tourism companies, where many innovation activities are initiated by the creative rather than by the organized,
formal R&D department. Through exploration-driven epistemic curiosity, individuals innovate around tailored tourist
experiences, personalized service delivery, and brand awareness in new marketing initiatives.
Third, mediator knowledge integration concerns an organization ’s capacity to synthesize scattered knowledge derived
from AI-driven data with the experience and knowledge of individuals and customers ( Thonglor et al., 2026). There is a
ton of data provided by AI; however, innovation doesn’t simply occur by interpreting and aggregating this data and then
embedding it into organizational processes ( Carayannis & Zota, 2026 ). Saleh (2025) and Wenting et al. (2026) explain
that knowledge integration blends AI-generated knowledge with organizational knowledge through, for example, joint
learning among individuals and across departments, social media discussion forums, and knowledge-sharing platforms
that translate dispersed information into actionable organizational knowledge.
This mediation process, therefore, emphasizes the social nature of innovation occurring through AI. That is to say, the
resulting innovation might not arise solely from technological intelligence, but from the integration of artificial and
human intelligence. The study shows that, among the mediators, innovative performance varies across organizations with
the same AI capability. The AI capability provides technological openings; digital self-efficacy enables the exploration of
those openings; epistemic curiosity further explores them; and knowledge integration ensures their realization. The
model displays how, although innovation is technologically enabled, it is socio-cognitively driven.
Moreover, the proposed framework assumes that the mediating effects of these mechanisms were, to some extent,
strongly mediated by other institutional and organizational constraints and environmental factors. Hence, government
institutions and constraints served as moderators, enabling and restricting the favorable effect of AI capability on the
cognitive process and, ultimately, on innovation performance. Initially, institutional support [e.g., the support of
government, development of ICTs, economic stimulus policies, programs on education and training, technology
transfers, universities, organizations, and public organizations for cooperation projects, for which organizations gain
more advantages by the enhancement of their AI capability (Carayannis et al., 2024). Sufficient institutional endorsement
could enable lower investment costs for deploying AI, greater availability of technical knowledge, the development of
robust digital infrastructure, and organizational experimentation with new AI tools (Horani et al., 2025; Olan et al., 2022).
In an enabling institutional environment, tourism SMEs can more effectively leverage their AI capabilities to achieve
desirable innovations, owing to the complementarity of external resources in addressing uncertainty and learning
(Secundo et al., 2025 ). The positive impact of AI capability on digital self-efficacy, epistemic curiosity, knowledge
integration, and ultimately innovation performance is then amplified.
Second, environmental constraints reflect unique circumstances in emerging economies for tourism SMEs, including a
lack of financial resources (Trupp et al., 2025), a lack of sufficient digital infrastructure (Shahadat et al., 2023), instability
of the institution, uncertainty related to technology ( León-Gómez et al., 2025), complexity of the regulations, a shortage
of skilled employees (Trupp et al., 2025), market volatility (Kahveci, 2023), and disruptions related to political unrest or
armed conflicts ( Poyiadjis & Sophocleous, 2026 ). While some previous innovation studies regard these constraints as
inhibitors of organizational performance (e.g., Alos-Simo et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2025b), the current study views them
as organizational capabilities with both positive and negative effects. Intense environmental constraints can indeed
reduce the effectiveness of AI capabilities, as organizations may lack the capacity and resources to invest in and fully
utilize this technology.
However, moderate environmental constraints can also stimulate creativity, innovation, and organizational resilience by
prompting organizations to explore alternative uses of their technology to compensate for limited resources (e.g., Trieu
Page 12 of 21
F1000Research 2026, 15:1559 Last updated: 29 OCT 2026

et al., 2023; Xue et al., 2026 ). Thus, the effects of environmental constraints on innovation are twofold and conditional
upon organizational capabilities and management responses. This contingency perspective suggests that AI-enabled
innovation cannot be understood without accounting for external context, i.e., institutional and environmental factors.
For similar levels of AI capability, organizations operating within different institutional ecosystems and facing different
levels of pressure can achieve different levels of innovation (Maziliauske, 2024). As a consequence, the current research
builds on contingency theory to explain that AI capability can yield innovation performance only when coupled with the
right institutional context and when operating under conditions of external environmental uncertainty.
At the organizational level, the research also focuses on two particularities of tourism SMEs. SMEs typically have flatter
structures, shorter communication lines, less bureaucratization, more managerial flexibility, and faster decision-making
processes than larger organizations ( Leso et al., 2023 ). These organizational features are conducive to faster experi-
mentation with new AI technologies, but they make these organizations more dependent on individual competencies and
on informal learning mechanisms within the organization. Consequently, innovation within tourism SMEs occurs
primarily through interactions among employees, managers, customers, and new digital technologies, and is rarely the
result of formalized R&D functions ( León-Gómez et al., 2025 ; McLeod et al., 2024 ). However, due to the lack of
organizational barriers, innovation can be adopted quickly when opportunities arise, and individual skills, such as self-
efficacy, curiosity, and the ability to integrate new information into the organization ’s knowledge base, become
particularly critical.
From this perspective, innovation performance within the AI-service technology framework is a multidimensional
outcome of applying AI capabilities to create organizational value in tourism SMEs. The study model assumes that
organizations’ AI capability (composed of AI knowledge, data quality, IT infrastructure, and digital literacy) may enable
them to enhance multiple aspects of innovation simultaneously. For example, organizations are empowered to develop
product, process, and market innovation concurrently through employing the capabilities of an AI service. Specifically,
intelligent market sensing facilitated through AI may help identify new markets and produce unique tour packages, an AI
system enhances smart service or user experience, process and operations can be more agile through AI-aided prediction
or automation of tasks, and finally, customer insight through AI leads to micro-market segment customization and
personalized marketing communication. The multidimensional exploration perspective on innovation performance
offers a holistic reflection of AI capacity from a practical standpoint. Therefore, rather than a technological breakthrough,
it is posited that the effective utilization of organizations ’ competencies embodied in AI-service technology improves
inter-functional connections between departments, whose collaboration yields synergistic effects that leverage organi-
zations’ adaptive capacity, build competitive advantage, and achieve innovation and firm performance in the long run.
Implications
Theoretical implications
The theoretical contribution is a synthesis-informed framework that connects AI capability, employee-level cognitive
mechanisms, knowledge integration, and context. DCT provides the process logic for mobilizing and reconfiguring
capabilities, while TOE identifies technological, organizational, and environmental conditions that shape AI use. The
constraint-enabled perspective adds a conditional account of adaptation under scarcity. The contribution is conceptual:
the present study does not test the framework, estimate mediation or moderation, or demonstrate causal effects. Its
theoretical value depends on the traceability of each proposed link to specific reviewed studies, which should be made
visible in the supplementary evidence matrix.
The study builds on the TOE by showing that IT implementation is determined not only by technological, organizational,
and environmental factors but also by internal organizational capabilities and external organizational context; more
importantly, these capabilities are shaped by institutional supports and environmental constraints. In a more complete
theoretical context, the successful IT implementation should not be regarded as mere Technology-Organization-
Environnement interaction but the interaction of the Technology, the Organization and the Environment, and this kind
of integration between DCT and TOE will more deeply reveal the role of dynamic capabilities and the Technology-
Organization-Environment context in AI-enabled product, service, process and marketing innovation in SMEs tourism,
which would overcome technology adoption model by providing a more comprehensive perspective and more
convincing explanation.
Managerial implications
The framework indicates a few (testable) priorities that do not necessarily follow: boost employees ’ AI knowledge and
digital capabilities; establish accessible data and infrastructure; generate opportunities to experiment and learn; and build
routines for evaluating and integrating AI outputs with employees’ and customers’ knowledge. These initiatives may turn
access into innovation, but the organization’s resources, sector, and ecosystem will shape their impact. Managers should

challenge the common assumption that resources create innovation, just as they should challenge the idea that building AI
capabilities always does.
Policy implications
The study highlights how institutional context plays a key role in fostering AI-driven innovation. Specifically, policy
should enhance AI-focused training and access to knowledge, as well as digital infrastructure; provide financial support
and technical guidance to build AI capabilities within tourism SMEs; and coordinate government, HE institutions,
technology providers, and tourism firms towards the development of digital capacities and the spread of AI innovations.
In short, by developing a facilitating institutional context and environment, policy will help tourism SMEs leverage their
AI capabilities to implement sustainable innovation processes and maintain their competitive position in the long term,
particularly in emerging economies where institutional and resource constraints prevail.
Conclusion
This review integrates bibliometric mapping and an SLR to synthesize AI and innovation literature on tourism SMEs and
to establish a new constraint-enabled conceptual framework. The reported selection process yielded 71 studies from 387
initial records. The framework establishes a relationship in which AI capability precedes innovation performance, with
digital self-efficacy, epistemic curiosity, and knowledge integration forming three parallel mediating mechanisms.
Institutional support and environmental constraints are theorized to moderate the translation of these mechanisms into
innovation performance. These links constitute a theorization, and this study does not posit these relationships as
empirically verified. The strength of the contribution, therefore, hinges on the transparency of reporting the search and
screening process, and on establishing a traceable mapping of the 71 cited studies to the proposed relationships.
The review is constrained by a single database and the search string, which includes "mentions SMEs" and "emerging
economies," which may have missed relevant papers with alternative wording. The paper is missing the search date, the
database fields and filters used, the VOSviewer parameters, the number of reviewers, and the specifics of the exclusion
criteria and coding strategy, which are needed to make the review fully reproducible. Furthermore, the identified
mediators and moderators are not supported by the included evidence matrix to demonstrate path specificity across
the studies. Therefore, the proposed framework should be viewed as a theoretically grounded research agenda rather than
a causal framework. Future research should publish the extraction matrix, empirically test the identified paths using
appropriate methods, and investigate whether the effects of constraints vary with the seriousness of the constraint and the
country’s institutional profile.

Summary of the article
This article investigates how artificial intelligence (AI) capability influences innovation performance
in tourism small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) in emerging economies. Using a
bibliometric analysis and a systematic literature review (PRISMA-guided, Scopus, 2020–2025, 71
articles retained), the authors map the intellectual structure of AI–tourism innovation research
and synthesise dominant theories (RBV, Dynamic Capabilities Theory, and the TOE framework).
They then propose a conceptual model in which AI capability (AI knowledge, data quality, AI
infrastructure, digital skills) influences innovation performance (product, service, process, and
marketing innovation) indirectly through three human-centred mediators — digital self-efficacy,
epistemic curiosity, and knowledge integration — moderated by institutional support and
environmental constraints. The article's contribution is a "constraint-enabled" perspective that
reframes resource scarcity in developing economies as a potential enabler, rather than only a
barrier, of innovation.
Relevance
The topic is timely and under-researched. Most AI–tourism literature focuses on large firms and
advanced destinations, so a context-specific framework for tourism SMEs in emerging economies
addresses a genuine gap and has clear theoretical and practical value.
Strengths
Timely and well-motivated gap. The focus on tourism SMEs in emerging economies is a
valuable correction to the large-firm bias in existing AI–tourism research.
○
Appropriate dual methodology. Combining bibliometric analysis with a systematic
literature review is well suited to an emerging, interdisciplinary field and follows recognised
