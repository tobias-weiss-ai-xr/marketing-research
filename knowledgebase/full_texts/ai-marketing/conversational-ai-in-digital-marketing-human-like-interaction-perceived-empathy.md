Abstract: This study examined associations among Human -Like Interaction, Perceived Empathy, and
Customer Loyalty in the use of conversational artificial intelligence (AI) for Indonesian e -commerce. A
cross-sectional survey collected questionnaire responses from 300 purposively selected consumers who
had experience with e -commerce chatbots or virtual assistants. The analysis used partial least squares
structural equation modeling (PLS -SEM) in SmartPLS 4 to assess 18 indicators across three reflective
constructs. The reported measurement properties met the specified reliability and validity thresholds. The
reported PLS-SEM results indicated positive direct associations among Human -Like Interaction, Perceived
Empathy, and Customer Loyalty, alongside a positive specific i ndirect association through Perceived
Empathy. However, discrepancies between the structural model and the direct -effects table prevent a
definitive interpretation of the estimated effect magnitudes and mediation type. However, coefficients in
the structur al diagram differed from those in the direct -effects table, so the magnitudes and type of
mediation could not be confirmed from the reported outputs. The study distinguishes perceived
conversational naturalness from perceived understanding and company-directed loyalty intentions. These
findings concern self -reported perceptions and intentions rather than observed repurchase behavior. Its
cross-sectional design and purposive sample preclude causal conclusions and population -wide
generalization. The results provide a basis for evaluating interaction quality and perceived understanding
in chatbot-supported customer service.

INTRODUCTION
Conversational artificial intelligence (AI) increasingly mediates encounters between online
retailers and their customers. Beyond supplying product information, chatbots may support
recommendations, transaction-related requests, and the resolution of service problems (Fu et al.,
2023; Cheng et al., 2022). Although conversational AI can efficiently address customer inquiries
and service requests, successful interactions do not necessarily translate into stronger loyalty
toward the service provider. Customers may perceive an automated interaction as useful without
developing a corresponding intention to repurchase, recommend the company, or maintain a
long-term relationship. Research on verbal embodiment shows that conversational properties
can shape brand-related judgments, but it also suggests that the way people experience a dialogue
matters more than the mere presence of an automated agent (Bergner et al., 2023) . This
distinction motivates an examination of whether customers' perceptions of human-like exchanges

Human-like communication is multidimensional, and the perception of a natural exchange
does not imply that an AI system possesses human emotions. In online shopping, empathy and
social presence have been related to trust, whereas anthropomorphism can also produce
unfavorable reactions, depending on the attribute and context being examined (Fu et al., 2023).
Additional research connects interactivity and perceived humanness with trust (Ding & Najaf,
2024) and describes how people attribute social qualities to technology (Xu et al., 2022). Studies
of chatbot anthropomorphism indicate that customer reactions can be shaped by perceived social
presence (Konya-Baumbach et al., 2023)  and by the combination of conversational language,
perceived competence, and authenticity (Nguyen et al., 2023) . Experimental evidence further
indicates that the usefulness of human -like design is conditional rather than uniform across
customer relationships (Han, 2021; Igarashi et al., 2026). Accordingly, this study treats Human-
Like Interaction as the customer's evaluation of communication naturalness, contextual
appropriateness, fluency, continuity, and personalization, rather than as a manipulation of chatbot
design.
An encounter may appear conversationally fluent while still failing to address what a
customer actually needs. Perceived Empathy captures a different judgment: whether customers
feel that the conversational service recognizes their circumstances and respon ds attentively to
their concerns (Markovitch et al., 2024) . Research on artificial empathy has distinguished the
experience of being understood from a system's underlying emotional capabilities (Liu-
Thompkins et al., 2022) . Research on emotional tone and empathic language suggests that
perceived warmth is another relevant aspect of service evaluation (Rohden & Espartel, 2026; Yun
& Park, 2022). Empathetic messages in service recovery may also elicit different evaluations from
messages that emphasize a solution, indicating that warmth and task performance need not be
interchangeable (Haupt et al., 2023). For this reason, separating Perceived Empathy from Human-
Like Interaction is conceptually necessary before examining how both relate to loyalty.
Customer Loyalty in the present study refers to stated preferences and intentions toward
an e -commerce company or brand, including repurchase, recommendation, and relationship -
maintenance intentions. It is not measured as the frequency of chatbot use or as  observed
retention. This operationalization is informed by research on e -satisfaction and e -loyalty
(Anderson & Srinivasan, 2003). Earlier work distinguishes satisfaction with a chatbot from loyalty
to the system (Hsu & Lin, 2023) , while research on chatbot affordances and identification links
the interaction to relationship-related outcomes (Lee & Li, 2023). Other studies connect AI service
quality with e -brand loyalty through trust and customer experience (Shahzad et al., 2024)  and
examine service encounters in which chatbot -related judgments can have consequences for
customer loyalty (Ozuem et al., 2024) . These findings make a company -level outcome relevant,
although they do not imply that any particular conversational characteristic alone determines
loyalty.
Theoretically, the Computers Are Social Actors (CASA) perspective explains why people may
respond socially to cues produced by a technological interface (Nass & Moon, 2000) . The
conditions under which CASA applies to contemporary technology have also been questioned
(Heyselaar, 2023). Social Presence Theory offers an adjacent account of perceived interpersonal
proximity, but social presence is not measured as a separate variable in this study (Poinsot et al.,
2022; Jin & Youn, 2023). Relationship Marketing Theory directs attention to sustained evaluations
of the company rather than satisfaction with a single exchange (Morgan & Hunt, 1994). Studies of

how customers engage with machines (Fakhimi et al., 2023; Song et al., 2024). These perspectives

the full mechanisms proposed by the theories.
Recent scholarship already addresses chatbot -assisted retention, conversational trust,
perceived empathy, and brand loyalty, so the general connection between conversational AI and
loyalty is not unexplored (Chen et al., 2023 ; Chi & Hoang Vu, 2023 ; Shahzad et al., 2024) .
Nevertheless, the referents and intermediate judgments are often different: chatbot continuance,
encounter satisfaction, rapport, and loyalty toward a company cannot be used as interchangeable
outcomes. For example, research on rapport and electronic word of mouth focuses on a different
response from company -oriented purchase intentions (Fatima et al., 2024) , while studies of
conversational style examine customer engagement under specific service conditions (Roy
Bhattacharjee et al., 2025) . Research on chatbot identity framing also situates trust and brand
loyalty in a distinct consumer evaluation setting (Umair et al., 2026). Research in digital banking
further underscores that the provider , setting, and service construct matter when interpreting a
relationship with loyalty (Hang et al., 2026) . Although previous studies have examined chatbot
characteristics in relation to trust, customer engagement, and loyalty, important distinctions
remain between favorable evaluations of conversational technology and loyalty intentions toward
the company providing the service. This study addresses that distinction by examining Human -
Like Interaction and Perceived Empathy as separate but potentially related customer judgments
associated with loyalty toward the same e-commerce provider in Indonesia.
The study therefore examines whether Human-Like Interaction is positively associated with
Customer Loyalty (H1) and Perceived Empathy (H2), and whether Perceived Empathy is
positively associated with Customer Loyalty (H3). It additionally examines the spec ific indirect
association from Human-Like Interaction to Customer Loyalty through Perceived Empathy (H4).
All three constructs refer to the same e -commerce company or brand identified by each
respondent, preserving the distinction between evaluations of an  interaction and judgments
about a provider. Partial least squares structural equation modeling (PLS -SEM) evaluates the
measurement model and the reported direct and indirect relationships. The proposed
contribution is a bounded comparison of conversationa l characteristics, perceived
understanding, and provider -directed loyalty intentions within one survey model, without
claiming causal mediation or observed behavioral change.

METHOD
Research Design
This study used a quantitative explanatory cross-sectional survey to examine relationships
among Human-Like Interaction (X), Perceived Empathy (M), and Customer Loyalty (Y). The unit
of analysis was an individual consumer who had used conversational AI pro vided by an e -
commerce company or brand. The proposed model included a direct X-to-Y path, an X-to-M path,
an M -to-Y path, and their specific indirect association. SmartPLS 4 was used for partial least
squares structural equation modeling (PLS -SEM). As all  constructs were measured in the same
survey, the design describes contemporaneous associations and cannot establish the sequence or
causal direction of these relationships.
Research Context and Participants
The study was situated in Indonesia's e -commerce and online retail sector . The sample
comprised 300 respondents selected through purposive sampling. The reported eligibility criteria

were a minimum age of 18 years, prior use of an e -commerce company's conversational AI for
information or problem resolution, purchase or service experience with the company being
evaluated, AI-service use within the preceding six months, and identification of that company or
brand. Respondents were asked to anchor all three construct assessments to the same provider,
reducing ambiguity between evaluations of a chatbot and loyalty to the company it represented.
The nonprobability sample was not designed to represent all Indonesian online shoppers.
Measures and Research Instrument
The structured online questionnaire employed a five -point Likert scale from 1 (strongly
disagree) to 5 (strongly agree) and contained 18 reflective indicators. Six Human-Like Interaction
indicators addressed natural language, resemblance to human conversat ion, contextual
appropriateness, fluency, continuity, and personalization. Six Perceived Empathy indicators
addressed understanding of needs, situationally appropriate responses, attention to problems,
recognition of customer needs, sensitivity to feelings , and consideration of customer
circumstances. Six Customer Loyalty indicators concerned continued use of the company's
offerings, company preference, recommendation, repurchase, maintenance of the relationship,
and reluctance to switch. The source scales,  verbatim items, and any adaptation or pilot -testing
records are not presented in the available manuscript; conclusions about content validity should
consequently remain limited to the documented construct definitions and model diagnostics.
Data Collection and Preparation
The available description identifies an online questionnaire administered with voluntary
participation, consent, and confidentiality assurances. The reported data -handling procedure
considered response completeness, participant eligibility, potential dupli cates, unusual answer
patterns, and missing information. The descriptive characteristics and subsequent model
assessment were reported for 300 respondents. Specific fieldwork dates, recruitment sources,
exclusion counts, and any pilot -stage outcomes were n ot included in the source account, so no
additional procedure is inferred here. Because all three constructs were assessed by self -report
from the same respondent, shared-method variation remains a relevant interpretive limitation.
Measurement and Structural Model Evaluation
The reflective measurement model was evaluated using indicator outer loadings,
Cronbach's alpha, Dijkstra–Henseler’s ρA, composite reliability ( ρc), average variance extracted
(AVE), and the heterotrait -monotrait ratio (HTMT). The manuscript used a loading  criterion
around 0.70, internal -consistency values above 0.70, AVE above 0.50, and an HTMT reference
threshold of 0.85. Structural assessment included inner variance inflation factors (VIF),
standardized path estimates, explained variance (R²), effect size (f²), and the reported PLSpredict
results. Direct hypotheses H1 to H3 and the specific indirect association H4 were evaluated with
bootstrap-based t-statistics and p-values at the stated 5% level. The source document does not
specify bootstrap resample counts, confidence-interval bounds, or PLSpredict fold and repetition
settings, which limits independent reproduction of those inferential and predictive assessments.
Interpretive Scope
Positive statistical paths were interpreted as associations among the perceptions reported
by these respondents, not as intervention effects. Customer Loyalty operationalized stated
company-oriented intentions rather than transaction logs or customer reten tion records. The
direct-path estimates in Table 10 and those in Figure 1 are different; both original sets are
retained in the results to avoid an unsupported numerical replacement. Accordingly, the existence
of the reported indirect association can be de scribed, whereas the magnitude of a unified final
structural model and the mediation category cannot be settled from the displayed outputs alone.

Discussion
The reported positive relationship between Human -Like Interaction and Perceived
Empathy suggests that respondents who experienced more natural, continuous, and contextually
appropriate chatbot communication also tended to feel better understood. CASA helps  interpret
why socially familiar cues might attract relational judgments from customers, without requiring
the system to possess human intentions (Nass & Moon, 2000). Whereas Fu et al. (2023) examined
the relationships between human -like chatbot characteristics and customer trust, the present
study focuses on perceived understanding as a distinct relational judgment associated with
conversational naturalness. This distinction is consistent with Liu-Thompkins et al. (2022), who
differentiate the customer experience of artificial empathy from genuine human emotional
experience. The reported positive association suggests that customers who perceive chatbot
communication as natural and contextually appropriate also tend to fee l better understood.
Nevertheless, the findings concern perceived interaction quality rather than the actual emotional
capabilities of conversational AI. Our result concerns this perceived relational quality, not a tested
change in the chatbot's emotional intelligence. It is therefore compatible with prior work while
remaining limited to the aggregate self-reported Human-Like Interaction construct.
The association involving Perceived Empathy becomes particularly meaningful when
separated from an interface's general conversational fluency. Markovitch et al. (2024) examined
perceived empathy in evaluations of chatbot versus human service, while Haupt et al. (2023)
studied the differential effects of empathy -oriented and solution -oriented messages in service
recovery. Work on empathy in service recovery and frontline human–AI encounters offers related,
but not equivalent, perspectives (Carrilho et al., 2025 ; Yang et al., 2025) . Those studies involve
interaction features or service conditions that this survey does not experimentally vary. A chatbot
could respond smoothly yet fail to recognize a complaint, and an informative response could be
useful without being experienced as c aring. The present indicators address consumers'
judgments of being understood rather than objective recognition accuracy or emotional
competence. This distinction makes the positive X -to-M relationship theoretically interpretable
without overstating what was measured.

A positive direct association between Human -Like Interaction and Customer Loyalty was
also reported, but the outcome differs from chatbot reuse. Bergner et al. (2023) show that verbal
features of conversations can shape consumer–brand relationships, whereas Hsu and Lin (2023)
examine satisfaction and loyalty associated with customer-service chatbots. Research on service-
chatbot anthropomorphism and persuasion offers additional contexts for interpreting brand -

indicators to an e -commerce company or brand rather than to continued engagement with the
conversational tool itself. Research on loyalty to chatbot usage itself reinforces this distinction
(Rita et al., 2026) . That difference is consequential: a customer may enjoy the chatbot without
preferring the retailer, and may remain loyal to a retailer because of factors outside the chatbot.
The smaller figure -based direct path relative to the empathy -to-loyalty path is  suggestive,
although the discordance with Table 10 prevents a settled numerical comparison.
The reported Perceived Empathy –Customer Loyalty relationship indicates that
respondents who felt recognized in the service interaction also tended to express stronger
company-oriented loyalty intentions. Related research has linked AI service quality to e -brand
loyalty via customer trust and experience (Shahzad et al., 2024), and Ozuem et al. (2024) connect
chatbot-based service recovery with loyalty in a different service context. Research on responsive
and anthropomorphic exchanges similarly examines customer experience, which differs from
company-level retention (Truong & Chen, 2025). Neither comparison establishes that perceived
understanding independently causes repeat purchasing. Within this survey, loyalty was assessed
through intentions and preferences rather than transaction records. Recent research on chatbots,
satisfaction, an d repeat purchase also emphasizes the importance of separating encounter
evaluations from downstream behavior (Zhao et al., 2026). The result instead identifies perceived
understanding as a relational evaluation worth distinguishing from technical competence,
especially when companies use automated channels to manage customer contact.
The reported specific indirect path brings the three constructs together, but its
interpretation must account for the contradictory coefficient sets. An indirect association of 0.253
is printed in Table 11, closely matching the product of the X -to-M and M -to-Y paths in Figure 2.
Table 10 provides different direct -path estimates, and no confidence limits for the reported
indirect effect are supplied. For that reason, the result supports only a qualified account of a
positive reported indirect relationship; i t does not establish partial mediation or the temporal
process by which empathy might arise before loyalty. This restraint is consistent with the cross-
sectional survey design and avoids transforming a statistical decomposition into a behavioral
mechanism.
Rather than proposing an entirely new relationship between conversational AI and
customer loyalty, this study contributes to the literature by distinguishing three conceptually
related but empirically separate evaluations: the perceived human -likeness of c hatbot
communication, the perceived understanding conveyed during the interaction, and loyalty
intentions directed toward the service provider. This distinction provides a more specific
perspective on customer relationships with AI -enabled services by sepa rating favorable
evaluations of conversational technology from intentions to maintain relationships with the
company itself. Within the Indonesian e-commerce sample, the reported results offer a basis for
examining these relationships without equating chatbot satisfaction with company loyalty. Chen
et al. (2023) , Lee and Li (2023) , and Shahzad et al. (2024)  already examine relational
consequences of AI-enabled customer service. At the same time, other scholarship distinguishes
social presence, rapport, trust, and engagement as separate responses to conversational

technology (Konya-Baumbach et al., 2023; Fatima et al., 2024; Fakhimi et al., 2023). The present
study contributes by assessing perceived communication characteristics, perceived
understanding, and intentions directed toward the same company in the Indonesian e-commerce
sample. HTMT values below 0.85 are compatible with their empirical distinction, while access to
exact items remains necessary for a stronger assessment of conceptual overlap.
Several boundary conditions matter when placing the results alongside international
studies. A customer-journey perspective also suggests that the conversational encounter is only
one part of a broader relationship with a retailer (Lemon & Verhoef, 2016) . The respondents
evaluated AI services used for product information, recommendations, transactions, and
complaints, rather than a single controlled interaction type. Experiments on chatbot human -
likeness indicate that results can depend on the interface, purchasing relationship, or treatment
condition (Nguyen et al., 2023; Igarashi et al., 2026). Work on conversational styles in hospitality
also addresses engagement under conditions that differ from this survey's provider -directed
loyalty measure (Roy Bhattacharjee et al., 2025) . The sample's heterogeneity in recent chatbot
use may therefore coexist with unmeasured differences in service expectations and encounter
severity. None of these contextual possibilities was separately tested, so they are treated as
explanations for future investigation rather than results of the current model.
For managers, the findings justify examining the quality of chatbot exchanges as part of a
wider customer relationship assessment, particularly whether conversations remain relevant,
continuous, and responsive to stated needs. The analysis cannot rank these design features, show
that adding anthropomorphic cues improves outcomes, or confirm reductions in customer churn.
A practical evaluation would therefore distinguish chatbot satisfaction from brand preference and
complement survey-based intentions with behavioral measures where available. The limitations
are substantial: purposive recruitment restricts generalization, a single survey cannot determine
temporal order, and same -source self -reports create potential common -method concerns.
Unspecified fieldwor k and measurement -development details, together with the inconsistent
structural coefficients, further constrain reproducibility. Longitudinal, experimental, and cross -
platform research could test when perceived understanding precedes loyalty and whether t he
reported associations correspond to actual behavior.

CONCLUSIONS
The study reports positive relationships among Human -Like Interaction, Perceived
Empathy, and Customer Loyalty in a sample of 300 Indonesian e -commerce users. The findings
indicate that Human -Like Interaction, Perceived Empathy, and Customer Loyalty repres ent
empirically distinguishable constructs within the examined Indonesian e -commerce sample, as
supported by the reported discriminant validity results. The positive direct and indirect
associations suggest that perceived conversational naturalness and perceived understanding are
relevant to customers' company -directed loyalty intentions. However, inconsistencies in the
reported structural coefficients prevent definitive conclusions about effect magnitudes and
mediation classification. Accordingly, these findings should be interpreted as associations among
customer perceptions and intentions rather than evidence of causal changes in purchasing or
retention behavior . Because the direct effects differ between Figure 2 and Table 10, the final
magnitudes and classification of mediation cannot be inferred with confidence from the displayed
figures. These findings concern self -reported company-related loyalty intentions, not observed
customer retention or the causal effects of chatbot design.

This analysis positions Perceived Empathy as a distinct customer judgment within a
parsimonious model of conversational AI and company -directed loyalty. The study draws on
CASA, social presence, and relationship marketing as complementary perspectives with out
asserting that their full mechanisms were tested. The reported measurement properties are
encouraging, but they do not eliminate limitations arising from missing item wording and
common-method concerns. In managerial terms, conversational relevance and  perceived
understanding are reasonable aspects of service experience to evaluate, not proven methods for
increasing purchases.
Future studies could combine documented questionnaire development and probability -
based or diversified recruitment with longitudinal observations, experiments, and transaction -
level outcomes. Such evidence would help determine whether customer interpretati ons of
conversational AI precede changes in company loyalty and whether associations differ across
retailers, service problems, or customer groups. The current cross -sectional results should be
read as a bounded empirical account of perceptions within the sampled context.
