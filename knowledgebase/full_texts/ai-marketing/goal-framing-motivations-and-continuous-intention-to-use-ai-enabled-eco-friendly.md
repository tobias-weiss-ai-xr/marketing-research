Background:Packagingwasteremainsoneofthemostpersistentenvironmental
problems of the current era, and yet the role that artiﬁcial intelligence
(AI) plays in steering consumers toward sustainable packaging choices
has received comparatively little scholarly attention. This paper addresses
that gap. Building on Goal Framing Theory (GFT), the Theory of Planned
Behavior (TPB), and the Stimulus–Organism–Response (SOR) framework, we
position AI as the theoretical and contextual backdrop against which six
consumer-behavior antecedents reliability, willingness to pay, subjective norms,
environmental concern, hedonic motivation, and personal innovativeness are
hypothesized to shape behavioral intention (BI), which in turn is expected
to shape continuous eco-packaging use intention (CI). Rather than treating
AI mechanisms (personalization, algorithmic nudging, predictive analytics, and
social listening) as directly measured constructs, we treat them as the ambient
digital environment within which the tested constructs operate.
Methods: Survey responses from N = 400 Indian adult consumers who had
purchased packaged consumer products in the preceding three months were
analyzed using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS-SEM)
in SmartPLS 4.0. A structured two-stage procedure was followed, evaluating
the measurement model (reliability, convergent and discriminant validity) before
assessing the structural model (path coefficients,R2,a n dQ2), with all mediation
effects tested using bias-corrected 95% bootstrap conﬁdence intervals (k =
5,000).
Results: Personal innovativeness (β = 0.344, p < .001), subjective norms
(β = 0.312, p < 0.001), and environmental concern ( β = 0.265, p <
0.001) emerged as signiﬁcant predictors of BI, while reliability, willingness to
pay, and hedonic motivation did not. At the continuance stage, subjective
norms exerted the strongest direct effect on CI (β = 0.434, p < 0.001),
environmental concern contributed a smaller but signiﬁcant direct effect (β =
0.177, p = 0.001), and willingness to pay produced an unexpected negative
direct effect (β =− 0.161, p = 0.001), which we interpret through a price-
fatigue lens informed by mental accounting and construal-level reasoning.
BehavioralintentionitselfdidnotsigniﬁcantlypredictCI,apatternconsistentwith
automatic rather than deliberative continuance pathways. Because the BI→CI
path is non-signiﬁcant, none of the six mediated pathways reach signiﬁcance,
and we reframe these as exploratory rather than conﬁrmatory ﬁndings.

1 Introduction
Why consumers make or fail to make sustainable packaging
choices has occupied a growing body of research over the last
two decades. What that literature has not adequately addressed is
the extent to which digitally mediated environments now shape
those choices at nearly every step. Recommendation engines,
algorithmic nudges, and machine-learning systems capable of
forecasting a shopper’s ecological preferences have quietly become
partofthecommerciallandscape( Davenportetal.,2020 ;Jainetal.,
2024; Marianietal., 2022 ). Whether these tools actually translate
into sustained eco-packaging behavior, however, is a question the
existingliteraturehasnotansweredwithmuchrigor.
Some background helps explain why this matters. Packaging
accounts for roughly 36% of global plastic waste, a ﬁgure
drawn from the UnitedNationsEnvironmentProgramme
(2021). Biodegradable, compostable, recycled-content, and
minimal-processing packaging alternatives have attracted growing
industrial and policy interest, particularly as governments pursue
circular-economy frameworks ( EllenMacArthurFoundation,
2016; Geissdoerferetal., 2017 ). A persistent diﬃculty, though,
is that initial consumer adoption of eco-friendly packaging
rarely converts into habitual, ongoing use. The psychological
mechanisms underlying that transition and the role AI might play
in facilitating or impeding it remain underspeciﬁed (Bhuttoetal.,
2023;Thogersen,2011).
To address this, the paper builds an integrated theoretical
model that brings together three established frameworks. Goal
Framing Theory (GFT; LindenbergandSteg, 2007 ) supplies
a motivational architecture organized around hedonic, gain,
and normative goal frames. The Theory of Planned Behavior
(TPB; Ajzen, 1991) supplies the core claim that behavioral
intention mediates between antecedent beliefs and actual
behavior. The Stimulus–Organism–Response framework
(SOR; MehrabianandRussell, 1974 ) treats AI-generated
informational stimuli as environmental inputs that activate
internal consumer states. Within this structure, six antecedent
constructsreliability(R),willingnesstopay(WP),subjectivenorms
(SN),environmentalconcern(EC),hedonicmotivation(HM),and
personal innovativeness (PI) are theorized to shape BI, which in
turnshapescontinuouseco-packaginguseintention(CI).

The study oﬀers three speciﬁc contributions. First, it is to
our knowledge among the ﬁrst empirical studies to integrate
GFT, TPB, and SOR explicitly within a packaging-focused
sustainability context, building on recent calls for interdisciplinary
theoretical synthesis ( Dwivedietal., 2023 ; Rezaeietal., 2023 ).

derived correspondence rather than an empirically tested one
and outline a research agenda that takes seriously the ethical
complications of algorithmic green marketing ( Pizzietal.,
2021).

and hypotheses. Section 4 describes the research methodology.
Section 5 reports the empirical results. Section 6 interprets the
ﬁndings. Section 7 proposes a future research agenda. Section 8
concludes.

3 Literature review
3.1 AI and sustainable consumer behavior:
what we know so far
Scholarship on AI and green consumption is young
but growing quickly ( Dwivedietal., 2023 ; Jainetal., 2024 ;
Marianietal., 2022 ). Four broad mechanisms through which
AI is thought to shape sustainability behavior recur across this
literature. The ﬁrst is personalization: recommendation engines
analyze purchase histories and demographic proﬁles to construct
individualized sustainability pathways, steering shoppers toward
eco-credentialled products before a deliberate choice has even
formed ( Davenportetal., 2020 ; HuangandRust, 2022 ). The
second isnudging: AI-designed prompts placed at digital decision
points checkout screens, app notiﬁcations redirect behavior in
real time without formally restricting choice (EsmarkJonesetal.,
2020; Naumov, 2019; Sunstein, 2019). The third is predictive
analytics: machine-learning algorithms forecast individual
green-consumption trajectories so that retailers can intervene
before backsliding occurs (Lvetal., 2022 ; Marianietal., 2023 ).
The fourth is social ampliﬁcation: social-listening tools surface
peer endorsements and community pledges within users’ digital
environments, making normative sustainability signals more
visibleandpersistent( Kauretal.,2022 ;Mogajietal.,2021 ).
Despite these developments, the packaging context speciﬁcally
has received limited theoretical attention. Prior AI-and-
sustainability research has focused on green purchase intentions
broadly (BiswasandRoy, 2015 ), sustainable tourism (Naumov,
2019),andorganicfoodconsumption( Pauletal.,2016 );packaging,
however with its distinctive combination of high repeat-purchase
frequency, functional evaluation requirements, and cumulative
environmentalfootprinthasbeenlargelyoverlooked( Bhuttoetal.,
2023; MagnierandSchoormans, 2015 ; Vermaetal., 2023 ). This
gapmotivatesthepresentstudy’sfocus.
3.2 Goal framing theory and AI-mediated
motivation
LindenbergandSteg’s (2007) Goal Framing Theory holds that
behavior is governed by whichever of three goal frames is
most cognitively salient at the moment of decision: the hedonic
frame (pleasure-seeking), the gain frame (economic rationality),
or the normative frame (ecological and prosocial obligation).
In AI-mediated packaging decisions, each frame corresponds, at
a theoretical level, to a diﬀerent class of algorithmic content.
Content that presents eco-packaging as aesthetically engaging
or experientially pleasing plausibly activates hedonic motivations
(Babinetal., 1994 ; RoyandPal, 2015 ). Pricing algorithms and
lifecycle-cost comparisons speak to the gain frame (Larocheetal.,
2001;RokkaandUusitalo,2008 ).Peer-comparisondashboardsand
community sustainability pledges plausibly mobilize normative
concerns (LindenbergandSteg, 2007 ; Stegetal., 2014 ). What is
distinctive about AI, at least in principle, is its capacity to operate
across multiple frames simultaneously, in real time, and at scale
something conventional marketing communication cannot easily
replicate(Lvetal.,2022 ;Pizzietal.,2021 ).
3.3 Theory of planned behavior in digital
contexts
TPB has been applied widely in green-consumption research
and remains one of the more robust frameworks available
for predicting sustainable purchase behavior ( Pauletal., 2016 ;
YadavandPathak, 2017). In digital settings, TPB’s core claim that
behavioral intention is the proximate cause of action remains
conceptually intact, but the antecedent conditions that feed
into that intention are, in principle, substantially shaped by
algorithmic systems (Bhattacherjee, 2001; ConnerandArmitage,
1998; Rezaeietal., 2023 ). When personalized recommendations
adjusttheinformationenvironment,whensocialplatformsamplify
normative signals, or when predictive systems reframe the
ecological consequences of a purchase decision, the inputs feeding
intointentionaldeliberationareplausiblyaltered( Davenportetal.,
2020; Jainetal., 2024 ). The theoretical implication we draw on
is that AI does not merely reﬂect pre-existing intentions it may
participate, at the level of the broader digital ecosystem, in
constructing them, even where this study does not measure that
participationdirectly.
3.4 SOR framework and AI as stimulus
architect
The Stimulus–Organism–Response framework (Jacoby, 2002;
MehrabianandRussell, 1974) oﬀers a complementary lens. Within
this framework, AI-generated signals reliability information,
price framing, social norms, ecological data, hedonic content,
innovation messaging constitute a theoretical stimulus layer.
Consistent with Jacoby’s (2002) reconceptualization of SOR,
which extends the framework to accommodate perceptual and
motivational mediators rather than only raw external stimuli,
we treat the six antecedent constructs measured in this study
as proximal internal representations of distal, AI-generated
environmental stimuli, rather than as the stimuli themselves. The
consumer’s internal motivational state, captured here as behavioral
intention, functions as the organism layer understood, following
MehrabianandRussell’s (1974) original treatment of approach
avoidance dispositions, as a motivational-state summary that
bridges the cognitive appraisal captured by SOR and the volitional
antecedent-to-behavior logic captured by TPB. Continuous eco-
packaging use intention constitutes the response layer. What
the SOR framing adds to TPB, at the theoretical level, is the
proposition that AI actively conﬁgures the stimulus environment
in ways that may systematically bias consumer processing toward
particular outcomes (EsmarkJonesetal., 2020 ; LurieandMason,
2007; Sunstein, 2019) a proposition that carries both commercial
promise and ethical risk, and one this study examines through its
consequences for the measured antecedents rather than through
directmeasurementofAIexposureitself.

3.5 Construct-level review and AI
relevance
3.5.1 Reliability
In the eco-packaging literature, reliability refers to consumers’
perceptions of whether sustainable materials adequately perform
their protective function ( MagnierandSchoormans, 2015 ;
Venkateshetal., 2003 ). AI could, in principle, bolster these
perceptions through machine-learning quality dashboards,
blockchain-veriﬁed certiﬁcations, and predictive failure-rate
displays at the point of purchase (Kauretal., 2022 ; Lewisetal.,
2007;NordinandSelke,2010 ).
3.5.2 Willingness to pay
Willingness to pay (WTP) a premium captures the trade-oﬀ
between economic cost and environmental beneﬁt (Larocheetal.,
2001; RokkaandUusitalo, 2008 ). AI pricing intelligence could,
in principle, reduce perceived sacriﬁce through personalized cost
beneﬁt framing (Davenportetal., 2020 ). Our results, however,
indicate that sustained price-premium messaging may eventually
generate resistance rather than commitment a point developed
furtherinthediscussion.
3.5.3 Subjective norms
Socialpressurefromsigniﬁcantothershaslongbeenrecognized
as a key predictor of behavior within TPB (Ajzen, 1991). In AI-
mediated environments, this pressure could plausibly be ampliﬁed
through social-listening algorithms, inﬂuencer campaigns curated
by recommendation systems, and peer-comparison dashboards
(Marianietal., 2022 ; Mogajietal., 2021 ; Vermaetal., 2023 ). The
ﬁnding, reported below, that subjective norms are the strongest
direct predictor of CI ( β = 0.434) suggests that whatever
ampliﬁcation mechanism is at work merits further, more direct
empiricalattention.
3.5.4 Environmental concern
Environmental concern awareness of ecological problems
coupled with a felt obligation to act (DunlapandJones, 2002 ;
StegandVlek, 2009 ) activates GFT’s normative goal frame.
Carbon-footprint dashboards, real-time environmental impact
displays embedded in e-commerce interfaces, and AI-curated
ecological news feeds could, in principle, elevate concern salience
across consumer touchpoints (Davenportetal., 2020 ; Naumov,
2019;Pizzietal.,2021 ).
3.5.5 Hedonic motivation
The hedonic pathway to sustainable consumption is
often overlooked, yet there is good reason to think that
pleasure and aesthetic gratiﬁcation ( Babinetal., 1994 ;
HirschmanandHolbrook, 1982 ) can operate as independent
motivatorsforeco-packagingadoption.AIcontentpersonalisation
could, in principle, surface visually compelling, texturally
distinctive packaging to aesthetically sensitive consumers, weaving
hedonic narratives into what might otherwise be a purely
normative green message (EsmarkJonesetal., 2020 ; RoyandPal,
2015;Schmitt,1999).
3.5.6 Personal innovativeness
The propensity to try new things ahead of one’s peers
(AgarwalandPrasad, 1998 ; Rogers, 2003) is particularly relevant
in a domain where eco-packaging formats are rapidly evolving.
AI-powered early-adopter segmentation could, in principle,
identify innovation-oriented consumers from behavioral signals
early product-trial frequency, novelty-seeking browsing and
target them with communications about novel eco-packaging
materials,therebyinitiatingdiﬀusioncascadesthateventuallyreach
mainstream consumers (Bhuttoetal., 2023 ; Nguyenetal., 2019 ;
SiauandWang,2018 ).
4 Theoretical framework and
hypothesis development
4.1 The integrated AI-lens model
The proposed model (Figure1 presents the full framework;
Figure2 presentstheAIEnablementIntensityMatrix)isorganized
around three nested structures. At the outermost level, the
SOR architecture positions the six antecedent constructs as the
stimulus layer and continuous eco-packaging use intention as
the behavioral response. Within the organism layer, behavioral
intention serves as the mediating motivational state. GFT provides
an organizing principle for the stimulus layer, grouping constructs
into hedonic (HM), gain (WP, R), and normative (EC, SN)
clusters. The AI lens then marks each stimulus as, in theory, not
static but algorithmically constructed, dynamically updated, and
continuously personalized across purchase touchpoints (Jainetal.,
2024; Lvetal., 2022 ) again, a theoretical characterisation of the
digitalenvironmentratherthanameasuredpropertyofthestimuli
inthisstudy.

antecedents into hedonic, gain, and normative goal frames a
classiﬁcation that neither TPB nor SOR provides on its own. TPB
supplies the behavioral-intention pathway that links antecedent
beliefs to downstream action, including the mediation logic tested
through H8–H13. SOR supplies the stimulus–organism–response

5 Research methodology
5.1 Research design and philosophical
stance
This study is grounded in positivist epistemology and follows
a hypothetico-deductive logic (BrymanandBell, 2015 ; Malhotra,

becauseitenablessystematichypothesistestingwithalargesample,
supports psychometric reliability and validity evaluation, and

2019). A cross-sectional design is also well precedented in the eco-
packaging and AI-adoption literatures on which this study builds

7 Discussion
7.1 Why personal innovativeness matters
most
The ﬁnding that stands out most clearly from these results
is the prominence of personal innovativeness as a driver of
BI (β = 0.344, p < .001). This is not entirely surprising
given existing innovation-adoption theory (AgarwalandPrasad,
1998; Rogers, 2003), but its magnitude relative to the other
antecedents including environmental concern, which is often
treated as the natural engine of green behavior warrants comment.
Whatappearstobehappeningisthat,intheeco-packagingdomain,
the novelty dimension of the product category is suﬃciently
salient that innovation-prone consumers are disposed toward
adoption in a way that goes beyond purely normative or ecological
motivation. This ﬁnding aligns with emerging evidence linking
innovation disposition to green-technology uptake more broadly
(Bhuttoetal., 2023 ; Nguyenetal., 2019 ; SiauandWang, 2018 ).
If AI systems are designed to identify early adopters through
behavioral data traces early product-trial frequencies, novelty-
seeking browsing patterns, ﬁrst-mover social sharing and to target
them with precision communications, diﬀusion-of-innovation
cascades could, in principle, be accelerated (Dwivedietal., 2023 ;
Lvetal., 2022 ; Rogers, 2003). We frame this as a theoretical
implication extrapolated from the measured constructs and the
broader AI literature, not as an empirical ﬁnding of the present
study,sincenoAItargetingmechanismwastesteddirectly.
7.2 Subjective norms: a more powerful
force than intention
Arguablythemosttheoreticallyprovocativeﬁndinginthestudy
is that subjective norms predict CI more strongly (β = 0.434)
than behavioral intention does which is, in fact, non-signiﬁcant.
This pattern departs from the standard TPB expectation, where
BI should be the proximate cause of behavior. One interpretation,
consistent with habitual-behavior theory (VerplankenandOrbell,
2003), is that once normative social pressure becomes suﬃciently
routinized through, for example, repeated exposure to peer-
comparison dashboards, inﬂuencer sustainability campaigns, or
social-listeningoutputsondigitalplatformsitmaybeginoperating
in a more automatic fashion, bypassing the deliberative intentions
that TPB treats as behavioral gateways. We present this as a
theoretical interpretation grounded in dual-process reasoning
rather than as a tested mechanism; the present cross-sectional
design cannot itself establish automaticity. If AI systems are
designedtocreateambient,repeatednormativecues,thisreasoning
suggests such systems could in principle prove more eﬀective
than systems designed to drive explicit purchase intentions
(Marianietal., 2022 ; Vermaetal., 2023 ), though this remains
an implication for future experimental testing rather than a
demonstratedresult.

7.3 Environmental concern: two routes to
continuance
Environmental concern operates through two distinct
pathways simultaneously: through BI (β = 0.265, p < .001)
and directly on CI (β = 0.177, p = 0.001). This dual-pathway
structure is consistent with environmental identity theory
(VanderWerﬀandSteg, 2016 ), which holds that for consumers
who have internalized ecological concern as part of their self-
concept, sustainable behavior can become less dependent on
deliberativerecalculationateachchoicepoint.ForAIapplications,
this suggests again as a theoretical implication rather than a tested
claim that interventions aimed at building ecological identity,
such as personalized sustainability dashboards, cumulative impact
trackers, or ongoing ecological news feeds, may in principle
produce more durable eﬀects than one-oﬀ communications
targeting transient intention formation (Pizzietal., 2021 ; Wang
etal.,2022).
7.4 The WTP negative effect: a
price-fatigue interpretation
The negative direct eﬀect of WTP on CI (β =− 0.161, p =
0.001) is, on its face, counterintuitive: why would consumers more
willing to pay a premium be less likely to continue using eco-
packaging? We interpret this ﬁnding through two complementary
theoreticallenses,extendingbeyondapurelypost-hocaccount.The
ﬁrst is mental accounting theory (Thaler, 1999), which describes
how consumers track expenditures against informal budgets and
may develop aversion to ongoing ﬁnancial commitment once the
cumulative cost becomes salient; high stated WTP may reﬂect a
willingness to payoccasionally rather than continuously, and the
gap between the two produces the negative eﬀect. The second is
construal-level theory (TropeandLiberman, 2010), which predicts
thathigh-level,abstractWTPdeclarationsmadeatsurveytimemay
not translate into consistent low-level, concrete repeat-purchase
behavior once price salience increases at the point of purchase. A
relatedliteratureonsunk-costaversiondocumentsasimilarpattern
of heightened sensitivity to ongoing costs among individuals who
have already made a ﬁnancial commitment (ArkesandBlumer,
1985),whichreinforcestheplausibilityofthisinterpretation.
Toassesswhetherthisnegativeeﬀectisitselfattributabletothe
BIpathway,weconductedasupplementarymediationcheckofthe
WTP→BI→CI indirect pathway; consistent with the null BI→CI
path reported inTable8, this indirect eﬀect was non-signiﬁcant,
indicating that the negative WTP→CI eﬀect operates as a direct
eﬀect rather than through BI. We treat this as an unexpected,
exploratory result warranting dedicated experimental follow-up
rather than a settled conclusion. Practically, this pattern suggests
that AI pricing communications directed at already-committed
eco-packaging users might do better to avoid foregrounding cost
diﬀerentials and instead frame each purchase in terms of its
marginal environmental contribution, drawing on commitment
and consistency principles ( Cialdini, 2001; EsmarkJonesetal.,
2020;Kauretal.,2022 ;Wangetal.,2022).
7.5 Non-signiﬁcant paths and the BI–CI
gap
The failure of reliability (β = 0.020, p = 0.600) and hedonic
motivation (β =− 0.006, p = 0.920) to signiﬁcantly predict
BI may reﬂect sample-speciﬁc characteristics. The study’s urban,
educated respondents may have already resolved questions of
functionaladequacyeco-packagingmayhaveimprovedsuﬃciently
that reliability is no longer a discriminating antecedent for this
population while aesthetic novelty may carry diminishing salience
upon repeated exposure. The non-signiﬁcant BI →CI path is
arguably the most important structural result in the study: it
suggests that, in the post-adoption continuance phase, what drives
behavior is not deliberative intentional re-evaluation but rather
more direct normative and ecological activation. This pattern is
consistent with dual-process theory (Evans, 2008) and implies, as
a theoretical extrapolation rather than a directly tested claim, that
automatic (System 1) AI channels habitual nudges, default eco-
packaging settings, ambient normative displays may outperform
deliberative (System 2) messaging in sustaining eco-packaging use
(Dwivedietal.,2023 ;Rezaeietal.,2023 ).
7.6 Reassessing the mediation hypotheses
(H8–H13)
Given the null BI→CI path and the resulting non-signiﬁcant
indirect eﬀects reported inTable8, we do not treat H8–H13 as
conﬁrmed mediation hypotheses. We instead reframe them, in
this revized manuscript, as exploratory hypotheses whose non-
signiﬁcantoutcomesarethemselvesinformative:theyindicatethat,
within this sample, behavioral intention does not function as the
primary conduit between the antecedents and continuance. The
theoretically more important ﬁnding is the pattern of signiﬁcant
direct eﬀects of SN and EC on CI, which together with the
null mediation results collectively point toward the automatic-
processing account developed above rather than a classical TPB-
styledeliberative-intentionaccountofcontinuance.

10 Limitations
Several limitations warrant explicit statement. First, and
most fundamentally, AI constructs were not directly measured,
experimentally manipulated, or otherwise operationalized in
this study; all AI-related claims in this paper are theoretical
extrapolations from the measured consumer-behavior constructs
and the broader literature, not empirical ﬁndings, and readers
should not interpret the AI-enabled framing of the model as
evidence of a tested AI mechanism. Second, the cross-sectional,
single-time-point survey design captures intention as reported
at one moment rather than actual repeated behavior; claims
regarding continuance, habit formation, and automaticity are
therefore theoretical interpretations aligned with dual-process and
habitual-behavior theory rather than direct behavioral evidence,
and longitudinal panel designs or ﬁeld experiments would be
required to verify genuine habit formation. Third, two HTMT
ratios exceed the conventional 0.85 threshold PI–HM (0.943)
and SN–CI (0.880) indicating these construct pairs are more
strongly correlated than the convention prefers, even though
supplementary cross-loading and Fornell–Larcker checks indicate
all four constructs remain empirically distinguishable at the item
level; future work should consider reﬁning these scales for the eco-
packagingcontext.Fourth,theSRMRvalues(0.108saturated;0.112
estimated) exceed the conservative 0.08 benchmark, and while this
isnotunusualforstructurallycomplexPLS-SEMmodels,werecord
it as a limitation of model ﬁt and recommend cross-validation
using CB-SEM on an independent sample. Fifth, the subjective-
norms scale’s Cronbach’s alpha (0.651) fell below the conventional
threshold, and although composite reliability supported its use, a
longer, re-validated SN scale is recommended for future studies.
Finally, purposive sampling from urban and semi-urban Indian
settings limits generalisability to rural populations and to other
national or cultural contexts; replication across more diverse
samplesisencouraged.
