Artificial Intelligence (AI) has transformed digital marketing by enabling personalized
customer experiences and fostering sustainable consumption practices. From a decision -
making perspective, understanding these relationships is also important for designing AI-
enabled marketing strategies that support more informed, sustainability -oriented
consumer choices and managerial decisions. This research investigated the relationships
among AI-Powered Personalization (AIP), Environmental Transparency (ET), Green Trust
(GT), and Sustainable Purchase Intention (SPI) in digital marketing environments to
develop an empirical framework for understanding AI -enabled sustainable consumer
decision-making. Quantitative approach was adopted, and Data were collected from 428
online consumers through a structured questionnaire using a five -point Likert scale. The
proposed research model was evaluated using Partial Least Squares Structural Equation
Modeling (PLS-SEM) to assess the reliability and validity of the measurement model  and
test the hypothesized relationships. The findings revealed that AIP significantly enhanced
ET (β = 0.68) and GT (β = 0.43). ET positively influenced GT (β = 0.39) and SPI (β = 0.28),
while GT exerted the strongest positive effect on SPI (β = 0.51). Fu rthermore, GT
significantly mediated the relationship between AIP and SPI (β = 0.22). The resulting
structural relationships provide a decision -support perspective by identifying the
mechanisms through which AI -enabled personalization can influence sustain able
consumer choices in digital marketing settings. The findings provide evidence -based
guidance for marketers to strengthen consumer trust, optimize AI-enabled personalization
and environmental communication strategies, and support environmentally respon sible
purchasing decisions.

2. Related Work
Current digital retail platforms deliver inadequate, tailored sustainability information, weak
environmental transparency, and uncertain trustworthiness, resulting in consumers' low trust and low
participation in sustainable purchases. From a decision -making perspective, these limitations indicate
that consumers often make sustainability-related choices under conditions of incomplete information,
uncertain environmental claims, and varying levels of trust in AI -enabled marketing systems. AI -
powered virtual try-on technologies have also been shown to influence online consumer responses,
with brand trust playing an important role in shaping purchase related outcomes [11].
An investigation into how AI chatbot functions impacted green customer satisfaction and green loyalty
was carried out [12]. Based on Stimulus–Organism–Response (SOR), Value–Belief–Norm Theory (VBN),
and PLS-SEM, data were collected from 762 respondents, re vealing a positive influence mediated by
pro-environmental behavior. The design was cross -sectional and self -reported, thus hindering
generalization. To investigate how Jordan's Electric Vehicle (EV) industry's AI -driven dynamic pricing
affects customer sa tisfaction [13]. A PLS -SEM with 454 users indicated that decision confidence
mediated fully decision satisfaction, and trust was negatively moderated. It was a cross-sectional study
and could not support any causal relationship or generalizability. Sustainable green marketing practices
in the readymade apparel sector have similarly been examined through Structural Equation Modeling,
highlighting the relevance of consumer-oriented sustainability strategies in purchase-related contexts
[14]. To analyze how bl ockchain-based trust, AI -driven personalization, and behavioral factors
influenced green payment intentions [15]. Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) analysis on the data
from 375 Malaysian consumers demonstrated significant positive relationships. The resu lts were
moderated by involvement; however, as it was cross-sectional data, it was difficult to generalize widely.
The influence of artificial intelligence on consumers’ lifestyle product choices and sustainable
behaviour was examined [16], demonstrating the relevance of AI -enabled technologies to
sustainability oriented consumer decision contexts. AI -driven virtual tr y-on technology and its
influence on green purchase intention were investigated [17]. Based on the experimental design of
task-based, questionnaire, and PLS -SEM were using the sample of 424 respondents, and found that
psychological pathways were significant, while the transparency of feedback was not. Moreover, green
preference was restricted due to price sensitivity in terms of green adoption.
Generative AI usage for shopping decisions and continued adoption behavior was investigated [18]
using data from 397 users through PLS -SEM and Artificial Neural Network (ANN). Results identified
enjoyment, perceived accuracy, and benefits as significant pr edictors. Cross -sectional data limited
causal interpretation and broader generalizability. User purchase intentions toward AI -generated
cultural and creative products were investigated [19] using SEM, exploratory and confirmatory factor
analyses, and ANN w ith 526 valid samples. Results identified attitude, perceived value, price, quality,
and enjoyment as key drivers. Cross -sectional data limited generalizability. AI adoption among young
consumers and its influence on sustainable consumption behavior and li festyle choices were
investigated [20] using the UTAUT2 framework and SEM analysis. Findings indicated that AI supported
sustainability-oriented decisions, while broader demographic and contextual factors remained
unexplored.
Green marketing and artificial intelligence contributions toward corporate sustainability across
environmental, social, and economic dimensions were investigated [21] using a mixed -methods
approach and PLS -SEM. Findings showed strong sustainability improve ments, although evidence

2.1. Research Gap
Existing research has not sufficiently explained how AI -driven personalization, environmental
transparency, and green trust jointly shape sustainable purchase intention within a digital purchasing
and decision-making context. Limitations were self -reported responses, only the group of consumers
could be studied, and no other relevant variables, cultures, or time trends could be involved [12].
Limited to the Malaysian customers, self-reported responses. A causal conclusion can't be drawn since
the cross-sectional design was used; no other technology, culture, and regulatory-related factors in the
purchase process with green payment were taken into account [15]. Most research relied on self -
reported data from specific populations and regions, limiting causal i nference, external validity, and
the generalizability of findings across diverse contexts and consumer segments. Beyond these
methodological limitations, an important conceptual gap remains in the decision -making literature.
Existing studies rarely integra te AI -enabled personalization, sustainability -related information
transparency, and trust formation into a single structural framework capable of explaining how
technology-supported interactions translate into sustainable consumer decisions.
The present research addresses these conceptual and analytical gaps by examining AI -powered
personalization, environmental transparency, green trust, and sustainable purchase intention within a
unified structural framework for digital marketing environment s. Rather than establishing causality,
the study provides an explanatory assessment of the direct and mediating relationships among these
constructs using PLS -SEM. This approach is intended to improve understanding of the mechanisms
through which AI -enabled marketing practices influence sustainability -oriented consumer decisions
and to generate evidence that can support managerial decision-making in digital era.

3.2. Variable Explanation
Research variables were divided into four sections, namely AI -based personalization, environmental
transparency, green trust, and sustainable purchase intention. Four constructs were chosen to
investigate the consumers' response to intelligent digital reta il systems and transparency of
sustainability-related information, as well as to assess green trust developed for environmentally
friendly statements, and consumers' sustainable purchase intention in online contexts. Within the
proposed framework, AIP represents the technology-enabled input, ET and GT represent information-
and trust -based decision mechanisms, and SPI represents the resulting sustainability -oriented
consumer decision outcome.
AIP: AI-powered personalization means artificial intelligence algorithms being utilized in digital retail
environments to tailor the user's recommendations, as well as their sustainable information.
ET: Environmental transparency is defined as how digital retail platforms clearly, truthfully, and openly

3.3. Questionnaire Design
The questionnaire aimed at examining the following four latent variables affecting consumers'
purchase behaviors in the digital commerce environments: smart recommendation services driven by
artificial intelligence, the disclosure of green information, gre en trust, and green purchase intention.
The questionnaire design and the items used to measure these constructs were presented in
supplementary file. The questionnaire was structured to capture the perceptual and behavioral
mechanisms underlying AI -enabled sustainable purchase decisions, including personalization,
information transparency, trust, and purchase intention.
The items were assessed on a 5-point Likert scale (1 = Strongly Disagree, 2 = Disagree, 3 = Neutral, 4 =
Agree, and 5 = Strongly Agree). After an initial pretest, the questionnaire was found to be clear,
relevant, and reliable. Adjustments were made to improve data quality.
3.4. Statistical Analysis
All statistical analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS version 26 and SmartPLS version 4. The
analytical procedure involved PLS-SEM modeling, CFA, and discriminant validity assessment to evaluate
the measurement properties of the constructs and estimate th e structural relationships within the
proposed decision-oriented framework. PLS-SEM was appropriate for this study because it enables the
simultaneous assessment of multiple latent constructs, direct structural relationships, and mediation
effects, making it suitable for examining the interconnected mechanisms underlying sustainable
consumer decision-making. The hypotheses were evaluated through path analysis by examining the
magnitude and statistical significance of the proposed relationships among AIP, ET, GT, and SPI, thereby
assessing how AI -enabled personalization, environmental information, and trust contribute to
sustainable purchase decision formation.

5. Discussion
In a digital purchasing context, this study examined how AI -powered personalization, environmental
transparency, and green trust shape sustainable purchase intention and contribute to the formation of
sustainability-oriented consumer decisions. The findings indicate that AI-powered personalization does
not operate only as a technological feature of digital purchasing but also as an important input in the
consumer decision process. The positive association it has with environmental transparency and green
trust indicates that personalized digital experiences can help contribute to sustainable decision making,
provided they are facilitated with environmental information and sustainable claims that are credible.
Here, the concepts of “personalization” are unders tood as a technology -based stimulus, and
“transparency” and “trust” as the two instruments through which consumers assess sustainability
information before making a buying decision. The relatively positive link between AI -based
personalization and environm ental transparency suggests that AI -based personalized retail
interactions could be especially impactful in making sustainable information more accessible and
salient. Concurrently, the positive correlation between green trust and sustainable purchase intention

6. Conclusion
This study investigated the effects of AI -Powered Personalization (AIP), Environmental Transparency
(ET), and Green Trust (GT) on Sustainable Purchase Intention (SPI) within an AI -enabled digital retail
decision-making context. A quantitative research desi gn and PLS -SEM were used to evaluate six
hypothesized relationships. The findings showed that AIP significantly influenced GT (β = 0.43, t = 8.27)
and ET (β = 0.68, t = 12.54). ET significantly influenced SPI (β = 0.28, t = 4.89) and GT (β = 0.39, t = 7.15),
while GT demonstrated the strongest relationship with SPI (β = 0.51, t = 9.46). The mediating effect of
GT between AIP and SPI was also significant (β = 0.22, t = 5.38). All six hypotheses were supported,
confirming positive structural relationships amo ng the proposed constructs. These findings indicate
that AI -powered personalization, environmental transparency, and green trust jointly contribute to
sustainable purchase decision formation in digital selling environments. From a decision -support
perspective, the study demonstrates that personalization should be integrated with transparent
environmental communication and credible trust -building mechanisms. The identified structural
relationships provide evidence-based guidance for retailers in configuring AI-enabled personalization
strategies and sustainability communication practices. Therefore, the study contributes to AI -enabled
and statistical decision-making by providing an interpretable framework for supporting more informed,
trustworthy, and sustainability-oriented consumer decisions in digital retail.
