# ClickstreamGPT: a transformer-based generative AI approach to clickstream prediction for E-commerce platforms

Electronic Commerce Research
https://doi.org/10.1007/s10660-025-10071-7
Abstract
Understanding how customers behave on digital platforms and predicting their in -
teractions in advance are valuable assets for fostering customer loyalty and stim -
ulating engagement within e-commerce applications. In this context, clickstream
(customer interaction patterns through their clicks) prediction can be useful in iden -
tifying future behaviors and supporting strategic institutional decisions. Thus, we
propose ClickstreamGPT, an innovative Generative Pre-trained Transformer (GPT)
approach for predicting clickstream behavior on e-commerce platforms. For this
study, our GPT-based model was trained using clickstream data from 130,000 cus -
tomers within an e-banking application, comprising 100 million clickstream records
across approximately 700 unique application screens and their execution flows.
Experimental results indicate that our proposed algorithm is capable of predicting
up to 37 distinct click patterns for each customer with an average accuracy of 78%.
This predictive capability can be crucial for institutions in anticipating customer
churn, optimizing marketing campaigns, and enhancing content recommendation
within e-commerce platforms.

1 Introduction
The proliferation of the internet has empowered businesses to offer their products
and services through e-commerce applications, significantly transforming customer
interaction dynamics. Financial institutions, in particular, have leveraged this trend
by offering financial operations through e-banking, a specific category of e-com -
merce [1, 2]. Nevertheless, e-banking has presented banks with new organizational
challenges, needing rapid adaptation. A critical challenge lies in fostering customer
loyalty and stimulating greater engagement with the e-banking services offered
[3]. Personalized advertisements and offers constitute crucial drivers for enhancing
customer loyalty and engagement [ 4]. Such personalization is effectively realized
through a comprehensive understanding of customer profiles in e-banking environ -
ments, predominantly informed by the analysis of navigation patterns on these plat -
forms [5].
Clickstream can be defined as the sequences of clicks generated by customers
while navigating a digital application. In this way, this strategy can be used to record
the customer’s journey in e-banking applications and gain a better understanding of
customer behavior. As clickstreams describe customer interaction patterns, therefore,
predicting future clicks can anticipate future behaviors and contribute to strategic
planning within institutions. Consequently, studies exploring the predictability of
customer actions through click sequences become necessary [6–8].
Given that customer interactions within e-banking apps are composed of sequences
of clicks, analogous to the syntactic structure of a textual sentence, it becomes rel -
evant to employ natural language processing techniques to predict future sequences
based on existing information. An important example of a model capable of this task
is the GPT (Generative Pre-trained Transformer).
GPT refers to an architecture for generative language models based on neural
networks. These models are built upon the concept of a pre-trained, decoder-only
Transformer [9–11]. They are capable of learning textual patterns and context during
training, enabling them to generate coherent and contextually appropriate text [9–11].
By feeding GPT with historical clickstream data from an e-banking app, where each
screen is defined as a token, the model can utilize existing patterns in click sequences
to generate subsequent tokens. This enables the prediction of the next screens that
customers will access, thus anticipating future customer behaviors.
Considering the presented scenario, the objective of this study is to employ an
innovative GPT approach to predict future customer intentions as expressed through
their clickstream within e-banking applications. Specifically, this study explores an
unprecedented application for GPT, extending its capabilities beyond natural lan -
guage and targeting the analysis of user behavior data, specifically the sequences of
clicks generated by users when interacting with e-commerce applications.

2 Theorical background
Increasingly, understanding human behavior on digital e-commerce platforms has
brought computer science closer to cognitive psychology [ 12]. With the conception
of new AI models—particularly the development and refinement of generative mod-
els based on Transformers—it has become possible to enhance the representation of
cognitive processes and online navigation patterns [13]. Thus, the interdisciplinarity
among AI, human behavior, and e-commerce establishes new opportunities to better
map and predict customer consumption patterns in digital environments [14].
Thus, this chapter delves into the theoretical foundations that support the concep-
tual framework of the study — beginning with a presentation of the state of the art
in the application of GPT models to e-commerce (Sect. 2.1). Subsequently, various
studies that employed AI models for clickstream prediction are presented and dis -
cussed (Sect. 2.2). It is important to note, based on the results reported in this sec -
tion, that non-generative AI models have inherent structural limitations and tend to
struggle when predicting longer clickstream patterns.
Specifically, human interactions are not purely sequential: they depend on history,
intention, and context [15]. Non-generative AI models, by not natively incorporating
global attention mechanisms, show limitations in predicting human behavioral pat -
terns in longer clickstream chains [ 16]. The introduction of Transformers and GPT
models, starting in 2018, revolutionized the way human behavior began to be mod -
eled and interpreted in digital contexts [ 17]. In Sect. 2.3, we discuss the potential
of Transformers and the self-attention mechanism [ 18] for mapping and predicting
clickstream behavior on e-commerce platforms. Furthermore, Sect. 2.4 discusses
the evolution of Generative Pre-trained Transformers (GPT) and their open-source
applications.
2.1 GPT and E-commerce
GPT’s application has shown great promise across various e-commerce contexts. For
natural language processing tasks, these models have been utilized to create product
descriptions, automate categorization, and detect fraudulent reviews. More recently
[19], introduced a novel method that leverages GPT to transform aspect-based rat -
ings into comprehensive textual reviews. The resulting texts exhibited strong coher -
ence and informative content, matching human-generated reviews in legibility and
practical utility. This feature directly enhances consumer decision-making during the
purchasing process.
Beyond traditional textual tasks, GPT has also been explored as a tool for simulat-
ing human behavior in controlled environments, through the methodology known
as Turing Experiments [20]. Although originally applied in areas such as behavioral
economics and social psychology [21], suggests that this approach can be adapted to
the e-commerce domain, allowing for the simulation of purchase decisions, reactions
to promotional offers, and preferences of consumers with varied profiles. The authors
also highlight that such models can reproduce cognitive biases or exhibit hyper-accu-
rate behaviors, which raises important ethical and operational implications for the use
of GPTs in automated recommendation and customer service systems.

2.2 Clickstream prediction
The use of clickstreams has been extensively explored as a tool to understand online
customer behavior through their digital navigations. For instance [ 5], explored the
use of complex networks to identify patterns in customer clickstreams and detect
malicious accounts on a specific social network. In their study, hierarchical graphs
were used to cluster customers based on the similarity of their clicks. This same
clustering method was employed by [26] to identify customer behaviors leading to a
financial action in a mobile application.
Beyond understanding behaviors through clickstreams, predicting future customer
actions based on clickstream pattern analysis is also discussed in various studies. A
common approach to explore this predictability is the Markov Chain (MC). In their
study [27], proposed three MC-based stream mining algorithms to discover frequent
sequential patterns from clickstream data. These patterns are used to construct the
transition matrix that will be used to predict the customer’s clickstream, such as the
next webpage that will be visited.
Extending the concept of MC, Hidden Markov Models (HMMs) are also used to
predict the future state along time series. In the study of [ 28], clickstream patterns
are extracted from noisy navigation sequences using a modified suffix tree and used
to model customer behavior through a modified version of HMM. In the work, an
accuracy of 81% is achieved. However, only the 5 most probable subsequent clicks
are generated.
In [29], a hybrid intelligent classifier combining HMM with the recurrent neural
network Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) [ 30, 31] was used to predict student
clicks in an educational system, anticipating unusual needs or behaviors in real time.

The prediction component was further enhanced in [ 32] by employing an encoder-
decoder algorithm, improving the accuracy of the first click in the next sequence of
future activities. In this approach, clickstream data is used to construct a Bayesian
Network (BN) [ 33] that calculates a score. This Bayesian score feeds the attention
mechanism of a Transformer model within an LSTM/GRU-RNN architecture [30].
The study by [7] proposes predicting purchase intent in e-commerce using click -
stream data. The study compares supervised classifiers — Logistic Regression,
Decision Tree, Random Forest, and XGBoost [ 34] — and introduces the Oracle
meta-classifier to combine their results. The Oracle outperformed binary classifiers,
achieving an accuracy of 94.5% and better performance in metrics such as precision,
recall, and F1-score for the purchase intention task.
In summary, traditional AI models such as Markov Chains, Hidden Markov Mod-
els, and Recurrent Neural Networks have been extensively applied to predict click -
stream sequences, yielding important results for the proposed task. However, these
models have inherent structural limitations, struggling to map long click sequences
and to contextually relate human behavior. Since human behavior involves complex
temporal dynamics and interactions, modeling such behavior requires approaches
capable of understanding the global relationships of dependencies between clicks —
and Transformers offer promising solutions to address these dependencies. The next
Sect. (2.3) explores these advances, discussing how Transformers and GPT models
can enhance clickstream mapping and prediction in e-commerce platforms.
2.3 GPT on clickstream: mapping human behavior
GPT-based models mark a significant advancement in artificial intelligence, owing to
their capacity to simulate and, in some respects, understand the intricacies of human
behavior, from text generation to image creation [10, 21]. At the core of GPT models,
the Transformer architecture employs a self-attention mechanism [ 10]. This mecha-
nism allows the model to assign relative importance to each vocabulary element, fac-
toring in its position, order, and contextual meaning throughout the training dataset.
This global contextual awareness is pivotal for the following properties:

1.   Coherent Generation [35]: The GPT’s ability to learn long-range dependencies,
through the application of the global attention mechanism, allows this genera -
tive AI to map and model the probability distributions of large word sequences,
generating a coherent non-linear correlation across extensive training sets.
 2.   Contextual Representations [36]: The self-attention mechanism enables contex-
tual learning of terms, whereby each word displays distinct semantic definitions
through the mapping of its weight correlations with the generated sentence or
adjacent terms.
 3.   Fine-tuning and Human Feedback [37]: Fine-tuning and Reinforcement Learn-
ing with Human Feedback (RLHF) are post-training stages that involve collect -
ing human feedback to reward more relevant algorithm responses. While not the
self-attention mechanism itself, self-attention is the architectural foundation that
allows the model to be refined and adapt its behavior based on this feedback.

 4.   Large-Scale Pre-training [ 35]: Large-scale pre-training allows the model to
learn complex patterns, grammar, facts, logical reasoning, and even human lan -
guage-like inferences. This process is significantly enabled by the Transformer’s
self-attention mechanism, which supports parallel processing of vast amounts of
data, making the training of such extensive models feasible and efficient.
Specifically, the ‘Coherent Generation’ property inherent to the Transformer archi -
tecture can be crucial for modeling clickstream data. In this work, we established
a fundamental analogy: each user click is functionally comparable to a word, and
a sequence of clicks (clickstream) can be considered a textual sentence. Thus, the
Transformer’s global attention mechanism allows the model to directly weigh the
significance of each click in relation to all other clicks in the same sequence (click -
stream). This means that, instead of only considering adjacent clicks or a fixed history,
the Transformer can identify and assign importance to any click within the sequence,
regardless of its position, to comprehend the overall context of user behavior. This
characteristic enables the establishment of non-linear correlations and contextualiza-
tions, a deep understanding of the behavior flow, and the prediction of subsequent
actions with greater accuracy.
The ability of ‘Contextual Representations’ can also play a crucial role in the con-
struction of the proposed model. In a clickstream environment, the meaning of a
click, such as ‘chat_support’ (speaking with the attendant), varies drastically accord-
ing to the context. For instance, in the clickstream ‘home/financing/chat_support’,
the consumer’s intention is linked to financing, whereas in the clickstream ‘home/
credit_card/chat_support’, the intention refers to credit cards. This property allows
ClickstreamGPT to assign distinct meanings to the same click (‘chat_support’),
depending on the sequence of previous interactions, which is fundamental for a pre -
cise understanding of the user’s future behavior.
The property of ‘Large-Scale Pre-training’ is equally indispensable. E-commerce
clickstream systems generate a massive volume of data, often with millions of users
and billions of clicks. In this scenario, a scalable prediction architecture capable
of processing such vast amounts of data is fundamental for understanding com -
plex patterns – an intrinsic characteristic of consumer behavior. GPT models, with
their ability to learn from extensive datasets, are therefore ideal for capturing these
characteristics.
Thus, GPT introduces significant advances in the ability of artificial intelligence
to model complex human behaviors, highlighting the Transformer architecture as
the main theoretical and computational foundation for this approach. Self-attention
mechanisms have enabled major breakthroughs in the global understanding of human
semantic and syntactic behavior, and can be adapted for clickstream mapping and
prediction tasks. The concepts discussed in this section constitute the fundamental
theoretical basis of the computational model proposed in this study.

networks. These models are based on the Generative Pre-trained Transformer archi -

The first model in the GPT series, GPT-1, was introduced by OpenAI in 2018,

Since the release of GPT-1 in 2018, the evolution of OpenAI’s Generative Pre-
trained Transformer models has been marked by exponential growth in scale, mul -
timodality, and contextual reasoning. GPT-2 (2019) expanded model size and text
coherence [35], while GPT-3 (2020) introduced large-scale autoregressive training
with 175 billion parameters, enabling strong few-shot learning [ 41]. GPT-4 (2023)
further improved reasoning, safety alignment, and multilingual capabilities, and its
multimodal derivative [42] and GPT-4o (2024) integrated text, image, and audio pro-
cessing within a single architecture [43]. The most recent GPT-5 (2025) consolidates
these advances with enhanced multimodal understanding, larger context windows,
and improved efficiency, establishing the current state-of-the-art in generative Trans-
former-based language modeling [40].
Since then, several open-source implementations of GPT have been developed to
make this generative architecture accessible for research, education, and experimen-
tation. Among them, GPT-NeoX [44] employs an autoregressive Transformer archi-
tecture comparable to that of GPT-3, featuring billions of parameters and standing
as one of the most robust and transparent open-source GPT-style models available.
The LLaMA [45] family by Meta AI adopts an autoregressive transformer architec -
ture closely aligned with GPT-3 and GPT-4, offering comparable design principles
while remaining independently trained. Finally, NanoGPT [ 46] offers a minimalist
yet faithful reimplementation of GPT-2 in PyTorch. Comprising only a few hun -
dred lines of code, NanoGPT is particularly suitable for educational and research
purposes and adaptation to domain-specific tasks. These open implementations have

3 Methodology: the ClickstreamGPT framework
Due to the recent surge of interest in generative AI models, Large Language Models
(LLMs) have emerged as powerful precursors for many industries. LLMs are lan -
guage models consisting of deep neural networks with millions of parameters trained
on massive datasets [47]. Notably, LLMs exhibit a remarkable ability to understand
natural language, allowing them to construct structures associated with specific con-
texts suggested by users [48, 49]. Specifically, the capability of GPT-based models to
generate syntactically sound text structures can be leveraged in other domains.
This chapter describes a novel application of GPT algorithms to model customer
behavior in e-banking, here referred to as ClickstreamGPT. By analyzing the sequen-
tial patterns of user navigation, ClickstreamGPT enables the prediction of future con-
sumption. The presented method leverages the sequential structure of user navigation
(clickstreams) and utilizes GPT algorithms to forecast future consumption patterns.
3.1 Dataset
This study employs a dataset sourced from a large Brazilian banking institution,
comprising customer clickstream events generated from an e-banking application.
The dataset utilized navigation data from 130,000 customers, encompassing 100 mil-
lion clickstream records on application screens and a vocabulary consisting of 700
unique screens. Data was collected between January and September 2023, containing
the following fields: unique customer identification, session ID, and the sequence of
screens accessed in each session. The products offered in the financial institution’s
e-banking application are grouped into main categories: checking account, credit
card, financing, and insurance.
During the data preprocessing phase, representative clickstream sets were deter -
mined for each customer, derived from the screens accessed during sessions. A ses -
sion is defined as the time a customer interacts with the application, during which
they may access one or more screens. Thus, a session is represented as an array where

covered during pre-training. Recent advancements have significantly improved the

3.2.1 Model selection
Large-scale models such as GPT-NeoX (based on GPT-3) and LLaMA (based on
GPT-3 and GPT-4) possess a high number of parameters and strong generalization
capabilities, which consequently require substantial computational resources for fine-
tuning and deployment. For instance, GPT-NeoX comprises 20 billion parameters,
while LLaMA is available in versions starting from 7 billion parameters—making
it impractical to adapt ClickstreamGPT using large-scale models within the compu -
tational infrastructure available for this study. In contrast, NanoGPT (derived from
GPT-2) operates with 1 million to 124 million parameters (Table 1). After several
training tests conducted with NanoGPT, ClickstreamGPT achieved relevant results
for prediction using 25.5 million parameters, reinforcing the choice of a more light -
weight tool for our prediction task. The computational infrastructure used to train
ClickstreamGPT included a 3rd Generation Intel® Xeon® Platinum 8370 C processor
in a hyper-threaded configuration with eight processors and 32 GB of memory.
Regardless of computational constraints, the clickstream prediction problem
addressed in this study involves a highly specific context rather than a general text-
generation scenario. Hence, the parameter range provided by NanoGPT adequately
satisfies the modeling requirements for ClickstreamGPT. For example, our experi -
ments employed a context window of 84 tokens (NanoGPT supports up to 1,024),
while large-scale models such as GPT-NeoX and LLaMA allow up to 8,192 tokens.
Reducing the context window lowers the quadratic asymptotic cost of the attention
mechanism (O(n²)), enabling training on lower-capacity GPUs and improving algo -
rithmic stability and adaptability in medium-scale datasets—especially when viewed
from the perspective of large language models (LLMs).
Beyond computational efficiency, NanoGPT enables rapid prototyping, easy adap-
tation to non-textual symbolic sequences such as clickstreams, and efficient training
on medium-scale data. Its open architecture also allows direct customization of core
Transformer components—tokenization, positional encoding, and autoregressive
prediction—making it particularly suitable for this research. For these reasons listed
in this section, NanoGPT was selected as the GPT-based framework upon which
ClickstreamGPT was built.
3.2.2 Model adaptation and training setup
The dataset was tokenized, assigning unique tokens to each screen. This enabled the
application of transformers to clickstream data, which differs from natural language
in terms of structure and semantic rules. ClickstreamGPT adapted modules from
NanoGPT [46] to accommodate the unique characteristics of clickstream data, which
differ significantly from natural language. Unlike natural language, clickstreams lack

clickstreams were implemented and adapted for ClickstreamGPT.
During the training phase of ClickstreamGPT, 10,000 iterations and 25.5 million
parameters were employed using the Adam optimizer with β1 = 0.9 and β2 = 0.95.
A cosine learning rate schedule was adopted, where the learning rate was initially
increased and then decayed to zero. Dropout was not utilized. To optimize the mod -
el’s accuracy and computational efficiency, various experiments were conducted. The
final parameter configuration for ClickstreamGPT is detailed in Table 2. Specifically,
in our study, the ClickstreamGPT used 25 million parameters.
Finally, to adapt the NanoGPT architecture to the clickstream domain, specific
modules and training functions were modified. The standard text-based tokeniza -
tion pipeline (dataset.py) was replaced by a symbolic encoding routine that assigns
unique integer tokens to each screen, allowing the model to interpret user naviga -
tion sequences as structured symbolic sentences. In the model definition (model.py),
the vocabulary size and embedding dimensions were adjusted to fit a smaller input
space of approximately 700 unique tokens, while the context window was reduced to
84 tokens to optimize computational efficiency. Additional adjustments were imple-
mented in the training loop (train.py), including the use of an Adam optimizer with
β₁ = 0.9 and β₂ = 0.95, a cosine learning rate scheduler, and the removal of dropout
to ensure stability in medium-scale data. Furthermore, logical semantic and syntac -
tic dependencies between screens were defined during preprocessing to represent
the temporal and functional flow of user interactions. These collective modifications
enabled ClickstreamGPT to transform user navigation sequences into a structured,
learnable representation consistent with the generative principles of Transformer-
based language models.

4.2 Clickstreamgpt: architecture and training
One of the primary challenges encountered during the development of this project
arose post-model training. At this stage, we faced the need to fine-tune specific con -
figurations related to screen generation. A variety of options and parameters were
explored to identify the optimal settings for our application.
Context was the primary parameter we tuned. We initially used an 8-month cus -
tomer interaction history as the basis, but quickly realized that shorter timeframes
were sufficient for the model to capture customer trends, since, as Fig. 3 shows,
clickstreams related to this e-banking product do not vary greatly over the months.
Consequently, we progressively reduced this window to a single month.
The calibration of the temperature and top-k parameters proved crucial in enhanc-
ing the effectiveness of our results. Finding the optimal temperature value was essen-
tial to balance the randomness of generated outputs with the reproduction of relevant
patterns. Similarly, adjusting the top-k parameter was vital for ensuring an appropri-
ate selection of tokens during text generation, avoiding the inclusion of too many
irrelevant options or overly restricting the model’s choices. These adjustments were
instrumental in achieving a balance between diversity and relevance in the generated
screens, leading to more accurate and meaningful predictions of customer navigation
within the application.
A temperature value of 0.7 was selected to balance accuracy (0.76) and the number
of unique screens (10 unique screens) used in predictions, as illustrated in Fig. 4. This
temperature setting ensured the generation of consistent clickstream patterns while
allowing for some degree of randomness, which occasionally resulted in the genera-
tion of screens that were somewhat disconnected from the context.
To address this issue, we also employed the top-k parameter, which selects the k
most probable screens from the set of possible unique screens. As shown in Fig. 5, a
top-k value of 5 yielded a maximum accuracy of 0.77, enhancing the model’s preci -
sion by narrowing down the search space for potential screens while maintaining a
diversity of 10 unique screens.

5 Discussion
This chapter discusses the main findings derived from the implementation and vali -
dation of the ClickstreamGPT model, positioning them in relation to previous studies
and theoretical foundations presented earlier. The objective is to critically analyze
the model’s performance, its methodological contributions, and its implications for
understanding and predicting user behavior in e-commerce environments. Section 5.1
compares ClickstreamGPT with previous approaches in clickstream prediction, high-
lighting the advantages of transformer-based generative architectures over traditional
methods. Section 5.2 deepens the interpretation of the obtained results, linking them
to behavioral and computational perspectives. Section 5.3 focuses on the application
of ClickstreamGPT within the broader e-commerce context, examining its scalabil -
ity, limitations, and adaptability to different market domains. Finally, Sect. 5.4 pres-
ents the main methodological and practical limitations identified, outlining directions
for future research and potential enhancements to the proposed framework.
5.1 ClickstreamGPT and previous studies
For predicting purchase intentions from clickstreams on e-commerce platforms, the
study by [ 7] utilized conventional machine learning algorithms, including Logistic
Regression, Decision Trees, Random Forest, and XGBoost [ 34]. Although relevant
for this discriminative task, these models are inherently limited by their heavy reli -
ance on manual feature engineering and their inferior generalization capability in
complex scenarios, especially in tasks involving deep semantic understanding, gen -
eration, or contextual reasoning [34, 50–53].
In contrast, GPT’s demonstrate superior generalization capability, being adept at
executing diverse tasks such as text generation, translation, summarization, and com-
plex problem-solving, with minimal need for fine-tuning. This robustness stems from
their advanced contextual learning, which allows them to infer complex relationships
and apply computational reasoning in sophisticated linguistic tasks very effectively
[10, 35, 41]. In the present study, the proposed ClickstreamGPT model achieved an
average accuracy of 78% in predicting sequences of up to 37 future clicks per user,
surpassing the binary classification accuracy reported by Ehsani & Hosseini [ 7] and
demonstrating the superior ability of Transformer-based generative architectures to
model complex, sequential consumer behaviors in e-commerce environments.
These characteristics are corroborated by the results of [ 7]. In their study, Ehsani
& Hosseini [ 7], although achieving notable accuracy, restricted themselves to a
binary classification task (purchase intention or no purchase intention). In contrast,
the present research advanced by predicting a complete sequence of 37 clicks for
each evaluated consumer, which covers a user’s average monthly interaction journey
1 3

F . Mancini et al.
in an e-banking environment. This sequential complexity of the clickstream high -
lights the superiority of GPT models in interpreting and predicting contextual and
longitudinal patterns, corroborating their performance in natural language processing
tasks such as text classification and content generation.
In prior research [27, 28] Markov processes were employed for the prediction of
clickstream patterns. Markovian models constitute valuable tools due to their explicit
representation of states and transitions [ 33], rendering them particularly advanta -
geous for comprehending underlying processes, such as the classic task of speech
recognition [54]. Nevertheless, these approaches encounter considerable limitations:
they exhibit difficulties in managing large data volumes and are poorly suited for
intricate tasks like text classification. Notably [28], emphasizes that its model is capa-
ble of predicting only one subsequent click, whereas [ 27] achieves the prediction of
a maximum of two clicks. Both studies [27, 28] identify the challenge of Markovian
models in capturing patterns within longer event sequences as their primary draw -
back, thereby restricting their predictive capability in authentic clickstream-based
navigation contexts. ClickstreamGPT framework developed in this study was trained
on a large-scale, temporally structured dataset comprising 130,000 real user sessions
(100,000 for train and 30,000 for validation), allowing the model to learn sequential
dependencies across months of interactions.
Traditional sequential models, like Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) and their
variants (e.g., LSTMs), have historically faced vanishing gradient problems and inher-
ent difficulties in effectively capturing long-range dependencies in data sequences
[55]. In contrast, Transformer-based architectures, notably ClickstreamGPT, over -
come these shortcomings by employing a global attention mechanism. This mecha -
nism allows each element in the sequence to be processed in parallel and establish
direct connections with any other element, facilitating the modeling of extended con-
texts and mitigating gradient issues [10].
Acknowledging the limitations of RNNs in capturing long-range dependencies
[29], put forward a hybrid approach for click prediction within a Learning Manage -
ment System (LMS). This work incorporated a Bayesian Network (BN) alongside
a Transformer attention mechanism within RNN architectures, with the objective
of optimizing probability assignments in clickstream chains. Nevertheless, the out -
comes revealed a substantial decrease in accuracy for predictions exceeding 5 clicks,
underscoring the persistent challenges of this hybrid methodology in modeling more
extended sequences. ClickstreamGPT, in turn, achieved an accuracy of approxi -
mately 0.78 for the prediction of a sequence of 37 clicks.
Hechavarria & Shafiq [ 29] observed that predicting longer click sequences was
hindered by the inability of Bayesian Networks (BNs) to adequately manage the
exponential growth of the state space in a Transformer model. This implies that the
quantity of possible click combinations expanded exponentially with increasing
sequence length. Consequently, despite the BN’s application in optimizing weights
within the attention mechanism, its efficacy in capturing complex, long-term depen -
dencies remained constrained.
ClickstreamGPT, as its designation implies, is a quintessentially generative net -
work [ 10, 36]. Whereas traditional RNN models were initially conceived for dis -
criminative objectives—such as classification and regression—the execution of

generative tasks by RNNs constitutes an adaptation that frequently necessitates intri-
cate architectural modifications. Even when incorporating Transformers with global
attention mechanisms into their pipelines, such adaptations may prove insufficient,
and these sophisticated models continue to encounter difficulties in yielding satisfac-
tory outcomes for inherently generative tasks [55, 56]. Conversely, ClickstreamGPT
undergoes pre-training on extensive corpora utilizing causal self-attention, which
empowers them to model long-range dependencies and produce coherent, contextu -
ally rich outputs across diverse domains [10, 36].
Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) [ 56] can also be employed in click -
stream prediction activities. However, GANs primarily model data distributions
through adversarial training, which makes them effective for generating realistic
samples but less suited for sequential, context-dependent tasks [ 56] like clickstream
prediction. In contrast, ClickstreamGPT is a generative model specifically optimized
for autoregressive sequence modeling. They can learn long-range dependencies and
contextual patterns directly from data, enabling more coherent and semantically
meaningful sequence generation [ 10, 56], which is crucial for tasks such as click -
stream prediction where temporal order and user context matter.
5.2 ClickstreamGPT and E-commerce
Although e-banking platforms are typically classified as a subset of financial e-com-
merce, their behavioral dynamics differ significantly from conventional B2C and
B2B environments [ 57–60]. These differences — such as reduced customer churn,
higher switching costs, and longer interaction cycles — directly influence the predic-
tive behavior captured by ClickstreamGPT. Therefore, understanding these specifici-
ties is essential to evaluate both the strengths and the contextual limitations of the
proposed model.
Specifically, the e-banking platform used in this study belongs to a large Brazilian
bank. This platform is characterized by its specific product offerings, including credit
card services (contracting, limit increases, and requesting supplementary cards),
checking account services (payments and money transfers), financing (contracting,
payments, and full settlement), and insurance (contracting, payments, and cancella -
tion). In addition to these products, this study also incorporated data from essential
operational screens, such as login, password changes, and contacting support. Fur -
thermore, e-banking platforms, being e-commerce specific to financial services, typi-
cally exhibit a reduced customer churn rate compared to B2C or C2C e-commerce
models, like marketplaces. This disparity is intrinsically linked to the higher switch-
ing costs and the long-term relationship nature characteristic of banking services, in
contrast to the faster transaction dynamics and lower exit barriers found in market -
places [61].
Thus, the present study investigated an e-banking application with a specific and
well-defined scope. The vast amount of data—700 relevant screens, 130,000 users,
and 100 million clickstream records —validates ClickstreamGPT’s ability to predict
clicks in a real and complex e-banking environment. However, when considering
future applications of ClickstreamGPT in other e-commerce categories, it’s crucial
that these specific characteristics are meticulously evaluated. A relevant concern

their databases. The sheer volume of data demands a high degree of management,
and updates often require planning and significant processing power. Furthermore,
changes in data models can directly impact the performance and functioning of cor -

learn from their trained model [ 35].This means that upon receiving new data, the

Pertaining to specific considerations, seasonal aspects are paramount, particularly

e-commerce platforms is consistently observed during November (Black Friday) and
December (Christmas festivities) [63]. Our initial proposal involved an 8-month con-
text window for the prediction task, as elaborated in the Results chapter. Neverthe -
less, subsequent experimentation indicated that the clickstream prediction accuracy
for a 1-month context was commensurate with the results achieved using an 8-month
window.
Specifically, and as illustrated in Fig. 3, no significant changes were observed in
the clickstreams of the e-banking product categories evaluated in this study. There -
fore, these results indicate that purchase seasonality was not relevant for the present
work, justifying the use of a one-month context window for the dataset.
5.3 Limitations
Among the limitations of the GPT method for clickstream prediction, the ‘cold
start’ problem stands out [ 63]. While this is a classic issue in predictive systems,
and despite GPT models offering innovative approaches, their inherent architecture
can still present difficulties in generating accurate recommendations for new users or
products with insufficient interaction history. Additionally, the GPT method imposes
a high computational cost for training and inference. Its large scale demands a mas -
sive amount of hardware resources (GPUs, memory), which can limit its applicability
in scenarios with budgetary or infrastructure constraints.
Specifically, this study employed a model based on a fixed window of 37 clicks
(clickstreams) for each consumer evaluated. This number was derived from the aver-
age number of sessions and screens accessed monthly by users of the e-commerce
platform (Tables 2 and 3). However, the inherent variability in clickstream length
among different users is a well-established characteristic of digital behavior [ 61].
Despite the advances and innovations presented in this study, the generalization pro-
posed by this study may limit the prediction of the consumer journey behavior, con-
sidering that the click patterns of users on the e-commerce platform evaluated in this
study have important standard deviation.
While GPT models excel at capturing complex sequence patterns, they’re suscep-
tible to overfitting on static historical data [64, 65], which hurts their ability to predict
distinct future behaviors. Maintaining accuracy in dynamic e-commerce demands
continuous adaptation. However, because we used an 8-month training window for
ClickstreamGPT, potential overfitting wasn’t explicitly evaluated in this work.

Understanding and predicting user behavior in digital environments, particularly
on e-commerce platforms, represents a central and complex challenge in building
predictive systems [ 65–67]. Human behavior is inherently nonlinear and dynamic,
influenced by a myriad of rapidly evolving internal and external factors [68, 69]. Spe-
cifically in e-commerce, this makes the modeling of user trajectories a complex and
multifaceted problem. While ClickstreamGPT has shown important results in pre -
dicting clickstreams on e-commerce platforms, its application requires careful analy-
sis of the methodological limitations imposed by the dynamism of user behavior over
time. Even though strategies like fine-tuning [ 37] and continuous learning [ 35] can
mitigate challenges in identifying previous consumption changes, the model’s abil -
ity to adapt to rapid and unpredictable shifts in consumer behavior remains a critical
point to consider.
6 Conclusions
Generative Pre-trained Transformers (GPT) have traditionally been recognized
for their proficiency in natural language generation. This study introduces Click -
streamGPT, which extends the conventional application of GPTs. The presented
results position it as a versatile tool capable of addressing emerging challenges, such
as customer behavior prediction in e-commerce systems.
The proposed method for predicting clickstreams, based on historical customer
interactions within a mobile application, achieved a mean accuracy of 78% when
forecasting an average of 37 future clicks for each evaluated customer. These find -
ings demonstrate not only the model’s robustness but also its potential to significantly
enhance organizational understanding of customer profiles and behavior, particularly
given its relevant accuracy and expanded predicted click volume compared to previ-
ous works (which typically predicted only 1 to 5 clicks). By proactively identifying
customer needs and signs of dissatisfaction through clickstream analysis, the method
offers a promising avenue for fostering customer loyalty and engagement.
Although this study utilized ClickstreamGPT within a financial services e-com -
merce context, the platform’s architectural features facilitate its deployment in diverse
e-commerce sectors. This is because the ClickstreamGPT algorithm leveraged self-
attention algorithms and global attention mechanisms from Transformers, enabling
the learning of long-range dependencies and contextual patterns directly from data.
This capability leads to more coherent and semantically meaningful sequence gen -
eration, which is crucial for tasks such as clickstream prediction, where temporal
order and user context matter.
Additionally, a crucial feature for practical applicability in large enterprises with
e-commerce solutions is the maintenance and updating of their databases. In this con-
text, the GPT architecture offers the advantage of continual (incremental) learning.
This means that, upon receiving new data, ClickstreamGPT can dynamically update
its knowledge base, facilitating model updates. This property is vital for managing
large data volumes and changes in data models that can impact corporate applications.
ClickstreamGPT, in particular, offers significant potential for enhancing consumer
journey mapping. This includes predicting future churn (via integration with exist -

ing clickstream-based user clustering tools), optimizing new product marketing
campaigns, and anticipating subsequent consumer clicks. Such capabilities directly
improve user interface experience and content recommendation.
Future studies should focus on analyzing and implementing overfitting evaluation
strategies, potentially through larger data window proposals. Additionally, develop -
ing a variable click window prediction method, aligned with user behavior, is a criti-
cal area for future research. Extending the prediction horizon beyond one month—to
3 or 6 months—would also represent a substantial advancement for ClickstreamGPT.
