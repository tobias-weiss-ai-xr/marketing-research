1 INTRODUCTION

The accelerated advancement of digital technology over the past two decades has
led to substantial changes  in marketing management practices. Among the most
transformative innovations in this domain is Artificial Intelligence (AI). AI can be defined
as a computational system designed to perform functions that normally require human
cognitive abilities, including learning, decision-making, pattern identification, and natural
language processing. The adoption of AI in marketing management has shifted the
conventional marketing paradigm, which was largely reliant on intuition and managerial
experience, toward a more accurate and data-driven decision-making approach (Huang &
Rust, 2021; Davenport et al., 2020).
This transformation is closely linked to the growing volume of consumer data
generated across multiple digital platforms, including social media, e-commerce systems,
mobile technologies, and customer relationship management (CRM) applications. The
vast amount of data produced within the digital environment necessitates sophisticated
analytical capabilities to ensure effective processing and utilization. In thi s regard, AI
technologies particularly machine learning, deep learning, and natural language
processing allow organizations to analyze large -scale datasets and generate strategic
insights in real time (Shankar, 2018; Davenport et al., 2020). Consequently, AI functions
not merely as an operational support tool but also as a strategic resource that plays a
critical role in shaping marketing decision-making processes.
Within contemporary marketing literature, AI is widely recognized as a
fundamental enabler of  marketing personalization and automation. AI -driven
personalization empowers firms to customize communication, product suggestions,
pricing strategies, and overall customer experiences according to individual consumer

2 METHOD

This research adopts a Systematic Literature Review (SL R) methodology to
systematically identify, assess, and synthesize scholarly publications related to the
implementation of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in marketing management. The SLR
method is selected due to its structured, transparent, and replicable pr ocedures, which
help reduce subjective bias and enhance the validity and reliability of the synthesized
findings (Tranfield et al., 2003). Unlike conventional literature reviews that tend to be
narrative and loosely organized, systematic reviews rely on clearly defined protocols for
searching, screening, and analyzing academic studies (Kitchenham & Charters, 2007).
Within management and marketing scholarship, this method has gained prominence as
an effective approach for mapping the evolution of research domains, uncovering existing
gaps, and developing evidence-based directions for future inquiry (Snyder, 2019).
To enhance transparency in the literature selection procedure, this study employed
the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta -Analyses (PRISMA)
framework. PRISMA offers comprehensive and structured guidelines covering the stages
of identification, screening, eligibility assessment, and final inclusion of studies in the
review process (Page et al., 2021). The adoption of the PRISMA protocol is intended to
strengthen methodological rigor and ensure that the selection of relevant literature is
conducted systematically and thoroughly documented. Moreover, this framework
facilitates replicability by other scholars and enhances the over all credibility and
reliability of the research findings.

The literature search was performed using internationally recognized scientific
databases indexed by Scopus and Web of Science, including ScienceDirect,
SpringerLink, Emerald Insight, and Wiley Online Library. These databases were selected
due to their extensive coverage of high -quality, peer -reviewed international journals
relevant to marketing, management, information systems, and artificial intelligence
(Mongeon & Paul-Hus, 2016). The search strat egy utilized a combination of key terms,
such as “Artificial Intelligence,” “AI in Marketing,” “Marketing Management,”
“Machine Learning in Marketing,” “AI -driven Marketing,” and “Marketing Analytics.”
These keywords were systematically combined using Bool ean operators (AND, OR) to
enhance both the accuracy and comprehensiveness of the search process, in line with
established methodological standards for systematic literature reviews (Briner & Denyer,
2012).
To ensure the quality and relevance of the selected studies, this research defined
clear inclusion and exclusion criteria. Articles were included only if they were published
in peer-reviewed journals indexed by Scopus or Web of Science, released between 2015
and 2025, written in English, and specifically addressed the application of AI within the
context of marketing or marketing management. Conversely, studies were excluded if
they consisted solely of conference abstracts without full -text availability, lacked
relevance to marketing, appeared as duplicate records, or did not present a clearly defined
research methodology. The establishment of these criteria follows established
methodological guidelines for systematic reviews, ensuring the rigor, consistency, and
reliability of the literature included in the analysis (Petticrew & Roberts, 2006).
The literature selection stage follows the PRISMA flow which consists of
identification, screening, feasibility evaluation, and final inclusion. At the identification
stage, all articles obtained from the database are collected and documented. The screening
stage is carried out by reading the title and abstract to evaluate its suitability with the
research objectives. Articles that pass this stage are then analyzed in full-text assessment
at the feasibility evaluation stage to ensure that all inclusion criteria are met. Articles that
meet all of these requirements are then included in the final inclusion stage for in -depth
analysis. This process increases transparency and reduces the potential for selection bias
in research (Page et al., 2021).

3.1 Descriptive analysis of literature

Descriptive analysis of the literature is an important first step in systematic
literature review to understand the general characteristics of research related to Artificial
Intelligence (AI) in marketing management. This analysis provides an overview of
publication trends, geographical distribution, journal sources, and methodologica l
approaches used in previous studies. Based on the results of a literature selection using
the PRISMA protocol, it was found that the number of publications discussing the
application of AI in marketing management showed a significant increase in the last
decade. This increase is in line with the acceleration of digital transformation and the
adoption of data -driven technologies in modern organizations, which are driving the
integration of AI in various marketing functions (Jarek & Mazurek, 2019).
The annual distribution of publications indicates that before 2015, scholarly work
explicitly focusing on AI in marketing remained relatively scarce. However, beginning in
2017, the volume of publications increased rapidly, reflecting significant progress in
machine learning and deep learning technologies, as well as the growing availability of
big data within the digital business landscape. This trend highlights the rising interest
among both academics and practitioners in leveraging AI as a strategic tool to enha nce
marketing efficiency and effectiveness (Kumar et al., 2019). Furthermore, the expansion
of AI -enabled digital platforms and the widespread adoption of cloud computing
infrastructure have further accelerated the integration of AI into marketing practice s
worldwide.

3.2 Core applications of artificial intelligence in marketing management

Artificial Intelligence (AI) has increasingly become a central element in multiple
marketing management functions, serving not only as an analytical instrument but also as
a decision support system that enhances the effectiveness of overall marketing strategies.
Based on the synthesis of the reviewed literature, the primary applications of AI in
marketing management can be categorized into five key domains: customer insights and
segmentation, personalization and recom mendation systems, marketing automation and
campaign optimization, sales forecasting and pricing optimization, and customer service
supported by conversational AI. These domains illustrate a fundamental shift in how
organizations understand customer behavior, formulate marketing strategies, and improve
overall business performance.

3.2.1 Customer insights and market segmentation

One of the most significant applications of AI in marketing lies in its ability to
generate deeper customer insights through the analysis of large and complex datasets. AI
allows organizations to integrate multiple data sources, including transaction records,
social media activity, customer interactions, and digital behavioral data, to uncover
sophisticated patterns in consumer beh avior (Wedel & Kannan, 2016). This capability
substantially enhances the precision of market segmentation compared to conventional
approaches. Machine learning, as a key subset of AI, facilitates behavioral -based
segmentation rather than relying solely on demographic variables. Advanced clustering
techniques, such as k-means clustering, hierarchical clustering, and neural network–based
models, enable the identification of previously undetected customer segments that
traditional analytical methods may overlook (Ma & Sun, 2020). As a result, organizations
are better equipped to design more precise and effective marketing strategies.
Moreover, AI enables the generation of customer insights in real time, allowing
organizations to detect behavioral changes prompt ly and respond with appropriate

3.2.2 Personalization and recommendation systems

Personalization represents one of the most impactful applications of AI in
contemporary marketing. AI technologies enable organizations to create tailored
customer experiences by leveraging individual preferences, past behaviors, and
contextual factors (Puntoni et al ., 2021). Evidence suggests that AI -driven
personalization contributes positively to customer satisfaction, engagement levels, and
conversion performance. AI -powered recommendation systems such as collaborative
filtering techniques and deep learning models allow firms to automatically suggest
products or services that align with customer interests. These systems are extensively
implemented across e -commerce platforms, digital media services, and streaming
industries (Libai et al., 2020). By processing vast volumes of customer interaction data,
AI can generate highly accurate and contextually relevant recommendations at scale.
Furthermore, AI facilitates hyper-personalization, whereby customer experiences
are dynamically adjusted in real time according to an individual’s immediate context and
behavioral signals. For instance, AI can tailor website interfaces, email campaigns, an d
digital advertisements to match specific customer preferences (Verhoef et al., 2021). This
advanced level of personalization not only enhances the overall customer experience but
also improves marketing effectiveness. Empirical findings indicate that AI -enabled
personalization strategies significantly outperform generic marketing approaches in terms
of conversion rates (Kietzmann et al., 2018).

3.2.3 Marketing automation and campaign optimization

AI also plays an important role in marketing automation, allowing organizations
to automate a variety of marketing activities, including email marketing, social media
marketing, and digital advertising (Davenport et al ., 2020). AI enables smarter
automation compared to traditional automation because it can learn  from data and
improve performance on an ongoing basis. AI enables real -time campaign optimization
by analyzing campaign performance and automatically adjusting strategies. For example,
AI can optimize targeting, messaging, and campaign timing to improve m arketing
effectiveness (Chintagunta et al., 2021).
Additionally, AI enables predictive marketing, where organizations can predict
the outcome of a campaign before it is launched and optimize marketing strategies based
on those predictions (Ma & Sun, 2020).  This reduces the risk of campaign failure and
improves marketing efficiency. Programmatic advertising is also an important example
of the application of AI in marketing automation. AI enables automatic ad purchases
based on customer data and conversion pr obability (Jarek & Mazurek, 2019). This
increases the efficiency and effectiveness of digital advertising.

3.2.4 Sales forecasting and pricing optimization

AI has also become an essential tool in sales forecasting and pricing optimization.
Machine learning techniques enable organizations to predict product demand with a high
level of precision by analyzing historical sales data alongside external variables such as
economic indicators and market dynamics (Wedel & Kannan, 2016). AI -driven
forecasting suppor ts more effective production planning, inventory control, and the
formulation of marketing strategies. With more accurate demand predictions,
organizations can minimize operational inefficiencies, reduce costs, and enhance overall
performance (Ma & Sun, 2020).
In addition, AI facilitates the implementation of dynamic pricing strategies,
allowing firms to adjust prices in real time based on fluctuations in demand, competitive
conditions, and customer behavior (Kietzmann et al ., 2018). This capability enables
organizations to optimize revenue generation and improve profitability. Furthermore, AI

3.2.5 Customer service and conversational AI

Customer service is one of the key functional areas that has experienced
substantial transformation due to the adoption of AI technologies. AI -powered chatbots

3.3 The role of Artificial Intelligence in strategic marketing decision-making

Artificial Intelligence (AI) has progressed beyond its traditional role as an
operational support tool and has become a strategic asset in marketing decision -making.
In contemporary marketing management, AI enables organizations to shift from decisions
primarily guided by intuition and managerial experience toward a more objective,
predictive, and prescriptive approach grounded in data analytics. This transition enhances
decision accuracy, reduces uncertainty, and strengthens overall marketing effectiveness.
Based on the synthesis of the reviewed literature, the strategic contribution of AI to
marketing decision -making can be categorized into four key dimensions: predictive
decision-making, prescriptive decision-making, real-time decision-making, and strategic
marketing optimization.

3.4 Challenges, ethical issues, and governance of Artificial Intelligence in marketing
management

While Artificial Intelligence (AI) offers transformational potential in marketing
management, its implementation also poses complex strategic, ethical, and governance
challenges. In the context of the publication of high-reputable international journals, the
discussion of this aspect is crucial because it shows the depth of critical analysis,

Based on the synthesis of the literature, the main challenges and issues can be

capability gap, and (5) regulatory and ethical governance framework.

3.4.1 Data governance and privacy risk

The application of AI in marketing is highly dependent on the availability and
analysis of large-scale data, including customer behavioral patterns, preferences, location
information, and psychographic profiles. This heavy reliance on data increases the
potential risks associated with privacy violations and the misuse of personal information
(Martin & Murphy, 2017). Practices such as personalization and predictive analytics often
require the collection and processing of sensitive customer data, making issues such as
informed consent, data ownership, and transparency increasingly critical. As customers
become more aware of how their personal d ata is collected and utilized, unclear or
unethical data management practices can erode trust and negatively affect brand
relationships (Bleier & Eisenbeiss, 2015).
Regulatory frameworks such as the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)
in the European Union highlight the necessity for AI-driven data management systems to
adhere to principles of legality, fairness, and transparency. Consequently, organizations
must establish robust data governance frameworks to ensure regulatory compliance while
safeguarding their reputation and maintaining customer trust. Despite these
advancements, an important research gap remains regarding how organizations can
effectively balance the benefits of hyper-personalization with the need to protect customer
privacy, without compromising the overall customer experience.

3.4.2 Algorithmic bias and fairness

One of the critical challenges in AI implementation is algorithmic bias. AI learns
from historical data; if the data contains bias, then AI decisions are also potentially biased

3.4.3 Transparency and explainability

One of the main criticisms of AI is its often perceived "black box" nature. Many
algorithms, especially deep learning, are difficult to explain intuitively (Rai, 2020). In the
context of strategic marketing, managers need to understand the ration ale behind AI
recommendations to be able to make decisions responsibly. Lack of explainability can
hinder AI adoption at a strategic level.
Explainable AI (XAI) is an important approach to improve the transparency of AI
systems. XAI allows organizations to explain how decisions are made, thereby increasing
internal (manager) and external (consumer) trust. The literature shows that algorithm
transparency can increase customer trust in AI systems, but it can also elicit negative
reactions if customers feel to o "watched" (Dietvorst, Simmons, & Massey, 2015).
Therefore, a balance is needed between transparency and customer comfort perception.

AI-based transformation is not only technological but also organizati onal. Many
organizations face capability gaps in AI implementation due to a lack of data science
competencies, technology infrastructure, and organizational cultures that support

IT, marketing, and strategic management. Without effective collaboration, AI

3.4.5 Regulatory and ethical governance framework

The adoption of AI in marketing necessitates a well -defined governance
framework to ensure its ethical, transparent, and responsible use. Such a framework
should encompass several key components, including robust data govern ance

These elements are essential to ensure that AI systems operate in accordance with
organizational standards, regulatory requirements, and ethical principles.

practices.

3.5 Future research directions and theoretical contributions

The advancement of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in marketing management has
created substantial opportunities for future research, particularly in examining how AI
can be effectively integrated into strategic decision -making and value creation for both
customers and organizations. Although prior studies have demonstrated that AI enhances
operational efficiency, enables advanced person alization, and improves predictive
accuracy, important conceptual gaps remain regarding its impact on the roles of marketing
managers, organizational structures, and the broader dynamics of strategic decision -
making. Future research should focus on develop ing comprehensive theoretical
frameworks that explain the interaction between AI capabilities and human expertise in
strategic marketing contexts. In particular, greater attention is needed to understand how
the integration of human judgment and machine in telligence can lead to more informed,
adaptive, and optimal marketing decisions (Shrestha, Ben-Menahem, & von Krogh, 2019;
Davenport et al., 2020).
Another important avenue for future research involves the advancement of
explainable AI (XAI) within the mar keting domain, with the goal of enhancing the
transparency and accountability of AI-driven systems. Limited transparency in AI models
can constrain their adoption at the strategic level, as marketing managers require a clear
understanding of how algorithmic decisions are generated in order to trust and effectively
utilize these systems (Arun Rai, 2020). Consequently, future studies should investigate
how improved explainability can strengthen both organizational confidence and customer
trust in AI technologies.
