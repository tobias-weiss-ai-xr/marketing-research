Abstract
Artificial intelligence-powered virtual influencers, as digital characters
emulating human characteristics, are transforming the way brands interact with
consumers and are rapidly becoming a growing field of research in marketing
literature. However, despite this burgeoning interest, bibliometric studies that
holistically evaluate the accumulated knowledge in the field remain limited. This
study aims to examine 258 publications indexed in the Web of Science (WoS) Core
Collection within the disciplines of Business and Management, comprising peer -
reviewed English articles, through a comprehensive bibliometric analysis. Data
were evaluated using performance analysis and science mapping techniques via the
bibliometrix package and Biblioshiny interface in the R programming language.
The findings demonstrate that research on virtual inf luencers expanded rapidly
during the 2021 –2026 period, particularly from 2024 –2025 onwards, with an
annual growth rate of 111.68%. Furthermore, journals such as the Journal of
Retailing and Consumer Services and the Journal of Business Research, as well as
China-based authors and institutions, stand out in the field. Keyword and co -
occurrence network analyses reveal that the literature has matured around the
concepts of credibility, authenticity, and trustworthiness.

INTRODUCTION
The advancement of digital technologies and the widespread adoption of artificial
intelligence (AI) across numerous fields today have transformed the way brands interact with
consumers. Particularly, the generation of engaging textual and visual content through AI enables
deeper consumer engagement in sectors such as entertainment and retailing (Rungruangjit et al.,
2024; Yu et al., 2024). According to data from KBV Research (2024), the global virtual influencer
market is projected to reach a size of 37.8 billion dollars by 2030, gr owing at an annual rate of
approximately 38%. (KBV Research, 2024).
Social media influencers, who can range from ordinary users to high -profile celebrities,
play a vital role in digital marketing due to their capacity to shape individuals' opinions and
influence consumer behavior (Ouvrein et al., 2021). These advantages offered by the influencer
ecosystem have gained a new di mension with advancements in artificial intelligence, leading to
the emergence of next-generation virtual influencers (VIs). There is a growing interest  in these
computer-generated virtual influencers that emulate human characteristics, personalities, and
behaviors (Arsenyan & Mirowska, 2021; E. Kim et al., 2025). Virtual influencers are increasingly
preferred by brands over h uman influencers due to their versatility, full controllability, and
resilience to scandals (Yin et al., 2025). Furthermore, these virtual characters provide a distinct
advantage as they are not subject to physical and physiological limitations such as fatigue, aging,
or illness  (Appel et al., 2020) . Brands can fully customize the appearance, messaging, and
narrative of these characters in line with their goals; thus, they can largely eliminate the risks of
scandal or reputational damage that traditional celebrities might cau se, while also achieving
significant cost advantages (M. Kim & Baek, 2024; Tan, 2021; Yang et al., 2023).
The economic potential and sectoral impact generated by virtual influencers have also
become a compelling subject of interest among scholars (Audrezet et al., 2025). Despite the rapid
growth of the field in recent years, the number of studies that holistically evaluate the accumulated
knowledge and map out the intellectual structure of the literature remains quite limited. Notably,
there is a conspicuous lack of comprehensive bibliometric studies systematically mapping the
developmental trajectory of virtual inf luencer research, influential authors, institutions,
collaborations, and emerging research trends. This deficiency hampers a clear understanding of
the field's current state and its future research directions. Consequently, there is an evident need
for bibliometric analyses that can elucidate the structural characteristics of the virtual influencer
literature.
Driven by this gap, the primary objective of this study is to conduct a comprehensive
bibliometric mapping of research focused on "virtual/AI influencers" within the Web of Science
(WoS) database. The study aims to identify the general publication and citation trends, as well as
the most productive and influential authors, institutions, and journals in this emerging field.
Concurrently, through co-authorship, co-citation, and keyword co-occurrence network analyses,
it seeks to reveal the core research themes (clusters) and evolving trends over time within the
literature. Ultimately, by providing a structured framework of the current state of virtual
influencer literature, this research aims to delineate a strategic roadmap that will guide future
inquiries for both scholars and marketing professionals.

1. METHODOLOGY
1.1. Data Collection and Search Strategy
In this study, data for the bibliometric analysi s were retrieved from the Web of Science
(WoS) Core Collection database. The search query was executed on May 30, 2026, utilizing the
following keyword combination in the topic search field:
("AI influencer*" OR "artificial intelligence influencer*" OR "v irtual influencer*" OR
"digital influencer*" OR "computer-generated influencer*")
Three refinement criteria were applied to the initial search results: (1) the subject area
was restricted to "Business" or "Management," (2) only English -language publication s were
included, and (3) the document type was limited exclusively to peer -reviewed "Articles."
Following the application of these constraints, a final sample of 258 articles was obtained for
analysis. The initial query returned 795 records. After restricting the subject area to Business or
Management, 399 records remained; after limiting the language to English, 389 records remained;
and after restricting the document type to peer-reviewed Articles, the final sample of 348 articles
was obtained.  The term "digital influencer*" was deliberately included alongside "virtual
influencer*" and "AI influencer*" because it is also used in the marketing literature to refer to
computer-generated, AI-driven personas, and excluding it risked omit ting relevant studies that
use this terminology interchangeably with "virtual influencer." Because this term can, in
principle, also retrieve studies discussing human digital-native influencers, the title and abstract
of each retrieved record were manually  screened, and records that did not concern computer -
generated or AI -based influencers were excluded prior to arriving at the final sample of 258
articles.
The retrieved dataset was analyzed using the bibliometrix package and the Biblioshiny
interface with in the R programming language. Within the scope of the analysis, multiple
bibliometric techniques were employed, including performance analysis , collaboration network
analysis, keyword co -occurrence analysis, and trend topic analysis. All network analyses (co-
authorship and keyword co-occurrence) were conducted using Biblioshiny's default and selected
algorithmic settings. The keyword co-occurrence network was constructed based on the "Author
Keywords" (DE) field. The association strength me thod was used to  normalize the raw co -
occurrence frequencies, thereby correcting for the general frequency of each term/actor before
assessing relatedness. Community/cluster detection was performed using the Louvain algorithm.
For visualization purposes, the top 50 nodes ranked by occurrence/citation frequency were
retained in each network (co -authorship and keyword co -occurrence), of which the ten most
central nodes are reported in the corresponding tables. The same normalization, clustering, and
node-threshold settings were applied to the co-authorship network.
In accordance with the widely adopted classification in the bibliometric research
tradition, the techniques utilized are addressed along two complementary axes: performance
analysis and science mapping. Performance analysis aims to quantitatively demonstra te
productivity and citation indicators at the author, institution, country, journal, and document
levels, thereby answering the questions of "who is producing, where, and to what extent" within
the field. Science mapping, on the other hand, aims to visual ize the intellectual and conceptual

This study aimed to present a comprehensive bibliometric mapping of the virtual
influencer (virtual/AI influencer) literature based on a dataset of 258 articles indexed in the
Business and Management categories of the Web of Science Core Collection database. By jointly
employing performance analysis and science mapping techniques, the study delineates both the
quantitative landscape and the conceptual structure of the field, thereby consolidating scattered
findings in the literature into a holistic framewor k. The empirical findings demonstrate that
research on virtual influencers has become one of the fastest -expanding niche areas within
marketing literature during the 2021 –2026 period, particularly from 2024 –2025 onwards,
exhibiting an annual growth rate of 111.68%. The fact that this acceleration occurred concurrently
with the public release of large language models and generative artificial intelligence tools
concretely illustrates the profound extent to which technological transformations can reshape the
academic research agenda.
The performance analysis findings reveal that the literature is heavily concentrated
around specific actors. While journals such as the Journal of Retailing and Consumer Services
(40 articles) and the Journal of Business Research (21 articles) stand out in terms of publication
volume, studies by Thomas and Fowler (2021) with 350 total citations and Lou (2023) with 318
total citations constitute the foundational references of the field regarding citation impact. In terms

The findings regarding the conceptual structure indicate that rather than being shaped by
a unique, i ndependent theoretical foundation, the virtual influencer literature has developed by
adapting traditional advertising and source credibility frameworks to a new context. Keyword and
co-word network analyses reveal that beneath the primary core of ‘virtual  influencer’, concepts
such as ‘credibility,’ ‘authenticity,’ and ‘trustworthiness’ form a robust second layer. This finding
demonstrates that consumers’ tendency to attribute human characteristics to AI -based entities,
along with debates regarding the cre dibility and trustworthiness of these entities, lies at the
theoretical core of the field. In the trend topic analysis, the terms ‘virtual influencer’ and ‘virtual
influencers’ carry a median year of 2025, while emerging concepts such as ‘intention,’ ‘agents,’
and ‘intelligence’ reach a median of 2026, confirming that the present study focuses precisely on
the most contemporary and dynamic axis of discussion in the field.
The findings offer both theoretical and practical implications. From a theoretical
perspective, the maturation of virtual influencer research around concepts such as
anthropomorphism, authenticity, and source credibility highlights the need for a deeper
integration of the field with human-AI interaction and consumer psychology literatures. From a
practical standpoint, it can be argued that while leveraging the advantages of virtual influencers—
such as cost efficiency, absolute controllability, and resilience to scandals—brands must prioritize
communication strategies that strengthen the perc eptions of authenticity and trustworthiness
among consumers. For marketing managers, integrating the theoretical frameworks highlighted
by the studies with the highest citation impact (e.g., source credibility, anthropomorphism) into
campaign designs provi des a strategic roadmap that can enhance the effectiveness of virtual
influencer applications.
As with other bibliometric studies, this research also carries certain limitations. First, the
dataset was retrieved from a single database (WoS Core Collection), thereby excluding alternative
databases such as Scopus, Google Scholar, or Dimensions from the analysis. Restricting the
subject area to "Business and Management" during the search process and including only English-
language peer-reviewed articles may have led to the exclusion of relevant studies produced in
other disciplines, such as psychology, communication, and computer sciences. These restrictions
indicate that the thematic and conceptual structure identified in the study primarily reflect s a
business- and marketing-oriented perspective on virtual influencers; consequently, it may not
fully capture findings from other disciplines that approach this phenomenon from different
theoretical angles. Similarly, the exclusion of non -English-language publications from the
analysis may have led to an underrepresentation of research conducted in regions with substantial
virtual influencer activity but limited English -language publication output (e.g., China, South
Korea, Japan). Additionally, no keyword harmonization or thesaurus -based merging procedure

In light of these limitations, several recommendations can be put forward for future
research. First, replicating the findings obtained in this study using alternative databases like
Scopus and through an interdisciplinary scope (e.g., psychology, communication, information

theoretical foundation of the field. Third, systematic review and meta -analysis studies to be

support for the bibliometric findings. Finally, a comparative analysis of virtual influencer usage
across different sectors (e.g., retailing, tourism, public health) will enrich both the academic and
practical contributions of the field.
