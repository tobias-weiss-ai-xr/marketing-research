Abstract: Scientific design of patent licensing contract and sales channel selection are key to breaking the green technology
promotion dilemma of biodegradable materials. Under market scale information asymmetry, we construct a three-echelon supply
chain principal agent model including a patent technology supplier, a biodegradable material manufacturer and a terminal sales
company. By comparing game equilibrium solutions when the termi nal sales company chooses single channel or dual channel
sales, we explore the optimal patent licensing contract design and channel selection strategy. We further compare the results with
those under symmetric information, explore information value and discuss information disclosure strategy. The study shows that:
Both the supplier and the company make strategic decisions on c ontract design and channel selection under information
asymmetry. Reasonable matching of patent licensing contract and  channel selection can effectively motivate the manufacturer
to increase green technology investment and accelerate green technology diffusion in the supply chain. Information asymmetry
deviates the supply chain from the optimal state. Internal information sharing negotiations can alleviate its negative impacts only
when disclosure costs and benefits meet specific conditions. Ex ternal government subsidies can significantly alleviate the
negative impact and boost the manufacturer’s green technology i nvestment. Market scale and consumer green preference
coefficient have a synergistic effect on improving supply chain  profit, consumer surplus and social welfare, and this effect
becomes more significant when government subsidies are introduced.
Keywords: Biodegradable Materials; Green Technology Promotion; Patent Lic ensing Contract Design; Channel Selection;
Information Asymmetry.

1. Introduction
Against the background of advancing global green and low
carbon strategy, the research and development (R&D) and
application of biodegradable materials have become the core
path to address plastic pollution and achieve a circular
economy (Haq et al., 2025). At the policy level, many
countries and international organizations have strengthened
overall strategic planning. The European Union advances
R&D and industrialization of biodegradable materials
through policy planning and financial support, and China
emphasizes the synergetic promotion of carbon reduction,
pollution reduction, green development and economic growth.
Both provide solid policy support for the technological
innovation and large-scale application of biodegradable
materials. In practice, the core supply chain enterprise, such
as biodegradable material manufacturer, adopts green
technologies, includ ing the efficient strain directional
breeding, microbial fermentation synthesis and blend
modification. These technologies significantly improve
product degradability, promote the application and
transformation of environmental protection technologies,
effectively reduce the negative environmental externality of
plastic pollution, and support the control of “white pollution”
(Calero et al., 2021; Akram et al., 2024).
However, promoting green technology in the
biodegradable material suppl y chain still faces multiple
constraints. On the one hand, the design of the patent
licensing contract is inadequate. The incentive and restraint
mechanism is unbalanced. This leads to low efficiency in
green technology transfer. Cases such as Biotec’s
“thermoplastic processable starch (TPS)” technology
infringement (https://case-law.vlex.com/vid/biotec-
biologiche-naturverpackungen-v-892790603) and Occlutech
GmbH’s resorbable cardiac occl uder patent dispute (https://
www.toutiao.com/article/7566079380427751976/?upstream
_biz=doubao&source=m_redirect.) expose structural defects
in the profit distribution of current patent licensing contract .
These defects restrict the vertical transfer of green technology

Biodegradable materials have specific characteristics, such as
sensitivity to degradation conditions and diverse certification
requirements. These characteristics set higher demands for
channel coordination. However, the current market follows a
dual track pattern: online e-commerce traffic is scattered, and
offline channels rely on traditional distributors. This pattern
makes channel coordination highly difficult, so the value of
green technology is hard to pass on to end consumers. Take
Haizheng Biotech’s PLA lunch boxes as an example. The
product can degrade in marine environments through a high
temperature extrusion process. But the traditional distributors
focus on the “low cost” of traditional plastic lunch boxes.
They cannot effectively pass on technical advantages like full
biodegradability and industrial composting certification. As a
result, the product fails to reach core customers such as chain
restaurants and food delivery platforms, leading to the
undervaluation of its technical value.

making complexity. The biodegradable material industry has

Based on this, our study aims to answer the following
questions:
(1) Under different market s cales, how should the patent
technology supplier design the licensing contract to
encourage effective green technology adoption? How should
the terminal sales company choose the channel to drive green
technology market transformation?
(2) What is the linkage betw een patent licensing contract

can the green technology investment level for biodegradable
materials be maximized?
(3) How does market scale information asymmetry affect
the profit of the supply chain participant and the overall
supply chain profitability for biodegradable materials? How
to ease profit distribution imbalance caused by information
asymmetry and improve supply chain operation efficiency?
(4) How do different market scales and consumer green
preference affect supply chain system performance?
To address the above questions, we draw on the
biodegradable material industry’s characteristics. Under
market scale information asymmetry, we build a three-
echelon supply chain decision  model. The model includes a
patent technology supplier, a biodegradable material
manufacturer and a terminal sales company. We analyze
patent licensing contract design and channel selection
strategy in green technology promotion. We also analyze each
entity’s optimal strategy selection under asymmetric
information. Furthermore, we explore information value and
information disclosure strategy.
This study makes four key contributions. First, this study
breaks the traditional “exogenously given contracts”
assumption in patent licensing research. It treats the patent
licensing contract as an endogenous decision variable and
constructs a more realistic principal-agent model. It explores
the green technology licensing contract design logic of the
patent technology supplier under market scale information
asymmetry, and reveals the core  role of contract parameter
design in identifying actual market scale and reducing
technology promotion friction. Second, this study
innovatively incorporates c onsumer channels and green
preferences to investigate th e optimal channel selection
strategy, and analyzes the reverse driving effect of green
technology investment by the biodegradable materials
manufacturer on the operational decisions of the terminal
sales company. It provides a theoretical reference for
improving channel efficiency and facilitating green
technology popularization. Third, this research integrates
upstream patent licensing contr act design and downstream
sales channel selection to construct a unified decision-making
framework. It clarifies the promotion mechanism of contract
design and channel selection on green technology investment,
and explores the joint influence path of the two strategies on
technology investment, which provides a theoretical basis for
solving practical industrial problems regarding the mode and
scale of enterprise green investment. Finally, targeting the
profit distribution imbalance caused by severe information
barriers in the biodegradable materials industry, this study
introduces government subsidy as a moderating variable. It
further explores how government subsidy facilitates green
technology investment and mitigates the profit distribution
imbalance within the supply chain, providing a scientific
decision-making basis for rea lizing transparent industrial
operation and promoting the high-quality development of the
environmental protection materials industry.

2. Literature Review
This study relates to three aspects: green technology
investment decisions for biodegradable products, green
technology patent contract design, and channel selection in
the biodegradable materials supply chain.
2.1. Green Technology Investment Decisions
for Biodegradable Products
As consumers’ green preferences grow and their demands
for sustainable development rise, green technology has
become a core driver of competitiveness in the biodegradable
materials supply chain (Zhang et al., 2019; Zhu et al., 2023).
Existing studies focus on the implementation benefits and
influencing factors of green technology investment. Currently,
the worsening plastic waste pollution is one of the major
global environmental threat s. Biodegradable plastics
produced with green technology can effectively reduce
environmental pollution and energy consumption (Gioia et al.,
2021; Li et al., 2024). Guo et al. (2023) found that the green
technology of biodegradable film mulching can increase crop
yields and farmers’ incomes in agricultural production. Li and
He (2024) confirmed that consumers’ green preferences have
a positive impact on the profit of green building material
manufacturer and retailer. Xia and Chen (2025) found that
more green investment by manufacturer makes it easier to
achieve Pareto improvement in the supply chain. However,
green technology investment in biobased, biodegradable and
compostable plastics can red uce carbon footprints and
alleviate environmental pollutio n, but it still faces multiple
constraints including price competition, unstable raw material
supply, immature technology, and lack of relevant legal
protection (Moshood et al., 2021; Nizamuddin and Chen,
2024). Some scholars have analyzed the factors that influence
green technology investment. Sui and Gao. (2023) pointed out
that a company’s resource endowment positively affects its
green investment behavior, and technical cognition can
strengthen this effect. Jijue et al. (2024) revealed that market

participation can influence green investment behavior
through economic income, market information and green
cognition. Wang et al. (2024) confirmed that industrial
collaboration models effectively promote green technology
diffusion. Most existing studies assume that the manufacturer
directly owns the right to use green technologies, but they
rarely focus on how the manufacturer obtains legal
authorization for green technologies through patent licensing.
They also fail to explore how patent licensing can increase the
value of green technology investment. Only by clarifying the
mechanism of the patent li censing contract in green
technology investment can we solve the problems of
insufficient green technology investment and low efficiency
value conversion, and drive th e efficient and large scale
diffusion of green technology.
2.2. Green Technology Patent Contract Design
As green technology patent infringement disputes
frequently occur in the biodegradable materials field, the
patent licensing contract have gradually become a focus of
academic research. It is a core factor to ensure the compliant
promotion and accelerate the diff usion of green technology.
(Jiang et al., 2024; Block et al. 2025). Kulatilaka et al. (2006)
confirmed that dynamic royalty contracts have more
significant efficiency advantages than fixed fee contracts in
green technology transfer when no capital constraints exist.
Ye and He (2018) focused on the pollution control field and
found that fixed fee licensing is more helpful for the
promotion of pollution control technologies. Sarmah et al.
(2020) emphasized that fixed fee models may reduce the
enthusiasm of foreign enterprises for R&D investment. Wu
(2019) found that compared with static patent fees, dynamic
patent fee mechanisms can effectively ease the intensity of
price competition in the green materials market and help
technology owning enterprises maintain higher sales profit
margins. Jin et al. (2024) confirmed that asymmetric
information on market size drives supplier to strategically
select terms of license contracts. Lin and Zhang (2024) found
t h a t  t h e  t w o  p a r t  t a r i f f  c o n t racts promote the licensing of
carbon emission reduction technologies. Pal and Guin (2025)
found that supply chain participants can achieve optimal
profits only if they choose option contract technologies and
set fixed option prices and exercise prices. Zheng et al. (2025)
further revealed that under asymmetric cost information, the
wholesale price and one time transfer payment in the optimal
contract. These contracts come from supplier of
biodegradable film technology. Both factors are closely
related to the supplier’s cost type. Despite important progress
in green technology patent licen se  contract  desi gn, e xisting
studies still have some limitations. First, most existing studies
base on the assumption of symmetric information. This
assumption differs greatly from the real situation of the
biodegradable materials industry, which has high technical
barriers and complex certification systems. Second, most
existing studies focus on traditional manufacturing scenarios.
Specialized research on the emer ging field of biodegradable
materials is relatively scarce. Although Zheng et al.’s (2025)
study involved technology promotion, it only considered
traditional offline sales channels. It failed to fully reflect the
demand for multi-channel integration innovation models of
biodegradable materials enterp rises in practice. Therefore,
based on existing studies, we will focus on breaking the
symmetric information assumption. It will combine the
characteristics of the biodegradable materials industry. It will
also explore the coordinated  optimization mechanism of
patent license contracts and channel selection under
asymmetric information environments.
2.3. Channel Selection in the Biodegradable
Materials Supply Chain
Channel selection is a key link in the value conversion of
green technologies for biodegradable materials. Sound
channel selection not only drives enterprises to actively
develop green technologies, but also accelerates their efficient
promotion (Yang et al., 2019; Pal et al., 2023). Existing
literature mainly starts from the supply side to study the
channel selection strategies of manufacturer and retailer. Li et
al. (2016) confirmed that manufacturer will choose dual
channel sales for green products only when consumers’
loyalty to retail channels and greening costs meet specific
conditions. Wu et al. (2023) proved that in optimistic markets,
manufacturer can gain higher profit through the resale model
if online channel promotion prices are higher than those of
traditional channels. In pessimistic markets, however, the
reverse pricing order can bring higher returns to manufacturer.
Lin et al. (2023) found that green product manufacturer
prefers direct sales channels when significant differences
exist in the cost of marketing efforts, as this selection prevents
traditional retailers from switching to non-green products due
to the high effort costs of promoting green products. Wang et
al. (2024) showed that green technology investment helps
both platform and manufacturer achieve Pareto-level profit
and drives platforms to choose the resale model. Tao et al.
(2024) further pointed out that higher consumer green
awareness positively affects green technology levels and the
profit of all supply chain parties, and as this awareness grows,
manufacturer shifts from a single resale channel to dual
channel sales of green products. Wang et al. (2024) found that
manufacturer will introduce online channels for dual channel
sales of green products when retailers share demand
information. Gao et al. (2025) noted that as consumers’
preference for the green economy increases, consumers prefer
to buy green products through platform channels. Zhang et al.
(2025) found that manufacturer prefers a dual channel model
with both online direct sales and online resale when the
government provides R&D cost subsidies. However,
manufacturer will choose dual channel sales of green products
only if specific conditions are met when the government
provides per-unit output subsidies. Existing studies generally
ignore the key role of heterogeneous consumer preferences.
In fact, the complex certification and delayed benefits of
biodegradable materials signif icantly increase consumers’
demand for channel professionalism and information
credibility. For example, environmentally conscious
consumers often prefer professi onal retail channels or brand
owned direct channels that can provide professional
certification explanations  and traceability information.
Therefore, it is urgent to explore the optimal channel selection
strategy from the dual perspectives of consumer channel
preferences and green preferences, combined with the product
characteristics of biodegradable  materials. This strategy can
effectively promote the effi cient transmission of green
technology value to end consumers and support the healthy
development of the biodegradable materials market.

with a fixed fee under high market scale, and the two part
tariff contract with “royalty + fixed fee” under low market
scale. The terminal sales company will flexibly choose sales
channel based on consumers’ offline channel preference,
green preference, market scale, etc. (2) The reasonable
matching between patent licensing contract design and
channel selection strategy can encourage the biodegradable

materials manufacturer to significantly increase its
investment in green technology, accelerate the diffusion and
application of green technology in the biodegradable
materials supply chain. (3) Asymmetric market scale
information can cause the bi odegradable materials supply
chain to deviate from the optimal state. Although information
sharing negotiations can reduce the negative impact of
information asymmetry, supply chain members themselves
cannot always reach such negotia tions. Internal information
sharing requires that members’ bargaining power to meet
certain thresholds. However, ex ternal government subsidies
can significantly reduce the negative impact of information
asymmetry by encouraging the biodegradable materials
manufacturer to invest more in green technology, increasing
the profit of all supply chain members, and improving
consumer surplus and social welfare. (4) In terms of
increasing the profit of the biodegradable materials supply
chain, improving consumer surplus and enhancing social
welfare, market scale and the consumer green preference
coefficient have a synergistic promoting effect, and this effect
becomes more significant when government subsidies are
introduced.
7.2. Managerial Implications
The managerial implications of this study are as follows:
(1) The patent technology supplier should adopt a pure
fixed-fee contract under a hig h market scale to reduce risks
and guarantee revenues. Under a low market scale, it should
adopt a two-part tariff contract to ease capital pressure and
share risks. The terminal sa les company shall accurately
capture information on mar ket scale and consumer
preferences, optimize channel selection, and promote the
value transformation of green technology.(2) In view of
supply-chain performance deviation caused by information
asymmetry, government subsidy can be applied to relieve
enterprises’ cost burden, lower information barriers and
improve supply-chain efficiency.(3) All supply-chain
participants should attach importance to the synergy between
market scale and consumer green preference, actively share
market information, optimize individual decisions, and
achieve a win-win situation for both economic and
environmental benefits.
7.3. Limitations and Future Research
This study investigates technology licensing from a patent
supplier to a single biodegradable materials manufacturer. In
practice, however, patent suppliers commonly adopt ordinary
licensing contracts for multiple manufacturers. Accordingly,
future research can extend the model to a multi-manufacturer
supply chain and explore optimal patent licensing contract
design in such complex market scenarios. Although multi-
agent settings increase contractual design complexity, they
also improve model practicality. Furthermore, this study only
considers market scale inform ation asymmetry, where the
patent supplier is the information-disadvantaged party, and
examines corresponding information disclosure strategies. In
practice, rapid R&D technological updates may enable patent
suppliers to become information-advantaged by concealing
information regarding future technological upgrades and
competing technologies. Future  studies can establish game
models incorporating product and substitute competition to
further explore licensing contr act design, channel selection,
and information disclosure strategies.
