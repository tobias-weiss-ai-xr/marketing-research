# A Study on Community Anti-Fraud Practice for the Elderly from the Perspective of Technology-Service Coupling-A Case Study of the “Private-Domain Eagle Eye” APP

A Study on Community Anti-Fraud Practice for the
Elderly from the Perspective of Technology-Service
Coupling-A Case Study of the “Private-Domain Eagle
Eye” APP
Mingna Xia, Jia Yongfang*
School of Humanities, Inner Mongolia University of Technology, Hohhot, 010080, China
*Corresponding author: jiayf2020@imut.edu.cn
Abstract: Against the backdrop of rapid development of the digital society, the number of elderly
netizens keeps growing. Private -domain social scenarios such as WeChat groups and short -video
private messages have gradually become high -risk spaces for fraud targeting the elderly. Fraud tactics
including false marketing of health -care products, pension-related financial scams, and impersonation
of relatives or community staff keep evolving. Embedded in acquaintance -based social relations, such
frauds are highly concealed and difficult to identify and handle. Nevertheless, current anti -fraud
governance for the elderly is confronted with the practical dilemma of separation between technical
tools and humanistic services. Intelligent anti -fraud systems excel at identifying and warning of risk
information yet lack follow -up intervention procedures implemented at the grassroots community level.
Traditional community-based anti-fraud services rely on offline lectures and home visits. Restricted by
manpower supply, they can hardly realize real -time capture and hierarchical disposal of risk clues.
Technology-service coupling combines the risk -identification advantages of artificial intelligence
technology with the human -intervention strengths of social work for the elderly, so as to build a
collaborative governance model featured by technology empowerment and service guarantee. Taking
the research-practice project “Private -Domain Eagle Eye” anti -fraud mini -program as the research
carrier, this paper adopts field theory and conducts pilot practice in Community W of City H.
Quantitative research methods are applied to analyze practical characteristics of fraud against the
elderly in private -domain scenarios, interpret the operation logic of dual -track technology -service
coupling, sort out practical dilemmas encountered in implementation, and explore feasible anti -fraud
paths for the elderly suitable for urban communities in China. It provides practical references for the
protection of the elderly’ s property rights and interests under the background of smart -community
construction.
Keywords: technology-service coupling; anti -fraud for the elderly; private -domain scenarios;
community governance
Introduction
With mobile internet deeply integrated into daily life, more elderly people gain access to the digital
world, and social software has become an important carrier for them to acquire information and engage
in social interactions. According to Bourdieu’s field theory, a field refers to a network space composed
of objective relations among different positions. Private -domain social interaction constitutes a special
action field, where internal rules, capital and actors interact with one another and create breeding
ground for pension -targeted fraud. Within private -domain fields, fraudsters build trust through
emotional capital and conduct deceptive inducement by taking advantage of information asymmetry.
Different from public online platforms, WeChat group chats and private messages are highly closed-off,
which makes external supervision difficult and brings new challenges to grassroots anti -fraud
governance.
At present, two major governance paths have taken shape for anti -fraud work among the elderly in
China. The first is the technology -oriented governance path, which relies on artificial intelligence and
big data to realize risk identification and call -warning services. The second is the
grassroots-service-oriented path, where community grid workers and social workers deliver anti -fraud
lectures and conduct home -based reminders. These two paths belong to different action fields. The

technical field pursues identification efficiency and focuses on algorithmic judgment, while the
community-service field centers on human needs and attaches importance to case -based assistance and
social-support-network construction. Lacking institutional connection channels, they have given rise to
the governance contradiction of  “unimplementable early -warnings and clue -deficient services”.
Technology-service coupling promotes factor flow between the two fields, breaks barriers between
technical systems and community services, and realizes process embedding of algorithm identification,
risk warning, professional intervention and long-term education.
This paper takes the “Private -Domain Eagle Eye” anti -fraud mini-program as the practical carrier
and carries out pilot research in Community W of City H. Boasting a high proportion of elderly
residents, this community frequently encounters fraud -risk clues arising from private -domain
marketing and serves as a typical field -research sample. Therefore, this investigation adopts
questionnaire-based quantitative research, semi -structured interviews and action research. Based on
field theory, it analyzes the occurrence logic of private -domain fraud, explores the operation
mechanism of the technology -service coupling model, summarizes practical dilemmas and
optimization strategies, and offers experience-based references for anti-fraud governance for the elderly
in urban communities.
1. Concept Definition and Analytical Framework
1.1 Core Concepts
1.1.1 Private-domain fraud against the elderly
The term “private -domain marketing” was mentioned in the 2026 315 Gala. It refers to deceptive
behaviors including false marketing, investment -related fraud and identity -impersonation scams
targeting the elderly in closed social fields such as WeChat private chats, group chats and short -video
private messages. Fraudsters build emotional bonds through acquaintance -based social interactions.
Information flows sluggishly within such fields, making it hard for external parties to detect risk clues
in advance.
1.1.2 Technology-service coupling
Originating from system theory, the concept of coupling, when applied to governance scenarios,
means that technical systems and community -service systems are no longer simply spliced together.
Instead, they realize standard interoperability, process docking and two -way information feedback. In
the anti -fraud field for the elderly, technical systems undertake private -domain information
identification, risk grading and clue pushing; community -service systems receive early -warning clues
and implement practical interventions. Case samples accumulated in service practice are fed back into
technical systems to drive algorithm iteration, forming a bidirectionally-circulating organic system.
1.1.3 “Private-Domain Eagle Eye” APP
The “Private -Domain Eagle Eye” APP is a lightweight community -oriented anti -fraud tool
developed for this project. It contains three terminals: an elderly -friendly user terminal, a social-worker
operation terminal and a community -management terminal. Leveraging natural -language processing
and speech -recognition technologies, it detects risks in private -domain texts, voice messages and
short-video copywriting, outputs three-level risk results (high, medium and low), and binds hierarchical
intervention procedures for social-work-based community-governance pilot applications.
1.2 Analytical Framework Based on Field Theory
From the perspective of field theory, private -domain social interaction forms a virtual independent
action field. Fraudsters, elderly residents, community workers and technical tools are all actors within
this field, who possess different forms of capital. Fraudsters own emotional -discourse capital; the
elderly possess economic capital; community workers have service -related and institutional capital; and
technical tools provide data-driven technological capital.
This study constructs a three -dimensional analytical framework. First, the private -domain-field
dimension analyzes relational logic and practical characteristics of fraud occurrence. Second, the
coupling-operation dimension explores how technical systems and community -service systems realize
factor embedding within the field. Third, the constraint -condition dimension analyzes obstacles to the
coupling-model operation from four perspectives: technical limitations, grassroots manpower, user

literacy and institutional compliance. On this basis, optimization paths for community anti -fraud work
are proposed.
2. Practical Characteristics of Fraud against the Elderly and Governance Dilemmas in
Private-Domain Fields
In Community W of City H, 100 questionnaires were distributed and 95 valid questionnaires were
retrieved. Meanwhile, 20 semi -structured interviews were conducted with community grid workers,
social workers and elderly residents. Questionnaire data show that 42 % of surveyed elderly
respondents have encountered marketing information about health -care-product sales or
pension-investment projects in WeChat groups or private messages, and 17 % have received messages
sent by impostors claiming to be relatives or community staff. Fraud risks in private -domain fields have
become a non-negligible practical problem for communities.
2.1 Main Characteristics of Fraud against the Elderly
First lies the field -operation logic of emotion priority. Instead of committing fraud directly in
private-domain fields, fraudsters accumulate emotional capital via casual chatting, free -gift offers and
health-science popularization. They gradually gain the elderly’s trust before launching deceptive
inducement. Deceptive behaviors are embedded in daily social interactions and thus become much
harder to identify. Second, fraud types are concentrated. Three prevalent scam types are identified in
survey samples: false marketing of health -care products that exaggerate product efficacy by exploiting
the elderly’s health -related anxiety; pension -investment scams that induce capital input with promises
of high returns; and identity -impersonation fraud where perpetrators fabricate excuses to demand
money by posing as relatives or public-service staff. Third, field closure leads to delayed clue discovery.
Private-domain chat contents are only visible to internal participants, so external intervention can
hardly be realized. More than 60 % of risk clues are discovered by family members or communities
only when property losses are imminent, leaving obvious time lags for risk disposal. Fourth, discourses
keep iterating. Fraudsters constantly evade obvious violation -related keywords and adapt to
communication contexts within the field, which weakens the identification effect of traditional
keyword-interception methods.
2.2 Governance Dilemmas of Community Anti-Fraud Work
Clues are mostly discovered passively. Communities can hardly take the initiative to access
residents’ private-domain social scenarios. Most clues are obtained through police reports or feedback
from family members, resulting in insufficient capacity for early risk warning. Disconnection between
the technical field and the community -service-field means existing anti -fraud tools focus on
individual-user warnings and lack functions for clue transfer and task assignment for communities.
Even if algorithms identify risks, early -warning prompts cannot be transformed into practical risk
resolution without support from community -service-field actors. The adaptive capacity of the elderly
within the field varies: some elderly people suffer from insufficient digital literacy and weak ability to
distinguish online social -interaction risks. Meanwhile, they are wary of new software, so subjective
cognition restricts the implementation of technical tools. Cross -actor collaboration among public
security bureaus, civil -affairs departments and communities is inadequate. Belonging to separate
governance fields, they lack regular information -circulation mechanisms, leading to poor connection of
cross-department disposal procedures after communities discover clues.
From the field -theory perspective, neither algorithm -based tools in the technical field nor
public-oriented lectures in the community -service field can change the internal action logic of
private-domain-fraud fields. Therefore, it is practically necessary to realize the coupling of
technological capital and service-oriented capital.
3. Operation Measures of Technology-Service Coupling
The “Private -Domain Eagle Eye” APP promotes embedding of technical systems into
community-services for the elderly. Technical systems output risk clues; social-work practitioners carry
out hierarchical interventions. Case samples accumulated in service practice are fed back into technical
modules to drive system optimization and update, thus realizing bidirectional coupling between the two

systems.
3.1 Age-Adaptive Technical Design
Developed through secondary development based on speech -recognition frameworks, this
mini-program is positioned as a community -pilot tool rather than ground -level-algorithm-oriented
research and development. The elderly -friendly terminal adopts large -font and high -contrast
age-adaptive design. Users can submit texts, voice messages and screenshots for risk detection without
registration. Risk levels and judgment grounds are output within 10 seconds. Results support voice
broadcast, and an entrance for one -click consultation with community workers is embedded to fully
accommodate elderly users’ usage habits.
The social -worker operation terminal receives hierarchical early -warning tasks. High -risk alerts
require telephone verification or home -visit investigation within 24 hours; medium -risk alerts push
anti-fraud-reminder tasks; low -risk subjects are included in key -concern ledgers. After completing
persuasion, psychological counseling and anti -fraud publicity, social workers input disposal records
into the system to form complete service closed loops. The community -management terminal realizes
visualized statistical charts of regional risk data and supports input of local fraud discourses for
continuous iteration of identification models. For privacy -protection purposes, contents submitted by
users are only used for real-time risk judgment and will not be stored for long unless necessary.
3.2 Intervention Procedures of Social Work
Based on different risk levels within the field, professional social -work methods are embedded into
disposal chains. For high -risk scenarios, social workers conduct case work, visit households to expose
scam logic, provide psychological comfort, coordinate with family members, and contact
public-security departments when necessary to stop losses and implement persuasion. For medium -risk
scenarios, grid workers and social workers deliver home -visit reminders and popularize
easy-to-understand anti -fraud materials. Low -risk groups are included in regular -concern lists, and
anti-fraud-themed group activities are organized to promote experience exchange among the elderly.
Meanwhile, community-level anti-fraud publicity is carried out according to high-incidence-fraud types
counted by the system. Low -aged healthy elderly residents are encouraged to form a “silver -age
anti-fraud-propagandist” team to activate the elderly’s own anti -fraud capital and reverse their
passive-victim status in private-domain fields.
3.3 Pilot-Effect Optimization
After community -oriented operation training, community workers use this mini -program to judge
risks of suspicious private -domain information collected within their jurisdiction. Questionnaire
statistics show that the comprehensive satisfaction of community workers and elderly residents reaches
91 %. The mini -program can rapidly identify typical fraud discourses, and digital ledgers simplify
community-work recording procedures. Some potential risks are intervened in at embryonic stages.
Nevertheless, the pilot also reveals practical contradictions in the operation of the coupling model:
limitations exist in dialect -speech recognition; newly -emerging fraud discourses may trigger
identification omissions; some elderly people resist online tools; rising fraud -related clues will increase
community manpower burdens. Multiple practical constraints still exist for factor coupling between the
two fields.
4. Operation Dilemmas of the Technology-Service Coupling Model
4.1 Limitations of the Technical Field
Discourses in private -domain-fields keep updating, and fraudsters constantly adjust expressions to
evade identification rules. Algorithm models require continuous supplementation of
community-collected case samples. Short -term service pilots cannot support long -term iteration. In
addition, recognition of colloquial and dialect-based chat contents still has deficiencies. Algorithms can
only serve as primary -screening tools instead of achieving fully accurate judgment, so manual recheck
procedures must be retained.

4.2 Manpower Shortages within the Field
Technical systems may output batches of early-warning information even on holidays, yet all offline
disposal consumes community human capital. Community affairs are complicated during holidays and
staff resources are limited. Massive surges of early -warning clues will easily lead to task overload.
Manpower contradictions remain insignificant within small -scale pilot communities, but manpower
shortages will become key factors restricting coupling-effect release once the model is promoted widely.
V olunteers and silver-age anti -fraud propagandists can serve as supplementary forces, whereas their
stability and professional competence cannot be guaranteed.
4.3 Subjective Practical Barriers among the Elderly
Some advanced -age elderly people lack the capacity to independently operate the APP. Others
refuse to submit private -chat contents for detection out of privacy concerns. Technical tools can only
cover groups proficient in smart -phone operation. For elderly residents with poor digital competence,
the platform exerts limited effects, and traditional home -visit investigations and family -member
supervision have to be adopted. Thus technical empowerment has objective boundaries.
4.4 Multi-party-collaboration and Data-privacy Constraints
This practice is merely a community pilot focusing on mechanism -level discussion. Interfaces with
business systems of public -security and civil -affairs departments have not been realized, and
cross-department clue transfer still relies on manual communication. Anti -fraud tools involve sensitive
user information, while grassroots communities lack professional data -security
operation-and-maintenance capabilities. For large -scale implementation, systems for data collection,
storage, permission control and desensitization must be improved to prevent
personal-information-leakage risks.
5. Optimization Paths for the Technology -Service Coupling Model from the Field -Theory
Perspective
5.1 Strengthen Technical Adaptability
It should be clarified that online platforms are merely auxiliary governance tools, and anti -fraud
governance cannot be fully entrusted to technologies. Adhere to lightweight development suitable for
the elderly. Establish a two-way supply mechanism for anti-fraud cases from public-security authorities
and local community -collected cases to continuously update identification -sample databases.
Implement the mechanism of “AI primary screening plus manual recheck”. Algorithm outputs serve
only as references, and final risk judgment shall be made by community workers in combination with
local practical conditions. Meanwhile, abolish rigid requirements for independent operation by elderly
users and support submission by social workers or family members to lower usage thresholds for the
elderly.
5.2 Enrich Human Capital
In response to grassroots manpower shortages, build a multi -dimensional team consisting of “social
workers + grid workers + silver -age anti-fraud propagandists + university-student interns”. Professional
social workers are mainly responsible for high -risk case -oriented interventions; community grid
workers undertake clue investigation and home -visit docking; low -aged elderly residents in
communities are recruited as silver -age anti -fraud propagandists to give play to
neighborhood-mutual-assistance advantages; university social -work-major interns share basic work
such as ledger sorting and publicity -material production. Meanwhile, maintain hierarchical -disposal
platforms and simplify data-processing work to reduce sorting burdens for grassroots communities.
5.3 Carry out Classified Anti-Fraud Services
It is particularly important to face up to differentiated digital -literacy levels among the elderly and
reject one -size-fits-all promotion of technical tools. Provide accompanied practical -operation training
for elderly residents capable of using mobile phones. Do not force senior citizens with advanced age

and low digital literacy to use software; instead, implement protection via family -member supervision
and regular home -visit investigations. Questionnaire findings indicate that many elderly people fall
victim to fraud due to lack of emotional -companionship from children in private -life fields. Anti-fraud
work shall not only focus on property -right-protection but also take the elderly’s emotional needs into
account, so as to narrow the survival space for fraudsters who take advantage of emotional capital.
5.4 Open up Collaborative-Governance Chains
Implement laws related to personal -information protection in society, adhere to the principle of
minimum-necessary data collection, complete data desensitization and permission control, establish
safety-protection plans for vulnerable groups, and stick to information -security bottom lines. Promote
the establishment of regular clue -transfer mechanisms among communities, public -security bureaus
and civil -affairs departments. Major community -discovered risk clues shall be rapidly transferred to
public-security authorities, and typical cases released by public -security departments shall be fed back
to communities for science -popularization purposes. Social workers actively explore
government-purchased-service modes to introduce social -organizations into anti -fraud services and
guarantee sustainable operation of anti-fraud models.
5.5 Realize Community-Oriented Services in Light of Local Conditions
Field-resource conditions vary among different communities, so identical implementation schemes
cannot be copied mechanically. Large -scale urban communities can fully activate complete functions
including data visualization and task assignment. By contrast, micro -communities shall simplify
data-statistics workload and focus on risk detection and simple ledgers. In township communities
where digital competence is relatively poor, dependence on online tools shall be weakened, and priority
shall be given to offline -volunteer-assistance modes, so that models can match local field -condition
characteristics.
6. Conclusion
Private-domain social interactions constitute a unique action field. Fraudsters take advantage of
field closure and emotional -interaction logics to commit fraud against the elderly. Neither pure
technical governance nor pure offline services can resolve current anti -fraud dilemmas. The
technology-service coupling model promotes two -way flow of technological capital and
community-service capital, and forms a complete governance closed  loop of “identification  –
 early-warning – intervention – long-term cultivation”, which provides new practical paths for
community-based anti-fraud work for the elderly.
Analysis of the “Private -Domain Eagle Eye” APP pilot based on field theory shows that the
coupling model possesses governance value, yet it is constrained by multiple factors including
data-sample-iteration pressure, grassroots -manpower supply, digital literacy of the elderly and data
compliance. Technologies themselves cannot replace community -social-work services; the two form a
complementary and symbiotic relationship. Technologies focus on solving the problem of “early
detection” of risk clues, while social -work practitioners complete on -site interventions based on
human-centered demands within the field. Looking ahead, community anti -fraud work for the elderly
shall adhere to the people -oriented principle, be rooted in practical community -field conditions, enrich
grassroots-service resources while optimizing technical tools, improve cross -department-collaboration
mechanisms, and build a comprehensive protection network jointly driven by technologies, services
and institutions. Limited to a single urban community, this study has a small sample scope. Its
conclusions need further verification through more community -based practices. Future research can
expand investigations into township -level communities and continuously improve implementation
paths for technology-service coupling.
