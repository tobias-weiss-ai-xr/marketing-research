ABSTRACT
Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) is reshaping digital marketing by enabling personalised, scalable, and innovative content,
while simultaneously raising concerns regarding authenticity and consumer trust. This study examines how Perceived Authentici ty,
Perceived Personalization, and Perceived Usefulness/Innovation influence Brand Perception and Purchase Intention among Generation
Z consumers, with Brand Perception serving as a mediating mechanism. A quantitative, cross-sectional research design was employed
using data from 425 Generation Z consumers collected through a structured q uestionnaire based on five-point Likert-type scales. The
data were analysed using descriptive statistics, reliability analysis, Pearson and Spearman correlations, multiple regression , variance
inflation factors, HC3 robust standard errors, and bootstrapped mediation analysis based on 5,000 resamples. The findings indicate that
Perceived Authenticity (β = .234), Perceived Personalization (β = .160), and Perceived Usefulness/Innovation (β = .407) significantly
and positively influence Brand Perception. Brand Perception significantly predicts Purchase Intention (β = .520). Significant indirect
effects were observed for Perceived Authenticity (β = .122), Perceived Personalization (β = .083), and Perceived Usefulness/Innovation
(β = .212) through Brand Perception. The findings demonstrate that consumer responses to GenAI marketing translate into purchase
intention substantially through broader brand evaluations. The study highlights the importance of combining AI -enabled innovation
and personalization with authenticity and consistent brand positioning.
KEY WORDS: Generative AI Marketing; Brand Perception; Purchase Intention; Generation Z

1. INTRODUCTION
The rapid development of Generative Artificial
Intelligence (GenAI) is transforming digital marketing by
enabling brands to produce content that is faster, scalable,
personalised, and responsive to changing consumer preferences.
Unlike earlier consumer -facing AI applications that were
primarily associated with recommendation systems and
automated services, GenAI increasingly participates directly in
the creation of advertising and brand communication (Longoni &
Cian, 2022; Kim et al., 2022; Smith & Hutson, 2024). Generative
AI enables marketers to develop dynamic and personalised
messages based on consumers' preferences and behavioural
patterns, while also reducing production costs and improving
marketing efficiency (Kshetri et al., 2023; Israfilzade & Sadil i,
2025). These capabilities create considerable opportunities for
brands to improve consumer engagement and purchase -related
outcomes (Mehta et al., 2022; Sivathanu et al., 2022).
However, the increasing use of AI in marketing also
creates an important consumer -perception challenge. Although
AI-generated content can generate positive responses through
novelty, usefulness, and personalisation (Mayahi & Vidrih, 2022;
Ratta et al., 202 4), consumers may simultaneously question its
authenticity, credibility, and emotional quality (Sharma & Lal,
2024; Aljarah et al., 2024). Awareness or disclosure of AI
involvement has been associated with reduced empathy,
credibility, and favourable advertising attitudes in some contexts
(Arango et al., 2023; Baek et al., 2024). Such concerns are
particularly important because authenticity is closely associated
with consumer trust, brand relationships, and purchase decisions
(Zhang & Patrick, 2021; Cascio Rizzo et al., 2023; Wahyuni &
Zulfikar, 2024). At the same time, well -designed AI-generated
content may strengthen brand credibility and consumer
engagement, indicating that consumer responses to AI-generated
marketing are not uniformly positive or negativ e (Feuerriegel et
al., 2024; Hendrayati et al., 2024). Generation Z is especially
relevant in this context because this consumer group may
scrutinise machine-generated content more critically (Wortel et
al., 2024).
Against this background, the present study examines the
relationships between Perceived Authenticity, Perceived
Personalization, Perceived Usefulness/Innovation, Brand
Perception, and Purchase Intention among Generation Z
consumers. Specifically, the study aims to examine whether
perceived authenticity, personalization, and
usefulness/innovation influence brand perception; assess whether
brand perception significantly influences purchase intention; and
determine whether bra nd perception mediates the relationships
between these AI-marketing perceptions and purchase intention.

The study therefore moves beyond examining the direct effects of
AI-generated marketing perceptions by identifying brand
perception as a potential mechanism through which consumer
evaluations translate into purchase intention. This focus addresses
the need for greater empirical understanding of how consumers
process AI -generated marketing and subsequently form

2. REVIEW OF LITERATURE
The development of consumer -facing artificial
intelligence has transformed digital marketing from conventional
predictive and analytical applications toward increasingly
interactive and generative forms of consumer engagement. Before
the public launch of C hatGPT in December 2022, consumer -
facing AI was largely associated with recommender systems and
automated service provision (Longoni & Cian, 2022; Kim et al.,
2022; Rizomyliotis et al., 2022). Subsequent applications have
expanded to AI chatbots, service r obots, virtual influencers, and
other interactive systems. Consumers may respond to AI chatbots
as parasocial entities (Lee & Park, 2022), while human -like
characteristics in service robots can generate favourable
responses (Blut et al., 2021). Similarly, perceived empathy in
chatbots (Liu-Thompkins et al., 2022), humorous emojis (Liu et
al., 2023), and virtual social media influencers (Thomas &
Fowler, 2021) have been associated with positive consumer
responses. These developments indicate that consumer re actions
to AI are shaped not simply by technological functionality but
also by how consumers interpret the characteristics and quality of
AI-mediated interactions.
One particularly important dimension of these evaluations
is perceived authenticity. Consumers value authenticity and
honesty in their interactions with organisations and are more
likely to engage with marketing messages perceived as genuine
and emotionally resonant (Zhang & Patrick, 2021; Cascio Rizzo
et al., 2023). Brand authenticity has also been identified as an
important consideration in consumers' purchasing decisions
(Brandwatch.com, 2023). This issue becomes particularly
relevant when marketing cont ent is generated by AI because
research has produced mixed evidence regarding consumer
responses. While AI -generated content may generate positive
reactions through novelty and complexity (Mayahi & Vidrih,
2022; Ratta et al., 2024), other studies identify concerns relating
to authenticity and emotional connection (Sharma & Lal, 2024;
Aljarah et al., 2024). Awareness that content has been generated
by AI can reduce empathy and emotional engagement (Arango et
al., 2023), while disclosure of AI -generated conte nt has been
found to negatively influence perceived credibility and
advertising attitudes (Baek et al., 2024). Consequently,
transparency and authenticity may play an important role in
determining how consumers evaluate AI -generated marketing
content (Campbell et al., 2022; Zelch et al., 2023).
The importance of authenticity is particularly evident in
social-media-based brand communication, where perceived
authenticity contributes to stronger brand identities and
relationships with followers (Campagna et al., 2023; Leung et al.,
2022). Consumers' authenticity perceptions may depend on how
content is produced (Audrezet et al., 2020), and the increasing use
of Generative AI for automated content creation may therefore
jeopardise perceptions of brand authenticity (Wahid et al., 2023).
Silver et al. ( 2021) similarly proposed that changes in perceived
brand authenticity may help explain consumer aversion to the
automation of activities traditionally performed by humans. More
recent research further associates perceived authenticity with
reduced sceptici sm, stronger emotional connections, and
enhanced purchase intentions (Wahyuni & Zulfikar, 2024).
Transparent, ethical, and consistent communication has also been
emphasised as important for developing consumer trust,
particularly among value -driven Generation Z consumers (Lee,
2025; Fitriyana, 2025). Accordingly, perceived authenticity has
been identified as an important determinant of both trust and
purchase intention (García -Salirrosas & Rondon -Eusebio, 2022;
Wahyuni & Zulfikar, 2024).
Alongside authenticity, personalization has become a
central feature of Generative AI -enabled marketing. AI enables
marketers to analyse large volumes of consumer information and
generate dynamic, personalised advertising messages tailored to
individual preferences and behavioural patterns (Smith & Hutson,
2024; Israfilzade & Sadili, 2024). Generative AI can also
accelerate content production, reduce production costs, and
enable marketers to respond more rapidly to changing consumer
expectations and market conditions (Kshetri et al., 2023).
Israfilzade and Sadili (2025), in their review of Generative AI in
e-commerce, highlighted its ability to facilitate hyper -
personalised recommendations, dynamic content generation, and
immersive visual experiences, while also identifying ethical,
data-quality, and scalability challenges. These developments
suggest that personalization may strengthen consumers'
evaluations of AI -enabled brand communication, although its
effectiveness depends on how consumers perceive the re sulting
content and brand interaction.
A related dimension is perceived usefulness/innovation,
which reflects the extent to which consumers recognise functional
value and innovative characteristics in AI -enabled marketing
experiences. AI-generated content can improve digital marketing
performance and consumer engagement, although its direct and
indirect influence on purchase intention remains subject to further
empirical validation (Hendrayati et al., 2024; Sands et al., 2022).
AI can analyse extensive consumer data to produce content that
resonates with target audiences, potentially increasing favourable
responses and purchase intentions (Mehta et al., 2022; Sivathanu
et al., 2022). AI involvement may also increase perceptions of
novelty, particularly for high -involvement products, although
novelty can sometimes be accompanied by lower perceived
quality (Moura et al., 2023). Further, perceived
anthropomorphism, trust, and usefulness of AI -powered

platforms have been found to positively influence purchase
intentions (Israfilzade, 2025), while AI -enabled social -media
engagement can enhance customer satisfaction and purchase

factors such as  faith, consciousness, and perceived control can

purchase intention (Lopes et al., 2024). These findings
collectively indicate that consumers' functional and innovative
evaluations of AI may contr ibute to favourable downstream

The literature also suggests that these individual
perceptions may operate through broader brand -related
evaluations. Brand credibility, reflecting consumers' perceptions
of a brand's reliability and trustworthiness, reduces uncertainty
and plays an import ant role in purchase decisions (Erdem &
Swait, 2004). Although Generation Z consumers may scrutinise
machine-generated content more critically (Wortel et al., 2024),
well-designed AI -generated content can strengthen brand
credibility and consumer engagemen t (Feuerriegel et al., 2024;
Hendrayati et al., 2024). The Theory of Planned Behavior
proposes that behavioural intentions are shaped by consumers'
attitudes and beliefs (Ajzen, 1991), while empirical research
indicates that credible brands are more likely  to be selected
because credibility reduces uncertainty and increases confidence
in purchase decisions (Lee & Chen, 2021; Mohamed & Ünsalan,
2025; Silalahi et al., 2022; Yang & Hu, 2022). Thus, the way
consumers perceive AI-generated marketing may first in fluence
their evaluation of the brand before translating into behavioural
intentions.
The relationship between brand -related evaluations and
purchase intention is further supported by research on product
involvement and advertising. Product involvement can shape
purchase intentions and moderate the influence of brand diversity,
product information, and trust on online purchase intentions (Kim
et al., 2005). Wirya and Syah (2022) reported a positive
relationship between product involvement and purchase intention
in advertising, whereas Butt (2014) found no significant direct
relationship between product involvement and purchase intention
but identified a mediating role for brand attitude. These findings
are relevant to the present study because they suggest that
consumer responses to marketing stimuli may not translate
directly into purchase intention; rather, brand-related evaluations
may serve as an intervening mechanism.
Overall, the literature establishes that AI -generated
marketing can generate both favourable and unfavourable
consumer responses. On the one hand, AI offers personalization,
usefulness, novelty, efficiency, and engagement (Mehta et al.,
2022; Sivathanu et al., 2022; Smith & Hutson, 2024; Israfilzade
& Sadili, 2025); on the other hand, concerns about authenticity,
credibility, emotional connection, and the disclosure of AI
involvement remain evident (Arango et al., 2023; Baek et al.,
2024; Brüns & Meißner, 2 024). Brüns and Meißner (2024), in
particular, found that Generative AI social -media content was
associated with lower perceived brand authenticity and negative
follower responses. At the same time, the literature demonstrates
that trust, usefulness, ease of use, credibility, and other consumer
evaluations can ultimately influence purchase intention (Erdem &
Swait, 2004; Israfilzade, 2025; Lopes et al., 2024). However, the
existing literature has largely examined these relationships
separately, and comparat ively limited empirical attention has
been given to how perceived authenticity, personalization, and
usefulness/innovation jointly shape brand perception and how
brand perception subsequently translates these perceptions into
purchase intention. This provi des the basis for examining Brand
Perception as a mediating mechanism between consumers'
perceptions of AI -generated marketing and their purchase
intention.

3. CONCEPTUAL FRAMEWORK AND
HYPOTHESES
The conceptual framework proposes that consumers'
perceptions of AI -generated marketing—Perceived Authenticity
(PA), Perceived Personalization (PP), and Perceived
Usefulness/Innovation (PU) —influence Brand Perception (BP),
which subsequently shapes Purchase Intention (PI). Although AI-
generated advertising can improve efficiency and personalization
(Smith & Hutson, 2024; Kshetri et al., 2023; Israfilzade & Sadili,
2025), concerns regarding credibility and authenticity may
influence how consumers evaluate the brand (Puntoni et al., 2020;
Brüns & Meißner, 2024). Evidence that perceived authenticity
mediates the relationship  between media richness and value co -
creation in AI-powered branded applications further suggests that
consumer perceptions can operate through intervening
mechanisms (V o et al., 2024). Similarly, the introduction of AI-
generated content may produce negati ve initial responses, while
brand perception and perceived effort can mitigate these effects
(Rhee & Lee, 2024). Concerns regarding AI disclosure and its
effect on brand trust further highlight the importance of how
consumers interpret AI involvement (Lefk eli et al., 2023).
Accordingly, the study proposes that PA, PP, and PU positively
influence BP, while BP positively influences PI. The framework
further proposes that BP mediates the relationships between the
three AI-marketing perceptions and PI. This fra mework leads to
the following hypotheses.
H1: Perceived Authenticity has a significant positive effect on
Brand Perception.
H2: Perceived Personalization has a significant positive effect on
Brand Perception.
H3: Perceived Usefulness/Innovation has a significant positive
effect on Brand Perception.
H4: Brand Perception has a significant positive effect on
Purchase Intention.
H5: Brand Perception significantly mediates the relationship
between Perceived Authenticity and Purchase Intention.
H6: Brand Perception significantly mediates the relationship
between Perceived Personalization and Purchase Intention.
H7: Brand Perception significantly mediates the relationship
between Perceived Usefulness/Innovation and Purchase
Intention.

4. METHODOLOGY
The study adopted a quantitative, cross-sectional research
design to examine the relationships among perceived authenticity
(PA), perceived personalization (PP), perceived
usefulness/innovation (PU), brand perception (BP), and purchase
intention (PI) in the context of AI-generated marketing. The study
focused on Generation Z consumers, and data were collected
through a structured questionnaire using five -point Likert -type
scales. A total of 425 usable responses were retained for analysis.
Each construct was measured using multiple items: PA and PP
with six items each, PU with six items, and BP and PI with eight
items each. Composite scores were computed using the mean of
the respective items, with higher scores indicating stronger
perceptions or intentions.
Data analysis was conducted using R. Descriptive
statistics were used to summarize the constructs, while
Cronbach's alpha assessed internal consistency. Pearson's
correlation was employed to examine associations among the
constructs, with Spearman's correl ation used as a robustness
check. Multicollinearity was assessed using variance inflation
factors (VIF). Multiple regression models were estimated to
examine the predictors of brand perception and purchase
intention. Since heteroscedasticity was detected in the regression
diagnostics, HC3 heteroscedasticity -consistent standard errors
were used for robust statistical inference. Finally, the mediating
role of brand perception was examined through an observed -
variable path mediation model using 5,000 bootstrap resamples;
indirect effects were considered significant when their 95%
bootstrap confidence intervals excluded zero. The results of these
analyses are presented in the following section.

6. DISCUSSION
The findings demonstrate that consumers' perceptions of
AI-generated marketing are significantly associated with how
they evaluate the brand and subsequently form purchase
intentions. Perceived Authenticity, Perceived Personalization,
and Perceived Usefuln ess/Innovation all positively influence
Brand Perception, with Usefulness/Innovation showing the
strongest effect ( β = .407). This suggests that although
authenticity remains important in AI -generated communication,
consumers' recognition of the functional  and innovative value of
AI may play a particularly important role in shaping favourable
brand evaluations. This is consistent with previous research
showing that AI can enhance personalization, efficiency,
engagement, and consumer responses (Smith & Hutso n, 2024;
Kshetri et al., 2023; Israfilzade & Sadili, 2025). Brand Perception
also has a strong positive effect on Purchase Intention (β = .520),
supporting the view that favourable brand evaluations reduce
uncertainty and strengthen consumers' confidence i n purchase
decisions (Erdem & Swait, 2004). Most importantly, the
mediation results demonstrate that Brand Perception acts as an
important mechanism linking AI -marketing perceptions with
Purchase Intention. The indirect effects of Authenticity and
Personalization are significant while their direct effects are
nonsignificant, indicating indirect -only mediation, whereas
Usefulness/Innovation retains a significant direct effect alongside
its indirect effect, indicating partial mediation. These findings
extend existing research by showing that consumer responses to
AI-generated marketing do not necessarily translate directly into
purchase intention but may operate through consumers' broader
evaluations of the brand.

7. CONCLUSION AND IMPLICATIONS
The study concludes that Perceived Authenticity,
Perceived Personalization, and Perceived Usefulness/Innovation
are important determinants of Brand Perception in AI -generated
marketing, while Brand Perception is a strong predictor of
Purchase Intention among Generation Z consumers. The findings
particularly highlight Brand Perception as the central mechanism
through which consumer perceptions of AI -generated marketing
influence behavioural intention. Theoretically, the study
contributes by integrating three  dimensions of AI -marketing
perception into a single framework and demonstrating the
mediating role of Brand Perception. Practically, marketers should
not focus exclusively on the technological capabilities of
Generative AI; they should ensure that AI -generated content
remains authentic, relevant, useful, and consistent with the
brand's identity. Personalisation should create consumer value
without compromising perceived genuineness, while the
functional benefits of AI should be communicated clearly. The
study is subject to limitations arising from its cross -sectional
design, self -reported measures, and focus on Generation Z
consumers, which restrict causal interpretation and broader
generalisation. Future research could employ longitudinal or
experimental designs, examine other consumer generations and
contexts, compare AI -generated and human -generated content,
and investigate additional mediators or moderators such as trust,
transparency, perceived credibility, or AI disclosure.
