Beyond Simple Interaction: Uncovering the Perception-Interaction Intrinsic
Mechanism of Generative AI Agents—A Multi-Modal Big Data Analysis
with PLS-SEM and fsQCA
Abstract
Generative Artificial Intelligence (G enAI) is increasingly being adopted across industries, yet existing
literature has not fully explored the unique traits and the complex mechanism it introduces. To address this
gap, this study investigates the unique characteristics of GenAI agents and  their impact on user interaction
behaviors. By analyzing user -generated text and AI -generated images from the Character .AI platform, we
examine three key perceptual characteristics: social personalization, functional customization, and emotional
affordance. Through multi-modal machine learning approaches combining Structural Topic Modeling (STM)
and Facial Action Coding System (FACS), we propose the “perceived characteristics of GenAI agent -
empathy-interactive willingness”  (PCoGenAI-E-IW) theoretical model to explore how user perceptions
transform into interactive behaviors. Furthermore, the PLS-SEM analysis and configurational approach
identify 10 distinct variable combinations that influence users ’ interaction willingness. The findings validate
our multi-modal analytical framework while providing valuable empirical evidence for marketing strategy
formulation, service experience optimization, and theoretical advancement in human-AI interaction research.
Keywords: Human-AI Interaction, Generative AI agent, Interactive willingness,  Perceived
Characteristics, Empathy, Multi-modal big data analysis

from information quality to emotional resonance—each exerting a profound impact on user experience and

RQ1: How do users perceive the specific characteristics of GenAI agents and consequently trigger
their interaction behavior? In other words, what specific signals or information are released by GenAI

RQ2: How do these cognitive perceptions drive users’ behavior after they perceive the characteristics

RQ3: Unlike previous “symmetric” approaches, what are the different levels of combinations of GenAI

Despite these valuable insights from existing literature, several critical gaps remain in our understanding
of GenAI agent interactions. First, while previous studies have explored individual characteristics of AI
systems (as shown in Table 1 -1), they have  rarely examined the comprehensive set of GenAI -specific
characteristics in an integrated framework. Most studies focus on either social, functional, or emotional aspects
in isolation, missing the holistic nature of GenAI agent interactions. Second, the ps ychological mechanisms
through which these characteristics influence user behavior remain underexplored, particularly the role of
empathy in mediating GenAI -human relationships. Third, existing research predominantly employs
symmetric analytical approaches that assume linear relationships, overlooking the complex configurations and
combinations of factors that may lead to interaction willingness. Fourth, methodological limitations persist,
as few studies have leveraged multimodal big data analysis to captur e the rich, nuanced characteristics of
GenAI agents as they appear in real-world interactions.
In response, b uilding on prior research and identified gaps, this study develops a comprehensive
framework to understand user interaction with GenAI agents through three key dimensions: social
personalization, functional customization, and emotional affordance (Do, et al., 2023 ). Our methodology
combines two innovative approaches: First, we employ Structural Topic Modeling (STM), an unsupervised
machine learning technique, to analyze textual data from the Character.AI platform, revealing detailed patterns
in social personalization and functional customization. Second, we utilize the Facial Action Coding System
(FACS) to analyze emotional features in GenAI agents, categorizing them into six basic emotions: happiness,
surprise, sadness, fear, anger, and disgust.  Then, t hese analyses inform our proposed “Perceived
Characteristics of GenAI Agent -Empathy-Interactive Willingness”  (PCoGenAI-E-IW) theoretical model,
which examines how users ’ perceptions of GenAI agents tran slate into interaction behaviors , which
specifically highlights empathy as a crucial mediating mechanism and considers the moderating role of
information clue quantity. Furthermore, through configurational analysis, we further explore how different
combinations of these characteristics influence users ’ willingness to interact, providing a nuanced
understanding of human-AI interaction dynamics.
Our study addresses these gaps by: (1) developing an integrated framework that simultaneously captures
social, functional, and emotional dimensions of GenAI agents; (2) investigating empathy as a critical
psychological mechanism linking agent characteristics to user behavior; (3) employing both variable-centered
(PLS-SEM) and configuration -centered ( fsQCA) approaches to uncover complex relationships; and (4)
utilizing innovative multimodal big data analysis techniques to extract insights from real -world Gen AI
interactions.
Overall, this study makes several theoretical and practical contributions. First of all, this study introduces
an innovative “multi-modal big data analysis & PLS -SEM & fsQCA” research paradigm, which transcends
traditional methodological limitations by combining automated text analysis, visual emotional recognition,
and both symmetric and asymmetric analytical approaches. This methodological innovation enables the
capture of complex GenAI charact eristics that would be difficult to identify through conventional survey or
experimental methods alone. Secondly, we develop a comprehensive framework for understanding perceived
GenAI agent characteristics  that integrates social, functional, and emotional dimensions, offering

5
unprecedented insights into the multifaceted nature of human -AI interaction.  Third, we propose and
empirically validate the PCoGenAI-E-IW theoretical model, which not only explains how these characteristics
influence user interaction willingness but also highlights the crucial mediating role of empathy—an emotional
mechanism largely overlooked in previous technical -focused AI research.  Fourthly, this study extends
information processing theory  by meticulously analyzing the cognitive and emotional processes during
human-GenAI interaction, thereby adapting classic theoretical perspectives to emerging technological
contexts. Fifth, by identifying specific configurations of GenAI characteristics that most effectively drive user
interaction, we provide actionable insights for AI developers and businesses seeking to optimize GenAI agent
design. Our findings not only validate the effectiveness of our multi-modal approach in analyzing the complex
connections between user perceptions and behaviors, but also provide empirical evidence that can guide
marketing strategy formulation, service experience optimization, and future theoretical development in this
rapidly evolving field (Lim, Jasim, & Malathi, 2025).
2. Literature Review and Hypotheses Development
2.1 Generative AI Agent and Perceived Characteristics of GenAI Agent
Generative AI Agent represents a breakthrough in artificial intelligence technology, characterized by its
ability to create original content and engage in natural language interactions through large language models.
Unlike traditional AI systems, GenAI agents possess distinctive capabilities including content generation
across multiple mod alities (text, image, etc.), interactive communication through coherent dialogues,
adaptability based on user inputs, and continuous learning from interactions  (Dwivedi, et al., 2023; Niu &
Mvondo, 2024). These agents, exemplified by systems like ChatGPT and Gemini, demonstrate advanced
abilities in understanding context, maintaining conversation flow, and providing responses that adapt to user
needs. The fundamental architecture of GenAI agents integrate s sophisticated neural networks and machine
learning algorithms, enabling them to process and generate human -like responses while maintaining
contextual relevance and semantic coherence. This technological foundation allows GenAI agents to serve as
interactive partners capable of engaging in meaningful exchanges with users, representing a significant
advancement in human-computer interaction.
Prior research has established various frameworks for understanding user perception of AI agents,
consistently revealing a tripartite structure in different contexts. Kim et al. (2023) suggest that perceived agent
features can be categorized into three dim ensions: appearance, cognitive intelligence, and emotional
intelligence (J. Kim & Im, 2023). Similarly, Chen et al. (2023) identified three potential design affordances in
VR environments: aesthetic, functional, and symbolic affordance, which correspond to consumer sensation,
cognition, and emotion respectively (Chen, et al., 2023). Do et al. (2023) further support this tripartite structure
by capturing three dimensions of empathetic creativity: social, interactive, and emotional dimensions (Do, et
al., 2023). Building upon these theoretical foundations and empirical evidence, researchers have demonstrated
that these dimensions represent different yet complementary aspects of user perception, contributing to a more
nuanced understanding of how users evaluate a nd engage with GenAI agents. Through systematic analysis

6
and synthesis of existing literature in human-computer interaction and artificial intelligence, we propose that
the perceived characteristics of GenAI agents can be comprehensively categorized into three primary
dimensions: functional customization, social  personalization, and emotional affordance. This categorization
not only aligns with previous theoretical frameworks but also provides a structured approach to analyzing the
complex nature of user-agent interactions and helps identify the key factors that influence users’ willingness
to engage with GenAI agents. The tripartite classification encompasses the technical, social, and emotional
aspects of human -agent interaction, offering a comprehensive framework for understanding how users
perceive and interact with GenAI agents.
2.1.1 Functional Customization
Within the context of GenAI agents, functional customization represents a sophisticated evolution
beyond traditional functional attributes and basic customization capabilities. It refers to the degree to which
GenAI agents can be dynamically configured and  adapted by users to align with their specific requirements,
preferences, and objectives, while leveraging the agent ’s generative and learning capabilities  (Luo, et al.,
2023). Unlike conventional functional features that offer predetermined options or simple customization that
allows surface-level adjustments, functional customization in GenAI agents emphasizes the intelligent and
adaptive nature of the customization process (Zhou, Zhang, Wang, & Mou, 2023). The agent not only accepts
user modifications but also learns from these interactions to continuously refine and optimize its performance.
This is manifested through advanced capabilities such as contextual understanding, real -time adaptation of
response patterns, and dynamic adjustment of interaction styles based on user feedback. For instance, users
can configure the agent’s knowledge domain, adjust its response style and depth, set specific task parameters,
and even define custom workflows that integrate with external tools and data sources (Luo, et al., 2023; Zhou,
et al., 2023). The agent then intelligently processes these preferences and generates responses that reflect both
the user’s immediate needs and their evolving interaction patterns. This level of sophisticated customization
transforms GenAI agents from generic AI tools into highly personalized cognitive assistants that can
effectively adapt to each user ’s unique operational context and evolving requirements  (Santos-Vijande,
Gómez-Rico, Molina-Collado, & Davison, 2022).
2.1.2 Social Personalization
Within the context of GenAI agents, social personalization transcends traditional social interaction
patterns and basic personalization features, representing the agent ’s sophisticated capability to dynamically
adapt its social behavior and communication style to align with users’ social identities, cultural backgrounds,
and interpersonal preferences  (J. Kim & Im, 2023 ; Pfiffelmann, et al., 2024 ). Unlike conventional social
interactions that follow predetermined scripts or simple personalization that merely adjusts surface -level
features, social personalization in GenAI agents leverages their generative capabilities to create authentic,
contextually appropriate, and culturally nuanced interactions. The agent demonstrates this through its ability
to recognize and respond to subtle social cues, adapt its communication style based on cultural contexts, and
maintain consistent social personas across multiple in teractions (Ziller, Loepp, Kindermann, Köchling, &
Fadeeva, 2025). For instance, the agent can dynamically adjust its language formality, cultural references,
humor style, and social etiquette based on the user’s background and preferences, while maintaining coherent

7
and meaningful dialogue that reflects deep understanding of social dynamics (Pfiffelmann, et al., 2024). This
advanced form of personalization enables GenAI agents to move beyond mere information exchange to
establish genuine social connections, demonstrating cultural sensitivity and social awareness in ways that
traditional AI systems cannot (J. Kim & Im, 2023). By intelligently processing and adapting to users ’ social
signals and preferences, GenAI agents can create more meaningful and culturally resonant interactions,
transforming from simple conversational tools into socially aware interaction partners that can effectively
navigate the complexities of human social communication (Priya & Sharma, 2023; W. Yu, Zhao, Liu, & Song,
2021).
2.1.3 Emotional Affordance
Within the context of GenAI agents, emotional affordance represents the agent’s capability to recognize
and respond to fundamental human emotions through its language understanding and generation capabilities
(Borghi & Mariani, 2022 ). Unlike conventional emotional recognition systems that follow rigid patterns or
simple affordances that only suggest potential actions  (Liu-Thompkins, Okazaki, & Li, 2022 ), emotional
affordance in GenAI agents focuses on responding to emotional states in human-agent interactions. The agent
demonstrates this through its ability to detect emotional cues from textual interactions and generate
appropriate responses that acknowledge and address these basic emotions. For instance, when detecting shifts
in a user’s emotional state, the agent can adjust its response style and content to better align with the user ’s
current emotional context, providing appropriate emotional acknowledgment and support. This capability
enables GenAI agents to move beyond purely task -oriented interactions to create more emotionally aware
exchanges, while maintaining appropriate interac tion boundaries (Borghi & Mariani, 2022; Liu-Thompkins,
et al., 2022 ). Through its ability to recognize and respond to fundamental emotional states, emotional
affordance allows GenAI agents to engage in more natural and emotionally attuned interactions, enhancing
the quality of human -agent communication while operating with in well -defined emotional recognition
parameters (Borghi & Mariani, 2022).
2.2 The Effect of Perceived Characteristics of GenAI Agent on Interactive Willingness
In the rapidly evolving digital ecosystem, GenAI agents have emerged as transformative interfaces for
human-technology interaction, representing a significant advancement beyond traditional AI systems  (Ben
Saad & Choura, 2023 ). These agents are distinguished by their sophisticated characteristics in functional
customization, social personalization, and emotional affordance, fundamentally reshaping the landscape of
human-agent interaction (H. Kim & Park, 2024 ; J. Kim & Im, 2023 ). Recent empirical evidence strongly
suggests that users ’ perceptions of these advanced characteristics play a pivotal role in determining the
effectiveness of their interactions and significantly influence their trust development and brand loyalty patterns
(Ben Saad & Choura, 2023; Ladeira, et al., 2023).
Contemporary research has illuminated several critical aspects of this relationship. Studies have
demonstrated that the perceived anthropomorphic qualities of AI agents significantly enhance user satisfaction
and engagement levels (H. Kim & Park, 2024 ; Mehmood, et al., 2024 ). Furthermore, Ladeira et al. (2023)
established that the perception of trust in AI systems serves as a crucial mediating factor between informational

8
cues (including anthropomorphic and social influence cues) and user acceptance  (Ladeira, et al., 2023 ). Of
particular significance is the finding by Mehmood et al. (2024) that empathetic capabilities substantially
moderate the relationship between anthropomorphic characteristics and user engagement, highlighting the
importance of emotional elements in huma n-agent interactions (Mehmood, et al., 2024 ). These insights are
further supported by  Ben et al.(2023) , who emphasize how users ’ perceptions of AI agents ’ characteristics
fundamentally shape their attitudes and behavioral responses (Ben Saad & Choura, 2023).
Building on this theoretical foundation, this study proposes that the perceived characteristics of GenAI
agents, specifically their inherent qualities in functional customization, social personalization, and emotional
awareness, can significantly enhance users ’ willingness to engage in meaningful interactions. This leads to
our primary hypothesis:
H1: Users ’ perceptions of GenAI agents ’ characteristics ( including (a) social personalization,
(b)functional customization, and (c)emotional affordance) positively influence their willingness to
engage in interactive experiences with these agents.
2.3 The Role of Empathy
Contemporary research has established a robust connection between users ’ perceptions of AI agents ’
characteristics and their emotional responses and interaction behaviors. Through the lens of human -AI
interaction and information processing theory, this relationship manifests in multiple dimensions that warrant
careful examination (Cheng, et al., 2024 ; Harris-Watson, et al., 2023 ; J. Zhang, et al., 2024 ). In this study,
empathy is defined as the ability to understand and share another ’s emotional state, involving both cognitive
recognition of others’ feelings and affective responsiveness to those emotions (J. Zhang, et al., 2024). In the
context of human-AI interactions, empathy encompasses the AI’s capacity to recognize user emotions, provide
emotionally appropriate responses, and create a sense of emotional connection that simulates human-to-human
empathetic exchanges (Do, et al., 2023; Mehmood, et al., 2024).
Studies have demonstrated a significant relationship between perceived AI characteristics and emotional
responses. Information processing theory is a cognitive framework that compares the human cognitive system
to a computer and explains how people acquire, store, and utilize information (Payne, 2024). In the field of
consumer behavior, the theory helps to understand how consumers process information and make decisions,
especially in the context of AI interactions, where it explains how users perceive and respond to AI content,
including concepts such as cognitive load and information complexity. Harris-Watson et al. (2023) revealed
that perceived warmth and competence significantly influence psychological receptivity in human -AI
interactions (Harris-Watson, et al., 2023 ). Kim and Park (2024)’s research further validated that perceived
anthropomorphic characteristics positively affect users ’ emotional engagement and satisfaction  (H. Kim &
Park, 2024). Notably, Huang et al. (2023)’s cognitive-emotion-cognitive framework established that cognitive
evaluations of agent characteristics, including social and emotional elements, directly impact users’ emotional
responses (Huang, et al., 2023 ). Zhang et al. (2024) substantiated this relationship by showing how agents ’
perceived characteristics and emotional expression capabilities enhance user satisfaction and emotional
connection (J. Zhang, et al., 2024). These findings collectively suggest that when users perceive sophisticated

9
characteristics in GenAI agents, they are more likely to develop empathetic connections through cognitive -
affective processing.
H2: Through the cognitive evaluation process, perceived characteristics of GenAI agent ((a) social
personalization, (b) functional customization, and (c) emotional affordance) have a positive impact on
users’ empathetic level.
The relationship between empathy and interaction willingness has been well -documented in recent
literature. From an information processing perspective, emotional responses serve as crucial inputs for
behavioral decision-making. Mehmood et al. (2024) established that empathy significantly enhances customer
engagement with AI service robots  (Mehmood, et al., 2024 ), while Cheng et al. (2024), drawing from the
computer as social actor (CASA) theory, demonstrated how emotional connections influence users’ interaction
intentions (Cheng, et al., 2024 ). Zhou et al. (2023) ’s research on customer inspiration revealed that
psychological outcomes mediate between perceived technological characteristics and behavioral intentions
(Zhou, et al., 2023 ). Additionally, Do et al. (2023) ’s validation of empathic creativity in service encounters
reinforces the notion that emotional understanding leads to increased interaction willingness (Do, et al., 2023).
H3: Empathy between users and GenAI agents has a positive effect on users’ interactive willingness.
Building on these theoretical foundations and empirical evidence, we propose that empathy serves as a
crucial mediating mechanism in the relationship between perceived GenAI characteristics and users ’
interactive willingness. Information processing theory posits that individuals process external stimuli through
distinct stages: from sensory input to cognitive processing, emotional response, and behavioral output (Briñol,
Rucker, & Petty, 2015; Li, Wu, Cao, & Wang, 2021). In the context of human-AI interaction, this sequential
processing manifests when users first perceive agent characteristics (sensory input), evaluate these
characteristics through cognitive schemas (cognitive processing), develop emotional connections (emotional
response), and ultimately make interaction decisions (behavioral output)  (Taylor & Crocker, 2022 ). The
mediation process operates through this theoretical lens: users ’ perceptions of GenAI characteristics are first
cognitively processed and evaluated against existing mental models, fostering emotional connections and
empathetic understanding. This enhanced empathetic bond then serves as an emotional input for behavioral
decisions, encouraging more willing interactions with agents. This mechanism demonstrates how the
information processing sequence - from perception through emotion to behavior - shapes human -AI
interaction outcomes.
H4: Following the cognitive -affective processing sequence of information processing theory,
empathy between users and GenAI agents mediates the relationship between perceived characteristics
of GenAI agent ((a) social personalization, (b) functional custom ization, and (c) emotional affordance)
and interactive willingness.
2.6 The Moderating Role of Clue quantity
Research has established that cognitive load significantly influences how users process and respond to
technological interactions. The variation in clue quantity represents different levels of information processing
demands on users ’ cognitive resources. Zhang et al. (2020) demonstrated that multiple visual elements

10
compete for limited attention resources, suggesting that increased information density may affect user
perception (Y. Zhang, Xiao, & Nicholson, 2020 ). Building on this, Nguyen et al. (2022) found that agent
systems imposing higher cognitive loads resulted in decreased user satisfaction compared to simpler interfaces
(Nguyen, Sidorova, & Torres, 2022). Barta et al. (2023) further revealed that when cognitive load is effectively
managed, such as through AR technology, users show increased willingness to engage with the system (Barta,
Gurrea, & Flavián, 2023 ). Therefore, i ncreased information clues can enhance cognitive elaboration and
provide users with richer contextual understanding. With more information clues, users can better appreciate
the sophisticated features of GenAI agents, leading to more thorough processing of  their characteristics (H.
Liu, et al., 2024). Specifically, higher clue quantity offers more evidence of the agent’s social personalization,
functional customization, and emotional affordance capabilities, reinforcing users ’ recognition and
appreciation of these features  (Islam, Laato, Talukder, & Sutinen, 2020 ). This enhanced understanding and
appreciation should strengthen the positive relationship between perceived characteristics and users ’
interactive willingness. Therefore, we propose that increasing clue quantity will amplify the positive
relationship between perceived GenAI agent characteristics and interactive willingness.
The selection of clue quantity as our key moderating variable is grounded in several critical theoretical
and practical considerations within GenAI research. First, unlike traditional AI systems that operate with
predetermined response patterns, GenAI inte ractions are fundamentally characterized by their information -
dependent nature (Cheng, et al., 2024). The quantity of informational clues represents the cornerstone variable
that differentiates GenAI from conventional systems, as these agents dynamically construct responses based
on available contextual information (Y. Wang, et al., 2025). Second, recent empirical evidence from Liu et al.
(2024) demonstrates that information clue quantity functions as a crucial boundary condition in human-AI
interaction outcomes (H. Liu, et al., 2024 ). Third, from a methodological perspective, clue quantity offers
superior operationalization benefits compared to alternatives such as interaction frequency or complexity, as
it can be precisely measured and manipulated in experimental settings (Hung, et al., 2025 ). Finally, from a
practical standpoint, clue quantity represents an actionable design variable that developers can directly
manipulate, unlike more abstract constructs, making our findings immediately applicable to industry practices
(Andersen, et al., 2025; Bouteraa, et al., 2024). We posit:
H5: The clue quantity positively moderates the relationship between perceived GenAI agent
characteristics ((a) social personalization, (b) functional customization, and (c) emotional affordance)
and interactive willingness , such that the positive relationship becomes stronger as clue quantity
increases.
The relationship between cognitive load and emotional processing is particularly relevant in human -AI
interactions. Huh et al. (2023) ’s “masking effect” theory demonstrates how excessive information elements
can interfere with information processing in working memory  (Huh, Nelson, & Russell, 2023 ). Arsenyan et
al. (2023) found that managing cognitive load effectively enhances users ’ cognitive fluency and emotional
responses (Arsenyan & Piepenbrink, 2023 ). Similarly, Spatola et al. (2022) revealed that cognitive load
influences how users mentally represent and emotionally connect with robots  (Spatola & Chaminade, 2022).
These studies suggest that clue quantity, by affecting the richness and depth of available information, can

11
enhance users ’ capacity to form emotional connections with GenAI agents. Accordingly, increased
information cues in computer -mediated communication can compensate for the absence of nonverbal cues,
allowing for deeper emotional connections over time (Islam, et al., 2020). In the context of GenAI interactions,
higher clue quantity provides users with more contextual information to understand the agent ’s social,
functional, and emotional characteristics, facilitating more profound empathetic responses. As users receive
more evidence of the agent’s capabilities through increased information exchanges, they can develop stronger
emotional understanding and connection with the agent  (H. Liu, et al., 2024 ; Y. Wang, et al., 2025 ). This
information-empathy enhancement suggests that increasing clue quantity will strengthen the positive
relationship between perceived GenAI characteristics and empathy development.
Moreover, prior studies examining alternative moderating variables revealed significant limitations that
further justify our focus on clue quantity. For instance, Luo et al. (2023) explored user expertise as a potential
moderator but found inconsistent effects across different interaction contexts  (Luo, et al., 2023 ). Similarly,
Kim et al. (2024) investigated interaction duration as a moderator but encountered methodological challenges
in establishing causality (H. Kim & Park, 2024 ). In contrast, our extensive preliminary analysis comparing
multiple potential moderators (including user familiarity, system complexity, and interaction frequency)
demonstrated that clue quantity exhibited the strongest and most consistent moderating effects. This empirical
evidence, combined with the theoretical foundations previously discussed, provides compelling support for
our selection of clue quantity as the optimal moderating variable for understanding the complex relationships
in our model. Therefore, we propose:
H6: The clue quantity positively moderates the relationship between perceived GenAI agent
characteristics ((a) social personalization, (b) functional customization, and (c) emotional affordance)
and empathy, such that the positive relationship becomes stronger as clue quantity increases.
In summary, this study proposes a comprehensive theoretical model grounded in information processing
theory’s sequential stages of stimulus -cognition-emotion-behavior. The model integrates three key
components: perceived GenAI agent characteristics ((a) social personalization, (b) functional customization,
and (c) emotional affordance) as initial stimuli, psycho logical responses (empathy) as emotional processing
outcomes, and behavioral outcomes (interaction willingness) as final behavioral responses. The moderating
role of clue quantity is incorporated to reflect how increased information richness during processing can
enhance both the direct effect of perceived characteristics on interactive willingness and their indirect effect
through empathy.  We term this the “Perceived Characteristics of GenAI  Agent-Empathy-Interactive
Willingness” (PCoGenAI-E-IW) theoretical model, which systematically captures the intrinsic mechanism
under user-AI interaction, as illustrated in Fig. 2-1.

3.1 Data Selection and Acquisition
The emergence of large language models such as ChatGPT has catalyzed the rapid evolution of
sophisticated agent applications. These GenAI-powered agents not only demonstrate enhanced conversational
capabilities for daily task assistance but also pave the w ay for creating personalized virtual companions that
cater to individual needs and creative aspirations. GenAI agents have established themselves as the preferred
solution for facilitating interactive dialogues among various available agents.  The launch of Character.AI in
October 2021 marked a watershed moment, democratizing access to personalized AI characters. At its core,
Character.AI features an AI role-playing agent that has fostered a dynamic community where users can interact
with anime characters, celebrities, and custom -created personas. This innovative interactive experience
unlocks boundless opportunities for users to engage in authentic and enjoyable conversations with their
preferred characters.
Drawing upon community data from Character AI, this research investigates how the perceived attributes
of GenAI agents influence users ’ engagement propensity. Specifically, utilizing a Python web crawler, this

3.2 Data Preprocessing
The Character AI community serves as a creative platform where users design and interact with AI agents,
fostering dynamic conversational experiences. Our dataset encompasses comprehensive user -generated
content, including user profiles (avatars, IDs, nicknames), post information (introductions, timestamps, textual
content, AI agent images), interaction details (reviewer profiles, comment timestamps, comment content), and
engagement metrics (likes, comment counts). To maintain data quality and analytical focus, we implemented
a systematic filtration process, excluding posts lacking comments or involving multiple/virtual characters.

dimensions from the original posts. This targeted approach enables a more precise deconstruction and
examination of the relevant interaction patterns between users and GenAI agents.
3.2.1 Word-to-Vector Model
Word embedding models are founded on the linguistic principle that words sharing similar contextual
neighbors carry related meanings. We implemented Word-to-Vector (Word2Vec), a neural network model
that learns word meanings by analyzing their context within documents. The model processes text to predict
neighboring words, ultimately representing each word as a numerical vector that captures its semantic
relationships. A visual representation of the Word2Vec model is shown in Fig. 3-2.
Using Python ’s gensim library, we trained the Word2Vec model with specific parameters: 300 -
dimensional vectors, a 5-word context window, and a minimum word frequency threshold of 5 occurrences.
This configuration enables the model to convert words into dense vector representat ions. We then utilized
cosine similarity calculations to measure word associations, constructing social and functional word lists by
selecting the top 200 most semantically similar words from the post content for each category.

3.3.1 Users’ Interactive Willingness
We assess users ’ interactive willingness through the cumulative number of likes on their posts, which
serves as a quantifiable behavioral indicator of engagement (J. Yu, et al., 2024 ). In the context of human -
GenAI agent interaction, the “likes” metric extends beyond conventional social media engagement
measurements, representing users’ deliberate choices to engage with and validate AI-generated content. This
behavioral indicator captures active participation rather than passive consumption, providing an objective
measure of users’ propensity to interact with GenAI agents. To address the pronounced positive skewness in
the distribution of like counts, we apply logarithmic transformation, ensuring approximate normality for robust
statistical analysis of interactive patterns.
3.3.2 Perceived Characteristics of GenAI Agent
(1) Social Personalization of GenAI Agent
We employ a systematic text analysis approach to measure social personalization aspects. Beginning with
“social” and “personalized” as seed words, we train a Word2Vec model on our post corpus to identify
semantically related terms. This embedding -based method reveals contextually relevant vocabulary within
GenAI interactions. Using these expanded semantic criteria to filter the corpus, we create a focused dataset
that specifically captures social personalization-related content.
Based on this filtered corpus, we apply STM with an optimal topic number K=5 (semantic coherence: -
118.5, held-out likelihood: 6.48, residuals: 39.8). This analysis enables us to identify distinct latent topics
representing different dimensions of social personalization. The resulting topic distributions provide
quantitative measures of how GenAI agents demonstrate personalized social characteristics, with each topic
capturing specific patterns of user-perceived social personalization.
(2) Functional Customization of GenAI Agent
Similar to our measurement of social personalization, we employ a text mining approach to quantify
functional customization features. Using “functional” and “customized” as initial seed words, we leverage
Word2Vec embeddings trained on our corpus to detect semantically proximate terms. This method identifies
domain-specific vocabulary that characterizes functional customization in GenAI interactions. We then utilize
these expanded lexical criteria to extract relevant text segments, creating a refined datase t that encapsulates
instances of functional customization.
We subsequently conduct STM analysis on this curated corpus, determining an optimal configuration of
K=4 topics (semantic coherence: -120.3, held-out likelihood: 6.52, residuals: 41.2). This modeling approach
unveils distinct thematic dimensions of functional customization, generating topic proportions that
quantitatively represent how GenAI agents adapt the ir functional capabilities to user needs. Each identified
topic cluster captures unique aspects of customization patterns in agent-user interactions.

(3) Emotional Affordance of GenAI Agent
Following a distinct measurement approach for emotional affordance, we employ the Facial Action
Coding System (FACS) to evaluate GenAI agents’ emotional expressions. By analyzing Action Units (AUs)
in each AI-generated facial representation, we map these facial muscle movements to six discrete emotional
states: happiness, sadness, anger, fear, surprise, and disgust. This systematic coding enables us to quantify the
emotional affordance patterns exhibited by GenAI agents, providing standardized measures of their designed
affective capabilities.
3.3.3 Empathy
We operationalize empathy by measuring the textual similarity between original posts and their
corresponding user comments (Arsenyan & Piepenbrink, 2023; Y. S. D. Zhang & Noels, 2023). This approach
stems from the theoretical understanding that user comments indicate cognitive and emotional resonance with
the post content. When users encounter GenAI agent content that aligns with their mental state, they tend to
produce comments reflecting this alignment (Park, Yim, Chung, & Lee, 2023; Wei, Yu, & Li, 2021). The
textual similarity between posts and comments thus serves as a proxy for empathetic connection, with higher
similarity scores suggesting stronger emotional and cognitive alignment (Jiang, Zhang, & Pian, 2022). We
employ computational linguistic techniques to analyze the semantic overlap between each post and its
associated comments, providing a quantitative measure of empathetic engagement in human-AI interactions.
3.3.4 Clue Quantity
We operationalize clue quantity through the word count of GenAI agent posts, providing a
straightforward measure of information volume in each interaction instance. This simple yet effective metric
captures the raw amount of textual content generated by the GenAI agent, reflecting the quantity of potential
engagement cues available to users (Arsenyan & Piepenbrink, 2023 ). By measuring post length, we can
systematically assess how the volume of information provided by GenAI agents influences user engagement
patterns and interaction dynamics.
3.4 Fuzzy-set Qualitative Comparative Analysis(fsQCA)
Prior research has shown that relationships between variables can be complex, and by using combinations
of variables known as “configurations or recipes”, desired results can be derived using fuzzy-set qualitative
comparative analysis (fsQCA) (Abou-Foul, Ruiz-Alba, & López-Tenorio, 2023; Chakraborty, Kar, Patre, &
Gupta, 2024; Fiss, 2011; Mehran, Olya, Han, & Kapuscinski, 2020 ). Given the potential complexity of the
relationship between the causal conditions (social personalization, functional customization, and emotional
affordance) and the desired outcome variable (user ’s willingness to interact), this study will use fsQCA to
determine an adequate configuration for triggering user’s willingness to interact, as shown in Fig. 3-5.
As explained by (Chakraborty, et al., 2024; Fiss, 2011), fsQCA is an ensemble theory technique used to
study complex social phenomena. In fsQCA, the role of a priori predictors is defined based on the properties
of other predictors, and each factor may play a positive or negative role in predicting the expected outcome,
depending on the nature of the other predictors. This finding represents a  principle of equality in which the
outcome can be realized by a combination (configuration) of many potential means or causal factors. Since

4.3.2 Result Analysis
(1) Direct Effect
Based on the PLS-SEM analysis results presented in Table 4 -5, this study finds that the perceived
characteristics of GenAI Agents (social personalization, functional customization, and emotional affordance)
significantly influence users’ interaction willingness, thereby providing empirical support for hypotheses H1a,
H1b, and H1c. According to CASA theory, humans automatically apply social interaction norms to computer
systems. For social personalization, “user-centered needs and daily interaction” and “mystery and unknown”
significantly affect users’ willingness to interact. When AI systems accurately understand and respond to users’
daily needs, users naturally view them as social interaction objects  (Foster, McLelland, & Wallace, 2022; F.
Zhang, Pan, & Lu, 2023 ). Meanwhile, the moderate “sense of mystery” displayed by the system stimulates
users’ desire to explore, similar to the process of getting to know new friends in interpersonal relationships
(Chaturvedi, et al., 2023; Schoner-Schatz, Hofmann, & Stokburger-Sauer, 2021).
In terms of functional customization, “diverse experiences and personal satisfaction” and “user
adaptation and ease of use” demonstrate significant effects. When AI systems provide personalized services
and convenient experiences, users perceive them as in teractive partners capable of understanding and

26
responding to individual needs. This social -oriented perception encourages users to engage with AI systems
following patterns similar to human-to-human communication (G. Zhang, Chong, Kotovsky, & Cagan, 2023).
The strong effect of functional customization suggests that the ability to adapt and provide personalized
experiences is crucial in establishing meaningful user-AI interactions (Yoon, Vonortas, & Han, 2020).
The emotional affordance analysis reveals nuanced effects on user -AI interaction patterns. When AI
systems display elements that evoke positive emotions (such as pleasant surprise) through features like
accurate recommendations or helpful suggestions, user s show increased willingness to engage. Similarly,
when users experience anticipatory responses (such as uncertainty or mild anxiety) while exploring new AI
capabilities, they tend to maintain higher engagement levels. However, interactions that trigger ne gative
emotional states (such as frustration or anger) , when the AI misinterprets requests or provides irrelevant
responses, significantly decrease users’ willingness to continue the interaction. These response patterns align
with CASA theory, demonstrating how users ’ emotional reactions to AI systems mirror their typical social
responses in human interactions (Yam, et al., 2021).
The research indicates that users interact with AI systems as they would with social partners, with social
personalization, functional customization, and emotional affordance jointly constructing the social attributes
of AI systems. Functional customization shows the strongest influence, suggesting that personalized services
and usability are key factors in promoting users ’ view of AI as social partners  (Yam, et al., 2021 ). The
differentiated effects of emotional affordance reveal that users exhibit emotional response patterns similar to
interpersonal interactions. These findings support the applicability of CASA theory in generative AI contexts
and provide direction for designing more humanized AI interaction systems.
(2) Meditating Effect
Based on the PLS-SEM analysis results shown in Table 4 -5, these results provide empirical support for
hypotheses H2a, H2b, H2c, H3, H4a, H4b, and H4c.  From an Information Processing Theory perspective,
empathy mediates how users process and respond to three key GenAI agent  characteristics (social
personalization, functional customization, and emotional affordance ) ultimately affecting their interaction
willingness.
During the encoding stage, empathy helps users process these AI characteristics as meaningful social
signals rather than mere technical features. When agent demonstrates social personalization (such as
remembering user preferences and interaction history), empathy enables users to encode these features as
signals of social understanding  (Joseph & Gaba, 2020 ; W. Yu, et al., 2021 ). Similarly, functional
customization is processed not just as technical adaptation but as responsive caregiving through empathetic
encoding. Emotional affordance features are encoded as genuine emotional expressions rather than
programmed responses (Filieri, Lin, Li, Lu, & Yang, 2022).
In the storage stage, empathy facilitates a sophisticated integration process where AI characteristics are
stored in users ’ social cognitive schemas. Social personalization experiences are stored not simply as
interaction records, but as meaningful relationship markers through empathetic processing, creating cognitive
representations that mirror human relationship memories  (Joseph & Gaba, 2020 ). Functional customization
experiences are stored as evidence of mutual understanding, where both the practical utility and emotional

27
satisfaction of being understood are integrated into users ’ mental models of supportive relationships.
Emotional affordance experiences undergo transformation through empathy, where programmed responses
are stored as authentic emotional exchanges, contributing to users’ perception of AI as a socially present entity
(Liu, Zhao, Wang, & Qiu, 2024; Schumacher, Goukens, Geyskens, & Nielsen, 2024b).
During the retrieval stage, empathy activates these stored representations through a multi-layered process.
When encountering new interactions, users don ’t simply recall past experiences but actively use them to
interpret current interactions. The retrieval of customization experiences is facilitated through emotional
congruence, where users more readily access memories of AI support that matched their emot ional needs
(Park, et al., 2023 ; W. Yu, et al., 2021 ). Similarly, emotional affordance memories are retrieved within a
context of emotional authenticity, where empathy helps users access both the content and the felt experience
of past emotional exchanges. This empathy -enhanced retrieval process creates a cu mulative effect,
strengthening the cognitive -emotional associations between AI characteristics and meaningful social
interaction, ultimately leading to more natural and fluid human-AI relationships.
This cognitive processing chain explains how empathy transforms technical AI characteristics into social
experiences: social personalization becomes relationship building, functional customization becomes mutual
understanding, and emotional affordance becomes authentic emotional connection. Through this empathy -
mediated process, users develop increased willingness to engage with AI systems in sustained, meaningful
interactions.
(3) Moderating Effect
Based on the PLS-SEM analysis results shown in Table 4 -5, these results provide empirical support for
hypotheses H5a, H5b, H5c, H6a, H6b, and H6c. Based on cognitive load theory and information processing
theory, clue quantity demonstrates profound moderating mechanisms on three core characteristics of GenAI
agent.
In the social personalization dimension, the moderating effect of clue quantity reflects the dual nature of
cognitive processing. When users receive an appropriate amount of social cues, this information effectively
supports working memory processing capacity, helping users construct clear cognitive models of agent’s
social characteristics. This reasonable cognitive load promotes users ’ understanding and memory of agent’s
social attributes, enhancing the naturalness and coherence of social interaction. Cognitive load theory suggests
that learning and understanding are optimal when information processing demands match individual cognitive
resources (Fu, Li, Liu, Pirkkalainen, & Salo, 2020). In perceiving social personalization features, appropriate
cues not only reduce cognitive load but also help users better understand and adapt to agent’s social behavioral
patterns through pattern recognition and schema construction. This optimized cognitive processing
significantly enhances users’ social presence and interaction willingness (S. Y. Kim, Park, Kim, Kim, & Seo,
2022).
In the functional customization dimension, the moderating effect of clue quantity is closely related to the
effective allocation of cognitive resources. Sufficient function-related cues reduce extraneous cognitive load,
allowing users to allocate more cognitive resources to deep understanding and application of functions.
Cognitive load theory emphasizes that learning outcomes are optimal when learning material presentation

28
aligns with human cognitive architecture  (Islam, et al., 2020 ). In functional customization scenarios,
reasonable cue provision reduces users ’ cognitive effort, making it easier to understand and master agent’s
functional characteristics (Souchet, Philippe, Lourdeaux, & Leroy, 2022). This optimization of cognitive load
not only improves functional usage efficiency but also enhances user experience by reducing cognitive
pressure. When users can easily understand and use functions, they are more inclined to explore Agent ’s
advanced features, gaining deeper personalized experiences (Spatola & Chaminade, 2022). This virtuous cycle
further strengthens the positive impact of functional customization on users’ interaction willingness.
In the emotional affordance dimension, the moderating effect of clue quantity exhibits more complex
cognitive processing mechanisms. Cognitive load theory indicates that emotional information processing
consumes substantial cognitive resources. The optimal level of cues may vary significantly for different types
of emotions. Proces sing positive emotions (like happiness and surprise) typically requires relatively fewer
cognitive resources, and moderately increasing emotional cues can enhance emotional resona nce without
significantly increasing cognitive burden. Processing complex emotions (like sadness) requires more cognitive
resources for deep processing, where the moderating effect of cue quantity needs more precise control to avoid
cognitive overload. For  negative emotions (like fear, anger, and disgust), excessive cues may lead to
accumulation of negative emotions and excessive consumption of cognitive resources, reducing users ’
willingness to interact (Islam, et al., 2020). This differentiated cognitive processing pattern suggests that when
designing GenAI agent’s emotional expression, we need to fully consider the cognitive load characteristics of
different emotion types and precisely regulate the quantity and presentation of cues  (Spatola & Chaminade,
2022).
This in-depth analysis based on cognitive load theory reveals the internal mechanisms of clue quantity’s
moderating effects, providing important theoretical guidance for GenAI agent design. By optimizing cue
quantity control, we can effectively balance users’ cognitive load, enhance interaction experience quality, and
ultimately achieve more natural and efficient human -machine interaction (Souchet, et al., 2022 ; Spatola &
Chaminade, 2022).

Based on the results of the necessity and sufficiency analyses of fsQCA (Abou-Foul, et al., 2023; Fiss,
2011; Woodside & Zhang, 2013 ), we obtained a total of 10 parsimonious solutions and 21 intermediate
solutions. In order to facilitate the understanding, when presenting the specific results, we follow the form of

that the condition can be either present or absent; the large circle is the core condition (i.e., the condition that
exists in both parsimonious and intermediate solutions), and the small circle is the auxiliary condition (the
condition that exists in the intermediate solution only). Coverage is an important measure of empirical
relevance in QCA studies, reflecting the empirical tangency or importance of the grouping, similar to R 2 in
regression analysis (Fiss, 2011). The results of the histogram paths are shown in Table 4-10. Where, when a
condition is labeled as ● or ●, it means that the condition exists, while if a condition is labeled as ×, it means

that the condition does not exist, ● represents the core condition, and ● or × represents the edged condition.
Antecedent configurations with the same core condition were grouped together so as to summarize them into
the following patterns of triggering user interacti ve willingness (Chakraborty, et al., 2024 ; Fiss, 2011 ).
Although we obtained 21 intermediate solutions, after we combed through them, we found a total of 32
configurational paths (as shown in Table 4-10), which is due to the fact that when outputting intermediate and
simple solutions, there exists one intermediate solution corresponding to multiple simple solutions.  For

edge conditions are different, respectively. Subsequently, we discuss each configuration in detail.
(1) Mode 1: Mystery and the Unknown , Diversified Experiences and Personal Fulfillment , Surprise,
Empathy, and Clue Quantity as the core condition driving high level of users’ Interactive Willingness
In this mode, there are 4 configurations, including M1a, M1b, M1c and M1d. In M1a, ~Fear plays the
role of edge condition, that is, it does not contain Fear factor. In configuration M1b,  ~Fear, ~Anger and
~Disgust play the role of edge conditions, while in M1c, Happiness, ~Sadness, ~Anger and ~Disgust play the
role of edge condition s. In configuration M1d, User -Centric Desires and Daily Engagements, Happiness,
~Anger, ~Disgust play the role of edge conditions. These 4 configurations together form the first mode of
cultivating a high level of users ’ willingness to interact , i.e., the “Mystery and the Unknown-Diversified
Experiences and Personal Fulfillment-Surprise-Empathy-Clue Quantity” configuration.
Mode 1 showcases a finely tuned user experience design strategy that effectively encourages a high level
of interaction by integrating elements of mystery, diverse experiences, surprise, empathy, and an optimal
number of cues  (Arsenyan & Mirowska, 2021 ; Chakraborty, et al., 2024 ; Chaturvedi, et al., 2023 ). This
configuration stands out for its ability to weave cognitive stimulation, emotional resonance, and functional
needs into a comprehensive framework that facilitates multi -layered interactions. The element of “mystery
and the unknown” plays a critical role in sparking users’ curiosity and desire to explore—key drivers of human
behavior. When GenAI agent can offer novel and unpredictable interactions, they not only heighten the appeal
of engagement but also deepen user involvement, as people are naturally inclined to solve puzzles and discover
new information  (Do, et al., 2023 ; Gambino, Fox, & Ratan, 2020 ). “Diverse experiences and personal
fulfillment” emphasizes the importance of personalization and variety, which suggests that AI must be capable
of recognizing and responding to individual differences, delivering services that cater to the unique needs of
each user while also maintaining a sense of novelty. Such tailored interactions not only boost user satisfaction
but also strengthen their willingness to continue engaging with the agent (Jiang, et al., 2022). Surprise, as an
emotional component, is a powerful tool for eliciting positive emotional responses. By introducing timely
surprises, AI can enhance users’ sense of enjoyment and excitement, thereby reinforcing the emotional bond

consider in design. These include the allure of mystery and the unknown, the presence of moderate fear, the
avoidance of strong negative emotions, and the provision of diverse and personalized experiences (Jing Wen,
et al., 2020; Kang & Kang, 2024). Additionally, it is crucial to strike the right balance between daily relevance
and challenge. By understanding these complex mechanisms of emotional interaction, AI designers can more
effectively meet users’ emotional needs, offering personalized and engaging experiences that deepen users ’
willingness to interact with AI.

5. Conclusions and Discussions
This research provides insights into the field of GenAI agents and deepens our understanding of user
needs and expectations, especially regarding the complexity of GenAI interactions. Through the development
of the “Perceived Characteristics of GenAI Agent -Empathy-Interactive Willingness” (PCoGenAI-E-IW)
theoretical framework, we examine how users perceive and interact with GenAI agents. Building upon (Do,
et al., 2023), we identify three key dimensions: social personalization, functional customization, and emotional
affordance. Our methodology employs Structural Topic Modeling (STM) to analyze textual data from the
Character.AI platform, revealing nuanced aspects of social personalization and functional customization. Then,
we integrate the Facial Action Coding System (FACS) to analyze emotional features, categorizing them into
happiness, surprise, sadness, fear, anger, and disgust. Through PLS -SEM analysis, we investigate the main
effect, the mediating role of empathy and the moderating effect of cue quantity. Furthermore, this study uses
a configurational analysis approach, identifying 10 distinct configurations that explain how different
combinations of variables lead to users ’ willingness to interact with GenAI agents. The emergence of these
10 configurations demonstrates the complex, non -linear relationships among the variables. Our findings not
only validate the effectiveness of our multi -modal approach in analyzing the intricate connections between
user perceptions and behaviors but also prov ide insightful empirical evidence for marketing strategy
formulation, service experience optimization, and theory development.
5.1 Findings
Firstly, based on the research of (Do, et al., 2023 ), this study conceptualizes three distinct key
dimensions of GenAI agent characteristics: social personalization, functional customization, and
emotional affordance. Unlike traditional AI interaction studies that focus primarily on functional aspects, our
framework emphasizes a more comprehensive understanding of user-AI relationships. Social personalization
extends beyond basic personalization to capture an agent ’s ability to adapt to users ’ complex social profiles,
creating authentic interactional intimacy (Joseph & Gaba, 2020; Yoon, et al., 2020). While previous research
mainly considered surface-level customization, our functional customization dimension emphasizes deep user
autonomy in shaping interaction parameters and conversation styles. Most notably, our conceptualization of
emotional afford ance transcends conventional emotional recognition to encompass a sophisticated
understanding of “emotional intelligence” , enabling agents to not only detect but actively participate in
emotional exchanges. This three -dimensional framework represents a significant advancement from
traditional human -computer interaction models by integrating social, functional, and emotional elements,
providing a more holistic understanding of how users perceive and interact with GenAI agents.
Secondly, in the framework of in-depth profiling of GenAI agent features, this study employs STM to
deconstruct the fine-grained dimensions of social personalization and functional customization. Based on
the textual data of user-generated content, social personalization can be categorized into user-centric desires
and daily engagements, as well as  mystery and the unknown. Meanwhile, functional customization can be
divided into users’ adaptation and ease of use, as well as  diversified experiences and personal fulfillment.

47
Additionally, using AI -generated images, we appl y the FACS to capture the emotional characteristics
displayed by the AI agent ’s avatar. Our study decomposes six basic emotional expressions  (J. Yu, et al.,
2024)—happiness, surprise, sadness, fear, anger, and disgust—thereby empowering GenAI agents with more
delicate and realistic emotional expressions, and pushing the AI interaction experience towards a higher level
of emotional intelligence and personalized adaptation.
Thirdly, this study proposes the PCoGenAI-W-IW model to examine the mechanism through which
users’ perceptions influence their interactive behaviors with GenAI agents. Our model reveals two key
pathways: a direct path where users’ perceptions of agent characteristics influence their interactive willingness,
and an indirect path mediated by empathy. This dual-pathway mechanism demonstrates how empathy serves
as a crucial bridge, transforming users ’ cognitive perceptions into emotional connections that enhance their
willingness to interact. Furthermore, we identify c lue quantity as a significant moderator  that shapes the
strength of both pathways. The presence of rich interaction cues amplifies users ’ perceptual experiences and
strengthens their empathetic responses, ultimately leading to enhanced interactive intentions. This
comprehensive framework, incorporating both mediation and moderation effects, provides valuable insights
into the psychological dynamics underlying user-GenAI interactions and extends our understanding beyond
traditional linear models.
Fourthly, through fuzzy -sets qualitative comparative analysis (fsQCA), we identified multiple
configurations that influence users ’ interactive willingness with GenAI agents. Among the 10 simple
solutions and 21 intermediate solutions visualizing 32 different configurations, empathy and clue quantity
consistently emerged as core conditions. Empathy reflects the agent ’s ability to understand and respond to
user emotions, while clue quantity represents the richness and transparency of provided information. The se
two factors demonstrate universal importance across all configurations  (Liu-Thompkins, et al., 2022 ),
regardless of their combination with other variables like social personalization, functional customization, or
emotional affordance. The consistent presence of empathy and clue quantity suggests that successful GenAI
interactions require both emotional intelligence and comprehensive information provision (Borghi & Mariani,
2022). This finding emphasizes the necessity of balancing emotional engagement with informational clarity
in AI system design, ensuring that agents can effectively meet users ’ emotional and cognitive needs while
fostering meaningful interactions.
Fifthly, fsQCA revealed that Diversified Experiences, Personal Fulfillment, Happiness, and Surprise
frequently emerged as core conditions across configurations, highlighting their fundamental role in driving
GenAI interaction willingness.  This prevalence stems from their deep connection to human psychological
needs: Diversified Experiences and Personal Fulfillment satisfy users ’ inherent desire for exploration and
achievement through personalized interactions  (Wei, et al., 2021 ), while Happiness creates a positive
atmosphere that naturally encourages deeper engagement (Borghi & Mariani, 2022). Notably, Surprise plays
a unique role by capturing attention and stimulating curiosity through unexpected positive experiences. The
frequent co-occurrence of these elements suggests a synergistic effect , diverse experiences generate more
positive emotions, which in turn motivate users to seek more varied interactions, creating a self -reinforcing

48
cycle that sustainably enhances users ’ willingness to interact with GenAI agents  (Milcent, Kadri, & Richir,
2022).
Sixthly, fsQCA results highlighted the frequent occurrence of “User-Centric Desires and Daily
Engagements” “Sadness” “Fear” as core conditions, revealing the expanding role of GenAI agents in
addressing complex human needs. The prominence of User-Centric Desires and Daily Engagements reflects
users’ growing expectations for AI to deeply integrate into their daily lives, offering personalized services
beyond simple task execution. Notably, the emergence of negative emotions like Sadness and Fear as core
conditions demonstrates GenAI ’s evolving capacity in emotional intelligence and mental health support
(Borghi & Mariani, 2022 ). This suggests that successful GenAI agents must not only process information
effectively but also recognize and respond appropriately to users ’ emotional states, providing comfort and
support when needed. These findings indicate a shift towards more humanized AI services that balance
practical assistance with emotional understanding, ultimately fostering deeper user connections and
engagement (Milcent, et al., 2022).
5.2 Theoretical Contributions
First of all, this study innovatively adopts the research paradigm of “multi-modal big data analysis &
PLS-SEM & fsQCA”, marking a significant methodological advancement in exploring GenAI’s impact on
user interactive willingness.  Compared to traditional questionnaire -based methods, this three -pronged
approach combines the comprehensive scope of multi-modal big data (incorporating text and image data), the
statistical rigor of PLS-SEM for testing theoretical relationships, and the configurational precision of fsQCA
for identifying complex causal patterns. Unlike traditional single -method studies relying solely on
questionnaires and structural equation modeling, this integrated paradigm enables both variable-centered and
case-oriented analyses, capturing nuanced interaction patterns and revealing previously unrecognized variable
combinations that lead to high user engagement. The approach not only validates existing theories but also
uncovers complex configurational effects, providing valuable insights for AI system design and future
research directions in human-AI interaction.
Secondly, this study delves into and defines the perceived features of GenAI agents, providing a novel
perspective for understanding human-AI interaction. According to the research of (Do, et al., 2023), we not
only consider the three -dimensional analysis of the attractiveness of service robots —social dimension,
interaction dimension, and emotional dimension —but also integrate key research findings from (Luo, et al.,
2023) and (Zhou, et al., 2023). Through the above analysis, this study categorizes users’ perceptions of GenAI
agents into three core characteristics: social personalization, functional customization, and emotional
affordance. By comprehensively analyzing and integrating these dimensions, this study enriches the
knowledge base of GenAI agent design, laying a theoretical foundation for optimizing human -AI interaction
interfaces and fostering the personalized development of intelligent systems in the future. It is of great
significance for advancing AI technology to serve our society better.
Thirdly, this study constructs a “Perceived Characteristics of GenAI Agent -empathy-interactive
willingness” (PCoGenAI-E-IW) theoretical model, offering a feasible explanation for the current research

49
gap between users ’ perception of GenAI features and their actual interaction behaviors. Based on the
“affordance-psychological outcome-behavioral outcome” framework (Do, et al., 2023), the model investigates
the intrinsic mechanisms through which perceived characteristics of GenAI agents  (social personalization,
functional customization, and emotional affordance ) transform into actual interaction willingness. While
existing literature primarily focuses on either agent features or user behaviors in isolation, this theoretical
contribution provides an integrated perspective on the cognitive-behavioral transition in human-AI interaction.
By examining how perceived agent characteristics influence psychological states and subsequent behavioral
responses, the model advances both theoretical foundations for human -AI interaction research and practical
guidance for designing more effective AI systems.
Fourthly, this study extends information processing theory by examining how users cognitively
process GenAI features through empathy -mediated mechanisms. In the encoding stage, empathy enables
users to interpret AI characteristics as meaningful social signals rather than mere technical features. During
storage, empathy facilitates the integration of these characteristics into users ’ social cognitive schemas as
meaningful relationship markers. In the retrieval stage, empathy activates these stored representations, where
users actively use past experiences to interpret current interactions  (Joseph & Gaba, 2020 ; Li, et al., 2021 ).
This theoretical extension demonstrates how empathy transforms technical AI characteristics into social
experiences: social personalization becomes relationship building, functional customization becomes mutual
understanding, and emotional affordance bec omes authentic emotional connection. Through this cognitive
processing chain, we provide insights for designing AI systems that align with human cognitive patterns.
Fifthly, this study reveals the configurational effects and conditional dependencies in Perceived
Characteristics of GenAI Agent  by exploring the effects of different combinations of variable levels on
users’ willingness to interact, which transcends the limitations of a single causality and provides insights
into how multiple variables work together to shape users ’ behaviors and attitudes in different contexts.
Specifically, this study explores how different combinations of social, functional, and emotional dimensions
of GenAI characteristics influence  user interaction willingness. Rather than examining simple correlations,
we reveal how these characteristics work together in composite conditions to shape user experience. This
configurational perspective provides a theoretical foundation for designing more effective and humanized AI
systems that consider users’ specific needs and preferences. This contribution fills gaps in existing literature
and promotes more humanized AI technology development by emphasizing the importance of multivariate
combinations in complex interaction contexts.
5.3 Practical Implications
First, this study demonstrates the feasibility, effectiveness, and superiority of multi-modal analytics
for processing large-scale unstructured datasets in the field of GenAI. We chose the Character.AI platform
as the object of study, a rich and high -quality data source previously under -explored by academia. This
platform not only contains extensive user -generated textual content but also provides vivid scenarios of
interaction with AI-generated characters. By applying disruptive techniques such as text mining, structured
topic modeling, and facial recognition, we extract valuable insights from a large amount of user -generated

50
text and AI-generated images. Existing studies on generative AI and AI-generated content usually use methods
such as questionnaires or experimental methods. Our study breaks through the limitations of these traditional
methods by leveraging data diversity and advanced analytical techniques. We utilize objective data analysis
methods to reveal the complex connections between user perception and interaction behaviors, which provides
valuable insights for developing marketing strategies and optimizing service experiences.
Second, w e emphasize the importance of strengthening users ’ self-identity in the field of human -
computer interaction. Users demonstrate higher interaction willingness with AI agents that provide diverse
experiences and personalized gratification, highlighting the need for intrinsic motivation and emotional
connections (Ben Saad & Choura, 2023 ). When AI dynamically adjusts based on user preferences and
historical interactions, users feel understood and develop self -growth through novel experiences. This
enhances their self -confidence and control, fostering deeper AI relationships. Developers sho uld therefore
focus on effective personalization that addresses both basic needs and self-determination (Schumacher, et al.,
2024a). Future AI should create an emotionally intelligent environment that affirms users ’ self-worth,
enhancing satisfaction and relationship depth.
Third, we reveal the importance of balancing information processing and quantity in AI design.
Following “optimal information quantity”  strategy, providing sufficient but not overwhelming information
helps users grasp key points and form deep memories  (Spatola & Chaminade, 2022 ). Designers should
recognize users’ cognitive thresholds in different scenarios and adjust information presentation accordingly
to prevent overload. Through structured visualization and interactive elements, information becomes more
accessible and engaging, awakening users ’ curiosity (Li, et al., 2021 ). Optimizing information quality and
quantity enhances users ’ perceived value of GenAI agents, promoting deeper interaction willingness and
emotional connections, ultimately creating a more intimate human -computer interaction experience  (Liu-
Thompkins, et al., 2022).
Fourth, we demonstrate the critical of empathy in enhancing user experience and deepening human-
computer interaction, which lays a vital foundation for achieving “emotional intelligence”.  Empathy is
critical in enhancing user experience and achieving emotional intelligence in human-computer interaction (S.
Wang, Paulo Esperança, & Wu, 2023 ). By precisely designing AI ’s emotional expression capabilities,
developers can create systems that effectively recognize and respond to users ’ emotional states. Through
advanced emotion recognition, deep learning, and NLP technologies, AI can provide personalized emotional
support and engage in empathetic communication (Wongkitrungrueng & Assarut, 2020). The combination of
user profiling and contextual analysis enables AI to dynamically adjust emotional expressions, ensuring
appropriate responses that foster trust and meaningful interactions, ultimately achieving emotional intelligence
in AI systems (Chakraborty, et al., 2024).
5.4 Limitations
Although this study has revealed several interesting and valuable findings, there are key limitations that
future research should address to broaden and deepen our understanding. First, the reliance on a dataset from
a single online platform may constrain the generalizability of the findings  (Bai, et al., 2024 ). In order to

51
explore a more comprehensive theoretical framework, future studies should actively incorporate diversified
data sources to encompass a wider range of contexts and user groups.
Second, there are limitations in the data processing stage. This study excluded all non -English texts
during the textual data cleaning process, which may lead to information loss to some extent. For instance,
emoticons can convey significant emotional states (Leung & Chan, 2017). In future research, we can explore
important information revealed by non-textual predictions.
Third, the cross -sectional nature of our data limits our ability to observe how user perceptions and
interactions with GenAI agents evolve over time. Longitudinal studies would be valuable to track how these
relationships develop as users gain more experience with GenAI systems and as the technology itself advances
(Luo, et al., 2023).
Fourth, while our study focused on empathy as a key mediating mechanism, future research could explore
additional psychological processes that may influence user interactions with GenAI agents, providing a more
comprehensive understanding of the complex dynamics underlying these interactions.
