Abstract
This paper investigates the role of Artificial Intelligence (AI), Digital
Financial Capabilities (DFC) and Customer Trust in digital FinTech
Innovation in emerging markets. The statistical research design used
was quantitative, cross-sectional, and a questionnaire with five-
point Likert scales was used for the collection of data, reaching 300
adult FinTech users. Intelligent automation, predictive analytics,
personalized financial services, fraud detection and AI powered
decision-support were used to evaluate the impact of AI. The impact
of AI was evaluated using the following trends: intelligent
automation, predictive analytics, personalized financial services,
fraud detection, and AI powered decision-support. DFC identified
the skills that the users required to access, understand, assess and
safely utilize digital financial technologies and Customer Trust
addressed security, reliability, transparency, privacy and
responsible service practices. The concept of FinTech Innovation
stood for better digital financial products and service delivery,
customer experience, personalization and efficiency. Descriptive
findings indicated positive perceptions on all constructs ranging
from mean scores of 3.92 (SD = 0.71) for AI to 3.79 (SD = 0.76) for
Customer Trust and 3.95 (SD = 0.69) for FinTech Innovation for
DFC. The reliability tests showed low item-total correlation, with the
overall .24 item-total alpha range from .89 (AI) to .92 (FinTech
Innovation), and .91 (Customer Trust), .87 (DFC), and .89 (AI). The
results highlighted that the capital markets in emerging markets need
technological intelligence, users who can apply the technology and
trustworthy digital environments. It is crucial to address issues of
customer-friendly technology governance, customer security,
privacy safeguarding, transparency, and transformational digital
financial education in the governance of inclusive and sustainable
innovations in the financial sector with the help of artificial
intelligence technologies, the study has pointed out.

This study has practical importance since it offers a holistic view of technological and customer
dynamics influencing FinTech innovation in the emerging markets. The authors' analysis of AI, digital
financial skills, and customer trust helps to refine the understanding of why some financial technologies
working with AI are more successful in their innovation efforts than others. The outcomes can help financial
institutions and FinTech companies create more accessible, reliable, secure and customer-friendly AI-
powered services. The study can also help facilitate the policies of policy makers and financial regulators
concerning digital financial literacy, responsible use of AI, consumer protection, data security, and trusted

previous research on FinTech by linking innovation based on Artificial Intelligence to capabilities and trust
among users in the particular context of emerging markets.

3. Research Methodology
3.1 Research Design
The study was conducted using quantitative, cross-sectional research design, stressing the
understanding of Artificial Intelligence, Digital Financial Capabilities, Customer Trust and FinTech
Innovation in emerging markets. As the constructs selected were measured by numerical responses from
participants and the perception of each participant was measured by standardized items from the
questionnaire, the quantitative approach was used. This cross-sectional study enabled the researcher to obtain
data from the respondents at one point in time, enabling the researcher to get a systematic picture of the
respondents' experience of AI-based financial technologies.
3.2 Population of the Study
Participants included adults in the EM who are using FinTech and/or digital financial services. The
audience included digital banking, mobile banking, digital wallet users, online payment platforms, digital
lending platforms, investment platforms, AI-powered financial and other technological products and services.
The use of digital financial technologies, such as internet and mobile payment platforms, was vital to accessing
sufficient feedback from the population on AI integration, digital financial skills and customer confidence.
3.3 Sample Size
There were 300 respondents in the study. All samples were of adult users who had had some real-life
experience with at least one type of digital financial and/or FinTech service. Target group members had to
have an age of 18 years and more and be well versed in digital financial technologies in order to be able to
understand the contents of the questionnaire. The number of 300 respondents was sufficient to explore the
four constructs and gain reliable empirical information about innovation in emerging markets that uses AI
enabled FinTech services.
3.4 Sampling Technique
The method of studying was Purposive convenience sampling technique. The selection of key
informants was done by purposive sampling to ensure that the key informants have the necessary experience
from digital financial technologies and by convenience sampling methods to gain access to the key informants
that are active in digital and online networks. The questionnaire was shared via social media, online forums,
professional networks, and other digital channels.
3.5 Data Collection Procedure
The data collection technique used was a primary data collection method called structured self-
administered questionnaire. The questionnaire was sent out by the online method to targets. The participants
were informed of the purpose of the research and type of their participation before they participated in the
research. They informed them that they were not to be forced to take part and that their responses were
confidential. The questionnaire was kept up online till a total of 300 full and usable responses were received.
The answers were subsequently gathered, classified and tabulated for statistical analysis.

The questionnaire was broken down into two parts. The first section gathered respondents'

5. Discussion
The potential of Artificial Intelligence in FinTech has now come to the forefront to write and automate
documents, to analyse complex data, to tailor services, reduce fraud and even to speed up decision making.
The influence of artificial intelligence in financial service delivery is revolutionizing the conventional design
of financial service experience affecting the traditional financial products offered and how they are used. The

other areas in which digital finance can help enable AI innovation. Moreover, AI applications drive

systems. Therefore, the use of AI must be regarded less as just an automation tool and more as an overall
technological competency that can be used for continuous innovation among financial services.

But trust is definitely a central pillar of the FinTech innovation landscape: after all, digital financial
services are dependent on customers believing in the financial transactions, technologies, data management
and service providers. With the rise of AI, there are new elements of trust that come into play as users may
not be aware of algorithms and their application, or where their information is coming from, how it is being
used, or even how it is being collected. The trust component in FinTech has gained significant focus in recent
times, where its multi-dimensional nature is dependent on perception regarding technological expertise,
security, transparency, privacy, and responsible organizational behaviours (Devlin et al., 2025). Moreover, Ai
insights & data from financial services suggests that there's a direct correlation with customer trust and the
usage of AI answers in finance: when customers feel the tools & platforms are secure & reliable. (Kumar et
al., 2025).
This technological progress interweaves with the capacities and expertise of users and organisations
operating in digital financial ecosystem and in turn influences FinTech innovation. Currently, the institutional
environment does not lend itself easily to the implementation of AI innovations, especially when it cannot
sufficiently provide transparency and accuracy that helps to reduce uncertainty, as well as proper digital
framework and skilled operators, they need to be implemented. If supported by financial literacy, availability,
security and adequate institutional conditions and arrangements, emerging economies have proven to be able
to contribute to the wider financial inclusion by means of digital financial technologies (DFT) over the past
few years (Akanfe et al., 2025). Furthermore, the study reveals that in the context of FinTech in emerging
market contexts, considerations must not only focus on greater financial accessibility and service delivery
improvements, but also on facing the new challenges of privacy protection, cyber security, and consumer
protection (Ngo et al., 2025).
When we think about it from a holistic perspective, it's clear that we need to consider all three - AI
capability, digital financial skills, and customer trust. A person who offers insight into both the technology
and the cases it can help to improve and has the ability to apply it effectively. Trust by customers is required,
as it generates an important relational platform to access the digital financial services in the future. Recently
there was evidence that disputes this interconnected approach as they provide evidence for interdependent
relationships between digital financial literacy, participative engagement with FinTech and financial
capability which can drive a successful functioning digital financial ecosystem (YUN, 2026). At the same
time, trust will serve as a key factor in ensuring that technological evolution does not create uncertainty and
resistance, but an even larger customer base, (Sholevar & Bachmann, 2025). The key take-away is that the
FinTech development exercise in these emerging markets must be a human-centred exercise where technology
development and building capabilities goes hand-in-hand, accompanied by measures of safeguarding against
social and psychological risks, ensuring a high level of privacy safeguard and ensuring transparency.
6. Conclusion
The research drew on three key areas that are ripe for innovations and developments in FinTech:
Customer trust, Digital financial skills and Artificial Intelligence. AI and digital was the enabler that allowed
more automation, personalisation, decision support, fraud detection and delivery of financial services, and
digital financial capabilities to access, understand, evaluate and use technology-based financial services. The
importance of customer trust remained crucial to fostering trust on digital platforms, especially those
concerning financial services and online transactions. The results corroborated a holistic approach, where
technological development is not sufficient to ensure sustainable development of FinTech. Capable users and
reliable digital environments like improved innovation also required reliable methods of protection and open
service delivery, as well as responsible data practices. The study thus reinforces the importance of bringing
together the innovation of AI technologies, digital financial education, consumer protection, privacy
protection, and trust measures for emerging markets' digital financial transformation to be inclusive, secure
and viable.
