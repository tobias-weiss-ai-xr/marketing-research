Determinants of Usage Continuance and Payment Intentions for Generative Artificial

Abstract
Based on perceived value theory, this study developed a framework for examining how various
dimensions of perceived value affect users’ intentions to continue using and paying for
generative artificial intelligence (AI) tools. An online survey was then conducted among 330
Taiwanese students, including ChatGPT and Claude users, to validate the developed framework.
The survey yielded several crucial findings. First, enjoyment, social influence, relevance, and
responsiveness significantly and positively influenced the students’ intention to use ChatGPT;
however, among these factors, only enjoyment significantly influenced their intention to use
Claude. Second, price significantly influenced payment intentions for both AI tools, whereas
relevance affected payment intention for only Claude. Third, enjoyment, social influence, and
responsiveness did not significantly influence payment intentions for either AI tool. These
insights can help developers and researchers understand students’ behavioural intentions towards
generative AI chatbots. They also offer valuable guidance for improving the design and user
experience of educational AI systems and for developing targeted marketing strategies for such
systems.

1. Introduction
Advances in artificial intelligence (AI) technology have fundamentally altered the landscape
of daily life (Arce-Urriza et al., 2025; Gupta et al., 2024; Hughes et al., 2025; Kim & Lee, 2024;
Lee et al., 2023). The emergence of generative AI has led to the development of services such as
AI chatbots, AI image generators, and AI writers, which can simplify complex tasks and support
creative activities (Gratas, 2023; Gupta et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2025; Nguyen & Elbanna,
2025). These technologies are being increasingly adopted across various industries because of
their robust data analysis capabilities and automated workflow processes, which enhance
productivity, convenience, and creative thinking (Hughes et al., 2025; Storey et al., 2025;
UserTesting, 2025).
Chatbots have rapidly become one of the most prominent generative AI applications (Arce-
Urriza et al., 2025; Ferraro et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2025; Ma & Huo, 2023; Triantafyllopoulos
et al., 2024; UserTesting, 2025). For instance, ChatGPT attracted over 100 million users within
only a few months after its launch by OpenAI in December 2022, becoming the fastest-growing

report created by Digital Web Solutions (2025), ChatGPT’s website traffic peaked at

and sustained user engagement. Furthermore, OpenAI’s paid services, including ChatGPT
Enterprise, Team, and Edu, have exceeded one million subscribers, suggesting that generative AI
chatbots are not only widely adopted in enterprise settings but are also experiencing increasing
demand in educational contexts (Wang & Xue, 2024). In education, AI chatbots improve
interactivity, content quality, and student motivation, thus contributing to the creation of smarter
learning environments (Chen et al., 2020; Hughes et al., 2025; Pillai et al., 2023). Given these
developments, investigating key factors influencing users’ behavioural intentions towards AI
chatbots is crucial.
When confronted with a novel service or product, individuals’ cognition, attitude, values,
and behaviours are shaped by the technological characteristics of the product or service (Akram
et al., 2024; Kaur et al., 2020; Lin & Huang, 2024). Perceived value reflects individuals’
assessment of the overall utility of a product through a cognitive comparison process (Lin et al.,
2020; Wang & Lin, 2024; Zeithaml, 1988). This perception strongly influences consumers’
behavioural intentions (Chen et al., 2018; Hsu et al., 2015; Lin et al., 2020; Turel et al., 2010;
Wang & Lin, 2024). Thus, analysing perceived value is essential for understanding the

acceptance and usage of emerging technologies (Akram et al., 2024; Dam, 2025; Wei et al.,

Generative AI chatbots are reshaping platform experiences, which in turn influence users’
decisions to adopt premium versions (Chatbot Marketing, 2025; DeepConverse, 2025;
UserTesting, 2025). Studies have indicated that when users perceive generative AI chatbots to
have value, they are more likely to continue using these chatbots and to pay for upgrades (Chang
et al., 2019; Chen et al., 2022; Huang et al., 2025; Singh et al., 2021; UserTesting, 2025).
Perceived value can be assessed across dimensions such as enjoyment, quality, price, and social
influence (Ciunova-Shuleska et al., 2022; Rizomyliotis et al., 2022; Seo & Um, 2023; Singh et
al., 2021). Value perceptions affect attitudes and behaviours and thus are often key drivers of
users’ decision-making processes (Akram et al., 2024; Dam, 2025; Chang et al., 2019; Chen et
al., 2022; Lu & Shi, 2025; Wang & Lin, 2024).
In the educational domain, generative AI chatbots are transforming students’ learning
experiences and providing personalised support to address learning challenges, thereby
enhancing learning efficiency and effectiveness (Almassaad al., 2024; Garrel & Mayer, 2023; Yu
et al., 2024; Wang & Xue, 2024). Understanding students’ behavioural intentions towards these
tools can help institutions and developers optimise their design and implementation (Hughes et
al., 2025; Yilmaz & Yilmaz, 2023). Usage continuance and payment intentions are crucial factors

for evaluating the long-term potential of generative AI in education.
Although prior studies (Almassaad al., 2024; Garrel & Mayer, 2023; Yu et al., 2024; Wang
& Xue, 2024) have explored the role of AI chatbots in enhancing learning motivation and
outcomes, limited research has been conducted to determine how students’ perceived value
influences their behavioural intentions, particularly their willingness to continue using and
paying for AI chatbot services. Most studies on this topic have emphasised technological features
or learning outcomes, with relatively few studies systematically examining the value-based
mechanisms that drive sustainable AI chatbot adoption in an educational context. To address this
gap, the present study investigated students’ intentions to continue using and pay for generative
AI chatbot services, particularly exploring how perceived value affects these intentions.
Specifically, this study addresses the following research questions:
RQ1: How does perceived value influence students’ intention to continue using generative
AI chatbots?
RQ2: How does perceived value influence students’ intention to pay for generative AI
chatbots?

Generative AI is an emerging technology in which deep learning models—such as long

models—are used to generate relevant content (Arce-Urriza et al., 2025; Ferraro et al., 2024;
Nguyen & Elbanna, 2025; Storey et al., 2025; Triantafyllopoulos et al., 2024).
As the capability of generative AI to execute complex tasks continues to grow, individuals

ChatGPT is a generative AI chatbot that was launched by OpenAI in 2022. Compared with
earlier technologies, ChatGPT has considerably more advanced language comprehension,
dialogue generation, and creative capabilities (Ferraro et al., 2024; Triantafyllopoulos et al.,
2024). ChatGPT can generate various types of textual content, from simple conversations to
complex articles; effortlessly summarize and highlight key points in text; and even write and
debug code (Dwivedi et al., 2021; Garrel & Mayer, 2023; Gupta et al., 2024; Storey et al., 2025).
Currently, ChatGPT is based on the GPT-5 model, which has multimodal communication

The success of ChatGPT has prompted major technology companies to invest resources in

2.2 Perceived value
The formation of customer-perceived value is based on an overall assessment of the benefits

ARTICLE IN PRESS
ARTICLE IN PRESS

derived from a product or service relative to its perceived costs (Kim et al., 2007; Lin et al.,
2020; Wei et al., 2025; Zeithaml, 1988). Thus, when customers use or purchase a product or
service, they evaluate its overall utility on the basis of how well it meets their needs (Kim et al.,
2007; Lin et al., 2020; Lu & Shi, 2025; Zhang et al., 2024). Perceived value has a significant
direct effect on customer purchase behaviour (Habibi & Zakipour, 2022; Hsiao & Chen, 2016;
Hsu et al., 2015; Lin et al., 2020; Rather & Hollebeek, 2021; Singh et al., 2021). The constituent
elements of perceived value may vary among different consumers. Value is most commonly
defined as the ratio of quality to price; however, considering only these two factors when
evaluating value is overly simplistic (Lin & Lu, 2015; Wang & Lin, 2024).
Sheth et al. (1991) posited that user-perceived value encompasses five dimensions:
functional value, social value, emotional value, epistemic value, and conditional value. Their
research indicated that consumer purchase decisions are influenced by not only a product’s
functionality but also the singular or combined effects of the aforementioned five value
dimensions. Sweeney and Soutar (2001) subsequently reconceptualised perceived value into four
dimensions: emotional value, social value, quality (performance or quality) value, and monetary
(price or value for money) value. Emotional value refers to the affective utility derived by
consumers from a product or service. Social value denotes utility that enhances a consumer’s
social self-concept. Quality value represents the utility derived from a product’s or service’s

ARTICLE IN PRESS

perceived quality and expected performance. Finally, monetary value signifies the utility gained
from reduced short- or long-term costs.
In research on information technology usage behaviour, numerous scholars have extended
the measurement dimensions of perceived value based on value theory, adapting this construct to
various research contexts. Perceived value strongly influences users’ decision-making processes;
however, its dimensional framework differs across contexts and user groups. Several scholars
(Chang et al., 2019; Dam, 2025; Seo & Um, 2023; Singh et al., 2021; Wei et al., 2025; Zhang et
al., 2024) have argued that conceptualising perceived value as a single-dimensional construct is
overly simplistic and narrow. Instead, they emphasise that perceived value should be perceived
as a multidimensional construct encompassing functional, emotional, social, and monetary
aspects. Building on this perspective, the present study adopted a multidimensional approach to
perceived value, integrating insights from the literature and extending this construct to the
context of generative AI chatbots. Table 1 presents a summary of representative studies that have
employed perceived value theory as a foundational framework, indicating how different value
dimensions vary across contexts and influence users’ behavioural intentions. In the present study,
an integrated research model grounded in perceived value theory was developed to identify key
emotional, social, quality, and monetary value dimensions that drive users’ intentions to continue
using and pay for generative AI chatbot services.

3.1 Effects of perceived value on usage continuance and payment intentions
3.1.1 Emotional value
Emotional value is defined as the affective utility derived by consumers from a product or
service (Sweeney & Soutar, 2001). When a person experiences positive emotions such as
happiness or joy while using a product or service, they are more likely to perceive it as valuable
(Akram et al. 2024; Lin & Lu, 2015; Seo & Um, 2023). Numerous studies have considered
enjoyment to be a component of emotional value and have found that emotional value influences
people’s intention to continue using services or upgrade to paid versions of these services (Chen
et al., 2018; Lu & Shi, 2025; Zhang et al., 2024).
Enjoyment can be used to evaluate the degree of pleasure experienced by individuals (Kim

et al., 2007; Lin & Lu, 2015; Seo & Um, 2023). A person is more willing to use a product or
service frequently when they perceive its usage to be enjoyable (Akram et al. 2024; Kim et al.,
2007; Masao & Salehudin, 2023; Shin & Choo, 2011). Furthermore, people may be more willing
to opt for paid versions of a product or service if they derive pleasure from using it (Dam, 2025;
Ha et al., 2023; Hsiao & Chen, 2016; Kang et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2024).
The aforementioned discussion indicates that individuals’ intention to continue using or to
pay for generative AI services is stronger when they experience a sense of enjoyment during the
usage of such services. Therefore, the following hypotheses were proposed:
H1. Enjoyment positively influences users’ intention to continue using generative AI
chatbots.
H2. Enjoyment positively influences users’ intention to pay for generative AI chatbots.

3.1.2 Social value
Social value is product- or service-derived utility that enhances a consumer’s social self-
concept (Sweeney & Soutar, 2001). Consumers perceive a product or service to be valuable
when they believe that its usage can elevate their status, image, or recognition within their social
circle (Hamari et al., 2020; Hsiao & Chen, 2016; Lin et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2024). Many
scholars have divided social value into different factors for investigation. For instance, Ajzen

ARTICLE IN PRESS
ARTICLE IN PRESS

(1991) explored social value by considering subjective norms, emphasising that individuals’
behaviours are influenced by the opinions of their significant others, such as family, friends, or
colleagues. Venkatesh et al. (2003) examined social value by considering social influence,
investigating the degree to which individuals perceive the influence of surrounding groups. To
understand the influence of groups on individuals’ information technology acceptance and usage
behaviour, the present study investigated social value in depth by considering social influence.
In the context of the present study, social influence refers to the degree to which consumers
perceive that people important to them (such as family or friends) believe that they should use
information technology (Lin et al., 2020; Wei et al., 2025). When many people in an individual’s
surroundings are using a particular product or service, the individual is often strongly influenced
by peer opinions to begin or continue using that product or service (Huang, 2020; Kiran &
Sailaja, 2025; Malik & Singh, 2022). Moreover, individuals’ decisions to purchase a product or
service are often influenced by others’ opinions and recommendations (Chen et al., 2018;
Kancherla et al., 2023; Lin et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2024). Therefore, users’ intention to
continue using or to pay for generative AI chatbots is influenced by recommendations from
people close to them, such as family, friends, or colleagues. Consequently, the following
hypotheses were proposed:
H3. Social influence positively affects users’ intention to continue using generative AI

H4. Social influence positively affects users’ intention to pay for generative AI chatbots.

3.1.3 Quality value
Quality value is the utility perceived by consumers from the quality and expected
performance of a product or service (Hsiao & Chen, 2016; Sweeney & Soutar, 2001). People
consider a product or service valuable when its quality meets their expectations (Amin et al.,
2024; Golalizadeh et al., 2023). Researchers have identified quality value as a crucial factor that
directly influences users’ behavioural intention towards information technology (Hsiao & Chen,
2016). Quality value can be evaluated in terms of factors such as performance, reliability,
relevance, and responsiveness (Amin et al., 2024; Niu & Mvondo, 2024). The user experience of
generative AI chatbots is influenced by the relevance of the content generated by these chatbots
and their response speed (Arce-Urriza et al., 2025; Jiang et al., 2022; Syarifudin et al., 2024).
Therefore, the present study considered content relevance and responsiveness as key aspects of
quality value.
Content relevance indicates the degree of correspondence between the generated content
and the input query, and responsiveness indicates the timeliness of the content generation (Liu et
al., 2023; Niu & Mvondo, 2024). In the context of AI chatbots, service quality is a crucial factor

influencing users’ behavioural intention. For instance, the ability to answer questions (in terms of
relevance and immediacy) affects users’ satisfaction, which in turn influences their purchase
intention (Jiang et al., 2022; Syarifudin et al., 2024). One study found that users’ intention to
continue using bicycle-sharing platforms is higher when these platforms can quickly respond to
the users’ needs (Shao et al., 2020). Liu et al. (2023) determined that users’ continued usage of
chatbot services is more likely when these services provide relevant information rapidly.
Moreover, users are more willing to pay for a product or service if they perceive that it has high
quality and improves their work efficiency (Ahmad & Zhang, 2020; Przegalinska et al., 2019;
Syarifudin et al., 2024).
The aforementioned discussion indicates that users’ intention to continue using or pay for
generative AI chatbots is influenced by the relevance and timeliness of the responses generated
by the chatbots. Therefore, the following hypotheses were proposed:
H5a. Relevance positively influences users’ intention to continue using generative AI
chatbots.
H5b. Responsiveness positively influences users’ intention to continue using generative AI
chatbots.
H6a. Relevance positively influences users’ intention to pay for generative AI chatbots.

3.1.4 Monetary value
Monetary value is the utility derived by consumers from a perceived reduction in the cost of
a product or service (Sweeney & Soutar, 2001). Users are more likely to consider a product or
service to be valuable if they perceive its expected benefits to exceed its costs (Kim et al., 2007).
Research has indicated that consumers are more likely to be attracted to a product or service
when it offers good value for money, reflected in reasonable pricing and high functional cost-
effectiveness (Aslam et al., 2021; Kiran & Sailaja, 2025; Malik & Singh, 2022, Singh et al.,
2021).
Price refers to the monetary amount that consumers are willing to pay for a product or
service (Malik & Singh, 2022). Users are more inclined to pay for a product or service when they
perceive that purchasing it would yield them considerable benefits (Hsiao & Chen, 2016;
Mäntymäki et al., 2020; Yapp & Yeap, 2023). The aforementioned text indicates that users are
more willing to pay for generative AI chatbots when they perceive that these chatbots provide
value for money and considerable benefits. Therefore, the following hypothesis was proposed:
H7. Price positively influences users’ intention to pay for generative AI chatbots.

3.2 Effect of usage continuance intention on payment intention
Usage continuance intention refers to a user’s intention to use a product or service again
(Akram et al., 2024; Pang et al., 2020). Intention to pay is often considered a crucial factor in
examinations of intention to continue using a product or service (Hamari et al., 2020). In the
context of this study, intention to pay is defined as the willingness of a regular user to become a
paid subscriber (Mäntymäki et al., 2020; UserTesting, 2025). Users are more likely to be willing
to pay for upgrades to a product or service if they have a strong intention to continue using the
product or service (DeepConverse, 2025; Digital Web Solutions, 2025; Gabriel et al., 2023;
Hamari et al., 2020). Therefore, the following hypothesis was proposed:
H8. Users’ intention to continue using generative AI chatbots positively influences their intention
to pay for these chatbots.

The content of the questionnaire used in this study was adapted from the relevant literature

6. Discussion
On the basis of perceived value theory, the present study conducted an in-depth
investigation into students’ intentions to continue using and pay for generative AI chatbots. We
constructed a comprehensive theoretical model encompassing four dimensions of value:
emotional value (enjoyment), social value (social influence), quality value (relevance and
responsiveness), and monetary value (price). The results of this study indicate that the
aforementioned four dimensions significantly influence users’ intentions to continue using and
pay for generative AI chatbots. The findings of this study not only enrich the application of
perceived value theory in the domain of emerging technologies but also provide a
multidimensional perspective for understanding users’ behaviour in relation to AI chatbots. Our
empirical findings are detailed in the following sections.

6.1 Effects of emotional value on usage continuance and payment intentions
The results of the present study indicate that enjoyment (i.e., emotional value) significantly
affects users’ intention to continue using AI chatbots. This finding is consistent with those of
previous studies, which have indicated that the degree of pleasure, happiness, satisfaction, and
enjoyment experienced by users during their interaction with information technology has a
significant positive effect on their intention to continue using the technology (Akram et al. 2024;
Kim et al., 2007; Lin & Lu, 2015; Shin & Choo, 2011; Wei et al., 2025). Our study also explored
the differences in user behaviour between ChatGPT and Claude. We found that the effect of
enjoyment on usage continuance intention was stronger for Claude than for ChatGPT. This
finding may be attributable to Claude’s superior user experience design, which provides users
with a more entertaining and engaging interaction process.
The results of this study suggest that enjoyment does not significantly influence users’
intention to pay for paid features of a generative AI chatbot. This finding aligns with that of
Hsiao and Chen (2016), who reported no significant association between the enjoyment
experience of nonpaying users and their intention to pay in the future. The aforementioned
finding may be attributable to the similarity in entertainment features between free and paid
versions, leading users to perceive that additional payment for extra features or an enhanced

ARTICLE IN PRESS
ARTICLE IN PRESS

experience is unnecessary.
To gain a deeper understanding of the nuances of user experience and its influence on users’
behavioural intention, this study conducted a qualitative survey to collect user feedback on their
experiences. First, the respondents reported distinct emotional responses to the visual designs of
the two platforms. Claude’s warm colour scheme was described as being pleasant and relaxing,
whereas ChatGPT’s pure white background was perceived as being professional but somewhat
impersonal. This difference in visual experience may have influenced the respondents’ overall
emotional engagement. Second, some users reported that they formed a strong emotional
connection with Claude, perceiving its response style as being similar to conversing with a friend
who adheres to moral standards. This emotional connection may explain why enjoyment had a
stronger significant positive effect on usage continuance intention for Claude users than for
ChatGPT users. However, the aforementioned emotional connection did not necessarily translate
directly into a stronger intention to pay.

6.2 Effects of social value on usage continuance and payment intentions
The usage and recommendation of AI chatbots by significant others influences users’
behavioural intention regarding these chatbots (Hamari et al., 2020; Malik & Singh, 2022; Shloul
et al., 2024). The present study found that social influence had a significant positive effect on

ARTICLE IN PRESS
ARTICLE IN PRESS

usage continuance intention for ChatGPT users but not for Claude users. This finding may be
attributable to ChatGPT being a pioneering natural language processing tool with excellent
content comprehension and generation capabilities, which have resulted in it attracting extensive
media attention and corporate interest. This high level of exposure has not only enhanced the
social influence of ChatGPT but also facilitated the formation of ChatGPT user communities for
exchanging knowledge (Shloul et al., 2024; Storey et al., 2025). This network effect may
reinforce ChatGPT’s standing in users’ minds, thereby promoting its continued usage. By
contrast, limited community interaction occurs on Claude, which can be attributed to multiple
factors, including the platform’s characteristics, market strategies, or differences in user
demographics. This limited community interaction may restrict the diffusion of social influence,
thereby weakening its effect on usage continuance.
The present study indicates that social influence does not significantly affect intention to
pay for either platform. This finding contrasts with those obtained from major technology
adoption frameworks, such as the unified theory of acceptance and use of technology, the theory
of reasoned action, and the theory of planned behaviour, which identify social influence as a
strong predictor of behavioural intention. A plausible explanation for this discrepancy is that,
when making payment decisions for emerging technologies, users may prioritise rational
evaluations and individual needs over peer norms or social pressures. The collected respondent

ARTICLE IN PRESS
ARTICLE IN PRESS

feedback also suggests that decisions regarding paid services are shaped more by perceived
benefits, functional utility, and overall assessments of value than by external expectations or
validation.
Overall, these findings suggest that in the context of generative AI chatbots, social influence
plays a limited role in shaping payment intention, whereas personal utility, holistic evaluations,
and functional applicability are dominant factors influencing this intention. This interpretation is
consistent with the findings of studies indicating that as technologies mature, the role of social
influence diminishes, whereas individual assessments and utilitarian considerations become more
prominent (Graf-Vlachy et al., 2018; Seo & Um, 2023; Wang & Lin, 2024).

6.3 Effects of quality value on usage continuance and payment intentions
This study indicates that relevance significantly affects users’ intention to continue using
generative AI chatbots. This finding aligns with those of Arce-Urriza et al. (2025), Amin et al.
(2024), and Liu et al. (2023). However, our study reveals that responsiveness does not
significantly influence usage continuance intention. This nonsignificant finding warrants further
reflection. In the current AI service landscape, rapid response has become a baseline expectation
rather than a source of competitive differentiation (UserTesting, 2025). Because most
mainstream platforms already provide consistently fast responses, users may perceive

ARTICLE IN PRESS
ARTICLE IN PRESS

responsiveness as a ‘hygiene factor’. Such factors are necessary to meet minimum service
expectations but are insufficient for strengthening attachment or promoting continued use
(Alharbi et al., 2025; Hung et al., 2025; Thakur, 2025).
This study also found that relevance and responsiveness have significant positive effects on
intention to continue using ChatGPT; however, they do not have significant effects on intention
to continue using Claude. This discrepancy may be attributable to ChatGPT’s emphasis on
content relevance and responsiveness; it sometimes even provides multiple answer options,
which increases user interactivity and choice, thereby promoting usage continuance intention. By
contrast, Claude’s relatively cautious and conservative answering strategy may result in
responses that do not fully meet users’ needs, which may negatively affect the overall user
experience.
Regarding payment intention, prior studies (Ahmad & Zhang, 2020; DeepConverse, 2025;
Przegalinska et al., 2019; UserTesting, 2025) have suggested that users are more willing to pay
when chatbots deliver highly relevant responses that meet their needs. Our findings partially
support this viewpoint. In this study, relevance significantly influenced intention to pay for
Claude but not ChatGPT. Therefore, although relevance is a key driver of willingness to pay, its
effect may depend on how users perceive the platform’s overall value proposition and
differentiation strategy. Specifically, when relevance is perceived as a standard feature rather

ARTICLE IN PRESS
ARTICLE IN PRESS

than a premium benefit, it might be insufficient for stimulating payment intention.
Finally, this study indicates that response speed does not significantly affect intention to pay
for either platform. This finding likely reflects a market reality in which users are already
satisfied with current response speeds and therefore do not expect substantial improvements from
paid upgrades. This insight is consistent with the findings of studies suggesting that once
technological attributes reach baseline adequacy, their effects on monetisation decisions diminish
unless upgrades provide distinctly superior value (Alharbi et al., 2025; Thakur, 2025).
Accordingly, firms aiming to enhance users’ intention to pay should emphasise differentiation
through content quality, personalisation, or unique features rather than relying solely on further
improvements in response speed.

6.4 Effects of monetary value on usage continuance and payment intentions
This study found that monetary value significantly influences users’ intention to pay for
generative AI chatbots. The intention is stronger when users perceive that the benefits acquired
from a chatbot service exceed its fee. This finding aligns with those of prior studies (Chang et al.,
2019; Kim et al., 2007; Mäntymäki et al., 2020), which indicate that users are more likely to pay
for upgraded generative AI chatbot services when they perceive the upgrade fee as being
reasonable and offering good value for money (DeepConverse, 2025; UserTesting, 2025).

This study further reveals that ChatGPT and Claude have a significant positive influence on
users’ intention to pay. This finding may be attributable to the notable differences between the
free and paid features of these platforms, which motivate users to pay for higher-quality services.
For instance, although ChatGPT’s free version allows users to obtain information through
dialogue, upgrading to the paid version offers not only faster and more extensive question-
answering capabilities but also additional features such as file uploads, access to more advanced
models, and the option to use other generative tools. Consequently, after trying the free version,
users evaluate the platform’s paid upgrade on the basis of factors such as price, benefits, and
functionality. If they perceive the required payment as reasonable and can clearly discern the
value of the paid upgrade, they are more likely to opt for the paid version to experience its
enhanced features.

6.5 Correlation between usage continuance intention and payment intention
When users perceive a generative AI chatbot as a platform worthy of continued use, their
intention to pay for additional features may be higher (Arce-Urriza et al., 2025; DeepConverse,
2025; Digital Web Solutions, 2025; UserTesting, 2025). This finding is attributable to users
having developed sufficient familiarity with the platform and formed usage habits. Users are
more likely to opt for paid upgrades if they understand the platform’s characteristics and benefits

and recognise the additional value of its paid features. The decision to pay ensures continued
access to the service and the ability to obtain higher-quality features. However, this study found
that usage continuance intention did not significantly influence intention to pay for ChatGPT or
Claude. A possible reason for this finding is that in the course of continued chatbot use, users
may discover that the free version sufficiently meets their needs, thus perceiving no necessity to
upgrade to the paid service (Aloa, 2025). Consequently, usage continuance intention does not
significantly affect users’ intention to pay for generative AI chatbots.

7. Conclusion
7.1 Academic implications
On the basis of perceived value theory, this study developed a comprehensive theoretical
framework for investigating the key factors influencing users’ intentions to continue using and to
pay for generative AI chatbots. The theoretical contributions of this research are detailed as
follows.
First, this study extended perceived value theory to the context of users’ behavioural
intentions towards generative AI chatbots. On the basis of the theoretical insights of Zeithaml
(1988) and Sweeney and Soutar (2001), we considered enjoyment, social influence, relevance,
responsiveness, and price as representing the different dimensions of values proposed by Sheth et

ARTICLE IN PRESS
ARTICLE IN PRESS

al. (1991) and then included these factors in a theoretical framework including usage continuance
and payment intentions. This framework not only provides a profound understanding of the
characteristics of AI technology but also reflects the complex decision-making process employed
by consumers when they evaluate emerging technologies.
Second, this study found that enjoyment, social influence, and relevance have significant
effects on usage continuance intention; it also determined that relevance and price have
significant effects on intention to pay. These results are in agreement with the findings of
previous research (Kiran & Sailaja, 2025; Shang & Wu, 2017; Singh et al., 2021; We et al., 2025;
Zhang et al., 2024) and deepen the understanding regarding users’ behavioural intentions towards
generative AI chatbots. Notably, this study highlights the differential effects of various
dimensions of value on users’ decision-making related to generative AI chatbots, thus providing
a new perspective for understanding users’ adoption of emerging technologies.
Finally, this study examined the differences in the investigated relationships between
ChatGPT and Claude. This cross-platform analysis broadens the understanding of users’
behavioural intentions towards generative AI applications and indicates how the characteristics
of different AI systems influence user perceptions and behaviours.

7.2 Practical implications

This study examined Taiwanese students’ intention to continue using and to pay for
generative AI chatbots. Thus, it obtained unique insights with various practical implications for
developers and managers of generative AI platforms.
First, the research findings emphasise the importance of the user experience for the student
population. The significant positive effect of enjoyment on usage continuance intention suggests
that platforms should pay particular attention to providing a pleasurable and engaging interactive
experience when targeting younger users. Relevant measures may include designing more
intuitive and visually appealing interfaces as well as offering more personalised and creative
features to enhance enjoyment. Second, the significant positive effect of social influence on
students’ usage continuance intention highlights the importance of peer effects among the student
population. Platforms can consider developing social features specifically targeting the student
demographic, such as study group functionalities, to enhance the usage motivation of existing
users. Third, the significant effects of relevance on usage continuance and payment intentions
underscore the potential applications of AI models in academic and career development.
Platforms should consider developing specialised features tailored to student needs, such as tools
for learning material organisation. Such features may not only increase the platform’s utility but
also attract students to upgrade to paid versions.
Fourth, the significant effect of monetary value (reflected in price perception) on payment

intention highlights the importance of developing pricing strategies that align with students’
financial capabilities. Platforms may adopt measures such as student-exclusive discounts,
flexible subscription periods (e.g. semester-based plans), and modular payment options that
allow students to select services matching their needs and budget. Moreover, platforms can
collaborate with educational institutions to create institutional subscription plans that offer
students affordable and value-driven access to paid generative AI chatbot services.
Finally, the difference in user behaviour patterns between ChatGPT and Claude indicates
that platform developers should formulate differentiated strategies on the basis of their
platform’s unique characteristics and the specific needs of the platform’s target student
demographic. For example, platforms emphasising social features can collaborate with student
and teacher organisations to integrate generative AI chatbots into campus activities. In addition,
platforms focusing on academic applications should invest resources to enhance the
professionalism and accuracy of their AI models in specific subject domains. Overall, the
findings of this study offer valuable insights to the developers of generative AI chatbots for the
educational sector.

8. Limitations and Recommendations for Future Research
Although this study obtained valuable insights regarding students’ behavioural intentions

ARTICLE IN PRESS
ARTICLE IN PRESS

towards generative AI chatbots, it has certain limitations that warrant attention. First, the sample
of this study only comprised Taiwanese students, and this study only focused on AI chatbots
employed in the educational domain. Therefore, caution should be exercised when the findings
of this study are generalised to other populations and AI application domains. Future studies
should be conducted in cross-cultural and cross-domain settings to investigate and compare the
antecedents of users’ behavioural intentions towards generative AI tools in different
environments. Such research would contribute to a more comprehensive understanding of the
commonalities and differences in usage behaviours for generative AI chatbots globally.
Moreover, it would indicate how cultural background, socioeconomic conditions, and
educational systems influence users’ intentions to continue using and to pay for generative AI
chatbots.
Second, this study employed a cross-sectional research design, with samples collected
during a single time period. This method is suitable for analysing user perceptions at a particular
time point but cannot reflect changes in usage behaviour over time. Future studies are
recommended to adopt longitudinal methods involving the long-term tracking of usage
behaviours over time. Such tracking can reveal how users’ behavioural intentions towards
generative AI chatbots evolve and the driving factors underlying these changes. Furthermore,
given the rapidly evolving nature of AI tools, user perceptions and value judgements may shift

ARTICLE IN PRESS
ARTICLE IN PRESS

over time, which further highlights the necessity of longitudinal designs.
Finally, ethical and privacy issues in AI technology usage are becoming increasingly
important topics. Future research can explore users’ attitude towards and concerns regarding AI
chatbots in terms of privacy protection, data security, and academic integrity, including issues of
copyright relating to AI-generated content and potential changes brought about by AI in the job
market. Furthermore, the field of generative AI is evolving at an exceptionally rapid pace, with
new models, features, and applications continually emerging. Consequently, usage patterns and
user perceptions may rapidly shift in response to technological advances, making it challenging
to generalise findings from one point in time to the future. Therefore, future research should
interpret findings in light of this continual evolution and place strong emphasis on the dynamic
and fast-changing nature of the field.
