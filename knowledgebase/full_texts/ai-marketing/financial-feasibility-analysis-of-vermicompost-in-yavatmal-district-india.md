Abstract

The study assessed the financial feasibility and marketing efficiency of vermicompost production in Yavatmal
district, Maharashtra. Primary data for 2025 –2026 were obtained from 60 vermicompost producers selected
from six villages in two tehsils, while additional marketing information was collected from wholesalers,
commission agents and village traders. Financial feasibility was evaluated using the payback period, net
present value, benefit-cost ratio and profitability index, whereas marketing performance was assessed through
marketing cost, market margin, price spread and the producer's share in the consumer's rupee. The reported
payback period was 0.44 years, indicating relatively rapid recovery of the initial investment. The net present
value was Rs. 16,645.82 and the benefit-cost ratio was 1.84, while the profitability index was reported as 5.87.
Three marketing channels were identified. Channel I, comprising direct sales from producer to consumer,
recorded the lowest price spread of Rs. 655.64 per tonne and the highest producer's share in the consumer's
rupee at 93.46 per cent. The producer's share declined to 78.05 per cent in Channel II and 72.43 per cent in
Channel III. Under the surveyed conditions, the findings indicate that vermicompost production had
favourable financial indicators and that shorter marketing channels may improve producer returns by reducing
intermediary costs and margins.

1. Introduction

In India, the total production of vermicompost is estimated to be around 25 to 30 million tonnes per year. The
country has witnessed a steady increase in vermicompost production due to growing awareness of organic
farming, soil health management and sustainable agricultural practices, supported by government schemes such
as Paramparagat  Krishi Vikas Yojana (PKVY), Rashtriya Krishi Vikas Yojana (RKVY) and the National
Mission on Sustainable Agriculture (NMSA). Among the states, Maharashtra ranks first, with an estimated
production of 4.5 –5.0 million tonnes per year; Uttar Pradesh ranks next, producing approximately 4.0 –4.5
million tonnes; and Madhya Pradesh follows, with about 3.0 –3.5 million tonnes. The remaining production is
contributed by other states, including Rajasthan, Gujarat, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Telangana,
Odisha, Bihar, Kerala and others ( Bellitürk, 2018; Oyege & Balaji Bhaskar, 2023; Torun Kayabaşı & Yılmaz,
2021).

Vermicompost has increasingly been examined as a biologically processed organic amendment because it can
improve nutrient availability, microbial activity and soil physical condition while supporting crop production in
low-input and organic farming systems (Bellitürk, 2018; Oyege & Balaji Bhaskar, 2023; Yıldız et al., 2025).
From an economic perspective, the adoption of vermicompost is also associated with enterprise -level income
opportunities, especially where production units are integrated with local input markets and short marketing
chains (Ceyhan et al ., 2015; Kumar et al ., 2021; Satish et al ., 2025). Therefore, assessing both financial
feasibility and marketing efficiency is necessary to understand whether vermicompost production can be
sustained as a viable farm-based or small-scale enterprise in Yavatmal district.

Recent evidence indicates that the agronomic performance of vermicompost is linked to its physicochemical
properties and substrate composition, while its application can influence soil conditions and crop response
across production systems (Choudhary, 2025; Guzmán Camposeco et al., 2024; Manzoor et al., 2024; Rehman
et al., 2023).

Evidence from crop -level economic assessments further indicates that the economic implications of
vermicompost use are context -specific and should be evaluated in relation to production costs and realised
returns (Raimondo et al., 2023).

However, an integrated, location -specific assessment of both financial feasibility and marketing efficiency of
vermicompost production in Yavatmal  district is lacking within the scope of the literature reviewed for this
study. This gap limits the basis for jointly interpreting investment appraisal indicators with channel -wise
marketing costs, price spread and the producer’s share under the local conditions examined.

1.1 Objective

Accordingly, the objective of the present study was to assess the financial feasibility of vermicompost
production and to evaluate the marketing efficiency of the identified marketing channels in Yavatmal district.

2. Methodology

The present study was based on data collected from 60 vermicompost producers selected from six villages in two
tehsils of Yavatmal district. Data were collected using a questionnaire through personal interviews during 2025 –
2026.

methods were used.

3. Results and Discussion

3.1 Financial Viability of Vermicompost

Value (NPV), Benefit -Cosio (BCR), Internal Rate of Return (IRR) and Profitability Index (PI), the results of
which are presented in Tables 1 to 4. The payback period was found to be 0.44 years, indicating that the initial
investment of Rs. 2,831.09 could be recovered within approximately five to six months from the net annual cash
revenue of Rs. 6,345.18, indicating quick capital recovery. As shown in Table 1, the NPV of the enterprise over
the five-year period, discounted at 9 per cent, was estimated at Rs. 16,645.82. Since this value is positive, the
enterprise can be regarded as economically viable, as the present worth of returns exceeds the present worth of

3.2 Marketing of Vermicompost

The marketing efficiency of vermicompost in Yavatmal district is presented in Tables 5, 6 and 7. Three
marketing channels were identified: Channel I - Producer to Consumer; Channel II - Producer to Wholesaler to
Organisation/Consumer; and Channel III - Producer to Village Trader to Wholesaler to Consumer. Table 5
shows that, in Umarkhed and Mahagaon tehsils, the majority of producers sold vermicompost through Channel
I. According to Table 6, the marketing cost incurred by producers was lowest in Channel I, at Rs. 655.64 per
tonne. Table 7 shows that the net price received by the producer was highest in Channel I, at Rs. 9,373.39, while
the price spread was lowest at Rs. 655.64. The producer’s share in the consumer’s rupee was 93.46 per cent in
Channel I, 78.05 per cent in Channel II and 72.43 per cent in Channel III. Thus, Channel I was the most
efficient.

The higher producer’s share and lower price spread in Channel I indicate that direct marketing reduced
intermediary costs and allowed producers to retain a larger proportion of the consumer price. This result is
consistent with the economic logic of shorter marketing channels, in which fewer intermediaries generally
reduce cumulative marketing costs and margins, thereby improving returns to producers (Budrin & Soldatova,
2018; Kumar et al., 2021). However, the feasibility of direct marketing may depend on producers’ ability to
reach consumers consistently, maintain product quality and arrange transport and packaging efficiently. Thus,
although Channel I was the most efficient in the present study, Channels II and III may still be relevant where
producers lack direct access to institutional or retail buyers.

4. Conclusions

Vermicompost production in the surveyed area showed favourable financial and marketing indicators. The
payback period of 0.44 years suggests relatively rapid recovery of the initial investment, while the positive net
present value of Rs. 16,645.82 and benefit -cost ratio of 1.84 indicate that discounted returns exceeded costs
under the study conditions. The profitability index was reported as 5.87, supporting a positive financial
interpretation. Marketing analysis showed a clear advantage for the shortest channel. Channel I, involving direct
sale from producer to consumer, had the lowest price spread of Rs. 655.64 per tonne and the highest producer's
share in the consumer's rupee, at 93.46 per cent. The corresponding shares in Channels II and III were 78.05 per
cent and 72.43 per cent, respectively. These results suggest that shorter marketing channels may improve
producer returns by limiting intermediary costs and margins. The findings should be interpreted within the
surveyed production and marketing conditions of Yavatmal district.

5. Limitations

This study was confined to 60 vermicompost producers from six villages in two tehsils of Yavatmal  district and
reflects the production and marketing conditions observed during 2025 –2026. The marketing assessment was
based on the identified channels and selected intermediaries within the study area. Consequently, the financial
and marketing indicators should be interpreted as location - and period -specific rather than as estimates
applicable to all vermicompost enterprises. Wider geographical coverage and observations across additional
production years would strengthen the generalisability of future assessments.
