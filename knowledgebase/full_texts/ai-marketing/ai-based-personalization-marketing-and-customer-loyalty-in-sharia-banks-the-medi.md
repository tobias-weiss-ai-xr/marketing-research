 The rapid digitalization of Islamic banking has expanded opportunities to
use artificial intelligence (AI) for personalized marketing; however,
personalized services do not necessarily translate directly into customer
loyalty. This study examined the relationship between AI-based marketing
personalization and customer loyalty, with customer experience and trust
examined as individual and sequential mediators. A quantitative
explanatory research design was employed using a cross-sectional survey
of 350 Bank Syariah Indonesia customers in Banyumas Regency. Data
were collected using structured questionnaires and analyzed through
measurement model assessment, path analysis, and mediation analysis.
The findings indicated that AI -based marketing personalization ha d
positive effects on customer experience and trust, whereas its direct effect
on customer loyalty was not statistically supported. Customer experience
had positive effects on trust and customer loyalty, while trust also had a
positive effect on customer loyalty. The mediation analysis indicated that
AI-based marketing personalization was indirectly associated with
customer loyalty through customer experience, trust, and the sequential
mediation pathway from customer experience to trust. These findings
suggest that AI -enabled personalization generates relational value when
customers perceive personalized interactions as relevant, beneficial, and
trustworthy. The study concludes that Islamic banks should integrate AI -
based personalization with efforts to stre ngthen customer experience and
trust rather than relying solely on technological sophistication. Practical
priorities include providing relevant recommendations, maintaining
transparent data practices, and enhancing customers' control over their
personalization preferences to support sustainable banking relationships
and strengthen customer loyalty.

INTRODUCTION
The development of digital technology has shifted the marketing paradigm from
mass marketing toward approaches that are increasingly data-driven, individualized, real-
time, and oriented toward customer experience. Within this transformation, artificial
intelligence (AI) has emerged as a strategic technology because of its ability to process
large volumes of customer data to generate predictions and recommendations, automate

interactions, and support more adaptive marketing decisions. From a marketing
perspective, AI is no longer positioned merely as a tool for task automation but is
increasingly integrated into customer value -creation processes through its ability to
identify behavioral patterns and individual needs. Studies examining the relationship
between AI and customer experience indicate that the intersection of these fi elds has
developed into a multidisciplinary research area encompassing marketing, management,
and information technology (Peruchini et al., 2024) . Accordingly, AI -based marketing
should ideally create interactions that are increasingly relevant, contextual, responsive,
and valuable to individual customers rather than merely improving a co mpany's
operational efficiency.
One of the most important implications of AI development in marketing is the
growing capacity of companies to implement AI -based marketing personalization. By
analyzing transaction data, digital behavior, preferences, and customer interaction
histories, AI  enables companies to provide product recommendations, promotional
communications, and services tailored to individual customer characteristics. The
literature on AI -driven personalization indicates that personalization can enhance
communication relevance, customer engagement, and satisfaction while simultaneously
raising concerns regarding privacy, data use, and the balance between personalization
benefits and customer comfort (Katikar, 2024). Accordingly, the success of AI -based
personalization is determi ned not only by an algorithm's ability to generate accurate
recommendations but also by how customers experience these interactions and perceive
the company's security, transparency, and credibility. This issue is particularly important
in financial servic es because personalization relies on sensitive information directly
related to customers' financia l activities and circumstances  (Minaryanti et al., 2026;
Zungu et al., 2025).
Digital transformation has also changed consumer expectations of service
providers. Customers no longer evaluate services solely on the basis of their core
functions but increasingly consider convenience, speed, relevance, responsiveness, and
the overall quality of their interactions with a company. In the context of AI, this shift has
made customers increasingly accustomed to services capable of recognizing their
preferences and providing personalized recommendations. However, personalization can
also crea te higher expectations: as customers increasingly expect relevant and
individualized services, inappropriate or inaccurate recommendations may adversely
affect their experiences. Studies examining AI and customer experience indicate that AI
can shape custo mer experience through capabilities related to recognition, interaction,
and personalization, although the quality of that experience remains influenced by
customers' perceptions of the technology (Peruchini et al., 2024; Tulcanaza-Prieto et al.,
2023). Customer experience therefore represents an important mechanism through which
AI capabilities may be translated into customer-perceived value.
At the same time, AI -based personalization creates a potential tension between
relevance and trust. As organizations use greater amounts of customer data to generate
personalized services, customers may increasingly require assurance regarding data

security, privacy, transparency, and responsible data management. In digital financial
services, these concerns are particularly consequential because relationships between
customers and banks involve the management of funds and sensitive financial
information. Studies of AI -based personalization in financial services indicate that
personalization and trust are closely related because customers must have sufficient
confidence in an institution before they are willing to provide personal information or
permit its use in exchange for more personalized services (Kanaparthi, 2024). Trust
should therefore be understood not merely as an outcome of digitalization but as a
relational mechanism through which AI -based personalization may contribute to
enduring customer relationships.
This phenomenon is particularly relevant to the banking industry because banks
manage extensive customer data and increasingly frequent digital interactions. AI can be
used to identify customer needs, generate next -best offers, provide personalized product
recommendations, automate service interactions, detect potentially risky activities, and
improve digital interactions. Research in the banking sector indicates that customer
perceptions of convenience, personalization, trust, satisfaction, and loyalty are important
components of AI -enabled customer experiences (Tulcanaza-Prieto et al., 2023) .
Accordingly, the challenge for banking marketing management extends beyond
increasing digital channel adoption to converting digital interactions into relational value
that strengthens trust and loyalty. Within this framework, customer loyalty represents  a
strategic outcome because it reflects the continuity of customer relationships through
continued service use, relationship maintenance, positive recommendations, and reduced
switching intentions.
The digital transformation of (Bank BSI.co.id, 2025) provides a relevant empirical
context for examining this issue. The launch of BYOND by BSI in November 2024
marked an important stage in BSI's digital transformation. By early January 2025,
BYOND had been downloaded by more than 2 million users and had re corded
approximately 15 million transactions. Within approximately two months of its launch,
the number of active users approached 3 million, with approximately 85.7% of previously
active BSI Mobile users having migrated to BYOND. By March 2025, the number  of
BYOND users had reached approximately 3.5 million, while 98.03% of BSI transactions
were conducted through electronic channels, with 813 million transactions recorded
throughout 2024. This growth continued through the end of 2025, when BYOND users
reached approximately 6 million and BSI's total mobile banking users exceeded 9 million.
Throughout 2025, BYOND recorded more than 730 million transactions with an
approximate value of IDR 820 trillion.
This growth in digital channel usage demonstrates substantial technology
adoption but does not necessarily establish the success of relationship marketing. High
numbers of users and transactions may indicate extensive use of digital services, but these
indicators alone do not establish whether customers have positive experiences, trust the
system and institution providing the services, or intend to maintain their relationships
over the long term. Moreover, as customer data are used more intensively to gener ate

therefore exists between the expectation that AI -based personalization should create
relevant experiences, strengthen trust, and ultimately foster customer loyalty and the
empirical reality that increased digital service usage alone does not explain how loyalty

by Islamic banks but how AI -enabled marketing personalization can contribute to
customer loyalty through customer experience and trust.
Previous studies have demonstrated the importance of customer experience, trust,
and service quality in fostering loyalty in Islamic banking. (Albaity & Rahman, 2021) ,
for example, demonstrated the importance of trust and attitude in explaining customer
loyalty toward Islamic banks. Research in Indonesia has also shown that brand image and
promotion are associated with trust and customer loyalty among BSI customers (Basrowi
et al., 2023) . Other research on Islamic digital -only banks has indicated that customer
experience can strengthen brand trust, which subsequently contributes to brand equity
and customer loyalty. These findings suggest that customer experience is not merely an
outcome of digital services but can also function as a mech anism for developing long -
term customer relationships. A recent study on BSI also found that Islamic value
propositions and digital customer experience influenced customer satisfaction, whereas
trust did not demonstrate a direct effect on satisfaction with in the model examined,
indicating that relationships among experience, trust, satisfaction, and loy alty remain
empirically varied.
Although research on digital banking, customer experience, trust, and loyalty in
Islamic banking has expanded, several research gaps remain. First, much of the Islamic
banking literature continues to emphasize the service quality –satisfaction–loyalty
relationship, whereas AI as a mechanism for marketing personalization has received
comparatively limited attention. Prior research on service quality and customer
satisfaction in Islamic banking indicates that digital banking, customer loyalty, customer
trust, and customer experience have become established research themes, but
opportunities remain to develop more integrative models reflecting emerging
technological developments (2024). Second, research on AI and customer experience in
banking has generally exam ined perceptions of AI -enabled services, convenience,
personalization, satisfaction, or loyalty separately or through relatively limited relational
models. Third, the sequential pathway from AI -based marketing personalization to
customer experience, trust,  and ultimately customer loyalty remains relatively
underexplored, particularly in Indonesian Islamic banking. The research gap therefore
lies not in the absence of studies on AI or customer loyalty individually but in the limited
understanding of the relational mechanisms through which AI-based personalization may
contribute to customer loyalty in Islamic banking.
This gap has become increasingly important as recent research has begun to
examine AI-enabled services empirically within Islamic digital banking. A recent study
found that problem -solving capability, customization, and information quality could

Accordingly, the novelty of this study lies not merely in incorporating AI as a variable

In the context of Islamic banking, this model also extends digital banking research by
incorporating relational characteristics associated not only with functional service
outcomes but also with trust, ethics, security, and Sharia principles.
This research is particularly relevant as Indonesia's Islamic banking industry
undergoes accelerated digitalization and increasingly intensive technology adoption. BSI
provides an important empirical setting because of the rapid expansion of its BYOND
ecosystem and its growing mobile banking user base. Under these conditions, banks
require empirical evidence not only regarding whether customers use digital technologies
but also regarding the mechanisms through which technology -enabled personalization
may contribute to customer retention and loyalty. Accordingly, this study aims to examine
the effect of AI-based marketing personalization on customer loyalty and investigate the
mediating roles of customer experience and trust, including the sequential mediation
pathway of AI -based marketing personalization → customer experience → trust →
customer loyalty.

METHOD
This study employed a quantitative explanatory research design to examine the
relationship between AI -Based Marketing Personalization (AI -PM) as the independent
variable and Customer Loyalty (CL) as the dependent variable, with Customer
Experience (CX) and  Trust (TR) as mediating variables. The research model was
grounded in Service-Dominant Logic and Relationship Marketing Theory to explain the
relationships among AI-based personalization, customer experience, trust, and customer
loyalty.
A cross-sectional survey design was employed, with primary data collected using
structured questionnaires during a single study period. The unit of analysis consisted of
individual Islamic bank customers who had experience using digital banking services and
exposure to personalized services or marketing communications.
The study focused on customers of Bank Syariah Indonesia (BSI) who used its
digital banking services, including the BYOND by BSI platform. Respondents were
selected based on their experience with BSI's digital services, enabling them to evaluate
AI-based marketing personalization, customer experience, trust, and customer loyalty.
BSI's digital banking environment provided the empirical context for examining
the relationships among AI -Based Marketing Personalization, Customer Experience,

Syariah Indonesia (BSI) in Banyumas Regency. Of the total respondents, 31% were male
and 69% were female, indicating a higher proportion of female participants. The majority
of participants (69%) were over the age of 35, reflecting a productive age group typically
active in financial and banking activities. Furthermore, 61% of respondents had been BSI
customers for more than two years, indicating a significant level of long -term customer
engagement and loyalty toward the bank.

H1: AI-Based Personalization Marketing has a positive effect on Customer
Experience
Based on the hypothesis testing results, AI -Based Personalization Marketing has
a positive effect on Customer Experience. These findings indicate that the better the
implementation of artificial intelligence (AI)-based personalization, the more positive the
experience perceived by customers when interacting with a company or digital platform.
This finding is reinforced by recent research on AI -driven personalization, which shows
that AI-based personalization significantly enhances Customer Experience (Danarhuda
& Solekah, 2025; Hardcastle et al., 2025; Indah Febriyanti & Tuti, 2023; Istianingsih et
al., 2026). The study also found that Customer Experience serves as a crucial mechanism
linking AI personalization to customer behavioral outcomes. Theoretically, AI
personalization acts as a stimulus within the S-O-R framework, making interactions more
relevant and helping customers obtain experiences that meet their needs. These findings
also align with (Suhartanto et al., 2026; Wardoyo & Rizana, 2026) , who reported that
AI/hyper-personalization enhances Customer Experience.

H2: AI-Based Personalization Marketing has a positive effect on Trust
Hypothesis testing results indicate that AI -Based Personalization Marketing
influences Trust. This suggests that when customers perceive an AI system as capable of
understanding their needs and providing relevant recommendations, their level of trust in
the company or platform can increase. This relationship can be explained through
Relationship Marketing Theory. In relationship marketing, long -term relationships
between companies and customers are built not only through transactions but also through
Trust and commitment. Trust emerges when customers perceive that a company is
capable of delivering benefits, meeting expectations, and managing the relationship
reliably. Research on AI -driven personalization also demonstrates a link between
personalization, Trust, and the quality of digital relationships. Recent studies have found
that Trust in AI is a key mechanism in the relationship between AI-driven personalization
and customer relationship quality (Laili & Hasan, 2024; Muflih et al., 2024; Munandar et
al., 2026). H3. AI-Based Personalization Marketing → Customer Loyalty.
H3: AI-Based Personalization Marketing has a negative effect on Customer Loyalty
Theoretically, AI-based personalization marketing can enhance customer loyalty
because personalization makes the relationship between the customer and the company
more relevant and valuable. However, test results demonstrate that AI -based
personalization m arketing has a negative effect on customer loyalty. This result is
consistent with research by (Jayapal, 2025), which found that AI personalization does not
always have a direct effect on loyalty and may operate through trust or engagement. These
findings diverge from the research of Wardoyo and Rizana (2026), who specifically found
that AI personalization has a positive and significant effect on consumer loyalty and that
customer experience mediates this relationship among digital platform users. In other
words, personalization does not automatically foster customer loyalty; its effect may be
mediated by experience and trust.
H4: Customer Experience has a positive effect on Customer Loyalty
The results for H4 indicate that customer experience has a positive effect on
customer loyalty. This finding implies that the more positive the experience a customer
has while interacting with a company, the greater their inclination to maintain the
relationship with that company. Theoretically, a positive experience can lead to a
favorable evaluation of the company. Customers who find the product search process
easy, recommendations relevant, service responsive, transactions convenient, and
problem resolution effective will have stronger reasons to continue using the product or
service. This relationship aligns with Customer Experience Theory, which positions
experience as a crucial element in shaping long-term relationships between customers and
companies. Recent research on AI -driven e-commerce by Wardoyo and Rizana (2026)
found that customer experience plays a vital role in generating strategic outcomes,
including loyalty. In fact, personalization was found to have a strong indirect effect on
loyalty through customer experience.

H5: Customer Experience has a positive effect on Trust.
Theoretically, the relationship between customer experience and trust can be
explained through the perspective of relationship marketing. A positive and consistent
customer experience provides tangible interactions that serve as a basis for customers to
assess the company's reliability. Positive customer experience can enhance trust because
customers gain firsthand experience regarding the company's capabilities, consistency,
and reliability. When customers perceive that a company delivers interactions that  are
relevant, effortless, convenient, and aligned with their expectations, they gain experiential
evidence that serves as a foundation for building trust. In the context of AI and e -
commerce which is particularly relevant to this study Istianingsih et al. (2026) developed
a model integrating AI-driven hyper-personalization, customer experience, e-trust, and e-
loyalty among Indonesian e -commerce consumers using PLS -SEM. Their research
demonstrated that AI-driven hyper-personalization enhances customer experience, while
customer experience and e-trust play crucial roles in explaining e-loyalty. These findings
reinforce the argument that the experience generated through AI -driven personalization
must translate into trust before it can optimally impact loyalty.
H6: Trust has a positive effect on Customer Loyalty
Trust is a fundamental pillar in establishing long -term relationships between
customers and companies. Consequently, higher levels of customer trust correlate with a
greater likelihood of customers maintaining their relationship with the company. From
the perspective of Relationship Marketing Theory, trust is a central variable in fostering
long-term relationships. Customers who trust a company feel more secure when
conducting transactions, sharing information, utilizing services, and making repeat
purchases. Research by (Hassan et al., 2025; Rachmawati & Utami, 2021; Srivastava &
Gurme, 2026)  on AI in consumer behavior indicates that consumer trust contributes
positively to brand loyalty, whereas privacy concerns can disrupt this relationship.
Studies on AI-driven e-commerce have also found that trust positively influences loyalty
and that personalization can strengthen the dynamics of the trust -satisfaction-loyalty
relationship. These findings align with relationship marketing research in Indonesia,
which demonstrates that trust can act as an intervening variable linking relationship
marketing activities to loyalty.
H7: Customer Experience mediates the effect of AI -Based Personalization
Marketing on Customer Loyalty
The impact of AI personalization on loyalty is not solely direct. AI personalization
first generates a more relevant experience, which subsequently drives loyalty. This
explanation aligns well with the S -O-R (Stimulus-Organism-Response) perspective: AI
personalization serves as the Stimulus (S), Customer Experience as the Organism (O),
and Customer Loyalty as the Response (R). In other words, AI personalization enhances
loyalty by improving the customer experience. These findings closely mirror those of
Wardoyo and Rizana (2026), who explicitly identified the mediating role of customer
experience. Other research on AI-driven e-commerce such as that by Mittameedi (2026)
has also found that personalization exerts a strong indirect effect on loyalty through

H8: Trust mediates the effect of AI -Based Personalization Marketing on Customer
Loyalty
AI personalization can enhance trust when customers perceive that the company
understands their needs, provides accurate recommendations, and delivers consistent
service. This trust subsequently serves as a foundation for the development of loyalty.
From the perspective of Relationship Marketing Theory, long -term relationships are not
based solely on transactions but on the establishment of trust between the customer and
the company. When customers believe that a company possesses the capability and
integrity to deliver promised benefits, their perceived risk decreases. Research by
Purnama (2026) on AI personalization and e-loyalty indicates that online trust can act as
a mediator between AI -powered personalization and e -loyalty. These findings suggest
that the impact of AI personalization on loyalty mediated by trust is contextual in nature;
personalization fosters trust when customers perceive that the technology delivers
benefits while respecting privacy.
H9: Customer Experience and Trust serially mediate the effect of AI -based
personalization marketing on customer loyalty.
Theoretically, the impact of AI -based personalization marketing on customer
loyalty does not always occur directly; rather, it can unfold through gradual psychological
and relational mechanisms. In this study, these mechanisms are explained by integrating
Relationship Marketing Theory, Service-Dominant Logic, and the Stimulus –Organism–
Response (S-O-R) framework. Customer experience is posited to shape trust. Consistent
positive experiences provide customers with information regarding the service provider's
competence, reliability, and quality. In banking relationships, experiences that are
consistent and meet expectations can reduce customer uncertainty and lay the foundation
for the formation of trust. Thus, customer experience is not merely an outcome of
evaluating interaction quality but can also serve as an antecedent to a deeper relational
evaluation: trust. Trust is subsequently positioned as the second mediator linking
customer experience to customer loyalty. In the banking context, loyalty possesses a
strong relational character because customers must mai ntain a relationship with the
institution that manages their funds, personal data, and financial transactions. The
argument regarding this sequential mechanism is also supported by prior research in
Islamic banking. Yusfiarto et al. (2023) found that the r elationship between service
quality and customer loyalty can operate through a serial mechanism involving customer
satisfaction and trust. Kadir et al. (2023) also demonstrated that service quality,
satisfaction, and trust are linked to customer loyalty in  Indonesian Islamic banking and
that trust plays a crucial role in this mediation mechanism.

CONCLUSION
This study addressed the research question by demonstrating that AI -based
marketing personalization contributed to customer loyalty primarily through customer
experience and trust. The findings indicated that AI -based marketing personalization
enhanced the  relevance and value of customer experiences, while positive customer
experiences strengthened trust in the bank (Amelia, 2025). In turn, greater trust was
associated with customers' intentions to maintain their relationships with the bank and
demonstrate stronger loyalty. Thus, the relationship between personalization and
customer loyalty was not attributable merely to the provision of personalized services but
also to the ability of personalization to create meaningful customer experiences and
strengthen confidence in the bank (Istianingsih, 2026). In practice, relying solely on
sophisticated AI technologies to generate personalized recommendations may be
insufficient; banks should ensure that personalization enhances interaction quality,
provides meaningful customer benefits, and is implemented transparently and reliably.
Overall, the findings indicate that AI -based marketing personalization can contribute to
customer loyalty when relevant customer experiences strengthen trust in the bank.
The finding that AI -based marketing personalization enhanced customer
experience suggests that Bank Syariah Indonesia (BSI) should ensure that its AI -driven
recommendations, offers, and communications are relevant to customers' needs.
Marketing and technol ogy teams should periodically evaluate the relevance of
personalized content and minimize recommendations that are repetitive, contextually
inappropriate, or intrusive. Given the role of customer experience in strengthening trust,
the bank should clearly communicate how customer data are used for personalization and
provide customers with appropriate control over their data and personalization
preferences. Marketing, technology, and data protection teams should therefore
coordinate to ensure that personaliz ation benefits are delivered without compromising
data security, privacy, or customer trust. The sequential mediation findings further
indicate that customer loyalty was associated with personalization through the pathway
of customer experience and trust. Accordingly, management should evaluate the
effectiveness of AI -based personalization not only through transaction volumes or
responses to promotional campaigns but also through customer experience and trust
indicators. Although the findings supported this sequential mediation pathway, the cross-
sectional design limits conclusions regarding temporal and causal ordering. Future
research should therefore employ longitudinal or experimental designs to examine
whether positive customer experiences subsequently strengthen trust and whether
increased trust translates into sustained customer loyalty. Future studies could also
incorporate constructs such as customer engagement or perceived usefulness to explain
variations in customers' responses to AI-based marketing personalization.
