ABSTRACT
This study investigates how user -generated content (UGC)
storytelling, platform interactivity, and generative AI influence
purchase intention through digital marketing mediation in
Indonesian digital platforms. Employing PLS -SEM analysis with
300 respondents aged 18 -45 years, findings reveal that UGC
storytelling significantly enhances both digital marketing
effectiveness and purchase intention directly, with digital
marketing partially mediating this relationship. Generative AI
demonstrates dual functionality as both independent purchase
driver and moderator, amplifying digital marketing's influence
on purchase intention. Unexpectedly, platform interactivity
shows no significant positive effect on digital marketing and
exhibits negative direct effects on purchase intention,
challenging prevailing theor etical assumptions. The model
explains 51.1% variance in digital marketing and 65.6% in
purchase intention, demonstrating substantial predictive
power. Theoretical contributions include integrating Narrative
Transportation Theory with AI -mediated consumer behavior
frameworks while revealing boundary conditions for platform
interactivity effectiveness. Practical implications emphasize
prioritizing authentic UGC storytelling curation, strategic
generative AI integration, and critical evaluation of interactive
features' actual conversion contributions rather than assuming
universal benefits in digital marketing strategies.

INTRODUCTION
The proliferation of digital platforms has fundamentally disrupted traditional marketing
paradigms, catalyzing a shift from brand -controlled messaging to consumer -driven narratives
that shape purchase decisions (Batat, 2024) . User -generated content (UGC) has emerged as a
critical marketing asset, with consumers increasingly relying on authentic peer narratives rather

than firm-generated content (FGC) when making purchase decisions (Thi Nguyen & Dang, 2022;
Yeo et al., 2022). This transformation reflects deeper changes in consumer trust dynamics, where
UGC - particularly in storytelling formats - demonstrates superior persuasive power by providing
credible, relatable, and emotionally resonant product experiences (Djafarova & Bowes, 2021).
Recent empirical evidence reveals UGC's substantial impact on purchase intentions,
particularly when delivered through interactive digital platforms. T Ahmad Wani,
(2016)demonstrate that UGC recommendations and visual content generate stronger purchase
intentions than brand -generated alternatives, especially among younger consumers and in
impulse purchase contexts. The effectiveness of UGC is further amplified through storytelling
mechanisms that foster emotional engagement and authentic connections between consumers
and brands (Shen et al., 2022). User-generated storytelling content (UGCS) functions as a natural
content marketing strategy, stimulating electronic word -of-mouth that directly influences
purchasing behavior through enhanced product appeal and social sharing (Latifah et al., 2022).
Platform interactivity represents a complementary yet underexplored dimension of this
phenomenon. Interactive features  - including real -time Q&A, personalized responses, and
multimedia engagement - significantly enhance consumer participation and brand loyalty (Hsiao,
2024; Nasrabadi et al., 2024) . Zhu et al., (2022) establish that content creator interactivity
moderates UGC effectiveness, with higher interactivity producing more influential consumer
responses. The format of UGC itself matters considerably; short -form video content generates
stronger purchase intentions than static graphics through mechanisms of perceived value and
reduced psychological distance (Afshardost, 2013; Alcántara-Pilar et al., 2024).
Despite these insights, critical gaps persist in understanding how UGC storytelling and
platform interactivity jointly influence purchase intentions. First, existing research examines
these elements in isolation rather than investigating their synergistic effects (Alcántara-Pilar et
al., 2024). Second, the psychological mechanisms  - particularly dual pathways through trust and
engagement - require systematic empirical validation (Xu et al., 2021) . Third, boundary
conditions such as digital literacy and social presence that may moderate these relationships
remain unexplored (Falloon, 2020).
This study addresses these gaps by developing and testing an integrative framework that
examines how UGC storytelling and platform interactivity synergistically drive purchase
intentions through dual mediation mechanisms (trust and engagement) while identifying
moderating boundary conditions (digital literacy and social presence). By employing structural
equation modeling with data from 458 Indonesian digital platform users, we advance theoretical
understanding of platform-based marketing effectiveness while providing actionable insights for
brands leveraging UGC strategies across Instagram, TikTok, and emerging social commerce
platforms (Djafarova & Bowes, 2021; Tsai & Hsiao, 2025) . Our findings contribute to digital
marketing scholarship by integrating Narrative Transportation Theory with Uses and
Gratifications Theory, revealing the complex mechanisms through which authentic consumer
narratives and interactive platform affordances collaboratively shape purchase decisions in
contemporary digital ecosystems.

effectiveness through multiple mechanisms. UGCS creates authentic emotional connections,
improving brand attitudes, emotional value, and participation behaviors while stimulating
electronic word -of-mouth and purchasing behavior (Fredendall et al., 2005) . Narrative
transportation effects prove stronger in user -generated versus professional content, particularly
across commercial domains and diverse customer touchpoints (Ramkumar & Ellie Jin, 2019; van
Laer et al., 2019). UGC demonstrates superior credibility and trustworthiness, with review videos

Generate AI, Purchase Intention and Platform Interactivity
Artificial intelligence demonstrates significant positive effects on purchase intention across
diverse marketing and e -commerce contexts. AI experiences enhance perceived value and
purchase intention by deepening consumer engagement, curiosity, confidence, and trust toward
technology-driven products (Pérez-Pérez & Pérez-Pérez, 2025). AI positively influences consumer
experiences and social media engagement, ultimately increasing satisfaction and purchase
intention (Beyari, 2025) . AI -generated product recommendations significantly impact purchase
intention, with consumers showing higher buying intentions toward AI -recommended products
compared to influencer suggestions (Aoki & Matsui, 2025). Multiple mediating factors strengthen
AI's influence: social media engagement and compelling content (Chaveesuk et al., 2020) ,
emotional value perceptions (Hossain et al., 2022) , credibility and trust through AI interactions
(Rasheed et al., 2023) , and perceived personalization enhancing relevance and usefulness (Bilal
et al., 2024) . AI advertising attributes including informativeness and novelty positively influence
purchase intention (Onofrei et al., 2022) . However, effects vary by product category, consumer
characteristics, and implementation strategies, with AI proving more effective for search
products than experience products (Latif; Bungin; & Kodrat, 2025; Peña-García et al., 2020).

Platform Interactivity and Digital Marketing
Platform interactivity, defined as the capability to facilitate reciprocal communication, user
control, and real -time responsiveness between users and systems or among users themselves,
significantly enhances digital marketing effectiveness across multiple dimensions (Maksum et al.,
2025). Interactivity positively influences user engagement and loyalty through interactive
features like chat, discussions, and notifications that increase satisfaction and trust (Londoño-
Giraldo et al., 2024) . Interactive features enable consumers to obtain relevant information and
personalized experiences, strengthening brand trust and driving purchase decisions while
mediating relationships between brand trust and purchasing behavior (Hanaysha, 2022) .
Interactivity builds brand image and positive brand attitudes through personalized, responsive
experiences, enhancing perceived quality and emotional attachment (Mulcahy & Riedel, 2020) .

Digital Marketing and  Purchase Intention
Digital marketing significantly influences purchase intention through multiple
mechanisms. Digital marketing interactions serve as critical external factors enabling consumers
to access opinions that positively transform purchasing decisions (Armutcu et al., 2023) . E -
marketing strategies including electronic word -of-mouth, content marketing, and social media
influencers consistently demonstrate positive effects (Pham, 2024) . Perceived interactivity and
social media engagement behaviors positively correlate with purchase intention (Cabri & Fioretti,
2022). Information quality and credibility of visual eWOM enhance perceived usefulness, driving
purchase intention (Indiani & Fahik, 2020) . Rich media presentations and emotional factors
including hedonic enjoyment strengthen purchase intentions, while positive attitudes toward
digital advertising and perceived advertising value significantly amplify purchasing outcomes (Li
& Meshkova, 2013).

Mediation
Digital marketing mediates UGC storytelling's influence on purchase intention by providing
interactive and immersive platforms that amplify engagement, trust, and brand immersion.
Storytelling marketing leverages videos, social media, and digital events to communicate brand
narratives, enhancing brand perception and purchase intention (Pascucci et al., 2023) . Digital
marketing amplifies storytelling effects through branded virtual worlds and interactive
experiences that mediate narrative engagement's impact on purchase intention (Romero-
Colmenares & Reyes -Rodríguez, 2022) . Content marketing, as a digital marketing component,
fosters engagement and trust, indirectly increasing purchase intention by delivering valuable
material (Wang et al., 2025) . However, UGC's direct effect on purchase intention depends on
additional factors including brand familiarity and perceived value (Diwanji & Cortese, 2020).
Digital marketing mediates platform interactivity's influence on purchase intention by
enabling interactive features that foster engagement, trust, and emotional connection essential
for converting consumer interest into purchasing behavior (Taylor et al., 2021) . Platform
interactivity through real -time feedback, multiple sensory cues, and interactive communication
systems positively influences purchase intention by enhancing engagement and reducing
uncertainty (Gignac, 2009). Digital marketing platforms provide technological infrastructure for
real-time communication, content co -creation, and community engagement that strengthens
consumer-advertiser connections (Alalwan et al., 2019) . Immediate feedback and information
richness deepen consumer understanding, while augmented reality and virtual influencer
campaigns demonstrate that interactivity enhances user experience and emotional engagement,
ultimately driving purchase intention (Zare Ebrahimabad et al., 2024).

engagement through hedonic enjoyment and perceived autonomy predict purchasing behavior

Research Framework
The conceptual framework illustrates a complex structural model examining direct,
mediating, and moderating relationships among UGC storytelling, platform interactivity,
generative AI, digital marketing, and purchase intention. UGC storytelling and platform
interactivity function as antecedents influencing both digital marketing effectiveness and
purchase intention through dual pathways, suggesting partial mediation mechanisms.
Generative AI demonstrates triple influence: direct effect on purchase intention, potential
influence on digital marketing, and moderating role strengthening the digital marketing -
purchase intention relationship. Digital marketing occupies a central mediating position,
channeling influences from UGC storytelling and platform interactivity while being amplified by
generative AI's optimization capabilities. This framework integrates content authenticity (UGC),
technological affordances (interactivity, AI), and marketing systems to comprehensively explain
purchase intention formation in digital ecosystems (Figure 1).

METHODS
Research Desain
This research employs a mixed -method approach with explanatory sequential design,
utilizing surveys in the quantitative phase to test the research model, followed by in -depth
interviews in the qualitative phase to deepen understanding of psychological mechanisms. The
research population comprises consumers actively using digital platforms who have been
exposed to UGC within the last six months. Purposive sampling technique was employed with
criteria: (1) aged 18 -45 years; (2) active on at least one digital platform; (3) have made online
purchases. Based on Hair et al. (2019), the minimum sample size for SEM is 200 respondents.
This study targeted 400 respondents to anticipate non -response, with a total of 300 respondents
utilized for final analysis.

Technique Data Analysis
Quantitative analysis employed Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling (PLS -
SEM) using SmartPLS 4.0 software. The analytical procedure consisted of: (1) evaluation of the
measurement model through assessment of convergent validity (factor loadings >0.70, AVE
>0.50, composite reliability >0.70) and discriminant validity using Fornell -Larcker criterion and
HTMT ratio <0.85; (2) assessment of structural model quality through R² values, predictive
relevance (Q² >0), and effect sizes (f²); (3) testing  direct effect hypotheses using path coefficients
and bootstrapping procedure with 5,000 resamples to determine significance levels; (4)
examining mediating effects through specific indirect effects using bootstrapping and calculating
variance accounted for (VAF); (5) testing moderating effects through interaction terms and multi -
group analysis. Model fit criteria followed Hair et al. (2019) guidelines: SRMR <0.08, with
additional evaluation of path coefficient significance at p <0.05 and t -statistics >1.96  for
hypothesis confirmation.

DISCUSSION
UGC Storytelling and Digital Marketing Effectiveness
The findings confirm that UGC storytelling serves as a critical antecedent to digital
marketing effectiveness, validating theoretical predictions from Narrative Transportation Theory
and empirical evidence suggesting UGC's superior persuasive power over brand -generated
content. This robust relationship substantiates the mechanism through which authentic
consumer narratives fundamentally reshape digital marketing strategies by creating emotionally
resonant touchpoints that traditional marketing communications cannot replicate.
The strong effect observed aligns with van Laer et al., (2019) conceptualization of narrative
transportation, wherein user -generated stories create deeper mental and emotional absorption
compared to professionally crafted content. Our findings extend this theoretical framework by
demonstrating that narrative transportation does not merely influence individual consumer
responses but systematically enhances the overall effectiveness of digital marketing ecosystems.
This occurs through multiple mechanisms: UGC storytelling generates authentic emotional
connections that improve brand attitudes and participation behaviors (Troise & Camilleri, 2021) ;
it stimulates electronic word -of-mouth that amplifies marketing reach beyond paid promotional
efforts; and it creates social proof mechanisms that enhance credibility across digital
touchpoints.
The empirical confirmation validates the assertion that UGC transforms passive audiences
into active co -creators of brand narratives (Pascucci et al., 2023) . When consumers encounter
peer-generated stories rather than corporate messaging, they experience reduced psychological
distance and enhanced identification with product experiences. This cognitive -affective process
fundamentally alters how digital marketing content is processed, moving from peripheral route
persuasion characteristic of traditional advertising to central route processing wherein
consumers deeply engage with message arguments presented through authentic peer

The direct effect of UGC storytelling on purchase intention, while significant, reveals partial

pathways of influence: a direct emotional -persuasive route wherein compelling narratives
immediately shift purchase intentions, and an indirect route wherein UGC content enhances
overall digital marketing ecosystem effectiveness that subsequently influences purchase
decisions. The existence of these parallel pathways has important theoretical implications,
suggesting that UGC storytelling operates simultaneously as both content (direct influence on
consumers) and meta-content (influence on marketing system effectiveness).

Platform Interactivity: Unexpected Findings and Theoretical Implications
Contrary to theoretical expectations grounded in Uses and Gratifications Theory and
empirical predictions from platform interactivity literature, this study reveals that platform
interactivity demonstrates no significant positive relationship with digital marketing effectiveness
and exhibits a negative direct effect on purchase intention. These counterintuitive findings
contradict substantial prior evidence demonstrating interactivity's positive influence on
engagement and purchase outcomes (Holliman & Rowley, 2014) , demanding careful theoretical
reconsideration and suggesting important boundary conditions for interactive platform design.
Several theoretical mechanisms may account for this discrepancy. The cognitive load hypothesis
suggests that excessive interactivity may overwhelm consumers with information and choices,
paradoxically reducing decision quality and purchase confidence. (Iglesias-Cuzcano et al.,
2022)acknowledge this possibility, noting that while interactivity enables convenient information
acquisition, excessive interactive features may trigger information overload that diminishes
rather than enhances purchase intention. This cognitive burden diverts mental resources from
systematic evaluation of purchase -relevant product attributes toward managing interactive
interface elements, ultimately degrading decision quality.
The authenticity dilution mechanism provides an alternative explanation wherein high
platform interactivity is perceived as manipulative or commercially motivated, thereby
undermining the authentic peer -to-peer communication that makes UGC compelling. When
interactive features are perceived as brand -orchestrated rather than consumer-driven, they may
activate persuasion knowledge that prompts defensive processing and resistance to influence
attempts. This interpretation aligns with Crittenden et al., (2019)  observation that interactivity
effects vary substantially depending on perceived authenticity and trust elements. Additionally,
contextual factors specific to Indonesian digital consumers may moderate interactivity's
effectiveness. Cultural dimensions such as uncertainty avoidance or communication style
preferences may influence how interactive platform features are perceived and utilized. If
Indonesian consumers primarily use digital platforms for passive content consumption rather
than active interaction, interactive affordances may remain underutilized, failing to generate the
engagement benefits documented in Western contexts where participatory digital culture is
more established.
The negative direct effect on purchase intention suggests potential distraction and
heightened scrutiny mechanisms operating simultaneously. Interactive features may divert
cognitive resources away from purchase -relevant information processing toward engagement
with interactive elements themselves, creating a resource allocation problem wherein
consumers invest attention in platform interactions at the expense of systematic product
evaluation. This attention diversion reduces cognitive capacity for evaluati ng product attributes
and forming informed purchase judgments. Alternatively, interactivity may heighten critical
evaluation processes wherein active engagement prompts more systematic and critical
information processing. When consumers actively interact with content rather than passively
consuming it, they may scrutinize product claims more carefully, identifying potential
weaknesses or inconsistencies that passive consumption would overlook. This enhanced critical
processing paradoxically reduces purchase confidence, particularly for products with ambiguous
quality signals or limited social proof, ultimately suppressing purchase intentions despite
increased platform engagement.

Digital marketing emerges as a critical mediating mechanism through which UGC

overall digital marketing effectiveness that subsequently drives purchase decisions. This
mediation mechanism substantiates Jun et al, (2025) framework wherein storytelling marketing
leverages digital channels to communicate brand narratives that boost purchase intention
through enhanced engagement and brand perception. Our findings extend this
conceptualization by empirically demonstrating that UGC storytelling content integrates into
broader digital marketing systems that amplify and distribute authentic consumer narratives
across multiple touchpoints. The mediating pathway operates through three interrelated
processes: distribution amplification, wherein digital marketing channels exponentially extend
the reach of compelling UGC stories beyond their original creators' networks by strategically
curating and redistributing authentic consumer narratives through paid social media promotion,
email marketing, and website integration; contextual optimization, wherein digital marketing

influence is maximized, transforming isolated stories into systematically integrated persuasive

Practical Implications
The research findings generate actionable insights for practitioners designing UGC -
leveraged digital marketing strategies. First, brands should prioritize UGC storytelling content
over traditional brand -generated messaging, given storytelling's potent direct and indirect
effects on purchase intentions. This involves developing systematic processes for identifying,
curating, and amplifying compelling customer narratives across owned and paid digital channels.
Second, generative AI integration emerges as strategic imperative rather than optional
enhancement. The dual role of AI as purchase driver and marketing amplifier suggests that AI
adoption generates compoundi ng returns. Practitioners should invest in AI -powered
personalization systems that tailor content, timing, and channel selection to individual customer
characteristics while using AI -generated creative content to increase engagement. Third,
platform interactivity requires careful strategic consideration given unexpected null effects.
Rather than assuming interactive features universally benefit engagement and conversion,
marketers should conduct rigorous testing examining how specific interactive elements

CONCLUSION
This study empirically validates UGC storytelling's dual -pathway influence on purchase
intention, operating directly through narrative transportation mechanisms and indirectly
through digital marketing effectiveness enhancement. UGC storytelling demonstrates the
strongest effect among examined constructs, confirming authentic peer narratives' superiority
over brand -generated content in contemporary digital ecosystems. Generative AI emerges as
both independent purchase driver and critical moderator, amplifying digital marketing's
conversion efficiency through personalization and optimization capabilities. Unexpectedly,
platform interactivity shows no positive relationship with digital marketing effectiveness and
exhibits negative direct effects on purchase intention, challenging prevailing theoretical
assumptions and suggesting cognitive load and authenticity dilution mechanisms. Digital
marketing's partial mediation of UGC storytelling effects reveals distribution amplification,
contextual optimization, and social proof cascades as key mechanisms transforming isolated
narratives into systematically integrated persuasive elements. These findings advance theoretical
understanding by integrating Narrative Transportation Theory with AI -mediated consumer
behavior frameworks while providing actionable insights for practitioners: prioritize UGC
storytelling curation, integrate generative AI strategically, and critically evaluate interactive
features' actual conversion contributions rather than assuming universal benefits.

LIMITATION
This study’s cross-sectional design limits causal inference, while purposive sampling 0f 300
Indonesian users (18 -45 years) restricts generalizability across cultures and demographics.
Platform interactivity’s null effects may reflect measurement limitatios in distinguishing
interactivity types. The study relies on general AI perceptions rather than specific apllications,
risks common method bias, and overlooks boundary condition including product categories,
consumer expertise, and competitive contexcts.
