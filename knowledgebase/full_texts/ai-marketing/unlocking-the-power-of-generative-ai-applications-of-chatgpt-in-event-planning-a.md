Abstract
This study investigates the real-world applications of ChatGPT in the meetings, incentives, conventions, and
exhibitions (MICE) industry, with a focus on event planning and marketing content creation. Adopting a qualitative
methodology, the research includes semi-structured interviews with industry professionals and analysis of two case
studies from Southeast Asia. Findings demonstrate how ChatGPT is utilized in proposal writing, agenda design,
creative brainstorming, multilingual translation, and brand storytelling. Results reveal that ChatGPT effectively
reduces content production time, enhances interdisciplinary collaboration, supports cultural adaptation, and broadens
regional communication. However, challenges related to content accuracy, ethical transp arency, and human-AI
collaboration boundaries remain significant. This paper contributes to the academic understanding of generative AI
applications in the MICE field and provides actionable recommendations for practitioners and educators. It also
outlines future research directions to assess long-term impacts and global scalability.
Keywords: Generative AI, ChatGPT, Event planning, Content Creation, MICE industry, Digital marketing
1. Introduction
The rapid development of generative artificial intelligence (AI) has brought forth new
opportunities for industries that rely heavily on content creation and communication. Among these,
the MICE (meetings, incentives, conventions, and exhibitions) sector has shown particular interest
in leveraging AI tools to enhance efficiency and creativity in planning and executing events.
ChatGPT, a large language model developed by OpenAI, has demonstrated strong capabilities in
generating natural language text, making it a valuable tool for drafting event proposals, marketing
copy, scripts, and more.
The COVID-19 pandemic accelerated digital transformation across the global event industry,
shifting many events to virtual or hybrid formats. This transition created an urgent need for new
technologies that could support the production of high-quality digital content under time and
resource constraints. As event organizers navigate this evolving landscape, generative AI tools like

2. Literature Review
2.1 Generative AI and Content Marketing
Generative AI models, including ChatGPT, are designed to produce human-like text based on
prompts. These tools have been widely adopted in industries such as journalism, education, and
digital marketing for tasks like content generation, summarization, and sentiment analysis (Kaplan
& Haenlein, 2023). In content marketing, AI tools help marketers scale content production,
personalize messages for target audiences, and optimize campaigns based on performance data
(Chung et al., 2022). Other studies have demo nstrated how generative AI enables dynamic
audience engagement and real-time campaign refinement (Dwivedi et al., 2023; Jarek & Mazurek,
2019). Liu and Sundar (2022) examined how anthropomorphic design of AI content affects
audience trust. Furthermore, Seiffert-Brockmann et al. (2021) highlighted that generative models
can support strategic communication planning in fast-paced media environments.
Recent scholarship has also highlighted the broader strategic implications of AI-generated content.
For example, Huang and Rust (2021) argue that AI is not merely a tactical tool but a transformative
force in reconfiguring marketing functions. Marinchak et al. (2018) demonstrated how AI adoption
improves customer engagement metrics and brand consistency across multiple digital channels.
Moreover, Kietzmann et al. (2018) noted that while generative AI enhances productivity, it also
introduces new ethical challenges, particularly in relation to source transparency and content
authenticity. These insights provide a foundation for understanding ChatGPT’s potential role
within the highly communicative environment of the MICE industry.
2.2 Content Creation in the MICE Industry

websites, email campaigns, press releases, and social media engagement. Traditionally, this
content has been manually produced by marketing teams under tight deadlines. With the rise of
digital events, the volume and complexity of content requirements have increased, making
automation tools more appealing (Getz & Page, 2016). Liu et al. (2021) noted that digitalization
in MICE operations is becoming more prevalent post-COVID. Jin and Weber (2019) discussed
how content localization influences international delegate engagement. Huang et al. (2020)

2.3 AI in Event Planning and Management
While existing studies have explored the use of AI for logistics, ticketing, and customer
relationship management in events (Law et al., 2020), fewer have examined the creative and
planning aspects. ChatGPT, in particular, can support brainstorming, agenda  setting, and
multilingual translations—areas that are essential for international and large -scale events.
However, questions remain regarding the reliability of AI-generated content and its alignment with
brand identity (Xiang et al., 2021).
Tussyadiah and Miller (2019) highlighted immersive and AI-powered planning tools as key drivers
of experiential value. Mariani and Borghi (2022) advocated for AI-human co-creation in service
design and scheduling. Neuhofer, Buhalis and Sinarta (2019) also stressed that organizational
readiness is critical for AI adoption in hospitality and events. Ivanov and Webster (2017) proposed
that AI integration must consider ethical and managerial boundaries.
Recent research has further elaborated on the role of AI in event scenario planning and stakeholder
communication. For instance, Gursoy et al. (2022) examined AI-driven personalization systems
that tailor event experiences based on attendee profiles and behavioral patterns. Similarly, Sigala
(2023) emphasized that AI in event planning must be supported by transparent governance
frameworks and data ethics principles to ensure long-term sustainability. In a study by Neuhofer

3. Research Method
This study employs a qualitative research approach combining semi-structured interviews with
event professionals and in-depth case study analysis. The aim is to understand real -world
applications and implications of ChatGPT in event planning and content creation.
Participants: Five professionals from the Southeast Asian MICE industry, including event
planners, marketing managers, and content strategists, were recruited through purposive sampling.
The participants were selected based on their demonstrated engagement with digital transformation
initiatives and hands-on experience with AI tools in event planning or marketing. Their affiliations
included regional convention bureaus, independent event consulting firms, and cross -border
marketing agencies, offering a rich diversity of perspectives. All participants had used ChatGPT
or comparable generative AI tools in at least one major event within the past two years. Prior to
the interviews, each participant completed a brief pre-survey detailing their role, organization type,
and specific AI use cases to guide the interview questions more effectively.
Data Collection: Interviews were conducted via Zoom, each lasting approximately 45 –60
minutes. To ensure consistency and in-depth exploration, a semi-structured interview guide was
developed based on themes identified in the literature review. The guide included open-ended
questions regarding participants' experiences using ChatGPT or similar generative AI tools,
specific use cases in event planning or marketing, perceived advantages and limitations, and
projections for future implementation. Participants were also encouraged to describe or share
actual outputs such as AI-generated drafts, event scripts, or promotional content.
The interview questions were organized into the following five thematic categories to facilitate
targeted yet flexible exploration: (1) Basic Background and AI Experience; (2) Applications in
Event Planning; (3) Applications in Marketing and Promotion; (4) Human-AI Collaboration and
Workflow; (5) Challenges, Risks, and Future Outlook. All interviews were audio-recorded with

In addition, two event cases were analyzed in depth:
Case A: ASEAN Digital Transformation Dialogue 2023 (Jakarta, Indonesia) — A regional
policy and technology dialogue hosted by a Southeast Asian think tank in collaboration with
several ASEAN ministries and ICT organizations. This annual forum brings together leaders from
government, academia, and the private sector to discuss  strategies for inclusive digital
transformation across the ASEAN region. In 2023, the organizers used ChatGPT to support the
generation of their bilingual event guidebook, create talking points and draft welcome speeches
for ministers, and compose localized press releases for distribution in multiple ASEAN member
states. The tool also assisted in summarizing panel content for media use.
Case B: Mekong Eco-Creative Festival 2023 (Chiang Rai, Thailand) — A cross-border cultural
and sustainability-themed event organized by a network of eco -entrepreneurs and cultural
institutions from the Greater Mekong Subregion. The festival featured artisan markets,
environmental design showcases, and interactive storytelling workshops with local communities.
ChatGPT was employed in the early planning stages to co-develop program descriptions, generate
multilingual promotional texts in Thai, Lao, and English, and help craft speaker biographies and
media kits. The AI also supported the creation of content for interactive installations focused on
climate change education.
Each case involved document analysis (e.g., AI-generated drafts, marketing collaterals), interviews
with project leads, and comparison with similar past events that did not use AI tools.
Data Analysis: Thematic analysis was used to identify recurring patterns in the interview
transcripts and case materials. Transcripts and documents were coded using NVivo software and
grouped under five emergent themes: (1) efficiency and productivity, (2) creative ideation, (3)
multilingual and cultural adaptation, (4) content accuracy and quality control, and (5) collaborative
dynamics between human teams and AI systems. To enhance rigor and consistency, qualitative
data analysis tools such as NVivo were employed to facilitate systematic coding. Interview
transcripts were segmented into meaningful excerpts (code segments), each labeled with one or
more thematic tags corresponding to the five guiding categories of the interview guide. These
categories ensured alignment between data collection and analysis. Visualization features such as
word frequency charts, code co-occurrence matrices, and pattern diagrams further supported
thematic comparisons across participant responses and between the two case studies.

a subset of transcripts and resolved discrepancies through discussion (Nowell et al., 2017).
Additionally, the study applied longitudinal coding principles as proposed by Saldaña (2021) to

layering provided a richer understanding of when and how ChatGPT was introduced into the

socio-technical systems perspective (Orlikowski, 2000), which recognizes that AI adoption in
MICE contexts is influenced not only by technological features but also by organizational
structures, stakeholder expectations, and ethical considerations.
4. Finding and Discussion
4. 1. Enhanced Efficiency and Productivity
Saving Time and Streamlining Workflow Across all participants and both cases, AI integration led
to a notable reduction in content development time. For the ASEAN Digital Transformation
Dialogue, event organizers reported saving up to 40% of their usual time during the production of
event materials. ChatGPT allowed teams to instantly produce baseline content such as welcome
letters, panel descriptions, and speaker intros. Similarly, for the Mekong Eco-Creative Festival,
early-stage content such as workshop outlines and vendor profiles were produced with ChatGPT
before being reviewed by the communications team.
Participants emphasized that ChatGPT enabled asynchronous collaboration among geographically
dispersed teams. By working from AI-generated drafts, teams could review and revise content
more efficiently across different time zones, particularly for multilin gual versions. One
interviewee noted that "the AI acted like a first-draft assistant, so we no longer had to wait for
someone to finish before others could contribute."  In addition, ChatGPT’s ability to auto-
summarize long documents allowed planning teams to condense briefings, reports, and vendor
proposals into digestible outlines. This helped reduce information overload and improved the
speed of internal decision-making. The tool’s adaptability also allowed team members with less
content writing experience to contribute meaningfully to pre-event communications. These time
savings did not just improve efficiency—they also enabled more strategic allocation of resources.
Instead of focusing on low-value writing tasks, team members were able to invest their time in
content curation, visual design, and stakeholder engagement, which added value to the overall
attendee experience.

Breaking Through Creative Blocks ChatGPT was widely adopted to overcome idea stagnation,
especially in brainstorming meetings. It provided a range of ideas for event themes, slogans, and
community storytelling angles. At the Mekong Eco-Creative Festival, creative content for art-
based climate installations was developed by combining AI-generated ideas with input from local
artists. Organizers reported that ChatGPT helped them “see new angles” and overcome team-wide
creative blocks.
According to interview data, event professionals found ChatGPT particularly useful in the early
phases of content ideation, especially when initial concepts were vague or directionless. By
inputting general prompts such as “Generate eco-themed workshop names” or “Suggest slogans
for digital inclusion,” participants were able to generate a wide array of possibilities in seconds.
This iterative process, described by one interviewee as “brainstorming with a limitless partner,”
helped accelerate decision-making during creative meetings. The tool also encouraged cross-
functional input. Junior staff or team members less confident in writing were able to contribute
ideas by prompting ChatGPT and presenting options to the group. This democratization of ideation
was seen as particularly valuable in hybrid teams or when working across departments.
Moreover, the case studies revealed that ChatGPT facilitated more inclusive creativity. For
example, in Chiang Rai, the organizing committee used prompts to generate story outlines based
on local folklore and environmental symbols. These outputs were then refined through community
consultation, allowing local voices to shape the final creative content. These findings align with
literature that suggests AI can serve as a stimulus for divergent thinking (Seidel et al., 2020),
offering creative scaffolding rather than fixed solutions. The use of ChatGPT in these contexts
provided a balance between novelty and relevance, enabling teams to break through creative inertia
without compromising cultural or thematic coherence.
4. 2. Multilingual and Cross-Cultural Applications
Expanding Regional Accessibility Both events required trilingual or bilingual communication.
ChatGPT was used for first-pass translation, notably reducing manual work. For instance, the
Dialogue’s press releases were simultaneously produced in English and Bahasa Indonesia, while
the Festival used English, Thai, and Lao. Though final outputs needed refinement by native
speakers, the AI provided culturally adaptable drafts that streamlined the communication process
and expanded regional accessibility. Participants indicated that ChatGPT allowed them to produce
translation drafts in multiple languages faster than relying solely on human translators. While not
flawless, these drafts served as a strong foundation, especially for time-sensitive materials like
press releases and social media posts. As one participant described, "We could get the rough

4. 3. Content Accuracy and Editorial Oversight
Interviewees generally stated that although AI-generated content is valuable for reference, it
requires further review to ensure that the information is correct. Common errors include mistyped
job titles, outdated data, and tone that doesn’t match the brand positioning. To address this issue,
both case teams established an "AI draft review mechanism" where senior colleagues conduct fact-
checking and semantic adjustments to ensure the professionalism and brand consistency of the
content. Ensuring Reliability and Trustworthiness Participants consistently emphasized the
importance of verifying AI-generated content. Errors included misattribution of organizational
affiliations, outdated facts, and overly generic phrasing. To address this, both event teams
implemented an internal "AI review protocol" requiring a human specialist to fact-check and revise
all AI-produced material before release. Despite these issues, AI was viewed as a valuable tool to
accelerate the first 70% of content creation.
Interview transcripts revealed that most teams followed a multi-step content validation process.
First, team members used ChatGPT to generate initial drafts of event documents including scripts,
schedules, and promotional blurbs. Next, designated reviewers—often senior staff or native
speakers—conducted line-by-line reviews to check factual accuracy, tone alignment, and message
coherence. In some cases, planners used side-by-side comparison templates in spreadsheets to
evaluate AI drafts against brand messaging guidelines.

4. 4. Human-AI Collaboration
Research shows that the most effective use case for ChatGPT is in collaborative working mode
within cross-departmental teams. For example, marketing interns drafted the first draft of the event
script using ChatGPT, while senior managers optimized it for tone and content. This "AI draft +
manual editing" process not only increases output speed, but also lowers the threshold for content
creation, allowing entry-level members to participate in core communication tasks.
Enhancing Interdisciplinary Team Dynamics The most effective use of ChatGPT occurred when
AI was used collaboratively within interdisciplinary teams. Marketing interns used AI to generate
content skeletons, which were then passed to senior staff for polishing and brand alignment. This
division of labor not only improved speed but also democratized content creation, allowing junior
staff to participate more confidently. Teams also expressed increased openness to experimentation
and iterative editing.
Based on the interview transcripts, the integration of ChatGPT reshaped team workflows by
promoting a more horizontal and inclusive content development structure. Rather than top-down
content assignments, team members across levels could initiate first drafts using AI, enabling a
sense of shared ownership over the messaging. One participant noted, “The AI gave our interns
the confidence to propose ideas they would’ve never suggested before.” This collaborative model
encouraged what Saldaña (2021) refers to as "dialogic coding," where iterative feedback loops
foster learning and reflection among contributors. Teams often co-edited documents using shared
platforms, with ChatGPT outputs acting as neutral starting points that reduced initial disagreement
and smoothed collaborative editing.

4. 5. Ethical, Strategic, and Brand Considerations
The use of AI also raises ethical and cultural issues. For example, the organizers of the ASEAN
Dialogue established an internal "AI Usage Policy" requiring that all ChatGPT drafts be marked
with the source of generation and manually reviewed before they can be published. The Mekong
Festival works with local storytellers to rewrite the narrative texts produced by ChatGPT to ensure
that the content delivered is consistent with local perspectives and cultural context. These strategies
demonstrate that AI is not only an operational tool, but also a key variable in the development of
brand reputation and strategy.
Navigating Responsibility and Authenticity Organizers raised concerns regarding intellectual
property, transparency in AI use, and the preservation of cultural authenticity. To mitigate risk, the
Dialogue’s host think tank drafted an AI use policy outlining disclosure standards and human
review mandates. Meanwhile, the Festival organizers engaged local storytellers to adapt AI-drafted
texts, ensuring they retained a community-driven voice. Participants also discussed the strategic
advantage of AI in future-proofing event operations.
Interview transcripts revealed a shared recognition of ethical ambiguity in the use of generative
AI. Participants emphasized the importance of disclosing AI involvement in public -facing
materials to avoid misrepresentation. This is consistent with Nowell et al. (2017), who highlight
that ethical rigor in qualitative processes enhances research and operational credibility. Several
interviewees suggested developing a labeling system that identifies AI -assisted content,
particularly in official speeches or media kits. Strategically, participants saw AI as a double-edged
sword. On the one hand, it offered competitive advantage through agility and scale; on the other
hand, it risked diminishing the perceived authenticity of grassroots initiatives. At the Mekong
Festival, AI-generated content was intentionally "localized" by partnering with cultural custodians
to ensure alignment with community values. This hybrid workflow aligns with ethical design

5. Conclusion
This study points out that generative AI such as ChatGPT has become an effective auxiliary tool
for content production in the exhibition industry, which can improve efficiency, enrich creative
sources, and improve communication methods with audiences. In the future, it is recommended to
strengthen AI literacy training for practitioners and establish an internal AI content review
mechanism to balance efficiency and accuracy. In addition, higher education institutions should
also actively incorporate AI applications into exhibition management courses to help students
master the skills required for future workplaces.
This study illustrates that ChatGPT can serve as a strategic ally for professionals in the MICE
industry, particularly in streamlining planning and marketing content workflows. Through
qualitative interviews and two detailed case studies in Southeast Asia, we demonstrated how
generative AI can enhance efficiency, stimulate creative ideation, support multilingual and
culturally adaptive content, and foster inclusive human-AI collaboration. The findings affirm the
value of ChatGPT not only as a time-saving tool but also as a catalyst for cross-functional
participation and innovation in the planning and execution of events.
However, the study also identified critical challenges, including risks of automation bias, the
necessity of human editorial oversight, ethical dilemmas in content attribution, and the importance
of maintaining cultural authenticity. These challenges highlight the need for strategic governance
frameworks that integrate technological potential with professional judgment and community
values.
