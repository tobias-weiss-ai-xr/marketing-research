Consumer Research on Intellectual and Developmental Disabilities:
A Literature Review Using the TCCM Framework

Doctoral Student, Graduate School of Economics, The University of Tokyo
Sara Okabe

Abstract
   Growing societal awareness regarding diversity, inclusion, and equity has led to increased
academic attention  placed on consumers with disabilities. However, most  of the  existing
studies have been focused on physical disabilities, while research on consumer behavior
involving individuals with intellectual and developmental disabilities remains underdeveloped.
In this study, a comprehensive scoping review of 27 relevant articles using the TCCM (Theory,
Context, Characteristics, and Methodology) framework is conducted , the literature is
organized and future research directions  are presented. Theoretical diversity is evident, but
only a few theories appear across multiple studies, indicating that the field is still in its early
stage of theory development. From a contextual perspective, research has often been
conducted to examine digital technologies, unique consumption behaviors, and the use of
social media. Future studies are likely to address emerging topics such as artificial intelligence,
influencers, and social interactions.  In terms of characteristics, scholars can categorize prior
research by type of participant, type and severity of disability, and dependent variables. In
future work, more detailed segmentation, interactions with other identities, and the impact of
corporate marketing strategies could be explored. Methodologically, while most studies have
used interviews or surveys, the combination of diverse approaches may enhance the
understanding of consumers who face difficulties in verbal communication. Longitudinal
research is also essential to consider temporal and social influences.

I. Introduction
Societal attention to diversity, equity, and inclusion is growing, leading to an increased
recognition of the need to consider marginalized consumers, which includes individuals with
disabilities, in marketing and consumer research (Arsel et al., 2022; Lteif et al., 2025; Okabe
& Motoki, 2024, 2025). Prior research into consumers with physical disabilities has been
expanded, covering areas such as accessible tourism, corporate social responsibility
perceptions regarding firms that employ individuals with disabilities, and the tailoring of
products to disability-specific needs (Li et al., 2023; Qiao et al., 2022). Studies have also
examined consumer stereotypes and digital consumption practices, highlighting the
diversity of consumption behaviors among individuals with disabilities (Annett-Hitchcock &
Xu, 2015; van der Sluis et al., 2024).
However, much of the existing research on consumer behavior is focused on individuals
with physical disabilities, which leads to people with intellectual and developmental
disabilities often being overlooked (World Health Organization, 2023). These conditions
affect cognitive, behavioral, and developmental functions and are estimated to impact
approximately 2–3% of the population (Kampert & Goreczny, 2007; National Institute of
Child Health and Human Development, 2021). Despite their growing societal presence,
research shows that positions such individuals as active consumers in the marketplace
remains limited.
Moreover, the market surrounding individuals with intellectual and developmental
disabilities is expanding. The global intellectual and developmental disability services
market is projected to grow from USD 13.15 billion in 2025 to USD 24.68 billion by 2035
(Market Research Intellect, 2026). However, these developments also highlight the specific
vulnerabilities and support needs of these individuals, which underscores the necessity of
marketing strategies that consider their decision-making contexts (Centers for Medicare &
Medicaid Services, 2023; Consumer Product Safety Commission, 2021).
The existing research has been focused primarily on medical, welfare, and employment-
related issues (e.g., Bush & Tassé, 2017; Sappok et al., 2019; Totsika et al., 2022), with
limited attention given to consumer behavior beyond the area of support services. While

Ⅱ. Methodology
We present a scoping review of the consumer research on intellectual and developmental
disabilities. Scoping reviews are suitable for mapping fragmented research fields and
identifying gaps for future investigations (Munn et al., 2018; Westphaln et al., 2021). This
approach is appropriate for this study because the existing studies are dispersed across
disciplines and lack a unified theoretical foundation, making structured mapping more
suitable than formal evidence synthesis. Thus, in this study, the TCCM framework (theory,
context, characteristics, and methodology), which facilitates structured analysis and the
identification of future research directions (Strzelecki et al., 2024), is adopted. To ensure
transparency and reproducibility, we used the Web of Science database, which is focused on
peer-reviewed journal articles across disciplines. While this approach may limit the breadth
of coverage, it enables a systematic and transparent identification of the relevant studies. We
used the keywords “Consumer Behavior,” “Consumer Research,” and “Marketing” in
combination (via AND searches) with 22 terms related to intellectual and developmental
disabilities, as identified by the International Statistical Classification of Diseases and
Related Health Problems (ICD)-10 classification (World Health Organization, 2019). The
ICD-10 is a global diagnostic tool used for health statistics and international disease and
mortality data comparisons (Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare, 2014). Chapters F70–
F79 pertain to intellectual disabilities, whereas F80–F89 refer to developmental disorders.
Accordingly, this review is focused exclusively on studies that fall within these two
categories. In addition, genetically defined conditions such as Down syndrome were
excluded to maintain conceptual consistency with the ICD-10 classification framework
adopted in this study. In other words, we conducted the search as follows: (Consumer

The search was set to cover the period from 1990—the year the WHO introduced the

any articles that primarily addressed medical contexts (e.g., drug efficacy or side effects,
health conditions) or etiological symptom factors. We included only those studies in which
individuals with intellectual and developmental disabilities or their caregivers were engaged
as consumers or those in which the presence of individuals with intellectual and
developmental disabilities influenced the behavior of other consumers. Although recent
scholarship has begun to define service recipients in health care, care, or training programs
as “consumers,” thereby examining support services from a marketing and consumer
behavior perspective (McClimens & Hyde, 2012), such interpretations are beyond the scope
of this review. Rather, we focused exclusively on studies that explore consumer decision-
making in the context of market-based goods and services. These criteria were applied to
maintain a clear analytical focus on market-based consumer behavior rather than to provide
exhaustive coverage of all related conditions and service contexts. We considered only peer-
reviewed journal articles that had been published or accepted for publication and were
written in English. Ultimately, 27 articles met the inclusion criteria and were selected for
review (see Figure 1).

 (2) Type and condition of disabilities
In consumer research concerning intellectual and developmental disabilities, identifying
the type of disability that participants possess is crucial because intellectual and
developmental disabilities encompass a variety of symptoms and severity levels, and these
differences may be reflected in distinct consumer behaviors (Neely-Barnes, Graff, et al.,
2008; Neely-Barnes, Marcenko, & Weber, 2008). A large proportion of studies have been
focused specifically on ASD (59%), with their target groups often clearly defined (e.g.,
Chiscano & Darcy, 2024; Frączek et al., 2021). This concentration suggests that other types
of intellectual disabilities have remained underexplored. In addition, little research had been
conducted to consider the intersection of disability with other identities, such as gender or
sexual orientation, despite evidence from related fields having suggested that such
intersections can shape experiences and outcomes (Abbott, 2013).
Overall, the current research lacks sufficient granularity in the categorization of disability
types and conditions, which highlights the need for more nuanced segmentation and
intersectional perspectives.

(e.g., Daniel et al., 2009; Nuñez-Gomez et al., 2020). The existing findings suggest that

Ⅳ. General Discussion
This study is aimed at providing a structured overview of the research landscape rather than
a comprehensive synthesis of findings. Given the limited number of studies identified, the
focus is placed on the mapping of key themes and the identification of research gaps rather
than on deriving generalized conclusions.
Consumer behavior research related to individuals with intellectual and developmental
disabilities has been increasing annually and continues to gain importance. However, only 27
studies were included in this review, and research that positions such individuals as
“consumers” in terms of decision -making in the marketplace remains limited. Furthermore,
few such studies have been published in mainstream marketing or consumer behavior journals.
Moving forward, the advancement of research from a wide range of perspectives is necessary
for the inclusion of intellectually and developmentally disabled consumers in the marketplace.
1. Theory
In the research on consumers with intellectual and developmental disabilities, a wide
range of theories and conceptual frameworks from various fields have been employed.
However, few of these have been consistently applied across multiple studies. Moreover,
many of the cited concepts lack the structure of a formal theory and remain at the level of
broad constructs such as QOL. Thus, theoretical development specific to consumers with
intellectual and developmental disabilities is still in its infancy. Nevertheless, insights from
other academic domains and research on other minority groups may offer promising
directions for future research.
One such promising direction is the incorporation of perspectives from disability studies,
with a particular emphasis on the social model of disability, in which disability is
conceptualized as arising from the interaction between individuals and their social and

developmental disabilities shape consumer attitudes and market outcomes.
2. Context
Recent research has been focused primarily on digital technologies, distinctive consumer
behavior patterns, and social media. While these areas have provided important insights,
they also indicate a concentration of research contexts. Future studies should expand this
scope through an examination of emerging technological environments, such as AI- and
robot-assisted services, and their impact on not only individuals with intellectual and
developmental disabilities but also on other consumers in shared service settings (Gfeller,
2024). In addition, although the research on social media has increased, the roles of
individuals with intellectual and developmental disabilities as content creators or influencers
remains underexplored (cf. Bonilla-del-Río et al., 2022). Investigating how the online
activities of these individuals shape consumer perceptions and interactions represents a
promising direction. Furthermore, greater attention should be given to social interactions in
marketplace environments. Prior research has suggested that interactions between
individuals with and without disabilities may reduce stigma and influence consumer
experiences (Sailor et al., 1993). Future research should therefore examine these mutual
effects, as well as the role of support networks such as caregivers and families, in shaping
access to resources and decision-making processes.

The existing research has been focused primarily on individuals with intellectual and
developmental disabilities and their caregivers, and limited attention has been given to other
consumer types. Future studies should examine how the inclusion of individuals with
intellectual and developmental disabilities—particularly as employees in service settings—
affects the perceptions and behaviors of other consumers. Such research may provide
insights into both market outcomes and broader issues such as employment opportunities
and well-being. Another key limitation of the existing research is the lack of segmentation.
Many studies have treated intellectual and developmental disabilities as a homogeneous
category, despite substantial variation in type and severity. Future research should adopt
more fine-grained classifications to better capture the differences in consumer needs and
behaviors. In addition, intersectionality remains largely unexplored. Characteristics such as
sex, age, and sexual orientation may interact with disability status in shaping consumer
experiences, which suggests the need for more nuanced analyses. This perspective can also
be extended to examine how the caregiver characteristics influence support dynamics and
outcomes. Finally, greater attention is needed to elucidate the link between consumer
behavior research and concrete marketing outcomes. While some studies have examined
responses to advertising, further research should investigate how variations in marketing
strategies affect both individuals with intellectual and developmental disabilities and the
individuals within their support networks.
4. Methodology
The existing research has largely relied on interviews and survey-based approaches and
often employed either qualitative or quantitative methods in isolation. This suggests the
need for greater methodological integration. Future research should adopt mixed methods
designs to capture both the depth of individual experiences and the broader behavioral
patterns at play. In addition, expanding the range of data collection and analysis techniques
represents an important direction. The adoption of multimodal approaches that incorporate
text, voice, and visual data may help overcome limitations in verbal expression (Roomkham
et al., 2022). Furthermore, recent developments in AI and machine learning offer
opportunities to analyze large-scale behavioral data, such as social media activity and service
usage, as well as to explore the use of simulated respondents (e.g., silicon samples) for
hypothesis generation (Sarstedt et al., 2024). Finally, longitudinal approaches are needed to
capture changes over time and the impact of broader social and technological

Ⅴ. Limitations
This study has several limitations. First, the literature search was limited to the Web of
Science database, so the scope should be expanded in future research through the
incorporation of additional databases such as Scopus or PsycINFO. Second, the exclusion of
certain conditions, including Down syndrome, may have resulted in the omission of relevant
insights from adjacent research domains. These limitations should be considered when the
findings are interpreted.
In addition, a strict systematic review format based on a defined research question was not
adopted in this study, which rather took a scoping approach to provide a comprehensive
overview of recent research. Future studies could build on this review to conduct more
rigorous reviews with greater alignment to specific research questions.

Ⅵ. Conclusion
In this scoping review,  27 peer -reviewed studies on consumer behavior related to
individuals with intellectual and developmental disabilities  that had been published between
1990 and 2025  were examined . Although the number of studies has increased over time,
research in this domain remains limited and dispersed across disciplines.  By organizing the
literature via the TCCM framework, several key limitations  were highlighted in this study .
The existing research is characterized by fragmented theoretical foundations, a concentration
in specific contexts such as digital technologies, limited attention  given to diverse consumer
segments, and a reliance on a narrow range of methodologies. These patterns indicate that
the field has yet to develop a coherent and cumulative body of knowledge. This fragmentation
also points to important directions for future resea rch. Advancements in this  field require
greater theoretical integration, broader contextual exploration, more nuanced consumer
segmentation, and the adoption of diverse methodological approaches. In particular,
positioning individuals with intellectual and developmental disabilities as active consumers in
marketplace settings remains an important but underexplored area.  Overall, this review
