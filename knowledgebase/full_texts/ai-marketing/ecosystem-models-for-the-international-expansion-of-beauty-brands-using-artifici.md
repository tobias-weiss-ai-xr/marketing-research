Introduction. Contemporary beauty brands increasingly rely on social media,
digital platforms, personalized recommendations, and direct communication channels.
Traditional international expansion models based on sequential geographic growth
through distributors and retail networks require substantial resources and do not always
provide the speed of adaptation needed in foreign markets.
Research on online consumer behavior confirms the importance of trust, social
influence, and peer recommendations in purchase decisions (Chaudhary et al., 2024). The
global beauty market continues to evolve (McKinsey & Company, 2025), while e -
commerce expands opportunities for cross-border sales (United Nations Conference on
Trade and Development [UNCTAD], 2024). At the same time, AI tools provide additional
capabilities for personalization and consumer behavior analysis (Lo et al., 2025).
Against this backdrop, an ecosystem approach integrates technological,
marketing, analytical, and organizational components. Integrating AI with digital
communities and localized communication channels is particularly important. The
effectiveness of this approach depends on data quality, the fit between the value
proposition and audience needs, and trust in communication channels (Garg & Bakshi,
2024; Rizomyliotis et al., 2024).
Aim and objectives of the study. The study aims to develop an approach to the
international expansion of beauty brands through an ecosystem model integrated with
artificial intelligence and expert communities.
The objectives are to design the architecture of the ecosystem model, systematize
its components and AI tools, identify key metrics, define expected outcomes, and
formulate recommendations for applying the model to international expansion.
Results and discussion. The proposed ecosystem model combines ten
interrelated components, E0–E9, covering digital sales, personalization, communities,
micro-influencer marketing, analytics, communications, social media, logistics, and
regulatory compliance. Their integration cre ates a unified system of brand- consumer
interaction across the customer journey (Table 1).

The core direct-to-consumer (D2C) platform, E0, enables direct brand-consumer
interaction and the accumulation of engagement data. Building on this infrastructure,
E1 uses recommender systems to create individualized product offerings (Lo et al.,
2025). E2 focuses on digital community development, while natural language
processing (NLP) tools can support sentiment analysis. E3 provides localized
communication through micro- influencers; research in the beauty segment confirms
the importance of credibility, t rust, and audience interaction (Garg & Bakshi, 2024;
Rizomyliotis et al., 2024).
E4 supports customer journey analytics, including A/B testing and behavioral
forecasting. E5 uses user-generated content (UGC) as a source of social proof. E6 and
E7 deliver personalized communication through email, push channels, and social
media. The eff ectiveness of these components should be assessed using customer
acquisition cost (CAC), customer lifetime value (LTV), return on advertising spend
(ROAS), conversion rate, and retention rate based on actual brand-level data.
E8 links digital sales with physical order fulfillment. Inventory management and
supply chain optimization are particularly important for international expansion
because delivery delays and high return rates affect the customer experience and sales
unit economics. E9 provides the regulatory infrastructure required for data protection
and compliance in target markets.
Dependence on external digital platforms also creates ecosystem risks. Changes
in platform algorithms or terms of service may affect brand access to target audiences
and customer acquisition costs. Brands should therefore develop owned digital assets,
including customer relationship management (CRM) systems, proprietary email lists,
loyalty programs, and brand-owned communities.
Conclusions. The proposed ecosystem model for the international expansion of
beauty brands integrates ten components, E0–E9, ranging from a D2C platform and
AI-enabled personalization to community management, micro- influencer marketing,
analytics, logistics, and regulatory compliance. Their integration establishes a unified
system of consumer interaction and provides a foundation for adapting the brand to
international markets.
The model’s principal advantage is the integration of digital sales,
personalization, community engagement, and behavioral analytics. The quantitative
indicators presented are target benchmarks and require empirical validation using data
from specific brands.
Further research should test the model and estimate the effects of its individual
components on CAC, LTV, ROAS, and retention rates across international markets
with different levels of digital maturity.
