Abstract – Emerging technologies, particularly
Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI), are
reshaping the hospitality industry, offering new
opportunities for personalization, customer
engagement, and operational efficiency. This study
focuses on understanding how hospitality professionals
perceive the potential benefits  and challenges of
GenAI, specifically by analyzing LinkedIn discussions
using Text Mining techniques. Through sentiment
analysis, topic modeling, and TF -IDF analysis, the
research explores the prevailing attitudes, key themes,
and significant terms related to GenAI in the
hospitality sector. Results reveal that while there is
excitement about GenAI's revolutionary potential for
enhancing customer service, marketing campaigns, and
overall operational productivity, concerns regarding
data privacy, ethical implications, and the protection of
consumer trust remain significant barriers. The study
offers valuable insights for researchers, hotel
managers, and industry stakeholders to navigate the
integration of GenAI into hospitality practices,
emphasizing the nee d for ethical guidelines and
responsible implementation to maximize its benefits.
Keywords – Generative AI, hospitality, LinkedIn,
text mining.

2. Literature Review

The rapid emergence of Generative Artificial
Intelligence (GenAI ) has attracted increasing
scholarly and professional attention, particularly for
its transformative potential in hospitality. Existing
research highlights how AI -driven tools, such as
chatbots and virtual concierges, enhance customer
service efficiency, o ptimize marketing
personalization, and support operational innovation.
At the same time, studies emphasize unresolved
challenges, including ethical dilemmas, data privacy
risks, and the need for regulatory frameworks to
safeguard consumer trust. Beyond the  hospitality
sector, public perceptions of GenAI have been
explored across diverse contexts through sentiment
analysis and text mining, revealing ambivalent
attitudes that combine enthusiasm for its creative and
practical applications with skepticism towar d its
limitations and unintended consequences. This body
of literature provides a critical foundation for
understanding how professionals in hospitality
engage with GenAI, situating their perspectives
within broader debates on technological adoption,
opportunities, and risks.

2.1. Generative AI and Hospitality

The hospitality sector has demonstrated great
promise for changing customer service with
generative AI, especially models like ChatGPT.
Effective handling of consumer questions and
complaints by these AI systems results in quick
answers and solutions that shorten wait times and
raise customer satisfaction [1 2]. Through the
resolution of the majority of general questions,
ChatGPT improves the customer experience by
improving smooth and happy interactions [9], [13],
[14], [1 5].  Furthermore, AI -driven chatbots and
virtual concierges allow human workers to
concentrate on more intricate and customized visitor
interactions [16], [17].
More and more personalized campaigns are being
created in marketing with generative AI. AI
customizes marketing messages and offers by
examining consumer data, increasing promotional
initiatives' efficacy and relevancy. Higher conversion
rates and better client retention are the results of this
tailored strategy [8], [9], [13], [15], [17]. Moreover,
AI-driven technologies can produce exciting
material, including virtual tours and customized
vacation suggestions, which draw prospective clients
and improve their decision-making [17].
Even if generative AI has much to offer the hotel
sector, several obstacles and moral issues still need to
be resolved. AI systems depend heavily on enormous
volumes of personal data to work correctly. Hence,
data privacy and security are serious issues.
Maintaining consumer trust and preventing possible
breaches that could damage a company's reputation
needs making sure that this data is used morally and
securely [8], [9], [15], [16], [17]. The absence of
well-defined AI laws also represents a significant
obstacle to the broader use of AI technology in the
hotel industry [9], [15].
In conclusion, managing the related risks and
ethical issues is critical even if generative AI presents
significant chances to improve customization,
operational effectiveness, and competitive advantage
in the hospitality sector. Through ethical standards
and responsible practices, hospitality companies may
use AI to propel innovation and expansion while
preserving client confidence and happiness.

3. Methodology

To explore how hospitality professionals perceive
the impact of Generative Artificial Intelligence
(GenAI), this study adopts a text -mining approach
applied to professional discussions on LinkedIn.
Given the platform’s role as a global network  for
industry experts, it provides a relevant and diverse
source of opinions from managers, consultants, and
academics.
The methodology combines a systematic process
of data collection, selection, and cleaning with
advanced computational techniques to ensure
analytical rigor. Sentiment analysis, topic modeling,
and TF -IDF analysis are employed to capture
prevailing attitude s, recurring themes, and salient
terms related to GenAI in hospitality. This multi-
layered strategy enables a comprehensive
understanding of professional perspectives while
ensuring the reliability and validity of findings.

3.1. Data Recollection

The current study investigates how hotel industry
professionals perceive generative artificial
intelligence (AI) and its effects. To this end, views
from experts in the field —administrators,
consultants, and academics (professors and
researchers)—have been collected. Since LinkedIn is
a professional network that can unite leaders and
specialists from many sectors, it was selected as the
data-collecting platform.
First, a thorough LinkedIn search was conducted
using terms connected to the hotel sector and
generative artificial intelligence. Among other terms
used in this search were "Generative AI,"
"hospitality," "hospitality industry," and "artificial
intelligence in hospitality." For a current and
pertinent time range that offers an updated picture of
viewpoints, the analysis period was chosen to run
from April 2023 to April 2024.
The articles were selected according to strict
inclusion and exclusion standards to guarantee the
accuracy and usefulness of the gathered information.
Written by authors recognized as hospitality business
experts, the articles mainly addressed the use and
perception of generative AI in the hotel sector.
Among these were university professors, managers
of hotels and resorts, and consultants with
backgrounds in putting technology i nto the hotel
sector. Published in April 2023– April 2024, the
articles must be in English. To be sure the pieces
were genuine and original, more Google searches
were done to confirm that they did not come from
other sources, such as newspapers. Articles th at
mentioned generative artificial intelligence but did
not specifically address how it might be used in the
hotel sector were not eligible. Furthermore, excluded
were pieces authored in languages other than
English, publications outside the designated per iod
range, and writers with unclear profiles or without
clear proof of their professionalism in the hospitality
industry. Recall that only whole publications were
considered, not just comments on already -published
pieces.

3.2. Sentimental Analysis

Sentiment analysis, sometimes known as opinion
mining, is a natural language processing method that
uses word choice, context, and tone to categorize text
as positive, negative, or neutral. This area, studies
attitudes and feelings regarding themes, services, and
products [32], [33]. Simple lexicon-based methods to
complex machine learning models are examples of
the many methods used extensively in business,
politics, and social sciences to measure public
opinion [34], [3 5]. Though helpful, sentiment
analysis has limitations like irony, sarcasm, and
context-dependent sentiments, which calls for
continuous study to increase precision [3 6]. Two
future possibilities are improving multimodal
sentiment analysis—which combines text, audio, and
visual data—and creating more sophisticated models
to comprehend intricate emotional expressions [37].
A sentimental analysis was done on the papers to
ascertain the general opinion on generative AI in the
hospitality sector. Sentiment analysis recognizes and
classifies the views represented in the work, usually
optimistic, harmful, or neutral. This reveale d general
opinions and sentiments of experts on the effects of
generative AI on the sector. Remarkably, raw text
data can be processed by systems like Google Cloud
Natural Language without much cleaning. Even from
unprocessed text, these technologies can successfully
extract meaning and emotion because they are made
to handle a range of text types.
They reduce the need for data cleansing by using
advanced natural language processing algorithms that
can handle text changes, punctuation, and noise.

3.3. Topic Modeling

Topic modeling is a powerful text analysis
method that has drawn considerable interest in
information retrieval and natural language processing
(NLP). This statistical technique is applied to find
and classify the underlying themes, or "to pics," in a
sizable corpus of text documents [3 8]. Using word
usage patterns within a text corpus, topic modeling
algorithms find clusters of terms that frequently
occur together. Afterward, these clusters are read as
the "topics" covered in the papers.
Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) is one of the
most often applied topic modeling techniques [3 8].
(LDA), a generative probability model is based on
the supposition that every document is a combination
of a few subjects and that every word in the
document is related to one of those topics [3 9]. This
method makes finding themes throughout several
papers and understanding a text corpus's theme
structure possible.
Several procedures were taken to guarantee
successful text mining analysis in processing the
articles on the perception of generative AI in the
hospitality sector. The quality and applicability of the
study depended critically on the data preparation and
cleaning [40]. First, the text was cleaned to guarantee
consistency by eliminating extraneous elements,
including HTML tags, numbers, too much
punctuation, and whitespace. Special characters were
also eliminated. This process normalized the text by
changing all characters to lowercase. The familiar
words "the," "is," and "at" —which do not
significantly advance the analysis —were then
eliminated. More significant words might be
examined when the data noise decreased by this stage
[41]. The text was next lemmat ized. As stemming
lowers words to their root form, lemmatization
converts words to their base or dictionary form
(lemma); for instance, "running" would be
transformed to "run" [4 2]. Through the consolidation
of several word forms, this procedure lowers the
dimensionality of the text and raises the analysis's
accuracy.
Following these preparation procedures, the text
was tokenized. Tokenization was breaking down the
text into smaller pieces, such as words or phrases, to
provide a bag-of -words representation  and a term -
document matrix that could be utilized for further
analysis [43]. Using topic modeling, the set of
articles' underlying themes were found for the text
analysis. Topics in the documents were found via
topic modeling, especially by Latent Dirichlet
Allocation (LDA) [44].

3.4. Keyword Frequency Analysis

Term Frequency -Inverse Document Frequency
(TF-IDF) analysis was carried out after tokenization.
An essential term in a document is assessed
statistically using the TF-IDF metric about a group of
documents [45]. TF-IDF analysis makes finding
relevant terms in certain articles easier by giving a
numerical value that shows how important a word is
to a document in a corpus. Both topic modeling and
sentiment analysis are enhanced by TF -IDF analysis
[46].
It is essential to do TF-IDF analysis immediately
after text cleaning because text cleaning eliminates
noise, extraneous components, and inconsistencies
from the data and guarantees that the TF -IDF scores
fairly represent the significance of significant phrases
[47]. High TF-IDF ratings may be attributed to
redundant or irrelevant phrases without appropriate
cleaning, which would skew the study and produce
false results [48].  Cleaned text guarantees that TF -
IDF concentrates on essential phrases. Particularly
helpful is TF-IDF research when examining how
professionals on LinkedIn see generative AI in the
hospitality sector. It can draw attention to particular
words and expressions that come up often and are
particular to conversations on generative artificial
intelligence. This helps professionals in the hotel
sector better comprehend the most pertinent and
significant features of generative AI. Finding these
important phrases facilitates the concentration on the
main ideas and feelings conveyed in the articles,
which results in more focused and wise conclusions
regarding the effects of generative AI in the
hospitality industry.

"Technological advancement" indicate a positive
view of AI's capacity to bring about major changes in
the sector [1 8]. There is a constant theme in
industries like IT and construction regarding the
possibility of AI bringing about major technological
breakthroughs [22], [23].
Workforce Displacement due to AI in Hospitality,
on the other hand, emphasizes the requireme nt of
workforce transition management plans and raises
concerns about job loss and unemployment [2 2],
[23]. This worry is comparable with opinions in the
construction sector, where automation is viewed as a
way to increase productivity and threaten jobs [22].
The Service Innovation and Customer Feedback
theme demonstrates how AI can improve customer
happiness and service delivery using technological
adaptation and personalization [5],  [22]. Similar high
hopes are voiced in the IT and education industries,
where AI improves productivity and user experience
[5], [23].
Furthermore, subjects like Ethical and Security
Concerns of AI in Hospitality tackle more general
trust and moral conundrums, exposing the divergent
views on the ethical ramifications of AI [2 3].
Reliability and Accuracy of AI Responses raise
questions regarding the consistency and transparency
of AI outputs [18], [25]. Discussions about AI in the
artistic and educational domains, where precision and
morality are critical, also raise these issues [5],  [25].
Innovative applications of AI in hospitality and A I in
training and professional development highlight AI's
creative applications in generating exceptional
experiences and promoting professional development
[26]. These conversations show how the adoption of
AI is dual, combining apprehension about its
practical and ethical issues with excitement for its
creative potential [5],  [22]. Professionals weighing
the inherent risks against the possible benefits of AI
reflect the conflicted emotions reflected in the
Balancing Expectations and Concerns of the AI
Adoption theme [2 3]. This nuanced viewpoint is
reflected in the creative industries, where concerns
about ethical violations and copyright problems
offset the possibility of AI boosting creativity [25].
Future studies should look into the causes of the
largely passive attitude toward generative AI in the
hospitality sector, contrasting it with more divisive
views in fields like construction and education.

Longitudinal studies might also chart the changes
in attitudes and subjects as AI technology develops
and is more included in business operations.
More research must be done on the ethical
ramifications of AI, especially about data privacy and
employment displacement. A worldwide view of AI
adoption's acceptance and difficulties could be
obtained by comparing research across many
geographical areas and cultural settings. At last, a
multidisciplinary study fusing knowledge from
human resources, ethics, and technology could create
all-encompassing plans to control the socioeconomic
effects of AI and guarantee that its adoption into
different sectors is both advantageous and fair.

6. Conclusions

Using topic modeling and LinkedIn comment
analysis, a sophisticated and subtle viewpoint on
generative AI in the hotel sector is revealed.
Professionals who see the possible advantages and
major obstacles related to AI adoption have a
balanced perspective, as seen by the largely impartial
opinion. Positive opinions show hope for improved
efficiency, personalization, and service innovation, as
well as a positive view of the revolutionary potential
of AI. On the other hand, consistent with research
from other sectors, including construction and
education, the noteworthy negative comments
highlight worries about cons umer trust, data privacy,
and ethical ramifications. This contradicting opinion
implies that although there is excitement about AI's
possibilities, serious concerns also need to be
addressed.
An extensive summary of the generative AI
debates is given by th e eleven themes that have been
identified. The widespread worries about data
leakage and privacy reflect a strong emphasis on
cybersecurity and protecting personal information.
Topics like service innovation and seeing the future
with AI emphasize the upbeat assessment of AI's
capacity to propel technology forward and enhance
consumer experiences. However, the dread of losing
workers and ethical and security issues show serious
doubts about how AI would affect employment and
moral issues. These results emph asize the need to
combine AI's creative potential with strong
approaches to handle its moral and practical issues.
The report highlights the importance of continuous
communication and investigation to negotiate the
difficulties of AI integration and guarantee that its
advantages are realized while reducing possible risks.
