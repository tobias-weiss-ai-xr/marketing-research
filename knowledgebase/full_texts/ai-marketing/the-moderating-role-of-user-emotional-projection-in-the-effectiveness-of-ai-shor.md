  Abstract
With the advancement of generative artificial intelligence technology, AI short
videos have assumed an increasingly important role in brand marketing.
Nevertheless, users exhibit markedly different marketing responses to identical AI
short videos, a variation largely moderated by their emotional projection.
Drawing upon emotional communication theory and media psychological
projection theory, this study employs literature integration and theoretical
construction to systematically review empirical stud ies published between 2020
and 2026 on platforms such as Douyin, Bilibili, and YouTube. It focuses on the
mechanism through which AI content characteristics —specifically the degree of
anthropomorphism and the lifelikeness of narrative scenarios —influence u sers’
purchase intention, and further examines the moderating effect of user emotional
projection within this process. The findings indicate that AI content
characteristics can significantly enhance purchase intention by stimulating users’
emotional projec tion and parasocial interaction; however, this effect is
substantially moderated by the individual’s level of emotional projection.
High-emotional-projection users are more inclined to form trust -based
attachment relationships, thereby strengthening marketing effectiveness, whereas
low-emotional-projection users tend to activate authenticity -scrutiny
mechanisms, which attenuates the persuasive impact. Moreover, the influence of
emotional projection on marketing effectiveness exhibits a nonlinear threshold
effect: the positive effects of content characteristics are fully realized only when
emotional projection reaches a certain level. This study constructs an integrated
analytical framework of “AI content characteristics → emotional projection →
parasocial in teraction → trust formation → purchase intention,” revealing the
pivotal moderating role of users’ psychological mechanisms in AI short -video
marketing and providing theoretical grounding for e -commerce content
optimization in the context of intelligent communication.

In recent years, the rapid development of short -video platforms has been reshaping
the digital marketing landscape. According to the 56th Statistical Report on Internet
Development in China, as of June 2025, the number of online video users in China
had reached 1.085 billion, accounting for 96.7% of the total internet population.
Against this massive user b ase, AI short videos are playing an increasingly vital role
in content creation, brand marketing, and social communication. The full -chain
empowerment—from script generation and dynamic storyboarding to
post-production editing —has given rise to an entirely  new mode of content
production. Compared with traditional human -made short videos, AI short videos,
through intelligent generation, emotionalized presentation, and personalized
recommendation, can achieve higher -frequency and more precise audience reach
while providing brands with new communication channels and user experiences. In
this process, users’ emotional identification with, and psychological projection onto,
AI short videos has emerged as a critical factor shaping marketing effectiveness.
However, “technological empowerment” does not automatically equate to
“marketing effectiveness.” Users’ reactions to equally well -crafted AI short videos
differ significantly. Some users develop strong emotional attachment to AI virtual
personas, treating virtual characters as companions and translating this attachment
into actual consumption behavior. Others, upon recognizing the AI identity of the
content, activate “authenticity scrutiny,” questioning or even resisting the message.
The influence of technological factors on marketing effectiveness is substantially
moderated by users’ psychological factors. This suggests that a crucial psychological
“regulating valve” —the degree of user emotional projection —operates between AI
technological features and marketing ef fectiveness. Therefore, this study aims to
explore the moderating role of user emotional projection in AI short -video
marketing effectiveness, reveal the mechanisms through which it influences
audience behavior along the cognitive -emotional pathway, and of fer theoretical
support and practical insights for AI short -video marketing.2. Ease of Use (Heading

2. Theoretical Framework
The theoretical foundation of this study primarily draws upon emotional
communication theory, media psychological projection theo ry, and research on AI
short-video marketing. Emotional communication theory emphasizes that media
content influences audiences not only through information transmission and
cognitive processing but also through emotional evocation that shapes attitudes an d
behaviors. Advertising research has demonstrated that emotional cues embedded in
narratives can elicit empathic responses from audiences, constituting a persuasive
pathway independent of rational argumentation. When audiences are moved by a
story, their defensive mechanisms related to persuasion knowledge tend to relax,
allowing brand information to permeate more smoothly. This provides the basic

proposition for the present study: the intensity of emotional arousal directly
influences marketing persuasion effectiveness.
Media psychological projection theory further illuminates the psychological
mechanism underlying emotional bonding. During media interaction, audiences
project their own emotional states, social cognition, and value expectations onto
media characters, thereby generating a sense of identification and emotional
involvement. The “parasocial interaction” theory proposed by Horton and Wohl
indicates that audiences can form imaginary intimate bonds with media figures that
resemble real interperson al relationships, and these bonds rely on sustained
emotional projection. In the context of AI short videos, although virtual characters
lack intrinsic emotions and intentions, anthropomorphic appearances, empathetic
language, and lifelike scenarios can st ill elicit user emotional projection, resulting in
a parasocial-interaction-like viewing experience.
Research on AI short -video marketing further suggests that users perceive an
“authenticity discount” towards AI -generated content —even when the content
quality is indistinguishable from human creations, awareness of its AI identity often
reduces trust and emotional investment. Concurrently, the “uncanny valley” effect
indicates that when anthropomorphism approaches but does not fully attain
human-likeness, users’ sense of affinity may decline and even trigger rejection. This
implies that the effectiveness of AI short -video marketing relies not only on
technological realization but also on users’ psychological acceptance mechanisms.
Synthesizing the above, thi s study proposes an analytical framework of “emotional
affordance → emotional projection → parasocial interaction → marketing
effectiveness.” Here, “emotional affordance” refers to the symbolic features and
narrative elements of AI short -video content that  can activate user emotional
projection, including anthropomorphic appearance, empathetic language, culturally
resonant narratives, and immersive scenarios. When emotional affordance is high,
users’ emotional projection is activated, parasocial interaction  is established, and
marketing messages are received smoothly; conversely, when emotional affordance
is insufficient, users maintain an analytical “spectator” stance, and marketing
effectiveness is hindered. Based on this, the study advances the following core
propositions: first, the level of emotional affordance of AI short videos positively
influences user emotional projection; second, user emotional projection positively
moderates the marketing effectiveness of AI short videos; third, the perception of
the AI label mediates the relationship between emotional projection and marketing
effectiveness.2.2. Maintaining the Integrity of the Specifications

3.2. Literature Search and Screening
To systematically retrieve high -quality literature directly relevant to the research
question, this study employed CNKI, the We b of Science Core Collection, and Scopus
as the primary retrieval databases, adopting a combined strategy of subject
searching and snowballing. The search terms combined “AI short video,” “virtual
streamer,” “AI -generated content” with “marketing effective ness,” “purchase
intention,” “persuasion effect” and “emotional projection,” “parasocial interaction,”
“anthropomorphism.” The retrieval time span was set from 2020 to 2026 to cover
the key developmental period of short -video marketing research against the
backdrop of rapid iteration of generative AI technology.
The initial database search yielded 286 articles, which were reduced to 231 after
deduplication. Through reading titles and abstracts for preliminary screening,
articles clearly irrelevant to the re search topic were excluded, retaining 86. After
full-text reading and secondary screening based on inclusion and exclusion criteria,
32 core articles were finally included. The included literature encompasses
mainstream short-video platforms such as Douyin , Bilibili, and YouTube, and covers
diverse content genres including AI cute pets, virtual streamers, AI health science
communication, and AI rural narratives, thus providing good coverage and
representativeness in terms of research context.

4. Detailed Analysis
On the basis of the aforementioned theoretical framework and methodological
approach, this study systematically analyzes AI marketing short video s to explore

existing empirical evidence, it becomes apparent that the anthropomorphism of AI
imagery and the lifelikeness of narrative scenarios do not directly translate into
marketing effectiveness; rather, their effects are significantly moderated by the level
of user emotional projection. This moderating effect further reflects the coupling
relationship among the three elements of human, technology, and content within the

4.1. Emotional Projection: From Information Reception to Emotional
Compensation
Traditional e -commerce marketing models largely follow a  “stimulus-response”
paradigm, treating short videos as mere channels for information transmission. In
the context of AIGC, however, users’ viewing behavior has evolved into a process of
“meaning negotiation.” Zhao Hanlu’s (2026) analysis of YouTube commen ts
revealed that some users, when watching AI rural short videos, frequently used
expressions such as “like an old friend” and “understands my homesickness,”
indicating that high -emotional-projection users are not objectively evaluating AI
technology but a re instead initiating relational decoding —endowing AI characters
with intentionality and personality continuity through a psychological completion
mechanism. Similarly, the survey study by Huang Jiaqi and Chen Xiawei (2026)
found that users with higher emo tional resonance scores perceived the
persuasiveness of AI health videos as significantly greater than those with lower
resonance.
These findings suggest that emotional projection is not a byproduct of technological
deficiency but a natural human tendency to seek emotional compensation in the
digital environment. For high -projection users, AI short videos transcend their
commodity-display function and become media carriers for confirming self -identity
and accommodating emotional needs. In this context, mark eting conversion is no
longer the outcome of pure informational persuasion but a byproduct of

psychological resonance. This mechanism explains why some AI short videos,
despite possessing only ordinary technical quality, can elicit strong user
responses—the key lies in whether the content has triggered emotional projection.
4.2. The Moderating Effect of Emotional Projection: Resonance
Amplification and Threshold Activation
The influence of emotional projection on AI content characteristics is not a simple
linear addition; rather, it exhibits clear boundary conditions. Ma Xiaofei et al. (2026)
demonstrated that AI cute -pet and virtual -streamer videos with a higher degree of
anthropomorphism are more likely to evoke users’ empathetic comments; users
spontaneously supplement personality backgrounds for AI characters in comment
sections, engaging in “character dialogue” behavior. This phenomenon is
particularly prominent among high -emotional-projection users, indicating that
anthropomorphic signals provide a psych ological space for emotional projection:
users soften the technological boundary through autonomous emotional filling,
generating a perception of “sincere companionship,” which in turn amplifies
marketing persuasiveness.
Conversely, for low -emotional-projection users, even when confronted with equally
highly anthropomorphic AI short videos, their reactions tend more toward
“authenticity scrutiny.” Chen Ming and Du Qian (2026) pointed out that some users,
upon recognizing the AI identity, perceive an “emotio nal deception,” which then
negatively impacts brand trust. This suggests that when emotional projection
remains unactivated, users maintain an analytical cognitive stance, and
anthropomorphic features may paradoxically reinforce the perception of
“non-humanness,” thereby suppressing marketing effectiveness.
Taken together, emotional projection exerts a “threshold effect” in AI short -video
marketing: only when the level of projection reaches a certain threshold can
anthropomorphic and lifelike features be tr ansformed into marketing effectiveness
through the parasocial interaction pathway; below this threshold, the effects of
content characteristics are counteracted by users’ psychological defense
mechanisms. This moderating mechanism reveals the deep -seated r eason why
similar AI short videos perform unevenly across different audience segments.
4.3. Practical Implications: From Technical Metrics to Emotional Fit
The above analysis points toward an optimization direction for AI short -video
marketing practice: th e mere pursuit of upgraded technical parameters (e.g., higher
fidelity, more complex rendering) does not linearly enhance conversion outcomes.
The findings of Huang Jiaqi and Chen Xiawei (2026) in the health communication
domain corroborate this point: for  high-emotionally-involved users, optimizing
personified narratives and emotional pacing can significantly boost behavioral
intention, whereas for low-involvement users, information density and clarity of the

Therefore, the content strategy for AI short videos should return to an
audience-centered approach and implement differentiated adaptation according to
users’ emotional projection tendencies. For high -projection groups, content creation
should downplay functional  indoctrination and strengthen narrative openness and
personified expression, enabling AI characters to serve as vehicles for constructing
parasocial relationships. For low -projection groups, redundant emotional packaging
should be stripped away, with a fo cus on information efficiency and credibility
endorsement. At the algorithmic recommendation level, platforms could also
incorporate users’ degree of emotional involvement as a key reference for content
matching, thereby preventing highly emotionally packa ged content from being
mismatched with low-projection users and causing experience attrition.
5. Conclusion
In summary, this study confirms that user emotional projection plays a key
moderating role in AI short -video marketing. The positive effects of AI co ntent
characteristics depend on the activation and depth of emotional projection.
High-emotional-projection users are able to form an effective cognitive -emotional
linkage,thereby enhancing marketing effectiveness, whereas
low-emotional-projection users ma y question and resist the content. The results
indicate that users’ psychological mechanisms are indispensable in shaping AI
short-video marketing effectiveness, and emotional projection, as a moderating hub,
provides an important theoretical basis for und erstanding audience psychology in
the era of intelligent communication, as well as empirical support for research on AI
short-video marketing mechanisms.
