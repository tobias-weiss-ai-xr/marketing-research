Abstract
Artificial intelligence (AI)-driven customer analytics has emerged as a transformative force in personalized
marketing within modern digital commerce ecosystems, driven by  the rapid growth of big data, real -time
consumer interaction platforms, and intelligent recommendation technologies. This systematic review
investigates the impact of AI -driven customer analytics on personalized marketing strategies across digital
commerce platforms during the period from 2019 to 2026. The study explores how AI technologies, including
machine learning, predictive analytics, recommendation systems, natural language processing, sentiment
analysis, marketing automation, and AI-enabled customer relationship management systems, affect customer
engagement, purchasing behavior, personalization performance, and organizational outcomes in digital
environments. The review was conducted following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and
Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) framework to ensure methodological rigor, transparency, and systematic synthesis
of existing literature. Relevant academic publications were collected from major scholarly databases, including
Scopus, Web of Science, ScienceDirect, IEEE Xplore, SpringerLink, Emerald Insight, Wiley Online Library,
and Google Scholar. After applying screening, eligibility, and quality assessment criteria, 126 peer -reviewed
studies were selected for qualitative and thematic analysis.  The findings indic ate that AI -driven customer
analytics substantially enhances personalization accuracy, customer segmentation, recommendation quality,
and real-time engagement across e-commerce websites, mobile commerce applications, social media platforms,
and omnichannel retail environments. The review further reveals that predictive analytics, recommendation
engines, conversational AI systems, and automated marketing technologies contribute significantly to
customer retention, loyalty, conversion optimization, and overall customer satisfaction. In addition, big data
infrastructures and cloud computing technologies were identified as essential enablers of adaptive and real -
time personalized marketing strategies.  The study also highlights the importance of consumer trust,
transparency, privacy protection, and ethical governance in shaping user acceptance of AI -powered
personalization technologies. Several studies reported concerns regarding algorithmic bias, behavioral
surveillance, cybersecurity vulnerabilities, and regulat ory compliance in digital commerce ecosystems.
Furthermore, the review emphasizes the increasing influence of social media analytics, AI-powered advertising
systems, and influencer engagement technologies on consumer behavior and purchasing decisions.  Overall,
the study concludes that AI -driven customer analytics has become a strategic foundation for personalized
marketing, customer relationship management, and competitive advantage within global digital commerce
ecosystems.

Governance Challenges in AI-Driven Customer Analytics
Consumer privacy has become one of the most significant ethical concerns in AI -driven customer
analytics because personalized marketing systems depend on extensive collection, processing, and
interpretation of customer data. Digital commerce platforms commonly gather information from
browsing histories, purchase records, search behavior, location data, social media interactions, device
identifiers, customer reviews, and engagement patterns to construct detailed consumer profiles. These
profiles allow firms to deliver personalized recommendations, targeted advertisements, dynamic
offers, and individualized communication  (Chaturvedi & Verma, 2023 ). The literature shows that
privacy concern increases when customers feel that platforms collect excessive personal information or
use behavioral data without clear consent. Personalized marketing can improve customer experience
when it provides relevant and convenient recommendations, but it can also create discomfort when
consumers perceive that their actions are be ing continuously monitored. Behavioral surveillance
marketing is ethically sensitive because it transforms ordinary digital activities into commercial
intelligence used to predict, influence, and shape purchasing behavior. Researchers have argued that
this creates tension between personalization benefits and consumer autonomy. Customers may accept
data collection when they understand its purpose and receive meaningful value in return, but they may
resist when platforms appear opaque, intrusive, or manipulat ive (Saurabh et al., 2022). The literature
also highlights that privacy concern is strongly connected with trust, perceived control, consent clarity,
and platform credibility. Therefore, ethical personalized marketing requires responsible data collection,
transparent communication, and customer -centered privacy management  (Ogbuke et al., 2022 ).
Algorithmic bias represents a major governance challenge in AI -driven customer analytics because
recommendation and personalization models may produce unequal or unfair outcomes when trained
on incomplete, imbalanced, or historically biased data. In digital commerce, algorithms influence
product visibility, advertising exposure, promotional offers, credit -related marketing, customer
segmentation, and recommendation ranking (Carmody et al., 2021 ). When these systems reproduce
existing social, demographic, or behavioral inequalities, customers may receive different levels of
service quality, pricing access, product recommendations, or promotional opportunities. The literature
emphasizes that bias can emerge from data selection, model design, platform objectives, or hidden
assumptions embedded in analytical systems.

METHODS
This study followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta -Analyses
(PRISMA) guidelines to ensure a systematic , transparent, and rigorous review process for examining
the role of AI -driven customer analytics in personalized marketing within digital commerce
environments between 2019 and 2026. The PRISMA framework was selected because it provides a
structured methodological approach for identifying, screening, evaluating, and synthesizing scholarly
literature in a manner that enhances research reliability, reproducibility, and analytical transparency.
The review process began with the formulation of clearly defined research objectives and thematic
boundaries focusing on artificial intelligence technologies, customer analytics systems,
recommendation engines, personalized marketing strategies, consumer behavior, marketing
automation, social commerce analytics, natural  language processing, customer relationship
management, and ethical governance in digital commerce. Relevant studies were systematically
searched using multiple academic databases, including Scopus, Web of Science, ScienceDirect, IEEE
Xplore, SpringerLink, Emerald Insight, Taylor & Francis, Wiley Online Library, and Google Scholar to
ensure broad disciplinary coverage across marketing, information systems, artificial intelligence, data
science, and digital business research domains. The search strategy inco rporated a combination of
keywords and Boolean operators associated with the core concepts of the study, including “AI-driven
customer analytics,” “personalized marketing,” “digital commerce,” “machine learning in marketing,”
“recommendation systems,” “con sumer behavior analytics,” “marketing automation,” “social media
analytics,” “customer relationship management,” “predictive analytics,” “sentiment analysis,” and
“artificial intelligence in e -commerce.” The search process initially identified 1,487 record s from
database searches and supplementary reference tracking procedures. Duplicate articles, conference
overlaps, inaccessible documents, editorials, book reviews, and non -peer-reviewed sources were
removed during the preliminary screening stage, resulting in 1,062 unique records eligible for title and
abstract review. Studies were screened according to predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria
designed to ensure relevance and methodological consistency throughout the review process.

LIMITATION
This systematic review contains several limitations associated with the scope of the literature selection,
methodological variability among reviewed studies, and the rapidly evolving nature of artificial
intelligence technologies within digital commerce environments. The review was restricted to peer -
reviewed studies published in English between 2019 and 2026, which may have excluded relevant
research published in other languages, industry reports, technical white papers, conference
proceedings, or unpublished empirica l investigations that could provide additional insights into AI -
driven customer analytics and personalized marketing practices. The reviewed studies also
demonstrated substantial heterogeneity in research design, analytical frameworks, technological focus,
sample size, industrial context, and measurement approaches, making direct comparison across studies
challenging in some thematic areas. Many of the selected studies concentrated heavily on developed
digital markets and large -scale e -commerce platforms, p otentially limiting the generalizability of
findings to developing economies, small businesses, or industries with lower levels of digital
transformation and AI adoption. Another limitation relates to the rapidly changing technological
landscape of artificial intelligence, machine learning, recommendation systems, and customer analytics
infrastructures, as some technologies discussed in earlier studies may already have evolved
significantly by the end of the review period. Furthermore, the review focused pr edominantly on
digital commerce environments and may not fully capture AI -driven customer analytics practices
occurring in hybrid or non-digital commercial systems. Despite these limitations, the review provides
a comprehensive synthesis of current scholarly knowledge regarding AI-driven customer analytics and
personalized marketing within contemporary digital commerce ecosystems.
