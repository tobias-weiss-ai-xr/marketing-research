Abstract
Losing customers is one of the most persistent challenges confronting real estate businesses
operating in highly competitive markets, yet comparatively little attention has been paid to the
strategic value of winning those customers back, especially within emerging economies on the
African continent. This paper investigates how artificial intelligence -driven customer
reactivation capability shapes sales conversion outcomes among real estate companies
operating in Lagos, Nigeria. Anchored in relationship marketing theory and the commitment -
trust framework, the study surveyed 202 marketing practitioners drawn from 30 well -established
real estate firms across the Lagos Metropolitan area. Results from regression analysis showed
that AI -enabled customer reactivation capability exerts a statistically significant and positive
effect on sales conversion performance (β = 0.557, p < 0.001), explaining 31.0% of the variation
in conversion outcomes. In particular, the capacity of AI to flag customers at risk of
disengagement, craft targeted reactivation strategies, produce actionable insight for re -
engagement, and design personalized recovery campaigns proved to be key contributors to
conversion success. Overall, the results indicate that real estate firms that invest in AI -based
tools for detecting, understanding, and reconnecting with disengaged or lost customers stand to
markedly improve their conversion outcomes. This paper adds to the sparse body of research on
AI-driven customer recovery within African real estate markets and offers practical direction for
firms aiming to extract greater lifetime value from customers through deliberate reactivation
efforts.

Introduction
As the commercial nerve centre  of Nigeria, Lagos hosts a real estate sector that functions within
one of the most fast -paced and fiercely contested markets anywhere on the African continent.
Home to a population of over 20 million people and experiencing relentless urban expansion that
continues to fuel demand for both residential and commercial space, real estate companies in the
city are under considerable pressure to win and hold on to customers (Lagos State Government,
2020). This is a market defined by large transaction sizes, prolonged sales processes, and steep

costs associated with winning new clients, all of which make the recovery and retention of
customers a matter of strategic urgency for firms hoping to remain profitable over the long run.
Within such a setting, customer churn whether the loss of prospective or existing clients —
amounts to far more than a missed sale; it represents a genuine financial setback given the effort
and expense already sunk into generating leads and building relationships.
Conventional customer relationship management practice has tended to concentrate on winning
new business and holding on to currently active clients, frequently paying little attention to the
strategic opportunity presented by customers who have already drifted away or left outright. And
yet, evidence gathered across numerous industries points to former customers as a genuinely
valuable resource: they already understand the firm's offerings, have previously shown interest in
what it provides, and are typically cheaper to win back than entirely new prospects (Thomas et
al., 2004; Stauss & Friege, 1999). Within real estate specifically, where buyer decisions hinge
heavily on trust and familiarity, reconnecting with past clients may prove an especially
productive strategy.  Artificial intelligence has become a genuine game -changer in customer
relationship management, giving firms the ability to sift through enormous volumes of customer
data, forecast behavioural  trends, and deliver personalized communication at scale (Sharma &
Patel, 2020; Akter et al., 2021). CRM platforms built on AI can pick up early warning signs that
a customer is drifting away, sort disengaged customers according to their specific reasons for
leaving, and roll out reactivation campaigns fitted to each individual's history and preferences.
Such capabilities carry particular weight for reactivation efforts, which depend on getting the
targeting, timing, and degree of personalization right in order to succeed.
Even so, solid empirical research examining AI -driven customer reactivation strategies is still
thin on the ground, particularly with respect to real estate markets in emerging African
economies. Existing work has looked at how AI supports lead generation and customer retention
(Zhang & Li, 2020; Ahmad & Parsa, 2021), yet the specific pathways by which AI -enabled
reactivation capability shapes sales conversion have drawn comparatively little scholarly interest.
This omission carries particular weight in a market such as Lagos, where steep customer
acquisition costs paired with intense rivalry mean that every departed customer is a potential
source of recoverable value.  This paper sets out to close that gap by investigating how AI -
enabled customer reactivation capability affects sales conversion performance among real estate
firms operating in Lagos. It specifically pursues two questions: (1) Does AI -enabled customer
reactivation capability shape sales conversion performance within the Lagos real estate sector?
and (2) Which elements of AI-supported reactivation strategy contribute most strongly to driving
sales conversion? In doing so, the study offers both scholarly and practical value by generating
empirical evidence of the part AI plays in customer recovery within an emerging African market
context. It builds on relationship marketing theory by showing how AI -supported reactivation
strategies can help re -establish trust and commitment with customers who have disengaged,
while also providing real estate firms with practical direction on how to extract greater customer
lifetime value through deliberate recovery initiatives.

Literature Review
AI-Enabled Customer Reactivation Capability
Customer reactivation capability describes an organization's capacity to win back the loyalty of
customers who have gone dormant or abandoned the brand altogether (Verhoef, 2021; Boulding
et al., 2020). It covers the full range of strategies, processes, and technological tools that firms
rely on to spot, examine, and reconnect with former customers, all in pursuit of restoring the

relationship and unlocking renewed commercial value. This is a distinct undertaking from
customer retention, which is concerned with sustaining relationships that are already active;
reactivation efforts, by contrast, are aimed squarely at customers who have already left — a
challenge that calls for a different set of approaches and competencies.
Bringing artificial intelligence into reactivation strategy has considerably strengthened
organizations' capacity to carry out effective customer recovery programmes. AI -enabled
reactivation capability is made up of several interlocking components. To begin with, AI systems
make it possible to flag customers who are at risk of leaving, using predictive analytics that
scrutinize behavioural patterns, engagement metrics, and interaction histories (Zhao et al., 2022).
Machine learning models are able to pick up on faint indicators of disengagement — dropping
email open rates, fewer website visits, or a shift toward negative sentiment in communications —
that typically show up before a customer actually churns, allowing firms to step in proactively
before the relationship is lost entirely.  Second, AI supports the analysis of already -churned
customers so firms can understand what drove them away. Drawing on customer data,
transaction records, and feedback, AI tools can surface patterns and root causes behind churn,
helping firms tackle the underlying problems within their reactivation strategies (Kumar et al.,
2020). Third, AI supports the design of targeted reactivation strategies through customer
segmentation and personalization. Rather than relying on one -size-fits-all recovery tactics, AI
systems can group former customers according to variables such as lifetime value, reasons for
leaving, and likelihood of returning, then shape outreach accordingly.
Fourth, AI takes over the execution of reactivation campaigns, delivering personalized messages
at the right moments through customers' preferred channels. Automated email sequences,
tailored offers, and targeted content can all be rolled out at scale, keeping engagement with
disengaged customers consistent and well -timed (Johnson & Lee, 2021). Finally, AI systems
track and fine-tune reactivation performance as it happens, allowing strategies to be continually
refined based on response rates and conversion results.

Sales Conversion Performance
Sales conversion performance stands as a core metric in real estate marketing, capturing the
share of leads or prospects who go on to become paying customers. Within the context of
reactivation strategy, sales conversion refers specifically to the proportion of previously
disengaged customers who resume dealings with the firm, going as far as completing a
transaction such as a property purchase, a rental agreement, or an investment commitment
(Kumar & Bansal, 2020).  A range of factors shape sales conversion, among them the quality of
leads, the timing of outreach, how personalized the communication is, and how compelling the
offer appears to the prospect. For reactivation strategies in particular, conversion hinges heavily
on a firm's ability to grasp why customers left in the first place, to address their concerns
directly, and to give them a genuinely persuasive reason to come back (Thomas et al., 2004). AI -
enabled reactivation capabilities boost conversion by supporting sharper targeting, more relevant
offers, and better-timed engagement.

Theoretical Review
Relationship Marketing Theory
Relationship marketing theory, first set out by Morgan and Hunt (1994), offers a useful
theoretical grounding for making sense of customer reactivation strategy. The commitment -trust
framework holds that building a successful relationship rests on two pillars: trust (confidence in a

partner's reliability and integrity) and commitment (a lasting desire to preserve a relationship one
values). When both are present, they encourage cooperative behaviour , cut down uncertainty,
and produce outcomes that benefit both sides of the relationship.
Customer defection generally signals that either trust or commitment has broken down. A
customer might leave because they have lost faith in the firm's ability to deliver value, or because
a competitor has won their commitment with a stronger offer. Reactivation strategies, then, need
to tackle whatever underlying breakdown caused the departure in the first place. AI -enabled
reactivation capabilities help rebuild trust and commitment in a number of ways. By digging into
the reasons behind customer defection, AI allows firms to show that they genuinely understand a
customer's concerns a vital opening step in restoring trust. By delivering offers that are
personalized and relevant, AI signals a firm's commitment to meeting each customer's particular
needs. And by sustaining timely, consistent engagement, AI conveys reliability. Taken together,
these mechanisms mean AI-enabled reactivation strategies can rebuild the relational groundwork
needed for business to resume.

Technology Acceptance Model
The Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), put forward by Fred Davis in 1989, remains one of
the most influential theoretical frameworks used to explain and predict how people come to
accept new technology. TAM proposes that two core beliefs drive an individual's acceptance and
use of new technology: perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use. Perceived usefulness
captures the extent to which someone believes a given system will improve their job
performance or productivity. Perceived ease of use refers to how much a person believes that
using a system will be free of unnecessary difficulty or effort. Together, these beliefs shape
attitudes toward using the technology, which then feed into behavioural intentions and,
eventually, actual usage.  TAM's simplicity combined with its strong empirical track record has
cemented its place as a foundational theory in information systems research, applied across fields
as varied as e -commerce, healthcare, education, banking, and customer relationship
management. Its central claim — that people gravitate toward technologies they find useful and
easy to operate — has held up consistently across decades of empirical testing. The model has
nonetheless been expanded over time to account for additional influences such as social pressure,
facilitating conditions, and individual variation, giving rise to extensions including TAM2 and
the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology (UTAUT).
For the purposes of this study, TAM offers a useful way of understanding how real estate
marketing professionals come to adopt and put AI -enabled customer reactivation tools into
practice. Whether AI reactivation strategies succeed depends not only on the technology's
inherent capability but also on marketing staff's willingness to fold these tools into their
everyday work. When marketing professionals see AI reactivation tools as genuinely useful for
spotting at-risk customers, working out why they left, and building effective ways to reconnect
with them, they are considerably more likely to take these tools on board with enthusiasm. In
much the same way, when such tools come with intuitive interfaces, demand little training, and
slot smoothly into existing CRM systems, perceived ease of use gives adoption an added boost.
That adoption then goes on to determine how well reactivation campaigns are designed and
carried out, which ultimately shapes conversion outcomes. TAM therefore sheds light on both
the organizational process of adopting the technology and the performance results that follow
from integrating it effectively.

Empirical Review
A number of studies have looked into how AI -powered CRM capabilities relate to customer
outcomes across different settings. Zhao et al. (2022) examined the use of data analytics for
customer reactivation in competitive markets, finding that predictive analytics considerably
improved firms' ability to identify customers likely to return, which in turn allowed reactivation
resources to be allocated more efficiently. Their research, conducted within the
telecommunications and retail sectors, showed that AI-supported reactivation strategies produced
conversion rates 25% higher than conventional methods.
Kumar et al. (2020) looked at customer reactivation strategies spanning several industries and
put together a framework identifying key drivers of success, including the timing of outreach, the
degree of personalization in offers, and the choice of communication channel. Their findings
showed that former customers who received personalized reactivation messages were 40% more
likely to return than those who received generic offers. The study underscored how important it
is to understand the specific reasons behind a customer's defection in order to design effective
recovery approaches.  Johnson and Lee (2021) explored the role incentives play in customer
reactivation programmes, finding that financial incentives such as discounts and exclusive deals
were effective in motivating lapsed customers to return. Their work also showed that the
effectiveness of incentives differed by customer segment, with higher -value customers
responding more favourably to enhanced personalized service than to straightforward price
discounts. Within real estate specifically, Boulding et al. (2020) studied reactivation strategies in
property markets and found that former clients who had had positive experiences with a firm in
the past were considerably more likely to return when they were approached through
personalized re -engagement efforts. Their research pointed to the importance of preserving
positive brand perceptions even after a customer has defected.
Turning to the African context, Bayo and Olamide (2020) studied AI applications within
Nigeria's telecommunications industry and found that AI -driven customer segmentation
combined with automated communication reduced churn by enabling firms to intervene at the
right time. Ali and El -Sayed (2021) examined AI adoption in Egypt's real estate sector, showing
that AI tools improved how leads were qualified and allowed sales resources to be deployed
more efficiently. Gakuru and Njoroge (2020) investigated AI applications in customer service
within Kenya's real estate market, finding that automated systems led to faster response times
and greater customer satisfaction.
Even so, the specific relationship between AI -enabled customer reactivation capability and sales
conversion performance remains largely unexamined, particularly within the Nigerian real estate
context. The bulk of existing research treats customer satisfaction, engagement, or retention as
the outcome of interest, stopping short of directly examining what reactivation strategies mean
for conversion. This study addresses that gap by directly investigating the relationship between
AI-enabled reactivation capability and sales conversion performance.

Methodology
This study adopted a quantitative, cross -sectional survey design to explore the relationship
between AI-enabled customer reactivation capability and sales conversion performance among
real estate firms in Lagos. A cross -sectional approach suited the study's aim of capturing
perceptions of reactivation capability and conversion outcomes at one point in time, which
allowed the relationships between the variables of interest to be examined (Creswell & Plano
Clark, 2018).  The population targeted for this study consisted of marketing professionals and
managers drawn from 30 well -established real estate firms operating within Lagos Metropolis.
These firms were chosen on the basis of having at least ten years in operation and a demonstrated
track record of engaging with digital marketing tools. A purposive sampling approach was used
to select respondents who had firsthand knowledge of their organization's customer reactivation
strategies and sales conversion performance.
Sample size was calculated using Taro Yamane's formula, applied to a population of 480
marketing professionals spread across the 30 firms. This produced a sample size of 202
respondents, reached through the administration of structured questionnaires. The sample drew
on professionals from a range of firms, including Adron Homes, Knight Frank Nigeria, UPDC
Plc, and JLL Nigeria, so as to capture representation across different segments of the market.

sector. The moderate positive relationship uncovered (β = 0.557, p < 0.001), together with the
sizeable share of variance explained (31.0%), shows that the way firms identify, understand, and

The size of this effect is broadly consistent with what earlier studies have found in other settings.
Zhao et al. (2022) studied the use of data analytics for customer reactivation in the
telecommunications and retail sectors and reported that AI -supported reactivation strategies
achieved conversion rates 25% higher than traditional methods. The present study extends these
findings into the real estate sector, showing that comparable effects hold true even in the context
of high-value property transactions.
Kumar et al. (2020) examined customer reactivation strategies across a range of industries and
found that personalized reactivation communications achieved conversion rates 40% higher than
generic offers. This study's findings sit comfortably alongside that conclusion, suggesting that
the personalization AI tools make possible is a central mechanism through which reactivation
capability drives conversion. The descriptive results showed that AI tools' capacity to generate
insight for reaching dissatisfied or disengaged customers received the highest rating (Mean =
3.96), reinforcing how central data-driven understanding is to effective reactivation strategy.
The finding that AI systems' ability to identify customers at risk of leaving received the second -
highest rating (Mean = 3.95) echoes work by Johnson and Lee (2021), who found that early
identification of at -risk customers meaningfully improved reactivation success rates. Predictive
analytics give firms the chance to step in before customers disengage completely, potentially
heading off churn rather than simply responding to it after the fact. Given how extended real
estate sales cycles tend to be, with decisions often taking shape gradually, catching early signs of
disengagement may carry particular value in this sector.
The finding that the ability to build targeted reactivation campaigns received the lowest rating
among the four reactivation dimensions examined (Mean = 3.93) suggests that although firms are
making good use of AI for identifying at -risk customers and generating insight, they may be
somewhat less advanced when it comes to executing automated, targeted recovery campaigns.
This lines up with observations made by Boulding et al. (2020), who noted that many
organizations struggle at the execution stage of reactivation strategy even when they have strong
customer analysis capabilities in place. This gap points to an opportunity for real estate firms to
strengthen their reactivation performance by investing further in campaign automation and
personalization tools. These findings carry meaningful theoretical implications.
First, the study extends relationship marketing theory by illustrating how AI-enabled reactivation
strategies can help rebuild trust and commitment with customers who have disengaged. Morgan
and Hunt's (1994) commitment -trust framework points to communication quality and shared
values as antecedents of trust. AI -powered reactivation strategies strengthen communication
quality through personalization and well -timed outreach, while also signalling  shared values by
directly addressing customers' specific concerns. Through these mechanisms, reactivation
strategies can help restore the relational foundations needed for business to resume.
Second, the findings extend the Technology Acceptance Model by showing that the perceived
usefulness of AI reactivation tools translates into concrete conversion outcomes. When
marketing professionals view AI tools as genuinely useful for identifying and reconnecting with
lost customers, they are more inclined to adopt and apply them effectively, which in turn
produces improved conversion performance.

Conclusion
This study set out to examine how AI -enabled customer reactivation capability affects sales
conversion performance within the Lagos real estate sector. The findings offer robust empirical
support for the conclusion that AI -enabled reactivation strategies meaningfully strengthen
conversion performance, accounting for 31.0% of the variance in conversion outcomes. The
study shows that AI's capacity to identify at -risk customers, analyze reasons for defection,
generate actionable insight, and execute targeted campaigns work together to improve
conversion rates. For real estate firms in Lagos and comparable emerging markets, these findings
highlight the strategic importance of investing in AI -enabled reactivation capability. In a climate
where customer acquisition costs keep climbing and competition remains intense, the ability to
win back lost customers represents a genuine source of competitive advantage. Firms that make
deliberate use of AI tools to identify, understand, and reconnect with disengaged customers will
be better positioned to maximize customer lifetime value and sustain long -term growth. As AI
technology continues to advance, real estate firms that embrace these tools for customer recovery
stand to gain not just stronger conversion performance but more durable, resilient customer
relationships as well. The evidence from this study suggests that such investments deliver
tangible returns, both in recovered revenue and in strengthened customer loyalty.
