Abstract
The main objective of this study is to explore the solutions of artificial intelligence (AI) and their role in reshap-
ing modern marketing orientations. To accomplish this objective, technological leaps were deconstructed,
and the impact of accelerated intelligent transformations was analyzed across five key themes: the advertising indus-
try, retail commerce, customer service, and customer relationship management (CRM). The final theme was privacy
and data security as pressing ethical concerns that must be considered when using AI. The study explains how intel-
ligent applications enhance cognitive abilities to understand customer preferences and requirements, thereby
improving audience targeting, building smooth, enjoyable purchasing journeys, redefining traditional service models,
and establishing a new phase of strong customer relationships through personalized, individualized experiences.
Furthermore, it demonstrates the support that intelligent technologies provide to customers in protecting their
data and privacy. The analysis draws on the latest developments in academic research and professional fieldwork,
enhanced with examples from advanced digital environments and pragmatic interpretations of the implications
of adapting these solutions for marketing practices. By linking theoretical concepts to applied contexts, this study
proposes practical solutions and recommendations for the dissemination of AI applications in contemporary market-
ing strategies.
Keywords AI solutions, Advertising industry, Retail commerce, Customer service, Customer relationship
management, Privacy concerns
Introduction
In the current era of rising artificial influence, machines
have emerged from their programmatic silence to
advance marketing into a new stage of digital cognition,
where AI solutions converge with human senses’ ability
to understand consumer behavior and build personalized
experiences, revolutionizing how we think and learn.
Indeed, digital computers are increasingly perform -
ing cognitive tasks once the preserve of humans, and
our world is becoming more artificial [1, 2]. Examples
include heating and cooling systems designed to main -
tain specific temperatures in our homes. Not only that,
but these machines are also likely to have long-term
effects on the replacement of human jobs in fields such
as medicine, banking, transportation, and administrative
services. AI systems have recently gained global recogni -
tion and acceptance as an inspiring force for change in
the business sector [3, 4]. More explicitly, this force will
reshape the features of many industries, not as a means

The dialectics of AI between optimism and pessimism:
a perspective on marketing
The birth of AI
The origins of AI can be attributed to the computer scien-
tist and mathematician Alan Turing, who devoted broad
attention to studying how to simulate human thought
processes using computers. Fate would have it that this
term saw the light after Turing’s death [26, 27]. The term
“artificial intelligence” was officially coined at a confer -
ence hosted by Dartmouth College in 1956, when a semi -
nal paper was presented, co-authored by John McCarthy,
Marvin Minsky, Nathaniel Rochester, and Claude Shan -
non [28]. McCarthy et al. [29] concluded that it was pos -
sible to develop machines capable of simulating human
abilities in learning and intelligence, describing AI as “the

science and engineering of making intelligent machines, ”
making qualitative progress in research efforts to inte -
grate machine and human intelligence [30]. In general,
the Dartmouth Conference aimed to enable machines to
utilize language, perform mathematical operations, use
neural networks, and engage in creative thinking. Based
on this, the initial focus of research projects was on prov -
ing the theory and solving problems with computers that
relied on complex algorithms to address some of the
issues raised at that time [31, 32].
While early studies viewed digital computers merely as
tools for number processing, a domain where processing
systems interact with inputs and human behavior, some
pioneering thinkers believed that these devices might
go beyond being mere calculating machines. Essentially,
these thinkers’ ideas were based on the assumption that
the human mind and digital computers are primarily two
similar symbolic systems [33]. Consequently, research
efforts have shifted toward developing a mechanistic
model of intelligence that incorporates concepts related
to human reasoning and processing, extending beyond
bounded rationality to solve problems quickly and effi -
ciently [34]. In essence, the goal was to reduce reliance on
humans for research and thinking to overcome existing
issues, allowing computers to learn independently [35].
These historical and intellectual roots provide a basis for
understanding the ongoing debate about the future of AI,
including whether it will offer valuable opportunities or
pose challenges and risks. We will address these aspects
in the next section, focusing on their impact on market -
ing transformation.
Artificial intelligence and future marketing trends
Although AI constantly strives to advance, it often loses
ground and eventually returns to the same starting point
[21, 36]. Intriguingly, consider the boundaries of AI and
ask, “What level of superiority in intelligence has such
technology achieved nearly 70 years after the Dartmouth
conference?” Such a question may be so complex that it
is difficult to find a clear answer. This is not surprising,
as the combination of the words “intelligence” and “arti -
ficial” presents a challenge to the human mind. Because
the answer to such a question is future-oriented, it will be
limited to speculation, which is divided into two groups
[37, 38]. The first has a pessimistic outlook and expresses
concerns regarding the consequences of these technolo -
gies. Second, there is optimism about the ability of non-
biological agents to emulate the depth and richness of the
human mind. Specifically, if properly programmed, such
machines will be more accurate, wiser, and more produc-
tive than humans in performing various tasks. Therefore,
they refer to this event as the “technological singularity, ”
which is expected to occur between 2030 and 2050 [33,
39]. In this setting, Tom Stonier [40], a German biolo -
gist and philosopher, argued that the current situation, in
which computers as “idiot savants” perform some aspects
of human intelligence, would not last long. As our under-
standing of the human brain deepens, intelligent systems
will evolve along a different path with a broader scope
and greater precision in their operations, becoming a
more powerful intellectual force than before.
From a reserved standpoint, pessimists who hold a
bleak view of AI are divided into two camps. The first
adopts a skeptical stance on the possibility of develop -
ing human-equivalent intelligence. According to them,
the matter is more intricate than some people believe,
and the progress of such a technology is inherently
slow, requiring the overcoming of numerous technical
and mathematical hurdles to become a reality. The sec -
ond camp believes that the development of intelligent
machines will paint a dark picture of humanity’s future.
In this direction, Nick Bostrom, a professor of philoso -
phy at the University of Oxford, stands at the forefront of
the skeptics. Bostrom [41] argued that the track record of
AI includes a series of unrealistic expectations and false
promises. Following the decade after the invention of
computers, we have yet to see intelligent machines. The
widely held belief that computational intelligence will
reshape the trajectory of human history by 2050 is also
incorrect, as it would take millennia for the technology to
reach full fruition. Subsequently, Bostrom [42] in “Super-
intelligence: Paths, Risks, and Strategies” expressed fears
about creating such intelligence because it will make
the fate of future generations of humanity dependent
on the behavior of intelligent systems. Stephen Hawk -
ing, the renowned physicist and astronomer, in “Will AI
Outsmart Us?” 2018, issued a similar warning about AI,
emphasizing the dangerous scenarios it could create that
directly threaten human life and the economy, particu -
larly the potential loss of many human jobs to robots and
machines.
On the other side of the AI debate stand optimists.
Minsky [43] argued that current indicators of AI could
offer general insights into a limited intellectual domain,
most evident in playing chess, defeating human program
designers, and proving mathematical theorems. Anyway,
these findings provide a solid foundation for developing
machines with superior intelligence. In the view of Fei -
genbaum and Feldman [26], pioneers of writing in AI,
our ignorance of the biological teacher (human brain)
may be the main reason for the simple structure of intel -
ligence that computers currently possess, which they
called “brute force. ” This is not unlike the way scientists
centuries ago pointed to birds as natural evidence of the
possibility of constructing heavier-than-air mechanisms
capable of flight against the Earth’s gravity. Similarly,

Amid the divided perspective on AI and its future,
optimists enjoy broad support and acceptance in both
academia and business. In this regard, Floridi [47]
explains that many pessimistic views and predictions

ligent machines that threaten to eliminate and displace
many people from their jobs and perhaps even lead to
the destruction of humanity. These views are myths
promoted by those who portray AI as the ultimate
catastrophe that will destroy humanity, as if the misery
caused by wars, social injustice, pollution, global warm -
ing, and high unemployment rates are just elements of
science fiction. Concerning marketing, Paul Roetzer
[17], “founder and CEO of the Marketing AI Institute, ”
noted that with the rapid advancement of intelligent
technology, one has two choices: either sit back and
watch the marketing world become smarter or take the
initiative to capitalize on the opportunities that tech -
nology presents to gain a competitive advantage. Point -
edly, business organizations should not ignore AI and
strive to harness all its capabilities to benefit from this
technology, proactively enhancing performance and
service quality through a balanced use of it alongside
their human resources’ skills in marketing.
The idea of intelligent machines has long been associ -

but as is well known, “change is the law of life. ” AI is now
infiltrating consumers’ lives to optimize their shopping

smart lighting systems such as Philips Hue, and even date

comprehensive digital revolution that will redefine mar -
keting strategies [48, 49]. The growing prevalence of the
Internet and smartphones has prompted companies to
reinforce customer interactions through intelligent sys -
tems, alongside several factors, including the massive
amount of data available about the market, the increasing
role of computing in advanced data analysis for educated
decision-making, and the declining cost of managing
computing operations [19, 50]. Interestingly, businesses
can harness AI to obtain profound insights into customer
behaviors and preferences, enabling them to provide cus-
tomized offers that appeal to customers [51]. According
to a McKinsey & Company report [52], 70% of custom -
ers expect tailored marketing experiences. Consequently,
companies will reap the benefits of these experiences,
experiencing tremendous and rapid growth potential, up

partnership between AI and marketing is unavoidable [5,

Methodology
Study design
A systematic literature review (SLR) is a research
method that aims to generate wide-ranging knowledge
by reviewing and summarizing existing publications
in a structured, transparent, and reproducible manner
[57]. Correspondingly, the current study adopted a care -
fully organized approach to examine the influence of AI
on marketing in specific areas, including the advertis -
ing industry, retail commerce, customer service, CRM,
and privacy concerns. According to Varsha et  al. [58],
the methodology included eight steps. First, the research
gap (research questions) was identified. Afterward, the
review methodology was created, and the related proce -
dures were confirmed. In the third step, the sources for
the literature survey and the search terms were deter -
mined. Subsequently, filtering indicators were estab -
lished for the collected articles to ensure that they met
the specified review criteria. In the fifth step, the qual -
ity of the selected articles was assessed to ensure the
review produced reliable, trustworthy results. Later,
the data extraction and summarization phase began for
each study. At this stage, the summary and classification
of the selected literature focused on concepts and their
relationships rather than listing previous studies [59].
The data were then analyzed and synthesized. The review
adopted a thematic approach to compiling and interpret -
ing the findings. The selected research was not presented
as a separate list but was integrated into a framework of
interconnected arguments. This approach helped high -
light coherent evidence supporting the comprehensive -
ness of the arguments and accurately identify research
gaps. Finally, the results are introduced in thorough and
consistent reports that provide vital information and val -
uable insights into the research.
Data collection
To organize and define the scope of the study, this review
charted a clear path for an in-depth investigation of the
research topics. Accordingly, a systematic search strat -
egy was developed, focusing on key terms and variables.
At this stage, the query used keywords such as “artificial
intelligence, its synonyms and technologies, ” along with
related marketing practices, aligning with the vibrant
concepts of “advertising industry, ” “retail commerce, ”
“customer service, ” “customer relationship management, ”
and “privacy concerns” (Table  1). Because the reviewed
literature did not show a decisive distinction between
these terms and applications, the current review adopted
the term artificial intelligence systems as a general
umbrella and the term artificial intelligence solutions as
a unified operational concept that includes (virtual influ -
encers, chatbots, intelligent service agents, virtual assis -
tants) to ensure consistency and clarity in distinguishing
the presentation of AI technologies.
Inclusion and exclusion criteria
The literature review in this study was prepared using
a selection protocol designed to identify the refereed
sources in academic circles, ensuring a balance among
the novelty of the presentation, methodological rigor, and
the quality of the results. The approved inclusion criteria
can be outlined as follows:
• Subject scope: The scope of the current study focuses
on analyzing the role of intelligent systems’ cognitive
capabilities in understanding and interacting with
consumer behavior, as well as their impact on busi -
ness operational efficiency.
• Source type: To ensure academic consistency and
meet the requirements of a comprehensive critical
synthesis, rather than merely providing descriptive
narratives, this study focused on original scientific
contributions indexed in Scopus and Web of Science.

and reports submitted by practitioners and industry
professionals, such as [60].
• Temporal boundaries: Given the remarkable develop-
ments in AI systems across a wide range of business
sectors over the past 20  years, the articles reviewed
were limited to 2005–2025. Priority was given to
building a knowledge base for the ongoing discus -
sion of AI solutions in marketing, with modern refer-
ences, such as [61].
• Language scope: The study focused on foreign-lan -
guage literature to ensure data came from peer-
reviewed scientific journals and to build a solid basis

Results and discussion
Theme 1: AI solutions in the advertising industry
AI holds the promise of generating immersive virtual
advertising that increases target audience engagement
and deepens their connection to the content. With its
ability to fuse fantasy and reality, intelligent technol -
ogy offers distinctive opportunities to produce and edit
engaging video content in a stereotypical style without
the need to reshoot scenes. It can also break the bounds
of human mortality by bringing dead celebrities back
into the media spotlight, making them rise from the
grave to participate in advertising. The digital advertis -
ing industry is confidently entering a phase in which
visual appeal intersects with emotional and realistic
impacts. All signs point to the current stage opening
the horizons to expectations of the global smart adver -
tising market growing to up to $2.2 billion by early
2030, with an annual growth rate exceeding 15.4%
between 2025 and 2030 [62, 63]. In this domain, virtual
influencers exemplify AI technologies with the highest
level of advertising visibility and spending [64]. They
are digitally created personae or entities that closely
resemble human appearance, traits, and behaviors. AI
systems often manage these personas to perform infor -
mational roles in social media networks and online
stores. Evidence from past research has verified that
virtual personas possess capabilities that make them an
effective alternative to human influencers in generating
diverse marketing advantages [65, 66], across enter -
tainment, beauty, fashion, tourism, and music [67, 68].
In addition to their low cost, virtual celebrities derive
their charisma from elegance, personal authenticity,
coherent storytelling, optimism, and creativity [69, 70],
positioning them as a flexible and functional marketing
solution for brand endorsement.
Virtual influencers are a major driver of the success
of digital advertising and marketing campaigns, even
though they are not human. Such celebrities use tech -
nology to introduce realistic content that holds people’s
attention, boosts interaction, and encourages purchase
intention, especially for persuasive messages aimed at
the youth market, which is more technologically inclined
[71–73]. Virtual influencers are also adept at forming
social and emotional connections, thus building friend -
ships with millions of followers [74, 75]. This can be seen
in LiI Miqyela, the most famous and highest-paid robot
influencer in the virtual world, who was ranked among
the top 25 content creators on the Internet in 2018 [76].
Miqyela has been involved in promotional campaigns for
Calvin Klein, Samsung phones, Amazon Fashion, and
Hugo Boss [77]. Other examples include Lu do Malagalu,
a virtual influencer for McDonald’s, Microsoft, and Red
Bull, and the fashionable virtual influencer Shudu Gram,
who has collaborated with leading fashion brands such as
Vogue and Lanvin. In this respect, recent reports indicate
that engagement rates achieved by virtual influencers
on digital networks are three times higher than those of
their human counterparts and that 50% of users on these
platforms actively follow the virtual world. For this rea -
son, Meta has invested heavily in AI and attracted crea -
tors and developers to produce engaging content in the
Metaverse [78, 79]. As a result, the digital trend and the
need to stay current in advertising have led many mar -
keters to work with virtual influencers as representatives
and promoters of their brands, such as Nike, Balmain,
Chevrolet, and AliExpress [80, 81]. Figure  2 depicts the

According to prior studies, the impact of AI on CRM sys-

Analytical Capabilities: Analytical capabilities are the
cornerstone of AI’s advantages in CRM, enabling a holis -

for delivering superior marketing experiences. Undoubt -

diverse channels to gain a profound understanding of
altered buying patterns [125]. To provide up-to-date
customer details, AI draws on multiple sources to col -

purchase records and personal meetings, to leverage data
on their interactions on digital platforms. Although mod-

process large amounts of customer data by converting

this data may be lost without proper tools to analyze and
manage it accurately. Companies are often good at col -
lecting customer data. However, they may struggle due to
limitations in both human and technological expertise in
achieving the expected return on their investment in data
analysis and extracting insights that enhance the value of
their marketing knowledge. This reality is supported by
the results of a study by Mela and Moorman [127], which
found that although companies’ allocations to market
data analytics increased by 198% from 2014 to 2018, the
actual impact of these investments on marketing perfor -
mance was only approximately 3.1%. On an unparalleled
scale, we are currently confronted with digital platforms
that generate vast quantities of data, which can often be
challenging for humans to comprehend. In this context,
Eric Schmidt [128], “Google CEO, ” pointed out during
the Guardian’s Activate Summit that “From the dawn
of civilization to 2003, five exabytes of data were cre -
ated. The same amount was created in the last two days. ”

tions in CRM is reinforced through the analysis of large
datasets, the preparation of reliable reports that compre -
hensively cover customer needs, and the potential to help
marketing managers evaluate their campaigns, as well as
determine the necessary resources to develop strategies
that keep pace with evolving customer needs. It is ulti -
mately clear that AI’s analytical capabilities can provide
deeper insights into how marketing activities respond
to market changes and which decisions are necessary to
improve a business’s performance [129].
Predictive Capabilities: Against the backdrop of diverse
and volatile purchasing patterns, companies often view
consumption practices as puzzles that are difficult to
comprehend [130]. Because the pivotal goal of market -
ing is to satisfy target audiences’ needs and desires better
than competitors, finding accurate predictive solutions
that can sense consumer behavior and purchasing deci -
sions has become imperative to roll out promising cam -
paigns. Regrettably, the development of marketing plans
frequently depends on the personal expectations of the
sales teams. Amid this complexity and the need for pre -
diction, AI represents the bright side of digital technol -
ogy, offering innovative solutions to discover purchasing
patterns through its predictive capabilities in large CRM
datasets. When a customer enters a company’s platform,
AI applications analyze their data, including personal
characteristics, shopping history, searched keywords,
product clicks or interactions, and social media activities
[125]. Supported by advanced statistical analysis of the
acquired data and machine learning, AI applications cre -
ate accurate predictive models to identify customer seg -
ments most likely to make a purchase and those who are
likely to churn [131]. In addition, these solutions serve as
good predictive tools to outline a clear path of expected
customer decisions, which may lead to upselling (i.e.,
upgraded products) or desires that could expand cross-
selling opportunities (i.e., ancillary products) [132, 133].
On this basis, companies can use the predictive capabili -
ties inherent in AI to make optimal decisions and develop
proactive marketing programs driven by accurate cus -
tomer segmentation and genuine opportunities to con -
vert leads into buyers. Intelligent solutions in CRM also
enable marketing departments to identify gaps in existing
offerings and suggest value-added incentives to support
products, thereby increasing customer satisfaction, fos -
tering long-term loyalty, and ultimately reducing churn
rates [129].

Theme 5: privacy and data security concerns regarding AI
Privacy concerns and data security are among the cru -
cial issues in the digital marketing literature, as they
reflect the darker side of technology applications. AI
systems have refocused attention on customers’ con -
cerns regarding risks to their data, particularly since
these systems have enabled marketers to collect exten -
sive data about customers, including comments, interac -
tions, and personal information, to analyze their behavior
and construct detailed profiles. Even if marketers use
the information they acquire transparently to develop
impactful marketing campaigns, the risk of unethical
intentions to misuse this data still exists [145, 146]. We
cannot deny that we live in an era of so-called networked
privacy or social surveillance, where government bod -
ies and companies monitor our online activities, posing
a serious threat to privacy and the right to freedom of
expression. One of the main reasons privacy protection
has become so complex is that customers are now more
socially connected and integrated through family, friend -
ships, and work, which means that others can access
their data [147, 148]. Thus, activity logs are susceptible to
hacking and unauthorized access, regardless of whether
the customer shares personal information. AI systems
have also surpassed the limits of archiving customer data
(financial, health, and educational) generated by daily
online interactions over extended periods. Accordingly,
computer tools can identify customers and reveal their
political and economic leanings in several ways, such as
through facial and voice recognition software or by mon -
itoring shopping interests or posts on digital platforms
[149].
Recent research has increasingly emphasized the con -
cepts of privacy and data security, revealing that custom -
ers exhibit somewhat limited confidence in the use of
AI systems due to the potential for their accounts to be
hacked and data stolen, as well as other issues outlined
in Table  4. In this regard, Thomas et  al. [150] tracked
the attacks and data breaches to which Internet users
were exposed from March 2016 to March 2017, disclos -
ing that 788,000 individuals were victims of keylogger
programs. While 12.4 million people were exposed to
phishing attacks targeting their sensitive financial data,
the number of people whose usernames and passwords
were stolen from their accounts was 1.9 billion. These
statistics present a bleak scenario of privacy issues and
customer confidence in AI applications, as their digital
activities and transactions often rely on a single account
that represents their identity, which could be hacked at
any moment. In the same context, the Privacy Rights
Information Clearinghouse [151] published statistics on
data breaches suffered by service organizations operat -
ing in the financial, healthcare, and hospitality sectors,
including cyberattacks and mobile device hacking, over
20  years (specifically, from 2006 to 2025). The report
states that 72,926 notifications of data security breaches
were received, and the number of individuals affected
reached 8.20 billion.

The service industry demonstrates a high level of
openness to adopting smart marketing transformations
by repositioning its traditional models to develop inte -

intelligent service agents and chatbots are technologies

every moment of customer interaction. It is advisable to
initiate the setup and fine-tune intelligent systems using
field data to reflect their operation in realistic scenarios.
Since they handle the most sensitive data, the operation
of intelligent systems must be carefully monitored to
identify any biases or flaws in their performance. For this
reason, the necessity of continually updating algorithms
is highlighted to prevent the recurrence of defects in the
operations of such systems and to enhance the precision
of the results.

it, as well as delete data that the system no longer needs

clear policies to disclose the purpose of data collection.

Conclusion
Amid the cognitive revolution in marketing driven by
AI solutions, machines are advancing confidently, with
an ambitious vision to demonstrate distinguished lev -
els of intelligence by sensing what customers think and
desire, ultimately creating an interactive environment
that generates personalized and differentiated experi -
ences. To deepen marketing knowledge of the impact of
intelligent cognitive transformations—from companies’
precise understanding of customer needs to digital envi -
ronment adaptability—the research curiosity focused on
the advertising industry, retail commerce, customer ser -
vice, and CRM, alongside customer privacy issues and
data security. Based on the review results, it is clear that
the success of virtual influencers in sponsoring marketing
campaigns for many companies, and even their ability to
surpass human influencers across different sectors, is due
to the characteristics of virtual personas. The advantages
of social proximity and conversational authenticity that
virtual voice assistants bring to online shopping experi -
ences are also reshaping customer–brand interactions,
enhancing purchase intentions, and boosting operational
efficiency, thereby driving growth in the retail market.
Moreover, the use of intelligent service agents at the
front lines of service providers positively impacts busi -
ness agility and rapid customer response, as intelligent
agents enhanced by natural language understanding and
machine learning are key conduits for improving cus -
tomer service, not to mention the need to mitigate cus -
tomer concerns about privacy and data security breaches
by implementing measures that strengthen the cyberse -
curity of intelligent systems, including secure data stor -
age and transparent data management practices.

The scientific contribution of this study lies in synthe -

by drawing on field evidence from the business world to
show how AI solutions affect the boundaries of market -

the building of long-term customer relationships. The
study encourages marketers to adopt a sustainable com -
munication strategy that uses virtual influencers as an
effective gateway to reach target audiences, while bal -
ancing cost-effective access to audiences with behavioral
guidance to enhance brand identity. The study offers an
intellectual addition through an integrated analysis of the
advantages of AI in the retail sector and reveals the role
of voice assistants (chatbots) in creating a shopping jour -
ney that goes beyond the traditional, complex pattern of
product search. The actual contribution to understanding
how AI solutions work lies in improving sales response
dynamics and accelerating the transition from inquiry
to transaction completion, consequently establishing
a guiding anchor for business decision-makers as they
build a sales model that delivers sustainable profits and a
strong competitive position.
The study also equips decision-makers with a knowl -
edge pathway to extrapolate globally leading practices
for integrating AI solutions into the service industry. The
research synthesis revealed the gains of employing intel -
ligent service agents, most notably the ability to bypass
human intervention and the routine tasks, which contrib-
ute to greater flexibility in service performance and help
meet customer expectations in service encounters. The
study goes beyond superficial discussions of AI-driven
CRM capabilities and refutes the assumption that balanc-
ing technical aspects with the spirit of human interaction
is difficult in this context. It explained the humanization
of algorithms and their capabilities to move beyond the
traditional role of CRM systems as data storage reposi -
tories, making them tools capable of personalized cus -
tomer interactions. The study thus presents practical
mechanisms to reduce churn rates and maximize rela -
tionship value through interactive automation that com -
bines human touch with digital intelligence. The review
addresses customer privacy and data security as the most
important ethical challenges in implementing AI-enabled
marketing, using a critical analysis of intelligent systems’
dual role as both a technological threat and a powerful
protective tool. It emphasized the importance of secu -
rity governance in the digital environment by consider -
ing aspects such as regulatory compliance and adherence
to the principle of transparency in data collection, as well
as the vital role of companies in promoting a culture of
digital awareness among customers and enabling them
to protect their accounts from cyber threats. The review
concludes with the formulation of a conceptual model
that links AI solutions to the five-study theme, providing
an integrated vision for business decision-makers on the
prospects of investing in smart systems without compro -
mising digital customer confidence.
Limitations and future research
Like the rest of the research, the current study had some
limitations in building a review that would knowledge -
ably enrich the subject of the research. While it focused
on specific topics from the literature that have formed
the core of the academic and practical debate surround -
ing intelligent cognitive transformations in marketing,
the issues raised require further research to keep pace
with future developments driven by intelligent com -
puting technology. Although the study revealed vir -
tual influencers’ ability to build large followings, there
remains a gap in understanding customer reactions to
hyper-realistic virtual personas and the risk of brands
falling into the “uncanny valley. ” Against this limita -
tion, the importance of mobilizing the research com -
munity to enrich the marketing literature with studies
that measure customer responses to these influencers
and identify the critical points at which fascination with
technical realism gives way to digital aversion is under -
scored. With the increasing use of chatbots in retail, an
important research gap emerges in the analysis of brand
image psychology, because intelligent language models
deliver product information to customers rather than
the company itself, ultimately threatening these brands’
ability to build a desirable communicative representation.
It is recommended to expand research into the role of
these models in shaping the brand’s mental image, with
a focus on language model engine optimization strategies
to ensure an accurate and fair brand image across digital
platforms. Developing autonomous intelligent systems
(agentic AI) capable of making independent decisions
in complex, unstructured service environments remains
a pressing challenge. To address this issue, we recom -
mend conducting a comprehensive, systematic analy -
sis of the development of smart governance models and
reasoning mechanisms to ensure that intelligent service
agents’ decisions align with company policies and cus -
tomer satisfaction metrics. While the study extensively
reviews theoretical concepts, analyzes models, and pro -
poses pathways for AI-based CRM, the dominance of
descriptive approaches and the lack of experimental
studies limit the ability to derive procedural generaliza -
tions that are sufficiently supported by practical evidence
and argumentation. Therefore, we highlight the need to

intensify experimental research on intelligent systems
and their role in advancing CRM capabilities. Given the
limited literature addressing the contradictory evidence
on AI, this study’s analytical framework focused on the
realized benefits and strategic value of adopting AI solu -
tions in marketing. AI’s shortcomings, including algo -
rithmic bias (targeting one customer group over another
or biased pricing) and data hallucinations (making false
marketing promises), represent gaps that future research
must address to restore balance to the scientific debate.
We urge researchers to expand the study of the paradox
of customer privacy and data security as critical issues
in ethical governance for AI-based marketing practices.
Finally, most of the review’s findings are derived from
business environments that are highly digitally conscious
and prepared, revealing a gap in AI solutions for emerg -
ing environments. Hence, future research should investi -
gate how companies in emerging economies, given their
infrastructure and culture, are prepared to adopt AI for
marketing purposes.
