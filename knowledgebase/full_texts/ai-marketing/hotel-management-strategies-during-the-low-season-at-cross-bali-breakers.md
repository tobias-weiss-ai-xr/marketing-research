ABSTRACT

This research aims to analyze the management strategies implemented by Cross Bali Breakers in
addressing the challenge of low room occupancy during the low season. The study employs a descriptive
qualitative approach, with data collected through interviews, observations, and documentation conducted
during the internship program in the Sales and Marketing Department. The data were examined using
SWOT analysis and mapped into IFAS, EFAS, IE Matrix, and QSPM to identify the hotel’s internal
strengths and weaknesses as well as external opportunities and threats. The findings indicate that the
hotel has adopted several strategies, including price-based promotions (flash sales), collaboration with
Online Travel Agents (OTAs), domestic staycation packages, and the promotion of wellness and surfing
experiences. Based on the IFAS matrix score of 2.50 and EFAS matrix score of 2.40, the hotel is positioned
within Cell V, indicating a hold-and-maintain position that supports product development and market
penetration. The QSPM analysis indicates that integrated wellness product development is the leading
strategic alternative, obtaining the highest Total Attractiveness Score (TAS = 4.42). Consequently, this
study recommends that hotel management shift from short-term price-based promotions to experience-
driven, sustainable marketing to ensure long-term competitiveness.
Keywords: Management Strategy, Low Season, Occupancy Rate, SWOT, QSPM

INTRODUCTION
The tourism industry in Bali has long been recognized as one of the main driving forces of
both the regional and national economy, particularly because hotel occupancy is closely related
to the economic performance of hospitality properties in Bali (Puspita, 2024). Natural beauty,
cultural richness, and the hospitality of the community have made Bali a leading destination that
attracts millions of tourists every year. However, behind the glamorous image of Bali tourism,
there are seasonal dynamics that significantly affect the performance of the hotel sector. In the
accommodation industry, seasonal demand is generally categorized into peak season, high season,
and low season, each of which has different implications for hotel management strategies
(Syarifudin, 2024). Within one calendar year, there are peak season periods with very high tourist
arrivals, high season periods with relatively stable visits, and low season periods marked by a
sharp decline in the number of tourists. These differences in characteristics require hotel
management to develop adaptive, creative, and sustainable strategies in order to maintain room
occupancy stability.
The low season is a particular challenge for hotels in Bali, including Cross Bali Breakers,
which is located in the Balangan area. Occupancy rate is one of the key indicators used to
understand, analyze, and improve hotel performance (Suradan, 2025). Occupancy data show that
in February 2025, the hotel’s occupancy rate reached only 47.5%, the lowest figure of the year.
This condition created a considerable gap compared with September, which recorded the highest
occupancy rate at 90.47%. This fluctuation demonstrates how vulnerable the hotel business is to
changes in tourist visitation patterns. Price-based promotional strategies such as flash sales may
temporarily increase occupancy, but they have not consistently maintained long-term occupancy
stability. In addition, excessive dependence on Online Travel Agents (OTAs) may increase sales
volume but also creates commission-related challenges for hotels (Handayani et al., 2023). This
raises a critical question: is the low occupancy rate caused solely by external factors, or are there
weaknesses in management strategies that have not yet adapted to market trends and increasingly
intense competition?
In this context, research on hotel management strategies during the low season is relevant
and important. Cross Bali Breakers was selected as the case study because it has distinctive
features, including the private pool villa concept, an iconic chapel facility, and a surfing lifestyle
theme that differentiates it from competitors. However, these strengths have not been fully
optimized in a sustainable marketing strategy. Experience -based marketing is increasingly
relevant in the lifestyle resort industry because guests tend to seek unique, meaningful, and
memorable stay experiences rather than merely low prices (Norfajrina, 2023). Therefore, this
study focuses on analyzing the management strategies implemented by the hotel using a

descriptive qualitative approach through interviews, observation, and documentation. SWOT
analysis, QSPM, and IFAS and EFAS matrix mapping were used to identify the hotel’s strengths,

management frameworks that emphasize the evaluation of internal and external factors (David,

The findings of this study are expected to provide practical contributions for the
management of Cross Bali Breakers in formulating more focused, sustainable, and guest -
experience-based strategies. In addition, this study is expected to serve as an academic reference
for students and practitioners in the hospitality industry in understanding the importance of
product diversification, digital promotion strengthening, and service quality improvement as key
factors in overcoming low season challenges. Strategic management requires organizations to
formulate and implement decisions that support long-term competitiveness (David, 2021). Thus,
this research not only provides a descriptive overview of the hotel’s condition but also offers
strategic solutions that can be implemented to maintain competitiveness and business
sustainability in Bali’s hospitality industry.

METHOD
This study employed a descriptive qualitative approach and was conducted at Cross Bali
Breakers, particularly in the Sales and Marketing Department. A descriptive qualitative approach
was selected because the study aimed to obtain a contextual understanding of how the hotel
formulated and implemented management strategies during the low season. The study focused
not only on the strategies applied but also on the managerial considerations, internal conditions,
market challenges, and organizational practices underlying these strategies. These context-
dependent processes could not be adequately explained through numerical data alone. Therefore,
the descriptive qualitative approach was considered appropriate for addressing the research
objective of analyzing the hotel’s low-season management strategies in their actual organizational
setting.
Cross Bali Breakers was selected because it was the location of the researcher’s internship
and provided direct access to the hotel’s sales, marketing, distribution, and reservation practices
during the low season. The internship also enabled the researcher to observe the implementation
of promotional activities and strategic practices in their natural setting. The numerical values
presented in the IFAS, EFAS, IE Matrix, and QSPM were used as structured summaries of the
qualitative findings and as decision -support tools rather than as inferential statistical
measurements.
Five key informants were selected using purposive sampling based on their direct
involvement in planning, implementing, or monitoring sales, marketing, distribution, and

108

reservation strategies. The informants consisted of the Director of Sales and Marketing, E -
Commerce Manager, Assistant Sales Manager, Marketing Communication Executive, and
Reservation Staff. These positions were selected because they represented complemen tary
strategic and operational perspectives. The Director of Sales and Marketing provided information
on overall strategic direction; the E-Commerce Manager explained digital distribution and Online
Travel Agent management; the Assistant Sales Manager provided information on sales activities
and market development; the Marketing Communication Executive explained promotional
communication; and the Reservation Staff provided information on booking patterns and
reservation channels. The informants had approximately six years of relevant professional
experience. Interviews were conducted on April 30, 2026.
Primary data were collected through interviews with the five key informants and participant
observation conducted during the internship program. Secondary data were obtained from the
hotel’s 2025 occupancy report and relevant internal documentation, including promotional and
sales-related records. Combining these sources enabled the researcher to compare managerial
explanations with observed practices and documented hotel performance.
The qualitative data were first organized, reduced, and grouped according to recurring
issues related to low-season management. Information obtained from interviews was compared
with participant observations and hotel documents. Recurring findings were then categorized into
internal strengths and weaknesses and external opportunities and threats. The credibility of the
findings was supported through source triangulation, by comparing information from informants
holding different responsibilities, and method triangulation, by comparing interview, observation,
and documentation data (David, 2021; Hunger & Wheelen, 2021).
The strategic analysis was conducted through the following three stages:
1. Input Stage
The identified internal and external factors were organized into the Internal Factor
Analysis Summary (IFAS) and External Factor Analysis Summary (EFAS) matrices.
Preliminary weights and ratings were assigned by the researcher based on the relative
importance, recurrence, and consistency of each factor across the interview, observation,
and documentation findings. The weight represented the relative importance of each
strategic factor, while the rating represented the hotel’s condition or response regarding
that factor. Weighted scores were obtained by multiplying the weight by the rating. The
assignments were not treated as statistically validated measurements. Instead, their
credibility was checked qualitatively by comparing the matrices with the information
provided by the key informants, participant observations, and hotel documents. Thus, the
IFAS and EFAS matrices were used to organize and summarize the qualitative evidence

109

systematically rather than to produce statistical generalizations (Harta et al., 2024).
2. Matching Stage
The internal and external factors were combined in the SWOT Matrix to formulate
strategic alternatives. Strength–Opportunity strategies were developed by matching
internal strengths with external opportunities; Weakness–Opportunity strategies were
formulated to address internal limitations by utilizing external opportunities; Strength–
Threat strategies used existing strengths to respond to threats; and Weakness–Threat
strategies were intended to reduce vulnerabilities and external risks. The total IFAS and
EFAS scores were subsequently mapped into the Internal–External Matrix. The IFAS
score was placed on the horizontal axis, while the EFAS score was placed on the vertical
axis. This mapping was used to identify the hotel’s strategic position and determine the
general strategic direction most consistent with its internal and external conditions
alternatives (David, 2021; Wardhana, 2021).
3. Decision Stage
The strategic alternatives generated from the SWOT and IE Matrix analyses were
compared using the Quantitative Strategic Planning Matrix. The factors and weights
identified in the IFAS and EFAS matrices were used as the basis for comparing the
alternatives. An Attractiveness Score was assigned to indicate the relative relevance of
each strategic factor to each alternative. The Total Attractiveness Score was calculated
by multiplying the factor weight by its Attractiveness Score. The QSPM results were
interpreted as an indicative ranking of strategic priorities based on the available
qualitative evidence. They were not interpreted as statistical proof that one strategy would
necessarily be more effective than another. The strategy with the highest total score was
considered the leading alternative, while strategies with closely comparable scores were
interpreted as complementary options that could be implemented together (Wardhana,
2021).

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
1. Analysis of Internal Factors (IFAS) and External Factors (EFAS)
Based on an in-depth identification of empirical conditions at Cross Bali Breakers, the following
key indicators were formulated
1. Strengths
The hotel has a unique product concept in the form of luxury private pool villas, a
distinctive wedding chapel facility, integration of facilities with a green surrounding
environment, and friendly staff service with a personal character.

2. Weaknesses
The hotel has a high dependence on Online Travel Agents (OTAs), which leads to high
commission costs, prices that are less competitive for some domestic market segments,
and limited variation in independent digital promotions.
3. Opportunities
Global market trends are increasingly shifting toward wellness tourism, the domestic
staycation market segment is growing, and there is potential for activity collaboration
with local and national wedding organizers.
4. Threats
The hotel faces highly aggressive price competition from similar competitors in the South
Kuta area, the development of new private accommodation or villas around the resort,
and fluctuations in domestic airfare prices

Based on the weighting and rating procedures described in the Method section, the IFAS
matrix obtained a total score of 2.50, while the EFAS matrix obtained a total score of 2.40. The
assigned values were qualitatively cross-checked against the interview findings, participant
observations, and relevant hotel documents. The IFAS score of 2.50 indicates that the hotel’s
internal condition is in a moderate position; its strengths are able to balance operational
weaknesses, although they have not yet been utilized aggressively. Meanwhile, the EFAS score
of 2.40 indicates that the hotel’s ability to respond to external factors is also in the moderate
category. IFAS and EFAS scores help organizations identify whether internal capabilities and
external responses are weak, moderate, or strong as the basis for strategic formulation (Harta et
al., 2024). Management is fairly adaptive in capturing opportunities but remains vulnerable to
competitive price pressure from competitors.
The moderate internal and external positions indicate that the hotel’s low-season challenge
does not arise from a complete absence of resources or market opportunities. Cross Bali Breakers
already possesses distinctive internal resources, including private pool villas, a wedding chapel,
a green resort environment, personalized service, and facilities that can support wellness and
lifestyle experiences. However, these resources have not yet been fully converted into a clearly
differentiated low-season value proposition. At the same time, dependence on OTAs and limited

independent digital promotion reduce the hotel’s control over distribution costs, guest

This interpretation suggests that low occupancy is not caused solely by seasonal demand.
It also reflects a partial mismatch between the hotel’s distinctive resources and the way those
resources are packaged and communicated to potential guests. Price -based promotions may
stimulate immediate bookings, but they do not necessarily communicate the experiential value of
the property. From a strategic management perspective, the findings demonstrate the importance
of aligning internal capabilities with changing market opportunities rather than relying primarily
on short-term tactical price reductions.

2. Internal-External (IE) Matrix Mapping and SWOT Strategy
By combining the IFAS score of 2.50 and the EFAS score of 2.40, the strategic position of
Cross Bali Breakers is graphically mapped into Cell V of the Internal-External (IE) Matrix. A
position in Cell V indicates a hold-and-maintain strategic position, commonly associated with
market penetration and product development. The IE Matrix is commonly used to match internal
and external evaluation scores in order to determine appropriate growth, stability, or defensive
strategies (Wardhana, 2021). This strategy emphasizes that management should avoid destructive
price cutting or price wars and shift toward creating added value while expanding its reach into
potential domestic markets. Such a strategy is also consistent with strategic manage ment
principles that prioritize long-term competitiveness over short-term tactical responses (David,
2021).
The strategic position explains why product development and market penetration emerged
as the most relevant directions. Product development enables the hotel to transform its existing
facilities into value-added experiences, while market penetration improves the ability of the hotel
to reach domestic and short-haul markets during periods of weak international demand. These
directions do not require the hotel to undertake a major expansion of physical facilities. Instead,
they emphasize the more effective use and combination of resources that are already available. In
strategic management terms, the findings illustrate the importance of strategic fit. The hotel’s
private villas, spa facilities, fitness facilities, surfing identity, restaurant services, and natural
environment provide the internal foundation for experience-based products. Meanwhile, the
growth of staycation and wellness-oriented demand provides an external opportunity. The
recommended strategy emerged because it connects these internal resources with a relevant
market opportunity while simultaneously reducing dependence on price competition.

The QSPM analysis placed integrated wellness product development as the leading
strategic alternative, with a TAS of 4.42, followed closely by domestic market penetration through
digital promotion and direct-booking campaigns, with a TAS of 4.38. The difference between the
two scores is only 0.04. Therefore, the result should not be interpreted as showing that the first
strategy is substantially superior to the second. Instead, the two strategies should be viewed as
complementary The wellness strategy emerged as the leading priority because it can utilize
several existing hotel resources simultaneously. Private villas can support privacy and relaxation,
spa and fitness facilities can support health-oriented activities, the natural resort environment can
strengthen the atmosphere of well-being, and surfing activities can be combined with recovery or
relaxation services. Through bundling, the hotel can offer a more distinctive guest experience
without relying primarily on room-rate reductions.
However, product development alone will not automatically improve occupancy. The
wellness package must be supported by market penetration activities, particularly targeted
domestic digital campaigns and direct-booking incentives. Digital communication is required to
introduce the package to the appropriate market, while direct booking can help the hotel maintain
closer guest relationships and reduce excessive dependence on OTA commissions (Handayani et
al., 2023). Consequently, the practical strategic priority is not merely to create a wellness product,
but to integrate product development with domestic -market communication and direct-
distribution management. The findings contribute to strategic management by showing that low-
season strategies should combine internal resource utilization with external market access. In the
hospitality context, competitiveness during periods of weak demand is not generated only through
lower prices. It can also be developed by reorganizing existing services into meaningful
experiences and communicating their value to clearly defined market segments. For hotel
practice, the findings support the development of bundled offers that combine accommodation
with spa treatments, fitness or surfing activities, food and beverage services, and direct-booking
benefits (Norfajrina, 2023).

CONCLUSION
The findings indicate that the management strategies implemented by Cross Bali Breakers
during the low season have included short-term price promotions, cooperation with Online Travel
Agents, domestic staycation offers, and the promotion of wellness and su rfing-related
experiences. Although price-based promotions can support short-term room sales, they have not
consistently addressed the structural challenge of maintaining stable occupancy during periods of
low demand.
The IFAS and EFAS analyses positioned the hotel in Cell V of the IE Matrix, indicating
that product development and market penetration are relevant strategic directions. The QSPM
analysis identified integrated wellness product development as the leading alternative, with a TAS
of 4.42, followed closely by domestic-market penetration through digital promotion and direct-
booking campaigns, with a TAS of 4.38. Because the difference between these scores was limited,
the strategies should be implemented as comp lementary rather than mutually exclusive
alternatives. Integrated wellness products require targeted digital communication and effective
direct-booking channels to produce meaningful occupancy outcomes.
This study was limited to a single resort and involved five informants whose
responsibilities were concentrated in sales, marketing, e -commerce, and reservations. The
strategic scores also represent context-specific judgments derived from qualitative evidence and
should not be interpreted as statistically generalizable measurements. Future research should
examine low-season strategies across different hospitality contexts, such as city hotels, boutique
hotels, villas, and resorts in other destinations. Stud ies may also employ mixed-methods,
comparative case-study, or longitudinal designs and include the perspectives of guests,
operational departments, travel agents, and other stakeholders. Further research could evaluate
the implementation of the proposed strategies using indicators such as occupancy rate, Average
Daily Rate, Revenue per Available Room, direct-booking proportion, guest satisfaction, and
length of stay.
