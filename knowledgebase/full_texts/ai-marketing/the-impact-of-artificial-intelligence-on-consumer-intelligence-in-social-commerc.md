ABSTRACT
Artificial Intelligence (AI) has become an important technology transforming modern
marketing and social commerce. AI -powered recommendation systems, chatbots,
predictive analytics, automated customer service, and personalized advertisements
influence how consumers search for information, evaluate brands and products, and
make purchase decisions. Social commerce provides a particularly rich environment
for AI applications because consumers continuously generate behavioural,
transactional, and social data th rough likes, comments, reviews, searches, shares,
purchases, and other interactions.
This study explores the effect of AI capabilities on Consumer Intelligence (CI) in social
commerce. Consumer Intelligence refers to the ability of firms and platforms to
understand consumer preferences, needs, behaviour, sentiment, purchase patterns, and
future intentions through the timely collection and analysis of reliable consumer data.
The study focuses on AI personalization, recommendation quality, responsiveness,
perceived reliability, consumer engagement, trust, and purchase intention.
A quantitative research design is proposed using primary data collected through a
structured questionnaire from social commerce users. A sample of 400 respondents is
considered for illustrative analysis using SPSS, including reliability analysis, Pearson
correlation, and an independent -samples t -test. The findings indicate positive
relationships between AI capabilities and Consumer Intelligence, highlighting the
importance of transparency, privacy, human oversight, and consumer trust in AI-driven
social commerce.
Keywords: Artificial Intelligence, Consumer Intelligence, Social Commerce, AI
Personalization, AI Recommendation, Consumer Engagement, Consumer Trust,
Purchase Intention.

INTRODUCTION
The swift progress of Artificial Intelligence has reshaped the landscape of digital marketing.
AI empowers businesses to handle massive amounts of structured and unstructured data,
identify behavioral trends, anticipate customer needs, and deliver personalized experiences at a
pace far beyond traditi onal marketing systems. Activities that once relied on historical sales
figures and manual segmentation are now enhanced through machine learning,
recommendation engines, conversational bots, predictive analytics, and automated content
platforms. A comprehensive review by Chintalapati and Pandey highlights consumer behavior,
content strategies, experiential marketing, market research, and operational processes as key
areas transformed by AI.
The rise of social commerce has amplified AI’s strategic role. Social commerce merges social
interaction with online shopping, enabling users to discover products, engage with sellers,
explore recommendations, share feedback, read reviews, and complete purchases within
socially connected platforms. Unlike traditional e -commerce, it produces continuous streams
of social and behavioral data. Consumers interact not only with brands but also with peers,
influencers, and reviewers, creating rich insights for organizations seeking to under stand
preferences and expectations.
One of the most prominent applications is AI-driven recommendations . These algorithms
study consumer traits and behaviors to suggest products or content. Yet, responses to such
recommendations vary. Research by Xie et al. shows that acceptance depends on product type,
with search goods facing less resistance compared to experience goods.
Similarly, AI chatbots  have become central to customer engagement. They provide
information, accessibility, personalization, entertainment, and interactive communication.
Studies reveal that these features influence the quality of exchanges, shaping brand
relationships and consumer behavior.
AI should therefore be seen not merely as a tool but as a mechanism for generating intelligence.
When applied effectively, it transforms raw consumer data into actionable insights, forming the
foundation of Consumer Intelligence . In social commerce, this is especially critical since
consumers act as both creators and receivers of information. A single journey—searching for a
product, watching influencer content, reading peer reviews, chatting with a bot, comparing
recommendations, and making a purchase —produces valuable data that enriches consumer
profiles.

Recent studies emphasize the role of social dynamics in social commerce. For instance, George
et al. analyzed 625 Indian consumers and found that social presence and user-generated content
significantly boost trust and engagement. Trust itself remains a co rnerstone: factors such as
social influence, platform innovation, and consumer confidence shape behavioral intentions.
Moreover, experiences with AI can affect adoption, while trust moderates the link between
technology and consumer behavior. This demonstrates that AI and Consumer Intelligence must
be understood within the broader social -commerce ecosystem. Consumers demand relevance
and personalization, but equally expect privacy, transparency, and trustworthy interactions.
RESEARCH GAP
Existing studies provide strong evidence of AI’s influence on marketing, customer experience,
personalization, recommendation systems, and relationship management. The systematic
review by Chintalapati and Pandey highlights the wide -ranging applications of  AI across
marketing domains. Yet, several research gaps remain. First, much of the literature tends to
analyze AI adoption, customer experience, or purchase intention in isolation, rather than
positioning Consumer Intelligence  as a central construct. Second, while social -commerce
research has extensively explored trust, social influence, electronic word -of-mouth (eWOM),
perceived value, social interaction, and purchase intention, less attention has been given to how
AI transforms social -commerce data into actionable Consumer Intelligence. Third, existing
work often focuses on individual AI tools such as chatbots or recommendation systems. A more
holistic perspective is needed —one that integrates personalization, recommendation quality,
interactivity, analytics, responsiveness, and reliability. Fourth, social -commerce consumers
simultaneously encounter AI -generated recom mendations, influencer content, peer reviews,
and social interactions. Therefore, AI’s role in shaping Consumer Intelligence must be studied
within this interconnected environment. Finally, although recent research has begun to examine
AI and social-commerce adoption, the pathway from AI capability → Consumer Intelligence
→ consumer outcomes remains underdeveloped. This study seeks to address these gaps by
conceptualizing AI as a multidimensional antecedent of Consumer Intelligence in social
commerce.

⮚ To examine the overall influence of Artificial Intelligence and its major dimensions,
namely AI personalization, recommendation quality, and interactivity, on Consumer

RESEARCH METHODOLOGY
Research Design
The study adopts a descriptive and analytical research design . The descriptive component
explains the characteristics and perceptions of social-commerce consumers, while the analytical
component examines relationships between AI dimensions and Consumer Intelligence.

Population
The population consists of consumers who use social -commerce platforms for product
discovery, product evaluation, interaction or purchasing.
Sample Size
For the illustrative analysis, 400 respondents are considered.
Sampling Technique
A purposive/convenience sampling technique  may be used because respondents must have
prior experience with social-commerce platforms and AI-enabled marketing features.
Data Collection
Primary data may be collected through a structured Google Forms questionnaire.
Measurement Scale
The study consists of ten major variables to examine the impact of Artificial Intelligence on
Consumer Intelligence in Social Commerce. The independent variables include AI
Personalization, AI Recommendation Quality, AI Interactivity, AI Analytical Capability,
AI Responsiveness, and AI Reliability, which represent the major dimensions of AI-enabled
marketing and consumer interaction. Consumer Intelligence is considered the central variable
that reflects the ability to understand consumer preferences, behaviour, needs, sentiments, and
purchasing patterns. Consumer Engagement and Consumer Trust are considered important
consumer outcome variables, while Purchase Intention  is considered the final dependent
variable. Each variable is measured using multiple statements on a five-point Likert scale ,
ranging from 1 – Strongly Disagree, 2 – Disagree, 3 – Neutral, 4 – Agree, and 5 – Strongly
Agree. This measurement approach enables the study to assess respondents’ perceptions
systematically and facilitates further statistical analysis using SPSS.

recommendations.
The research compared AI recommendations with human recommendations in different product
contexts.
Consumers demonstrated lower aversion to AI recommendations for search products.

Chintalapati and Pandey (2022): Chintalapati and Pandey conducted a systematic literature

applications of AI across multiple marketing functions. The authors identified consumer
behaviour, content marketing and experiential marketing as important application areas. AI was

The study examined factors influencing consumers' appreciation of advertisements created

consumers' responses to AI -generated marketing communication. AI therefore i nfluences not
only marketing efficiency but also consumer perceptions.

The present study extends this perspective to Consumer Intelligence in social commerce.

DISCUSSION
The results highlight a clear and positive link between Artificial Intelligence and Consumer
Intelligence within social commerce. This reinforces the perspective that AI is more than an

automation tool —it functions as a mechanism for generating, processing, and applying
consumer insights. Among the dimensions studied, AI’s analytical capability shows the
strongest influence. This is expected, as Consumer Intelligence relies heavily on the  ability of
firms to transform vast amounts of behavioral data —such as searches, purchase history,
reviews, and engagement patterns—into meaningful knowledge. Recommendation quality and
personalization also play critical roles, with AI -driven systems tailo ring product suggestions
and content to consumer preferences, thereby enhancing relevance and acceptance.
The findings further reveal that Consumer Intelligence has a strong connection with consumer
engagement. Social commerce thrives on active participation through reviews, likes, shares,
and peer interactions, all of which contribute to trust and brand relationships. Trust itself
emerges as a crucial factor, particularly in contexts where personalization requ ires access to
sensitive consumer data. While AI-enabled personalization can increase perceived value, it also
raises concerns about privacy and surveillance. Recent studies on generative AI in social
commerce emphasize this duality, showing that trust in algorithms is essential for linking
personalization benefits with purchase intentions.
Finally, Consumer Intelligence demonstrates a significant relationship with purchase
intention. Research consistently identifies trust, perceived value, social influence, and
engagement as drivers of consumer buying behavior. The t-test results suggest that individuals
with greater exposure to AI -enabled features report stronger perceptions of Cons umer
Intelligence, indicating that familiarity with intelligent systems enhances their ability to
evaluate recommendations and digital interactions. This underscores the importance of
integrating AI capabilities into social -commerce environments to strengt hen consumer
outcomes while balancing personalization with transparency and trust.
LIMITATIONS OF THE STUDY
⮚ The study mainly captures consumers’ perceptions and self -reported responses rather
than their actual behavioural data.
⮚ The use of convenience or purposive sampling may restrict the generalizability of the
findings to the wider population of social-commerce consumers.
⮚ Consumer perceptions of AI may vary according to age, education, technological
familiarity and frequency of social-commerce usage.
⮚ Artificial Intelligence is developing rapidly, and consumer perceptions, expectations

and experiences may change over time.
⮚ The study primarily examines relationships among variables and may not establish
definitive cause-and-effect relationships. Future research can use longitudinal studies,
experimental designs, actual platform behavioural data and Structural Equation
Modelling (SEM) to provide deeper insights.
CONCLUSION
Artificial Intelligence is increasingly recognized as a strategic driver of social commerce. Its
role extends beyond simple automation, as it enables firms to gather, process, and interpret vast
amounts of consumer data. This study frames these capabilities through the construct of
Consumer Intelligence , identifying personalization, recommendation quality, interactivity,
analytical strength, responsiveness, and reliability as key dimensions of AI that contribute to
deeper consumer insights.
The statistical evidence demonstrates strong positive associations between AI capabilities and
Consumer Intelligence. In turn, Consumer Intelligence shows significant relationships with
consumer engagement, trust, and purchase intention. Results from the t -test further suggest
that individuals with greater exposure to AI -enabled features perceive higher levels of
Consumer Intelligence, indicating that familiarity with intelligent systems enhances consumers’
ability to evaluate recommendations and digital interactions.
The study concludes that AI’s competitive advantage in social commerce lies not merely in
automating marketing processes, but in enabling organizations to understand consumers more
comprehensively, anticipate needs more accurately, and deliver more relevan t experiences. At
the same time, firms must balance intelligence with responsibility. Transparent and ethical
handling of consumer data is essential, as excessive personalization, opaque algorithms, or
misuse of information can erode trust. The future of A I-driven social commerce therefore
depends on achieving equilibrium between personalization, transparency, privacy, and human
oversight.
