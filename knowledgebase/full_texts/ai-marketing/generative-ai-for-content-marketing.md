Abstract
Generative artificial intelligence (AI) is a branch of AI that operates based on user-inserted prompts to
generate unique digital content. Marketers adopt generative AI for content marketing because of its
affordability, efficiency, user-friendliness, and human-like quality outputs. Key applications of generative AI in
content marketing include content planning, content creation, content personalization, content repurposing,
SEO content, real-time content marketing actions, and performance analysis. Despite its advantages however,
generative AI has limitations. There are issues of hallucination, biased content, lack of authenticity, and data
privacy concerns. To fully harness generative AI’s potential, marketers need to integrate its capabilities with
human oversight and a strategic approach to mitigate risks.

Generative AI Definition
In a more comprehensive technical sense, generative artificial intelligence (AI) is deep neural networks pre -
trained on large -scale data to develop a foundation model, which is subsequently customized to generate
original content in response to human comma nds (Cillo & Rubera, 2024) . Viewed from the users' perspective,
generative AI simply refers to a branch of AI that operates based on user -inserted prompts to produce novel
digital content (Wahid, Mero, et al., 2023). In this more straightforward definition of generative AI, the prompts
are the inputs. They can appear as natural human language, allowing users to communicate their intentions and
requests in plain text. Other than this, the prompts can also increase in complexity, manifesting as a broad array
of formats, such as long technical documents, mathematical equations, datasets, website links, code snippets,
audio, images, and even videos. Generative AI then processes these instructions and items provided by humans
to spawn the content. The content here is the output, and it is entirely unique. AI -generated content (i.e., the
content created by generative AI) is a synthesized product, not an exact duplicate of any specific data from the
training dataset. It is assembled by generative AI by combining elements from various data points into distinctive
outputs through the machine’s understanding of patterns, relationships, and structures acquired during the
training. Table 1 exemplifies the generative AI solutions available in the market.

Content Marketing Definition
Content marketing denotes the activities of identification, creation, and distribution of valuable content with
the goal of fostering customer engagement and, eventually, marketing performance (Hollebeek & Macky, 2019;
Terho et al., 2022). In practice, content marketing tasks can include content planning, content personalization,
content creation, content repurposing, real -time content marketing actions, content curation, search engine
optimization (SEO), and performance analysis. Executed effectively, these content marketing works can enhance
the likelihood of marketers satisfying their consumers and, hence, persuading them to engage. Once customer
engagement actualizes, marketers can reap benefits, such as through the improvement of custo mer lifetime
value, stock values, and gross ads profits (Borah et al., 2020; Kanuri et al., 2018; Meire et al., 2019).
Reasons to Use Generative AI for Content Marketing
There are four primary reasons to adopt generative AI for content marketing. First, generative AI is affordable.
Tools provided by generative AI providers such as ChatGPT, Midjourney, HeyGen, and ElevenLabs are available
at varying price points, with subscription fees ranging from free tiers to premium plans. This affordability allows
individual marketers and companies to experiment with these tools with minimal risks. If the tools fit marketers’
content marketing needs, they can continue using them. Otherw ise, marketers can just abandon them as the
loss is insignificant. Second, generative AI is relatively fast in processing prompts and producing outputs. This
aspect cuts the time and effort for content marketing works and, accordingly, improves efficiency.  Third,
generative AI is user-friendly. The usage of generative AI is highly intuitive—the operation of generative AI tools
is mostly done through natural language, not complicated commands and buttons. This user -friendliness of
generative AI enables marke ters to exercise content marketing tasks without coding skills. As such, it reduces
marketers’ reliance on human technical experts, which eventually benefits them financially. Fourth, generative
AI outputs are perceived to have human-like qualities. AI-generated content is often on par with or even better
than its human-made counterpart (see Hartmann et al., 2024; Reisenbichler et al., 2022) . Such quality outputs
signal the possibility of generative AI and its AI -generated content to enhance the effectiveness of content
marketing.

synthesize vast amounts of data fro m various sources to help develop innovative ideas and analyze data to
suggest actionable insights. These abilities demonstrate that generative AI not only can create but also inform
and enhance strategies, making it a comprehensive tool for content market ing. In this case, there are eight key
applications of generative AI to support content marketing activities: (1) content planning, (2) content
personalization, (3) content creation, (4) content repurposing, (5) real -time content marketing actions, (6)

Content Planning
Generally, generative AI can streamline content planning by analyzing data, inspiring ideas, and building the
foundation of effective content marketing strategies. Among the specific content planning tasks, generative AI
can support examining consumer behavior and engagement metrics to identify content themes that reverberate
with marketers’ target audiences. The technology can also suggest content calendars and propose topics tailored
to specific seasonal market demands. Moreover, generative AI can aid mar keters in distilling data from
competitor activities and industry insights to uncover gaps and opportunities for the development of their
content marketing strategies.
Content Personalization
As indicated above, generative AI facilitates content personalization. With generative AI, marketers can
personalize their content at scale in which they can create and deliver customized content targeted specifically
for individual segments or even indivi dual users on a large scale across a vast audience. Generative AI achieves
this by analyzing user data (e.g., preferences, behaviors, and demographics) and automating the production of
personalized content based on the analysis results. In this generative AI-assisted personalization, content
dynamically adapts, aligning with the target audience’s unique characteristics (e.g., language spoken and cultural
background), needs, and behaviors. Unlike traditional content personalization that is limited in scope, content
personalization at scale facilitated by generative AI enables marketers to produce tailored content and
experiences for thousands or even millions of customers while maintaining consistency across various
touchpoints, such as websites, emails, apps, and social media platforms.
Content Creation
The actual content creation improves significantly with generative AI. The technology can produce novel
multimedia content in the forms of texts, photos, audios, and videos. In addition, generative AI can also generate
multitype content —content types here refer to the quality of content. There are three overarching content

types: informational (i.e., content laden with information), emotional (i.e., content conveying emotional
messages), and transactional (i.e., content involving monetary values) content (Meire et al., 2019;
Shahbaznezhad et al., 2021; Wahid, Karjaluoto, et al., 2023). Beyond the capabilities to produce multimedia and
multitype content, generative AI also possesses multilinguality. Generative AI’s multilinguality denotes the
technology’s capability to process multiple languages for linguistic-related tasks, such as cross-language content
generation and translation. This capability helps marketers create multilanguage content, and it is particularly
useful for those brands operating in international markets where customers communicate in diverse languages.
Content Repurposing
Content repurposing corresponds to the activities of maximizing the value of existing content by transforming it
into new formats and adapting it for different platforms. Generative AI simplifies content repurposing. For
example, generative AI can condense  a long-form blog post into a series of social media posts, convert it into a
video script, or reimagine it as an infographic. Generative AI can also adjust the tone and style of content to suit
various channels. Additionally, relating to multilinguality a bove, generative AI can transform existing content in
a particular language into various languages to serve customers in global markets. By leveraging generative AI
for content repurposing, marketers can maintain a dynamic and versatile content marketing s trategy while
simultaneously expanding the reach and impact of branded content.
Real-Time Content Marketing Actions
Unlike planned content marketing activities, real -time content marketing actions focus on the current
conversations and trends wherein marketers identify, create, and distribute as events unfold. Such real -time
content marketing actions are highly critical as they can enhance virality, customer engagement, and even firm
values (see Borah et al., 2020 , in the research the real -time actions are called improvised marketing
interventions). Generative AI empowers marketers to execute the real -time actions. To attain this objective
nonetheless, the implementation of generative AI tools alone is insufficient . This is because generative AI
programs are built on pre -trained models that rely on datasets frozen at a specific point in time (e.g., OpenAI
GPT-4’s training cutoff in 2023), and therefore, they cannot natively recognize trends that emerge after their
training cutoff date. To overcome this limitation of non -updated training data, marketers need to integrate
generative AI tools with external real-time data sources such as Application Programming Interfaces (APIs), live
web scraping tools, or analytics pla tforms. For example, platforms like BuzzSumo, Hootsuite, or Sprout Social
analyze trending hashtags, posts, and discussions. These insights can be fed into generative AI tools. Once trends
are identified using external systems, generative AI can quickly pr oduce relevant content tailored to those
trends.
Content Curation
In general, content curation can be defined as the activities of discovering, organizing, and sharing relevant third-
party content that aligns with a brand’s strategy. Generative AI enhances content curation. Traditionally, content
curation requires market ers to sift through vast amounts of information to identify valuable and credible
resources. Generative AI automates this task by analyzing industry trends, monitoring news platforms, and
scanning social media to identify shareable content that resonates w ith target audiences. Tools like BuzzSumo
and Pocket can assist in finding articles, videos, or infographics, while generative AI systems can further evaluate
the relevance and credibility of the content —this content curation task may or may not utilize th e
aforementioned real-time actions of generative AI. Additionally, generative AI can provide value-added curation
by generating summaries or commentary for curated pieces, helping marketers contextualize external content
within their own messaging. By inte grating generative AI into their content curation work, marketers can

consistently deliver relevant content to engage their audience, build thought leadership, and maintain a steady
flow of valuable resources across digital platforms.
SEO Content
Generative AI enhances SEO content by automating and refining the processes required to improve a brand’s
online visibility. It can generate SEO -friendly titles, topics, meta descriptions, and content structures that align
with search engine algorithms. Ge nerative AI can also perform keyword analysis to identify trending and high -
performing keywords —this capability may or may not involve the aforementioned real -time actions of
generative AI. Used effectively for SEO content, generative AI may help marketers  improve opportunities for
content reach, search relevance, organic traffic, search rankings, content readability, and customer engagement.
Pertaining to this topic of SEO, generative AI has transformed consumer search behavior. Conventionally,
consumers go to search engine platforms when they seek information. Search engine platforms then spawn
relevant links that direct consumers to websites. The information in the search results is incomplete (i.e., only
links and several sentences or snippets of content being displayed). To fully answer their questions, consumers
must visit the websites the search engines recommend. Now, with generative AI at consumers' hands, search
behavior changes. When consumers have questions, they chat with generative AI tools, and the machines list
comprehensive answers for them. Consumers demand straightforward and thorough answers that solve their
problems. In respo nse to these changes in consumer search behavior, search engine platforms have begun to
imitate generative AI to counter and display replies to consumers' queries. Microsoft, for example, has launched
its AI-powered Bing. Consumers can ask Bing to answer their questions. Bing then provides specific and complete
answers (along with the links to the content sources). The asking -answering activity between consumers and
Bing is in the form of a natural digital conversation, and it can go on and on until the con sumers are satisfied.
This novel search behavior and display of search results naturally affect marketers’ overall SEO content
strategies.
At the time of the writing of this book chapter, there are no absolute answers for effective SEO content strategies
in the age of generative AI as t he integration of generative AI -powered search functions remains in its infancy.
Search engines and generative AI providers are still actively experimenting and refining these features. Besides
Microsoft, Google also has just integrated generative AI into its search engine machine  in May 2023.  OpenAI
advanced this trend by embedding a search function directly in to its ChatGPT in October 2024 (with this search
function in ChatGPT, OpenAI enables users to access real -time web data alongside its generative capabilities).
Considering such novelty, what marketers can do is also experiment. In their exploration, market ers need to
ensure that their content satisfies the emerging consumer expectations for detailed, conversational, and
problem-solving content and simultaneously monitor the ongoing changes closely so that their SEO content
favors the generative AI-powered search engine algorithms.
Performance Analysis
Generative AI amplifies performance analysis in content marketing by automating the evaluation of key metrics
and providing actionable insights. The technology can analyze customer engagement statistics, such as likes,
shares, comments, click-through rates, and conversion rates. Generative AI can also uncover patterns and trends
within the data, such as the types of content that drive the highest engagement or the optimal posting times for
specific platforms. Beyond analysis, generative AI can summarize fin dings and generate comprehensive reports
to help marketers communicate overall content marketing performance with their stakeholders. The
streamlined process of performance evaluation enabled by generative AI assists marketers in making data -
driven decisions to optimize their ongoing and future content marketing strategies.

Limitations of Generative AI for Content Marketing
Despite the benefits offered by generative AI for content marketing, marketers still should be cautious in utilizing
the technology as it has several limitations. One of them is the hallucination problem, wherein generative AI
often generates nonsensical or false outputs. As an instance, generative AI may mistranslate languages (see Bang
et al., 2023). Used in content marketing, mistranslated content may elicit undesirable consequences. Other than
hallucination, there is the risk of bias. Generative AI models are trained on large datasets that may contain
biases, which can unintentionally be reflected in the content they produce. This is particularly problematic in
sensitive contexts, such as addressing diverse audiences or creating inclusive content. From a consumer
perspective, AI -generated content is perceived as generic and less authentic (Brüns & Meißner, 2024) . This
unfavorable perception toward AI -generated content may deteriorate marketing performance. Data privacy is
further another concern. Generative AI tools often require access to consumer data for personalization, which
raises questions about compliance  with data protection regulations such as the General Data Protection
Regulation (GDPR) or the California Consumer Privacy Act  (CCPA). Failing to adhere to these regulations may
cost marketers legal and reputational risks. To overcome these generative AI’s  weaknesses, marketers should
adopt a balanced approach. They need to combine generative AI’s capabilities with human expertise —and
oversight—to maximize its potential while mitigating risks.
