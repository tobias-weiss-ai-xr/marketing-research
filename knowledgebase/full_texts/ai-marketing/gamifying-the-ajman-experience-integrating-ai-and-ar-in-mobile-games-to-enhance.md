Abstract
This study investigates whether the combination of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Augmented Reality (AR)
based mobile gaming applications can be used to increase tourism in the United Arab Emirates (UAE),
specifically focusing on promoting tourist attrac tions within the Emirate of Ajman to Gen Z consumers.
While other studies have studied gamification in tourism contexts, this research addresses a significant
knowledge gap through proposing an integrated theoretical model that combines Technology Acceptance
Model (TAM), Stimulus Organism Response (SOR) model  and Self Determination Theory (SDT).
The study employed an experimental quantitative methodology to collect survey-based data from 450 Gen
Z consumers who were UAE nationals, but strategically prioritized respondents who lived in Ajman (45.6%)
as their primary target market.
Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) was utilized to assess both the measurement models and the structural
models associated with the proposed theoretical framework. The results indicate a bi -directional
relationship; cognitive utility is primarily driven by the effect of AI personalization while affective states are
primarily driven by AR immersion and gamification mechanics. Additionally, both pathways significantly
influence the intent to utilize the application, and there exists a strong correlation betwe en the intent to
utilize the application and actual behavior of visiting Ajman (r= .584, p<.01). This study represents one of
the first empirical examinations of AI and AR gamification in the context of tourism marketing, providing
valuable information for  destination management organizations (DMO's) looking to attract Gen Z
consumers who are native to digital media.

1. Introduction
The tourism sector of the United Arab Emirates (UAE) is highly developed, providing substantial financial
support for the diversification of the country's economy. As an example of this, the UAE consistently ranks
among the leading destinations in the Middle East/North Africa (MENA) region with respect to international
visitor arrivals and their associated expenditure (UNWTO, 2020). In general, the majority of international
visitors visiting the UAE tend to travel to its largest cities such as Dubai and Abu Dhabi to see popular tourist
attractions including the Burj Khalifa or the Louvre Abu Dhabi. However, other emirates within the UAE
such as Ajman, Sharjah, Fujairah and Ras Al Khaimah have both rich cultures and natural environments that
can provide travelers with new experiences (Visit Dubai, 2021). Therefore, promoting tourism represents a
key initiative for supporting future economic growth for Ajman; in order to promote tourism in Ajman a
number of strategies and initiatives will need to be put into pl ace to enhance the contribution made by
tourism to Ajman's Gross Domestic Product (GDP), specifically through cultural, heritage and eco -tourism.
More recently, the UAE has made numerous investments into developing its tourism infrastructure and it
continues to use cutting edge technology, such as Artificial Intelligence (AI), to further improve the quality
of service for travelers. Initiatives such  as Dubai Tourism 2.0 highlight AI as a primary driving force behind
economic growth in the tourism industry (Dubai Tourism, 2021). Research conducted in recent years
emphasizes that AI is crucial to stimulate growth in sustainable tourism, enhancing both the operational
efficiency and smart tourism capabilities of tourism (Belkaid & Kummitha, 2026). One goal of the UAE
tourism sector is to utilize AI to enhance the personalized nature of marketing and communication, thereby
providing unique and custom-tailored assistance via chatbots and virtual assistants (Wüst & Bremser, 2025).
At the same time, the combination of augmented reality (AR) and gamification are emerging as effective
tools to better meet changing customer expectations. With more tourist journeys becoming creative,

1.1 Research Questions
The current study examines the causal relationships that underlie Gen Z tourist behaviors based upon the
identified knowledge gaps within the literature. This is achieved through four empirical research questions
as follows:
RQ1: What impact does the use of AI & AR technology have on Generation Z’s cognitive assessments
(perceptions of Perceived Usefulness & Ease of Use) regarding an e-tourism app?
RQ2: Do AR Immersion and Gamification Mechanics enhance the affective state (emotional engagement
and intrinsic motivation) of Generation Z users when utilizing the e-tourism app?
Q3: Are there direct relationships between Generation Z’s cognitive assessment and affective state that
predict their intention to use the AI/AR game-based e-tourism app?
RQ4: Is the generation Z user’s intention to utilize the e -tourism app predictive of actual travel intentions
(i.e., will they intend to visit Ajman?)

2. Literature Review

2.1 Mobile Games and Their Potential for Promoting Tourism
The increasing number of smartphone users has allowed for mobile games to become one of the most
effective ways to promote tourism. According to (Pradhan et al., 2023) ,mobile games are able to increase
tourist satisfaction, cultural knowledge and encourage tourist participation through the use of gamification.
Gamification is defined by (Pasca et al., 2021), as the application of game design elements in non -gaming
context. This allows for destination based games to provide a more engaging and longer lasting experience
than traditional advertising methods. Additionally, mobile games can encourage visitors to explore new
places and learn about the history, culture and heritage of their travel destination through educational
gaming (Xu et al., 2017).
Furthermore, mobile gaming provides a convenient way to access information on the go and engage in both
physical activities and gaming simultaneously. In addition to creating interest in a location or providing
education, gamification may also generate emotional attachment to a location which results in repeat visits
and loyalty (Mousavi & Ghasemi, 2021). Gamified apps could potentially support sustainable tourism
practices through environmental awareness initiatives and ecologically responsible behaviors s uch as
reducing waste and preserving natural resources ( Rosmadi et al., 2024; Koo & Chung, 2020). A study
conducted by Mousavi & Ghasemi (2021), demonstrated that gamification creates positive emotional
connections between tourists and their destinations which in turn leads to stronger intentions to visit again.
In order to enhance the visitor experience, Rosmadi et al., 2024 found that incorporating gamification into
tourism related apps enhances the visitor experience through rewards such as points, badges, leaderboards
and challenges.

2.4 Advanced Applications of Augmented Reality in Tourism
Virtual reality (VR) creates entirely simulated environments while augmented reality (AR) adds contextual,
relevant and interesting digital content over real-world views in real-time. The combination of both physical
and digital realities has changed the l andscape of tourism as AR has created immersive, interactive
experiences that can be experienced throughout each stage of a visitor’s experience (Siang et al., 2021).
One of the simplest but potentially one of the most impactful ways AR is creating value in tourism is through
immersive navigation and way -finding. In contrast to GPS and traditional maps, which do give directions
but fail to provide accurate visualizations of where you are going; AR enabled navigation applications will
utilize your camera and  provide on screen real -time directions, location of notable points of interest, and
contextually relevant data about what you are looking at (Rock Paper Reality, 2025). For example, if you are
visiting a large historic site or in an unfamiliar downtown ar ea or large resort hotel, using AR technology
will make it easier to navigate because arrows, labels and information boxes will be superimposed upon the
actual viewable environment. Using this type  of application will reduce mental fatigue related to trying to
remember directions, decrease the likelihood of becoming lost, and increase self -confidence in exploring
unfamiliar areas without having to rely on others for assistance. Additionally, many AR applications will
include information regarding nearby amenitie s such as ATMs, restaurants, transportation stations, etc.,
thus making the travel process more efficient and enjoyable. (Wang, 2025).
Another key advantage of utilizing AR in tourism is its potential to deliver richly interactive forms of cultural
and historical storytelling. When AR is combined with the ability to transform static historical/cultural
monuments into interactive multi dimensional narratives; AR presents a unique opportunity to bring history
alive for viewers. AR dependent applications allow visitors to visualize what buildings looked like thousands
of years ago, participate in historical events in their original setting, interact with virtual people from the
past (Onirix, 2025); as well as other types of experiential opportunities. As these experiences are inherently

2.5 AI and AR Technology Combined for Tourism Promotion
The travel and tourism industry is undergoing rapid change through the use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and
Augmented Reality (AR). (Goodfellow et al., 2016) have identified AI as the ability to mimic human
intelligence using machine technologies like N atural Language Processing and Deep Learning. AR is able to
add digital information to a physical object to create an interactive experience. When combined they offer
tourists better quality experiences through personalized and experiential ways. (Buhalis  & Sinarta, 2019)
show how both AI and AR can be used to create customized recommendations and location -specific data,
allowing visitors to gain greater insight into culture and transportation knowledge about their destination.
They further support that AI and AR enable smart destinations to develop through the use of real time data
analysis. (Sousa et al., 2024).
Gen-Z has been raised in a digital world, therefore, Gen-Z is highly receptive to the gamification of tourism
created by AI and AR. For example, according to (Guo et al., 2020), AR app based products enable users to
engage in self guided , game -like tours that are more engaging and enjoyable than traditional tours.
Additionally, AR enables communication by way of content translation and previewing hotels and menus.
Finally, AI provides personalized customization options within mobile gaming applications based upon user
behavior, as stated by Song et al. (2021). In total, gamified AI/AR platforms create opportunities for
personalized, experiential, and self - controlled experiences which align well with Gen -Z traveler
preferences.

3. Theoretical Framework and Hypotheses
This atusy  combines the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), the Stimulus -Organism-Response (SOR)
framework and Self-Determination Theory (SDT) in order to assess the impact of using an AI/AR gamified
application. According to TAM, the degree to which users intend to adopt new technology depends on their
perceptions of its usefulness (PU) and ease of use (PEOU) (Davis, 1989). As per the SOR framework,
environmental stimuli (the AI/AR features of the application) are likely to influence internal states (such as
cognitive or affective reactions) which can lead to behavioral reactions such as usage of the application
(Mehrabian & Russell, 1974). Finally, SDT has identified two types of motivations: external and internal. In
this context, internal motivation occurs when the  user experiences enjoyment through immersion in the
game mechanics of the application, and satisfies their basic psychological needs for autonomy,
competence, and relatedness (Ryan & Deci, 2000). In order to support these theoretical frameworks, we
have developed a bifurcated conceptual model with the following five main hypotheses:
• H1: AI personalization is a significant predictor of both perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use.
• H2: Both perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use are significant predictors of the user's intention
to use the application.
• H3: The AR immersion and gamification mechanisms used within the application will be significant
predictors of both emotional engagement and intrinsic motivation.
• H4: Both emotional engagement and intrinsic motivation will be significant predictors of the user's
intention to use the application.
• H5: The user's intention to use the application will be a significant predictor of their intentions to visit
Ajman.

5. Methodology

5.1 Research Design and Sampling
This study employed an exploratory, quantitative approach based on the use of the designed mobile -app
as the primary tool for collecting data from Generation Z participants who are aged 12 -26 and live in the
United Arab Emirates; where there was a specific  focus on participants living in the emirate of Ajman
(45.6%). Ethical clearance was approved by the Ajman University Research Ethics Committee. As well, this
study conformed to the ethical standards of voluntary participation, obtaining informed consent f rom
participants, and protecting participant data. Data was collected during six -weeks at the end of 2025
beginning of 2026. Participants interacted with the mobile -app for an average of forty -five-minutes, and
completed embedded survey items that allowed for "real -time" data collection, and therefore reduced
respondent's propensity for socially desirable responses. The final sample consisted of a total of 450 valid
responses (a response rate of 68%).
A priori power calculations were performed using G*Power (effect size f² = .15; α = .05; Power = .95; four
predictors). Based on these calculations it was determined that a minimum sample-size of 129 was needed.
Additionally, a recommended ratio of respondents per item is approximately 10:1; thus, a minimum sample
size would be 450. Therefore, the final sample used within this research (N = 450) provides sufficient
statistical power.

5.2 Measurement, Reliability, Validity, and Data Analysis
In terms of the measures used in the study to assess constructs, seven point Likert type scale (five per
construct) were used to measure each construct. Reliability was established as a result of applying
Cronbach’s Alpha (alpha = .70 or higher according to (Nunnally, 1978) with all constructs having an alpha of
.90 or greater, which is indicative of high internal consistency. Convergent validity was established via the
use of both item-to-construct correlation and average variance extracted (AVE) values. Discriminant validity
was also established through examination of the inter -construct correlations. Each of these procedures
ensures that the measurement tool effectively captures the theoretical constructs being examined.
To analyze the quantitative aspects of the research, descriptive statistical analyses and reliability tests were
completed using SPSS version 28, and structural equation modeling (SEM) analyses were completed using
AMOS version 28. The participants’ survey  responses were encrypted and housed on servers designed to
be compliant with applicable data protection regulation. As there was less than two percent missing data,
it was addressed using maximum likelihood estimation. Before conducting SEM analyses, data screening for
normality, outliers and multicollinearity (all VIF < 3.3) was conducted. To evaluate the measurement and
structural models, the two step approach suggested by (Anderson & Gerbing , 1988) was followed. First,
confirmatory factor analysis (CFA ) was used to establish whether the measurement model fit the data
appropriately. Second, after establishing that the measurement model met criteria for acceptable fit, the
structural model and hypotheses were evaluated. Lastly, behavioral data collected f rom the app (usage
frequency/behavior related to features/puzzle completion rates/AR usage) were analyzed using descriptive
methods to help contextualize the results obtained from the surveys.

(r=0.814) and Perceived Ease of Use (r=0.825) for the app, supporting H1. Both perceived use and ease of
use were predictors of the intention to use the app  (supporting H2), validating the Technology Acceptance
Model (TAM).
Affective Process (H3-H4): AR Immersive Experience and Gamification Mechanisms significantly predicted
both Emotional Engagement and Intrinsic Motivation (supporting H3). Both emotional engagement and
intrinsic motivation were also predictors of the intent ion to use the app (supporting H4), which validates
the Self-Determination Theory/Self-Regulation Theory (SOR/SDT) process.
Behavioral Outcomes (H5): There is a significant relationship between the intention to use the app and the
intention to visit Ajman (r=0.584, p < .001), supporting H5. This important result shows that there is an
association between digital engagement with an app and actual tourist travel behavior.
Mediation Models: An indirect effect analysis based on 5,000 bootstrap samples showed that all three
mediation paths were statistically significant. The full mediation path from AI Personalization to the
intention to use the app through both Perceived Usefulness and Perceived Ease of Use was found (indirect
effect = 0.495; p < 0.001; 95% CI: 0.425 -0.565). A similar pattern of results was obtained for the mediation
path from both AR Immersive Experience and Gamification Mechanisms to the intention to use the app via
both Emotional Engagement and Intrinsic Motivation (indirect effect = 0.428; p < 0.001; 95% CI: 0.365 -
0.485).

7. Discussion

7.1 Theoretical Implications
In addition to providing empirical support for a bifurcated theoretical framework combining TAM, SOR and
SDT, the study adds considerably to the body of knowledge in tourism literature. As an example, it is
demonstrated that in the case of hedonic tourism applications, two different types of utility have different
paths from the application. AI -driven app elements predominantly generate cognitive assessments (i.e.
usefulness and ease of use) while AR - and Gamification -based app elements create affective and
motivational states. Most importantly, the study has shown that both of these routes contribute toward
generating the ultimate behavioral intention to visit the destination; thereby indicating that reliance upon
traditional TAM models for assessing gamified tourism experiences would be insufficient.
The results extend previous literature regarding technology adoption within the tourism industry. For
instance, as stated previously, (Ting et al., 2025) identified that gamification positively impacts tourists'
intentions to visit a location. However, the  current study identifies the specific mechanisms whereby AI -
and AR-features of apps impact this outcome. In contrast to prior TAM-studies where cognitive factors were
primarily considered, the present bifurcation-model illustrates that affective factors are also equally crucial
for gamified -tourism-applications. This finding supports recent research by ( Stylos & Vassiliadis, 2023)
pertaining to Generation Z's preferences. Stylos and Vassiliadis emphasize the role of emotional
engagement in the process of technology adoption.
Furthermore, there was a very strong relationship between "Intention to Use the App" and "Intention to
Visit Ajman," as evidenced by their correlation coefficient (r = 0.574, p < .001), and therefore indicates that
digital-engagement effectively translates to real world behaviorally measurable outcomes. The above result
aligns with recent literature relating to how embedded tourism destinations are within online games. (Lian
et al., 2024) show that when tourism-related aspects are relevant or resonate with game participants, then
travel-intentions will be greatly influenced.

7.2 Practical Implications for Ajman Tourism
For Destination Management Organizations (DMO’s) in Ajman, this research presents practical guidance for
developing an App and creating a tourism marketing strategy.
Firstly, AI based features should be centered on providing personalized travel recommendations; intelligent
itinerary planning and smart chatbots to provide maximum perceived usefulness and ease of use as these
cognitive factors influence the GenZ consumer’s intentions to adopt technology as it directly relates to their
desire for functional convenience.
Secondly, AR and gamification features should center around; 3 -D visualizations of AJMAN’S attractions,
story driven mission-based gameplay that links the player to AJMAN'S rich cultural heritage and history, and
rewards-based achievement systems that fost er exploratory behavior. The affective elements created will
result in GenZ’s  emotional connection with your destination resulting in intrinsic motivation leading to
sustained usage of your App ultimately translating to visitors. Lastly, by combining the two approaches, AI
based-cognitive utility and AR/GAMIFICATION based -emotional connection, DMO’s can develop an all -
encompassing digital experience that meets the multi -faceted needs of GenZ consumers. A dual-approach
such as this has been shown to be more successful than focusing solely on one technological outlet and is
consistent with recent studies regarding gamification design for GenZ consumers (Stylos & Vassiliadis,
2023).
7.3 Implications for Sustainable Tourism
While the primary focus of this study is on using a mobile game to promote tourist travel to Ajman, it also
has implications related to developing sustainable tourism in Ajman. The use of an AI/AR mobile game to
engage with travelers as they move through t heir vacation experience may be used by Destination
Marketing Organizations (DMOs) to create stronger relationships between tourists and destinations.
(Rosmadi et al., 2024) and (Mahmoud et al., 2024), found that gamification could be used to encourage the
adoption of sustainable tourism practices and enhance traveler knowledge of environmental conservation.
Therefore, an AI/AR mobile game developed for Ajman could include educational information regarding the
emirate’s natural environment, cultural landmarks, and sustainable tourism methods to support both
increasing tourist travel to the area and promoting environmentally responsible activities.
7.4 Limitations and Future Research
The current study has multiple limitations. The first limitation is due to this being a cross -sectional study.
Because of its nature, it cannot establish cause -and-effect relationships among the studied variables. A
second limitation would include limiting  this study to Generation Z within the United Arab Emirates (UAE)
because it may have limited the ability to generalize the findings across different geographic locations
and/or generations. The third limitation is that this study used self-report measures of behavioral intention
instead of objective measures of real behavioral outcomes; therefore, future studies should utilize objective
measurement methods for actual app use. Lastly, no attempt was made to measure moderating variables
including prior knowl edge of destinations visited, culture, and previous travel experience. It will be
beneficial to identify and understand the effects of those moderating variables in order to develop a better
understanding of the model.

8. Conclusion
The combination of Artificial Intelligence and Augmented Reality mobile gaming is an excellent way for
Ajman to promote its tourism industry to Generation Z. Tourism marketing using both types of technology
will provide better ways to understand how each i nfluences cognitive and affective states (the bifurcated
pathway model) allowing the development of digital experiences that generate significant real world
tourism growth.
Research conducted here demonstrates that the primary driver of cognitive utility (usefulness and ease of
use) is related to AI features, whereas AR and game based features are the primary drivers of affective
states (emotional engagement and intrinsic mot ivation) associated with the two paths required to predict
the intention to utilize the app, which in turn also predicts the intention to travel to Ajman. This research
provides DMOs a clear indication that in order to attract Generation Z they need to inc orporate all three
types of features into their digital platforms to create a balance of functionality and fun.
Further research should expand this model to include other locations within the UAE and the Middle
East/North Africa Region; identify possible moderators (destination familiarity, culture etc.) for future
studies on this topic; and study the long range beh avior outcome from app utilization on actual tourism
