Highlight
This study presents several suggestions and practical implications for organizations aiming to leverage digital

analytics, artificial intelligence, and IoT -enabled systems can significantly enhance the efficiency, adaptability, and
accuracy of pricing decisions.

Abstract
The rapid adoption of digital economy principles has transformed marketing price mechanisms, enabling firms to
utilize Big Data, artificial intelligence, and Internet of Things technologies for dynamic and personalized pricing.
This study systematically examines fifty empirical studies through a meta -analysis to evaluate the impact of digital
economy applications on marketing pricing in the digital era. The first objective analyzes the basic characteristics of
research, revealing that most studies were published after 2020, predominantly from public universities, and
primarily employed experimental designs. Sample sizes ranged from 40 to 120 participants, providing adequate
statistical power for effect estimation. The second objective synthesizes effect sizes, with results indicating a
moderate positive impact (mean Cohen’s d = 0.52, 95% CI: 0.41 –0.63), while organizational size, industry type, and
technology adoption level significantly moderated effect magnitudes. Experimental studies demonstrated higher
effects than correlational studies, emphasizing the role of methodological rigor. The third objective integrates
knowledge from multiple studies, highlighting that digital innovations systematically enhance pricing efficiency,
adaptability, and responsiveness. Findings underscore the importance of study design, innovation level, and
contextual factors in shaping outcomes. This research offers actionable insights for managers to optimize data -
driven pricing strategies and informs policymakers in promoting fair, efficient, and competitive digital markets. The
study contributes to theory by linking resource -based and innovation perspectives with practical digital pricing
applications.

Introduction
In an era of rapidly advancing technology, countries around the world are increasingly interconnected across
multiple dimensions, including economic, social, cultural, and governance aspects. From an economic perspective,
a new form of economy has emerged, gaining popularity and spreading across many nations worldwide, namely,
the “Digital Economy.” The digital economy refers to an economic and social system that applies Information and
Communication Technology (ICT) as a key mechanism to drive reforms and development across various sectors,
including production processes, business operations, trade, services, education, healthcare, public administration,
and other socio -economic activities. The digital economy plays a crucial role in promoting economic development,
improving the quality of life for citizens, and creating systematic employment opportunities (Chaiwat Klinlamphu,
2019).

The widespread use of social media, coupled with the continuous increase in connected devices and the
development of Internet of Things (IoT) technology a system that automatically connects devices to the internet,
particularly ICT devices such as smartphones, tablets, and other smart devices has created new channels for
businesses and consumers to engage in economic activities. Devices such as smartphones, tablets, and other smart
devices connect to the internet via IoT technology, which is part of ICT and plays a vital role in driving economies
worldwide. Many countries have adopted such technologies as tools for economic development, as the application
of ICT has the potential to promote high -quality economic growth by leveraging innovation and developed human
capital (Sergey Perminov & Elena Egorova, 2005).

ICT has become a key driver in transforming business operations and market mechanisms globally. Traditional price
mechanisms, once determined solely by supply and demand, have been rapidly enhanced through strategic
innovations, including dynamic pricing, personalized pricing, and consumer behavior analysis via Big Data and AI,
grounded in microeconomic and consumer behavior theories (Hassan, 2025; Barua et al., 2001). Dynamic pricing,

for instance, is a strategy used by digital businesses to adjust prices in real time according to market conditions,

Meanwhile, personalized pricing enables businesses to set prices based on individual consumers’ capabilities and
preferences, raising concerns related to privacy and fairness (FTC, 2024; The Guardian, 2022).

Numerous studies have investigated and applied principles of the digital economy in the context of marketing price
management, aiming to enhance responsiveness to market demand, reduce operational costs, and strengthen
business competitiveness. However, existing research lacks systematic knowledge synthesis, which is essential for
drawing comprehensive conclusions that can serve as references for policy development and practical application.
Therefore, the researcher considers conducting a meta -analysis as an appropriate method to synthesize and
analyze quantitative research on the same topic, aiming to summarize findings from multiple studies and identify
trends, relationships, and the level of influence of digital economy applications on marketing price mechanisms in
the digital era.

Meta-analysis is particularly important in this context because current studies are often conducted in isolation,
lacking a comprehensive synthesis, which makes it difficult to identify clear trends or relationships among factors
affecting marketing price mechanisms in the digital economy. Meta -analysis allows researchers to aggregate
findings from multiple studies to draw clear, overarching insights, filling gaps in the literature caused by fragmented
research. It serves as a crucial tool for understanding and identifying influential factors in the application of the
digital economy in markets, leading to policy and strategic benefits that can be applied to develop economic
systems and marketing price mechanisms appropriately and efficiently, thereby enhancing national economic
capabilities and enabling price mechanisms to adapt effectively in the sustainable digital economy era.

3. LITERATURE REVIEW
Concept of Meta-Analysis
In recent decades, the rapid development of information and communication technologies (ICT) has transformed
global economic and social landscapes, giving  rise to the digital economy as a key driver of growth, innovation, and
competitiveness (Chaiwat, 2019; Tapscott, 1996). The digital economy is characterized by the integration of ICT
into all aspects of economic activity, from production, distribution, and service delivery to business management
and public administration. This integration enables real -time data collection, automated processes through
Internet of Things (IoT) devices, and the use of Big Data analytics, which collectively influence market dynamics
and pricing mechanisms (Perminov & Egorova, 2005; Google, Temasek, & Bain & Company, 2024).

Meta-analysis provides a systematic method for synthesizing research findings from multiple studies, allowing
researchers to quantitatively assess the relationships between digital technologies and pricing mechanisms. Unlike
traditional narrative reviews, which rely primarily on qualitative assessment and thematic comparison, meta -
analysis integrates statistical techniques to standardize outcomes and evaluate effect sizes, providing a
comprehensive understanding of patterns and causal relationships across studies (Nonglak, 1999; Glass, 1976;
Lipsey & Wilson, 2001). This approach is particularly relevant in digital economy research, where diverse studies
examine the effects of ICT applications, Big Data, and digital strategies on market behavior and price adjustments.
A key distinction of meta -analysis lies in its explicit definition of  variables. Researchers define study characteristics,
methodological factors, and outcome variables, ensuring comparability across studies and enabling statistical
evaluation. Data collection is conducted using structured instruments, such as coding sheets and data extraction
forms, which enhance reliability and consistency, particularly when multiple researchers are involved (Cooper,
2010; Borenstein et al., 2009). Outcomes are transformed into standardized indices, such as effect sizes, facilitating
quantitative comparisons and meta -regression analyses to examine moderating factors. These procedures allow
meta-analysis to provide deeper insights than narrative reviews, particularly in identifying conditions under which
digital economy applications most effectively influence pricing mechanisms (Valentine et al., 2010).

The process of meta -analysis generally involves five stages. The first stage is defining research problems and
objectives, focusing on trends, variances, and relationships among study characteristics and outcomes. The second
stage involves reviewing relevant literature to construct theoretical frameworks and formulate hypotheses. The
third stage is systematic data collection, including the identification and selection of  studies, creation of structured
instruments, coding study features and outcomes, ensuring data quality, and preparing datasets for statistical
analysis. The fourth stage is quantitative data analysis, including descriptive statistics, assessment of
heterogeneity, effect size calculation, and examination of moderator variables. The final stage involves
synthesizing results, discussing findings in relation to prior theory, and offering evidence -based recommendations
for both policy and practical application (Hedges & Olkin, 1985; Fei et al., 2026; Cooper et al., 2019).

Numerous studies have applied meta -analytic methods to examine how digital technologies influence pricing
mechanisms in modern markets. For instance, Gao (2025) analyzed over 200 international studies on digital
economy applications and found that ICT and Big Data significantly enhance pricing dynamics by enabling adaptive
and real -time price adjustments. Javaid (2024) highlighted the role of digital technologies in Industry 4.0,
demonstrating that flexible, data -driven pricing structures allow firms to respond efficiently to consumer demand.
Chukurna (2020) emphasized that in digital markets, pricing decisions increasingly rely on real -time customer data
and analytics rather than solely on cost considerations. Regional studies, such as Ha (2023), examined Southeast
Asian markets, indicating that consumer experience and emerging technologies directly influence pricing strategies
in highly competitive sectors such as e -commerce. In the Thai context, Jongwanich (2024), Channuwong et al.
(2026) and Caplanova (2025) observed that digitalization in both the private and public sectors improves market
transparency, pricing efficiency, and policy effectiveness, while the TPSo (2024) master plan demonstrates how
government-supported digital strategies can promote fair competition and data -driven pricing in e -commerce
markets.

The integration of meta -analysis with digital economy research offers critical advantages. By quantitatively
synthesizing findings, researchers can identify the mechanisms through which ICT, Big Data, and digital platforms
affect market behavior and pricing structures, evaluate the magnitude of these effects, and determine the
conditions under which they are  most influential. This evidence-based approach enables the development of more
effective pricing strategies, informs policy -making, and enhances competitiveness in digital markets. Furthermore,
it addresses gaps in the literature where studies have been fragmented or context -specific, providing a holistic
view of how digital technologies reshape pricing mechanisms across industries and regions.

In conclusion, meta-analysis serves as a powerful methodological tool for understanding  the complex relationships
between digital economy applications and market pricing mechanisms. By enabling systematic synthesis, rigorous
statistical evaluation, and identification of moderating factors, meta-analysis supports evidence-based insights that
can inform strategic decisions, policy development, and practical implementation in digital markets. Its application
in the study of ICT -driven pricing not only enhances theoretical understanding but also provides actionable
guidance for firms and policymakers seeking to leverage digital technologies for competitive advantage (Hassan,
2025; Barua et al., 2001; Perminov & Egorova, 2005; Eckhardt et al., 2019).

Concepts and Theories of Digital Economy
In recent decades, the rapid advancement of information and communication technologies (ICT) has transformed
economic systems and market behaviors worldwide, giving rise to the digital economy as a central driver of
innovation, competitiveness, and value creation (Chaiwat, 2019; Tapscott, 1996). The digital economy is
characterized by the extensive integration of digital technologies into all aspects of economic activity, including
production, distribution, service delivery, and management. These technologies, including Big Data, cloud
computing, and the Internet of Things (IoT), not only enhance operational efficiency but also enable real -time
market analysis, data -driven decision -making, and dynamic adaptation of pricing strategies to meet consumer
demand and competitive pressures (Perminov & Egorova, 2005; Google, Temasek, & Bain & Company, 2024).
Meta-analysis serves as a critical methodological approach for synthesizing research findings in this context.
According to Nonglak Wiratchai (1999), meta -analysis provides a systematic and quantitative means to aggregate
results across multiple studies, producing standardized effect sizes that allow for meaningful cross -study
comparisons. Unlike traditional narrative reviews, which rely on qualitative synthesis and descriptive summaries,
meta-analysis emphasizes statistical rigor and replicability,  defining variables, employing structured data collection
instruments, and converting outcomes into standardized indices (Glass, 1976; Lipsey & Wilson, 2001; Cooper,
2010). This enables researchers to examine relationships between study characteristics, independent variables,
and dependent outcomes across heterogeneous contexts, and to identify moderators and conditional effects that

influence results (Borenstein et al., 2009; Valentine et al., 2010). The meta -analytic process generally involves
defining research questions, reviewing relevant literature, collecting and coding data, performing statistical
analyses, and synthesizing results into actionable conclusions and recommendations (Hedges & Olkin, 1985;
Cooper et al., 2019).

In digital economy research, meta -analysis has been applied to assess the impact of digital technologies on pricing
mechanisms. For instance, Gao (2025) analyzed over 200 international studies, demonstrating that ICT and Big
Data significantly enhance pricing dynamics in digital markets by enabling adaptive and real -time price
adjustments. Javaid (2024) highlighted the role of digital technologies in Industry 4.0, where flexible, data -driven
pricing structures allow firms to respond efficiently to consumer demand. Chukurna (2020) emphasized that in
digital markets, pricing decisions increasingly rely on real -time customer data rather than cost alone. Regional
studies, such as Ha (2023), found that consumer experience  and emerging technologies affect price  adjustments in
competitive sectors, particularly e -commerce. In the Thai context, Jongwanich (2024) and Caplanova (2025)
reported that digitalization in private and public sectors enhances transparency and accuracy in pricing, while the
TPSo (2024) master plan illustrates how government -supported strategies promote fair competition and data -
driven price setting in e-commerce markets.

Theoretical frameworks underpinning the digital economy provide insights into these dynamics. Digital Ecosystem
Theory conceptualizes the economy as an interconnected network of producers, consumers, and organizations
linked through digital platforms, enabling the exchange of information and real -time market responses (Thailand
Academic Report, 2024). Digital Strategy frameworks emphasize organizational goal setting, technological
assessment, customer experience design, and effective data analytics to generate competitive advantage (Digital
Strategy Institute, 2024). Digital Transformation Theory complements this by highlighting the development of
flexible organizational cultures and digital workforce competencies, facilitating innovation adoption and long -term
competitiveness, supported by policy interventions and cross -sector collaboration (DPRISM, 2024; Thailand
Academic Report, 2024).
The interplay between meta -analysis, digital economy theory, and pricing mechanisms provides a robust approach
for understanding how ICT, Big Data, and digital strategies influence market dynamics. Meta -analysis allows the
quantification of effect sizes and identification of moderating factors, revealing the conditions under which digital
technologies most effectively drive pricing strategies. In practice, firms can leverage these insights to implement
dynamic and personalized pricing models, enhance consumer responsiveness, and maintain competitiveness in
rapidly evolving digital markets (Hassan, 2025; Barua et al., 2001; Eckhardt et al., 2019). Moreover, these findings
inform policymakers seeking to regulate digital markets, promote transparency, and ensure fair pricing structures,
particularly in regions experiencing rapid digital adoption, such as Southeast Asia and Thailand.

In conclusion, integrating meta -analytic methods with digital economy frameworks offers a comprehensive and
evidence-based approach for understanding and optimizing pricing mechanisms in digital markets. By synthesizing
results across multiple studies and linking them to theoretical and practical insights, researchers and practitioners
can develop data -driven strategies that enhance efficiency, adaptability, and competitiveness, while supporting
policy development and sustainable economic growth in the digital era.

Concepts and Theories of Digital Strategy
Digital strategy has emerged as a critical framework for organizations seeking to leverage information and
communication technologies to achieve competitive advantage, create value, and adapt to rapidly changing
market environments. Unlike traditional business strategies, which primarily focus on operational efficiency or
market positioning, digital strategy emphasizes the integration of technology into every facet of organizational
operations, including product development, customer engagement, data management, and decision -making
processes (Digital Strategy Institute, 2024).
At its core, a digital strategy provides a structured approach for organizations to identify technological
opportunities, assess capabilities, and design initiatives that align digital resources with organizational objectives.
This framework enables firms to implement innovations such as automated processes, cloud -based platforms, and
real-time analytics, which facilitate rapid adaptation to market fluctuations and consumer preferences. The
concept of digital strategy also incorporates the strategic use of data for informed decision -making, predictive
analytics, and personalized services, allowing organizations to create tailored value propositions for diverse
customer segments (Hassan, 2025; Barua et al., 2001).

Several theoretical perspectives underpin digital strategy. One is the Resource -Based View (RBV), which highlights
the role of technological assets, organizational competencies, and data capabilities as sources of sustained
competitive advantage in the digital era. Firms that develop and integrate digital capabilities effectively can
differentiate themselves through superior customer insights, faster innovation cycles, and more agile operational
processes (Bharadwaj et al., 2013). Another perspective is the Dynamic Capabilities Theory, which emphasizes the
ability of firms to sense technological opportunities, seize them through strategic initiatives, and reconfigure
resources to maintain competitiveness in rapidly evolving digital markets (Teece, 2007). These theories suggest
that digital strategy is not only about technology adoption but also about developing organizational structures,
processes, and cultures that support ongoing transformation and innovation.

The practice of digital strategy includes defining organizational goals, evaluating technological readiness, designing
customer experiences, implementing data governance policies, and continuously monitoring digital performance
metrics. Techniques such as customer journey mapping, predictive analytics, and real -time market monitoring are
commonly used to optimize strategy execution (DPRISM, 2024; Youthao et al., 2026). Furthermore, the Digital
Transformation Theory complements digital strategy by highlighting the importance of cultivating a flexible
organizational culture, enhancing workforce digital skills, and fostering cross -functional collaboration to drive
innovation and long -term competitiveness (Thailand Academic Report, 2024). Policy frameworks and governance
structures at both national and industry levels play a supportive role in ensuring that digital strategies are
sustainable, scalable, and aligned with broader economic objectives.

Digital strategy has profound implications for market pricing and business models in the digital economy. By
integrating Big Data analytics, machine learning, and automated decision -making tools, organizations can
implement dynamic pricing, personalized offers, and adaptive marketing strategies that respond to consumer
behavior and market conditions in real time. This not only improves operational efficiency but also enhances
customer satisfaction and loyalty, while allowing firms to maintain profitability in highly competitive digital
markets (Eckhardt et al., 2019; Chukurna, 2020).
In conclusion, digital strategy represents a comprehensive framework that guides organizations in leveraging
technology to achieve strategic objectives, create value, and maintain competitive advantage in the digital
economy. Grounded in theories such as the Resource -Based View, Dynamic Capabilities, and Digital
Transformation, digital strategy emphasizes the alignment of technology, organizational resources, and business
processes to enable innovation, market responsiveness, and sustainable growth in rapidly evolving digital
environments.

4. RESEARCH METHODOLOGY
Research Design and Data Collection Method
This study employed a Meta -Analysis research design, a form of secondary research under the broader framework
of Systematic Review. The approach involved systematically searching, selecting, evaluating, and synthesizing
quantitative data from previous studies related to the application of digital economy principles in driving market
pricing mechanisms.
Study Population, Sample, Sample Size, and Sampling Technique
The study population consisted of graduate -level theses, dissertations, and institutional research reports
concerning the application of digital economy principles in market pricing mechanisms, published between 2020
and 2025.

The sample included studies that met the following inclusion criteria:
The research title contains the keywords “digital economy” or “digital-era market pricing mechanism.”
The study employed experimental or correlational designs addressing the application of digital economy principles
in market pricing. The study reported basic statistics or significance tests sufficient for effect size calculation.
A purposive sampling technique was applied to select studies that met the above criteria.
Data Collection Procedure
Data collection was conducted from July 2025 to December 2025 through the following steps:
Identification: Relevant studies were  identified using academic databases (e.g., ThaiLIS, Thai Digital Collection, Thai
Journals Online, PSU Knowledge Bank) and library search systems from universities including Chulalongkorn
University, Srinakharinwirot University, Silpakorn University, Khon Kaen University, Mahasarakham University, and
regional Rajabhat universities. Keywords such as “digital economy” or “digital-era market pricing mechanism” were
used.

Screening: Titles and abstracts were screened, and preliminary eligibility was determined based on the inclusion
criteria.
Eligibility Assessment: Full texts were reviewed to assess quality using a standardized evaluation form developed
by the researcher. Information on study characteristics and statistical data from experimental and correlational
studies was recorded accurately.
Data Organization: Extracted data were categorized and prepared for subsequent meta-analytic procedures.
The PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta -Analyses) Diagram was employed to
illustrate the selection process, including the number of records identified, screened, assessed for eligibility, and
included in the analysis.

Data Analysis
Meta-Analysis techniques were used to synthesize findings  and examine the relationship between digital economy
applications and market pricing mechanisms. Analytical procedures included:
Effect Size Calculation: Cohen’s d or Pearson’s r were calculated to estimate the magnitude of effects depending
on the type of statistical data reported in the primary studies.
Moderating Effect Analysis: Multiple regression or ANOVA was conducted to assess whether moderators such as
organizational size, industry type, or digital technology adoption influenced the relationship between independent
variables (e.g., use of Big Data) and dependent variables (e.g., price adjustments).
Descriptive Statistics: Initial analyses included frequencies, percentages, means, standard deviations, ranges,
skewness, and kurtosis to describe study characteristics.
Variance and Regression Analysis: One -way ANOVA and multiple regression analyses were conducted to examine
differences in effect sizes based on study characteristics, with study characteristics as independent variables and
effect sizes as dependent variables.
Synthesis of Findings: Content analysis was used to integrate and summarize knowledge regarding the application
of digital economy principles in market pricing mechanisms, highlighting influential variables for further
interpretation and discussion.

Research Instrument Validation
Two instruments were used for data quality assessment:
Research Quality Assessment Form: Consisted of evaluation criteria based on a 5-point Likert scale and guidelines
adapted from the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) for cross-sectional analytical studies.
Research Characteristics Recording Form (Code Book): Captured detailed characteristics and statistical results from
primary studies.
The instruments were reviewed and validated by advisors and experts, and inter-rater reliability was calculated
following Cooper and Hedges (1994) to ensure consistency in assessment.
Ethical approval for research involving human subjects was obtained from the Sirindhorn College of Public Health,
Yala, Thailand.
5. RESULTS
Results according to Objective 1: To analyze the basic characteristics of research related to the application of
digital economy principles in driving marketing price mechanisms in the digital era.
A total of fifty studies examining the application of digital economy principles in driving marketing price
mechanisms in the digital era were included in this analysis. The descriptive assessment of these studies revealed
several key characteristics. The publication years ranged from 2015 to 2025, with the majority of studies being
published after 2020. Regarding institutional affiliation, approximately sixty percent of the studies originated from
public universities, while the remaining forty percent were conducted by private universities or research
institutions. In terms of research design, thirty studies employed experimental methods, accounting for sixty
percent of the sample, and twenty studies utilized correlational designs, representing forty percent. The sample
sizes of the studies varied between forty and one hundred twenty participants, with a mean of sixty -five and a
standard deviation of twenty.

The independent variables investigated across these studies encompassed four major categories. The first category
included publication and researcher characteristics, such as the year of publication, the institution, the field of
study, the type of research, the gender of the researcher, the total number of pages, and the number of pages
excluding appendices. The second category involved research content characteristics, including the research
objectives, the target sample group, the study context, and the number of independent and dependent variables
examined. The third category concerned research methodology characteristics, such as the research design, the
formulation of hypotheses, sample size, sampling techniques, experimental design, research instruments, data

Results according to Objective 2: To conduct a meta -analysis of research characteristics on the effect sizes of
studies concerning the application of digital economy principles in driving marketing price mechanisms in the
digital era.
Effect sizes reported across the fifty studies were computed, using Cohen’s d for experimental studies and
Pearson’s r for correlational studies, standardized to Cohen’s d for consistency. The overall mean effect size was
0.52 (95% CI: 0.41–0.63), indicating a moderate positive impact of digital economy applications on marketing price
mechanisms. Experimental studies yielded a mean effect size of 0.55 (range: 0.35–0.75), while correlational studies
had a mean effect size of 0.48 (range: 0.30–0.65).

Moderating analyses indicated that organization size, industry type, and the level of digital technology adoption
significantly influenced the magnitude of effect sizes. Larger organizations, service -oriented industries, and studies
incorporating advanced digital tools such as Big Data, artificial intelligence, or the Internet of Things demonstrated
higher effect sizes. One -way ANOVA showed that experimental studies had significantly higher effect sizes than
correlational studies (F = 4.7, p = 0.03), and sample size was associated with more stable effect estimates, while

6. CONCLUSION AND DISCUSSION
Discussion according to Objective 1. The descriptive analysis of the fifty studies revealed several consistent
characteristics. Most studies were published after 2020, reflecting the  rapid adoption of digital economy principles
in contemporary marketing contexts. Public universities contributed the majority of studies, and the research
designs were predominantly experimental, indicating an emphasis on rigorous causal investigation. Sample sizes
varied from 40 to 120 participants, demonstrating sufficient statistical power for effect estimation. These findings
align with previous literature emphasizing the growing focus on digital innovation and methodological rigor in
pricing research (Porter & Heppelmann, 2015; Chen et al., 2020).
The identified characteristics underscore the importance of considering multiple dimensions, including publication
and researcher attributes, research content, methodological design, and applied innovations, when interpreting
meta-analytic outcomes. For instance, experimental designs provide more robust causal inference compared to
correlational studies, which is consistent with best practices in evidence -based management research (Bangbon et
al., 2026; Shadish et al., 2002). Additionally, the predominance of studies from public universities may reflect the
availability of research funding and institutional support for rigorous empirical investigations in the field of digital
economy and marketing.

Moreover, the variation in sample sizes suggests differences in study contexts and research objectives, highlighting
the potential influence of sample characteristics on effect estimation. This is consistent with prior studies
indicating that larger and more representative samples tend to produce more stable and generalizable effect sizes
(Glass et al., 1981). The descriptive findings also reveal a trend toward integrating innovative digital tools and
methods in recent studies, which aligns with the theoretical perspective that organizational and technological
innovations are key drivers of marketing performance in the digital era (Teece, 2018; Brynjolfsson & McElheran,
2016).

Discussion according to Objective 2. The meta -analytic synthesis of fifty studies revealed a moderate positive
impact of digital economy applications on marketing price mechanisms, with an overall mean effect size of 0.52
(95% CI: 0.41 –0.63). Experimental studies exhibited higher effect sizes than correlational studies, indicating the
critical role of research design in capturing causal relationships. This observation aligns with the principles of
evidence-based management, where experimental designs provide more reliable estimations of intervention
effects (Shadish et al., 2002).

Moderating analyses further highlighted that organizational and contextual factors significantly influenced effect
sizes. Larger organizations, service-oriented industries, and studies incorporating advanced digital tools such as Big
Data, artificial intelligence, and IoT demonstrated stronger effects. These findings are consistent with the

Resource-Based View (RBV), which posits that organizational capabilities and technological resources enhance
competitive advantage and performance outcomes (Barney, 1991; Wang et al., 2021). Moreover, the results
support the theoretical premise that technological adoption and industry characteristics act as boundary
conditions that amplify the effectiveness of digital interventions (Porter & Heppelmann, 2015).

Variations in effect sizes across studies also emerged due to methodological differences, including clarity of
research objectives, comprehensiveness of measurement instruments, and the use of real -world sample
populations. This trend echoes prior meta -analytic research indicating that methodological rigor and the
representativeness of samples are pivotal in determining the stability and magnitude of observed effects (Glass et
al., 1981; Brynjolfsson & McElheran, 2016). Collectively, these findings underscore the need for careful
consideration of both study design and contextual factors when interpreting effect sizes in meta -analytic research
on digital economy applications in marketing.

Discussion according to Objective 3. The synthesis of evidence across the fifty studies confirms that digital
economy innovations systematically enhance marketing pricing mechanisms. Studies that applied higher levels of
innovation such as AI -driven pricing, Big Data analytics, and IoT -enabled decision support reported the largest
effect sizes, emphasizing the practical importance of technological and process innovation in pricing strategy. This
aligns with Innovation Theory and prior empirical work demonstrating that technological sophistication and data -
driven strategies improve operational efficiency and market responsiveness (Teece, 2018; Wedel & Kannan, 2016).

Furthermore, research characteristics such as clearly defined objectives, robust instruments, and the inclusion of
real-world sample populations were associated with stronger effect sizes, highlighting the importance of
methodological rigor for generating reliable insights. These findings corroborate previous literature emphasizing
that valid and comprehensive measurement approaches enhance the generalizability of results (Kumar et al.,
2021).
From a practical perspective, organizations can leverage these insights to implement data -driven and digitally
enabled pricing strategies. Tailoring the adoption of digital tools to organizational size, industry type, and the level
of technology utilization can maximize impact, allowing firms to dynamically adjust prices, optimize profitability,
and maintain competitiveness in rapidly changing digital markets.

The synthesized evidence also provides theoretical contributions by integrating insights from multiple studies,
demonstrating the cumulative impact of digital economy principles on pricing, and clarifying the moderating roles
of organizational and contextual factors. Future research should explore longitudinal effects, cross -industry
comparisons, and emerging technologies, including blockchain and predictive AI models, to further expand
understanding of digital pricing mechanisms.
7. IMPLICATIONS OF THE STUDY AND FUTURE RESEARCH DIRECTIONS

Practical Implications
This study offers several practical implications for organizations aiming to leverage digital economy principles in
marketing price mechanisms. First, the results highlight that the implementation of digital innovations such as Big
Data analytics, artificial intelligence, and IoT -enabled systems can significantly enhance the efficiency, adaptability,
and accuracy of pricing decisions. Organizations should therefore invest in advanced digital technologies and
integrate them into strategic pricing processes to optimize revenue management and competitive positioning.

Second, the moderating effects identified by organization size, industry type, and level of digital technology
adoption suggest that firms need to tailor their digital economy applications according to their specific
organizational and market contexts. Large organizations and service -oriented industries may experience higher
returns from digital interventions due to their resources and operational complexity, whereas smaller firms should
focus on scalable digital solutions that align with their capabilities and market needs.
Third, the findings emphasize the importance of rigorous research design, comprehensive measurement
instruments, and real -world sample utilization. These factors not only improve the reliability of observed effects
but also provide actionable insights for practitioners when interpreting research outcomes to guide decision -
making.

Theoretical Implications

The study contributes to the theoretical literature by systematically synthesizing evidence on the effect of digital
economy applications on marketing price mechanisms. It demonstrates the cumulative impact of digital
innovations across multiple studies, supporting theories such as the Resource -Based View (RBV) and Innovation
Theory, which link organizational capabilities and technological innovation to competitive advantage and
performance outcomes. Additionally, the study highlights the role of contextual moderators, offering a framework
for understanding how organizational and industry -specific factors interact with digital interventions to shape
pricing effectiveness.

Future Research Directions
While the meta -analysis provides valuable insights, several avenues for future research remain. First, longitudinal
studies are needed to examine the long -term impact of digital economy innovations on pricing strategies and
market performance. Second, cross -industry comparative studies could identify sector -specific differences in the
adoption and effectiveness of digital pricing mechanisms. Third, emerging digital technologies, such as blockchain -
based pricing, predictive AI models, and real -time dynamic pricing platforms, should be explored to assess their
influence on effect sizes and market outcomes.

Furthermore, future research should consider incorporating grey literature, unpublished studies, and industry
reports to mitigate publication bias and provide a more comprehensive understanding of the field. Finally,
investigating additional moderating factors, such as organizational culture, managerial expertise, and regulatory
environments, could further clarify the conditions under which digital economy interventions are most effective.
