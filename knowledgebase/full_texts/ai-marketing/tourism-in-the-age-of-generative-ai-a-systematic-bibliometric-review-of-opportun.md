cautious approach towards the integration of GenAI in tourism. Emphasis is placed on
promoting sustainable and responsible practices while addressing the risks associated with
technological adoption. This research serves as a foundational reference for academics and
industry stakeholders seeking to navigate the evolving intersection of GenAI, destination

1. Introduction
The tourism industry is a vibrant and dynamic sector that plays a crucial role in
the global economy (Thommandru et al., 2023). In other words, tourism remains a
driving force for inclusive economic growth by offering employment opportunities
across a wide range of sectors, from hospitality and transportation to local crafts
and adventure tourism. Effective marketing strategies are essential in promoting
these experiences, attracting tourists, and in enhancing destination competitiveness.
According to the United Nations World Tourism Organization (UNWTO, 2023),
international tourism receipts that year reached approximately USD 1.4 trillion,
nearing 93% of the pre-pandemic USD 1.5 trillion generated in tourism in 2019.
However, the tourism industry has recently faced significant challenges, such as
the COVID-19 pandemic (Kumar et al., 2022). Contemporary trends in the hospi -
tality and tourism sector emphasise the importance of understanding and adapting
to evolving customer needs and preferences, while also addressing challenges such
as overbooking, ‘tourism-phobia’ and sustainability (Ramos & Mundet, 2021). To
address these challenges, innovative solutions based on technology have been pro -
posed to enhance security and safety (Andreyeva et al., 2021), transparency (Baralla
et al., 2021) and decentralisation (Yazici, 2022) in the tourism sector, in an effort to
open a pathway for a more resilient and sustainable industry in the future.

As mentioned above, technology has revolutionised tourism, from trip planning
to visitor experience and destination management. Mobile apps and augmented
reality (AR) provide interactive experiences at tourist attractions, fostering loyal -
ty and satisfaction (Smith, 2019). The tourism industry has undergone significant
transformations with AI, particularly in the recent form of large language models
(LLMs) (Meier, 2024).
LLMs are language models that use machine learning techniques to understand
and generate text. These models are capable of processing and generating text in a
very sophisticated manner, which allows them to perform tasks such as answering
questions, completing text and translating between languages. ChatGPT, launched
by OpenAI in November 2022 to ‘generate human-like text’ (Aydın & Karaarslan,
2022, p. 22), has exerted a significant impact on various industries, including hospi-
tality (Dwivedi et al., 2024), tourism (Carvalho & Ivanov, 2024), healthcare (Li et al.,
2024) and leisure (Ray, 2024).
The integration of LLMs in tourism represents a major step in addressing sector
challenges, since they enhance personalisation, destination management and decision-
making (Kovač et al., 2023). In hospitality, ChatGPT transforms information search,
decision-making and service customisation, while personalised recommendations
can support practices of a more sustainable nature. While travellers increasingly
demand tailored experiences, these models help anticipate evolving preferences and
enable the industry to adapt accordingly (Christakopoulou et al., 2023).
This study provides an overview of how this emerging technology may influence
the tourism industry and analyses the main areas currently under academic focus.
It also identifies under-researched issues with potential implications for the field.
To the best of our knowledge, no academic study has systematically examined the
intersection between GenAI and tourism. This paper seeks to address that gap by
providing an overview of the current state of research in this emerging field. Previous
reviews have examined artificial intelligence in tourism from broader perspectives,
such as state-of-the-art assessments of general AI applications in hospitality (Galle -
go Gómez & Vaquero Frías, 2022), bibliometric analyses of AI research in tourism
and hospitality (Şengel & Işkın, 2024), and conceptual reflections on the academic
implications of ChatGPT (Nautiyal et al., 2023). However, these studies either
address AI as a broad technological category that includes machine learning, robot -
ics, recommendation systems and pre-LLM conversational agents, or they focus on
educational and conceptual issues rather than tourism applications. None of these
reviews provides a structured, PRISMA-based synthesis combined with bibliometric
mapping focused specifically on generative AI or LLMs. This work isolates GenAI
as a distinct technological paradigm by analysing its emerging applications, risks,
themes and research gaps within the tourism industry, thereby offering the first sys -
tematic overview of this rapidly developing domain.
The authors have selected the following research questions:
RQ1:  How do LLMs, such as ChatGPT, influence the personalisation of tourism
experiences?

2. Scope of the review
This review is delimited by explicit temporal, thematic and documentary bound -
aries, together with the conceptual definitions that form the analysis. The temporal
scope covers publications between January 2023 and May 2024. The launch of
ChatGPT in late 2022 represented a turning point, since most academic contribu -
tions explicitly addressing generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) in tourism and
hospitality appeared subsequent to that date.
The thematic scope is the intersection of GenAI and the tourism domain. GenAI
refers to machine learning models capable of generating new content such as text,
images or code, with LLMs as a subset focused on advanced text generation and
comprehension. ChatGPT, released by OpenAI in 2022, is a paradigmatic example
due to its rapid adoption and influence. Tourism here includes hospitality, destina -
tion management, leisure and travel-related services, and excludes non-tourism uses
of the expressions ‘hospitality’ and ‘leisure’.
The documentary scope is limited to peer-reviewed publications indexed in the Web
of Science (WoS). This choice ensures data quality and replicability while excluding
conference proceedings, grey literature and materials published before 2023.
By clarifying these boundaries and definitions, the review establishes a coherent
and transparent framework. This both enables the findings to be interpreted within a
well-defined field of inquiry and facilitates future studies in the replication or exten-
sion of the approach while using comparable criteria.
3. Method
In order to evaluate the intertwinement between LLM use and its application to
tourism, and other interactions, a comprehensive systematic review was undertaken
adhering to the PRISMA statement (Moher et al., 2015). Peer-reviewed articles and
conference papers indexed in the Web of Science (WoS) were initially selected for
analysis. Given that WoS is the most reputed source of information for bibliographic
analysis (Zupic & Čater, 2014; Pradhan & Zala, 2021), all documents were extract-
ed exclusively from the WoS database.

The query was split into two parts: the left-hand part that looked for a word
or group of words that referred to generative AI (GenAI) and the right-hand part
that included words and groups of words on tourism. The first part started with
“large language model*”. This surfaced articles with word groups such as ‘Large
Language Model’ and its plural, ‘Large Language Models’. The query resulted in
1,186 documents. The results were further refined by excluding years prior to 2023,
since the initial research pieces started to appear soon after the onset of ChatGPT,
and the set was thereby reduced to 403 documents. With these limits, however, the
authors noticed that a large proportion of the documents were not related to LLMs
but rather to conversational AI prior to GenAI, to traditional pre-LLM chatbots,
natural language processing techniques for the analysis of customer behaviour, etc.
The search query was further refined: TS  =  (“large language model*” OR
“ChatGPT” OR “generative AI” OR “Generative language models” OR “LLMs”
OR “bard” OR “copilot”) AND (“tourism” OR “travel industry” OR “hospitality”
OR “leisure” OR “destination management”). These terms were also sought in other
academic publications; Table 1 shows the set of keywords and the main references
that justify their inclusion. In WoS, the query was executed in the Topic field (TS),
which examines the Title, Abstract, Author Keywords, and Keywords Plus.
As can be observed, the search query sought LLMs and genericisations, since
academics tend to employ ‘ChatGPT’ as a synonym for LLM, and the same occurs
with ‘Bard’ (Google’s GPT, now called Gemini) and Microsoft Copilot.
By integrating bibliometric and thematic analyses, a hybrid methodology was
adopted. The bibliometric component quantitatively examined publication and

The 44 documents were individually analysed and summarised to provide a com-
prehensive overview of their key contributions. Keywords Plus, a feature in the WoS
database that automatically generates keywords for each document, was not availa -

and tightness of the bonds within a theme cluster.

4.7 Results overview
Throughout this document, information has been analysed and presented regard-
ing research endeavours that address our initial inquiry, namely, which research ini-
tiatives have been undertaken at the intersection of GenAI and tourism. Specifically,
four research questions were posed to investigate the personalisation of tourism
experiences in light of GenAI’s emergence, the challenges and opportunities that
GenAI offers the tourism sector, its impact on sustainable tourism and the major
threats that GenAI poses to the sector. Table 6 summarises the most significant
research documents that respond to each research question. These studies provide
invaluable insights into the potential implications of LLMs on the tourism industry
today.
The documents offer valuable perspectives on how GenAI influences personalised
tourism experiences (RQ1). Topics explored include the integration of LLMs in tour-
istic companies’ workflows to offer personalised experiences (Dwivedi et al., 2023),
the potential of automatic responses to enhance customer service in the hospitality
sector (Limna & Kraiwanit, 2023), context-aware recommendations in hospitality
(Remountakis et al., 2023), and the forecasted increase in tourists using LLM text
prompts for information retrieval and decision-making (Christensen et al., 2024;
Duong et al., 2024; Kim et al., 2024; Kim, Kim, Kim, & Kim, 2023; Kim, Kim, Park,
et al., 2023; Shi et al., 2025; Solomovich & Abraham, 2024; Tosyali et al., 2023;
Wong et al., 2023).
Regarding the challenges and opportunities that GenAI presents to the tourism
sector (RQ2), various responses have emerged. Several studies focus on tourism
education and research (Ivanov & Soliman, 2023; Sugie, 2023). Elmohandes and
Marghany (2024) anticipate that GenAI may transform human resource hiring prac-
tices in the hospitality sector. Many studies expect a positive or very positive impact
of GenAI on the tourism industry, and highlight its capabilities (Carvalho & Ivanov,
2024; Dwivedi et al., 2024; Iskender, 2023; Limna & Kraiwanit, 2023; Mich &
Garigliano, 2023; Shin & Kang, 2023; Wang, 2024). Prevailing optimism regarding
GenAI’s impact on the tourism sector is evident across the majority of publications.

The third research question (RQ3) explores how GenAI may facilitate sustainable

6. Discussion
The analysis shows that although research on GenAI in tourism and hospitality
remains in its emergence phase, it is also evolving rapidly. The limited number of
publications indicates that a stable theoretical core has not yet been formed, although
the pace of contributions suggests that consolidation may come faster than in previ -
ous technological shifts. The literature is divided into two main clusters: one cluster
is concerned with practical applications, automated customer service, multilingual

and a second cluster focuses on ethical, social, and regulatory concerns, including

has triggered both processes simultaneously, thereby creating a tension between
innovation and legitimacy. These patterns resemble the early trajectories of other

cited, seminal studies that swiftly consolidate new fields. This gap triggers questions
regarding how GenAI research will define its future agenda.

GenAI accelerates personalisation and service co-creation; on the other, it challenges
established notions of trust, authenticity and human agency in tourism experiences.
This calls for conceptual frameworks that account for both functional efficiency and
normative legitimacy as coexisting dimensions of technological impact.
From a managerial and policy perspective, the findings highlight pressing impli -
cations. Tourism businesses are already experimenting with GenAI for customer
engagement, marketing campaigns and knowledge management, and expect gains in
productivity and competitiveness. At the same time, the literature underlines risks
that managers cannot ignore: algorithmic bias in recommendations, data protection
concerns and the potential deskilling of staff. Regulators face the challenge of fos -
tering innovation while safeguarding consumer rights and industry reputation. The
coexistence of opportunities and threats means adoption of GenAI (Cano-Marin,
2024), and must be accompanied by careful governance, investment in training and
transparent communication in order to sustain trust.
Despite its contributions, this review has certain limitations that are inherent to
a rapidly evolving field. The analysis is based on a narrow temporal window (2023–
2024) and a relatively small corpus of 44 documents, which reflects both the inno -
vation of the topic and its still-limited coverage in academic outlets. Furthermore,
the bibliometric approach is well suited to map production and citation trends but
offers only an initial lens on the conceptual debates that are still under development.
These constraints should be considered when interpreting the findings, but they also
highlight promising avenues for future research.
7. Conclusions
The novelty of generative AI in tourism research is reflected by the relatively
small number of existing studies, rather than a limitation of the review protocol.
This paper, therefore, provides a timely baseline for subsequent reviews as the liter -
ature expands. Our systematic literature review is limited to the articles available on
the Web of Science database. This may exclude relevant research published in other
databases or literature, which can potentially lead to a biased understanding of the
topic. Furthermore, the literature review covers publications up to a specific date.
Since the fields of GenAI and tourism are rapidly evolving, newer studies and emerg-
ing trends may have inadvertently been overlooked, which could greatly influence
the findings and conclusions. The intersection of GenAI and tourism involves multi-
ple disciplines, and hence future reviews might encompass diverse perspectives and
methodologies from other relevant fields.
Future research should focus on longitudinal studies to understand the long-term
impact of ChatGPT and other LLMs on the tourism industry, while considering both
technological advancements and evolving industry needs. Moreover, conducting
cross-cultural studies could provide insights into how different regions and cultures
adopt and adapt to GenAI technologies in tourism, whereby unique challenges and

technologies, such as AR, virtual reality and blockchain, could provide a holis -

Further research could be carried out to explore how GenAI influences consumer
behaviour, satisfaction and overall experience in tourism, by including personalised
recommendations and customer service interactions. Addressing ethical considera -

studies should explore frameworks and guidelines to ensure responsible and secure
deployment of these technologies. Applications and case studies of a more practical
nature could be considered in order to demonstrate the real-world impact of LLMs

destination management organisations.
