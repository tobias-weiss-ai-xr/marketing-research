ABSTRACT
Objective: To identify the market agents involved and calculate the commercialization margins of habanero
pepper (C. chinense Jacq.) production in Othon P. Blanco, Quintana Roo.
Methodology: The research was conducted in two stages: fieldwork and documentary analysis. During the
first stage, 42 questionnaires were administered through convenience sampling. Information was collected on
market agents and purchase and sale prices across the different commercialization channels. In the second stage,
the collected data were complemented and validated using secondary information sources. Commercialization
margins were subsequently estimated based on price differentials along the marketing chain.
Results: Three commercialization channels were identified. The primary channel, corresponding to the
international market, accounts for 70% of total production. The relative margins were 10.81%, 13.26%, 45.02%,
and 30.91% for producers, wholesale collectors, destination wholesalers, and international retailers, respectively.
The second channel, oriented toward the regional market, receives 25% of production. Relative margins were
estimated at 56.25%, 18.75%, and 25% for producers, local collectors, and local retailers, respectively. The third
channel, corresponding to the local market, accounts for 5% of production. In this channel, the relative margins
were 50%, 22.22%, and 27.78% for producers, origin wholesalers, and regional retailers, respectively.
Limitations of the Study: The principal limitations included regional insecurity, limited producer availability,
and the considerable distances between cultivation areas.
Conclusions: Within the primary commercialization channel, intermediaries capture the highest profit
margins while assuming comparatively lower investment risk. In contrast, producers face greater production
and market risks while obtaining lower profit margins. Export-oriented commercialization represents the
channel with the highest profitability for greenhouse-grown habanero pepper production.
Keywords: Habanero pepper, marketing channel, marketing margins

Baja California Sur, San Luis Potosí, and Sonora, contributing 501.85 (40.35%), 261.3
(21.01%), 186.6 (15.00%), 174 (13.99%), and 120 (9.65%) tons, respectively (SIAP, 2023).
The states comprising the Yucatán Peninsula, Yucatán, Quintana Roo, and Campeche
have held the Denomination of Origin (DO) for habanero pepper since 2010, thereby
certifying its authenticity and regional identity (FIRCO, 2017). In 2022, the principal
habanero pepper-producing municipalities in the state of Quintana Roo were José María
Morelos (63.08%), Felipe Carrillo Puerto (15.40%), Bacalar (10.64%), Lázaro Cárdenas
(7.03%), Othon P. Blanco (3.63%), and Puerto Morelos (0.22%). Together, they generated
a production volume of 2,066 tons at an average price of MXN $23,020 (SIAP, 2023).
 Production techniques for habanero pepper cultivation under low- and medium-
technology greenhouse conditions have been progressively developed. Under these
systems, average annual yields range from 70 to 120 t ha 1 (Macías et al., 2013). The
commercialization channels for habanero peppers can be classified into three categories

producers to wholesalers, commercialization through regional intermediaries, and
production intended for self-consumption (Ramírez, 2017). Intermediaries predominantly
control the commercialization chain for habanero peppers in the Yucatán Peninsula.
Consequently, the current distribution of economic value places medium- and low-

responsibilities and production costs. Habanero pepper cultivation requires continuous
phytosanitary monitoring and the replacement of conventional pesticides with
environmentally friendly alternatives in order to comply with the stringent standards
demanded by international markets (Zapata et al., 2020). Analyzing the regional and
international commercialization chain of habanero peppers produced in the municipality
of Othon P. Blanco enables identification of how value is distributed throughout the
supply chain and determination of which agents achieve the highest commercialization
margins. This analysis also facilitates the identification of the intermediaries that benefit
most substantially from the commercialization process. Therefore, this research aims
to identify the market agents involved and calculate the commercialization margins of
habanero pepper production in Othon P. Blanco, Quintana Roo. The hypothesis proposes
that intermediaries capture the largest commercialization margins and that, in general,
the greater the number of intermediaries in the marketing chain, the higher the final price
consumers pay.
MATERIALS AND METHODS
 The research was conducted in the municipality of Othon P. Blanco, Quintana Roo,
Mexico. A non-probabilistic convenience sampling approach was employed. This method
involves selecting participants from the target population based on their accessibility and
availability (Golzar et al., 2022).
 A total of 42 questionnaires were designed and administered to the agents involved in
the commercialization chain, including producers, collection agents, wholesalers, retailers,
and distributors.
 Commercialization margins were estimated using price-difference analysis, given
that habanero peppers are marketed as a fresh product that undergoes no industrial
transformation prior to consumption.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
 In August 2023, 12 producers were identified in Othon P. Blanco, Quintana Roo, each
operating 2 to 3 greenhouses, with an approximate area of 0.75 hectares per greenhouse.
These production units achieved an average annual yield of 50 t ha1, generating an average
income of MXN $2,500,000, equivalent to MXN $50 kg 1. Domestic and international
markets are supplied year-round through production systems operating under both open-
field and greenhouse conditions. Harvesting of greenhouse-grown peppers extends from
October to July. Between one and four harvests are carried out weekly, depending on market
demand and fruit maturity. Average weekly harvest volumes range from 1,200 to 1,300 kg
ha1, resulting in a total average yield of 48 to 52 tons throughout the entire harvesting
period. In contrast, open-field habanero pepper harvesting occurs from September to
March, with biweekly harvests averaging 1,200 kg per harvest and approximately 20 tons
per production cycle.

 Between December and March, nearly 90% of open-field production is harvested,
leading to saturation of the regional habanero pepper market. This oversupply generates
a significant decline in prices for both producers and consumers. Conversely, during
June, July, and August, producer prices for greenhouse-grown habanero peppers increase
substantially, as greenhouse production is the only available supply during this period.
Approximately 70% of the total greenhouse-grown pepper production is marketed as
high-quality fresh fruit for the international market. Pepper exports are secured through
contract farming agreements established between producers and collection agents.
Likewise, collection agents guarantee commercialization through formal agreements with
destination wholesalers. Approximately 25% of lower-grade peppers are marketed in the
regional market, while the remaining 5%, corresponding to fruits that failed to develop
fully, are processed into sauce products intended for the local market.
Description of Commercialization Agents in Othon P. Blanco
 Producers: Carry out greenhouse, harvesting annually from October to July, performing
between 1 and 4 harvests per week, depending on market demand and the fruit’s maturity
stage.
 Local Collection Agents: These agents concentrate approximately 5% of total production.
Peppers destined for local consumption are medium-sized fruits measuring less than 6 cm
and are packaged in 20 kg burlap sacks. The product is transported in non-refrigerated
pickup trucks to local retailer warehouses, restaurants, and hotels.
 Wholesale Collection Agents: These agents concentrate approximately 70% of the first-
grade or extra-quality fresh pepper production. The product is collected directly from
production units and transported in refrigerated vehicles. Collection routes are strategically
organized to maximize producer visits and gather the highest possible volume of peppers
for the agent’s packing facility. Once at the facility, the product undergoes an additional
selection process and is packed into 3.63 kg (8 lb) boxes for export purposes.
 Origin Wholesalers: These agents concentrate 25% of total production. Their warehouses
are located in the city of Chetumal, strategically positioned near the Lázaro Cardenas,
Ignacio Manuel Altamirano, and Andres Quintana Roo markets. This advantageous
location enables next-day distribution to retail clients, including fruit stores, hotels,
restaurants, taco establishments, and rotisserie chicken businesses.
 Destination Wholesalers: These are import companies with warehouses located near the
principal consumption area in Los Angeles, California, USA. They supply major retail
chains such as Walmart, supermarkets, Aldi, Mexican grocery stores, among others.
 Local Retailers: These are local commercial establishments distributed across
communities near the production units.
 Regional Retailers: These include small- and medium-sized enterprises such as fruit and
vegetable stores, hotels, restaurants, convenience stores, taco establishments, and rotisserie
chicken businesses located in the region’s principal cities.
 International Retailers: These comprise large self-service retail chains located in
Los Angeles, California, USA, including Walmart, Aldi, supermarkets, and similar
establishments.

Commercialization Channels of Habanero Pepper
 The principal commercialization channel identified was the one oriented toward the
international market (export), whereas the secondary channels were those directed toward
regional and local markets (Figure 1).

 The highest commercialization margin was obtained by destination wholesalers,
followed by retailers, wholesale collection agents, and producers. Producers, despite bearing
the greatest production and investment risks, received the lowest economic returns due
to the number of intermediaries involved in the commercialization process. Destination
wholesalers achieved the highest profit margins due to international commercialization
and favorable exchange rates. A substantial imbalance exists between the large number of
producers and the limited number of buyers, including collection agents and wholesalers.
Consequently, producers have little bargaining power and are often compelled to accept
low market prices. Given the highly perishable nature of habanero pepper, producers are
unable to postpone commercialization for extended periods in pursuit of more favorable
prices. This situation is further exacerbated by limited access to timely information
regarding market conditions and prevailing prices at the moment of sale. As a result, market
dynamics ultimately determine transaction prices. Moreover, most producers face severe
limitations in investing in adequate storage infrastructure, refrigeration systems, or product
transformation technologies. Additionally, production costs remain high and account for
a substantial share of the final selling price. In contrast, input prices do not necessarily
increase at the same rate as the product’s market price.
Commercialization Margins for Channel Two (Regional Market)
 Table 2 presents the commercialization margins for the regional channel. Producers
sold habanero pepper at MXN $45 kg 1, obtaining a relative margin of 56.25%. Local
collection agents sold the product at MXN $60 kg 1, generating an absolute margin of
MXN $15 kg1, equivalent to 18.75%. Considering a final consumer price of MXN $80
kg1, retailers obtained an absolute margin of MXN $20 kg1, representing 25%. Relative
margins were calculated with respect to the final consumer price. The highest margin
within this channel was obtained by producers, followed by retailers and local collection
agents. Producers represented the most favored agents in this commercialization channel,
obtaining a relative margin of 56.25%, primarily because production costs were not included
in the analysis. In contrast, the margins obtained by collection agents and retailers were
18.75% and 25%, respectively. These margins are considered comparatively low because
commercialization occurs within a regional context in which production and consumption
centers are located at relatively short distances compared with those corresponding to the
primary commercialization channel.
Commercialization Margins for Channel Three (Local Market)
 Table 3 presents the commercialization margins for the local market channel.
Producers sold habanero pepper at MXN $45 kg1, obtaining a relative margin of 50%.
Origin wholesalers sold the product at MXN $65 kg 1, generating an absolute margin
of MXN $20 kg1, equivalent to 22.22%. Considering a final consumer price of MXN
$90 kg1, retailers obtained an absolute margin of MXN $25 kg1, representing 27.78%.
Relative margins were calculated with respect to the final consumer price. Within this
commercialization channel, producers were favored agents, as direct sales to wholesalers
enabled them to secure better prices. Origin wholesalers operate with large product

CONCLUSIONS
 Intermediaries achieve the highest commercialization margins in the primary habanero
pepper marketing channel. These agents obtain the greatest economic benefits, primarily
due to access to export-quality products and privileged information regarding prices across
different markets and commercialization periods. Within this channel, producers represent
the agents with the lowest profit margins despite assuming the highest production and
investment risks. Their limited bargaining power, dependence on intermediaries, and
exposure to highly volatile markets frequently compel them to accept unfavorable prices.
Destination wholesalers achieve the highest profit margins by participating in international
markets and benefiting from favorable exchange rate dynamics.

margins because the products marketed through these channels are generally of lower
quality and pass through fewer intermediaries, thereby reducing intermediation and storage
