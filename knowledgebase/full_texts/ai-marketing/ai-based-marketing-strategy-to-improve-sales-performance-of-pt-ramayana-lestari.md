Abstract: PT Ramayana Lestari Sentosa Tbk (RALS), one of Indonesia's oldest
department store retailers, has experienced a consistent decline in sales
performance and net profit in recent years, whereas comparable competitors have
demonstrated financial growth over the same timeframe. This study formulates an
Artificial Intelligence (AI) -based marketing strategy framework for RALS by
integrating the Technology -Organization-Environment (TOE) model to assess
organizational technology adoption readiness with the Generati ve AI
Recombination-Targeting-Iteration (RTI) framework to structure its strategic
deployment. Employing a descriptive qualitative approach, the analysis
synthesizes secondary document coverage encompassing annual reports, Public
Expose presentations spanning 2023–2026, and financial disclosures through a five-
stage diagnostic and strategic mapping process. The findings reveal that RALS's
readiness across the TOE dimensions is asymmetrical: the Technology and
Organization dimensions remain at a moderate lev el, whereas competitive and
macroeconomic pressures within the Environment dimension are critically high.
Consequently, a phased Gen-AI RTI marketing strategy is formulated across three
core pillars: (1) recombination, integrating fragmented legacy data as sets (Point of
Sale, mobile membership, transaction records) with Generative AI capabilities via a
cost-effective API gateway architecture; (2) targeting, prioritizing high -ROI
operational use cases across inventory forecasting, customer segmentation, and
collaborative Sales & Operations Planning; and (3) iteration, embedding continuous
model calibration driven by structured human -in-the-loop operational feedback
mechanisms. This research provides an adaptable implementation roadmap
aligned with RALS's fisc al and workforce constraints, enriching the empirical
discourse surrounding generative technology diffusion within conventional
department store retail ecosystems in emerging markets.

Methodology
This study employs a descriptive qualitative approach focused on PT Ramayana
Lestari Sentosa Tbk. The data utilized originates from secondary sources, namely the 2025
Annual Report of PT Ramayana Lestari Sentosa Tbk (2026)  RALS Public Expose materials
spanning the 2023 –2026 period, official publications from strategic partners (SPAR
International, n.d.) , financial statements, business reporting from reputable outlets (CNBC,
2025; IDN, 2026; IDX, 2026; PT Ramayana Lestari Sentosa Tbk, 2026; SWA, 2026), as well as
competitor benchmark documents (ETP, 2022)  . The analytical process was conducted
through five interrelated stages as follows:
1. Stage 1 – Identification & Diagnosis: A literature review of the TOE and Gen -AI RTI
frameworks, alongside a mapping of Ramayana’s business bottlenecks across inventory,
competition, and digitalization.
2. Stage 2 – Contextual Data Collection: A chronological reconstruction of Ramayana’s
technology transformation and digitalization milestones over the past decade based on
annual reports and supporting documentation, directly mapped to the relevant TOE
dimensions.
3. Stage 3 – TOE Readiness Analysis: An evaluation of technological readiness (data
readiness, infrastructure), organizational readiness (human resources, budget, culture),
and environmental factors (competition, market dynamics, regulatory landscape).
4. Stage 4 – Gen-AI RTI Strategy Formulation: The formulation of recombination
(integrating existing enterprise data with Gen -AI), targeting (identifying high -ROI use
cases), and iteration strategies (establishing continuous learning and feedback
mechanisms).
5. Stage 5 – Synthesis & Roadmap Development: The construction of an As -Is vs. To -Be
matrix and a phased strategic implementation roadmap.

Result and Discussion
Identification of Ramayana's Business Bottlenecks
The diagnostic mapping of business challenges was conducted by analyzing the
company's financial statements, the 2025 Public Expose presentation, and relevant
supplementary documentation. The findings delineate three primary operational
dimensions: inventory, competition, and digitalization.
1. Inventory Dimension: The challenges in inventory management center on aligning
product availability with fluctuating sales volumes and a restructuring store network.
Ramayana’s net inventory as of March 2025 stood at IDR 409.62 billion, representing a
14.4% contraction from IDR 478.45 billion in December 2024. Concurrently, the store

locations following six closures, before rebounding to 96 outlets in March 2025 with the
inauguration of a new store in Palu. The core bottleneck mapped within this dimension
is the synchronization of localized stock levels and SKU availability with shifting store -
level consumer demand.
2. Competition Dimension: Ramayana faces mounting headwinds stemming from
constrained consumer purchasing power, shifts in shopping behaviors, macroeconomic
volatility, and widespread retail -sector workforce restructuring. However, these
macroeconomic factors do not fully account for performance disparities across the sector,
as demonstrated by peer benchmarks. The primary vulnerability identified here is the
erosion of Ramayana's competitive positioning and operating performance amidst
shifting market conditions and intensified peer rivalry.
3. Digitalization Dimension: Ramayana possesses several baseline digital assets, including
Point of Sale (POS) systems, a mobile loyalty application, and transactional databases.
The central challenge is therefore not the absence of digital tools, but the depth to which
existing data and technologies are integrated into commercial workflows. The 2025
Public Expose primarily emphasized conventional retail measures (such as re -
merchandising, store space rationalization, social media promotions, cost containment,
and outlet refurbishment), leaving the strategic deployment of data and digital
technologies for marketing and executive decision-making largely unaddressed. The core
issue identified in this dimension is the structural gap between existing enterprise dat a
assets and their practical utilization across operational processes.

Longitudinal Reconstruction of Digital Transformation (2014–2025)
To establish analytical rigor, Ramayana's digital and operational initiatives across the
past decade are mapped to their corresponding TOE structural dimensions (Baker, 2012;
Tornatzky & Fleischer, 1990).

Strategic Master Licensing with SPAR International — Organizational Dimension
In 2014, Ramayana entered a master licensing partnership with SPAR International
to modernize its supermarket operations (Matahari Department Store, 2025 ). SPAR
provided operational standardization, specialized visual merchandising, localized retail
marketing toolkits, and structured personnel training programs(SPAR International, n.d.) .
Under the TOE model, this milestone represented an Organizational capability upgrade. It
prioritized retail managerial transfer over algorithmic technology adoption, reflecting the
thesis of (Reinartz et al., 2019 )  that traditional retail transformation mandates
organizational alignment prior to technological capitalization. Simultaneously, Ramayana
introduced the Ramayana Member Loyalty Card, marking an initial touchpoint with the
Technological dimension by creating the foundation for a centralized consumer database.
a. Digital Wallet Infrastructure Integration (2019) — Technological & Environmental
Dimensions
In 2019, Ramayana integrated third -party mobile digital wallets (OVO, GoPay, DANA)
across its cash registers nationwide (PT Ramayana Lestari Sentosa Tbk, 2026). This
directly addressed the Environmental shift toward cashless mobile payments while
modernizing the Technological front -end checkout infrastructure. In Hagberg et al.’s
(2016) retail digitalization taxonomy, this intervention is classified under the exchange’s
component, reflecting the earliest layer of operational digital adoption in legacy retail.
b. Third-Party Marketplace Omnichannel Expansion (2020) — Technological &
Environmental Dimensions
Confronted by pandemic restrictions in 2020, Ramayana established official brand
storefronts across major e -commerce marketplaces, including Shopee, Tokopedia,
Lazada, Blibli, and JD.ID (PT Ramayana Lestari Sentosa Tbk, 2026)  . This step signaled a
transition from pure physical store distribution to a basic multi -channel operational
posture (Verhoef et al., 2015 ). Verhoef et al., (2015)  emphasized that sustainable
omnichannel retail requires seamless cross -channel data and inventory integration.
Because Ramayana’s deployment was limited to external marketplace seller accounts
rather than an integrated proprietary platform, it represented a multi -channel sales
distribution tactic rather than an integrated omnichannel ecosystem. In the TOE model,
this represented an Environmental reaction to restricted store mobility, achieved by
leveraging external third-party Technological infrastructure.
c. Digital Marketing Re -alignment and Fintech Alliances (2025) — Technological,
Organizational, & Environmental Dimensions

TOE Readiness Analysis
Technological Readiness: Ramayana’s observable infrastructure consists of
functional POS registers and a proprietary mobile membership application (Ramayana
Member Card) offering reward tracking, merchant promotional access, digital catalogs, and
reward po int redemptions. By contrast, direct rival Matahari established an enterprise
partnership with global retail tech platform ETP Group to consolidate its omnichannel retail
architecture, unify physical and virtual shopping touchpoints, and institutionalize
algorithmic customer retention tools  (ETP, 2022)  . Peer analyses underscore that while
competitors such as PT Mitra Adiperkasa Tbk (MAPI) protected operating margins through
integrated omnichannel architecture and premium brand equity, RALS faced depressed
foot traffic exacerbated by unintegrated digital  assets(Untag Surabaya, 2025) . As noted in
predictive analytics literature, baseline data hygiene, codifiability, and accessibility govern
the failure or success of AI integration  (Hussain Z, 2024). As a result, Ramayana's internal
Technological readiness is evaluated as Moderate.
Organizational Readiness: Ramayana's organizational profile contains valuable
foundational competencies gained through its long -term partnership with SPAR
International (SPAR International, n.d.)   which provided operational frameworks that
should theoretically accelerate AI assimilation (Reinartz et al., 2019 ) . However, recent
financial distress has strained this organizational baseline. In fiscal year 2025, RALS
undertook major labor rationalizations, reducing its workforce by 427 employees (from
3,395 down to 2,968 active personnel) alongside four store closu res (Boli A.B., 2026). These
workforce adjustments sparked labor friction, highlighted by union advocacy from ASPEK
Indonesia regarding workforce separations. This workforce restructuring coincided with
sustained profit contractions: FY 2025 net profit retreated to IDR 265.29 billion from IDR

Simultaneously, the broader retail market presents bifurcated conditions: while low-
income consumer spending remains constrained by currency volatility and commodity
price pressures (Ashari, 2026) , Indonesia's overall e -commerce ecosystem grew by 18% in
volume and 21% in gross merchandise value throughout early 2026 . From a regulatory
standpoint, deploying customer data analytics requires strict legal compliance. Indonesia’s

and tightening statutory compliance.

Formulation of the Generative AI RTI Strategy
Drawing upon the diagnostic mapping, digital transformation trajectory, and the
TOE readiness assessment, an operational Generative AI deployment strategy is structured
using the Recombination, Targeting, and Iteration (RTI) framework. This methodology is
selected for its capital -efficient implementation profile, catering to RALS's current
budgetary parameters.

Scalable Recombination: Integrating Legacy Data Assets with Gen-AI
Scalable recombination denotes the synthesis of existing organizational data and
institutional knowledge with generative artificial intelligence capabilities (Shonubi, 2026) .
Its execution depends upon knowledge codifiability the organization's capacity to transform
disparate data records into machine -readable structures. Ramayana’s core transactional

assets, POS logs, mobile loyalty profiles, payment records, e -commerce transactions, and
internal RTools metrics represent vital data repositories that currently operate in functional
silos. Rather than undertaking a costly end -to-end data lake overhaul, R amayana can
integrate these sources through an API Gateway architecture.
This middleware approach enables real-time data exchange without disrupting core
transactional platforms, allowing for a phased, cost-controlled deployment. Structured data
is subsequently exposed to foundational Gen-AI models via function-calling APIs, enabling
the system to retrieve records on demand, synthesize cross -departmental trends, and
generate strategic recommendations. This establishes two operational tiers: (1) descriptive
analytics providing automated historical dashboards across outlets, and ( 2) predictive
analytics delivering store-level SKU replenishment projections calibrated against localized
sales cycles and cash-flow parameters.

Precision Targeting: Deploying High-ROI
The Gen -AI RTI framework concentrates analytical resources on the three high -
impact retail domains identified in Section 4.1, with a strategic emphasis on offerings
(tailored merchandising based on customer behavior) and settings (store environments
calibrated to local demand):
1. Inventory Management: Deploying predictive demand forecasting models that combine
historical sales logs, regional seasonality, and store inventory balances to estimate SKU
requirements per location. This replaces uniform nationwide allocation with localize d
inventory optimization, reducing overstock carrying costs, stockout -driven revenue
loss, and replenishment lag.
2. Competitive Positioning: Implementing customer analytics that harmonize transaction
histories, profile demographics, and promotional redemptions to power hyper -
personalized loyalty campaigns. Simultaneously, integrated Sales & Operations
Planning (S&OP) aligns demand signals, warehouse availability, and treasury limits into
coordinated execution plans, improving operational agility and customer retention.
3. Digitalization: Transforming enterprise data from passive historical logging into active
analytical infrastructure, transitioning operational and merchandising planning from
intuition-driven methods to automated, data-supported frameworks.
Iterative Improvement: Establishing Continuous Learning and Feedback Loops
The final component focuses on systematic algorithmic calibration driven by
structured human -in-the-loop operational validation. This continuous improvement
architecture operates across four cyclical steps: (1) generating initial automated
merchandising an d promotional recommendations; (2) managerial evaluation and
operational execution; (3) logging performance variances (such as sales realization,
customer redemptions, and allocation accuracy); and (4) feeding variance logs back into the
model to recalibrate operational weights and parameters for subsequent business cycles. In
demand forecasting, the model evaluates forecast error against actual point-of-sale data; in

and in S&OP, it benchmarks budget execution against store performance. This ensures that

Discussion
The empirical and strategic findings of this research validate model  (Cao, 2021)  ,
which demonstrates that retail AI applications generate the greatest enterprise value when
embedded throughout foundational merchandising operations rather than limited to
superficial, customer-facing interfaces. Ramayana’s recent history characterized by  front-
end promotional experiments alongside legacy back -end spreadsheet tracking reflects a
documented structural challenge: an operational disconnect between front -end digital
adoption and tangible enterprise earnings (Haque et al., 2024).
This study also illustrates how the Gen -AI RTI framework (Shonubi, 2026)  works
effectively alongside the traditional TOE model. While the TOE framework provides a
structured evaluation of enterprise adoption readiness, the RTI approach establishes an
operational framework for recombining, targeting, and refining data capabilit ies. This
paired methodology is especially useful for legacy department store operators in emerging
markets that need to modernize under tighter capital constraints than well -funded global
retail groups.
Research Limitations: A key limitation of this study is its reliance on public corporate
disclosures, investor materials, audited annual statements, and external market reporting,
without direct access to internal IT metrics or employee surveys. Specific i nternal
parameters such as the code architecture of internal mobile apps (RTools), specific server
configurations, and internal staff readiness could not be verified firsthand through primary
internal audits.
Conclusion
This study developed an applied AI -driven marketing strategy framework for PT
Ramayana Lestari Sentosa Tbk by integrating the Technology -Organization-Environment
(TOE) diagnostic model with the Generative AI Recombination -Targeting-Iteration (RTI)
framework. The analysis shows that RALS exhibits an unbalanced organizational readiness

profile: Technological and Organizational readiness remain moderate supported by
standard POS systems, a growing mobile loyalty base, and retail operational history, but
limited by constrained analytical tooling, recent workforce reductions, and tight margins.
In contrast, Environmental pressures are high, driven by more digitally advanced
competitors (Matahari, MAPI), evolving consumer shopping habits, and formal personal
data privacy laws.
To address this dynamic, an applied Gen -AI RTI marketing strategy was designed.
It centers on: (1) connecting existing data silos (POS, membership, transactions) using a low-
cost, secure API Gateway middleware architecture (recombination); (2) targeting pr oven,
high-impact retail use cases: SKU -level demand forecasting, automated customer loyalty
segmentation using RFM metrics, and coordinated S&OP merchandise planning (targeting);
and (3) implementing continuous model refinement via structured human -in-the-loop
