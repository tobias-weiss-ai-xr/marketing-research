Abstract
Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) is rapidly reshaping how banks design, produce, and personalize
marketing communications. This paper examines the adopti on, applications, and strategic implications of GenAI
in banking marketing, drawing on industry market -sizing data, adoption surveys, and documented case studies
from global and regional banks. The global GenAI -in-banking market is estimated to grow from r oughly $1.4
billion in 2025 to over $4 billion by 2030, reflecting a compound annual growth rate above 23%. Within this trend,
marketing has emerged as one of the most visible use -case categories, spanning hyper -personalized offers,
conversational chatbots , automated content generation, and AI -optimized campaigns. The paper analyses
quantitative adoption data across banking functions, evaluates consumer sentiment toward AI -generated content
and personalization, and presents illustrative case studies includi ng Wells Fargo, Bradesco, HDFC Bank, and Axis
Bank. It further discusses the operational benefits — cost reduction, speed, and precision — alongside key risks
such as data privacy, regulatory compliance, model hallucination, and brand -trust erosion. The pa per concludes
with strategic recommendations for banks seeking to responsibly scale generative AI within their marketing
function.

Keywords: Generative AI, Large Language Models, Banking Marketing, Personalization, Customer Engagement,
Chatbots, Financial Services.

Introduction
Marketing in the banking sector has historically relied on broad segmentation, mass communication, and
relatively static product messaging. Over the last decade, digital transformation and the proliferation of customer

           September 2026                                IJCMTH                               DOI :  10.67851/ijcmth.vol.1.issue.2.051                                       Page 2
data have pu shed banks toward data -driven personalization. The emergence of Generative Artificial Intelligence
— large language models (LLMs) and diffusion -based image/video generators capable of producing human -like
text, visuals, and conversation — has accelerated this shift dramatically.
Unlike earlier predictive -AI systems that primarily scored or classified customers, generative models can create
new content: emails, advertisements, chat responses, product descriptions, and even personalized financial
narratives. For banks, this capability intersects directly with marketing, customer experience, and compliance
functions, making it one of the most consequential technology shifts of the current decade.
This paper investigates how generative AI is being adopted within  banking marketing specifically. It
combines secondary market data, adoption statistics, and documented industry case studies to answer three
questions: (1) What are the dominant use cases of GenAI in bank marketing? (2) What measurable adoption and
impact trends exist? (3) What risks and strategic considerations should guide responsible adoption?
Objectives of the Study
1. To examine the scale and growth trajectory of generative AI adoption in the banking sector.
2. To identify and categorize the principal generative AI use cases within bank marketing functions.
3. To analyse available data on customer sentiment, adoption rates, and functional deployment.
4. To evaluate the benefits, risks, and regulatory considerations associated with GenAI -driven marketing.
5. To propose strategic recommendations for banks planning or scaling GenAI marketing initiatives.

Literature Review
1. Kshetri et al. (2024) examined the applications, opportunities and challenges of Generative AI in marketing.
The study highlighted content cre ation, personalization and customer engagement as important marketing
applications, while also identifying ethical, organizational and implementation challenges. The study provides a
foundation for understanding how GenAI is transforming conventional marke ting practices.

2. Dwivedi et al. (2023) examined the organizational implications of ChatGPT and Generative AI across different
sectors. The study identified significant opportunities for productivity, communication and automation, while
emphasizing concerns related to misinformation, privacy, bias and responsible implementation. These findings are
particularly relevant to banking, where accuracy and trust are critical.

3. Moharrak and Mogaji (2025) investigated Generative AI adoption in the banking secto r using qualitative
interviews with bank managers and industry experts. The study emphasized managerial preparedness, regulatory
compliance, data privacy and operational challenges. It demonstrated that successful GenAI adoption requires a
balance between technological innovation and responsible governance.

4. Panda et al. (2026) conducted a systematic literature review of 134 studies on GenAI and consumer behaviour.
The study found that personalization, consumer decision -making, trust and technology adopt ion are major themes
in the emerging GenAI literature. It also highlighted the importance of transparency and ethical use in developing

Research Gap
Taken together, the reviewed literature suggests three things. First, generative AI's marketing -relevant
capabilities — personalization, content generation, and conversational engagement — are consistently identified
as high-value use cases across both aca demic and industry sources. Second, empirical evidence specific to banking
marketing, as distinct from banking generally or marketing generally, remains limited, with the most rigorous
available studies favoring qualitative interview -based methods (e.g., M oharrak & Mogaji, 2025) or conceptual and
bibliometric frameworks (e.g., Ravichandran & Bick, 2026) rather than quantitative, cross -source synthesis. Third,
few existing works integrate market -sizing data, adoption statistics, consumer sentiment data, and documented
case studies into a single analytical account. This paper addresses that gap by combining quantitative market and

Research Methodology
The study ad opts a descriptive research design based on secondary data to examine the adoption,
applications and strategic implications of Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) in banking marketing. The
study focuses specifically on the use of GenAI for personali zation, content generation, customer engagement and
conversational marketing.
Sources of Data:
Secondary data were collected from peer -reviewed research articles, industry reports, market research
publications, banking -industry reports and published case studies. Major sources include Research and Markets,
The Business Research Company, CoinLaw, Sta tista, Capgemini Research Institute, Celent/Zest AI, Bain &
Company, Salesforce and NiCE, along with relevant academic literature.
Data Analysis:
The collected information was organized and analysed using descriptive analysis, comparative analysis and
trend analysis. Market -size data and GenAI adoption rates were compared across years, while consumer -sentiment
indicators were examined to understand acceptance of AI -driven banking marketing. Published banking case
studies were also reviewed to identify pract ical applications and benefits.

Data Analysis and Findings
Market Growth Trajectory
The global generative AI in banking market was valued at approximately $1.43 billion in 2025 and is
projected to reach $1.77 billion in 2026, growing at a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of roughly 23.7%.
Extending this trajectory through the CAGR reported for the 2026 –2030 period (approximately 23.3%) implies the
market will surpass $4.09 billion by 2030 — nearly a threefold increase in five years. This growth is attrib uted to
expanding digital banking adoption, rising demand for hyper -personalized services, and increasing investment in
AI infrastructure.

Functional Distribution of AI Adoption
AI adoption is not distributed evenly across banking functions. Fraud detection and prevention lead
adoption by a wide margin, reported at 87% of global financial institutions, reflecting the maturity and risk -
reduction value o f that use case. Marketing -adjacent functions — sales and administration — currently report
comparatively lower direct adoption (around 18%), suggesting meaningful headroom for growth as generative
tools mature and governance frameworks catch up. Trading ( 33%), credit approval, and KYC/AML (28% each)
occupy the middle of the adoption curve.

Findings
1. Generative AI adoption in banking is growing rapidly, with the market projected to exceed $4 billion by
2030.
2. Financial-sector GenAI adoption increased from 52% in 2024 to 61% in 2025.
3. Personalization, content generation and chatbots are key GenAI applications in banking marketing.
4. 72% of consumers perceive personalized offers as more valuable.
5. 73% of consumers trust GenAI -generated content, indicating growing acc eptance.
6. Marketing-related AI adoption remains relatively low, showing significant scope for future growth.
7. GenAI can improve marketing speed, cost efficiency, customer engagement and campaign precision.
8. Major concerns include data privacy, regulatory comp liance, hallucination, bias and customer trust.

Conclusion
Generative AI has significant potential to transform banking marketing through personalization, automated
content creation and improved customer engagement. However, successful adoption requires strong governance,
data protection, regulatory compliance and human supervision. Banks that balance AI innovation with customer
trust and responsible implementation can achieve greater marketing efficiency and competitive advantage.

Scope for future study
Future studies can explore customer acceptance, satisfaction and loyalty towards GenAI -based banking
marketing and compare adoption between public and private sector banks. Further research may examine the
impact of GenAI on marketing performance, ROI, employee readiness, privacy, ethics and regulatory compliance,
as well as its long-term influence on banking marketing strategies.
