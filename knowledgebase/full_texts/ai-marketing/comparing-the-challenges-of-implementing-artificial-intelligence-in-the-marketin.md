Abstract
At present, the structure and dynamics of the marketing technology (MarTech) market are
shaped by the development of artificial intelligence (AI). This paper is the first to present a cross-
country comparison of MarTech markets. It aims to identify experiences in AI implementation,
determine prospects for market development and show that the adoption of AI in corporate
marketing varies among user segments... As this approach has not been discussed in Russian
scholarly literature, the segmentation is carried out using evidence from participants in the
Russian MarTech market.
The article includes a literature review, a comparison of the Chinese and Russian MarTech
markets, an analysis of user segments, and an investigation of AI adoption in corporate
marketing. The literature section is organized according to the impact of AI on marketing
technologies within different market segments. The comparison of marketing technologies
markets is based on the analysis of search queries by internet users in Russia and China, across
different product segments of MarTech. The data for Russia were obtained using the Yandex
Wordstat service, while the data for China were collected from the Baidu search engine and the
5118 platform.

Introduction
The rapid development of the marketing technology (MarTech) market is evidenced
by its exponential growth. According to Research and Markets (2025), the global
MarTech market is projected to expand from USD 617.4 billion in 2025 to USD 2.24
trillion by 2032, corresponding to a compound annual growth rate of 20.28%. The State
of Martech 2025 report indicates that the total number of solutions reached 15,384
tools in 2025, with 77% of the growth observed in 2024 driven by AI-based products
(Brinker, 2025).
There are various approaches to integrating AI with marketing technologies.
Companies show a preference for a hybrid approach to AI adoption: 60% combine
standalone AI tools with built-in AI functionalities, 23% rely exclusively on newly
developed AI solutions, and 17% integrate AI capabilities into their existing systems
(Brinker & Riemersma, 2024). The year 2025 marked significant progress in agent-
based AI, capable of autonomously performing complex tasks. According to McKinsey,
mentions of job vacancies related to agent-based AI increased by 985% between 2023
and 2024; investments in this field totaled USD 1.1 billion in 2024 (Yee et al., 2025).
Modern marketers are actively integrating AI into various aspects of their
marketing activities, which is reflected in the segmented structure of the MarTech
market. According to the classification proposed by Scott Brinker (Brinker, 2016), six
MarTech market segments can be distinguished. AI impacts all segments, affecting
both the creation and delivery of consumer value as well as the organization and

As shown in Table 1, the literature considers AI to be a key driver of the
development of modern marketing technologies across all market segments. Chinese
studies primarily explore this process through applied AI marketing domains such as
user insight identification, content management, interactive placement and content
performance monitoring, and creation of short-form videos (Guowei et al., 2021; Ren
et al., 2022; He, 2022; Shen et al., 2025). However, in the Chinese literature (with the
exception of the MarketingLearn.com, 2024; 2025), the full spectrum of marketing
technologies is not treated as a segmented market structure. In Russian literature, there
is a shift towards the digitalization of market interactions, barriers to the adoption of
technology and AI, and the role of AI in managing customer experience (Chernova,
2021; Gerasimenko et al., 2023; Suray & Teplaya, 2024). The DataInsight study (2025)
emphasizes technologies and AI in Russian e-commerce. Slepenkova and Zhu (2025)
analyze the structure of the Russian MarTech market based on Brinker’s segmentation
methodology. However, the study is limited to the Russian market and does not
provide a comparison with other national MarTech markets.
Thus, the focus in Chinese and Russian scholarly literature is either on individual
AI marketing functions or on marketing digitalization and barriers to AI adoption.
However, a comparative analysis of the structure of national MarTech markets from
the perspective of product segments has yet to be conducted. This gap highlights
the importance of the present study, given that the development of MarTech market
segments is currently being driven by AI. Russian and Chinese literature emphasizes
marketing technologies from various MarTech market segments. This leads to the
following hypothesis:
H1: The structure of the Russian and Chinese marketing technology markets differs across
product segments.
The existing literature more often explains companies’ need for AI-based
marketing technologies through organizational conditions for technology adoption.
Studies have shown that the implementation and use of these technologies depend
on digital culture, employee competencies, data readiness and IT infrastructure (Mou,
2025; Malthouse & Copulsky, 2022).
Another group of studies links the use of AI to company size, resources and the
level of AI adoption, highlighting financial and organizational barriers faced by small
firms (Ayinaddis, 2025; Pérez-Campdesuñer et al., 2025; Dong et al., 2023). However,
these studies primarily consider AI adoption in general, without focusing specifically
on the application of AI in corporate marketing. On the other hand, Chen et al. (2022)
and Hossain et al. (2022) demonstrate that the effectiveness of AI in marketing depends
on the intensity of its adoption and the maturity of marketing analytics. Nevertheless,
the influence of company size and prior AI experience on firms’ need to adopt AI in
marketing technologies remains insufficiently explored.

2. Comparison of MarTech markets in China and Russia
2.1. Market comparison methodology
The importance and authority of the MarTechMap crowdsourced database, which
is based on the division of the marketing technology market into six product
segments according to S. Brinker, is universally recognized. However, the scope of
the map excludes the Chinese and Russian markets, for which no crowdsourced
map is currently available. Additionally, there is a notable absence of independent
annual reviews that aggregate supplier data by MarTech segment. Consequently, a
direct comparison of the MarTech markets is an arduous task, and to the best of the
authors’ knowledge, it has never been done. The research model employed by the
authors in the second section of this paper is grounded in practical and theoretical
approaches presented in contemporary sources. For instance, the Gartner website
contains a paragraph in the “Methodology” section titled “How Forecasts Work.” This
paragraph indicates that one of the factors for forecasting the IT market (including the
Software and Services segments) is the analysis of internet user queries. Therefore,
the authors of the present article, lacking data on the MarTech markets in Russia and
China, used this approach to obtain indicators of market structure. These indicators
can also be used to predict the near future, as shown in research literature (Mao et al.,
2011; Bulczak, 2021).

3. AI Adoption in different user segments
3.1. Research design and data collection
In Chinese scientific literature, user segments in the MarTech market are categorized by
industry and functional characteristics. The existence of such segments in the Russian
MarTech market has not yet been studied. In this paper, the authors address this issue
in the context of AI implementation for marketing purposes by Russian companies. In
the third section of the paper, the authors relied on the research model applied by S.
Brinker et al. during the surveys conducted in December 2024 and the first quarter of
2025 on AI adoption in company marketing stacks for MarTech market segmentation
(Brinker, 2025)).
This study uses a questionnaire developed by the authors of this article to analyze
the Russian MarTech market. The questionnaire has a specific structure and logic.
Following a screening question (Q1) about the use of marketing services within
company activities, the first section of the questionnaire begins with ‘Experience in
Using Marketing Technologies’. Respondents are then asked to select the MarTech
market segment and sub-segment to which they believe their technologies belong.
Questions Q9–Q11 cover the preferred number of functions, the pricing model
for services and problems encountered when implementing and using marketing
technologies. The second section (Q12–Q16) of the questionnaire is devoted to the use
of AI in company marketing. The third section, ‘ About Your Company’ (Q17–Q20),
collects information about the company and the person completing the questionnaire.
To study and segment consumers in the Russian MarTech market, a survey of
Russian users of marketing services was conducted from September to November
2025. Responses were collected from 407 participants. The following methods were
used to obtain respondents’ answers:
1. Direct distribution of the questionnaire to respondents;
2. Sharing the survey link within the professional community Telegram Data
Practicum Chat;
3. Distribution of the questionnaire through the Russian survey platform
Anketolog, used for survey creation, administration and analysis;
4. Formal face-to-face questionnaire interviews at conferences: 1) MarTech Spring,
AdTech Talks ’24; 2) Marketing Data Analytics 2025.
Sample characteristics. To confirm the use of MarTech, a screening question was
included at the beginning of the questionnaire: “Do you use marketing technologies
(MarTech) in your work for the company?” Out of 519 respondents, 407 confirmed
that they use marketing services. The respondent companies varied in size: 22.1% were
small enterprises with 1–50 employees, 30.0% were medium-sized enterprises with 51–
200 employees, and 47.9% were large enterprises with 201 or more employees. In terms
of professional roles, the sample included technical and marketing specialists (26.0%),
content specialists (16.2%), advertising specialists (9.6%), managers (31.2%), individual

Conclusions
The study showed that, in contemporary research literature, AI is considered a key
factor in transforming marketing technologies across all MarTech market segments
and subsegments. The size and dynamics of these segments are determined by the
extent to which companies adopt AI for marketing purposes.
This article is the first to attempt a comparison of the structure of the MarTech
markets in Russia and China across six product segments, based on S. Brinker’s
methodology and in line with global practice. Monitoring of digital traces has shown
that users in China demonstrate a stronger interest in data management, whereas
queries in Russia are more focused on communication with customers and promotional
practices. Chinese users are predominantly attracted to issues of business analytics,
including marketing effectiveness and customer data analysis. The prevailing interests
of Russian users pertain to the management of customer relationships through review
management services, loyalty programmes, CRM systems and recommendation
systems. In these market subsegments, AI adoption in both countries is expected to
proceed at an accelerated pace.
The obtained data confirm the hypothesis of structural differences between the
Chinese and Russian MarTech markets, broken down by segment. These differences
reflect market-specific demands and different AI technology adoption trajectories.
Russian software providers should pay more attention to implementing AI in data
management, while Chinese marketers should consider the Russian experience in
developing customer relationships using AI.
A survey of 407 companies using marketing technologies in the Russian market
identified three distinct segments with respect to the process of AI adoption in
company marketing. Segment 1 consists of large companies with more than two
years’ experience of using marketing technologies, who employ AI in marketing
approximately three times per week. These companies primarily require a high degree
of solution customization, compatibility with comprehensive IT infrastructures, and
compliance with data security and regulatory requirements. Segment 2 comprises
small and medium-sized enterprises that are advanced in their use of AI. These
companies require effective, flexible solutions that are user-friendly and provide quick
returns. Segment 3 comprises small companies lagging in AI usage. These companies
need simple and convenient marketing tools with maximum ease of use to avoid
additional investment in staff training.
The findings appear to be relevant both theoretically and practically for researchers
and practitioners in China and Russia.
