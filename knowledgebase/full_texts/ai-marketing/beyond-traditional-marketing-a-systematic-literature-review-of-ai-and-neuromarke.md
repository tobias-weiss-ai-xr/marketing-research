ABSTRACT
This study explores the integration of artificial intelligence (AI) and neuromarketing in consumer
behavior research. By combining AI-driven analytics with neuromarketing techniques, it aims to improve
marketing precision and deepen understanding of consumer decision-making. A systematic literature
review was conducted, in accordance with PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews
and Meta-Analyses) guidelines, analyzing 43 peer-reviewed articles published between 2015 and 2024,
following a defined elimination process. Articles were retrieved from Scopus using predefined keywords.
The content analysis focused on four dimensions: thematic areas and research topics, theoretical
foundations, research design, and methodological patterns. The findings reveal a marked increase in
publications after 2018, with a notable peak in 2024 at 32.56%. Three dominant themes were identified:
neuromarketing techniques in consumer behavior (44.2%), AI applications in marketing and engagement
(27.9%), and ethical concerns in AI-driven research (27.9%). While psychological and cognitive theories
are frequently used, most studies rely on primary data. The growing use of EEG, machine learning, and
sentiment analysis signals a methodological shift. This review addresses a gap in the literature by mapping
the contribution of AI to neuromarketing. It provides a structured agenda for future research, illustrating
how AI can enhance traditional methods and generate new insights into consumer behavior.

1. INTRODUCTION
Consumer behavior and decision-making processes topics have always been questioned
areas in both the academic and industrial worlds. Improvements in data access and radical changes
in technology help answer this question by gathering consumer insights. Thus, strategic decisions
can be made in a more accurate way and based on real consumer expectations. Thanks to new
techniques and approaches in this area, brands now have the opportunity to reach consumers in a
much more effective and personalized way. The i ntegration of artificial intelligence and
neuromarketing is a topic of great interest to both academic circles and practitioners. However,
the theoretical and methodological foundations of this integration are not yet holistic and are
scattered. The intersection of these two disciplines creates a new paradigm in marketing research,
bringing about a transformational change in approaches to consumer analysis.
Recent advancements in AI and intense learning have significantly enhanced consumer
behavior analysis by integrating convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for emotion recognition.
However, challenges remain in accurately interpreting complex human emotions through
artificial intelligence, particularly in varying cultural and contextual settings (Alipour et al.,
2024). There is a significant research gap about how AI -driven neuromarketing can be
systematically applied across various industries and consumer se gments. Eye tracking,
electroencephalography (EEG), and galvanic skin response (GSR) are among the primary
neuromarketing tools used to uncover consumers’ subconscious reactions (Cuesta et al., 2019).
Artificial intelligence-supported data analytics and predictive modeling techniques are used in the
analysis of this physiological data, increasing both the accuracy and applicability of
neuromarketing research to a broader audience (Gill & Singh, 2020). When used in conjunction,
these technologies enable a deeper understanding of consumer preferences, resulting in more
personalized and effective marketing strategies (Harris et al., 2018). As neuromarketing and AI
continue to evolve, businesses will be able to leverage more profound insights into consumer
behavior, resulting in more meaningful interactions and data-driven strategic decision-making
(Lee et al., 2018). The purpose of this study is to present an overview of the combined use of
neuromarketing and artificial intelligence (AI) in research. In terms of data gathering and analysis,
this study focuses on recent literature exploring the interplay between artificial intelligence and
neuromarketing in marketing applications.
Although AI applications in marketing have been widely explored, their integration with
neuromarketing methodologies remains underdeveloped due to methodological inconsistencies
and the absence of standardized frameworks (Fortunato et al., 2014; Razbadauskaitė Venskė,
2024). Current research predominantly focuses on isolated neuromarketing techniques, such as
eye tracking, EEG, and GSR, rather than establishing a cohesive framework that systematically
incorporates AI-driven approaches (Kusá et al., 2023). Furthermore, the challenge of comparing
the findings of different studies due to the absence of standard evaluation criteria has resulted in
a fragmented methodology (Nesterenko & Olefirenko, 2023; Varghese, 2022). Differences in the
machine learning models used, as well as biometric measurement methods and experimental
conditions, also contribute to these inconsistencies (Bansal & Gupta, 2022). Such difficulties
make it challenging to reach generally accepted conclusions in the field and prevent the formation
of a common theoretical framework (Peyravi et al., 2020). Although existing literature reviews
have made significant contributions to integrating neuromarketing with artificial intelligence,
they are generally insufficient to address the overall development of the field from a holistic

2. LITERATURE REVIEW
 2.1. The Emergence and Theoretical Foundations of Neuromarketing
In recent years, the marketing approach has evolved from traditional product -focused
approaches to experience-based models that prioritize interaction with the consumer (Prahalad &
Ramaswamy, 2004). In this transformation, the effect of consumers’ subconscious processes on
decision-making mechanisms has become increasingly important (Suomala et al., 2012). In this
context, neuromarketing offers a deeper understanding of consumer behavior by employing
neuroscientific methods to measure cognitive and emotional reactions that individuals may not
be able to consciously express (Karmarkar & Yoon, 2016; Plassmann & Karmarkar, 2015).
Measuring neurophysiological signals with methods such as eye movements, skin
conductance, heart rate, or EEG, responses to marketing stimuli such as attention, memory,
preference formation, and emotional response can be analyzed, and thus stronger theoret ical

inferences can be made about consumer decision processes (Dimpfel, 2015; Kroupi et al., 2014).
In addition, these approaches based on neuroscience and behavioral psychology strengthen the
multidisciplinary structure of neuromarketing and enhance the precision of targeted marketing
efforts (Kawala-Sterniuk et al., 2021; Plassmann & Karmarkar, 2015).
Recent systematic reviews have revealed the diversity of tools and methods used in
neuromarketing research, highlighting how this field has evolved in conjunction with the

2020; Royo-Vela & Varga, 2022).
While neuromarketing research has significantly advanced the understanding of
consumers’ unconscious processes through neuroscientific methods, recent developments in data-
driven technologies have reshaped how consumer insights are generated in marketing.

2.2. The Rise of Artificial Intelligence in Marketing
Artificial intelligence (AI) has undergone tremendous development over the past few
decades, significantly reducing the workload of professionals across various industries. These
developments include the application of technologies such as big data analytics and machine
learning across a wide range of areas. AI is increasingly employed to assess customer buying
patterns, purchasing behaviors, preferences, and other data (Chatterji & Toffel, 2019; Sakib,
2023)
Significant advancements in marketing literature during the 2010s have increased
researchers’ interest in studying artificial intelligence phenomena, particularly data searching,
scanning, and learning. From this perspective, it is evident that artificial intelligence plays a
significant role in various marketing-related processes. According to Roberts and Fuller (2010),
some of these advancements include market research, client comprehension, segmentation, and
targeting. The majority of customer contact tools in the retail industry today are backed by
artificial intelligence technology, with smart customer experience campaigns built and run on the
foundation of long-term customer relationships (Kotler, 2003).
The rapid developments in artificial intelligence technologies in the 2010s have had a
profound impact on marketing research. Artificial intelligence has facilitated the processing of
big data and analysis in the field of marketing. Also, it allows to make easier to analyze pre- and
post-purchase consumer behavior (Durmuş Şenyapar, 2024). These developments contribute to
significantly improving the user experience.
Through machine learning algorithms and predictive analysis techniques, artificial
intelligence offers the opportunity to predict consumer expectations more accurately (Kopalle et
al., 2022; Rawnaque et al., 2020). This predictive ability allows companies to gain competitive
advantage by making their market positioning more effective.
As artificial intelligence increasingly shapes marketing analytics and decision -making
processes, its integration with neuroscience-based approaches has attracted growing scholarly
attention.

2.3. The Integration of AI and Neuromarketing
The integration of AI and neuromarketing is advancing the capacity to understand and
predict consumer behavior by combining neuroscience-based consumer insights with AI-powered
data analytics (Kroupi et al., 2014). Sentiment analysis and machine learning models increase the
accuracy of predictions of emotional and cognitive responses by processing large -scale
neurophysiological data (Kawala-Sterniuk et al., 2021). In this way, marketers can redesign
advertising strategies more effectively, optimize product development processes, and increase
consumer engagement. Especially with the widespread use of neuromarketing applications,
artificial intelligence-supported systems have improved analysis processes to better understand
consumer behavior and paved the way for data-driven strategies. By processing real-time data
obtained from neuromarketing techniques using artificial intelligence, changes in consumer
trends and purchasing power can be determined. Hence, businesses can dynamically adapt their
pricing strategies (Bruyn, 2020).
Neuromarketing tools such as neuroimaging and physiological measurement-based ones
provide in-depth insights into consumers’ unconscious reactions (Banjar et al., 2024). Recently,
AI has emerged as a lower-cost and more efficient alternative to traditional neuromarketing tools.
These technologies, thanks to their big data processing and pattern recognition capacity, enable
the development of more effective strategies, especially in areas such as advertising and product
design. Thus, this integrated approach increases the overall effectiveness of marketing activities
in many disciplines (Zurawicki, 2010).
The interaction between artificial intelligence and neuromarketing has increasingly gained
attention in the literature in recent years. These studies bring to the agenda not only the effects of
these technologies on digital marketing strategies but also the ethical issues they raise. For
instance, AI-supported marketing campaigns that focus on the effects of neuromarketing
algorithms on consumer privacy and data security are examined. Such studies provide important
contributions to understanding the ethical dilemmas and opportunities that arise at the intersection
of AI and neuroscience-based marketing applications. The study in question draws attention to
the increasing role of the processing of neurophysiological data through AI in targeted advertising
and the ethical dilemmas that these applications bring with them (Goncalves et al., 2024).
Furthermore, some recent studies have begun to explore the role of AI in consumer
behavior. Gaining a greater understanding of consumer behavior through methodological
principles and developments in fundamental neuroscience, neuromarketing and consumer
neuroscience will shape future marketing methods and enhance the sector (Gorgiev, 2020).
Because artificial intelligence (AI) has made it possible to study customer behavior, emotions,
and preferences, neuromarketing has been greatly improved. AI technologies, including support
vector machines (SVM), linear discriminant analysis (LDA), and artificial neural networks
(ANN), have demonstrated excellent accuracy in customer response classification and prediction
(Rawnaque et al., 2020). AI-powered technologies have been utilized to develop sustainable and
targeted marketing efforts, maximizing long-term strategy value and reducing environmental
impact (Bolón-Canedo et al., 2024). Moreover, the integration of AI, neuromarketing, and smart
retail environments has been explored, highlighting how AI-powered neuromarketing techniques
are revolutionizing consumer engagement and shopping experiences (Caratù, 2022). Their study
identified key trends such as real -time emotion detection and AI -driven personalized
recommendations in digital retail spaces. Recent studies continue to demonstrate the

3. METHODOLOGY
This article employs a structured approach to the methodological and theoretical analysis
of existing literature, with a focus on empirical studies. Our study adopts a PRISMA -guided
systematic review with content analysis approach, which allows for a more interactive and
interpretative engagement with the literature particularly useful when the research topic is
emerging or broad (Paul et al., 2021). However, to enhance rigor and transparency, we also
integrate systematic review techniques, PRISMA guided the identification, screening, eligibility
and inclusion steps; content analysis was used for synthesis. This hybrid approach leverages the
strengths of both methodologies, ensuring a comprehensive and methodologically sound
evaluation of the literature while maintaining the flexibility of a traditional review. Methodology
is organized in two stages: study selection using PRISMA framework and content analysis for the
quantitative synthesis of the studies (Okoli, 2015).

3.1. Data Collection and Study Selection (PRISMA Framework)
To explore the integration of artificial intelligence and neuromarketing in marketing
literature, several criteria were applied to select articles. These criteria included the use of AI and
neuromarketing approaches in marketing research, focusing on data collection or analysis
methods. Studies were required to be empirical, based on either hypothetical or real-world data.
The review focused on journal-published empirical articles, excluding conference papers, book
chapters, and research reports. Searches were conducted using the Scopus database, which
allowed for advanced searches combining keywords related to neuromarketing and AI. Within
the scope of this study, no pre-date limitation was applied in the literature review; however, only
studies published in 2015 and later were included in the analysis in line with the criteria of content
relevance and up-to-dateness. A study dated 1998 was also encountered during the literature
review; however, it was excluded because it was not directly related to the inte gration of
neuromarketing and artificial intelligence. There are two main reasons behind this preference.
First, the concept of neuromarketing was systematically defined in the academic literature
by Ariely and Berns (2002), and this study emphasized the potential of neuroimaging techniques
(e.g., fMRI) in marketing research. Secondly, the systematic integration of AI technologies with

retrieved from the Scopus database using predefined keywords related to neuromarketing and
artificial intelligence. During Screening, duplicate records and irrelevant studies were removed.
The Eligibility phase involved a detailed assessment of full-text articles based on predefined
inclusion criteria, focusing on empirical research with robust methodological approaches. Finally,
in the Inclusion phase, 43 studies that met all selection criteria were included in the final analysis.
This systematic approach minimizes bias and increases the reproducibility of the study findings.

3.2. Data Analysis: Content Analysis and Coding Procedure
Using the structured literature review methodology explained earlier, the 43 articles were
reviewed and grouped into thematic areas and key research topics. To ensure a systematic
classification, qualitative content analysis was employed to identify recurring themes in AI and
neuromarketing research (Schreier, 2012). The initial coding framework was developed based on
a review of the abstracts, and articles were categorized into thematic areas following a deductive-
inductive approach (Elo & Kyngäs, 2008). The thematic categorization was conducted by two
independent researchers, and inter-coder reliability was calculated using Krippendorff’s Alpha,
yielding a reliability score of 76.74% (Krippendorff, 2004). This approach ensured consistency
in coding and minimized subjective bias in the classification of studies. Following the application
of inclusion and exclusion criteria, data collection was conducted by extracting relevant studies
and their abstracts into an Excel database, forming the primary dataset for  analysis. These
abstracts, compiled within an Excel sheet, constituted the primary dataset for our analysis.
Subsequently, each abstract is thoroughly reviewed to identify initial codes that highlight
recurring themes and significant elements within each study, such as the use of AI in consumer
behavior or ethical considerations in neuromarketing. These initial codes were then organized into
broader thematic areas through thematic analysis. Each abstract was reviewed based on
predefined coding criteria to ensure consistency and accuracy in categorization.
The thematic coding process, which involved manually reviewing abstracts to identify
recurring themes, such as AI applications in consumer behavior or ethical considerations in
neuromarketing.  This method, though labor-intensive, ensures a comprehensive understanding
of each study’s focus. For instance, abstracts that mentioned using AI to enhance consumer
engagement were categorized under “AI in Marketing and Consumer Engagement.” Within each
thematic area, specific research topics were identified. For example, under “Neuromarketing
Techniques and Consumer Behavior,” topics like “predicting consumer preferences” and
“measuring emotional responses” were highlighted. These initial codes were categorized into
broader thematic areas using an inductive thematic analysis approach, allowing themes to emerge
organically from the data (Charmaz, 2006). To ensure methodological rigor, the identified themes
and topics were validated against existing literature, aligning them with established theoretical
frameworks (Ryan & Bernard, 2003). This validation process reinforced the robustness and
credibility of the categorization.
To assess the consistency of the coding process, inter-coder reliability was evaluated as
follows. Two experts with extensive knowledge in artificial intelligence and marketing conducted
the coding procedure. To ensure a thorough understanding of each step, they participated in a

4. FINDINGS
This section presents the key findings derived from the systematic review, focusing on the
integration of AI and neuromarketing in marketing research. The results are categorized into four
dimensions: (1) thematic areas and research topics, (2) theoretical analysis, (3) research design,
and (4) methodological patterns. Findings are analyzed at both individual and aggregate levels
across the examined publications. The key thematic areas dimension identifies dominant research
topics in AI-driven neuromarketing, highlighting emerging trends and research gaps. The
theoretical analysis evaluates the frameworks employed in AI-neuromarketing studies, assessing
their relevance and methodological soundness. The research design analysis examines
methodological choices, including sample selection, data collection techniques, and experimental
setups, identifying strengths and limitations. Lastly, the methodology section focuses on data
collection and analytical techniques, evaluating their effectiveness in addressing AI -
neuromarketing research objectives. Organizing the findings across these four dimensions allows
for a structured overview of the existing literature and supports the identification of key patterns
and research gaps.

4.3. Research Design
In addition to sampling strategies and data sources, the reviewed studies also vary in their
overall research designs, particularly regarding data collection procedures and study types, which
are addressed in the following subsection.

4.3.1. Sampling/dataset
Since the majority of the studies in this review are experimental or survey-based, the
limitations in sample size and representativeness are noteworthy. A substantial portion of studies
still relies on relatively small sample sizes, particularly those using experimental methods (e.g.,
Galdolage, 2022; Jai et al., 2021; Tupikovskaja-Omovie & Tyler, 2021). When artificial
intelligence analysis techniques are used, the use of large-scale secondary data becomes more
prominent. In this review, primary data sources dominate the research landscape, with most
studies collecting experimental, survey-based, or observational data (e.g., Pfeiffer et al., 2020;
Uhm et al., 2022; Yen & Chiang, 2021). However, a limited number of studies (e.g., M. Lee et
al., 2019) employed large-scale secondary datasets, such as review-based analyses. Although the
results indicate that only a small fraction of studies use secondary data, this trend may evolve as
AI-based neuromarketing techniques advance and enable the processing of larger datasets.
In addition, if a study used two separate datasets, both primary and secondary, the primary
data source was considered the main data type during the coding process. This approach provides
methodological consistency in classifying studies while also conside ring the increasing
importance of big data and artificial intelligence-based methods in neuromarketing research. On
the other hand, the participants in many studies were predominantly university students and young
adults (e.g., Esmark Jones et al., 2022; Juárez-Varón et al., 2020; Uhm et al., 2022) which limits
the generalizability of the findings to a broader consumer audience. A significant number of
studies also used convenience sampling methods, including MTurk, social media platforms, and

university-based recruitment (e.g., Chen et al., 2022; Fan et al., 2020; Yun et al., 2021), which
may introduce sampling bias and limit external validity.
A promising trend in current studies for 2024 is the application of AI-assisted participant
selection. In this method, more focused and qualified participant samples are created by using
recommendation algorithms or behavioral monitoring techniques. This situation demonstrates
that the trend toward artificial intelligence-based approaches in sampling processes is on the rise.

participants. Also, the most frequent recruitment channels included university-based pools (37%),
online crowdsourcing platforms such as MTurk (25%), and social media users (18%), with a
smaller portion targeting professionals or specific consumer segments (e.g., retail or e-commerce
users). These figures confirm the dominance of convenience sampling and illustrate the limited
generalizability of most existing research.

analysis, and image analysis are the most frequently used (e.g., Lee et al., 2019; Marchini et al.,
2015; Tupikovskaja-Omovie & Tyler, 2021). This suggests that AI-powered tools are becoming
more prominent in neuromarketing studies, providing deeper insights into consumer behavior. In
the realm of quantitative research, traditional statistical methods such as ANOVA, MANOVA,
regression, and structural equation modeling (SEM) remain widely used (25.6%) (e.g., Chang et
al., 2016; Simmonds et al., 2020; Um et al., 2020). However, modern quantitative techniques,
including fMRI, EEG, deep learning, and Bayesian statistics, represent an emerging area of
interest (16.3%) (e.g., Hakim et al., 2021; Jai et al., 2021; Yun et al., 2021). This indicates a
gradual shift towards AI-assisted methodologies in neuromarketing, particularly in consumer
behavior prediction and decision-making models.
One of the key findings of this study is the frequent integration of qualitative and
quantitative methods, particularly in neuroscientific research, where behavioral analysis is often
combined with physiological data collection (e.g., EEG, fMRI) (e.g., Fro nda et al., 2021;
Gunaratne et al., 2019; Pfeiffer et al., 2020). This suggests that while AI and machine learning
are transforming data collection, conventional statistical methods remain central in analyzing
results. Furthermore, an emerging trend is the combination of AI-driven automation with human
coding frameworks, particularly in studies where AI-generated data is supplemented with manual
analysis to enhance accuracy and contextual depth (e.g., Fu et al., 2020; Lee et al., 2019). As AI-
driven tools continue to evolve, future research may increasingly integrate fully automated AI-
based analysis methods, reducing the need for human intervention in data interpretation.
On the quantitative side, classical statistics (ANOVA, MANOVA, t-tests, regression, SEM)
remain prevalent (25.6% of all studies), while modern quantitative techniques such as fMRI, EEG,
deep learning, and Bayesian models represent an expanding but still smaller share (16.3%).
Overall, the findings indicate a growing tendency to combine behavioral and physiological
data, supported by AI-enabled analytical tools and complemented by human judgment,
underscoring the continued importance of theoretical and ethical oversight.

5. DISCUSSION: GAPS, CHALLENGES, AND FUTURE RESEARCH DIRECTIONS
In line with systematic literature review guidelines, a substantial portion of SLR studies
should be devoted to identifying research gaps and future research directions (Paul & Criado,
2020). This review presents findings obtained through content analysis and thematic coding
methods based on publications indexed in the Scopus database. It reveals trends in application
and theoretical approaches in the field. Although these tools indicate a certain level of
technological maturity in the field, a closer examination reveals several methodological and
conceptual gaps.

The analysis results indicate that the most frequently employed tools in the field of
neuromarketing include eye-tracking, electroencephalography (EEG), artificial intelligence-
based analysis methods, self-service technologies, and the Internet of Things (IoT). These tools
are beneficial for analyzing consumer behavior and interactions in digital environments, as well
as for developing data-driven marketing strategies. Moreover, it is noteworthy that advanced
methods, such as machine learning, artificial neural networks, functional magnetic resonance
imaging (fMRI), and Bayesian statistics, are becoming increasingly used in the field. The
contribution of these techniques is particularly remarkable in examining topics such as consumer
decision processes, trust perception, and emotional reactions.
The increasing accessibility of AI-powered analysis tools enables researchers to produce
in-depth consumer insights both in laboratory settings and on digital platforms. However, issues
such as the ethical use of data, privacy, and transparency stand out as fundamental challenges in
implementing these technologies. The ability of AI-based systems to interact with users not only
in technical but also emotional and social contexts (Pentina et al., 2023), is giving rise to new
types of consumer-AI relationships in the context of neuromarketing. Since these relationships
are shaped by AI systems that are always responsive, non-judgmental, and produce personalized
responses, users are more likely to develop emotional attachments to AI technologies (Brandtzaeg
et al., 2022). While this situation presents opportunities for marketing strategies, it also raises
ethical concerns.
Moreover, many studies remain largely tool-driven, offering limited theoretical integration
beyond descriptive insights. Some suggestions for future studies can be made in light of the
research findings. First, studies on the combined use of multiple methods (e.g., integration of EEG
and sentiment analysis) can be developed. Second, the effects of new technologies, such as
generative artificial intelligence and large language models (LLMs) in the marketing context,
should be investigated more comprehensivel y. Third, emphasis should be placed on
interdisciplinary studies that address not only the technical but also the psychological and ethical
aspects of human-AI interactions. In line with these recommendations, it is possible to establish
AI-supported neuromarketing research on a more holistic, ethically based, and theoretically sound
basis.
This study presents a focused literature review to examine how these two disciplines are
addressed together, specifically at the intersection between neuromarketing and artificial
intelligence applications. However, researchers should consider some limitations. Firstly, this
study only covers publications indexed in the Scopus database. Including other reputable
databases, such as Business Source Complete and Web of Science (WOS), in the analysis could
provide a more holistic and diverse view of the field. In addition, only published or accepted
studies were examined; current studies in the preprint or peer-review process were excluded from
the analysis. Although this study has several limitations, it makes a valuable contribution to future
research on neuromarketing and artificial intelligence in the field of marketing. Also, it provides
comprehensive literature for future studies in this field.
Although these technologies are commonly used in various marketing areas, including
sales management, customer experience analysis, and chatbot applications, some techniques, such
as EEG and IoT, are less studied in the literature. However, these areas may become important
research focuses on the future (Plassman et al., 2015). While advanced AI methods enable the
processing of large data sets and the analysis of complex consumer behavior patterns in greater

6. CONCLUSION
This systematic literature review examines studies that investigate the application of
artificial intelligence (AI) and neuromarketing techniques in marketing research This study
highlights the significant influence of AI on neuromarketing research and dig ital consumer
analytics and its potential to reshape the future of marketing literature. By offering a structured
synthesis and forward-looking research agenda, this review contributes to the consolidation of
AI-driven neuromarketing as a theoretically grounded and ethically informed research domain.
The main contribution of this study is to systematically analyze the artificial intelligence
and neuromarketing literature and provide a structural synthesis of the current state of the field.
First, thematic areas, methodological approaches, and theoretic al frameworks addressed in
previous studies were determined; thus, a general map of the literature was presented to
researchers. Secondly, the research designs, data collection methods, and analysis techniques used
in AI-focused neuromarketing research have been thoroughly evaluated; thus, the strengths and
limitations of current applications have been identified. Thirdly, new conceptual expansions and
research gaps have emerged in the field, aiming to guide future research by considering both
classical and contemporary approaches together. Finally, thematic trends and topics that have
emerged at the intersection of neuromarketing and AI have been identified; thus, an
interdisciplinary roadmap has been presented for researchers interested in the field.
