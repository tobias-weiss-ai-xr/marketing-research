ABSTRACT
Purpose – This research aims to analyze the integration of artificial
intelligence (AI)-based Search Engine Optimization (SEO) strategies by a
digital marketing practitioner to enhance the organic visibility of local
MSME halal commodities on the Shopee marketplace.
Design – Utilizing a descriptive qualitative single-case study design, data
was collected through an in-depth interview with an SEO practitioner in
Pekalongan and an evaluation of a specific MSME client's Shopee analytic
dashboard during a one-month observation period.
Findings – The findings reveal that AI acts as a critical "translator" bridging
human search intent and algorithmic logic. Through precise prompt
engineering for long-tail keyword extraction, title reconstruction, and
copywriting, the intervention successfully resolved the product's baseline
algorithmic blindness. Based on practitioner-reported observations, this
metadata restructuring yielded a substantial surge in top-of-funnel search
impressions and improved organic rankings for niche keywords without paid
advertising costs.
Research Implications – Normatively, AI-based SEO serves as a
preventive tool for Hifz al-Mal (wealth protection) by mitigating the risk of
inefficient promotional spending. However, a primary limitation is the
study's exclusive focus on top-of-funnel visibility indicators for a single
product listing, which does not guarantee actual financial sales conversions
or broad market success, limiting its generalizability.
Originality – This study provides an exploratory technical case note
demonstrating how artificial intelligence can be democratized at the micro-
economy level via third-party practitioners, protecting local halal products
from algorithmic marginalization.

INTRODUCTION
 The development of the digital economy over the last decade has triggered a fundamental
structural transformation in both the global and national business landscape. Digitalization is no
longer merely a complementary instrument, but has metamorphosed into the primary ecosystem
for trade activities and economic transactions (Henderi et al., 2024). This transformation is
marked by a massive shift in consumer behavior from conventional markets to electronic

beverage sector, expanding into a halal lifestyle that encompasses the Muslim fashion industry,
cosmetics, and personal care products (Mustajab, 2025).
The high demand for halal products requires businesses to not only comply with material
halal standards but also ensure their products are widely accessible to consumers. From an
Islamic economic perspective, ensuring the availability and easy access to halal products aligns
closely with the principles of Maqasid Sharia, particularly in the pillars of religious protection
(hifz ad-din) and property protection (hifz al-mal). Consumption of halal products is a
manifestation of upholding religious values, while the ability of businesses, especially micro and
medium-sized businesses, to survive and earn reasonable profits in the digital market is a form of
asset preservation recommended in Islam (Dewi et al., 2025). However, the reality on the ground
shows that the availability of halal certification and high product quality do not automatically
guarantee commercial success. Within a digital platform containing tens of millions of product
listings, a commodity's material superiority is meaningless if the product lacks adequate visibility
among consumers. This visibility creates a critical link between halal product availability and
marketplace discoverability. In an algorithmic ecosystem, this discoverability is systematically
shaped by how well a seller manages their product metadata encompassing the textual
architecture of titles, descriptions, and backend tags (Seraoui, 2026). High keyword relevance
within this metadata ensures the product accurately matches consumer search intent, which
directly dictates the volume of search impressions an item receives (Nagpal & Petersen, 2020).
Product visibility on marketplace platforms relies heavily on the search algorithms
designed by the platform provider. When consumers type in a search keyword, the algorithm
works to filter, sort, and display products deemed most relevant. Therefore, Search Engine
Optimization (SEO) is a crucial instrument and a key player in digital marketing, as evidenced by
Pramudita et al. (2019), who found that implementing SEO methods on e-commerce platforms
can generate significant metric spikes, including search traffic growth of up to 210.42%. SEO
strategies on e-commerce platforms involve a series of technical optimizations, from placing
relevant keywords with high search volumes, writing product titles structured according to
algorithm preferences, to designing product descriptions (copywriting) that are not only
informative but also algorithmically persuasive. Furthermore, empirical studies in digital
marketing confirm that securing a top marketplace ranking is not just about prestige, but it acts as
the primary catalyst for driving click-through rates (CTR) (Fiorentina et al., 2025). Consequently,
products that successfully master these SEO principles will secure these strategic ranking
positions, which is statistically directly proportional to increased click-through rates and final
sales conversions (Usmany et al., 2024).
With the advancement of computing technology, SEO practices have entered a new era
with the integration of artificial intelligence. Large-scale language models and natural language
processing technology enable computing systems to understand human search intent, analyze
millions of pieces of market trend data in real time, and generate precise text output (Irvani et al.,
2025). The use of artificial intelligence in digital marketing enables long-tail keyword research to
be conducted with pinpoint accuracy. AI is capable of predicting specific derivative phrases, such
as combining geographic identity, halal value, and product type, to target a highly focused
consumer market (niche market). Recent global studies on digital marketing indicate that utilizing
AI-driven tools for semantic keyword research significantly improves product discoverability by
accurately capturing nuanced consumer search intent in competitive e-commerce platforms
(Hasan, 2025). Furthermore, AI's capabilities in composing copywriting provide businesses with

Unfortunately, the adoption of this cutting-edge technology has created a widening digital
divide between large-scale corporate companies and Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises
(MSMEs) (Wibowo & Dewi, 2026). Regional MSMEs, which often serve as the backbone of the
local economy through the production of superior commodities such as home-based snack
foods, herbal drinks, and batik craft centers in the Pekalongan region, face highly complex
technological literacy challenges (Ahyani et al., 2023). Local MSMEs generally possess in-depth
expertise in production (product quality and compliance with halal standards), but struggle with
technological stuttering in digital marketing. Limited human resources, a minimal budget for
algorithm research, and unfamiliarity with updated marketing technology features make superior
regional halal products vulnerable to losing competitive visibility. As a result, local MSME
products are often overshadowed by products from non-local, factory-scale competitors or even
imported products managed with aggressive and systematic SEO optimization. This local
phenomenon mirrors broader global digital commerce trends, where international studies
consistently report that micro-businesses lacking SEO and algorithmic literacy suffer severe
market marginalization despite offering high-quality commodities (Damanik, 2024).
Facing structural imbalances in the algorithmic ecosystem, the presence of third parties,
namely digital marketing practitioners or service providers (digital agencies/SEO specialists),
provides a strategic solution and bridges the literacy gap experienced by MSMEs. Broader
international research on platform-based MSME competitiveness highlights that leveraging digital
intermediaries or tech-savvy third parties helps micro-businesses overcome severe digital literacy
gaps and compete effectively (Gebrian et al., 2026). These service providers act as accelerators by
using cutting-edge artificial intelligence tools to formulate SEO architectures for their MSME
clients. This practice of digital marketing assistance or outsourcing represents a new
phenomenon in contemporary Islamic business governance. This approach offers a potential way
to reduce the risk of financial loss due to inefficient conventional advertising costs (ads), which
often burn through MSMEs' funds without clear results, acting as a rational, strategic endeavor.
Through optimizations carried out by practitioners equipped with AI, local halal products can be
rediscovered by consumers, which in turn will keep the wheels of the Muslim economy turning
amidst the onslaught of mass-produced products.
A review of existing literature reveals distinct but fragmented trajectories in current
research. In the broader realm of AI marketing and SEO, recent studies have heavily emphasized
artificial intelligence as a transformative tool that significantly enhances long-tail keyword
accuracy, semantic search alignment, and content generation speed (Irvani et al., 2025; Sari &
Triyono, 2025). Furthermore, parallel investigations have demonstrated AI's capacity to
autonomously map complex consumer intent and automate personalized digital campaigns at a
macro scale (Praveen et al., 2026).
Within the specific context of marketplace optimization, academic research has primarily
focused on evaluating the algorithmic variables of large-scale corporate entities or is limited to
predictive analysis of supply chain management (Endra & Veri, 2025). Other scholarly works in
this domain have heavily scrutinized dynamic pricing algorithms and cross-border logistics
strategies employed by global marketplace giants, which are often inapplicable to micro-scale
merchants (Zeng & Yan, 2025).
Conversely, within the discourse of the Islamic digital economy and halal MSME
digitalization, current scholarship highlights the urgency of digital transition to maintain
competitiveness and fulfill Maqasid Sharia principles (Dewi et al., 2025; Ramadhani, 2026).
Additionally, broader studies emphasize that the global surge in halal lifestyle trends necessitates
an aggressive digital marketing posture from local producers to capture wider market shares
(Natsir et al., 2025). However, studies in this specific domain predominantly explore general
social media adoption, digital payment gateways, or basic e-commerce onboarding (Wiryanto et
al., 2025; Santoso et al., 2023). Similarly, technological adoption literature for micro-businesses is

systematically exploited at a micro-scale specifically through the intervention of SEO

that collaboration between SEO expertise, AI technology, and halal MSME commodities is key
to the resilience of the Islamic economy in an era of disruption.
Based on the contextual background, theoretical framework, and identified problem gaps
outlined above, this study narrows its focus to an exploratory analysis of how one digital
marketing practitioner applies artificial intelligence for Shopee SEO among a halal MSME client
in Pekalongan. Rather than making broad claims about overall market competitiveness or the
entire Sharia economic ecosystem, this study specifically seeks to dissect the technical
implementation of AI prompt engineering in formulating metadata for local commodities.
Furthermore, it observes the estimated impact on organic visibility post-optimization and offers a
normative interpretation of this approach through the lens of Maqasid Sharia, particularly the
protection of wealth (Hifz al-Mal). The findings of this study are expected to contribute a
focused technical case note to the scientific treasury of Islamic digital economics and provide
practical recommendations regarding the importance of third-party-based AI literacy for local
halal MSMEs.
METHOD
This research uses a descriptive qualitative approach with a single-case study design. The
single-case study design was chosen because this research aims to in-depth explore a specific
contemporary phenomenon within a real-life context, namely how high-tech interventions are
applied at the microeconomic level. The qualitative approach allows researchers to go beyond
statistical data to explore procedural understanding of the mechanisms, decision-making, and
logical patterns used by a practitioner in integrating artificial intelligence into Search Engine
Optimization (SEO) strategies. To ensure the quality and transparency of methodological
reporting, the manuscript for this study adheres to the Standards for Reporting Qualitative
Research (SRQR) guidelines.
To avoid the language of broad representativeness, the unit of analysis in this research
context is not the MSMEs themselves, but rather a specific digital marketing service provider
(Informant A) specializing in e-commerce SEO operating or handling clients in the Pekalongan
area and its surroundings. Given that not all practitioners openly use AI for halal product
optimization, one information-rich informant was determined using a purposive selection
technique, which selects the informant based on specific criteria deliberately established to be
relevant to the study's objectives.
The inclusion criteria for participants in this study included: (1) SEO practitioners or
consultants who actively manage Shopee marketplace accounts belonging to local MSME clients;
(2) handles clients focusing on halal commodities, specifically one product category: Muslim
fashion (the observation was narrowed down to one single MSME client's Shopee store); and (3)
The practitioner explicitly integrates artificial intelligence tools (Gemini 3.0 Pro, using Indonesian
language prompts with 3 to 5 iterations, followed by a manual review and editing process by the
practitioner) in their keyword formulation and copywriting workflows. Based on these criteria,
one main participant (SEO Service Provider) was selected whose identity data was anonymized as
"Informant A" to comply with research ethics regarding the privacy and confidentiality of their

because direct interviews with the MSME owners were not conducted, any claims regarding the
client's baseline conditions, consumer behavior, and perceived impact are based on the genuine
testimonials and feedback of the actual MSME client, which were relayed and recounted by
Informant A during the interview. To ensure the validity of the interview instrument, member
checking was conducted, which involved reconfirming the interview transcripts with participants
to avoid misinterpretation.
The second supporting instrument was the observation of digital documentation,
specifically the analytical dashboard of the "My Business" (Seller Center) feature in the Shopee
app managed by Informant A. Regarding ethical considerations, explicit informed consent was
obtained from the MSME client (facilitated through Informant A) allowing the researcher to
utilize their store's traffic and visibility metrics for academic purposes. However, to protect
sensitive commercial data, the MSME granted this permission under the strict condition that no
direct screenshots or visual captures of their internal dashboard could be taken, stored, or
published. Consequently, the digital documentation in this study relies entirely on the exact
metrics verbally reported and explained by Informant A during the dashboard observation,
without any visual evidence attached.
This research procedure was implemented through three systematic stages that took place
in April 2026. The first stage was pre-fieldwork, which included developing a research design,
initial observations of halal commodity search trends on the Shopee app, and establishing criteria
and obtaining permits for participants. The second stage was fieldwork. At this stage, primary
data collection was conducted through in-depth face-to-face interviews with Informant A, lasting
approximately 60 to 90 minutes. Concurrently with the interviews, the researcher also collected
secondary data in the form of documentation of store traffic metrics history, search interactions
(search impressions), and product listing performance from the client's Shopee dashboard, which
had been optimized using AI. The third stage was post-fieldwork, where the researcher
performed data transcription, reduction, analysis, and preparation of a report on the findings.
The qualitative data obtained was analyzed using a thematic approach. Following SRQR
guidelines for data processing, a systematic coding procedure was applied. The researchers
manually coded the interview transcripts and observational notes to categorize the data into
distinct analytical themes, specifically the technical AI prompt iterations and the Maqasid Sharia
implications. This analysis consisted of three activity streams. First, Data Condensation, where
interview transcripts and metric documentation were filtered to isolate the most essential
information. Second, Data Display, which is the process of organizing information into a
comprehensive descriptive narrative. The evolution of keyword structure and copywriting from
before to after the AI intervention was analyzed through descriptive textual analysis to
demonstrate how prompt engineering changed the product algorithm specifications. Third,
Conclusion Drawing, where narrative findings were linked to literature on Maqasid Sharia and e-
commerce.

RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
Baseline Visibility Problem
The findings systematically and chronologically describe the artificial intelligence (AI)-
based Search Engine Optimization (SEO) intervention process implemented by a digital
marketing practitioner (Informant A) for a Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprise (MSME) client
selling halal commodities in the Pekalongan region. Based on in-depth interviews, it was
identified that the baseline condition experienced by the client a local Muslim fashion brand
producing men's batik shirts was stagnant organic traffic on the Shopee e-commerce platform.
Although the products featured premium-quality materials and competitive designs, the product
titles and descriptions used by the MSME owner were overly generic, intuitive, and completely
ignored the logic of search engine algorithms.
To understand the root of this visibility problem and how artificial intelligence resolves it,
it is necessary to conceptualize AI not merely as an automated text generator, but as a critical
"translator" bridging human language and algorithmic architecture. Drawing on search intent
theory and consumer information behavior, Muslim consumers often utilize nuanced, context-
heavy, and sometimes highly colloquial queries when searching for specific apparel. Conversely,
standard marketplace retrieval systems prioritize textual relevance and structured metadata to
filter products, which often fail to capture the everyday search language used by consumers
(Karnam, 2026). By utilizing advanced natural language processing (NLP) capabilities, artificial
intelligence deciphers the complexity and nuances of human search intent and translates them
into a highly optimized, machine-readable format. This theoretical framing positions AI as an
essential intermediary that synchronizes human search behavior with e-commerce search engines,
setting the foundational logic for the technical SEO interventions that were subsequently
implemented by the MSME (Suresh, 2025).
To demonstrate this baseline problem concretely, Informant A provided specific
observations from the client's initial storefront. Emphasizing the client's critical algorithmic
blindness, Informant A explicitly stated during the interview:

"When I first audited their Seller Center dashboard, it was just completely quiet. They had premium
halal batik, but their metadata was just 'baju koko'. I told the owner, if you only use those two words,
you are fighting against millions of mass-produced items from big factories. Shopee's algorithm doesn't even
know your product exists because your text is too generic." (Informant A, personal communication,
June 7, 2025).

Before the intervention, the client's superior product was listed with an extremely generic
title: "Baju Koko Batik Muslim" (Muslim Batik Koko Shirt), accompanied by a bare-minimum
description stating only "Bahan katun, ukuran M L XL, warna coklat" (Cotton material, sizes M L
XL, brown color). Furthermore, a preliminary marketplace search observation conducted by the
practitioner revealed that querying the generic keyword "Baju Koko" in Shopee’s search bar
yielded hundreds of thousands of competing listings, instantly burying the client’s product deep
beyond the 50th page of search results. This inability to formulate appropriate product metadata
and the reliance on highly saturated keywords resulted in near-zero visibility and very low click-
through rates, ultimately stifling any potential for sales conversions. To provide a rigorous

AI-Based Keyword Extraction
To break the algorithm's deadlock, Informant A implemented a strategic intervention
using an artificial intelligence tool based on Large Language Models (LLM)  (specifically using
Gemini 3.0 Pro, using Indonesian language prompts with 3 to 5 iterations, followed by a manual
review and editing process by the practitioner). This intervention process was not fully
automated, but rather involved a highly precise prompt engineering process. The first stage was
long-tail keyword extraction.
Illustrating the prompt engineering logic, Informant A explained:

"I don't just command the AI to 'make a title'. That's useless. I treat Gemini as a bridge. I prompt it to
think like a Muslim buyer in Java looking for a specific premium item, and then translate that human
intent into exact long-tail keywords that the Shopee system can categorize. It is all about finding that
sweet spot between cultural search habits and machine logic."  (Informant A, personal
communication, June 7, 2025).

Informant A provided specific instructions to the AI to scan the search behavior of
Muslim consumers in e-commerce. Instead of using short-tail keywords with high competition,
the AI was instructed to search for derivative phrases. As a practical example, for Muslim
fashion, the AI output recommended phrases such as "men's Pekalongan batik koko shirt,"
"men's contemporary motif Muslim top," and "Shanghai collar shirt with premium cotton
material." The criteria for accepting these AI-generated keywords involved the practitioner
manually cross-referencing them with Shopee's internal search suggestion bar to validate
marketplace search volume.
The second stage was product title reconstruction. Based on Informant A's practical field
experience, the marketplace's algorithm prioritize the first 50 characters of a product title, the AI
was used to arrange the extracted keywords into a logical and persuasive title sentence.
Informant A illustrates this structural change ensure the search engine could index the
product in multiple halal lifestyle search categories simultaneously:
"I asked the AI to create a title with a maximum of 100 characters, which must include the words 'Koko
Batik,' 'Pekalongan,' and 'Premium.' The AI then changed the client's title from 'Muslim Batik Shirt'
to 'Men's Short-Sleeved Premium Cotton Koko Batik Pekalongan Shirt - Muslim Fashion.'"
(Informant A, personal communication, June 7, 2025).

The third stage focused on crafting the product description (copywriting). Product
descriptions are often overlooked by MSMEs, who typically simply copy and paste basic
specifications. With precise instructions, the AI formulated a comprehensive description text,
educating consumers about the historical value of local products, emphasizing the halal
certification number, and inserting secondary keywords naturally (keyword placement)
throughout the paragraph without appearing spammy. Furthermore, the AI also automatically
recommended 15 hashtags most relevant to the halal lifestyle ecosystem to be included at the end
of the description.

Algorithmic Implications and Maqasid Sharia Perspectives
The significant increase in organic visibility following the intervention of artificial
intelligence confirms the theoretical basis that the e-commerce ecosystem essentially operates
based on a textual data matching mechanism. This aligns with Riswan et al.'s (2024) statement
that marketplace AI operates based on a textual data matching mechanism. There is a clear
communication gap between buyer intent and the availability of keywords in the seller's catalog.
As previously established, the AI intervention effectively bridges this gap. The effectiveness of
the long-tail keyword strategy executed through AI prompts indicates that the niche market for
local sharia-based commodities actually has a massive share. However, so far, this niche has been
unreachable because local MSMEs have failed to formulate diction identical to what consumers
type in the search field. This aligns with the opinion of Andriana et al. (2024) who stated that AI
can communicate a product well so that it will be better known by consumers.
Within the philosophical framework of Islamic Economics, this phenomenon of textual
digitization is not merely a conventional marketing tactic, but rather can be normatively
interpreted to align with the achievement of Maqasid Sharia, particularly in the manifestation of
the pillars of Hifz al-Mal (protection of wealth) and Hifz ad-Din (protection of religion).
However, these Maqasid claims must be stated cautiously as a normative interpretation, not as an
empirical finding, because this study did not quantitatively measure consumer religiosity, halal
consumer behavior, or actual financial savings. From the perspective of Hifz al-Mal, the use of
artificial intelligence for organic SEO optimization represents a preventive risk mitigation

The implications of these findings indicate that the integration of artificial intelligence in
SEO optimization is an essential instrument for creating a more inclusive digital economic
ecosystem for local halal commodities. Eliminating algorithmic gaps is no longer merely a
technical marketing issue but has become a fundamental prerequisite for maintaining the
resilience and competitiveness of MSMEs amidst the expansion of the e-commerce market. More
broadly, these findings signal the need for a paradigm shift in strategies for empowering the
community's economy. Support for the existence of MSMEs is not enough to stop at product
halal certification, but must be expanded to mastering digital search infrastructure to ensure local
commodities are not marginalized by market algorithm bias.
This research provides exploratory contributions and conceptual updates to bridge the
literature gap in the discourse that intersects artificial intelligence innovation, e-commerce
marketing, and comprehensive Islamic economic studies. Much of the previous academic
literature tends to dichotomize artificial intelligence as an exclusive instrument that can only be
operated and benefit large corporate companies (Wibowo & Dewi, 2026). The study provides an
illustrative case showing that AI-assisted SEO may be used by local MSME support actors to
improve organic visibility in one marketplace context. Furthermore, this research offers a new
analytical framework for Islamic economics scholars regarding how prompt engineering in
language models is not only technically valuable but can be interpreted as a Hifz al-Mal
instrument to defend the existence of local halal products from the threat of algorithmic
marginalization in highly competitive digital markets.
As is customary in scientific work, this research has several limitations that should be
acknowledged academically. The main limitation lies in the adopted success measurement
metrics. The focus of this research is limited exclusively to "visibility" indicators (increased search
impressions, click-through rates, and number of organic visits). While high visibility is an absolute
prerequisite in the digital marketing pyramid, it does not always translate into an absolute increase
in financial sales conversions (closing rates). Consumers' final decisions to checkout on e-
commerce platforms are also dictated by a variety of complex external factors beyond the control
of SEO text optimization. Variables such as comparative selling price competitiveness between
stores, the availability of promotional programs (such as free shipping vouchers or double

research or a mixed-methods approach to conduct precise regression tests to determine the

Practically, these findings should serve as a basis for evaluation for regional policymakers,
particularly relevant agencies overseeing Cooperatives and SMEs, as well as university-based
sharia business incubators. The paradigm of MSME mentoring and training programs must
immediately evolve, shifting from simply teaching "how to register a marketplace account" to
providing advanced technical literacy, such as simple prompt engineering training for copywriting
and understanding algorithms. This way, the digital independence of local micro-businesses in
marketing halal commodities can be achieved comprehensively and sustainably.
CONCLUSION
This study provides an illustrative case showing that AI-assisted SEO may be used by
local MSME support actors to improve organic visibility in one marketplace context. Based on
the practitioner's evaluation in this single-case observation, the use of artificial intelligence tools
enabled the engineering of long-tail keywords and the creation of product descriptions
(copywriting) that aligned with Muslim consumers' search intentions. This textual transformation
is reported by the informant to have boosted organic impression metrics without burdening the
specific business with paid advertising costs. Within a normative Maqasid Sharia framework, this
approach represents a potential effort in the economic empowerment of the Muslim community
(maslahah mursalah), where technological assistance aims to ensure that local commodities can
compete and avoid being marginalized by free-market algorithms.
The development prospects derived from these findings suggest that algorithm literacy
and mastery of artificial intelligence must begin to be mainstreamed in the governance of the
sharia business ecosystem. Future practical implementation requires regional policymakers and
business incubators to initiate prompt engineering training programs for micro-entrepreneurs,
enabling holistic digital independence. Furthermore, to complement the Islamic digital economy
research roadmap, further studies are highly recommended to quantitatively measure the real
financial conversion rate (sales conversion rate) after SEO optimization to validate the
effectiveness of text visibility on revenue growth for halal businesses.
