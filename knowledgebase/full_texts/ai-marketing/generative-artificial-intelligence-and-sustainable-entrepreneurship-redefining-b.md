# Generative Artificial Intelligence and Sustainable Entrepreneurship: Redefining Business Leadership, Marketing Excellence, and Governance Practices

Dr. Santanu Roy

Abstract
The advent of generative AI is having a profound impact on entrepreneurial strategy, organizational innovation,
leadership, and long-term competitive performance. This study explored the impact of the adoption of artificial
intelligence on the process of entrepreneurial transformation toward sustainability in terms of leadership
support, business model innovation, sustainable competitive advantage, institutional support, cost effectiveness,
and firm performance. The study adopted a quantitative secondary-data design and had a managerial dataset of
325 observations. Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling was utilised to assess the measurement
reliability, construct validity, structural linkages, explanatory power, and indirect effects. It was discovered that
the measurement model had acceptable Average Variance and Cronbach's alpha values over 0.80. extracted
values greater than 0.65, demonstrating convergent validity and adequate dependability. The structural results
indicated that the use of AI had significant impacts on business model innovation, sustainable competitive
advantage, and firm performance. The top direct result of business model innovation was that it created a firm's
strongest direct link to its performance, followed by sustainable competitive advantage. AI adoption was
strongly encouraged by leadership support, and government support had positive impacts on AI adoption,
leadership support and cost effectiveness. Important indirect effects also suggested that AI has impacts on
organizational performance via innovation and sustainable competitive capability. The overall results indicate
that the strategic contribution of AI is highly reliant on leadership, innovation, institutional support, and
responsible organizational practices.
Keywords: Generative artificial intelligence; sustainable entrepreneurship; business model innovation;
leadership support; sustainable competitive advantage

Introduction
In today's business landscape, generative artificial
intelligence (GenAI) has become an AI revolution that
has reshaped the way organizations develop
knowledge, design products, communicate with
customers, and make decisions. While previous types
of AI used to analyze, predict or optimize existing
information, GenAI can create text, images, code, ideas,
or decision-support content, introducing a wider
range of possibilities for human–machine
collaboration in entrepreneurial contexts (Hermann &
Puntoni, 2024; Kirk & Givi, 2025). This evolution has
sparked growing interest among organisations
regarding GenAI adoption and the prospect of
redefining innovation, productivity, and competitive
strategy (Kumar et al., 2025). Concurrently, the
increased use of artificial intelligence (AI) has given
rise to new managerial, ethical, and policy challenges
for organizations to address, which demand a balance
between technological opportunity and accountability
and responsible use (Dwivedi et al., 2021). This has
sparked these debates, as conversational GenAI
systems are accessible, enabling entrepreneurs and
managers to incorporate AI into their everyday
business operations, strategic planning,
communication, and decision-making processes
(Dwivedi et al., 2023).
The elevation of GenAI is particularly considerable for
sustainable entrepreneurship, where companies aim
to generate sustainable economic value along with
environmental and social considerations. With a
growing importance of entrepreneurial success not
only in terms of profit but also in terms of resilience,
responsible innovation, resource efficiency, and social
contribution (Abbas & Bulut, 2024), the field of

Leadership is a essential factor that determines the
successful implementation of AI in business and its
impact on entrepreneurial results. The strategic value
that can be reached with the exploitation of AI
technologies hinges on managerial commitment,
organizational readiness, employee involvement and
the proper combination of these technologies into the
occurring decision systems. The automation–
augmentation view states that managers should
decide which tasks to automate and in which tasks to
apply human expertise to complement AI tasks
(Raisch & Krakowski, 2021). In sustainable
entrepreneurship, balancing digital transformation
with long-term stakeholder and sustainability goals is
crucial, as leaders navigate this landscape. Sustainable
leadership can support organisations in linking the
innovation process, responsible growth and strategic
decision-making with the broader entrepreneurial
purpose (Ribeiro & Leitão, 2024).
Marketing is yet another area that is transforming
significantly due to AI and GenAI. Previous studies
have shown that AI can transform customer analysis,
segmentation, personalization, customer service and
customer marketing decision-making (Davenport et
al., 2020). Furthermore, strategic frameworks show
that AI can improve marketing performance by
aligning various machine intelligence levels and tasks
to their respective customers and managers (Huang &
Rust, 2021). Yet, there are dangers to AI use, such as
over-automation, inappropriate targeting, and the
inability to decipher complex consumer behaviour
(De Bruyn et al., 2020). Recent studies focus on the
growing potential of AI in consumer research and
digital marketing (Mariani et al., 2022), and further
developments of machine learning are enhancing
customer analytics and marketing intelligence
(Herhausen et al., 2024). GenAI has been able to be
applied to various areas such as automated content
creation, campaign development, personalisation and
creative assistance (Chan & Choi, 2025). Yet, the
response of consumers to AI-generated ads can differ
based on their perceptions of authenticity, the nature
of the message, and their trust in AI systems (Chen et
al., 2024), as well as consumer attitudes towards AI
more broadly, which are influenced by trust,
convenience, identity, and consumer control (Puntoni
et al., 2021). Acceptance is thus still crucial to the
successful implementation of marketing
professionals' organizations (Iyer & Bright, 2024).
However, responsible AI governance has become
increasingly significant despite the opportunities. AI
governance structures are needed within
organizations to ensure transparency, accountability,
responsible technology use, and risk management
(Mäntymäki et al., 2022). The success of GenAI in
achieving sustainable entrepreneurial transformation
hinges on the synergy of leadership, marketing,
innovation, and governance. In the past, these
dimensions have been explored individually, and
there was a lack of integration between studies that
explore AI adoption and leadership, innovation,
sustainable competitive advantage, organizational
performance, and more broadly marketing and
governance.
Objectives:
Examine how AI affects organisational performance,
sustained competitive advantage, and business model
innovation.
Evaluate the importance of leadership and
institutional support for sustainable entrepreneurial
transformation using AI.
Understand the implications of AI-driven changes to
organizations for Marketing excellence and
responsible governance in the context of Generative
AI.
2. Methodology
2.1 Research Design
A quantitative, explanatory and secondary data
research strategy was used to explore the role of AI
adoption in transformational sustainable
entrepreneurship and/or organization. The study
placed itself in the context of generative artificial
intelligence, and for the empirical study,
organizational AI adoption measures were selected
from the existing dataset.
2.2 Data Source
The publicly available Mendeley dataset “Dataset of
the role of Artificial Intelligence Adoption on Business
Model Innovation, Sustainable Competitive Advantage,
and Firm Performance” was used in this study (Tran
Nha et al., 2026). The data have been compiled based
on responses received from 325 managers in
companies in various industrial sectors in Vietnam. It
was chosen because of its variables that encompass AI
adoption, leadership support(LS), business model
innovation, sustainable competitive advantage,
government support (GS), cost effectiveness (CE), and
business performance.
2.3 Study Constructs and Operationalization
The following constructs were analyzed: Artificial
Intelligence Adoption (AI), LS, GS, CE, Business Model
Innovation (BMI), Sustainable Competitive Advantage
(SCA), and Firm Performance (FP). AI was an
indicator for the implementation of artificial

The operational concept of sustainable

The Leadership Support construct was first directly

scale measure of marketing excellence in the data set,

model innovation, competitive advantage, and firm

constructs.
2.5 Conceptual Model
The empirical framework focused on AI as a key
organizational capability that is influenced by
leadership, governmental and economic factors. The
sequence of the main structures was envisioned as LS,
GS, and CE → AI Adoption → BMIS Competitive
Advantage → FP This model allowed them to evaluate
the value of adopting AI for sustainable
entrepreneurial and organizational results.
2.6 Data Preparation and Screening
The data set and the resulting statistics were
examined to check for valid constructs, measurement
indicators, structural relationships, and model
statistics. The analytical sample was a total of 325
managerial observations. Labels and indicators were
kept as per their original definition, and no other
observations or variables unrelated to the labels were
added to the analysis.
2.7 Measurement Model Assessment
Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modelling
(PLS-SEM) was used to test the measurement model.
The internal consistency reliability was measured
using Cronbach's alpha and composite reliability, both
of which were at and above 0.70, indicating good
internal consistency. Convergent validity was tested
through Average Variance Extracted (AVE) with a
score value of 0.50 or more considered as good
convergent validity. Outer loadings were also
researched to evaluate the impact of each indicator to
each construct.
2.8 Structural Model and Hypothesis Testing
The standardized path coefficient, t, p, confidence
interval and coefficient of determination were used to
evaluate the structural model. The statistical
significance of the relationship between the
constructs was determined by bootstrapping, which
was available in the data set. A p-value of 0.05 was
used for the significance level. Direct and indirect
relations between AI adoption, business model
innovation, sustainable competitive advantage and
firm performance were explored.
2.9 Model Fit and Predictive Assessment
The Standardized Root Mean Square Residual (SRMR)
and coefficient of determination (R²) values were
used to evaluate model adequacy. Total model fit was
evaluated using SRMR, and the proportion of variance
explained in endogenous constructs was evaluated
using R² values. These measures revealed evidence of
the explanatory and predictive power of the proposed
model.
2.10 Statistical Software
The analytical method used was PLS-SEM with PLS-
SEM Algorithm and Bootstrapping outputs from the
attached data set. These outputs were used to validate
reliability, validity, path coefficient, significance level,
indirect effect, model fit and explanatory power.
2.11 Ethical Considerations
The study was based on a secondary data set that was
publicly available and required no direct interaction
with people. New personal data or identifiable
information was never collected. Construct definitions
and statistical output were kept as they were in the
original document to ensure transparency and avoid
misrepresentation. The adoption of general AI was not
viewed as a direct indicator of specific generative AI
platforms, and government support was not equal to
full internal AI governance.
3. Results
The outcomes of the PLS-SEM were analysed
constructed on the measurement model, explanatory
power, structural path coefficients, effect size and
bootstrapped direct and indirect effects. The analysis
was conducted using 325 observations from the
managers who were part of the “Dataset of the role of
Artificial Intelligence Adoption on Business Model
Innovation, Sustainable Competitive Advantage, and
Firm Performance.” The results below do not reflect a
specific platform usage for GenAI but rather
organizational AI adoption, so the original AI
construct was retained while interpreting the
implications of AI adoption in the context of
generative AI and sustainable entrepreneurship.

4. Discussion
The results indicate that the use of artificial
intelligence has a meaningful impact on innovation,
sustainable competitive advantage and firm
performance and that conditions for the adoption of
AI are influenced by leadership and institutional
support. Business model innovation is significantly
linked to the positive relationship between AI
adoption and the ability to redesign value creation,
delivery and operation processes, suggesting that
companies are better able to redesign their processes
and become more innovative when AI is used in
managerial and strategic activities. The outcome
reinforces the overall perception of AI as an enabler to
digitally transform business and develop new value
propositions (Di Vaio et al., 2020). Also, it resonates
with recent research that suggests AI-enabled
business model innovation can enhance
organizational adaptability and strategic renewal,
especially for companies aiming for growth that is
sustainable in nature (Jorzik et al., 2024a; Jorzik et al.,
2024b). These findings are relevant from a GenAI
perspective as there is a growing awareness of the
support that generative systems can provide to
ideation, content creation, scenario development and
fast experiments in business model development
(Kanbach et al., 2024).
The solid positive connection between AI adoption
and sustainable competitive advantage also indicates
that businesses can translate AI capability into
sustainable advantage. A finding that is consistent
with evidence showing that AI can enhance the use of
resources, decision quality, innovation capacity and
organizational responses, which can impact the
competitiveness of organizations that are focused on
sustainability (Bickley et al., 2025). It also
complements the idea that business model innovation
can be a key avenue for AI's role in sustainable
competitive advantage for companies (Islam et al.,
2025). The indication of the positive impact of
sustainable competitive advantage on firm
performance confirms this interpretation, as firms
achieve the benefits of their technological adoption
when it is manifested as a capability that is hard for
other firms to replicate.
The most robust direct predictor of firm performance
was business model innovation. This finding
underscores the importance of not just adopting the
technology but also aligning business processes and
strategic models with the technology, to reap the
value of AI. The importance of AI capabilities
supporting organizational creativity and performance
has also been highlighted by previous studies, which
have linked AI capabilities to organizational resources
and managerial practices (Mikalef & Gupta, 2021).
This potential for AI's indirect impact on firm
performance through business model innovation
further bolsters the case for a capability-based lens on
understanding the adoption of AI. AI is not just about
creating value; it's about enabling innovation,
flexibility and new value creation strategies.
The support provided by leadership also
demonstrated a strong positive effect on the adoption
of AI, further emphasizing the role of managerial
backing in digital transformation. This finding aligns
with the automation–augmentation view, which holds
that management judgment plays a vital role in
deciding the role of AI in augmenting human skills
instead of replacing them (Raisch & Krakowski, 2021).
In sustainable entrepreneurship, leadership is crucial,
as managers need to balance the requirements of
innovation, employee acceptance, resource
investment, and long-term sustainability goals
(Ribeiro & Leitão, 2024). Additionally, the indirect
relationship between leadership and performance
shown by the other variables suggests that leadership
plays a role in performance beyond the direct effect of
managerial decisions, as it is also a factor that enables
and supports technology use and organizational
change, two of the other variables in this study.
The external institutional environment was positively
related to adoption of AI, leadership support and cost
effectiveness, suggesting that government support can
drive the readiness of organizations for emerging
technologies. These results align with the general
theme of the literature, which highlights the role of
policy, infrastructure, regulatory and institutional
factors in shaping AI adoption (Dwivedi et al., 2021).
Government support is not to be confused with
internal corporate governance, however. In addition
to mechanisms for accountability, transparency,
responsibility, risk oversight, and ethical technology
management, organizational AI governance also needs
to include mechanisms for handling ethical
technology management (Mäntymäki et al., 2022). As
GenAI becomes more widespread, and raises new
questions about data usage, machine-generated
content, decision accountability, and machine
deployment (Dwivedi et al., 2023), these
considerations become particularly relevant.

The results show that the implementation of AI in the
context of sustainable entrepreneurship is based on a
more complex system, including technologies,
leadership support, innovations, institutional
conditions, and sustainable competitive capacity. The
findings, therefore, underscore the fact that the
strategic potential of AI is not just about adoption, but
about transforming it into novel business models,
lasting competitive advantages, responsible
governance and better organizational performance.
5. Conclusion
The study contributes to sustainable entrepreneurial
and leadership changes with AI in a meaningful way in
the presence of effective leadership, innovation
capability and favorable institutional conditions. The
results indicated that these characteristics of the
implemented AI directly contributed to business
model innovation, sustainable competitive advantage,
and firm performance, with business model
innovation as the most significant of these direct
contributions to firm performance. Sustainable
competitive advantage also had a significant positive
influence, suggesting that the value of AI in terms of
strategic benefits is intensified when technological
benefits are translated into some sort of sustainable
and hard-to-imitate organizational benefits. Support
from leadership was also a strong enabling factor,
which was supportive of AI adoption and helped
indirectly achieve outcomes of innovation and
sustainability. The effects of government support on
adoption, leadership support, and cost-effectiveness
were additional influences on AI readiness. The
results indicate that the value creation process is not
just a technology problem; it is a process that depends
on organizational and institutional contexts in which
it is adopted. In the broader context of generative AI,
these findings highlight how companies can enhance
sustainable entrepreneurship by combining AI tools
and techniques with strategic leadership, innovative
business models and responsible governance
practices. Market responsiveness and value creation
were not measured directly, but the improvements
observed in innovation, competitiveness and
performance suggest that marketing excellence was
also observed, with implications for the use of GenAI.
In conclusion, the key takeaway for achieving
sustainable benefits from AI is responsible adoption,
robust leadership, and effective technology capability
to achieve strategic benefits.
