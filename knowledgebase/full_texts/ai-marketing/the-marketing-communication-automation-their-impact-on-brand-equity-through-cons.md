Abstract

As digital natives, Gen Z consumers are highly familiar with automated
communication marketing tools such as chatbots, email automation, interactive voice
response (IVR), and personalized advertisements. This study examines the impact of
marketing communication automation on brand equity through the lens of consumer
experience, focusing on Generation Z (Gen Z) consumers in Thailand. Using a quantitative
research approach, data were collected from 432 Gen Z respondents aged 18 –28
through an online questionnaire. Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) was employed to
analyze the relationships between marketing communication automation, consumer
experience, and brand equity.  The results indicate that the proposed model
demonstrates a good fit with the empirical data (Chi -square = 25.744, p = .421, Chi -
square/df = 1.030, GFI = .932, AGFI = .877, RMSEA = .020). The findings reveal that
marketing commu nication automation has a significant positive effect on consumer
experience, which in turn significantly enhances brand equity, confirming the mediating
role of consumer experience. The findings provide empirical support suggesting that,
within the Thai context, the integration of marketing communication automation could
be a relevant strategic consideration for businesses targeting Gen Z consumers.
Keywords: Marketing Communication Automation, Brand Equity, Consumer Experience,
Generation Z.

become a standard tool for marketers to achieve various objectives. One impor tant
application of AI in marketing communication is marketing communication automation,
which enables automated interactions between firms and customers. For example,
chatbots are commonly used to autonomously handle basic customer inquiries in both

The integration of technology into marketing operations takes various forms, with
marketing communication automation being one of the mos t widely used tools for
consumer communication. This technology plays a crucial role in organizational success
by enabling firms to manage customer interactions more efficiently and effectively
(Gibbons, 2025). According to Boozary et al. (2024), marketing  communication
automation allows businesses to maintain continuous and targeted communication with
customers. Moreover, it enhances the online shopping experience by delivering timely
and relevant responses through personalized content, product recommendat ions, and
exclusive discounts. Similarly, Gavrila et al. (2023) found that the automation of
marketing-related processes provides significant benefits for businesses, particularly by
supporting investments aimed at improving consumer satisfaction.
Despite its advantages, automated marketing communication also presents
challenges related to maintaining a human touch in interactions, ensuring data privacy,
and avoiding excessive consumer disturbance. Consumer experiences with automated
communication can vary depending on the type of technology employed and the
manner in which information is delivered (Rust & Huang, 2014). When automated
communication is not designed in alignment with consumer expectations, it may lead
to negative experiences, which can adversely affect brand image and weaken long-term
brand loyalty (Homburg et al., 2015). These issues highlight the importance of customer
experience as a key factor in determining whether marketing communication automation
generates positive or negative outcomes for brands.
Marketing communication automation can enhance customer experience by
providing timely, consistent, and personalized interactions across multiple customer
touchpoints. Such positive experiences contribute to favorable consumer perceptions,
strengthen emotional connections with the brand, and ultimately support the
development of brand equity. In this regard, customer experience serves as an important
mechanism through which marketing communication automation can influence long -
term brand value.
Beresford Research (2025) defines Generation Z (Gen Z) as individuals born
between 1997 and 2012. According to Kotler et al. (2023), Gen Z consumers are highly
familiar with digital technology and are among the first consumer groups to experience
marketing communication automation. This generation represents a particularly
important market segment, as Gen Z consumers are expected to become a high -
purchasing-power group in the near future.
Although marketing communication automation has been widely adopted i n
many countries, its implementation in Thailand remains relatively limited, despite
showing continuous growth. Several factors contribute to this slower adoption, including

among Gen Z consumers within the Thai context. The findings are expected to enhance
marketers’ understanding of how marketing communication automation can be
strategically utilized to improve customer experience and strengthen brand equity.

Literature review
 Marketing Communication Automation
 Cybellium (2024) defines marketing automation as "the use of technology
platforms and software designed to perform repetitive marketing tasks, streamline
complex marketing processes, and ultimately improve efficiency and effectiveness in
running marketing campaigns." The key marketing automation tools for communication
include chatbots, email, interactive voice response (IVR), and personalized ads (Gabric,
2023; Sean, 2026; Moyer, 2023).
 Chatbot
Crowder and Carbone (2023) explain that a chatbot  is a software applic ation
intended to conduct online conversations, either textually or with text -to-speech
capabilities that simulate human conversations and are used to provide some service
(even if that service is entertainment) to the user . Chatbots are designed to engage  in
personalized conversations with consumers, providing support and answering inquiries,
which enhances communication experiences between businesses and customers.

 Email Automation
Donnelly (2025) emphasizes that automated email marketing is a highly powerful
tool for customer engagement and business expansion, making it an essential asset for
enhancing marketing performance. The email marketing is a vital tool in digital marketing,
helping businesses enhance the effectiveness of their marketing campaigns. When used
strategically, it can engage customers, drive conversions, and strengthen brand loyalty.
However, inappropriate email marketing practices —such as excessive messaging,
irrelevant content, or lack of personalization —can annoy consumers and lead to
dissatisfaction. (Ameer, 2024).
 Interactive Voice Response (IVR)
 Masjedi ( 2024) states that Interactive voice response (IVR) is an automated
telecommunication technology that interacts with callers through voice or touch -tone
input. It uses pre-recorded messages, speech recognition, and dynamic menu options to
guide users to desired information or services. Interactive Voice Response (IVR) serves as
the initial point of contact when customers reach out, making it a powerful tool for
driving and enhancing the customer service experience. Survey findings indicate that, on
average, 73% of customers form their first impression based on their interaction with IVR
systems. (Masjedi, 2024)
 Personalized Advertising
 Charlesworth (2018) stated that personalized advertising refers to the
communication of advertisements to consumers, where each consumer rec eives
different marketing messages. Personalized advertising is sent to consumers by utilizing
differences in consumer data, such as demographic information, psychographic
information, behavioral information, location -based information, and content
information (Rowles, 2017; Morimoto, 2017). According to the study by Kedi et al. (2024),
it was found that personalized marketing can help improve sales efficiency and return
on investment (ROI)
Brand Equity
 Aaker (2004) stated that brand equity refers to the set of assets and liabilities
associated with a brand, its name, and symbols, which either add to or subtract from
the value provided by the company's products or services to customers.  Customer-
based brand equity, which is defined as having key components including brand image,
brand loyalty, and purchase intention (Chitale & Gupta, 2010; Keller, 1993). Allen (2024)
states that brand equity is important to businesses because it influences consumer
behavior, such as driving purchases. If brand equity is  strong, consumers will be less
sensitive to price and more willing to pay a premium. Additionally, they are likely to

 Consumer Experience
 Kotler et al. (2021) define Customer experience as the overall perception and
feeling that customers have about a brand throughout their entire journey. This includes
all touchpoints from brand discovery and purchase decisions to product usage and post-
purchase support. Customer experience plays a crucial role in customer loyalty and
competitive advantage. Companies that deliver a positive and seamless experience are
more likely to retain customers and generate pos itive word -of-mouth.Consumer
experience with marketing communication includes Convenience, Accurate, and Hedonic
aspects (Strycharz et al., 2019; Kyguoliene et al., 2017). However, Williams ( 2015)
summarized that customer experience can be divided into two  categories: functional
experience and emotional experience.
Consumer experience is widely recognized as a multidimensional construct
encompassing consumers’ cognitive and affective responses that arise from interactions
with products, services, and brands  across various touchpoints. Prior studies suggest
that consumer experience can be conceptualized through two key dimensions:
functional experience and emotional experience. Functional experience refers to
consumers’ cognitive evaluations of the functional  aspects of an offering, such as
perceived usefulness, efficiency, reliability, and performance during service encounters.
These evaluations influence consumers’ judgments regarding the practicality and value
of the service provided (Schmitt, 2010; Lemon & Verhoef, 2016).
In contrast, emotional experience captures consumers’ affective responses
generated throughout the interaction process, including feelings, moods, enjoyment,
and emotional attachment toward the brand. Emotional experience plays a critical role
in shaping c onsumers’ overall perceptions and post -consumption responses, as
emotional reactions often extend beyond rational assessments and influence attitudes
and behavioral intentions (Schmitt, 2010 ). Together, functional and emotional
experiences provide a holist ic understanding of consumer experience, as both
dimensions jointly shape consumers’ evaluations of brand -related interactions and
contribute to long -term relational outcomes such as brand equity (Lemon & Verhoef,
2016).

In 2025, Generation Z consumers are defined as individuals aged between 13 and
28 years. However, to avoid collecting data from vulnerable groups, this study included
only respondents aged 18 years and above. Consumers under 18 years old were
excluded because they are less exposed to marketi ng communication automation and
generally lack sufficient purchasing power. A screening question was included at the
beginning of the questionnaire to ensure that all respondents met the selection criteria.
The data collection process was conducted over a two-month period using an online
survey platform. Convenience and snowball sampling techniques were applied by
distributing the questionnaire through social media channels commonly used by Gen Z
consumers. A total of 432 valid responses were obtained, whic h exceeds the minimum
recommended sample size for Structural Equation Modeling (SEM). According to Ho
(2014), the minimum sample size should be at least 20 times the number of observed
variables. As this study includes 13 observed variables, a minimum of 2 60 samples was
required. Therefore, the final sample size of 432 respondents was considered adequate
for SEM analysis.
Prior to data analysis, data pre -processing was conducted. The researcher
screened the data by selecting only responses from Gen Z consumers aged between 18
and 28 years who completed all questionnaire items. Outliers were examined using
standardized values, and responses exceeding the acceptable threshold were removed
to ensure data quality and normality.
The quality of the questionnaire was assessed in terms of validity and reliability.
Content validity was evaluated by three experts who assessed the consistency and
relevance of the questionnaire items with the research objectives. The results indicated
that all items had Item -Objective Congruence (IOC) scores greater than 0.5. Reliability
was assessed using Cronbach’s alpha coefficient. The results showed that the marketing
communication automation construct had a Cronbach’s alpha of 0.820, customer
experience had a Cronbach’s alpha of 0.913, and brand equity had a Cronbach’s alpha
of 0.841, indicating acceptable reliability for all constructs.
The measurement items for marketing communication automation were adapted
from prior studies on marketing automation and digital communication syste ms.
Customer experience items were adopted from established scales in customer
experience and service marketing literature. Brand equity items were derived from well-
established brand equity measurement frameworks in marketing research. All items were
measured using a five-point Likert scale ranging from strongly disagree to strongly agree.
Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) was used to analyze the data and test the
hypothesized relationships. The statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS for
preliminary analysis and AMOS for SEM analysis.

Conclusion and Discussion
The findings of this study indicate that Marketing Communication Automation has
a significant positive influence on Customer Experience among Gen Z consumers,
encompassing both Functional Experience and Emotional Experience. This result can be
explained by the technological familiarity of Gen Z, who are highly accustomed to digital
platforms and automated communication systems. As a result, marketing
communication automation serves as an effective mechanism for enhancing their overall
experience with brands.
In terms of Functional Experience, marketing communication automation
enhances convenience, improves the accuracy and timeliness of information delivery,
and enables personalized communication that ali gns with individual consumer needs.
These functional benefits help reduce effort and increase efficiency in consumer–brand
interactions. Simultaneously, automation also contributes to Emotional Experience by
fostering positive feelings, creating a sense of comfort, and leaving favorable impressions
through consistent and responsive communication. These findings are consistent with
prior studies suggesting that user -friendly automation systems facilitate smoother
interactions and enhance customer experience (Adam et al., 2020; Coskun -Setirek &
Tanrikulu, 2019).
Moreover, the results demonstrate that Customer Experience derived from
Marketing Communication Automation has a positive impact on Brand Equity, including
Brand Loyalty, Brand Image, and Purchase Inte ntion. For Gen Z consumers, who
frequently interact with brands through digital and automated channels, positive
experiences generated through automation strengthen emotional attachment to brands,
enhance brand perceptions, and encourage repeat purchase be havior. This finding
supports previous research indicating that customer experience plays a crucial role in
shaping purchasing behavior, brand loyalty, and brand image (Mailgun, 2024). Overall,
the results confirm the mediating role of Customer Experience in linking Marketing
Communication Automation to Brand Equity.

Application of Research Findings
 Although the adoption of marketing communication automation in Thailand
remains relatively limited compared to Western markets, this study provides empirica l
evidence that such automation can effectively enhance customer experience among
Gen Z consumers and, in turn, strengthen brand equity. While Thai consumers have
traditionally been perceived as valuing personal interaction, the findings suggest that

Gen Z consumers respond positively to automated marketing communication when it

Therefore, businesses targeting Gen Z consumers are encouraged to integrate

advertising, firms can improve customer experience, reinforce brand relationships, and
enhance long-term brand equity in an increasingly digital marketplace.

Future Research
 Future research can be extended in several directions.
1. Future studies may expand the scope of the sample to include consumer groups
from other generations, such as Generation Y or Generation X, to enhance the
generalizability of the findings.
2. Future research may consider employing probability sampling techniques to
further strengthen sample representativeness and reinforce the robustness of
empirical findings.
3. Further studies may focus on specific product or service categories to provide
deeper insights into the role of marketing communication automation across
different consumption contexts.
