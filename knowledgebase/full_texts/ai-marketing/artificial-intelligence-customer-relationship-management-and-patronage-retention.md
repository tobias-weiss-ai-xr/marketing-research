Abstract
The infusion of artificial intelligence into customer relationship management practice has
reshaped the way real estate businesses engage with their clientele, and yet solid empirical
evidence connecting AI -driven customer relationship management to patronage retention
within emerging African markets is still hard to come by. This paper investigates how AI -
enabled customer relationship management shapes patronage retention among real estate
enterprises operating in Lagos, Nigeria. Drawing on the Technology Acceptance Model
together with relationship marketing theory, the study surveyed 202 marketing practitioners
drawn from 30 established real estate firms across Lagos Metropolis. Regression analysis
revealed that AI -powered customer relationship management capability exerts a strong,
positive influence on patronage retention (β = 0.671, p < 0.001), accounting for 45% of the
variance in retention outcomes. In particular, real -time engagement, personalized
communication, and automated response systems stood out as the strongest contributors to
sustained patronage. These results suggest that real estate firms that invest in AI tools
capable of delivering seamless, personalized, and prompt customer interactions are better
placed to cultivate durable customer relationships. This paper contributes to the still -limited
body of work on AI-CRM applications within African real estate markets and offers practical
direction for firms looking to harness technology as a source of competitive advantage.

Introduction
As Nigeria's commercial capital, Lagos hosts a real estate industry marked by fierce
competition, rapid urban growth, and customers whose expectations grow more sophisticated
by the year. With projections placing the city among the world's twenty largest megacities by
2050 (Lagos State Government, 2020), real estate firms face growing pressure to set
themselves apart through stronger customer engagement practices. Conventional approaches
to customer relationship management, built around manual processes and one -size-fits-all
communication, have increasingly fallen short of meeting the expectations of today's tech -
savvy property buyers and investors. Valued in the billions of naira, the Lagos real estate
market draws both domestic and international investors, producing a fiercely competitive

The wave of digital transformation sweeping across industries has reshaped how businesses
relate to their customers at a fundamental level. Within real estate, where property
transactions carry significant financial weight and unfold over lengthy decision -making
periods, the strength of customer relationships often determines whether a business thrives.
Customers now expect personalized attention, quick responses, and seamless experiences
across every point of contact expectation that conventional CRM systems, dependent on
manual data entry and generic communication templates, often fail to satisfy (Chaffey &
Ellis-Chadwick, 2019). This widening gap between what customers expect and what
organizations can deliver has created a pressing need for more advanced approaches to
managing customer relationships.  Artificial intelligence has emerged as a transformative
force within customer relationship management across a wide range of industries (Sharma &
Patel, 2020; Huang & Benyoucef, 2017). AI -powered CRM systems allow firms to automate
routine work, personalize interactions, and engage customers instantly capabilities that carry
particular weight in a sector as high -stakes and relationship -driven as real estate. These
systems draw on machine learning algorithms to analyze customer behaviour, anticipate
preferences, and deliver tailored communication at scale. For real estate firms, this might
mean chatbots handling initial enquiries around the clock, predictive analytics matching
properties to client preferences, and automated email sequences nurturing leads through
drawn-out sales cycles (Kim et al., 2020; Zhang & He, 2021).
Despite AI's growing uptake worldwide, empirical evidence from emerging African markets
remains sparse (Gakuru & Njoroge, 2020; Adjei & Mensah, 2020). Much of the existing
research has centred on developed economies, leaving a considerable gap in understanding of
how AI -powered CRM performs in settings shaped by distinct infrastructural constraints,
uneven digital literacy, and different cultural dynamics. As Africa's largest city and a major
technology hub, Lagos offers a particularly fitting setting in which to examine these
dynamics. Its real estate market combines the complexity typical of a major metropolitan area
with characteristics distinctive of an emerging economy, offering useful insight into AI's
potential to reshape customer relationships in comparable settings across the continent.
Patronage retention stands as a critical outcome for real estate firms, given that repeat
business and referrals underpin sustainable growth. Customers who remain loyal not only
generate recurring revenue but also act as brand ambassadors in a market where trust and
reputation carry substantial weight (Oliver, 1999). Within real estate, a retained client may
generate several transactions over the course of a lifetime — from a first home purchase to
subsequent upgrades, investment properties, and referrals passed along to family and friends.
The economic value bound up in such retained patronage far exceeds that of any single
transaction, making the cultivation of lasting customer relationships a strategic priority.
Yet the precise mechanisms through which AI -powered customer relationship management
shapes patronage retention remain largely unexplored, particularly within the Lagos real
estate context. While earlier studies have looked at AI's effect on customer satisfaction and
engagement, its connection to retention a deeper and more enduring outcome calls for further
investigation. This study addresses that gap by examining how AI -powered customer
relationship management capability affects patronage retention among real estate firms in
Lagos. Specifically, it investigates whether AI -powered customer relationship management
shapes patronage retention within the Lagos real estate sector, and which dimensions of AI -
enabled interaction most effectively cultivate lasting patronage. In doing so, the study
contributes both to academic literature and managerial practice by generating empirical
evidence of AI's role in building patronage retention within an emerging African market.

Literature Review
AI-Powered Customer Relationship Management
Customer relationship management refers to the strategies, processes, and technologies
organizations rely on to manage their dealings with customers across a range of touchpoints
(Kumar et al., 2021). Bringing artificial intelligence into these systems has fundamentally
altered how firms relate to their clients. AI-powered customer relationship management spans
capabilities such as real -time engagement, personalized communication, automated
responses, and intelligent handling of customer queries (Gómez et al., 2020).
AI technologies including machine learning algorithms, natural language processing, and
predictive analytics allow organizations to analyze vast quantities of customer data, spot
patterns, and deliver tailored experiences at scale (Akter et al., 2021). Within real estate, AI -
powered relationship management tools include chatbots that field initial enquiries,
automated email sequences that nurture leads, and intelligent recommendation systems that
suggest properties matching customer preferences (Kim et al., 2020; Zhang & He, 2021). The
strength of AI -powered relationship management lies in its capacity to deliver consistent,
timely, and personalized communication qualities that matter enormously in real estate,
where purchasing decisions are high -involvement and deeply relationship -dependent (Ali &
El-Sayed, 2021). By automating routine tasks and providing instant responses, AI tools free
up human agents to concentrate on the more complex interactions that call for emotional
intelligence and negotiation skill.

Patronage Retention
Patronage retention describes a deeply rooted commitment to consistently repurchase from or
continue patronizing a preferred product or service in the future, notwithstanding situational
pressures and competing marketing efforts capable of triggering switching behaviour (Oliver,
1999). It spans both behavioural dimensions (repeat purchases) and attitudinal dimensions (a
favourable disposition toward the brand) (Dick & Basu, 1994).
Within the real estate industry, patronage retention shows up in a number of ways: repeat
transactions with the same firm, referrals to friends and family, positive reviews shared
online, and continued engagement with the brand that extends beyond any single transaction
(Jain & Sinha, 2021). Retained customers carry considerable economic value, given that
holding on to existing customers costs substantially less than winning new ones, and that
retained customers tend to spend more while generating favourable word -of-mouth
(Reichheld & Sasser, 1990).  The drivers of patronage retention have received extensive
scholarly attention, with customer satisfaction, trust, perceived value, and relationship quality
consistently identified as key contributors (Morgan & Hunt, 1994; Kumar & Shah, 2021).
That said, the part played by technology -enabled relationship management in cultivating
retention remains an emerging area of inquiry, particularly where AI adoption is concerned.

Theoretical Review
Technology Acceptance Model
The Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), put forward by Fred Davis in 1989, ranks among
the most influential theoretical frameworks for making sense of technology adoption and
usage behaviour. TAM holds that two core beliefs govern an individual's acceptance and use
of new technology: perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use. Perceived usefulness
captures the degree to which a person believes a given system will enhance their job
performance or productivity. Perceived ease of use refers to how far a person believes that
using a system will be free of unnecessary difficulty or effort. These two beliefs shape
attitudes toward using the technology, which then feed into behavioural intentions and,

TAM's simplicity, coupled with its strong empirical track record, has made it a cornerstone
theory within information systems research, applied across settings as varied as e -commerce,
healthcare, education, and customer relationship management. Its central premise — that
people gravitate toward technologies they find beneficial and easy to use — has held up
consistently across decades of empirical study. The model has, nonetheless, evolved over
time to incorporate additional factors such as social influence, facilitating conditions, and
individual variation, through various extensions and adaptations.
For the purposes of this study, TAM offers a valuable lens through which to understand how
real estate marketing professionals come to adopt AI -powered customer relationship tools.
Whether AI -CRM systems succeed in practice depends not solely on their technical
capability but on marketing staff's willingness to weave these tools into their daily work.
When marketing professionals see AI relationship -management tools as genuinely useful for
strengthening customer relationships and streamlining their own workload, they are
considerably more inclined to embrace them wholeheartedly. Likewise, when such tools are
built with intuitive interfaces and demand little training, perceived ease of use gives adoption
a further boost. This adoption, in turn, shapes how effectively customers experience AI -
mediated interactions, which ultimately determines retention outcomes. TAM therefore
accounts for both the organizational process behind adopting the technology and the resulting
customer experience that shapes retention.

Relationship Marketing Theory
Relationship marketing theory took shape as a paradigm shift within marketing thought
during the 1990s, pushing back against the transaction -centred focus that had long dominated
marketing practice. Pioneered by scholars such as Morgan and Hunt (1994) and Grönroos
(1994), relationship marketing holds that marketing's central aim should be building and
sustaining long -term, mutually beneficial relationships with customers, rather than
maximizing individual transactions. This view recognizes that acquiring new customers costs
considerably more than retaining existing ones, and that long -standing customers generate
greater value through repeat purchases, favourable word -of-mouth, and a willingness to pay
premium prices.  At the core of relationship marketing theory sits the commitment -trust
framework, which treats trust and commitment as the central mediating variables in
successful relationship development. Trust refers to the confidence one party places in the
reliability and integrity of the other, while commitment reflects a lasting desire to preserve a
valued relationship. When trust and commitment are both present, they encourage
cooperative behaviour, reduce uncertainty, and produce relationship outcomes that benefit
both sides. Communication quality, shared values, and investment in the relationship all serve
as antecedents that help build these essential relational foundations.
AI-powered customer relationship management aligns closely with the principles of
relationship marketing in several important respects. First, AI systems support consistent,
personalized communication across multiple touchpoints, signalling the firm's commitment to
understanding and serving each customer's particular needs. Second, AI's capacity to
remember customer preferences, anticipate needs on the basis of behavioural patterns, and
deliver timely, relevant responses builds trust by conveying reliability and attentiveness.
Third, AI-facilitated interactions create a sense of relationship investment — customers read
personalized service as evidence that the firm values their business and is prepared to commit
resources to understanding them. Through these mechanisms, AI -powered relationship
management cultivates the emotional bonds and cognitive commitment that constitute
genuine patronage retention, turning transactional exchanges into lasting relationships.

Empirical Review
Empirical work on AI -powered customer relationship management has expanded
considerably in recent years, spanning a wide range of geographic contexts and industry
sectors. This review draws together findings from studies examining AI -CRM applications
and their bearing on customer outcomes, paying particular attention to real estate and related
service industries.  Zhang and Li (2020) carried out a comprehensive investigation of AI -
driven CRM systems within China's real estate sector, combining survey data from 150 real
estate firms with in-depth interviews of marketing executives. Their findings showed that AI -
enhanced customer segmentation and personalized marketing meaningfully improved
customer satisfaction and retention. The study placed particular emphasis on the value of
predictive analytics in flagging customers at risk of churn and enabling proactive
intervention. In a related vein, Ahmad and Parsa (2021) examined AI -powered CRM
adoption among real estate firms in the United Arab Emirates, drawing on a quantitative
survey of 200 marketing professionals. Their results pointed to significant gains in customer
engagement and lead conversion through automated follow -up systems and predictive lead -
scoring algorithms. The study also noted that AI tools cut response times from hours down to
minutes, a decisive factor within the fast-moving UAE property market.
Within the African context, Gakuru and Njoroge (2020) explored AI applications in customer
service across Kenya's real estate market using qualitative interviews with 25 real estate
firms. Their research found that chatbots and virtual assistants markedly improved response
times and customer satisfaction, particularly for initial enquiries and routine information
requests. The study highlighted how AI automation allowed Kenyan real estate firms to scale
up customer service operations without a proportional rise in staffing costs. Adjei and
Mensah (2020) examined AI -powered CRM for customer retention in Ghana through a
survey of 120 real estate professionals. Their findings showed that automated follow -up
systems and personalized property recommendations considerably strengthened client
retention, with customers reporting stronger emotional ties to firms that used AI to
demonstrate attentiveness.  Beyond real estate, studies in related service industries add
complementary insight. Pasić and Jovanović (2020) investigated AI -powered CRM systems
within Serbia's hospitality industry, finding that AI -enabled personalization and automated
service delivery improved both customer satisfaction and operational efficiency.
Bayo and Olamide (2020) studied AI applications within Nigeria's telecommunications
sector, showing that AI -powered customer segmentation and automated communication
reduced churn by allowing firms to intervene at the right moment. Ali and El -Sayed (2021)
examined AI adoption within Egyptian real estate, finding that AI tools improved the
accuracy of lead qualification and allowed sales resources to be deployed more efficiently.
Despite this expanding body of research, a considerable gap persists in the literature. Most
existing studies treat customer satisfaction, engagement, or retention as surface -level
outcomes, without directly examining the deeper construct of patronage retention. Moreover,
the specific mechanisms through which AI -powered customer relationship management
shapes retention remain largely unexplored, particularly within the Nigerian context. This
study addresses these gaps by directly examining the relationship between AI -powered
relationship management and patronage retention, drawing on empirical evidence from the
Lagos real estate sector.

Methodology
This study adopted a quantitative, cross -sectional survey design to examine the relationship
between AI -powered customer relationship management and patronage retention among
Lagos real estate firms. A cross-sectional approach suited the aim of capturing perceptions of
AI capability and retention outcomes at one point in time, allowing the relationships between
the study's variables to be assessed (Creswell & Plano Clark, 2018).
The target population comprised marketing professionals and managers from 30 established
real estate firms operating within Lagos Metropolis. These firms were selected on the basis of
having at least ten years of operational history and a demonstrated track record of engaging
with digital marketing tools. A purposive sampling technique was used to select respondents
with direct knowledge of their organization's customer relationship practices and marketing
performance. Sample size was calculated using Taro Yamane's formula, applied to a
population of 480 marketing professionals spread across the 30 firms. This produced a
sample size of 202 respondents, reached through the administration of structured
questionnaires. The sample drew on professionals from a range of firms, including Adron
Homes, Knight Frank Nigeria, UPDC Plc, and JLL Nigeria, so as to capture representation
across different segments of the market.
The regression model was specified as:

The overall significance of the regression model was assessed using F -statistics and

estate sector. The strong positive relationship uncovered (β = 0.671, p < 0.001), together with

This finding underscores the strategic weight of technology-enabled relationship management
in an industry where customer relationships form the bedrock of sustainable business success.
The size of this effect stands out when set against findings from comparable studies in other
settings. Zhang and Li (2020), examining AI -driven CRM systems within China's real estate

competitive advantages than firms operating in markets where such technologies have
already become commonplace.
The importance of prompt, accurate responses within the Lagos real estate market can be
understood through the lens of transaction complexity and risk. Property purchases rank
among the most significant financial decisions individuals make, involving considerable
sums of money, complex legal documentation, and long-term commitment. Within such high-
stakes settings, customers look to their service providers for reassurance and reliability. AI
tools that deliver immediate, accurate information help reduce uncertainty and build
confidence — foundational elements in the development of trust (Morgan & Hunt, 1994).
When customers experience consistent, dependable responses to their enquiries, they come to
trust more fully in the firm's competence and reliability, which in turn nurtures retention.
These findings also carry implications for the Technology Acceptance Model as applied
within the real estate context. TAM holds that perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use
jointly determine technology adoption. This study suggests that these perceptions operate on
two levels: among marketing professionals who adopt and implement AI tools, and among
customers who experience AI -mediated interactions. When marketing staff perceive AI tools
as useful and easy to use, those tools are more likely to be implemented well, producing
positive customer experiences in turn. When customers perceive AI interactions as useful —
delivering prompt, accurate information — and easy to use — intuitive and accessible —
they are more inclined to develop favourable attitudes toward the firm, which shapes
retention outcomes. This dual -level application of TAM extends the theory's explanatory
reach beyond user adoption alone to encompass customer experience outcomes as well.

Conclusion
This study examined how AI -powered customer relationship management capability shapes
patronage retention within the Lagos real estate sector. The findings offer robust empirical
support for the conclusion that AI -enabled relationship -management capabilities —
particularly real -time engagement, personalized communication, and automated response
systems — meaningfully strengthen patronage retention. With AI -powered relationship
management accounting for 45% of the variance in retention outcomes, the study
demonstrates that how firms manage customer interactions through AI tools carries
substantial implications for relationship quality.  For real estate firms operating in Lagos and
comparable emerging markets, these findings highlight the strategic value of investing in AI -
powered CRM capability. In an environment where customer expectations continue to climb,
the ability to deliver seamless, personalized, and timely interactions may prove decisive in
building the lasting customer relationships that sustain long -term growth. As AI technologies
continue to advance, real estate firms that thoughtfully integrate these tools into their
customer interaction processes will be better placed to meet customer expectations, cultivate
