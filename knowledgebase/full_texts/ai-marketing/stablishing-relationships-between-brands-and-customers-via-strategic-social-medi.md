Abstract
The study investigates how customer engagement and results in customer-brand relationships are impacted by perceptions of
novel experiences and self-congruence, primarily through social media chatbots in the telecom industry. Using the “three-fac-
tor theory of anthropomorphism”, it investigates customer satisfaction and involvement. Three hundred fifty-three respondents
from Greek telecommunications companies answered a 22-question structured survey disseminated online through Survey
Monkey using convenience sampling. Regarding moderating relationships, the data analysis test 19 hypotheses, of which 16
are supported and three are not supported. The study finds a linearly positive relationship between information-seeking and
a nonlinearly positive relationship between perceived contingency and consumer engagement in entertainment and social
interaction. Furthermore, brand relational outcomes like brand intimacy and purchase intentions are mediated by consumer
engagement in the relationship between perceived contingency and brand. These conclusions are supported by statistical
linear regression analysis, which provides insightful information for managers and marketing academics. The study challenge
existing assumptions about the role of self-congruence in consumer engagement with brand chatbots.

Introduction
In today’s hyperconnected world, customers expect busi-
nesses to be active and responsive on social media. The last
two years, internet users spent an average of 151 min daily
on social media, increasing from 147 min the previous year.

with many users reporting improved freedom of speech,
communication, and access to information (Statista 2024a).
A successful social media strategy requires an “always-
on” approach, emphasizing the need for a dedicated team
that responds to messages and comments within an hour.
Customer loyalty, trust, and brand success are all improved
by this dedication. Instead of just being present, branding in
today’s participatory culture entails cooperative, two-way

and Wu 2023). Chatbot integration in communication strate-

platforms (Insider Intelligence 2024).

Theoretical framework, research model
and literature support
Frame of reference, the three‑factor theory
of anthropomorphism, and the theoretical model
by Lin and Wu
Perceptions of the environment and human characteristics
are represented by implicit theories, also known as mindsets
(e.g., Murphy and Dweck 2016). Two theories explain per -
sonality traits: entity theory and incremental theory (Song
and Rim 2024). Per entity theorists, personality stability
refers to human traits being fundamentally immutable and
cannot be changed by willpower (Dutta 2024). Accord-
ing to the incremental theory, personality can be altered
because human characteristics are pliable and cultivable.
The effects of entity and incremental theories are similar
when “primed”. Studies on implicit theories that used dif-
ferent combinations of assessments and manipulations have
found similar results.
Mindsets shape cognitive and behavioral patterns. Entity
theorists seek “assurance evidence” for their self-capabil-
ity because they favor easy success and think skills can be
developed with little effort (Murphy and Dweck 2016).
Incremental theorists, on the other hand, emphasize par -
ticipation and effort for personal growth. They believe that
little effort to accomplish goals will not help them move
beyond their comfort zone and realize their full potential
(Mathur and Mahewwarab 2016). Consumer perceptions
of advertising are influenced by implicit theories (Ali and
Sheeraz 2023). People with a “fixed” mindset, for instance,
who think that abilities are innate, are more likely to prefer
advertisements that highlight a product’s inherent qualities
(Han et al. 2019).
On the other hand, people who have a “growth” mind-
set—believing that skills can be improved—react more
favorably to communications that highlight the possibility
of progress (Han et al. 2019). Epley et al. (2007) offer a
psychological explanation of anthropomorphism that sees
it as a mental process instead of a fixed characteristic of
people. They pinpoint two motivating factors that underline
this phenomenon: the desire to build social connections and
the need for competence, which permits effective interaction
with the outside world. People attribute human traits to non-
human entities because of these motivations. The underlying
motivations are like those involved in creating a theory of
mind, even when emotional states and behaviors are attrib-
uted to these entities (Epley 2018).
Epley et al. (2007) proposed a three-factor anthropo-
morphism theory and describe how and why people give
human characteristics to pets, objects, and natural phenom-
ena. Elicited agent knowledge, or the availability of knowl-
edge; effectance motivation, or the desire to be viewed as
an effective social agent; and Sociality motivation, or the
desire to connect and bond with others, are the three psy -
chological determinants upon which it is predicated. The
theory describes four types of independent variables: situ-
ational, cultural, developmental, and dispositional (Epley
et al. 2007). In order to learn more about the needs and pref-
erences of their target audience, marketers can use these
factors to affect how consumers anthropomorphize products
by giving them human characteristics (Karimova and Goby
2020).
However, anthropomorphic AI may make some custom-
ers uneasy, called the “uncanny valley”. According to recent
research, a one-size-fits-all approach is ineffective, and the
level of anthropomorphism should change depending on the
situation (Troshani et al. 2020). Epley et al. (2007) three-
factor theory of anthropomorphism has evolved and has been
the foundation for numerous studies in various domains,
including marketing, social cognition, human-humanized
AI agent interaction, and more (e.g., Klein and Martinez
2023; Lin and Wu 2023).
The theoretical framework of our study is grounded in
the three-factor theory of anthropomorphism proposed by
Epley et al. (2007). This theory posits that individuals attrib-
ute human characteristics to non-human entities based on
three psychological determinants: elicited agent knowledge,
effecting motivation, and sociality motivation (e.g., Jhan
et al. 2025). This framework is particularly relevant in the
context of AI-driven chatbots, where consumers’ perceptions
of these entities can significantly influence their engagement
and satisfaction (e.g., Khan et al. 2025).
Understanding how customers’ perceptions of themselves
influence their interactions with social media brand chat-
bots requires the inclusion of self-congruence as a moderator
(e.g., Alabed et al. 2022). Consumer behavior is strongly
influenced by self-congruence or the alignment of a person’s
perception of themselves with that of a brand. In order to
improve understanding of consumer-brand dynamics, our

The theory questions conventional wisdom regarding
interactions between humans and technology from a prag-
matic standpoint. As stated by Epley et al. (2007), the cogni-
tive process that underlies human interactions is also used
when anthropomorphizing non-human entities, according
to the three-factor theory. Based on people’s understand-
ing of themselves and others, this process makes assump-
tions about the ambiguous inner nature of social partners.
Although it can be difficult to distinguish between egocentric
and homocentric cognitive bases, techniques like mentaliz-
ing and mirroring can provide access to human social cog-
nition. Two human motivations impact this understanding:
sociality motivation, which is the need for social support and
connections, and effectance motivation, which is the desire
for cognitive control.

Perceived contingency and consumer
engagement with social media brand
chatbots
The perceived contingency has a positive indirect impact
on customer satisfaction (Cheng et al. 2015) and consumer
engagement (through information seeking and social inter -
action gratification (Lin and Wu 2023). A key idea in per -
tinent research by Sundar et al. (2016) is that interactive
elements in the brand-customer communication channel
favor customer engagement. Perceived contingency posi-
tively influences customer satisfaction (Cheng et al. 2015)
and consumer engagement through information seeking and
social interaction gratification (Lin and Wu 2023), but it
also faces counterarguments. The psychological aspects of
the Human–Computer Interactive (HCI) process are associ-
ated with perceived contingency. It is important to remember
that although HCI research highlights the subjective element
of engagement, it is also proposed that it usually involves
greater immersion and absorption of the pertinent material
(Agarwal and Karahanna 2000).
Liu (2024) suggests that postponement can enhance
customer satisfaction by increasing perceived benefits and
reducing perceived losses. It aligns with the idea that per -
ceived contingency, which involves expected outcomes
based on actions, can positively affect satisfaction. Lim and
Zhang (2022) conclude that perceived contingency plays a
key role in predicting how AI usage will be impacted by con-
sumer engagement, both directly and indirectly. Yan et al.
(2024) find that uncertainty in service recovery can enhance
post-recovery satisfaction and engagement, especially in
low-severity failure situations. It supports the notion that
perceived contingency can drive engagement by gratifying
interactions. However, according to Hsu and Lin’s (2023a, b)
research, brand loyalty is not directly impacted by perceived
contingency, one of the three elements of AI bot conversa-
tional quality. Interactive elements in brand-customer com-
munication significantly boost engagement. Trends for 2024
highlight the importance of personalized experiences, such
as augmented reality (AR) and virtual reality (VR), which
create immersive interactions (Barta et al. 2025).
Perceived contingency can also lead to complexity and
cognitive overload. Yan et al. (2024) indicate that uncer -
tainty may result in perceived insincerity and reduced satis-
faction in high-severity failure situations. The growing use
of personalized elements raises privacy concerns. Recent
works (e.g., Ban 2024) highlight the necessity of data pro-
tection in personalized communication since improper use
can damage confidence and harm satisfaction. While per -
ceived contingency and interactive elements can increase
customer satisfaction and engagement, their effectiveness
varies based on the circumstances. For these tactics to have
the most significant positive impact, brands must balance
them with complexity, privacy, and authenticity considera-

Perceived contingency, information seeking

Perceived contingency refers to how consumers view chat-
bot interactions as responsive and tailored to their needs.
Research shows that high perceived contingency leads to
greater gratification from information seeking, as respon-
sive chatbots provide timely, relevant information, enhanc-
ing user satisfaction (e.g., Liu et al. 2024). The satisfaction
of using social media brand chatbots to obtain information
was positively correlated with perceived contingency (Lin
and Wu 2023). Consumers interacting with highly contin -
gent chatbots report higher levels of gratification. In 2024,
chatbots became essential in customer service, emphasiz-
ing personalization and responsiveness (Alizadeh and Naz-
arpour Kashani 2024). Consumers expect immediate, rele-
vant responses aligning with perceived contingency. Studies
indicate that adaptable chatbots improve user satisfaction
and gratification.
However, some researchers argue that over-reliance on
chatbots can lead to a lack of human touch, which may
negatively impact consumer satisfaction (e.g., Ashfaq et al.
2020). Despite their rapid reaction times, chatbots may
not be as empathetic or comprehend human interactions.
Because of this, actively seeking information may not pro-
vide as much overall satisfaction. Furthermore, perceived
contingency positively impacts information seeking, which
increases trust and interest in chatbots. However, it may
also hurt information seeking since it lowers consumers’
perceived risk and uncertainty, which may deter them from
seeking more information (He and Rucker 2023).
Telecommunications companies cultivate enduring rela-
tionships with their customers by informing them about
news that impacts their brand (Kalogiannidis et al. 2023).
Accessing and reacting to information at any time and loca-
tion is made convenient by mobile social media tools (Cheng
et al. 2015), guaranteeing that customers must only wait a
brief amount of time. It ensures client satisfaction by pre -
serving and strengthening these connections (Kalogiannidis
et al. 2023). Chatbots need access to more personal informa-
tion as they get more responsive and personalized, which
can cause privacy problems and mistrust from customers.
Perceived contingency and the general satisfaction gained
from interactions may suffer. However, substantial evidence

perceived contingency and social interaction satisfaction
resulting from consumer interactions with chatbots used by
social media brands (e.g., Xiao et al. 2025). The results show
a positive correlation between perceived contingency and
social contact, which raises customer engagement. Social
contact is the most prevalent way users express satisfaction
from interacting with chatbots, indicating that they are moti-
vated to use them by their need for social connection (Lin

According to Qin (2020), brand-consumer interactions on
social media that are motivated by factors like self-identity
and information-seeking promote favorable opinions about
the brand. Chatbots satisfy users’ social and informational
needs by providing contingent responses, which increases
user satisfaction. Gogan et al. (2018) discovered that since
people use social media to satisfy their social needs, social
gratification significantly impacts users’ emotional com-
mitment and level of satisfaction. Among gratifications,
social gratification was the most important component (i.e.,
hedonic and utilitarian) impacting users’ satisfaction on
Weibo, a well-known social media platform in China. Given
that social connection is the main objective of social media,
these findings are not surprising.
On the other hand, Cavusoglu et al. (2025) argue that
while perceived interactivity is crucial, it is not the sole
determinant of user satisfaction and brand trust. Content rel-
evancy and the overall user experience also play significant
roles. It implies that even if a chatbot is highly responsive,
other elements must align to achieve high social interaction
gratification. Kalogiannidis et al. (2023) claim that the abil-
ity of a business to draw in and hold on to devoted clients is
its most valuable asset and is essential to long-term success.
Telecom marketers need to do more than create excellent
products, offer them at competitive prices, and make them
simple for the target market to obtain. To guarantee effective
marketing, they must also engage socially with current and
potential customers. Another study highlights that perceived
social media interactivity impacts brand trust and value,
but the relationship is complex and influenced by multiple
variables (e.g., Gökerik 2024). This complexity suggests
that perceived contingency alone may not guarantee social

incentives are also crucial (e.g., Ghaziabad 2024). The con-

fication significantly increases user satisfaction and encour-

intelligence are critical to user happiness and entertainment
satisfaction. If contingency is overused, these other crucial
components may be overlooked. High levels of perceived

Consumer engagement with social media brand chatbots
refers to consumers’ interactions and connections with
brands through AI-powered chatbots on social media plat-
forms. Mei et al. (2024) highlight that AI chatbots sig-
nificantly enhance consumer engagement by increasing
interaction frequency and improving the quality of user
experiences. The study found a strong positive correlation
between chatbot interactions and overall user satisfaction, a
key component of brand intimacy. Brand intimacy is nec-
essary for consumer-brand interactions to be strong (e.g.,
Guttena et al. 2024). Conversational chatbots can enhance
sentiments of brand intimacy, and social media interaction
strengthens these relationships (Bergner et al. 2023; Hudson
et al. 2016). This cognitive connection result is enhanced
when customers engage more effectively with social media
brand chatbots, according to Lin and Wu (2023), assert-
ing that consumer involvement enhances brand intimacy,
emphasizing the value of social interaction pleasure in build-
ing solid brand relationships.
Furthermore, Wang and Lee (2020) examined the ben-
eficial effects of brand intimacy and consumer engagement.
Having a significant positive impact on brand intimacy,
consumer engagement also mediates the effects of self-per -
ception and advice-seeking on brand intimacy (Wang and
Lee 2020). The idea is that customized chatbot interactions
can increase brand intimacy by providing customers with a
sense of value and understanding (supported by the 2025
Sprout Social Index). The Index highlights the significance
of authentic, original content in creating deeper consumer
connections (Mallick 2025). Barcelos et al. ( 2016) experi-
mented to investigate how consumers’ opinions of a brand’s
Facebook page and their likelihood of using it regularly are
affected by brand intimacy. The level of customer involve-
ment is a key determinant of brand intimacy. Bonding
between the brand and its customers results from a shift
in customer involvement from low to high (Wang and Lee
2020).

point out that managing complicated inquiries can be com-
plex, which irritates customers and diminishes brand inti-
macy. Chatbots can increase interaction, but their drawbacks
must be fixed to preserve good relationship outcomes. San-
tos et al. (2022) point out that perceived social pressure and
trust can impact consumers’ participation in social media
brand communities. Hollebeek et al. (2024) contend that
although AI-powered customer interaction can improve
service delivery, it may also lessen the perceived human

Discussion
Degree of responding to the research objectives
and the support of the initial assumptions
With an emphasis on self-congruence, the study investigated
the connection between Greek users of chatbots from tel -
ecom brands on social media and their perceptions of con-
tingency and consumer satisfaction. It sought to investigate
how gratification mediated the relationship between per -
ceived contingency and consumer engagement and how
engagement affected the results of consumer-brand relation-
ships. The relationships and effects mentioned were revealed
when the objectives were successfully met.
According to the first research objective, perceived con-
tingency and consumer engagement in entertainment and
social interaction have a nonlinear positive relationship,
while information-seeking has a linear relationship. Gratifi-
cation acts as a bridge between perceived contingency and
engagement for the second goal. The third goal identifies a
nonlinearly positive correlation between relational outcomes
(purchase intention, brand intimacy, affective commitment,
and chatbot-related behavioral intention) and consumer
engagement. Finally, all consumer brand relational out-
comes and perceived contingency are mediated by consumer
engagement.
According to the study, social media user engagement
and perceived contingency have a positive correlation, which
improves communication results. Additionally, it confirms
that improved engagement with brand chatbots promotes
affective commitment, mutual understanding, and brand
intimacy. It also emphasizes how consumers actively cre-
ate meaning and how important brand anthropomorphism
is to healthy consumer-brand relationships. Finally, chatbot
engagement specifically increases affective commitment but
does not affect other relational outcomes, even though self-
congruence on social media is crucial for creating consumer
engagement strategies.

Theoretical and research implications
The three-factor theory of anthropomorphism clarifies how
people perceive non-human objects as possessing human
characteristics, such as chatbots from social media compa-
nies. Rands can strategically build chatbots to extract agent
or anthropocentric knowledge by leveraging preexisting
human knowledge and familiarity. When chatbots exhibit
traits, people are accustomed to seeing, they become relat-
able to humans (Haugeland et al. 2022 ). Researchers can
investigate how different levels of anthropocentric knowl-
edge affect users’ perceptions and chatbot effectiveness.
The research implications of the elicited agent knowledge
include analyzing how chatbot responses align with common
human knowledge and behaviors. Whether users prefer chat-
bots that use information that can be identified as coming
from a human requires more investigation (Rese et al. 2020).
In terms of effectance motivation, or behavioral under -
standing, we can see the importance of consumers attempt-
ing to understand and predict the behavior of others.
Anthropomorphic chatbots can meet this need by provid-
ing intelligible and human-like responses. Researchers can
examine the connection between chatbot adoption and satis-
faction and effectance motivation. Therefore, more research
on the impact of transparent chatbot behavior on customer
trust is required, as is an investigation into how consumers
understand chatbot explanations and whether they boost sat-
isfaction (Xu et al. 2023).
Customers are highly motivated by social interaction or
sociality. Anthropomorphic chatbots can serve as social
mediators in situations where in-person interactions are
limited. Researchers could look into how consumers’ per -
ceptions of chatbots are influenced by sociality motivation.
Among the research gaps noted in the literature on inter -
active marketing are evaluations of how the conversational
style of chatbots affects perceived social connection (e.g.,
Tsai et al. 2021a, b). As mentioned in the literature on con-
sumer behavior, more investigation is required to ascertain
whether social motivation influences chatbot adoption and
loyalty (Rajaobelina et al. 2021).
Every situation and culture has a different impact on
anthropomorphism. Researchers can investigate how differ-
ent cultures perceive chatbots. The impact of situational ele-
ments, like task and context, on users’ propensity to human-
ize chatbots must be considered. Regarding the research
implications, there is a need for intercultural studies to inves-
tigate chatbot preferences and effectiveness and analyses of
how specific contexts (like shopping and customer service)
impact anthropomorphism (Blut et al. 2021).
The theory needs expansion to explore how anthropo-
morphism influences user experiences with chatbots, virtual
assistants, and robots, particularly in human–computer inter-
action. A gap in consumer behavior literature requires inves-
tigation into how chatbot design and anthropomorphism
affect consumer trust, satisfaction, and task performance and
how visual appearance and language shape perceptions (e.g.,
Klein and Martinez 2022). Researchers who study marketing
should look into the factors that make chatbots seem less
human-like and consider the inverse process of dehumaniza-
tion, which occurs when consumers dehumanize non-human
entities or others (Fernandes and Oliveira 2021). One of the
most significant research gaps in the literature on consumer
behavior is a thorough mechanism where consumers reject
anthropomorphism and prefer purely functional chatbots—
more research on the effects of dehumanization in customer
interactions with chatbots.
The associated ontological presuppositions of the three
psychological determinants of the theory in the context of
customer-brand relationships impact consumers’ interactions
with brands, primarily through social media brand chatbots
(Batat 2024). Numerous advantages of anthropomorphic
chatbot design can enhance the user experience (Haugeland
et al. 2022). Customer satisfaction, brand attachment, and
brand loyalty can all be raised by social media brand chat-
bots that take advantage of these ontological presumptions.
For example, studies have demonstrated that consumers’
perceptions of the hedonic and utilitarian qualities of chat-
bots positively impact their satisfaction level and fortify their
brand loyalty. Additionally, when anthropomorphism is used

362 R. Polimerou, G. Spais
to position a product as a “partner” rather than a “servant,”
it can raise consumer expectations and lead to discontent
during brand failures (Stockman et al. 2024).
Therefore, as noted by Nguyen et al. (2023), integrating
anthropomorphic chatbots into their social media strategy
effectively can help brands build stronger relationships with
their customers by providing them with more emotionally
fulfilling and engaging experiences. One of the theoretical
ramifications of the anthropomorphism viewpoint of chat-
bots in customer service is discussed. However, because
anthropomorphism can increase expectations and disap-
pointment when services fall short of, brands must exercise
caution when managing these relationships.
Our study challenges existing assumptions about the role
of self-congruence in consumer engagement with brand
chatbots. The implications for theory highlight the need
for a deeper understanding of the dynamics of digital con-
sumer-brand relationships and the importance of focusing
on user experience and engagement strategies that resonate
with consumers in a rapidly evolving digital landscape. The
lack of moderation by self-congruence in the relationships
between consumer engagement with social media brand
chatbots and various relational outcomes (brand intimacy,
chatbot-related behavioral intention, and purchase intention)
has significant theoretical implications.
The role of self-congruence in digital interactions must
be re-evaluated. The three-factor theory of anthropomor -
phism suggests that consumers may attribute human-like
qualities to non-human entities based on their perceptions
of those entities. The finding that self-congruence does not
moderate the relationship between engagement and rela-
tional outcomes challenges the assumption that consumers’
self-concept alignment with a brand or chatbot enhances
their emotional connection or behavioral intentions. Factors
other than self-congruence may be more critical in shaping
consumer perceptions and behaviors in digital contexts (Zhu
et al. 2024).
The second important theoretical implication relates to
the complexity of interactions with customers. Our results
demonstrate that human-chatbot interactions are impacted
by the chatbot’s perceived intelligence, responsiveness,
and usability rather than people’s opinions of themselves.
Because of this complexity, researchers should investigate
additional psychological constructs like trust, perceived
value, and emotional reactions to chatbot interactions that
may impact engagement and relational outcomes (e.g., Zhu
et al. 2023; Tsai et al. 2021a, b).
Third, we need to critically discuss the need for theoreti-
cal frameworks for brand relationships. Self-congruence as
a moderating factor is not supported, which suggests that
social media chatbots function differently than conventional
brand relationships. This calls for a more comprehensive
theoretical framework that considers the special qualities of
digital interactions and how technology shapes relationships
between consumers and brands.
The theoretical and research implications of the interplay
between artificial intelligence (AI), machine learning (ML),
and data analytics in digital marketing and promotions are
vast and multifaceted. Building on the existing discussion
(e.g., Basu et al. 2024; Lakshiha et al. 2024), we can extend
the exploration of these implications by integrating insights
from the systematic literature reviews (SLRs) mentioned,
which highlight the evolving role of AI and ML in shaping
consumer interactions and brand experiences.
The SLR studies emphasize how crucial it is to combine
theories of consumer behavior with AI and ML frameworks.
For instance, studying how AI tools like chatbots satisfy
customer motivations can improve motivation theories like
reflectance and sociality. Researchers ought to look into how
AI affects satisfaction and engagement. Because it can boost
loyalty and trust, anthropomorphism in AI—especially with
chatbots—is essential for consumer-brand relationships.
Future research should examine how different human-like
characteristics in AI affect consumer perceptions.
Situational and cultural factors also considerably influ-
ence customers’ perceptions of AI entities. Cross-cultural
research can aid in understanding how cultural norms affect
how people interact with AI in various markets. Further -
more, even though anthropomorphism enhances experi-
ences, it is important to address the causes of dehumaniza-
tion to balance usefulness and relatability. Brands can use
data for tailored strategies by integrating AI into digital
marketing. There is a need for empirical research on chat-
bot effectiveness, including how design affects customer
satisfaction and loyalty. Consumer perceptions of AI can be
monitored through longitudinal research.
Transparency and data privacy are important ethical
issues in AI-driven marketing. Researchers ought to inves-
tigate these ramifications to preserve consumer confidence.
The development of technologies such as natural language
processing presents more research opportunities to improve
marketing strategies. The interaction of AI, ML, and data
analytics in digital marketing stimulates much theoretical
and empirical research, advancing our knowledge of how
customers behave in a digital marketplace.
Practical and managerial implications
One area of marketing implications is strategic design, where
marketers can purposefully produce brand elements (such as
product names, animations, and appearance) to encourage
anthropomorphism. This perception can be enhanced by giv-
ing an automobile an external design that resembles human

features, such as headlights that resemble eyes or grilles that
resemble mouths. Designing a social media chatbot requires
making marketing decisions. By including characteristics
like friendliness and responsiveness, chatbots should be
made to seem human. Chatbots that look like people may be
more likable to consumers. An anthropomorphic approach to
emotional connection is essential for service industry mar -
keters, as it elicits emotional responses that enhance cus-
tomer attachment and loyalty. A company’s personality and
customer loyalty are vital considerations when managing a
brand. Anthropomorphism affects brand personality. Con-
sumers attribute human qualities (such as friendliness and
competence) to anthropomorphized brands. Furthermore,
anthropomorphism is a positive predictor of brand loyalty.
When consumers feel that brands are human, they are more
likely to make repeat purchases and develop stronger emo-
tional ties with them.
Marketers need to consider the strategic role of self-
perception reflection and how it affects brand management
strategies when analyzing consumer behavior. Consumers
view brands as reflections of themselves. Businesses can
strengthen the anthropomorphic connection by reflecting on
how their clients see themselves. In actuality, brands can
improve brand anthropomorphism by matching their values
and messaging to consumers’ self-concepts.
The strategic use of chatbots for social media brands,
including several strategic components, is the final focus
of our study. Humanization is the first tactical component,
which involves programming miming human comprehen-
sion, empathy, and speech patterns. Additionally, it fosters
a positive relationship between brands and consumers and
improves anthropomorphism. The second component, per -
sonalization, improves the self-perception reflection dimen-
sion by enabling responses that are customized to the cus-
tomer’s preferences. Maintaining brand authenticity is the
third component; marketers should ensure that chatbots and
other touchpoints employ consistent anthropomorphic cues.
The three-factor theory of anthropomorphism posits that
three key factors—sociality, agency, and warmth—drive the
tendency of individuals to attribute human-like character -
istics to non-human entities, such as chatbots. Lin and Wu
(2023) expand on this by exploring the non-linear relation-
ship between perceived contingency (the degree to which a
chatbot appears responsive and adaptive to user input) and
consumer engagement. Here are some practical implications
emphasizing strategies for telecom brands using strategic
social media chatbots: (1) Enhancing perceived contingency.
Creating chatbots that respond dynamically and contextu-
ally can improve the impression that they depend on user
requirements. The chatbot will become more responsive
and human-like by using data analytics to customize inter -
actions based on user history (e.g., Lian et al. 2022). (2)
Harmonizing agency and warmth. Chatbots need to appear
knowledgeable while keeping a friendly demeanor. An
empathetic interaction is fostered when one acknowledges
the user’s feelings, mainly frustration, before offering assis-
tance (e.g., Meng et al. 2023).
(3) To improve engagement, use non-linear engagement
techniques and pinpoint critical points in the customer
journey, such as service interruptions. Over time, chatbot
responses can be improved by putting feedback mechanisms
in place (e.g., Sachdeva et al. 2024). (4) Strategic content
delivery. Use a range of content formats (text, images, and
videos) to maintain user interest. A sense of contingency
can be developed by proactively communicating updates
or suggestions (5) Observation and adjustment. Analyze
engagement metrics regularly to modify tactics. Features
that improve perceived contingency and engagement can be
found through A/B testing. (6) Ongoing education and train-
ing. Putting money into machine learning will help chatbots
respond better over time and guarantee that human agents
can intervene to keep customers happy. By concentrating
on these tactics, telecom companies can use social media
chatbots to improve engagement and fortify customer rela-
tionships by developing a sophisticated grasp of anthropo-
morphic traits and perceived contingency.
The lack of moderation by self-congruence in the rela-
tionships between consumer engagement with social media
brand chatbots and various relational outcomes (brand inti-
macy, chatbot-related behavioral intention, and purchase
intention) has significant practical implications. We must
start by emphasizing the strategic advancement of chat-
bots. According to the findings, brands—especially those
in the telecom industry—should give priority to features
that improve user experience in chatbot design and opera-
tion rather than just aiming to match the chatbot’s persona
with the user’s self-concept. One way might be to improve
the chatbot’s ability to provide relevant information, address
problems quickly, and engage in meaningful dialogue with
users (e.g., Haugeland et al. 2022). The emphasis on engage-
ment metrics is the second intriguing implication. Metrics of
consumer engagement that are directly related to relational
outcomes should be the primary focus of marketers’ efforts.
It could involve analyzing user interactions to identify what
drives engagement and satisfaction rather than assuming that
self-congruence will enhance these relationships.
Third, regarding the tailored communication strategies,
given that self-congruence does not moderate the relation-
ship between engagement and outcomes, brands may need
to adopt more tailored communication strategies that reso-
nate with diverse consumer segments. Adapting interactions
according to user behavior and preferences instead of using a
one-size-fits-all strategy that presumes alignment with self-
concept may be necessary (e.g., Maddah et al. 2024). Fourth,
businesses should keep funding the training and develop-
ment of chatbots to adjust to customers’ wants and needs.

This might entail analyzing customer interactions with
machine learning algorithms to gradually enhance the chat-
bot’s responses and build a closer relationship with users.
Further research and limitations
Much research focuses on consumers’ behavioral intentions
and how to influence them. According to this study’s insight-
ful findings, the strategic use of social media brand chatbots
can impact these intentions. The findings of this study can
be used as a basis for further research by looking at more
aspects of behavioral intentions and giving more specific
information on their impacts. A survey was also conducted
to evaluate the study’s findings and comprehend how cus-
tomers engage with and relate to social media brand chat-
bots. Experimental studies can be used to change particular
variables and confirm the connections between them to carry
out additional research.
The study’s focus on telecommunications goods and
services in the Greek market is a significant factor. Future
studies may concern other industries, cultures, and nations.
Regarding the limitations of our study, the fact that we only
looked at telecommunications products and services in the
Greek market might have limited how broadly our find-
ings can be applied. Although our study provides insightful
information about brand relationships and customer engage-
ment in this particular context, it might not fully capture the
nuances of other sectors or cultural contexts. The telecom-
munications industry is very different from industries like
retail or hospitality because of its distinct consumer behav -
iors influenced by laws and technological developments
(e.g., Sima et al. 2020; Lunn 2013).
Furthermore, cultural factors like individualism versus
collectivism can influence how consumers view brand inter-
actions and anthropomorphism in ways our study did not
consider. Future studies should examine these dynamics
in various cultural and industrial contexts to confirm and
build on our findings. We can learn more about the impact
of social media chatbots and customer-brand relationships
by examining how people view self-congruence and new
experiences in various settings (e.g., Sheng et al. 2024). This
approach can potentially improve the three-factor theory of
anthropomorphism and offer valuable perspectives on cus-
tomer engagement strategies in an increasingly globalized
business environment.
Future research must carefully examine the methodo -
logical flaws in the three-factor theory of anthropomor -
phism, which emphasizes the importance of carefully
implementing anthropomorphic strategies in social media
marketing, even though it provides a valuable framework
for understanding their relationships with brands through
chatbots. The theory may not be generally applicable
across various cultures, age groups, or market segments,
which is the first limitation of generalizability. Perceptions
of anthropomorphy can differ significantly depending on
social and personal factors. Second, it can be challenging
to measure the extent of anthropomorphism and how it
affects the relationships between consumers and brands.
Standardized metrics for evaluating chatbots’ anthropo-
morphic traits are lacking. Third, there is a chance that
people will over-ascribe human characteristics to chat-
bots or brands, which could leave customers with inflated
expectations and possibly disappointment. Fourth, some
customers might be dubious of chatbots’ human-like inter -
actions because they believe they are manipulative or inau-
thentic. Fifth, current chatbot technology may not always
be able to support the intricate human-like interactions
that the theory envisions.
Based on the above theoretical implications outlined
regarding the lack of moderation by self-congruence in con-
sumer engagement with social media brand chatbots, several
areas for future research can be identified: (1) Exploration of
alternative psychological constructs. Future research should
examine psychological factors like trust, perceived value,
emotional reactions, and user satisfaction influencing con-
sumer engagement with brand chatbots. (2) Impact of chat-
bot attributes. Studies could analyze how chatbot character-
istics—such as perceived intelligence, responsiveness, and
usability—affect consumer interactions and enhance user
experience. (3) Comparative studies of digital versus tradi-
tional brand relationships. Research could compare engage-
ment with chatbots against traditional brand interactions to
understand differences in consumer behavior and emotional
connections. (4) Longitudinal studies on consumer-chatbot
relationships. The impact on purchase intentions and brand
loyalty over the long term can be determined by how engage-
ment changes over time. (5) Issues of ethics and customer
trust. Knowing the ethical implications of chatbots, such as
transparency and data privacy, is essential to understanding
how they impact consumer confidence. (6) Including interac-
tion across multiple channels. One way to learn more about
the interactions between brands and consumers is to look at
how chatbots work with other marketing platforms.
Regarding the study methodology, it could be enhanced
in several ways: (1) The results would be more broadly
applicable if a variety of sampling techniques were used,
such as stratified or random sampling (e.g., Berndt 2020).
(2) A long-term study might show how consumer interac-
tion with chatbots changes (e.g., Aggarwal et al. 2023).
(3) Including qualitative techniques like focus groups or
interviews would offer a more in-depth understanding of
consumer experiences (Maga and Bodlaj 2025). (4) Wider
comparisons would be possible if the study was extended to
other industries. More thorough insights into how customers

interact with brand chatbots may result from addressing
these factors.
Lastly, as consumer behavior and social media trends
evolve, anthropomorphic tactics might lose their applicabil-
ity and relevance. The deliberate use of anthropomorphism
presents moral questions regarding the manipulation of con-
sumer attitudes and behavior, which marketing academics
ought to address.
Conclusion
This study investigates the moderating influence of self-con-
gruence and the intermediary role of perceptions of novel
experiences in the relationship between customer engage-
ment and customer-brand outcomes. The study challenge
existing assumptions about the role of self-congruence in
consumer engagement with brand chatbots. The implica-
tions for both theory and practice highlight the need for a
deeper understanding of the dynamics of digital consumer-
brand relationships and the importance of focusing on user
experience and engagement strategies that resonate with
consumers in a rapidly evolving digital landscape. Based
on the “three-factor theory of anthropomorphism”, it seeks
to improve knowledge of the mental processes involved in
customers’ interactions with brands via chatbots on social
media. It also looks at how people perceive new experiences
in the telecom industry, and it has four research goals to back
up its conclusions.
Perceived contingency and consumer engagement in two
forms of gratification (entertainment and social interaction)
have a nonlinearly positive relationship. In contrast, accord-
ing to the first research objective, information-seeking has a
linearly positive relationship. Gratification serves as a medi-
ator between perceived contingency and customer engage-
ment for the second goal. According to the third objective,
consumer engagement and all relational outcomes (brand
intimacy, affective commitment, chatbot-related behavioral
intention, and purchase intention) have a nonlinearly positive
relationship. Finally, all brand relational outcomes and per-
ceived contingency are mediated by consumer engagement.
This study investigates the implications of anthropomor-
phism—the practice of giving non-human entities, such as
chatbots from social media brands, human characteristics.
This interpretation impacts brand-consumer interactions.
We use the three-factor theory of anthropomorphism to
investigate the ways in which psychological factors influ-
ence the relationships between consumers and brands. Our
study highlights how anthropomorphic chatbot design can
enhance the user experience.
Strategic design greatly benefits marketing since it allows
marketers to develop brand components that encourage
anthropomorphism, especially in the services sector. Effec-
tive brand management depends on the company’s per -
sonality and the loyalty of its customers. Examining how
consumer behavior reflects their perceptions is crucial for
marketers. Social media chatbots make improved anthro-
pomorphism and enduring customer relationships pos-
sible. Across all touchpoints, including chatbots, brands
must employ consistent anthropomorphic cues to maintain
authenticity. The three-factor theory of anthropomorphism
can be used to understand consumer-brand chatbot inter -
actions better. However, the methodological flaws in this
strategy highlight how crucial careful execution and ongo-
ing assessment are to digital marketing, especially on social
media channels.
Future studies should consider the possible decline of
anthropomorphic approaches as social media trends and
consumer behavior change. Academics studying market-
ing must also consider the ethical implications of influenc-
ing consumer attitudes. This study enhances social media
marketing and consumer behavior while offering a more
profound comprehension of customer-brand relationships
by integrating psychological processes related to consumer
interactions with telecommunications brands through social
media chatbots.
