Editorial on the Research T opic
Inclusive leadership in multicultural teams: innovations, chal lenges
and solutions
In leadership and organizational psychology studies, the development of inclusive
leadership qualities has become increasingly important due to the rapidly growing
multicultural workforce in modern workplace ( Agba and Agba, 2025 ). The interaction
between individuals from diverse professional, social, and cultural background has
been intensiﬁed due to workplace mobility, increasing globalization, and hybrid and
remote workplace arrangements. Although these advances create opportunities for greater
creativity and innovation, they also present challenges related to equitable participation,
social cohesion, psychological safety, and eﬀective communication. Therefore, exploring
how leaders can eﬀectively manage diversity and promote inclusion has become an
increasingly crucial area of focus for practitioners and researchers. Inclusive leadership has
been largely conceptualized as a leadership approach characterized by fairness, accessibility,
openness, and appreciation of individuals’ distinctiveness, while simultaneously fostering
a sense of belonging among group members ( Randel et al., 2018 ). Numerous studies
have demonstrated that inclusive leadership positively impacts employee engagement,
innovation, wellbeing, and organizational performance (Bao et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2021;
Umrani et al., 2024 ; Gong et al., 2021 ). Yet, signiﬁcant gaps persist in our understanding
of how inclusive leadership works within multicultural settings. Especially, related to
the contextual factors shaping leadership eﬀectiveness, the psychological mechanisms
underlying its eﬀects, and the challenges arising by evolving organizational forms and
emerging technologies. These challenges are particularly signiﬁcant in multicultural
groups, where variations in values, social norms, identities, and communication styles
may complicate team relationships and interaction. Additionally, the rapid adoption
of artiﬁcial intelligence and digital technologies has created new opportunities for
enhancing inclusion while simultaneously raising concerns regarding equity, bias, and the
preservation of human-centered leadership practices. Addressing these concerns requires a
multidisciplinary perspective that integrates organizational psychology, leadership theory,

Research Topic “ Inclusive leadership in multicultural teams:

to explore how inclusive leadership shapes individual and team

identiﬁed from the 14 articles published within this Research
Topic, each contributing meaningfully to its overall objectives.
These themes include: (1) inclusive climates, employee voice,
and identity; (2) leadership capabilities and psychological
mechanisms; (3) diversity, institutional inclusion, and technology;
and (4) creativity, innovation, and team dynamics. The ﬁrst
thematic area focuses on inclusive climates, employee voice and
identity. It has been identiﬁed that inclusive leadership inﬂuences
the employee outcomes through central mechanisms namely
belongingness and identiﬁcation ( Li et al., 2025 ). Öz and Meriç
found that teachers’ organizational citizenship behaviors, including
sportsmanship, civic virtue, conscientiousness, and altruism,
increase when they identify strongly with their organization.
This highlights the signiﬁcance of identiﬁcation as a predictor
of positive employee outcomes. Similarly, Shen and Shen found
that employee voice behavior is encouraged in the organizations
that promote an inclusive climate. Their results conﬁrms
that organizations with inclusive climates enhance employees’
conﬁdence in communicating ideas by enhanced voice eﬃcacy.
Additionally, satisfaction with voice channel construct strengthens
this relationship, especially when formal voice systems are
perceived as accessible and eﬀective. These ﬁndings highlight
that inclusion requires both institutional structures that enable
meaningful participation and supportive organizational climates.
Fu et al. study also placed identity processes at center of their
study. They found that the relationship between transformational
leadership and innovation is partially mediated by organizational
identiﬁcation. This research enhances the understanding of
how shared identities can facilitate alignment and collaboration
within complex organizational settings. Song et al. explored that
inclusion may results in unintentional outcomes. They found
that employees who are being trusted by the leaders may become
victim of coworker envy which leads toward workplace exclusion.
Additionally, these negative consequences mostly occur in highly
competitive environments. Therefore, this study points out that a
careful execution of inclusive leadership practices is required to
avoid unnecessary social tensions.
The second theme area centers on the leadership capabilities
and psychological mechanisms. As, it advances inclusion and
employee thriving. Gao et al. investigates how new generation
employees thrive at work, and their job performance is being
aﬀected by intellectual humility leadership. Based on social
exchange theory and job demand-resources theory, they concluded
that intellectual humility leadership which advances positive job
attitudes, consequently, enhances subordinate performance and
thriving at work. They further highlighted that these eﬀects are
stronger among subordinates with higher core self-evaluations,
emphasizing the interaction between individual characteristics and
leadership behaviors. Correspondingly, Xing et al. explored how
leader’s emotional intelligence enhances team innovation. They
examined how underlying mechanism namely knowledge sharing
and psychological safety that inﬂuence this relationship. Their
ﬁndings showed that leaders with high emotional intelligence who
create psychologically safe environments contribute to greater team
innovation. This highlights the signiﬁcance of the relational and
aﬀective dimensions of leadership within culturally diverse teams.
Liu X. et al. also investigated the leadership eﬀectiveness
by exploring the impact of team leadership on the professional
development of university teachers. They concluded that
organizational innovation climates strengthen the eﬀects of
leadership across the proposed pathways, while innovative self-
eﬃcacy partially supported the moderated mediation model
tested in their study. These ﬁndings highlight the importance of
supportive organizational contexts in enhancing the beneﬁts of
inclusive leadership. Accompanying these conclusions, Tian et al.
investigated how leaders’ egoistic and altruistic values inﬂuence
employee happiness and leadership eﬀectiveness. Their ﬁndings
indicate that altruistic values enhance employee wellbeing through
cognitive and emotional pathways, whereas egoistic values do not
generate the same level of beneﬁts. These ﬁndings suggest that
value-based leadership plays a crucial role in fostering collaborative
and supportive work environments.
The third theme focuses on diversity, institutional inclusion
and technology in current organizations. Javeed et al. examined
the relationship between diversity and resilience, by exploring
the role of gender diversity in marketing and ﬁnancial decision
during organizational disasters. They concluded that gender based
diverse leadership groups show more empathetic communication
and greater brand resilience. Guo et al. further explored the
role of Chinese trade union in advancing employment amongst
migrant workers. They found that the role of union is directly
linked with employment outcomes. The union participation results
in positive employment outcome by enhanced social insurance
coverage, formal contracts, and labor protection. Lastly, Tahir
et al. investigated the emerging role of artiﬁcial intelligence
in inclusive leadership within culturally diverse educational
teams. Their theoretical investigation proposed a governance
framework highlighting transparent AI protocols, interdisciplinary
governance structures, human oversight, and equity audits. The
study highlighted that inclusive and ethical AI implementation
is necessary to promote fair leadership practices and prevent the
reinforcement of existing gender inequalities.
The ﬁnal theme centers on creativity, innovation, and team
dynamics. It is widely recognized that inclusive leadership
regarded innovation. However, the mechanisms through which
the leadership inﬂuences creativity remain an emerging area
of research. Qiao et al. explored the relationship among
transformation leadership and team innovative performance. They
concluded that leaders could foster persistent psychological sources
like patient capital, and that backing innovation. Zhao and Ren
explore the role of organizational system in enhancing creativity.
They found that diverse and inclusive human resource practices
(DIHRP) improve employee creativity. They concluded that

Collectively, the studies included in this Research Topic provide
a broad and multifaceted understanding of inclusive leadership
in culturally diverse teams. The ﬁndings reveal that inclusive
leadership extends beyond interpersonal behaviors to encompass
technological systems, identity processes, institutional structures,
and organizational climates. These studies further demonstrate that
inclusion is not merely a moral obligation but also a strategic
resource that enhances creativity, innovation, resilience, wellbeing,
and organizational eﬀectiveness. In today’s rapidly changing world,
organizations continue to evolve in response to demographic shifts,
globalization, and technological advancements. Consequently,
managing these complex organizational environments through
inclusive leadership will remain an important area of investigation.
The ﬁndings presented in this volume provide valuable practical
guidance and theoretical insights for practitioners, researchers, and
policymakers seeking to build innovative, inclusive, and sustainable
organizations capable of thriving in increasingly complex and
diverse environments.
