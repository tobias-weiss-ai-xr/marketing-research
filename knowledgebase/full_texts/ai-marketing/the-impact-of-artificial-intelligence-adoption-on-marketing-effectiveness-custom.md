Abstract
The present study focused on measuring the perceptions of marketing
professionals about the impact of artificial intelligence (AI) on
marketing practices specifically AI adoption, customers engagement,
marketing personalization and campaign effectiveness. A quantitative
survey research design was used.  The data was collected from 166
purposively selected marketing professionals from Lahore, Pakistan.
A structured questionnaire was used to measure the perception of
participants of the study.  Depending on the categorical and ordinal
nature of the variables, descriptive statistics, association measures
based on non-parametric tests and ordinal logistic regression were
used. The results revealed that the exposure to AI was moderate to high
for most of the respondents and the extent of the adoption by the
organisations was mostly moderate to high. In general, AI was seen to
be effective in boosting customer engagement and optimisation of
campaigns.  The strongest statistical result was for personalisation,
where the likelihood of achieving better personalised marketing
experiences was greatly predicted by the higher level of AI usage with
an odds ratio of 4.78 (p < .001). The perceived marketing effectiveness
was not statistically significantly predicted by the use of AI. Key
concerns during implementation included the issues of data privacy,
adapting to a workforce, transparency and algorithmic bias. In
addition, personalisation, targeting, automation, predictive decision
making and AI agents emerged as the biggest present and future effects
of AI on marketing through the thematic analysis of the open-ended
answers.

Customer Engagement, Marketing Effectiveness
1. Introduction
Artificial intelligence (AI) has moved rapidly from being a specialized technological capability to
becoming a central component of contemporary marketing practice. Predictive analytics, recommendation
systems, conversational interfaces, generative AI and other machine-learning applications have been
embedded into marketing activities in a large number of organizations. McKinsey & Company (2025)
reported that artificial intelligence is now widely adopted across global organizations, with marketing and
sales emerging as a primary function for both usage surges and reported revenue benefits. McKinsey's insights
highlight that marketing, and sales departments frequently capture significant top-line impact from AI-driven
personalization, customer insights, and workflow automation. This is a step towards the new paradigm of
data-driven, automated and more personalised marketing, away from intuition-driven and mass
communication strategies. AI can handle a ton of customer data, find trends, handle repetitive tasks and assist
with quicker decision-making throughout the customer journey (Huang & Rust, 2021). Therefore, the impact
of AI on marketing must be considered not just based on whether organisations are using AI in their marketing
efforts, but also how much, which technologies are being used, what they are achieving and what capabilities
are required for the responsible use of AI in marketing.

Customer engagement is one of the most significant spaces of the AI-powered change. Organisations
can interact with customers timelier and more relevant through the use of recommendation engines, predictive
analytics, chatbots, natural language processing and image-recognition systems (Haleem et al., 2022). Lee
and Breckon (2025) discovered that the factors of personalisation, effectiveness, enjoyment, reliability and
ease of use are significant drivers of customer engagement with AI marketing applications. The benefits of
AI can only be judged by the effectiveness of the engagement, their ability to drive better campaign
performance, and the value and relevance of the customer interaction. Similarly, Salesforce (2026) states that
marketers are also shifting towards more conversational and personalised customer experiences, but many
organisations are still finding it difficult to connect the dots of AI usage and effective engagement on a scale.
It's therefore crucial to explore practitioners' perceptions of AI's effectiveness to gauge if the technology is
delivering value for the marketing function.
Another crucial aspect of the marketing impact of AI is personalisation. This AI capability allows for
the analysis of behaviour, transactional and contextual data to make highly specific product recommendations,
promotional messages, content, and digital experiences (Chandra et al., 2022). The use of personalisation in
e-commerce products and services, driven by AI technologies, has been found to be a major driver of
consumer behaviour, and has been shown to raise important questions about consumer governance and the
design of responsible algorithms (Turki, 2025). Effective personalisation can contribute to customer relevance
and a better campaign targeting, conversion opportunities and more efficient use of marketing resources in
practice. These are all linked to the overall organisational benefits such as stronger customer engagement,
higher sales, better data-centric decision making, optimisation of campaigns and perhaps cost savings. But
these advantages can vary based on an organization's AI implementation, data quality, technology
infrastructure, and employee knowledge.
The production of marketing content and services is also being transformed by Generative AI. Wahid
et al. (2025) showed that generative AI redistributes the content creation capabilities and shifts the role of
content marketing agencies towards more strategic knowledge, creativity, hyper-personalisation, and
innovation using AI in services. So, AI isn't just about streamlining current tasks, it's about transforming jobs,
processes, and the skills required of marketers. Generative AI is also regarded as a key emerging marketing
skill in the 2025 skills research by the American Marketing Association, and the study also shows that digital
marketing and data analytics skills are also identified as areas of competency that need improvement (O'Brien,
2025). When assessing the impact of AI in marketing, training and professional preparedness are key
considerations. In today's digital age, marketers must be data-literate, know the basics of AI, be able to use
AI to create content effectively, ethically mindful, adaptable, and cross-functional.
Meanwhile, the increasing use of AI brings with it ethical and managerial dilemmas. Concerns with
AI-driven marketing include privacy issues, automated profiling, algorithmic decision-making, transparency,
accuracy and accountability. However, Schilke and Reimann (2025) opined that the impact of AI disclosure
on trust can be multifaceted, suggesting that disclosure needs to be approached with great care and not as a
panacea. Additionally, there is a growing organisational focus on tackling privacy, explainability, reputation
and regulation risks associated with the use of AI, says McKinsey & Company (2025). To prevent the erosion
of customer trust, data protection, proper data representation, transparency in processes, proper governance
and human oversight are essential for responsible adoption of AI-powered marketing.
Despite the high interest in AI, there is a significant disconnect between adopting AI and making it a
marketing asset that is effective over time. The State of Marketing AI Report 2025, based on interviews with
almost 1,900 marketing professionals, reveals disparities in their understanding and use of AI, as well as
differences in the output they're looking for and the obstacles to its adoption (Marketing AI Institute, 2025).
The differences in the findings indicate the importance of understanding how marketers are using AI, the
extent to which their organisations actually use AI, the specific types of AI tools used, the benefits of using
AI, challenges in doing so, ethical considerations, training experiences and training plans for AI use.
Therefore, this study focuses on the marketing practitioners' perspective of the influence of AI on marketing.

1.1 Problem Statement
AI is now playing a significant role in reshaping marketing by automating tasks, personalizing content,
analysing data, engaging customers, and optimizing campaigns. While there is no doubt that organisations
vary in their level of AI adoption, the tools they are using, the skills of marketing professionals and their
ability to get measurable benefits from AI. Meanwhile, issues around data privacy, ethical usage,
transparency, and staffing continue to grow and are impacting on successful implementation. Hence, there is
a need to explore the adoption of artificial intelligence in various areas of marketing and understand the
potential effects of using AI in marketing effectiveness, customer engagement and factors that can affect the
successful implementation and use of AI in marketing.
1.2 Research Questions
The aim of this study is to examine the perceptions of marketing professionals about the usage and
effectiveness of artificial intelligence in marketing practices such as customer engagement, personalization
and campaign effectiveness in Pakistani context. More specifically the research covers the following research
questions.
1. What is the current level of adoption and use of artificial intelligence in marketing activities among
Pakistani marketing professionals?
2. What impact does the use of artificial intelligence have on customer engagement and marketing
campaign effectiveness as perceived by marketers?
3. How does Artificial Intelligence impact the personalization of marketing content and customer
journey?
4. How do AI-related training, skills, and professional exposure influence the effective adoption and use
of artificial intelligence in marketing?
2. Literature Review
2.1 AI Adoption and Marketing Effectiveness
As businesses strive to make more informed decisions, optimize operations, and stay agile in response
to consumer trends, AI has become a crucial part of their marketing strategies. Today, AI is used in various
ways such as predictive analytics, recommendation systems, natural language processing, chatbots, and
generative AI, enabling marketers to better handle large amounts of customer and market data (Haleem et al.,
2022). By leveraging AI, businesses can enhance customer engagement and improve forecasting and strategic
decision-making, as AI can help identify patterns in customer behaviour and react more quickly to market
trends (Huang & Rust, 2021; Kopalle et al., 2022; Mohd Amin et al., 2025). Likewise, Sahut and Laroche
(2025) sees AI as a significant instrument in enhancing customer experience and strategic marketing strategies
through personalisation, service automation and dynamic communication. These are further enhanced with
Generative AI, helping with creating content, creativity, and marketing communication on a large scale
(Prasanna & Kushwaha, 2025). All in all, recent research shows that AI is moving beyond simple automation
and into the mainstream of marketing processes, with implementation levels ranging from very low to very
high among organisations. This variation is a key factor to consider when assessing the true impact of AI on
marketing effectiveness within an organisation.
While having access to AI tools is important, the effectiveness of their incorporation into
organizational processes is crucial to the benefits of adoption. While the potential benefits of generative AI to
campaign performance, customer engagement, personalised customer experiences and data-driven campaign
decisions are identified, Salih et al. (2026) suggest that challenges in implementation may limit potential
benefits. Likewise, Sahut and Laroche (2025) highlight the strategic value when AI technologies are linked
to the related customer-experience and marketing goals. Other studies with marketing practitioners show that
while they believe AI can help them communicate more efficiently and boost their creativity, they also see

2.2 AI Personalisation, Customer Engagement and Campaign Outcomes
Personalisation represents one of the most prominent applications of AI in modern marketing.
Behavioural and transactional data can be fed into machine-learning systems to deliver personalised
recommendations, advertising and customer interactions, based on a user's preferences (Chandra et al., 2022).
In social media context, AI-driven personalization can boost perceived usefulness and trust, thereby fostering
deeper consumer engagement in the platform (Teepapal, 2025). The results show that effectiveness of
personalisation is not just about the accuracy of the algorithms, but also about how consumers interpret the
AI-driven experiences. This view is echoed by evidence related to conversational AI. The study by Niros et
al. (2025) on chatbot marketing suggests that customisation, anthropomorphism, and problem-solving
capabilities can positively impact customer-equity outcomes and brand satisfaction, however, the impact of
these features depends on the cultural orientation of consumers. Also, Priporas et al. (2025) prove that AI-
driven ads can impact customer engagement and emotional reactions, and the perception of your customer
base may differ when it comes to AI-produced communication. These insights highlight that customer
engagement is a multi-faceted phenomenon and the performance of AI-driven marketing strategies relies on
the technology's usability as well as the traits of the target audience.
AI-driven personalisation can also have a wider impact on campaign and consumer performance, as
evidenced by recent studies. A mixed-methods study by Tran (2026) revealed that predictive analytics played
a key role in enhancing personalisation and positively impacted on ROI of campaigns, suggesting a
relationship between AI capabilities and real marketing outcomes. Additionally, Qadri et al. (2026)
demonstrate that AI personalisation may interact with purchase-related outcomes via an increase in message
relevance, but that privacy concerns can dampen the link. The findings presented here challenging the notion
that personalisation is only judged by the sophistication of technology, and that it is only a case of relevance,
transparency and customer acceptance that can make personalised messages actually contribute to desirable
marketing outcomes. Personalised interaction is also a key factor in AI customer experience, as stated by
Sahut and Laroche (2025). Hence, the literature offers theory and empirical evidence on the role of AI in
enhancing customer engagement, optimising campaigns and personalising their marketing journeys.
Simultaneously, the differences in outcomes across contexts highlight the need to measure practitioners'
experiences, rather than taking for granted that AI always has positive outcomes.
2.3 Ethical Challenges, Skills and Future Marketing Adaptation
While AI-powered marketing has many advantages, there are also significant ethical and management
issues. As marketing systems rely on large amounts of consumer information and automatic decisions,
privacy, algorithmic bias, transparency and accountability are increasingly gaining significance (Kopalle et
al., 2022). After synthesizing 445 papers to identify key concerns, Hari et al. (2025) confirms that ethics is
one of the major hurdles to the use of AI in marketing and highlight the need for trust, ethical algorithmic
usage and ethical governance. The authors Gupta et al. (2025) also believe that greater integration of AI
presents policy and governance issues across borders, due to variations in regulatory frameworks influencing
AI-based data gathering and the use of automated marketing technologies in organisations. This is especially
the case with regard to personalisation and privacy. According to Qadri et al. (2026), consumer responses to
AI-driven personalisation can be undermined by privacy concerns, while increasing transparency in AI-driven
personalisation can help bolster consumer responses. Ethical practices are thus strategic, too, because poor
data privacy or less transparent practices of AI can erode consumer confidence in the value that personalisation
and AI-driven engagement offer.

3. Theoretical and Conceptual Framework
To support this study, the AI-Driven Customer Engagement Theory (AICE) of Lajevardi and Orouei
(2025), which outlines the potential of artificial intelligence to enhance marketing results via three core
mechanisms—personalisation, automation, and ongoing customer engagement—is used. The theory is that
AI helps organisations to better understand their customers' data, adjust marketing communications, automate
repetitive marketing tasks, and react more effectively to their customers' actions. AI adoption and use is seen
as the driver, and AI personalisation and automation as the mechanisms by which customer engagement and
marketing effectiveness can be improved. Other potential supplementary factors that can enhance the effective
use of AI in marketing include AI-related training, skills, and professional exposure. This theory is suitable
as it directly relates to the most important variables measured in the current study and has a recent theoretical
background to study the role of AI in marketing practices (Lajevardi & Orouei, 2025).

the extent of AI exposure and implementation, the use of AI-powered marketing tools, customer interaction,
campaign success, personalisation, perceived benefits, implementation challenges, ethical concerns,

that were identified by the respondents were accountability, information accuracy, regulatory compliance and
customer consent.

6. Discussion
The findings of this study show that artificial intelligence has become widely embedded in
contemporary marketing practice in Pakistan, although its effects are not uniform across all outcomes. The
majority of respondents said that they were moderately or highly exposed to AI and over four-fifths said that
their organisations were moderately or extensively using AI. The most common AI tools were chatbots,
predictive analytics and recommendation systems. This is in line with the latest studies that reveal the growing
role of AI in customer interactions, automation, and strategic marketing (Huang & Rust, 2021; Haleem et al.,
2022). According to Sahut and Laroche (2025), AI is transforming customer experience by enabling
personalised interactions and dynamic communication, whereas Gupta et al. (2025) highlight the growing use
of AI in business to enhance marketing capabilities and customer interactions. Similarly, Lee and Breckon
(2025) state that personalisation, effectiveness and reliability are significant factors in engaging with AI
marketing systems.
Overall, the majority of respondents view AI as a positive development: 78.3% said it was somewhat
or very effective in boosting customer engagement and campaign optimisation, and the top benefits mentioned
across the board were increase in customer engagement, increase in sales and data-driven decision-making.
The findings are consistent with research which suggests that AI can be used as a way to enhance the
responsiveness of marketing and interactions with customers (Lee & Breckon, 2025; Sahut & Laroche, 2025).
Overall perceived marketing effectiveness, however, was not significantly predicted by organisational AI
adoption. This means that more AI alone won't necessarily lead to better marketing results. As Sahut and
Laroche (2025) highlight, the meaning of value is linked to the ways in which AI can be applied to customer
experience and marketing operations, and as Vitellaro et al. (2026) demonstrate, marketing experts are
considering the value of generative AI based on its strategic value and the constraints and opportunities for

One of the surprising results was that there was a strong negative correlation between professional
exposure to AI and perceived marketing effectiveness. The more I was exposed to something, the less likely
I was to rate it as being more effective. This does not imply 'exposure is not an effective solution'. In the case
of more experienced respondents, they might have been more conscious of the limitations, challenges, and
governance risks associated with AI, and thus have been more critical in their assessment. Technological
constraints can impact the experience for customers, as reported by Hardcastle et al. (2025), and it is evident
that marketing professionals are aware of the advantages and disadvantages of generative AI, such as
regulatory, authenticity and professional issues, as noted by Vitellaro et al. (2026). This negative correlation
could be due to high levels of critical awareness among those with AI exposure, but not necessarily to the
negative impacts of AI experience. As this research was cross-sectional, causal conclusions cannot be drawn
from this relationship.
Issues related to ethics and implementation also figured prominently. The top three challenges were
identified as data privacy, adapting the workforce and transparency/algorithmic bias. The most frequently
mentioned ways in which organisations responded were by taking data protection measures, increasing the
diversity of data and algorithms, and making AI processes transparent. Hari et al. (2025) also highlight
privacy, algorithms and trust and ethical governance as major issues in AI marketing. While Gupta et al.
(2025) emphasize the need for ethical, regulatory and policy considerations when implementing AI, Teepapal
(2025) demonstrates that privacy matters can hinder positive consumer reactions to AI personalisation.
Therefore, the present results suggest that ethical governance is closely related to marketing outcomes as the
three elements of privacy, fairness and transparency relate to the extent to which customers and professionals
can trust AI-driven interactions.
Professional ability was also a key factor. The top three skills identified by the respondents were:
understanding AI principles and algorithms, data literacy, adaptability and AI-assisted content creation. There
was a large disparity between the two types of professional development, with online courses and on-the-job
training being the types most commonly used, while perceived marketing effectiveness was not significantly
associated with training type. This indicates that the type of training provided might not be the only factor in
the successful application of AI; depth, relevance and organizational support might play a bigger part.
Vitellaro et al. (2026) reported efficiency, creativity, and personalisation as the main reasons why marketing
professionals are interested in generative AI, highlighting the ongoing importance of human judgment and
creativity. This aligns with the current discovery that it is not training itself, but rather technical understanding,
adaptability and professional judgement that is important for effective use of AI.

training. The priorities align with those of Gupta et al. (2025) and Sahut and Laroche (2025), who sees AI as

7. Conclusion
The study concluded that AI has become an integral component of contemporary marketing in
Pakistan, especially in the areas of customer engagement, personalisation, campaign optimization, and data-
informed decision-making. The results revealed a moderate to high level of exposure to AI among most
respondents, and a moderate to extensive level of AI adoption among organisations. The value of AI was seen,
particularly in the area of personalisation, and the higher the level of its adoption, the more likely the outcomes
were to be strong. But, the fact that more companies use AI did not automatically equate to higher overall
marketing effectiveness, showing that successful results vary based on the use and management of AI.
Concerns about data privacy and adapting the workforce to the new technologies, transparency and
algorithmic bias also continued. The results also pointed out the need for more robust AI knowledge, data
literacy, adaptability and hands-on experience among marketers.
8. Recommendations
1. Organisations need to invest in AI tools that directly help them in the area of customer personalisation
and campaign optimisation.
2. Practical training should be regularly provided for marketing teams on the use of AI, data analysis,
and responsible use of AI.
3. Companies should enhance data protection measures to handle privacy concerns in AI-driven
marketing.
4. Organisations should ensure transparency of AI in marketing decisions and customer interactions.
5. Human oversight should play a vital role in AI-driven marketing strategies to enhance precision,
creativity, and accountability.
6. In the future, AI strategies need to target quantifiable marketing results and not use AI technologies
just for the sake of it.
