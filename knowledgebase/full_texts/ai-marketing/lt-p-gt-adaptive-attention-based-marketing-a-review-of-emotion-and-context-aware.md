Abstract
With the rise in mobile application usage, competition for user attention has become more intense, and the demand for
intelligent and personalized engagement strategies has grown. Despite the significant progress made in emotion
recognition, context-aware computing, and adaptive recommendation systems, the research is still scattered in many
diﬀerent fields. This review aims to fill this gap by exploring the new paradigm of Adaptive Attention-Based Marketing
(AABM) that combines real-time emotion detection, context-aware sensing, and adaptive decision-making to improve
mobile app engagement. The literature review was carried out in a structured manner using the Scopus database, and
the period of literature search was from 2018 to 2025. There were 408 records identified and screened, 40 studies were
included in the final qualitative synthesis. The review covers advances in multimodal emotion recognition, context
recognition, and Artificial Intelligence (AI) for personalization, such as machine learning, deep learning, contextual
bandits, and reinforcement learning. The results show that three key enablers for AABM are multimodal sensing,
contextual intelligence, and adaptive learning algorithms. Its applications span e-commerce, gamified systems, mobile
notifications, and the healthcare sector, showcasing its ability to enhance engagement, retention, and user experience.
However, challenges remain regarding privacy, transparency, multimodal data availability, and long-term evaluation. This
review brings together existing knowledge and identifies research gaps, as well as directions for the development of
privacy-aware and ethically responsible adaptive engagement systems.

Introduction And Background
Mobile apps have become an essential platform to conduct commerce, entertainment, learn, and interact in the
contemporary digital ecosystem. Mobile-first digital ecosystems have grown at a brisk pace, creating a fierce competition
for users' attention in apps and online services. As per the Digital 2025 Global Overview Report, the world population
with mobile phones reached about 5.78 billion (nearly 70.5%  of the world's population) in 2025, and the world
population with internet use crossed the 5.56 billion mark (DataReportal, 2025). India has become one of the fastest-
growing digital ecosystems. The Digital 2025 India report estimated approximately 806 million internet users in India in
early 2025, reflecting continued growth in mobile connectivity, app-based services, and digital consumer engagement
(DataReportal, 2025). As the number of mobile apps, digital commerce, and customized online services grows rapidly,

This ubiquity has provided a more competitive environment than ever before, where keeping the attention of the user

decreases  engagement but can also cause frustration, message saturation, and churn (Pielot et al., 2014). Marketing

in the optimization of engagement. The key to this change is the development of aﬀective computing and context-aware
systems that allow mobile applications to identify the emotional state of a user and his/her situational context. The

as multimodal methods that integrate the analysis of facial expression, speech prosody, and patterns of use (Khare et al.,

(battery level, network connectivity), to determine whether a user is commuting, working, or relaxing at home.
Collectively, these insights provide unmatched possibilities to create messages that are timely, contextually relevant, and
emotionally aligned.
The rise of Adaptive Attention-Based Marketing (AABM) is a result of the integration of emotion and context in marketing.
Unlike traditional personalization that is based on user behavior in the past and predetermined segmentation rules,
AABM is proactive and adaptive, constantly adding contextual information and user interactions in real time to marketing
decisions. Mobile apps can use machine learning models and dynamic decision-making processes to decide on the
content to be delivered, the timing for its delivery, and the manner in which it is presented to maximize user
engagement. For example, contextual bandit algorithms and reinforcement learning models have been used to optimize
the delivery of content and interventions over time, adapting to the context of users and the feedback they provide, thus
improving the eﬀectiveness of the campaigns and long-term user engagement (Cai et al., 2023). Emotion-aware systems
have shown enhanced consumer reactions to oﬀers or promotions based on their correspondence with real-time
aﬀective conditions in e-commerce and digital advertising, and psychological sensitivity is a key value in marketing
(Gupta and Bansal, 2023) (Khurana, 2026). Nevertheless, AABM implementation is not without diﬃculties. Privacy and
ethics are among  the most critical issues. To be able to infer the user emotions and contexts, it is necessary to access
sensitive behavioral and sensor data, which may also provoke questions of consent, transparency, and possible misuse.
Another identified serious ethical risk is the possibility of manipulative personalization, where systems use emotions to
fuel engagement or sales (Huang and Rust, 2021). To overcome such obstacles, the more recent literature focuses on
privacy-preserving models, including on-device inference, federated learning, and diﬀerential privacy, to make sure that
sensitive data is not sent to centralized computers (Khare et al., 2024). Moreover, Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI)
systems are also being suggested to achieve transparency in adaptive decision-making so that end-users could
comprehend why a specific message was provided at a specific time.

This review paper is placed in this fast-changing interdisciplinary context and analyzes the evolution of emotion and
context-aware adaptive marketing systems in mobile application environments in the last decade. Special focus is given
to literature published from 2018 to 2025, with some regard to seminal works in the past years, which laid the
groundwork for the theories of aﬀective computing, user engagement, and context-aware computing. The review covers
peer-reviewed journal papers, conference papers, preprints, and some industry and working papers on emotion
recognition, multimodal sentiment analysis, adaptive recommendation systems, mobile notifications, and engagement
optimization. The review has a global focus but focuses on developments of general interest in a variety of digital
consumer ecosystems. The conceptual framework of aﬀective computing and context-aware interaction has been
introduced in early studies, but recent developments in smartphone sensing, wearable technologies, multimodal artificial
intelligence, and reinforcement learning have enabled real-time adaptive marketing in a technically feasible and
commercially scalable manner. In this manner, this review aims to achieve three main objectives: (1) summarizing existing
emotion and context recognition technologies for mobile marketing; (2) reviewing the evolution of adaptive decision
engines and recommendation strategies for user engagement optimization; and (3) critically analyzing the ethical,
privacy, and trust-related issues of deploying such systems at scale. The review synthesizes these viewpoints to highlight
the potential for an interdisciplinary field in the near future, AABM, and to underscore the importance of responsible
design, transparent governance, and future empirical testing.
A systematic review approach was used to identify, screen and analyze literature relevant to the selected research
domains to ensure a structured and transparent literature synthesis. The second section presents the design of the
review, the literature search strategy, the screening process, and the analysis of the existing research on AABM.

Methodology of review
Review Design and Approach
The study is based on a structured narrative review with a bibliometric analysis approach to analyze recent
advancements in AABM  by leveraging real-time emotion recognition, context-aware computing, and mobile
engagement optimization. This review aimed to uncover the most salient technological trends, application fields,
research trends, and new challenges where mobile marketing, aﬀective computing, human-computer interaction, and AI
meet.
The methodological approach used is a combination of systematic literature retrieval and descriptive quantitative
analysis of publication metadata and qualitative thematic synthesis of the retrieved literature. This hybrid review format
allowed for a comprehensive overview of the research field as well as a detailed analysis of the conceptual evolution
relevant to AABM. The Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) flow diagram
illustrating the literature search, screening, eligibility assessment, and final study inclusion process adopted for this

Screening and Selection Criteria
The retrieved records were exported from Scopus in CSV format and screened through a multi-stage review process. The
first screening process included the elimination of duplicate and incomplete bibliographic records. Titles, abstracts, and
author keywords were then checked manually to assess their relevance to the scope of this study.
Inclusion Criteria
Studies were included in the review if they addressed emotion recognition, aﬀective sensing, or multimodal emotional
analysis and involved mobile applications, smartphone-based systems, or mobile interaction environments. Eligible
studies also explored context-aware computing, personalization, recommendation systems, or adaptive user interfaces,
with a particular emphasis on enhancing user experiences through intelligent adaptation. Furthermore, studies that
examined user engagement, mobile notifications, attention optimization, or digital interaction behaviors were
considered relevant. To ensure comprehensive coverage of the research domain, both empirical and non-empirical
contributions were included, provided they oﬀered conceptual, methodological, experimental, or review-based insights
that advanced the understanding of adaptive engagement optimization in mobile and context-aware environments.
Exclusion Criteria
Studies were excluded if they were restricted to non-mobile or unrelated application environments, or if they lacked a
direct focus on emotion recognition, context awareness, adaptive user engagement, or closely related research themes.
Duplicate records identified during the screening process were removed to avoid redundancy. In addition, studies that
did not provide suﬃcient bibliographic details, methodological descriptions, or empirical information necessary for
evaluation and analysis were excluded from the review. Following the screening and relevance assessment stages, the
remaining studies were retained and formed the final dataset for thematic synthesis, bibliometric analysis, and
interpretation of research trends related to adaptive engagement optimization in mobile and context-aware systems.

Advances in emotion and context recognition
Proper identification of feelings and situations of users has become the foundation of AABM. Traditional marketing used
fixed demographics and past usage, which did not take into consideration the dynamic aspect of human aﬀect and
situational changes. The recent studies have paid more attention to finer-grained, real-time identification of
psychological states and environmental indicators and have demonstrated that the marketing intervention, tailored to
these signals, can make user receptivity and engagement much higher.

and PCANet+ to detect subtle facial movements that are commonly linked to suppressed or transient emotions. Their
study has shown that the integration of handcrafted optical flow features and a lightweight neural network can be highly

that the manner in which the users reject, postpone, or react to the notifications has significant clues about stress and
mood (Kanjo et al., 2017). In a similar way, Lim et al. examined the dynamics of keystroke with respect to loneliness and

compared to daily patterns of use. Collectively, these pieces imply that indirect interaction information can be as
informative as direct sensing (Lim et al., 2024). In addition to this, Visuri et al. examined how smartphone users interact
with notifications to understand the importance and timing of the content. Their results revealed that notifications are
not all disruptive, and contextual cues may forecast user receptivity. This is in line with other studies in multimodal
sentiment analysis (Visuri et al., 2019). A survey by Lai et al.  gave an overview of methods of multimodal sentiment
analysis, which they state is more robust when text, audio, and visual data are combined with context (Lai et al., 2023).
Zhao et al. also synthesized fusion strategies in a broad review of deep multimodal data fusion, and transformer-based
models were identified as particularly useful in processing heterogeneous streams in real-time (Zhao et al., 2024).
Finally, attention prediction is an emerging dimension. Steil et al. discussed the way in which device-integrated and
wearable sensors can predict user attention in mobile interactions. Their findings revealed that eye gaze, motion, and
device state could collectively determine whether a user is attentive or distracted and provide direct implications for
timing adaptive marketing interventions (Steil et al., 2018). Overall, these papers indicate that the current developments
in micro-expression recognition, on-device privacy-preserving emotion analysis, multimodal sensor fusion, and attention
forecasting oﬀer the technological basis for AABM. With emotional sensitivity and contextual intelligence, future
marketing systems will be able to provide interventions that are highly personalized, yet also situationally appropriate
and ethically sound.

how emotion recognition, contextual sensing, adaptive decision-making, and engagement optimization are combined

AABM: conceptual framework
AABM is a paradigm change from the traditional one-size-fits all personalization to real-time adaptive marketing based
on the user's emotional state, context, and moment-to-moment receptivity. AABM is not just about segmentation or user
preferences, but about continuously analyzing and interpreting behavioral and contextual signals to provide timely,
relevant, and emotionally appropriate interventions. Three key components of AABM are as follows: (i) emotion- and
context-aware sensing, which gathers behavioral, emotional, and environmental signals; (ii) adaptive decision engines,
which interpret these signals to decide on the most suitable engagement response; and (iii) engagement optimization,

This vision is reinforced by application-specific prototypes. Kumaran et al. have created EChat, an adaptive messaging
interface that changes the UI elements based on the user's emotions detected. When the interface changed according to
the user, the users stated greater satisfaction and coherence in interactions with messages (Kumaran et al., 2023). Similar
results were observed in the entertainment field, as Singh and Dembla revealed that emotion-sensitive music
suggestions through transfer learning were significantly more accurate in matching playlists to the mood of the user
(Singh and Dembla, 2023). Likewise, Kheiri and Ding (Kheiri and Ding, 2026) presented a comprehensive survey of
emotion-aware recommender systems and emphasized that integrating users' emotion into the recommender models
can significantly enhance the quality of personalization, user engagement, and recommendation relevance, while also
introducing challenges in emotion detection, privacy, robustness, and ethical deployment. These studies collectively
show that emotion and context are important factors that mediate user attention, engagement, and satisfaction.
Adaptive Decision Engines
After inferences of user states, the AABM systems are based on decision engines able to modify actions in real time. The
classical recommender systems typically maximize short-term goals, including click-through rate (CTR), whereas the more
recent developments focus on reinforcement learning (RL) and contextual bandits to maximize short-term and long-term
goals. As an example,  Min et al. used deep contextual multi-armed bandits to do adaptive endpointing in speech
systems, minimizing errors and with low latency. Applying this principle to marketing, contextual bandits are able to
identify the best time and tone to use when sending messages (Min et al., 2023). Wang et al. modeled notification policies
with RL and showed that behavioral simulator-trained policies were more engaging and did not exceed user limitations
(e.g., frequency limits) (Wang et al., 2021). Applying this to real deployments, Yuan et al. introduced an oﬄine RL method
to optimize the notification strategies to allow pre-deployment training to be conducted safely and then enable live
rollouts (Yuan et al., 2022).
The importance of adaptive decision-making is also demonstrated in field deployments. Mishra et al. and King et al.
showed that context and aﬀective indicators of mobile sensors and wearables could predict receptivity to
interventions  (Mishra et al., 2021) (King et al., 2024). Their models performed well in terms of prediction, with
interventions only being possible when the user is the most receptive. In clinical practice, Aguilera et al. found that RL-
personalized messages in the DIAMANTE Randomized Controlled Trial (RCT) increased the number of physical activity
steps by 19% compared to random or control messages strategies in 24 weeks (Aguilera et al., 2024). On the same note,
Lauﬀenburger et al. in the REINFORCE trial discovered that RL-personalized communications were much more eﬀective at
improving medication adherence than the non-RL-personalized approaches (Lauﬀenburger et al., 2024).
On a large scale, He et al. demonstrated that contextual bandits were able to utilize the browsing context to enhance CTR
in large-scale mobile browsing recommendations, and Qassimi and Rakrak extended this approach to multi-objective
bandits that trade oﬀ CTR and retention in smart tourism recommendations (He et al., 2020) (Qassimi and Rakrak, 2025).
To optimize in the long-term, Zhang et al. (Zhang et al., 2022) suggested multi-task RL in recommender systems, which
increased the satisfaction of users in the long-term, and Zhang et al. (Zhang et al., 2023) proposed a dynamic multi-
objective RL framework, which optimized the performance of the recommender systems on multiple metrics at the same
time. These results underline the fact that decision engines should become multi-objective, context-sensitive, and
morally sound instead of being focused on myopic CTR optimization (Zhang et al., 2022) (Zhang et al., 2023).
Engagement Optimization
The last pillar of AABM is concerned with the implementation of adaptive sensing and decision-making into the
outcomes of tangible engagement. It has been proved that adaptive personalization enhances immediate and long-term
user engagement. User receptivity and aﬀective alignment systems are not only beneficial in increasing CTR but also in
increasing retention, satisfaction, and compliance in various domains. Adaptive interventions may lead to clinically
significant changes, as in DIAMANTE (Aguilera et al., 2024), whereas CORES (Lakehal et al., 2025) demonstrates
commercial value in the retail setting. Notably, oﬄine RL and simulators (Wang et al., 2021) (Yuan et al., 2022) have placed
an emphasis on how the engagement might be maximized without jeopardizing the trust of the user, by testing the
strategies in controlled settings, and only deploying them to live environments once that is achieved.

Overall, the AABM framework integrates emotion- and context-sensitive sensing, decision engines, and engagement
optimization into a feedback loop: sensing user state, predicting receptivity, adaptive decision-making, providing
contextually and emotionally congruent engagement, and learning based on feedback. The examined literature
demonstrates that this framework is technically possible, empirically proven, and useful in the fields ranging from health
to e-commerce. The second frontier is to consider the ethical factors, especially privacy, transparency, and manipulative
practices to make sure that AABM provides sustainable value to the users and organizations.
Case studies of adaptive attention-based marketing
Although conceptual models and algorithmic developments prove the technical viability of AABM, applied case studies
are important pieces of evidence on how these systems actually work. Table 2 includes the case studies that involve the
application of adaptive strategies in the real-life context, including gamified applications, online shops, and mobile
notification systems. These studies demonstrate that motivational design, survival models, and sequence-conscious
learning strategies can be translated into quantifiable increase in engagement and user experience.
Gamified Mobile Engagement through Push Notifications
Kunkel et al. explored the impact of motivational aﬀordances in push notifications on consumer behavior in a gamified
mobile application. They compared progression-based, social, and achievement-focused messages sent to the app users.
Based on behavioral data, they discovered that progression notifications were a big boost to engagement and retention,
whereas messages that were sent at the wrong time or inappropriately were potentially disengaging. The paper
emphasizes that customizing notifications based on the motivational state is better in capturing attention, which is in
line with the AABM principle of contextually and emotionally relevant interaction (Kunkel et al., 2023).
Modeling Notification Timing with Survival Analysis
Yuan et al. created a state transition model that is founded on survival analysis to forecast mobile notification
responsiveness. Based on massive behavioral logs, they were able to show that responsiveness was a dynamic process
that was dependent on past interactions with the user. Their model performed better than the logistic regression
baselines and was able to optimize the notification timing and frequency. The case shows how the concept of adapting
message delivery to receptivity in the AABM approach can be operationalized using statistical modeling (Yuan et al.,
2019).
Sequence-Aware Deep Learning for Pop-up Recommendations
Huang and Kao proposed C-3PO as a deep neural network (DNN)-based framework based on the utilization of click-
sequence data to suggest suitable pop-ups. The model also predicted the pop-ups that would draw the interest of the
user and reduce redundancy and annoyance. Experimental proof indicated that the method minimized irrelevant
prompts and enhanced the click-through rates compared with heuristic approaches. This example shows the importance
of sequence-sensitive personalization in adaptive marketing systems by learning behavioral sequences (Huang and Kao,
2019).
Complementary Product Recommendations in E-Commerce
Zhao et al. (Zhao et al., 2017) examined the possibility of integrating mixture models to suggest complementary products
through e-commerce push notifications. Their user purchase and browsing histories were included in their system to
make relevant product bundles. Empirical analysis showed that contextually selected complementary products had
better open and conversion rates than non-adaptive approaches. This case indicates that the ability to adapt the content
in the notification improves the marketing results and experience of the user (Zhao et al., 2017).
Knowledge Extraction from Notifications for Personalization
Li et al. (Li et al., 2018) introduced a system that extracts personal knowledge on smartphone push notifications
automatically. The system recognized template generalizations of common patterns like travel times or delivery updates
by analyzing more than 120 million notifications using template-based methods, maintaining the privacy of the process.

Challenges and ethical considerations
AABM  has technical and ethical challenges in its implementation. Technically, it is still challenging to create robust
models of real-time emotion and context recognition because of the noisy data, small training data, and the
generalizability of models to diﬀerent users and contexts. In addition, adaptive decision engines need to optimize both
short-term metrics (e.g. click-through rate) and long-term metrics (e.g. retention and trust), necessitating multi-objective
optimization methods.
AABM has ethical issues related to privacy, transparency, and autonomy. Table 3 outlines ethical challenges in AABM and
possible safeguards, including privacy-preserving methods and fairness checks. Facial recognition of emotions, typing, or
tracking of locations may be an intrusive form of surveillance and can lead to the abuse of sensitive information. Unless
the users know it, their aﬀective states and receptivity are inferred in real time, posing the risk of covert manipulation.
Moreover, the unequal treatment of diﬀerent demographic groups could also be caused by biases in sentiment or

Limitations of the review
There are several limitations to this review that must be taken into account when interpreting the findings. The literature
search was conducted within the Scopus database, which might have missed relevant literature available in other
databases like Web of Science, PubMed, IEEE Xplore, and ACM Digital Library. Second, the review was limited to
publications from 2018 to 2025; a few seminal studies were included to provide background concepts. As a result, some
earlier contributions may not have been captured comprehensively. Thirdly, only English-language publications were
included, which could add language bias and exclude relevant work that is published in other languages. Lastly, a
standardized screening and thematic analysis procedure was used, but classification and interpretation of studies may be
subject to some degree of subjectivity. The review, despite its limitations, gives a broad overview of recent advances
in AABM and outlines the research trends, challenges, and future directions.
Future research directions
Future work on AABM should be directed toward enhancing technical performance and responsible deployment. One of
the key challenges is the development of non-invasive multimodal emotion and context recognition models that can
integrate behavioral, emotional, and contextual signals in real-time without being computationally expensive for mobile
applications. These systems should also be more adaptable in terms of culture, user groups, and digital environments.
The use of transformer-based architectures and multi-objective reinforcement learning presents promising avenues for
enhancing adaptive decision-making, ensuring a balance between immediate engagement targets and long-term
outcomes like trust, retention, and user satisfaction. Meanwhile, privacy-preserving techniques such as federated
learning, on-device inference, edge AI, and explainable AI (XAI) mechanisms will play a crucial role in minimizing data

Conclusions
AABM is a major step forward in personalized mobile engagement as it combines real-time emotion recognition, context-
aware sensing, and adaptive decision-making into a single intelligent engagement framework. AABM allows for more
dynamic and emotionally resonant interactions that continuously respond to user behavior, context, and attentional
receptivity, as opposed to traditional personalization methods that are based on static preferences or demographic
segmentation. This review summarizes the progress in aﬀective computing, mobile sensing, reinforcement learning,
recommender systems, and adaptive marketing to establish AABM as a new interdisciplinary paradigm for mobile
engagement optimization.
The literature reviewed shows significant advances in multimodal emotion recognition, adaptive personalization, and
reinforcement learning-based engagement systems, which have been applied to gamified environments, e-commerce,
healthcare interventions, and mobile notification environments. However, several important research gaps remain
unresolved. Current research is constrained by the absence of large-scale multimodal datasets that combine emotional,
behavioral, and contextual signals in a variety of real-world settings. Furthermore, most existing evaluation methods
focus on short-term engagement measures and/or laboratory experiments, with limited evidence of real-world
deployments over extended periods. Issues of privacy safeguarding, explainability, algorithmic bias, and manipulative
personalization still impact user trust and large-scale adoption.
Thus, future research should focus on privacy-aware adaptive architectures, explainable decision systems, longitudinal
user studies, and standardized evaluation frameworks beyond click-through rates to trust, retention, and digital well-
being outcomes. The future viability of AABM will rely not only on technological innovations but also on the proper,
transparent, and ethical implementation of intelligent engagement systems in the changing digital environment.
